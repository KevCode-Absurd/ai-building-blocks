# Copy checklist

Every headline, name, subhead, button and page passes this before it goes live. If one answer is wrong, rewrite and check again.

## The promise
- [ ] The headline says what the reader gets at the end, not what the skill or page does.
- [ ] A stranger could picture the result after reading the headline once.
- [ ] The subhead explains how they get it and what they need to bring.
- [ ] Every number is true and checkable (a "30-point checklist" has 30 points).
- [ ] No vague heading or subhead: each names the deliverable, the reader or a number (see "Headings and subheads are never vague" in `COPYWRITING.md`).
- [ ] Logically true: whatever a heading promises, the content under it delivers.

## The words
- [ ] No jargon an outsider wouldn't know (see `docs/WRITING.md` for our own internal terms).
- [ ] No buzzwords without substance (streamline, optimize, leverage, innovative, powerful, seamless).
- [ ] No sentence tries to do more than one thing.
- [ ] Active voice throughout.
- [ ] No "almost", "very", "really", "quite".
- [ ] No exclamation marks.
- [ ] No em dashes.
- [ ] No AI tells: contrast reveals ("it's not X, it's Y"), negation lists, trailing pile-ons, self-answered questions, colon reveals, stock openers (full list in `COPYWRITING.md`).
- [ ] Customer words, not company words.

## The page
- [ ] One idea per section, in an order that builds.
- [ ] The example output is real (made by running the skill), not described.
- [ ] Buttons say exactly what happens, and the same action has the same words everywhere.
- [ ] It sounds like us: sharp, warm, a bit dry. Nothing that could sit unchanged on a competitor's site.

## Honesty
- [ ] No invented statistics, testimonials or results.
- [ ] No real brand names in examples; the example business is labelled.
- [ ] Practice numbers are labelled as practice numbers.
