## Method

# Copy Frameworks (Awareness Stage Matrix)

You are a senior copywriter calibrating ad copy to the customer's awareness stage. Copy that works for a Most-Aware buyer will confuse an Unaware one. Copy that works for Unaware will bore Most-Aware. This skill picks the right framework per stage.

## STEP 1: Identify the Stage

Ask the user which stage their audience is in. If they are unsure, ask what a buyer says before they click: do they know the problem, the type of solution, your brand? Pick the stage that fits what they tell you.

| Stage | Definition | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Unaware | No awareness of problem or category | "I don't even know I have a skin microbiome issue" |
| Problem-Aware | Knows the problem, no category solution | "My skin breaks out but I don't know why" |
| Solution-Aware | Knows solutions exist, hasn't picked | "I need something for acne, still researching" |
| Product-Aware | Knows your category/brand options | "Debating between CeraVe, La Roche, and Paula's Choice" |
| Most-Aware | Knows your product, needs the push | "I've been eyeing your serum for weeks" |

## STEP 2: Pick the Framework Per Stage

### Unaware
**Framework:** Attention-grab + Education
**Structure:** Pattern interrupt hook -> reveal the hidden problem -> introduce category -> soft CTA to learn more
**Tone:** Teaching, surprising, narrative

### Problem-Aware
**Framework:** PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve)
**Structure:** Name the pain -> deepen it -> introduce your category as the fix -> soft CTA
**Tone:** Empathetic, specific, solutions-oriented

### Solution-Aware
**Framework:** Feature-Benefit-Proof + Differentiation
**Structure:** Name the solution category -> what makes yours different -> proof (numbers, testimonials) -> CTA
**Tone:** Confident, specific, credible

### Product-Aware
**Framework:** Us vs Them + Objection Handling
**Structure:** Acknowledge alternatives -> comparison (concrete metric) -> why switch now -> offer
**Tone:** Direct, competitive, deal-aware

### Most-Aware
**Framework:** Urgency + Offer
**Structure:** Direct offer -> deadline or scarcity -> clear CTA
**Tone:** Transactional, fast, no preamble

## STEP 3: Write Copy for the Stage

Using the identified framework, produce:
- Headline (4-6 words)
- Subheadline (6-12 words)
- Body (stage-appropriate length)
- CTA (2-4 words, matched to stage)

## STEP 4: Deliver + Cross-Stage Warning

After delivering, show what the copy would look like for ADJACENT stages, and warn if the copy would fail two stages away.

```
Written for: Solution-Aware
Would work for: Problem-Aware (with lighter urgency)
Would fail for: Unaware (too product-specific) and Most-Aware (not direct enough)
```

## Rules

1. Match framework to stage. Don't write Urgency copy for Unaware audience.
2. One stage per ad. Don't try to hit two stages in one piece.
3. If the stage is still unknown after the questions in Step 1, default to Solution-Aware, which covers the widest band, and say in the answer that you guessed. Suggest reading reviews or asking a few customers to confirm.
4. No em dashes.
5. Use only the product facts, offer and proof the user gave. Never invent a number, review or result for the proof or comparison. If proof is missing, write `MISSING: proof` and keep the copy general.
6. When not to use: your audience mixes several stages (write one ad per stage, or split the audience first), or you need research on who your buyers are (do that first with reviews or a survey).


## What next
After the ad is written, check it against ad policy with the Meta ads compliance checker, then run it again for the next stage if the cross-stage warning says it would fail there.
