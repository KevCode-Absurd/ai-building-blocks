# 10 UGC Script Templates

**Each skeleton has three sections: Opener (0-3s), Middle (3-20s), Close (20-30s). Copy the structure, fill in the product.**

UGC sells because it sounds like one human telling another human something useful. If the creator sounds like an ad, the scroll continues. Every template below is engineered to sound spoken, not written.

---

## Template 1 — Testimonial (Problem Aware, Solution Aware)

**When to use:** You have a legitimate transformation. Creator has before/after or specific result.

**Opener (hook, 0-3s):**
> "I did not expect this to actually work."

or

> "Three weeks ago my skin looked nothing like this."

**Middle (story + proof, 3-20s):**
> "I had tried [3 specific alternatives]. Nothing stuck. Then my [friend / sister / coworker] sent me [product]. I started using it [when/how]. By [day/week] I noticed [specific visible change]. The thing that surprised me was [unexpected benefit]."

**Close (recommendation + CTA, 20-30s):**
> "I am not sponsored. I bought this myself. If you have been dealing with [problem], this is the one I would try first."

---

## Template 2 — Unboxing (Unaware, Problem Aware)

**When to use:** Product is visually interesting. Packaging or presentation is a selling point.

**Opener:**
> "Okay, we are opening this on camera because I have been waiting two weeks for it."

**Middle:**
> "So here is what is inside. [First item + quick reaction]. [Second item + detail]. The thing I want you to notice is [specific product feature]. Most brands in [category] do not include [detail]. This one does."

**Close:**
> "I am going to use this tonight and post day 7. Follow if you want to see."

---

## Template 3 — Tutorial (Solution Aware, Product Aware)

**When to use:** Product requires a routine or has a specific usage method.

**Opener:**
> "The reason most people get nothing from [category] is this one step they skip."

**Middle:**
> "Step one: [action + product]. Notice I am [specific technique detail]. Step two: [next step]. Step three: [final step]. The whole thing takes [time]. If you skip step [X], you are wasting your money."

**Close:**
> "Do this for 14 days. Take a photo on day 1 and day 14. Come back and tell me."

---

## Template 4 — Lifestyle (Unaware)

**When to use:** Cold audience. Product fits inside a daily moment. Aspirational framing.

**Opener:**
> "My 6 AM routine since I stopped buying 12 products."

**Middle:**
> "I wake up. Splash water. One drop of this. That is it. I used to do 7 steps. I used to spend 12 minutes. I used to buy $240 of product a month. Now it is one bottle and 90 seconds."

**Close:**
> "This is the one I use. Link is in the comments."

---

## Template 5 — Problem-Solution (Problem Aware)

**When to use:** Audience has a clear pain they can name.

**Opener:**
> "If your [specific pain] gets worse every [time period], watch this."

**Middle:**
> "Here is what is actually happening. [Explain mechanism in 2 sentences — what the pain IS]. Most products treat [wrong symptom]. This one treats [correct root]. The ingredient / feature that matters is [specific]."

**Close:**
> "You can find it at [brand]. Use it for 2 weeks. If it does not help, return it."

---

## Template 6 — Confession (Problem Aware, Solution Aware)

**When to use:** Creator can admit being wrong about something publicly.

**Opener:**
> "I was wrong about [topic] for [long time]."

**Middle:**
> "I thought [old belief]. So I bought [wrong category]. I spent [specific $ amount]. Nothing worked. Then [someone] said [reframe]. I tried [product]. Within [time], [specific result]."

**Close:**
> "If you are in the same loop I was in, this is the one I would stop and try."

---

## Template 7 — Comparison (Solution Aware, Product Aware)

**When to use:** Audience is shopping. Directly comparing 2-3 options.

**Opener:**
> "I bought three of these at the same time to find out which one actually works."

**Middle:**
> "Option 1: [brand]. $[price]. [Honest 1-line reaction]. Option 2: [brand]. $[price]. [Honest 1-line reaction]. Option 3: [your product]. $[price]. [Specific positive result]. The difference was [specific feature / mechanism]."

**Close:**
> "I returned the other two. This is the one I am sticking with."

---

## Template 8 — Bold Claim (Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware)

**When to use:** You have a specific, defensible, numerical claim.

**Opener:**
> "[Specific number] of [something] in [specific time frame]."

Example:
> "23 pounds in 6 weeks."
> "Zero breakouts in 43 days."
> "Four products replaced by one."

**Middle:**
> "Here is the proof. [Show photo / metric / receipt]. Here is what I changed: [only thing]. Here is what I did NOT change: [context]. The product is [name]. The mechanism is [1 sentence]."

**Close:**
> "You can verify it. [Link to study / reviews / brand page]."

---

## Template 9 — POV / Mirror (Unaware, Problem Aware)

**When to use:** You want the viewer to see themselves in the creator. First-person framing.

**Opener:**
> "POV: [specific universal moment]."

Example:
> "POV: it is 6 AM, your skin is flaking, and you are out of the serum that used to work."

**Middle:**
> "[Continue the scene as the viewer]. You grab [old solution]. It stings. You check your cabinet. Nothing new. You remember the one your friend kept posting about. [Product]. You try it. [Specific sensation]. First time in months your skin does not feel tight."

**Close:**
> "Same routine every morning since. Link in bio."

---

## Template 10 — Anti-Hype / Skeptic (All stages, especially Most Aware)

**When to use:** Audience is fatigued on ads. Skepticism is high.

**Opener:**
> "I did NOT want this to work."

or

> "If this is sponsored I am going to say so. It is not."

**Middle:**
> "I bought this expecting to return it. I tried it [X] times. The first [Y] days, nothing. Day [Z] I noticed [specific change]. I am still skeptical about [honest reservation]. But [specific positive] is real."

**Close:**
> "I am not the person who usually recommends this kind of thing. I am recommending this one."

## How to use these templates

1. **Pick the template that matches your awareness stage.** Cross-reference the awareness-stage-copy-matrix.
2. **Record a 15-second version first.** Get the opener right before extending.
3. **Do not read.** Memorize the beat, then say it as yourself. Reading kills the UGC effect.
4. **Shoot 3 takes.** Keep the one where you forgot the camera was on.
5. **B-roll the middle.** Opener and close are on-camera face. Middle can cut to product, routine, or result.
6. **Caption matches tactic.** If opener is Confession, caption is Confession. If opener is Bold Claim, caption is Bold Claim. Do not mix.

---

## Script hygiene rules

- **No em dashes in script.** You cannot say an em dash out loud. If you see one in your draft, rewrite the sentence.
- **No marketing phrases.** Ban: game-changer, revolutionary, life-changing, unlock, leverage, level up.
- **Contractions on.** "I was not" reads formal. "I wasn't" reads human. UGC = human.
- **One idea per sentence.** If you have a comma and an "and," split it into two sentences.
- **Specificity beats adjectives.** "$48 bottle" > "expensive bottle." "Day 14" > "two weeks later."
- **End on a clean sentence.** No "anyway" or "so yeah." The last line is the CTA or the closing thought.
