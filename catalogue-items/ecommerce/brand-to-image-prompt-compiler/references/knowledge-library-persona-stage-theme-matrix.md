# Persona × Stage × Theme Matrix

**The lookup table that picks the right theme for the right persona in the right stage. Pin this. Use this before every creative brief.**

---

## How to read this matrix

- **Rows:** 5 awareness stages (Schwartz / TEEP-mapped)
- **Columns:** Common persona archetypes (adapt to your account)
- **Cells:** The 1-2 theme(s) most likely to convert for that persona at that stage

Each cell is a recommendation, not a rule. Validate with your own test data.

---

## The matrix

|  | Value Seeker | Performance Buyer | Lifestyle Aspirant | Wellness / Purist | Skeptic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Lifestyle | Educational | Aspirational | Educational | Educational + Curiosity |
| **Problem Aware** | PAS | PAS | Aspirational + PAS | Educational + PAS | PAS + Confession |
| **Solution Aware** | Us vs Them | Features/Benefits | Lifestyle + Social Proof | Features/Benefits + Social Proof | Us vs Them + Social Proof |
| **Product Aware** | Social Proof + Urgency | Features/Benefits + Social Proof | Social Proof + FOMO | Social Proof + Features/Benefits | Social Proof + Risk Reversal |
| **Most Aware** | Urgency | Urgency | FOMO + Urgency | Urgency (soft, brand-aligned) | Urgency + Guarantee restated |

## Persona definitions

### Value Seeker
- Buys on price-to-benefit ratio
- Compares prices aggressively
- Responds to "get more for less"
- Skeptical of premium positioning

**Voice:** Direct, numeric, no-nonsense
**Hooks they respond to:** Bold Claim, Contrast
**What falls flat:** Aspirational identity, luxury framing

### Performance Buyer
- Buys based on outcome and proof
- Reads ingredient lists and spec sheets
- Responds to data
- Has tried category before

**Voice:** Technical, confident, specific
**Hooks they respond to:** Bold Claim, Contrast, Curiosity (if educational)
**What falls flat:** Vague promises, pure lifestyle

### Lifestyle Aspirant
- Buys based on identity and belonging
- Responds to visual and emotional cues
- Less price-sensitive
- Willing to pay for "feel"

**Voice:** Warm, aspirational, scene-setting
**Hooks they respond to:** Relatability, Confession
**What falls flat:** Technical spec sheets, heavy comparison

### Wellness / Purist
- Buys based on ingredient philosophy
- Strong opinions on "clean" / "natural" / "ethical"
- Loyal once trust is earned
- Research-driven

**Voice:** Educational, transparent, values-aligned
**Hooks they respond to:** Curiosity, Confession
**What falls flat:** Heavy urgency, discount-led pitches

### Skeptic
- Distrusts advertising by default
- Buys after significant validation
- Needs risk reversal
- Has been burned before

**Voice:** Anti-hype, direct, honest
**Hooks they respond to:** Confession, Contrast (with proof)
**What falls flat:** Bold Claims without evidence, urgency without reason

## How to use this matrix

### Step 1: Identify your persona
Pull from your Psychology Brief (see psychology-brief-template.md) or interview data.

### Step 2: Identify the TEEP / awareness stage
Run the awareness-stage-quiz.md or pull from your audience source:
- Cold prospecting → Unaware / Problem Aware
- Content viewers → Problem Aware / Solution Aware
- Product page viewers → Solution / Product Aware
- Cart abandoners → Product Aware
- Repeat buyers → Most Aware

### Step 3: Pull the theme from the intersection
Use the cell directly. If it says "Social Proof + Urgency," build an ad that combines those specifically.

### Step 4: Validate with 1 test ad
Before scaling, ship 1 ad at the recommended theme. Measure:
- Hook rate (is the opener earning the 5s?)
- CVR (is the theme closing?)
- CPA (is the economics working?)

If all 3 are above baseline, scale. If any underperforms, go to Step 5.

### Step 5: Try the SECOND option
Every cell has a primary theme. Most cells also have a secondary. Ship the secondary in week 2 if primary underperforms.

## Common patterns to watch for

### Pattern 1: Value Seeker + Most Aware = always urgency
If you have a Value Seeker persona in Most Aware stage, urgency always wins. Do not overthink.

### Pattern 2: Skeptic + Solution Aware = comparison with proof
Skeptics in Solution stage need Us vs Them with REAL data. Vague comparison ads underperform in this cell.

### Pattern 3: Wellness Purist + Problem Aware = education
Pure PAS agitation feels manipulative to this persona. Lead with education that happens to reveal the problem.

### Pattern 4: Lifestyle Aspirant + Unaware = aspirational first, not product
Introducing the product in a cold Lifestyle Aspirant ad kills the ad. They need to want the IDENTITY first, product second.

### Pattern 5: Performance Buyer + Product Aware = ingredient-led
Skip the hero image. Lead with the ingredient, the spec, the mechanism. Performance Buyers close on technical fit.

## Extending the matrix

If your account has a persona not listed above, extend the matrix by:

1. Defining the persona (voice, hooks, what falls flat)
2. Running 1 test ad per stage (5 tests total, minimum)
3. Locking in the winning theme per cell after 2 weeks of data
4. Documenting in your own version of this matrix

The goal is a persona-specific theme matrix for each major persona in your account.

---

## Anti-patterns

### Anti-pattern 1: Same theme for every persona at a stage
If every Problem Aware ad uses PAS regardless of persona, you are flattening your persona work. Each persona needs its own cell.

### Anti-pattern 2: Jumping the matrix
Running an Urgency ad to an Unaware buyer because "urgency drives CPA" ignores that the buyer has no desire yet. Urgency on no desire is manipulation and converts badly.

### Anti-pattern 3: Ignoring the "Avoid" column
Every theme has stages it should avoid (see theme-library-catalog.md). The matrix above respects those; do not override.

### Anti-pattern 4: Using this matrix as a production template
The matrix tells you the THEME, not the execution. Every cell still needs its own hook, visual, voiceover, and format choice based on format compatibility rules.

  └── Skeptic? → Anti-hype, proof, guarantees

  └── Cold (Unaware) → Educational or Aspirational
