# Buyer Psychology Interview Guide

**24 questions across the 4 TEEP stages. 6 per stage. Run this with 3-5 real customers. You will have a psychology brief that beats any survey.**

---

## How to use

1. Identify 3-5 recent buyers (ideally within 60 days of purchase)
2. Offer a $25-50 gift card or credit for 30 minutes
3. Record with permission. Take notes on EXACT phrases
4. Do not pitch, do not explain, do not defend — LISTEN
5. Ask "tell me more" twice before moving on
6. Feed the raw transcripts into the `/buyer-psychology` prompt for synthesis

---

## Ground rules before you start

- **You are a researcher, not a marketer.** Your job is curiosity, not confirmation.
- **No yes/no questions.** Every question must be open-ended.
- **Silence is fine.** Wait 5 seconds after they answer. They will say more.
- **Capture the exact phrasing.** "My skin felt papery" is infinitely better than "my skin felt dry".

---

## SECTION 1 — TRIGGER (6 questions)

Goal: find the EVENT that made them start looking.

**Q1.** Take me back to the first moment you noticed [PROBLEM]. Where were you? What were you doing?

**Q2.** What made that particular moment different from all the other times you had dealt with [PROBLEM] before?

**Q3.** Did something specific happen before that moment? A conversation, a photo, an event?

**Q4.** How long had you been living with [PROBLEM] before you decided to actually do something about it?

**Q5.** What did you tell yourself at that moment — in your head?

**Q6.** If you had to name the one thing that finally made you stop ignoring it, what would you say?

**What you are listening for:** the specific event, the internal monologue, the shift from tolerating to acting. This becomes your Trigger creative.

---

## SECTION 2 — EXPLORATION (6 questions)

Goal: map how they learned about the category.

**Q7.** Where did you look first? Google? Amazon? TikTok? Ask a friend?

**Q8.** What was the first product or brand you remember seeing?

**Q9.** What did you learn in the first week that surprised you?

**Q10.** Was there a moment where you realized the problem was bigger or different than you thought?

**Q11.** What was the most useful thing anyone said or wrote that helped you understand your options?

**Q12.** What made you eliminate some options before even trying them?

**What you are listening for:** channels, trusted sources, category misconceptions, elimination logic. This becomes your Exploration creative AND your channel strategy.

---

## SECTION 3 — EVALUATION (6 questions)

Goal: find the comparison criteria and the dealbreaker.

**Q13.** By the time you were ready to buy, which 2 or 3 products were you comparing?

**Q14.** What specifically made you consider ours over the others?

**Q15.** What almost stopped you from buying ours? What hesitation did you have?

**Q16.** Was there a moment you were about to close the tab and walk away?

**Q17.** What made you decide to trust us? Reviews, a specific claim, a guarantee?

**Q18.** If our product had been $10 more expensive, would you still have bought? What about $25 more?

**What you are listening for:** the 2-3 competitors, the specific moment of trust, the hesitation (which tells you the objection you must address). This becomes your Evaluation creative AND your LP proof stack.

---

## SECTION 4 — PURCHASE (6 questions)

Goal: figure out what closed the sale.

**Q19.** What was your state of mind the hour before you bought? Were you in a rush? On the fence? Convinced?

**Q20.** Did any specific thing happen right before the purchase that tipped you over?

**Q21.** Was there an offer, discount, or shipping benefit that mattered? How much?

**Q22.** How did you tell yourself "okay, I am doing this"? What was the internal permission?

**Q23.** Did you tell anyone you were buying it? If so, what did you say?

**Q24.** If I could rewind 30 seconds before you clicked buy, what would have made you NOT buy?

**What you are listening for:** the closer (offer, urgency, free shipping, timing), the internal justification, the social proof moment (telling someone). This becomes your Purchase creative.

---

## Bonus questions if you have time

- What do you wish the brand had told you BEFORE you bought?
- In one sentence, how would you describe this product to a friend?
- Have you recommended it? To whom?
- What would have to change for you to NEVER buy again?
- What would have to change for you to buy this again next month?

---

## After the interview

Same day, write up:

1. **Trigger event** (in the buyer's own words)
2. **Exploration channels** (where they looked)
3. **Evaluation finalists** (who you were compared against)
4. **Purchase closer** (what specifically moved them)
5. **Quotable phrases** (5-10 exact sentences you want to steal)

Do this within 24 hours of the call. Memory fades fast.

---

## How to synthesize across 3-5 interviews

After all 3-5 calls are done:

- **Pattern — Trigger:** What type of trigger event recurs? Name the most common one.
- **Pattern — Exploration:** Which channels show up in 3+ interviews? That is where your Exploration creative lives.
- **Pattern — Evaluation:** Which competitor name shows up in 3+ interviews? That is your primary positioning enemy.
- **Pattern — Purchase:** What offer or signal closed the sale repeatedly? That is your closer.
- **Recurring phrase cluster:** Which 2-3 phrases came up across multiple interviews? Those go straight into ad copy.

---

## What NOT to ask

- "Why did you buy?" (Too direct. They will rationalize, not remember.)
- "What could we do better?" (Shifts them into consultant mode.)
- "Would you recommend us?" (Yes/no dead end.)
- "Was the price fair?" (Loaded. They will soften.)
- "What would you pay for this?" (Hypothetical. Unreliable.)

---

## Pro moves

1. **Ask the same question two different ways 10 minutes apart.** If the answer changes, you have a rationalization. If it stays, you have the truth.
2. **If they stall, go concrete.** "Pull out your phone. Show me what you Googled." Specifics unlock memory.
3. **Record, always.** Transcripts catch phrases your note-taking misses.
4. **Share the synthesis back.** Your buyer will appreciate the closure, and you will catch your own misinterpretations.
