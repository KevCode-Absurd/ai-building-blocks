# TEEP Framework Card

**Trigger, Emotion, Evidence, Payoff. The 4-element ad psychology model. A strong ad hits all four. A weak ad hits one and hopes.**

---

## The 4 elements

| Element | What it does | What belongs here |
|---|---|---|
| **T — Trigger** | Makes the buyer notice that a problem exists or could be solved | A moment, event, or cue that the buyer just experienced |
| **E — Emotion** | Gives the trigger weight and urgency | Frustration, aspiration, fear, identity, validation, relief |
| **E — Evidence** | Answers "why believe this works" | Social proof, demonstration, credentials, comparison, data |
| **P — Payoff** | Answers "what do I get if I buy" | Functional, identity, social, relief, or time-money saved |

Every ad should carry all four. Weak ads skip Emotion (feel clinical) or Evidence (feel hypey) or Payoff (feel vague).

---

## Trigger types

A Trigger is rarely a feeling. It is almost always an event or cue:

1. **Physical** — symptom gets worse, gets noticed (skin flares, jeans tight, back hurts)
2. **Social** — friend mentions something, comment, wedding, reunion
3. **Calendar** — season change, birthday, new year, holiday
4. **Financial** — raise, bonus, refund, tax return
5. **Life-event** — pregnancy, move, new job, breakup, kid starts school
6. **Content** — creator video, friend's post, TV episode, article
7. **Competitive** — someone's transformation, someone's Instagram, someone's before/after

If your ad does not match a trigger type, it is not starting in the buyer's head. It is starting in yours.

## What each element sounds like

### Trigger
- "Wait, do I have that?"
- "I never thought about that before."
- "Why is that happening to me now?"

Your ad's job: name the problem or moment they have been ignoring.

### Emotion
- Frustration ("I've tried everything")
- Aspiration ("I want to feel like that")
- Fear ("what if this gets worse")
- Identity ("people like me")
- Relief ("finally")

Your ad's job: give the trigger a feeling. Not adjectives. Situations.

### Evidence
- Social ("48,000 customers")
- Scientific ("clinically tested")
- Credentialing ("built by dermatologists")
- Demonstration ("watch it work")
- Comparison ("vs the leading alternative")

Your ad's job: pick the proof type that matches the objection.

### Payoff
- Functional ("sleep better")
- Identity ("the person who has it together")
- Social ("compliments")
- Relief ("stop thinking about it")
- Time or money saved ("no more trial and error")

Your ad's job: name the specific outcome, not a category of outcome.

## Ad structure by TEEP element

### Video ads

```
0-3s:   Trigger hook
3-8s:   Emotion amplification
8-20s:  Evidence (show proof)
20-25s: Payoff reinforcement
25-30s: CTA
```

### Static ads

```
Headline:        Trigger or Payoff
Supporting copy: Emotion + Evidence
Visual:          Payoff implied (outcome, result, identity)
CTA:             Explicit payoff
```

---

## The single most common mistake

Running an Evidence-heavy ad to a buyer who has not been Triggered yet. "2x peptide concentration vs the leading serum" is Evidence. A buyer who has not yet been Triggered reads that as noise. The ad skips their brain and costs you budget.

Fix: open with Trigger. Carry Emotion. Land Evidence and Payoff.

---

## Coverage check

If you are running 4 ads and none of them name a specific Trigger, you do not have a creative rotation. You have one ad repeated four ways.

Healthy coverage:
- At least one ad per dominant Trigger type for your audience
- At least one ad per dominant Emotion
- Evidence varied across social, demo, and comparison
- Payoff stated explicitly, not implied

---

## Remember

- Every strong ad carries T, E, E, P.
- One element per region of the ad. Do not stack all four in the headline.
- If you cannot point to where Trigger, Emotion, Evidence, and Payoff live in your ad, the ad is too generic to work.
- No em dashes.
