# TEEP Framework Card

The 4-element diagnostic for any ad: Trigger, Emotion, Evidence, Payoff.

---

## T — Trigger

**Definition:** The moment or event that makes the buyer notice the problem.

### Types
- **External:** friend mentioned it, saw a TikTok, ran out of existing product, read a review
- **Internal:** 3pm crash, mirror moment, chronic friction, recurring pain
- **Seasonal:** new year, summer, post-vacation, end of quarter

### Good trigger markers
- Specific time of day ("3pm crash")
- Specific situation ("when I travel")
- Specific physical cue ("tight jeans in morning")
- Concrete external event ("doctor's checkup last week")

### Bad trigger markers
- Vague ("when I want to feel healthy")
- Generic ("when I'm busy")
- Universal ("when life gets hard")

---

## E — Emotion

**Definition:** The feeling attached to the trigger.

### Types
- **Frustration:** "I'm tired of this not working"
- **Aspiration:** "I want to be that kind of person"
- **Fear:** "I don't want X to happen"
- **Identity:** "This is who I am"
- **Validation:** "I want what she has"
- **Relief:** "I want to stop worrying"

### Good emotion work
- Shown, not stated ("I caught myself yawning in the client meeting")
- Specific, not generic ("the 4am panic" vs "anxiety")
- Permission-giving ("it's okay to just want something that works")

### Bad emotion work
- Over-dramatic ("change your life forever")
- Marketing-fake ("you deserve this")
- Adjective-heavy ("amazing, incredible, wonderful")

---

## E — Evidence

**Definition:** The proof the buyer needs to believe the solution works.

### Types
- **Social proof:** reviews, testimonials, creator use, sales volume
- **Scientific:** clinical studies, absorption rates, ingredient transparency
- **Credentialing:** doctors, scientists, publications, awards
- **Demonstration:** before/after, live results, time-lapse
- **Comparison:** side-by-side with competitors, chart format

### Good evidence
- Specific numbers ("97% absorption in Dr. X's study" not "proven effective")
- Attributed quotes ("Sarah K., 52, verified buyer")
- Visible proof (actual label, actual chart, actual before/after)
- Third-party sources (not just "we tested it ourselves")

### Bad evidence
- Vague claims ("clinically proven" with no study)
- Generic stars ("4.8 stars" without count)
- Self-referential ("we're the best")

---

## P — Payoff

**Definition:** The specific outcome the buyer expects post-purchase.

### Types
- **Functional:** "I'll sleep 40 minutes longer"
- **Identity:** "I'll be the person who wakes up at 6am"
- **Social:** "I'll get compliments"
- **Relief:** "I'll stop worrying about afternoon crashes"
- **Time/money saved:** "10 minutes saved every morning"

### Good payoff
- Specific and modest ("consistent energy" not "life-changing vitality")
- Tied to a concrete change ("no more 3pm crash")
- Framed in buyer's language, not brand language

### Bad payoff
- Grandiose ("transform your life")
- Abstract ("feel your best")
- Brand-centric ("unlock your potential with X")

---

## The TEEP Audit

For any ad, score 0-2 per element:
- 0: Absent
- 1: Weak / implied
- 2: Strong / explicit

**Total 6-8:** High-quality ad
**Total 4-5:** Missing critical element, fix before scaling
**Total 0-3:** Rebuild

---

## TEEP by Funnel Position

| Funnel | Lead Element | Supporting | Can Skip |
|--------|-------------|------------|----------|
| Cold prospecting | Trigger + Emotion | Payoff | Evidence (brief mention enough) |
| Mid-funnel retargeting | Evidence | Payoff | Trigger (assumed) |
| Bottom-funnel retargeting | Payoff + Urgency | Evidence | Trigger + Emotion (assumed) |

---

## Common TEEP Failures

1. **Payoff-only ads (worst):** "Feel amazing!" with no trigger, emotion, or evidence. Prospecting money going nowhere.
2. **Evidence stacking without emotion:** "97% absorption! Third-party tested! 2,400 reviews!" but no reason to care.
3. **Trigger without payoff:** "Are you tired at 3pm?" and then... nothing. Loop not closed.
4. **Emotion without evidence:** Heart-tugging but skeptical audience bounces off "too good to be true."

Any ad that feels flat is usually missing one specific TEEP element. Identify it, fix it, retest.
