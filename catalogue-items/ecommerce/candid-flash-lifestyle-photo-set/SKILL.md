# Candid Flash Lifestyle Photo Set

A set of stills that look as if one person shot one real evening on a small film camera with the flash on. The people could be the brand's buyers and the product is exactly the one that ships.

## Inputs
- **Product and variant** (required): one product per set.
- **Real product photos** (required), including one in use or in hand.
- **Buyer and moment** (required): who buys it, where and when they use it.
- **Brand world** (required): places, colours, never-show list. If the brand's world has no evening or gathering, ask before using this look.
- **Frame count** (optional): 8 is usual. **People or product only** (optional): a mix like 5 with people and 3 product only works well.
- **Where it will be used** (required): sets sizes and jobs.

## Steps
1. Run `product-truth-sheet-from-photos`. Name the product in the same exact words in every plan ("the 500 ml matte-black vacuum flask with a steel twist cap"), never by a general noun, and state what it is not ("not glossy, no plastic handle, not a bottle with a flip lid").
2. Work out the flash world from the brand and show it with the shot list: who is in frame (age band, mix, dress; fictional adults); 6 to 10 real objects from where the product is used, none that look like a competitor or carry another brand's mark; where (warm walls of cream, pink or terracotta, in real rooms; evening and night are the look's home, a morning or outdoor brand keeps its own time of day with the flash as fill); the 3 to 6 real things people do with it.
3. Write every action with its mechanics: which hand, holding what, where the other hand is. If the physics cannot be pictured, change the verb. Examples: thumbing a flask cap open while the other hand steadies the base; a bar of soap worked between both palms under a running tap; a bag zipped one-handed while the other hand holds the strap; a lamp switched on with a thumb. For room objects, people are around the thing, not holding it up. An action the product facts do not describe is not shown.
4. Choose frames, each with job and place of use.
   - With people (of 5, about 2 alone, 1 pair, 2 groups; never all groups): alone and absorbed (in use, feed ad); alone, the moment after (pleased, product still in hand; payoff); a pair (one shows, passes or does it for the other; sharing); a group (product in the middle of an evening; brand world, email header).
   - Product only: flash glint (where it lives at night, one hard shadow, a glint on its edge; social hero); scattered (out of the pack on a surface; for a single solid object, dropped where last used, such as a kettle on the counter beside a used mug); held up (adult hand toward the lens, label to camera, fingers on the sides; scale); frozen (mid-pour, mid-toss, mid-zip, only a motion the facts describe); stacked (several of the same product; bundle ad).
   - A children's product shows no child and no part of one: in an adult's hands, packed, or where a child would use it.
   Show the list (frame, who or treatment, action, job, size) for approval before anything is made.
5. Make one test frame with the product in a hand. Check it against the truth sheet; once approved, attach it to later frames beside the real photos. Attach the product's own photos to every frame that shows it, tagged with their job. Never attach a photo of real people: the model returns them; cast people in words.
6. Write each prompt as one paragraph in this order: "Candid 35mm film photograph, direct on-camera flash, warm amber colour cast, the feel of a small camera at a party, gentle grain" plus camera angle; WHERE (place, wall or surface); WHO (how many adults, hair, clothes, one thing that sets each apart); PRODUCT (the exact product in Image 1, label sharp and readable, its exact-words description, size against a hand, "not ..."); ACTION (the mechanics); FEELING (laughing hard at something off frame, or absorbed in the task); "Eyes on the task and each other, never at the camera"; THINGS IN THE ROOM (4 to 6); "Hard flash shadow on the wall behind, warm and joyful. No added words."
7. Look at every frame at full size beside a real photo of the product. When one fails, change the part of the prompt that failed; never resend the same words. Log each failure as one line.
8. Deliver frames named by number, job and content (`03_in-use_serum-drops`), apart from the real reference photos and marked generated, with a short honest note: keepers, small flaws, what was remade and why. Optional on request: turn an approved still into a few seconds of video with one small action and one reaction at real speed, slight handheld shake, no cut, nobody turning to the camera.

## Output
The flash world and approved shot list; the frames; the check notes. The look itself (every frame): hard-edged flash shadow just behind the subject, bright foreground falling to a darker background, warm amber cast, gentle grain, handheld, close, slightly off level, wide enough to show the room, never cool, never studio-even, never dim and moody.

## Checks
- Anyone looking at the camera fails the frame.
- The label is sharp, faces the camera and is not under the flash hot spot; the amber cast is on the scene (a white pack reads warm white, not orange).
- The product is the right thing, container, closure, colours and size in the hand; one use is one real amount.
- Hands could really hold that; five fingers; no prop waved rather than used; no mirror that doubles the product.
- No child or part of one, no real person's face, no competitor mark, no added words, date stamps or borders.
- Tone stays warm and joyful whoever is cast; tattoos, piercings and dark clothes are casting, not mood.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <photo of product in use | buyer moment | product fact>`. Show the product closed and at rest, from an angle a photo shows, until it arrives; no frame may depend on the missing line.

## Avoid
- The product laid out as in a catalogue; a staged-looking room.
- Using these as the first image of a product page: the cast and glare make colour and label hard to judge.
- Food, bags or contents that are branded or that the product could not hold.
- A dark or edgy tone; anyone posing.
