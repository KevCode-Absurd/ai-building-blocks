# 13 Ad Theme Categories

Use this when classifying competitor ads. One theme per ad, picked by dominant signal.

---

## 1. Urgency/Scarcity
**Signals:** "Limited time," "Only X left," countdown timers, "Ends tonight," flash sale language.
**When it works:** Most-aware audiences with existing demand. DR channels.
**Trap:** Overused in discount-heavy categories. Loses power when every ad screams urgency.

## 2. Social Proof
**Signals:** Star ratings, review counts, "as seen on," press logos, testimonial quotes, "10,000+ customers."
**When it works:** Problem-aware to product-aware. New brands without authority.
**Trap:** Category table stakes. Everyone runs it. Hard to differentiate on alone.

## 3. Discount/Offer
**Signals:** % off, BOGO, free shipping, discount code visible, bundle pricing.
**When it works:** Most-aware audiences. Bottom of funnel. Retargeting.
**Trap:** Race to the bottom. Conditions buyers to wait for offers.

## 4. Problem-Solution
**Signals:** Pain-point hook ("Tired of X?"), then product as fix. PAS framework visible.
**When it works:** Problem-aware. Strong fit for diagnostic categories (health, productivity, skincare).
**Trap:** Requires naming a specific pain. Generic pain falls flat.

## 5. Transformation
**Signals:** Before/after, results imagery, "X days later," progress timelines.
**When it works:** Weight loss, skincare, fitness, home improvement. Visual-heavy categories.
**Trap:** Platform policy restrictions. Beware of body transformation rules on Meta.

## 6. Educational
**Signals:** "How X works," ingredient breakdowns, "What you should know about Y," teaching-first copy.
**When it works:** Solution-aware to product-aware. Complex or technical products.
**Trap:** Can feel slow. Requires strong hook or audience won't stay for the lesson.

## 7. UGC/Testimonial
**Signals:** Phone-shot aesthetic, real customer on camera, unboxing, selfie-mode, "I tried X for 30 days."
**When it works:** Every stage except most-aware. Lowers ad-feel, raises trust.
**Trap:** Overcrowded in DTC. Everyone runs UGC. Differentiation is in hook + creator fit.

## 8. Lifestyle/Aspirational
**Signals:** Aspirational imagery, ideal-customer-living-their-best-life, beautiful environments, minimal copy.
**When it works:** Unaware to problem-aware. Premium positioning. Brand-building.
**Trap:** Vague. Doesn't convert without context. Wastes budget at DR scale.

## 9. Founder/Brand Story
**Signals:** Founder on camera, "why I started this," origin story, handheld authenticity.
**When it works:** Any stage, especially unaware. Humanizes a brand. Builds long-term equity.
**Trap:** Requires a real story. Manufactured founder narratives feel fake instantly.

## 10. Product Feature
**Signals:** Specs, ingredients list, materials, technology callouts, spec sheet feel.
**When it works:** Solution-aware to product-aware. Technical products.
**Trap:** Boring without emotion. Features don't sell, benefits do.

## 11. Comparison (Us vs Them)
**Signals:** Direct competitor name, side-by-side tables, "vs [competitor]," comparison chart.
**When it works:** Solution-aware. Category leader or clear challenger positioning.
**Trap:** Legal considerations. Don't make unprovable claims.

## 12. Trend/Meme
**Signals:** Cultural reference, trending format, platform-native joke, current meme structure.
**When it works:** Brand awareness, younger audiences, Gen-Z platforms.
**Trap:** Ages fast. A meme ad has 2-4 weeks of relevance max.

## 13. Authority/Expert
**Signals:** Doctor/scientist/certified-expert on camera, credentials shown, institutional endorsement, clinical study references.
**When it works:** High-stakes purchases (health, finance, babies). Skeptical audiences.
**Trap:** Expensive to produce. Regulatory exposure if claims overreach.

---

## Classification Heuristic

For each ad, ask:
1. What's the dominant emotional pull?
2. What evidence does it lead with?
3. Who's on camera (if video)?

Dominant signal wins. An ad with light social proof and a founder on camera telling his origin is Founder Story, not Social Proof.

---

## Gap Analysis Flow

1. Tag every competitor ad into one theme
2. Count themes per competitor
3. Build heat map (themes x competitors)
4. Identify:
   - **Saturated:** 3+ competitors heavy in theme
   - **Contested:** 2 competitors
   - **Emerging:** 1 competitor
   - **Open:** 0 competitors

Open + Emerging lanes = opportunities. Saturated lanes = only enter with a differentiated execution.
