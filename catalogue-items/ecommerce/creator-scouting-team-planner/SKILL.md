# Creator scouting team planner

You plan a small in-house team that finds creators for a store, and you check whether a TikTok Shop is a good fit. The method comes from two operators of large programs. The fit numbers are their opinions, so say to test them on the first 10 posts. This differs from a general creator partnership plan by adding the scouting team, the method for breaking out of one feed, and the halo read.

## Steps
1. **Staff scouts who know the buyer's feeds.** Pick scouts for what they know: the niche, the platform, and the creators the buyer follows. Do not pick or reject anyone by age, gender or looks. Hiring rules differ by place, so tell the owner to check local employment rules before recruiting. A solo owner can scout alone if they know the niche.
2. **Build three levels.** Scouts find creators. Relationship managers keep deals going. One lead, who can be promoted from the scouts. A solo owner gets a small version: one scout (the owner) and a weekly list of about 20 creators.
3. **Break the feed bubble.** From their own logged-in account, scouts search in the app by hashtag, keyword and location, and go through the follow lists of creators the buyer already follows. This shows more creators than one city's feed. They must follow the platform's terms: no VPNs, extra accounts or other tricks to reset the feed, and no bulk following.
4. **Start where the buyer is.** Find the creators your buyer already follows in one niche, then widen once you win that niche.
5. **Mix sizes like a portfolio.** On a small budget, stick to about 5,000 to 50,000 followers.
6. **Talk to creators directly.** Skip agencies. The source team learned birthdays, sent gifts and knew when a creator had a hard day. Only keep personal details a creator chose to share. Closeness is why creators keep posting. Each relationship manager checks in with every creator on their list about once every two weeks, and logs it.
7. **Keep briefs light.** Free product and basic guidelines. Leave creative control to the creator. A rigid script cuts views. You still check each post for claims you cannot back up.
8. **Disclosure.** Every brief says that gifted or paid posts must carry #ad or the platform's paid-partnership label.
9. **Treat content and shop as two games.** TikTok content and TikTok Shop are separate with separate algorithms and plans.
10. **Run the shop fit check.** Impulse price: under about $29, maybe $39 (one operator's view). Female-first audience, meaning women will buy it for themselves or someone else. Spillover: the product also sells on Amazon, your own site or other sites. The female-first test describes the buyer only. It never applies to staff or to creator selection. Add a fourth check, the margin: selling price minus landed cost, minus the shop's fees and the creator commission, must leave a margin the owner accepts. Show the sum. If landed cost is missing, write `MISSING: landed cost` and do not say go. Say go only if all four hold, otherwise no-go with the missing one named. The second operator says to test the fit rules on your first 10 posts.
11. **Read the halo, not just codes.** One founder says more than 30 percent of his shop sales led to buyers on Amazon, Target and Walmart. The Ridge founder says a shop captures only about 5 percent of the value it creates. Both are single-speaker claims. Watch Amazon search traffic, branded search and orders on other channels the week a video gets views.
12. **Test the halo with an off switch.** One story in the sources says a brand turned off spend in one place and revenue did not change. A store can try a short, planned pause in one channel and see if total revenue moves. Mark it as a test and keep it short.
13. **Choose tracking by channel count.** Selling in several channels, you can read the halo and skip codes. A store that sells only on its own site should keep codes or tracked links, since no-attribution is a harder bet there. Because lift can land elsewhere, a multi-channel seller can afford generous affiliate pay.
14. **Check for local bias.** The source's biggest named mistake was a head office favouring creators from its own city over the national buyer. Compare creators found by region with buyers by region.

## Checks
Run these before handing back. Fix any that fail, or list them under `CHECKS MISSED:`.
- The team size matches the people and hours the person gave.
- Scouts only browse in the feed-breaking step.
- The fit check shows each rule with the store's numbers.
- Every brief carries the disclosure rule.
- Single-speaker figures are labelled as claims to test.
- A store that sells only on its own site keeps codes or links.
- The off-switch test is marked as a test and kept short.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>`. With no buyer description, do not guess: ask. With no sales by channel, skip the halo read and say so.

## Avoid
- A team plan bigger than the people available. The source team is 15 to 25 people. Do not copy that for a solo owner.
- Choosing scouts by age, gender or looks. Choose them for niche and platform knowledge.
- Fake accounts that post or like to push engagement.
- Treating the fit numbers as rules.
- Keeping personal details a creator did not share.
