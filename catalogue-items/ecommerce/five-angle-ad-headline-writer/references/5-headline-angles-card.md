# 5 Headline Angles Card

The five rhetorical angles for any ad headline. Use this as a testing framework.

---

## The 5 Angles

### 1. Benefit-First
State the outcome. Lead with what the customer gets.

**When to use:** Clear benefit, provable claim, numbers available.
**Pattern:** "[Outcome]. [Specificity]."
**Examples:**
- "Absorb 5x faster."
- "Sleep 40 minutes longer."
- "97% absorption. Measured."

**Trap:** If the benefit is vague, this angle dies. Don't write "Feel your best."

---

### 2. Curiosity
Open a loop. Make the viewer want the next sentence.

**When to use:** Complex claim, education needed, contrarian position.
**Pattern:** "Why [surprising thing]", "What nobody tells you about...", "The reason [thing] fails."
**Examples:**
- "Why most collagen fails."
- "What's really in your pre-workout."
- "The reason your skin pills in winter."

**Trap:** Clickbait. Don't promise a reveal you don't deliver.

---

### 3. Question
Direct question naming the viewer's situation.

**When to use:** Solution-aware audience, clear shared pain, functional category.
**Pattern:** "Tired of [X]?", "Still [behavior]?", "Ever [specific experience]?"
**Examples:**
- "Tired of 3pm crashes?"
- "Still using [old category]?"
- "Ever tasted your greens powder?"

**Trap:** Don't ask yes/no questions that can be answered "no" and dismissed.

---

### 4. Direct Callout
Address by identity or behavior.

**When to use:** Clear subculture or identity, product fits a tribe.
**Pattern:** "For [specific identity]", "If you [specific behavior]", "[Identity], listen up."
**Examples:**
- "For people who skip breakfast."
- "If you lift, read this."
- "New moms. We see you."

**Trap:** Too broad = no hook. "For everyone who wants to be healthy" = useless.

---

### 5. Contrarian
Challenge a category assumption.

**When to use:** Real differentiator, provable claim, crowded category.
**Pattern:** "Your [X] is [counter-intuitive claim].", "Stop [conventional wisdom]."
**Examples:**
- "Your greens aren't green."
- "Stop stretching. Start strengthening."
- "Dishwashers wreck your knives."

**Trap:** If you can't back up the contrarian claim, audience calls the bluff.

---

## The Testing Logic

### Why test across angles, not within
Testing 5 variants of "Benefit-First" tells you which variant is best. Testing 1 variant from each of 5 angles tells you what kind of framing your audience responds to. The second answer is more useful.

### Which angles to test first
- If awareness is Unaware/Problem-aware: Curiosity + Direct Callout
- If Solution-aware: Benefit-First + Question
- If Product-aware: Contrarian + Direct Callout
- If Most-aware: Benefit-First + Urgency

### What a winner tells you
- Benefit-First wins = audience trusts your claims, wants the outcome
- Curiosity wins = audience wants to be taught, not sold
- Question wins = audience feels the pain, hasn't found the answer
- Callout wins = audience wants tribal belonging
- Contrarian wins = audience is skeptical of category, wants a challenger

Use this insight to calibrate ALL future creative, not just the next variant.

---

## Character Count Rules

- Headline: 4-6 words, <40 characters hard limit
- If it breaks 40 chars, cut words until it fits
- If cutting damages the idea, the idea is too complex for one headline
- Stack the important word first (scanners read first 2-3 words then decide)

---

## Banned Patterns

Never lead with:
- "Introducing..."
- "The new..."
- "Meet..."
- "Say hello to..."
- "Experience..."

These are launch announcements, not hooks.

Never use:
- Em dashes
- Exclamation marks (more than one is desperate)
- ALL CAPS (more than one word is aggressive)
