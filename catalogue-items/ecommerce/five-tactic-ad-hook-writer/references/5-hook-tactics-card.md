# 5 Hook Tactics Card

The five hook archetypes that cover 90% of high-performing ad openers.

---

## 1. Pattern Interrupt
Rhythmically jarring. Breaks the scroll's cadence.

**Signature openers:**
- "Wait..."
- "Okay, hear me out."
- "This is going to sound crazy but..."
- "I owe you an apology."
- "Pause."
- "Hold on."

**When to use:** When you need to break through social feed noise. Pairs with UGC voiceover.

**When NOT to use:** Premium/aspirational brands. The rhythm breaks the positioning.

---

## 2. Question Hook
Specific question that names the viewer's situation.

**Signature patterns:**
- "Tired of [specific pain]?"
- "Why does [specific thing] happen?"
- "Still using [specific category]?"
- "Ever wonder why [specific phenomenon]?"
- "Do you actually know what's in your [category]?"

**When to use:** Solution-aware audiences. Functional products. When there's a clear category pain.

**When NOT to use:** Unaware audiences (they won't answer the question because they haven't thought of it yet).

---

## 3. Bold Claim
Specific, provocative statement. Usually with a number.

**Signature patterns:**
- "[Number] [unit]. [Counter-intuitive outcome]."
- "We cut [thing] to zero."
- "[X] in [time]."
- "Actually [category]."

**When to use:** When you have a real specific claim. "97% absorption" not "amazing absorption."

**When NOT to use:** When the claim is vague. Generic claims repel more than they attract.

---

## 4. Direct Callout
Addresses the viewer by identity or behavior.

**Signature patterns:**
- "For [specific identity]."
- "If you [specific behavior], this is for you."
- "[Profession/identity], listen up."
- "New [identity]. We see you."

**When to use:** When the target is a clear identity or subculture. Apparel, lifestyle, identity-driven categories.

**When NOT to use:** Broad consumer goods. "For everyone" isn't a hook.

---

## 5. Story Hook
Opens a narrative loop. Viewer has to watch to close it.

**Signature patterns:**
- "I tried [X] in [timeframe]."
- "Here's what happened when..."
- "My [relationship] was [specific behavior]. So..."
- "[Specific time period]. No [category]. Let me explain."
- "Three weeks ago I..."

**When to use:** Video ads. UGC. When you have a real story to tell.

**When NOT to use:** Static ads (no payoff possible). Short formats (<6s).

---

## The Hook Length Rule

- Video opener: 3-7 words spoken in 1.5-2 seconds
- Static headline: 4-6 words, 40 character hard limit
- UGC voiceover: 3-10 words, conversational rhythm

If it's longer, it's a sentence. Sentences don't stop scrolls.

---

## Rhythm Test

Say the hook out loud. Does it sound like:
- A friend texting you? (good)
- A marketer trying to sell you something? (bad)
- A headline in a magazine? (sometimes good, depends on brand)
- A sentence you'd say to a stranger? (usually bad)

If the hook sounds like advertising, rewrite until it sounds like speaking.

---

## The 3 Hook Upgrade Moves

When a hook feels weak, try these:

### 1. Add a number
"Greens powder" -> "22 vegetables in every scoop"

### 2. Add specificity
"For busy people" -> "For moms who skip breakfast"

### 3. Add contrast
"Our collagen works" -> "97% absorption. Most are 20%."

One of these three usually turns a weak hook into a strong one.

---

## Banned Opener Phrases

Never start an ad with:

- "Introducing..."
- "Meet the new..."
- "The ultimate..."
- "The best..."
- "Say hello to..."
- "Experience the..."
- "Welcome to..."
- "Discover the..."

These are brand-speak, not human-speak.
