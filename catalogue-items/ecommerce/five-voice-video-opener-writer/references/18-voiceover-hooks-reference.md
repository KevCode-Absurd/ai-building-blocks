# 18 Voiceover Hooks Reference

Category-agnostic openers across 5 voice styles. Adapt the pattern, not the exact line.

---

## UGC (Real Customer) — 4 patterns

1. "Okay so I [unexpected behavior] for [time period]."
2. "[Time] in. Honest review."
3. "I've tried [N] [category]. This one's [differentiator]."
4. "So, you know that [universal experience]?"

**Voice notes:** Natural pauses, filler words allowed ("so", "like", "okay"), conversational contractions.

---

## Founder POV — 4 patterns

5. "[Personal relationship] was [specific behavior]. So I made this."
6. "We made [product] because [industry failure]."
7. "I'm the founder. Here's [revelation about category]."
8. "[N] months of [work]. One simple decision."

**Voice notes:** Slower pace, earned confidence, no hedging. Direct address works well.

---

## Educator — 4 patterns

9. "Most [category] are [surprising stat]. Let me explain."
10. "Here's what nobody tells you about [mechanism]."
11. "The reason your [thing] doesn't work is the [specific cause]."
12. "[Number]% [outcome]. [N]x more than [alternative]."

**Voice notes:** Measured, deliberate, credibility markers (numbers, specifics, no vague claims).

---

## Narrator — 3 patterns

13. "She [specific behavior]. This is what happened."
14. "In [year], one brand decided to [counter-intuitive action]."
15. "There's a reason [specific detail] keeps showing up."

**Voice notes:** Storytelling pace, slight theatrical weight, third-person distance.

---

## Casual Friend — 3 patterns

16. "Gonna be honest. I was wrong about [category]."
17. "You want the real review or the sponsored one?"
18. "Quick thing about your [routine/habit]."

**Voice notes:** Fast but warm, first-person, in-on-the-joke energy.

---

## Pattern Selection by Audience

### Skeptical audience
- Best: UGC (#1, #4), Casual Friend (#16, #17)
- Avoid: Narrator patterns (can feel staged)

### Trust-first audience
- Best: Founder POV (#5, #8), Educator (#9, #12)
- Avoid: Casual Friend (can feel unserious)

### Curious/problem-aware audience
- Best: Educator (#10, #11), UGC (#4), Founder (#7)
- Avoid: Pure narrator (too removed)

### Brand-loyalty audience
- Best: Founder POV (all), Narrator (#14)
- Avoid: Casual Friend (breaks premium positioning)

---

## Voice Casting Notes

### UGC
- Real customer or UGC creator, NOT a professional voice actor
- Phone-quality recording adds trust
- Should sound like they're figuring it out as they talk

### Founder POV
- Actual founder when possible
- Tight 1-take delivery, minimal editing
- Direct-to-camera shot

### Educator
- Professional voice actor OK
- Calm, informed, slightly bored (bored = expert)
- Can be off-screen narration over visuals

### Narrator
- Professional voice actor
- Polished, theatrical but not dramatic
- Third-person pronouns work better than second-person

### Casual Friend
- Internal team member or creator who has range
- Warm, quick, slight smile in voice
- Feels like a text from a friend, not a brand

---

## Timing Rules

- Hook duration: 2-3 seconds spoken
- Word count: 5-15 words
- If the hook is over 3.5s, cut words until it fits
- Pause after hook is as important as the words (1-beat pause = "wait for it")

---

## Common Mistakes

1. **Professional voice for UGC.** Instantly fake. Use real customer voice.
2. **Founder POV without specificity.** "I built this because I cared about quality" = forgettable. "My mom was drinking 10 Diet Cokes a day" = memorable.
3. **Educator without numbers.** The whole style is credibility-driven. Needs specifics.
4. **Narrator for direct-response ads.** Narrator adds emotional distance, which slows conversion.
5. **Mixing voice styles in one ad.** Pick one. Stick with it the full video.
