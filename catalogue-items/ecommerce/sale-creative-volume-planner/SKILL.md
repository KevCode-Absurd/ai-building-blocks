# Sale creative volume planner

You work back from a peak daily spend target to the number of ads needed, give a mix and a phase plan, and cap the plan to what the store can really make. For a non-sale volume plan, point to the creative volume planner skill. For tired ads, point to the fatigued ad reviver.

- **Peak daily spend target** for the sale (required), and the currency.
- **Last year's spend by ad** during the sale (required), the top 50 ads is enough, with the ad type of each.
- **Formats the team can produce** (required for the capacity check).
- **Offer and end date** (required). The end date must be real.
- **Meta page setup** (optional).
- **Share of new ads that become winners** (optional).

## How to do it
**Not benchmarks.** The steps below use a few ideas from one agency's sample. We have not checked them and they are not targets: the top 20 ads held under half of spend; offer ads absorbed more spend than evergreen ads at a similar one-day return; a starting mix of offer statics, offer-badged winners, catalog, long video and creator content. Never quote them as results. Measure the spread, the offer ad return and the mix in the person's own last-year sheet.

1. **Find what one ad can carry.** From last year's sheet, find the peak daily spend on a single ad and the typical working ceiling. Use the person's own figures. A typical winner carries a few times what a normal evergreen ad carries, so keep that ratio, but do not quote another store's dollar amounts.
2. **Back out the winners.** Winners needed = peak daily spend target / spend per winner. Example with placeholders: a target of [TARGET] a day with [PER WINNER] per winner needs [TARGET] / [PER WINNER] winners. Count ads that actually carry spend, not ads uploaded.
3. **Show why you need many more ads.** Spend spreads across a long tail of ads, so a few winners do not carry a big sale. Work out from the person's own last-year sheet how much of spend the top 20 ads held, and show that share. If it is under half, you need volume, not a few winners. If it is high, say so.
4. **Do a capacity check.** If the person gives a winner rate, ads needed = winners / winner rate. For example, at a winner rate of [WINNER RATE], [WINNERS] winners need [WINNERS] / [WINNER RATE] ads. The source's own view is that a plan with a dozen or more spend-carrying winners usually means 100 or more ads. A solo founder cannot make that. Say plainly: cap the spend target to what the person can make. Say which cap you used and why.
5. **Give the spend mix in two layers so it adds to 100 percent.** Layer 1: keep evergreen ads running at a fixed share of total spend. Let the person choose the share; if they have no view, test [EVERGREEN SHARE] against last year's split. Layer 2: split the rest (the non-evergreen spend) by ad type. Set the shares from the person's own last-year sheet, and write [SHARE] where they have not chosen one. The types are: offer statics, evergreen winners with an offer badge, catalog ads, long educational video (only in categories that use it), and creator and founder content with a sale banner (warehouse walk-throughs, staff content) plus early-access or VIP ads if used. If there is no founder or creator content, spread that share across the other types. Check that layer 2 adds to 100 percent and that evergreen plus layer 2 adds to 100 percent of total spend. Badge copies of fatigued winners with the offer rather than turning the originals off, so you do not reset what the account has learned.
6. **Write the offer ad rules.** Offer statics are the highest volume asset you can ship. Keep them simple: the discount and end date readable at thumbnail size, and the brand obvious. Put the offer and deadline in the first line of copy, because placements cut text. Check that a tall 9 by 16 version does not push the offer off the feed crop. Vary the product, the offer, or whether a person appears, to produce about 50 statics fast. Compare the offer ads' spend and return with evergreen ads in the person's own last-year sheet. Treat the offer ads' job as holding spend, not winning the bottom of the funnel.
7. **Write the offer into catalog ad copy.** Many catalog ads carry no message at all, so add the sale offer to the catalog copy. Do not quote any percentage for how many brands do this.
8. **Check the Meta page limit.** Ramping ad count can hit the page limit. Set up cloned or extra pages before you ramp. Say this is the source's warning and the person should confirm the limit in their account.
9. **Phase the creative.** Tease and early access (before launch), launch, mid-sale refresh, then a final 24 to 48 hours with a deadline. The deadline and scarcity must be true. Never invent a stock level, a countdown that restarts or an early-access list that does not exist. Have backup offers (gift with purchase, free shipping, bundle, deepest sitewide sale) with landing pages ready, loaded into the account in case you need to switch.
10. **Set the rules for reading results.** A sale distorts the feedback: a best seller at 50 percent off looks great, and an excellent ad on a weaker product looks bad. Judge sale creative at the ad set level, and lean more on click-through rate and cost per click than on return in this window. Outside a sale, the source says click metrics do not predict return. Do not copy a sale winner into evergreen without checking it. Sale customers may repeat less than customers from a quiet month. The sources' figures for this are not checked, so do not quote them. Check the store's own cohorts.
11. **Concepts over formats.** Diversity means distinct concepts, not formats. Many visuals can come from very few ideas. Say how many distinct concepts the plan covers.

## Checks
- The winners count is a shown sum.
- Capacity is checked, and the spend target is capped if the person cannot make the ads.
- The mix adds up to 100 percent: evergreen share of total spend plus the split of the rest, and evergreen ads stay on.
- Every deadline and stock claim is real.
- Source percentages are named as one agency's sample or left out; none is repeated as a benchmark.
- Concepts, not just formats, are counted.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <peak target | last year's spend by ad | formats | offer end date | page setup>`. With no last-year data, use a stated assumption and mark it. Look in the input before writing `MISSING:`.

## Avoid
- Counting ads uploaded as ads that carry spend.
- Turning off evergreen ads for the sale.
- Fake urgency, fake stock levels or a restarting countdown.
- Planning more ads than the team can make.
- Judging sale ads on return alone.
