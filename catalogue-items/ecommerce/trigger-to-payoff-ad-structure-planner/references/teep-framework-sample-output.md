## TEEP Ad Brief: "Three Weeks In"

### Trigger
3pm energy crash despite getting 8 hours of sleep. Opening hook establishes this is a real, specific, relatable moment.

### Emotion
Frustration (tired of wellness products that don't work) transitioning to cautious relief (this one's actually different).

### Evidence
Delivered at 0:15-0:22 mark: founder explains 97% absorption backed by third-party testing. Visual cue: ingredient label close-up.

### Payoff
Stated at 0:25: "Consistent energy. Not dramatic. Just reliable." Anti-hype framing calibrated to skeptical audience.

```

Visual: Cut to ingredient label close-up. Highlight "22 vegetables, 0g added sugar."

22-27s [Payoff]
Visual: Woman at 3pm, focused, working, no yawning.
Voiceover: "Three weeks in. No crash. Consistent energy. Not dramatic. Just reliable."

27-30s [CTA]
Visual: Product shot, URL overlay.
Voiceover: "Try it 30 days. Link's on screen."
```

## Production Notes

- Locations: Office setting (3pm) + kitchen (morning)
- Equipment: Phone quality acceptable, natural light preferred
- Assets needed: Existing product, existing ingredient label, screenshot of absorption study

- Music: Silence through Trigger. Soft bed starting at 15s.
- Estimated cost: $600-900

## Why This Structure Works for This Audience

Skeptical 32-48 women reject:
- Transformation promises ("life-changing energy!")
- Influencer hype
- Vague claims ("clinically proven")

They respond to:
- Specific moments they've actually had (3pm crash)
- Anti-hype framing ("not dramatic, just reliable")
- Verifiable evidence (third-party, label visible)

TEEP maps naturally to this audience because it trades vague for specific at every element.
