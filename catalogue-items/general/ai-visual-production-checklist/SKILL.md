# AI Visual Production Checklist

You get a one-page pre-flight sheet for the picture or clip you are about to make, and a short check to run on every result. It rests on one idea: a model told about a subject in words invents one, so show it real references and look at the result.

## Inputs
- **Brief** (required): the concept, format, ratio, length, approved words, job of the asset.
- **Reference images** (required for any real subject): the brand's own photos of the thing being shown.
- **Brand brain** (optional): the subject's record (what it looks like, how it is used, what never to show), brand world (places, props, light, people), motion and sound notes.
- **Mistakes register** (optional): lessons that apply to this kind of work.
- **Model notes** (optional): length, modes and prices of the chosen tool. Keep these separate from the principles so a new model is one edit.

## Steps
Fill the sheet below, then run the check after each result.

1. **Show the subject true.** Attach the subject's own photos and name each one in the prompt (`Image 1 is the front, as sold: copy its shape, colours and printing exactly`). One photo per job, not a collage. Repeat the two or three most visible facts in the same words every time; where words and a photo disagree, models follow the words. The colour of a thing is its own colour, not the colour of the background it was photographed on. It opens, pours, fastens and is used the way the brand says, step by step; an action nobody described is not shown. A scene without the subject still belongs to it: the object in focus is the subject, something it is made of, or the thing it replaces.
2. **Still before clip.** A picture costs cents and a clip dollars. Make and inspect the opening frame first; ask for the clip only when it is right, starting from that frame. An unusual camera position is set by the opening frame, not by words. A later clip starts on the last frame of the one before, or is an extension of it. Text-to-video is for exploring an open idea; it never replaces reference photos for an exact brand subject. Test the hardest motion beat first, with the smallest useful clip.
3. **Make frame 1 the ad.** Judged in under a second with the sound off. Something already happens in it. It reads at thumb size: one subject, clear edges, brand colours, nothing vital in the top and bottom bands where app buttons sit. If paused anywhere, it is still a good picture.
4. **Movement that could be filmed.** Real speed (no slow motion or time-lapse). One small, continuous action per clip. Materials behave: liquid has weight, fabric hangs, a lid has a thread. Everything the clip uses is in view when it starts. One name per object. Start and end states agree with the action (a cup must be lifted before it is lowered). One visible action and one camera behaviour per shot.
5. **Plan the sound.** Say what is heard (the tear, the pour, the room), and say whether there is music. "Natural sound only, no music" is a complete instruction; silence about sound is not. One speaker at a time, short enough to say in the time. A person listens to every clip: a reviewer that reads frames cannot hear. Keep voice, room sound, effects and music separately editable where the tool allows.
6. **People.** Generated people are fictional adults and say nothing only a real customer could say. No generated children. A founder, named customer or expert appears only from that person's own photo or footage, with their say. Describe hands fully (which hand, holding what, where the other one is); a hand never covers the label. For faces, choose complexion depth, undertone, adult age and texture separately; texture is proportionate to the zone, crop and light (nose pores can be clearer than cheeks), never uniform pores, waxy smoothing or sharpening halos. Keep approved age, marks and makeup across cuts. Repair one symptom at a time in one named area, locking everything else.
7. **Words in the picture.** A video model writes no words; captions, prices and calls to action are added afterwards in the brand's typeface. An image model sets words only when given the exact words, order and type. No claim, price or offer appears unless it is in the brand's facts.
8. **Set the scene.** Place comes from the brand world and the buyer's moment. Lifestyle scenes have a history (a mug left after breakfast), not random clutter; a studio hero stays clean. The subject inherits the scene's light direction, shadow hardness, colour temperature, reflections and contact shadows; changing the place means relighting. A phone look keeps the background recognisable and skin unretouched; one or two imperfections can help but never hide the subject or words.
9. **Frame deliberately.** Choose the framing (wide for context, full for fit, medium for action, close for a detail), then height, angle, viewpoint and focus as separate decisions. Describe a physical camera place and distance, not an imagined photographer. Alternatives differ in meaningful camera relationships, not height alone; a locked reference keeps its approved setup.
10. **Sequence with intent.** Say what the viewer thinks at the start, what changes, and the visible evidence that earns the change. Each shot adds one piece of information. Review the full edit without dialogue, then with sound.
11. **Separate direction from the prompt.** Write a reusable direction brief: purpose shown through visible cues, scene and action, view and light, each reference's role, what is fixed and what is deliberately open, and review criteria. Translate it into the tool's real controls; do not add or drop goals while doing so. A helper proposes; it never silently replaces approved words, facts or format. Unknown subject facts are not creative freedom.
12. **Edits.** Name the current state, the changed element and area, the target, and how it fits perspective, scale, light and contact; list what stays. Preservation instructions are acceptance criteria, not proof: review the returned image.
13. **Borrow from references wisely.** Decode a striking reference into one motif, one readable surprise and one payoff, then translate them into this audience's world. Its beauty is not evidence that it converts. Keep a few constants across a campaign with distinct compositions and jobs.
14. **Upscale last.** Upscaling preserves the approved shot and ratio and is checked against the source for identity, hands, text and edges. It is not redesign.
15. **Spend small.** Read the price before making anything. Smallest thing first: one still, then one short clip, then a batch of two or three, not ten, until the first has passed. Do not retry a refusal or failure with the same words. Save inputs, prompt, settings and outcome; change one controlling decision per attempt.

### Check after every render
1. The subject matches its reference photo: shape, colour, finish, printing, size against a hand.
2. The action asked for is the action shown, and it could happen.
3. Frame 1 already has the subject and motion.
4. Real speed, no slowed or sped-up passage.
5. Hands and faces have sound anatomy; crops are intentional; no stray letters, logos or captions.
6. The camera is where it was asked to be.
7. The sound has been listened to by a person.
8. For video, watch the whole clip at normal speed and step through critical transitions; compare identity, action, space and light, including the join to the next clip.

A failure is written as one line (what was asked, what came back) and added to the mistakes register.

Each asset has one job and is judged on it: a hook stops the thumb and holds three seconds; a demonstration lets a stranger say what happened; a proof picture shows a real, visible source; a page image answers one buyer question without reading the page.

## Output
The completed sheet (items 1 to 15 with the choices made and the reference images named) before rendering, then the check result with pass or fail per line and the one-line failure notes.

## Checks
- Every reference image is named with its job in the prompt.
- No clip was requested before its opening frame was approved.
- No invented word, claim or price appears in any picture.
- Sound was specified, and a person listened.
- Spend was stated before it was incurred.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>` (no reference photos, no usage steps, no stated sound). A missing fact is a question for the brand, never a guess: show the subject closed or at rest and say so.

## Avoid
- Vague directions ("make it feel premium") with no visible cues.
- Treating a plausible generated image as proof of efficacy or of a successful transition.
- Adding detail the approved plan did not contain.
- Treating more prompt length as more fidelity.
