## Main text
A greens powder and a greens gummy are meant for the same daily habit. They differ in what it takes to get each day's serving, and in what people expect one small pack can hold.

A powder means a scoop, a glass of water, mixing, and a shaker to rinse. Sprout is one pack of gummies a day, eaten straight from the pack, with no water, no mixing and no shaker.

The usual doubt about the gummy is size. Each Sprout pack holds 60+ ingredients, including 21 vitamins and minerals and 6 g of prebiotic fiber.

Sprout is clinically tested for nutrient absorption. A 2025 study followed 120 adults for 12 weeks and compared Sprout with a placebo, assigned at random. Every batch is also third-party tested for 70 pesticides and 4 heavy metals.

Tapping through opens a side-by-side of powder and gummy, the full per-pack ingredient list, and the study and testing details.

Greens powder vs. greens gummy: what one daily pack holds

## Notes

**Fit:** This format fits. These buyers are comparing solutions, and a comparison between two kinds of product, built from facts on file, answers their main doubt. No rival brand is named.

**Page sections it must cover:** List only. Don't write them here.
1. A first screen that opens with the same headline and the powder vs. gummy comparison: prep routine, and what one pack holds.
2. The full per-pack ingredient list, showing 21 vitamins and minerals consistently. The current ingredients panel says "over 20".
3. The study: its design, and that it measured blood vitamin C and folate only. Keep the approved line "Clinically tested for nutrient absorption."
4. Testing: 70 pesticides and 4 heavy metals, with the lab and the date as shown.
5. Reviews from the "Vs Powders" theme, quoted as the review platform shows them. Use one rating with its source.
6. Low Sugar vs. Sugar-Free, including any sugar-alcohol note. This matters for gut-focused buyers.
7. The offer, stated plainly:
   - the $29.99 first box requires the SMS sign-up
   - it renews at $49.99 each 4 weeks
   - the guarantee covers the first order only
8. The FDA disclaimer.

**Page check: claim (fails).** The current first screen makes a different claim from the ad:
- It opens with a discount bar that rotates several different percentages.
- Below that come product photos, then "60+ potent ingredients to revive whole body vitality". That line is a tagline carrying a risky claim, not the comparison.
- The rating shown is 4.8/5.0 (100,000), which conflicts with 4.75 (109,639), 4.74 (118,002) and 4.0 (2,411) elsewhere.
- "Us vs. Them" sits far down the page and is built on the price comparison you said not to use, plus a third column with no label.

The fix is to send the ad to a page built for it. That page's first screen should be the headline "Greens powder vs. greens gummy: what one daily pack holds", the two-column comparison and the per-pack facts, with the offer below them. The fallback is a version of the product page with a reworked "Us vs. Them" (no prices) moved to the top and the discount bar removed.

**Page check: label.** The current product page doesn't look like an article, so it needs no label. If the new page is laid out like an article, put "Advertisement" in front of or just above its headline. "Promoted" or "Presented by" alone isn't clear enough. Keep any label the platform adds to the ad.

**Claims left out for lack of proof or by instruction:**
- That a pack "replaces" a powder, or holds as much as one. There are no powder-side facts on file.
- The "$1.26 vs $3.60" price comparison (you said don't use it).
- "Revive whole body vitality", immunity, stress relief and mental clarity. The study only measured two vitamins in the blood.
- The study percentages (+40.1% vitamin C, +20.5% folate), until it's confirmed what they are measured against.
- All discount percentages.
- Ratings and customer counts, because the sources conflict.
- The survey figures (67% digestion, 52% energy). These come from customers self-reporting and weren't in the approved brief.

MISSING: what a typical greens powder scoop contains, from a source you can support, before any comparison by amount.
MISSING: whether the +40.1% and +20.5% study results are against placebo or against baseline.
MISSING: which rating and review count, and from which platform, is the one to quote.
MISSING: the content of the Low Sugar vs. Sugar-Free panel, including any sugar-alcohol warning.
MISSING: a landing page whose first screen carries this comparison.
