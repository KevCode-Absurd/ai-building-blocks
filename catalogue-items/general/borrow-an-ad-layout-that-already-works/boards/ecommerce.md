# For online stores: Borrow an ad layout that already works

## Extra things to give it
- Photos of every variant in the ad, named by view: front, open or out of the pack, in a hand, in use, close up. Add the product's truth sheet if you have one (`product-image-spec-writer`).
- The live offer in its own words (price, bundle, dates) and, for a range layout, the list of products you sell.

## Extra rules for a store
- The product must be exact: colour, pattern, material, shape and the print on the pack. If the reference shows more items than you sell, repeat the real one or show fewer. Several products in one frame share a common height and are never cropped to fit.
- A studio shot can't tell you how the product looks held, worn or on a table. An In use ad needs a photo of real use.
- Put each generated image beside its reference photo and zoom in before it passes. Same product, or it fails.
- The offer type uses the live offer, word for word. With no live offer, pick another type.
- A comparison layout sets your product against the usual kind of thing, unbranded. A row you can't fill for both sides, from a source, in the same unit, is dropped, never left blank or crossed out. Rival prices and guarantees carry the date you checked them.
- Press and award layouts need a placement you hold. Otherwise switch to Their words or Look inside.

## Limits
- Offer and Occasion ads work best on people who know you. Keep them to one in five of any set aimed at new buyers (see "Get image ads strangers stop scrolling for").
