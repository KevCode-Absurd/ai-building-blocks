# Carousel Ad Copy, Card by Card

A carousel is a script in cards. Card 1 sells the swipe to card 2, the way a first line sells the second.

## Inputs
- **Number of cards** (required): usually 5 to 7; the range is 3 to 10.
- **Buyer read** (required): stage, doubts, the buyer's words.
- **Business facts and proof** (required): offer, guarantee with conditions, claims allowed, proof held.
- **Brand brain** (optional): voice, forbidden claims.
- **Format rules** (optional): if a carousel format-rules block is in use, its rules win where they differ.

## Steps
1. **Choose the structure** for the buyer's stage:

| Structure | Best stage | Card flow |
|---|---|---|
| Story | Problem aware | The workaround they made, the moment, what I did not know (mechanism), what I tried (objection), what changed (proof), offer and guarantee |
| Myth and truth | Solution aware | A contrary line, myth/truth pairs, the mechanism, product and offer |
| Failed fixes | Solution aware | "You've probably tried...", each fix and why it fell short, what works and why, offer |
| How it works | Solution to product aware | The promise, one card per step, part or ingredient with its job, proof, offer |
| Proof stack | Product aware | Strongest real quote, more real quotes each answering one objection, guarantee and action |
| What to expect | Product aware | Time points the business can support, then offer |
| Before you buy | Product to most aware | One card per objection from research, guarantee, action |
| Range or bundle | Most aware | The bundle's value, one card per item with its job, framed price, action |

2. **Write card 1 as the hook that works alone** (many people only see it). It opens a gap that only the swipe closes and promises what the swipe reveals.
3. **One idea per card**, in one or two plain sentences. Each card makes the next one necessary.
4. **Keep a visual thread** (same layout, colours, framing, product) so it reads as one thing; note it.
5. **Bring the product in late for a cold buyer**: seen from the mechanism card, named on the offer card.
6. **Write the last card** with the action in words ("Tap Shop now to get yours by Friday", only with a real date), plus the offer and guarantee, in 20 words or fewer.
7. **Write the primary text** to carry the hook and a short version of the argument, for people who never swipe.
8. **Self-check** card by card: could a stranger follow it, is anything invented, does each card earn the swipe?

## Output
```
Structure: <name> | Stage | Cards: n
| Card | Beat | Picture | On-card text | Words |
...
Primary text: ...
Headline field options (with character counts): ...
Button: ...
Flags: gaps, assumptions, things to verify
```

## Checks
- Card 1 stands alone.
- Last card is 20 words or fewer and names the action.
- One idea per card; no card repeats another.
- Every quote is a real customer's, word for word, with source.
- No invented figure, deadline or offer term.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: proof` and swap proof cards for demonstration or mechanism cards. Write `MISSING: real date` and drop the date from the last card.

## Avoid
- A crowded last card that hides the action.
- Naming the brand or product on card 1 for a cold buyer.
- Near-identical cards that only reword each other.
