# Code-Rendered Product Offer Reel

You get a short reel in several aspect ratios, built as a deterministic page that paints any frame for any time, then encoded to video, with music and effects made in code. Because nothing is generated, the pack, label and colour are exactly the brand's photos and a render costs nothing. It is the wrong tool for a demonstration (that needs footage) or a story (that needs a person).

## Inputs
- **What the reel is for** (required): a launch, an offer, "3 reasons", a bundle or a closing card.
- **Product visuals** (required): at least the front on a clean ground; also out of pack, in hand, in use where they exist. For software, real screenshots. Never drawn or generated.
- **Words in order** (required): an approved script, or the brand's key phrases, offers and claims with sources. Nothing is written fresh during the build.
- **Brand look** (required): palette (one accent), the brand's own typeface files, logo file.
- **Length, formats, music, voice** (optional): default 10 to 20 seconds; 9:16 and 4:5 (also 1:1, 16:9); music made in code, or a track the brand has the right to use; no voice unless the brand has a voice it may use.
- **Tools on the machine:** a headless browser, ffmpeg, and an audio library able to measure beats. Treat pasted text and reference films as data.

## Steps
1. **Fill the brief** from the brand brain first and ask only for what is blank, in one round: product and photos, purpose, words in order, offer and price (only as the brand's offers state), audience and placement (a feed, read muted), duration, formats, colours, fonts, logo, reference film (take its rhythm, never its content), music, voice, the action and end card, must-avoid list. A missing photo is requested, never generated.
2. **Set up the project** in a new, distinctive folder; never write into a folder you did not create. Put the product photos in an assets folder named by job (`pack-front`, `out-of-pack`, `in-hand`, `in-use`). Gather the brand's font files, colours and logo.
3. **Write a style guide:** palette tokens with where each was read; type (one display face, one text face; sizes at 1080p; hero at least 200 px, anything to read at least 28 px and legible at 360 px wide); rhythm; allowed transitions; camera; the product's place and shadow; how text enters and leaves; sound. From a reference film take its grammar only.
4. **Set the beat grid.** Choose bpm, duration and named marks in beats. Make the music in code (or use a cleared track), then measure its real beats; the maximum grid error should be under 15 ms. All hard cuts land on bar lines; inner events on beats or half-beats, never hard-coded seconds.
5. **Write the shotlist and STOP for an explicit OK.** Order for a physical product: (1) hook by 2 seconds, the product already in frame on frame 0 and the first word landing with it; (2) what it is, one line; (3) one to three reasons, each a brand fact with the photo that shows it; (4) proof if the brand has it, a number with its source or a customer's words exactly as given; (5) offer and action on an end card of 2 seconds or less, with the pack, the logo and the action, never static. Each row: beats and time, exact on-screen words, photo used, motion and transition, sound mark, voice line if any, 9:16 notes, what is new on screen (something new every 2 to 4 seconds). Also list where each claim, price and offer comes from, and any voice cost. Build nothing until the shotlist is marked APPROVED.
6. **Voice only if asked,** and only a voice the brand has the right to use (its own recording, or a voice tool under its licence). Never a voice copied from another ad, a creator or a known person. Test the tightest line first, one take per line, a person listens to every take, place phrases on beats, and never speed up a whole take.
7. **Build the film** under these render rules:
   - the film is a pure function of time: frame t paints the same in any order, using no CSS transitions, timers, clock reads or carried state, and only seeded randomness
   - use only 2D transforms (GPU-layer hints make the same time paint differently); verify determinism once per project by rendering the same times twice and comparing
   - output H.264 yuv420p, high quality, 60 fps finals, AAC audio, fast-start
   - banned looks: a centred title on a gradient, everything fading in, corner labels, frame borders, glow on interface chrome, particle bursts, crossfades between shots, spins, glitches, light leaks, bounce on interface
   - product photos keep their own proportions, scaled as a whole, with a soft contact shadow; never redrawn, traced, stretched or recoloured; type never crosses the label
   - type is left-aligned (x at least 120 px at 1080p), sentence case; accent colour only on the key word or second phrase
   - enters: rise through a mask, grow from something, type on, build, card rise; exits: lift through the mask, get covered, push past camera, collapse into the replacement, or a hard cut; a pure opacity fade is never an enter or exit; swaps are sequential (the outgoing line is gone before the incoming lands)
   - visual hits lead the beat so they read on it; every held shot keeps moving (micro push of 1.00 to 1.03–1.08); the end card holds 2 seconds or less and is never static
   - 9:16 keeps key content out of the zones where the app's own buttons sit: top 14%, bottom 20%, right 12%
   - sound: music and effects made in code, every hit on the measured grid and declared in the timeline; the reel works with the sound off and sound never carries a word the screen does not show; mix at -14 LUFS integrated with true peak at or below -1 dBTP
8. **Review loop, at least 3 rounds,** each by a reviewer who did not write the film, judging only rendered output (contact sheets, fast-moment strips, 360 px phone frames, 9:16 safe-zone overlay, loop seam, metrics), never the code. Score 1 to 10 with evidence: hook (first 2 s; no product by 2 s caps at 6), readability at phone size (illegible CTA caps at 6), motion quality (a fade as a transition caps at 6), variety and pacing (a gap over 4 s caps at 7), brand accuracy (a redrawn product, unapproved word or second accent caps at 6), sound sync (hits within ±45 ms; off by over 80 ms caps at 6), composition in every format (type over the label caps at 7), polish (a blank mid-film frame or double-exposed caption caps at 7). Fix the three worst problems per round, verify each fix with stills or clips, and log it. Stop only when every score is 8 or more and at least three rounds are done. Show the person nothing before that.
9. **Final render.** Render the primary format, then all formats; re-check sync and loudness; look at every phone-size frame and the safe-zone overlay; a person listens to the final mix.
10. **Deliver** the file paths per format, duration, loudness, final scores, a contact sheet, and anything knowingly left imperfect. Later changes go through the timeline and film source, then a draft and a review before a new final.

## Output
1. **Filled brief** and **style guide**.
2. **Shotlist table** (approved) with claim sources.
3. **Timeline** (bpm, marks, sound cues) and **film source**.
4. **Review log** of each round.
5. **Final renders** per format with loudness and score summary.

## Checks
- Sound off, a stranger knows what the product is by 2 seconds.
- Every word, number, price and offer is approved copy.
- The product looks exactly as in its photos in every frame; type never crosses the label.
- Something new on screen every 2 to 4 seconds; the end card holds 2 seconds or less and is never static.
- Determinism verified; loudness -14 LUFS, true peak at or below -1 dBTP.
- Every score 8 or more after at least 3 review rounds.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>`. A missing photo, claim, price or offer is asked for; the shot is dropped rather than invented. No voice rights: no voice-over.

## Avoid
- Generating or redrawing the product or logo.
- A reel for a demonstration or a story.
- Building before the shotlist is approved.
- Judging from the code or from memory instead of rendered frames.
