# Contract-liability release bridge

A historical contract-liability roll-forward with inferred release and reconciliation residual.

It looks back at one past period. It shows how much of the money customers paid in advance was turned into revenue, and whether that matches what the company says it released.

## When not to use

- You need to split a contract into performance obligations. Ask your accountant or auditor, as this only checks the total roll-forward.
- You want to forecast future bookings or revenue. Build that in your own spreadsheet, as this only looks back.
- You only have a receivables balance with no deferred revenue. Check it with a simple aging report in your accounting tool.
- You are tracking unbilled revenue turning into invoices and collections. Use the Contract asset billing cash bridge instead.

## Method

1. Separate contract liabilities from receivables, refundable deposits and other payables; verify whether new additions are billings or cash advances under the source definition. If the source does not say which, write MISSING and do not call them cash collected.

2. Start with opening liability, add new deferred consideration and signed acquisition/FX/other changes, then deduct closing liability to infer revenue release. Release = opening + new advances + signed other changes − closing.

3. Compare with the disclosed release and total recognized revenue; distinguish revenue from opening balances from all releases during the period. Residual = disclosed release − inferred release. Release share = release ÷ total revenue.

## Limits

- Fixture proves an aggregate historical roll-forward and does not allocate performance obligations or forecast bookings.
- Incomplete FX, acquisitions, refunds or contract modifications leave the inferred release unresolved.
