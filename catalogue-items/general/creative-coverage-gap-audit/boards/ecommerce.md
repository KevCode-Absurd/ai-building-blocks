# Creative coverage gap audit: online store add-on

## Decide first: used up, or bought once
The gaps sit in different places.

| | Used up (cream, snack, powder) | Bought once (pan, jacket, bag) |
|---|---|---|
| Ads usually over-cover | The first try: taste, feel, an opening offer | The product as an object: studio pictures, a feature list |
| Gaps usually found | The daily moment it fits into; the buyer who tried it and stopped; what is in it and why; the returning buyer | The moment the old one fails; proof it lasts (a year on); care and how it is used; fit; returns and warranty; the comparison with keeping what they own; the gift |
| Motives most often unspoken | Control, ease, relief | Being seen well, identity, feeling safe about the money spent |
| Products section | Sizes, flavours, the bundle, the subscription | The second purchase: accessory, part, matching piece |

## Format checklist for stores
- Used up: a person to camera with a real moment of use; a still with three short points; a reaction to taste, feel or scent; a new flavour or batch as real news.
- Bought once: a demonstration (working, being cared for, after a year); a side-by-side with the thing it replaces; a still of it in a real room or on a real person; a customer a year on.
- What tends to fail: used up, an ingredient list with no result, "feel your best", a price-led ad for a premium product; bought once, a feature list with no moment, a studio picture with no scale, urgency with no reason, "quality" with nothing to show.

## Extra inputs and checks
- Product catalogue and bundles; real urgency only from recent changes.
- Formats needing a real person: confirm who may appear and under what rights before listing them as opportunities.
- Any product-use fact in a concept (how it opens, is worn or used) comes from the brand brain's product record, not the concept writer's imagination.
