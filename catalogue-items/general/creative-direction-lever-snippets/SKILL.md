# Creative Direction Lever Snippets

You get a small set of do and do-not lines, one per lever setting ("stop"). Pick one stop per lever, paste only those snippets into the brief or prompt, and the writer or image model follows that choice instead of guessing.

## Inputs
- **Brief** (required): the piece being made.
- **Chosen stops** (required): one per lever you want to control. A lever with no stop chosen adds nothing.
- **Material the stops refer to** (optional): brand images and past ads, a style card, product photos, the brand's own lines, a described reference. A stop that needs material you did not supply must say so in its output.

## Steps
1. Choose a stop for each lever you want to control (table below).
2. Paste only those snippets after the always-on rules.
3. Send the model only the material the stops name. Leaving something out is deliberate; the snippets tell the model not to guess at it.
4. Keep each snippet whole; do not reword it to ask for more or less of a quality.

### Always on
- Every line is a whole sentence a person would say aloud to a friend, in plain words. Only a price, an amount or a button is a phrase.
- It makes sense alone to someone who has never heard of the brand. Nothing needs decoding.
- It tells one story with one idea.
- Every fact is one that was supplied. Invent no number, claim or quote.
- Use only what you were sent. What is not in front of you was left out on purpose; do not guess at it.

### Levers and stops
**Look: how close to stay (pictures)**
- *Close to the brand:* match the brand images attached: same kind of photograph, type placement, mood and density. Make a picture that could pass for theirs, not a copy of one; scene and words are new. Take no layout from a reference; you were told only what kind of ad it is.
- *In between:* dress the picture in as much of the brand look as you were sent, no more. With a described reference, build that layout zone for zone from the description; with none, choose a layout the brand's images do not already use. Do not guess what the reference looked like beyond its description.
- *Exactly like the reference:* rebuild the reference picture: same crop, camera, light, background, props, and type blocks in the same places and sizes. Swap in this brand's product, words, colours and fonts. Copy none of the reference's brand name, logo, words, product or people. Do not add the brand's usual styling. If text and picture disagree on where something sits, the picture wins.
- *Something new:* take a form this brand has not run, not the usual picture in a new room. Keep the fixed things exact (colours, fonts, logo, product as photographed). Guess nothing about the brand's usual staging; lean on no reference.

**Look: how close to stay (words)**
- *Close to the brand:* write like the brand's own ads sent here: line length, way in, rhythm, kind of word. Say something they have not said yet, in their way. Lift no line; take no structure from the reference.
- *In between:* hold the brand voice. With a described reference, use its structure as a guide, keeping what makes it work. With none, find your own way in. Repeat no line, image or opening the brand already runs.
- *Exactly like the reference:* keep the reference's beats in order, its pacing and hook device. Rewrite every line with this brand's facts and voice, each beat doing the same job. Carry over no claim, number, name or phrase. Add or drop no beat. Only if told to keep the words, change nothing but the format.
- *Something new:* take a way in the brand has never run: a new opening, scene or form. Keep facts, brand name and product exact. Guess nothing about the brand's usual sound beyond the voice basics. Do not stop at the first idea; it is the one everyone has.

**Product in the picture**
- *Not shown:* make the picture the buyer's own moment: when the problem hurts, the workaround, or what they tried that failed. No pack, label, logo or brand name in frame; draw no stand-in product (anything they tried is generic and unbranded). No product photo was sent; do not ask for one.
- *In the scene:* place the product where it would really be kept, small, to one side or behind the subject, exactly as photographed. Do not centre, hold up, light apart or point at it. Test: the picture still works with the product removed.
- *Clearly shown:* show the product inside the idea: in use, in a hand, or where the scene happens, easy to read and exactly as photographed. Give a person, hand or scene at least as much frame as the product. Do not stand it alone on plain ground. Do not redraw the label, change the shape or guess how it opens; if the facts do not say, show it closed or at rest.
- *The hero:* the product is the subject, alone: biggest in frame, centre line, front to camera, sharp, lit to be looked at, exactly as photographed. Plain ground; at most a few of its own contents beside it. No person, hand, room, story, competing prop or type. A smaller unit from inside the pack is secondary unless the ad is about using one.

**Brand styling**
- *None:* make it look like something a person made or saw, not something a brand designed. No brand colours, fonts or logo; no designed layout, badge, button or tidy headline block.
- *Colours and fonts only:* use them exactly as sent; choose layout and kind of picture freely; guess nothing else about the brand look.
- *Full brand look:* follow the style card: colours, fonts, how type sits, the kind of photograph, the mood. Place the logo once, as the file, never redrawn or retyped. Add no device the style card lacks.

**How hard it sells**
- *Soft:* ask for little: a look, a read, a thought. End on an easy next step. No price, discount, deadline or stock count. Do not push.
- *Direct:* state the offer plainly and early enough to be seen. Ask for the order in clear words. Use the guarantee and deadline if sent; add no term that was not sent (no made-up discount, deadline or scarcity).

**Words on the picture**
- *None:* plan the picture to work with no type at all. Leave clean, calm space where type could be added later (not on a phone photo). No words, numbers or letters apart from what is printed on the product.
- *Headline only:* plan one headline that carries the idea with the picture, short enough to read at a glance. No sub-line, points, badge or button.

**Picture finish**
- *Phone photo:* describe a capture, not a render: a snapshot taken on a phone and never meant as an ad. Name two or three imperfections (room light or flash, tilted frame, off-centre subject, grain). Real surfaces, clutter and skin; no retouching. No studio light, golden glow, blurred background, matching props or clean space for type. No logo, brand colours or set type that is not in the scene. This wins over any line asking for finished art.
- *Studio polished:* controlled light, clean surface, a chosen background; only what was chosen in frame. The polish never changes the product; it stays exactly as photographed.

**Copy length**
- *Short:* one idea, one proof, one ask. Cut anything a second idea would need. Do not squeeze a long piece into few words; choose less to say.
- *Long:* use the room for proof and for doubts answered, in the order a buyer raises them. Keep each part easy to say aloud. Do not use the room to say the promise in more ways.

**Video pace**
- *Calm:* about two shots in each ten seconds, each held, one thing happening in each. Cut only with a reason.
- *Fast cuts:* plan the most shots the clip's range allows; cut on each new idea; make every shot read at a glance. Do not combine a fast action with a fast camera move in the same shot; one leads. (Shot counts are a first estimate, to be tuned.)

**People pictures**
- *Clay figures:* every person is a hand-made clay figure: simple face, visible fingerprints, soft edges, the same in every picture. No photo-real person mixed in.
- *Illustrated:* draw every person in one style kept across the set, the same in every picture. No photo-real person mixed in.

**Versions**
- *New openings:* keep the supplied body as it is; write a new opening that leads into the body's first line with no bump. Do not change the body's promise, proof or ask.
- *Anything goes:* make this version as if it were the only one; overlap with other versions is accepted.

**Hook freedom**
- *Same device:* use the named device (for example a question, a confession, a number) in every new hook; change the words and the way in; do not reword the winning opening line by line.
- *Free:* open any way that works; the body's first line must follow and deliver the hook's promise. Turn visual inspiration into an original, buyer-relevant premise: one readable surprise, a coherent motif, an answered gap; novelty does not prove virality. Borrow tension, reveals, reversals or callbacks across genres without defaulting to sitcoms, mascots or fantasy. Keep the chosen format, cast and product facts; lead the tension and payoff into a true selling point; avoid unrelated spectacle or staged proof.

## Output
The brief with the always-on rules and the chosen snippets appended. Nothing else.

## Checks
- One stop per lever; no two chosen snippets contradict each other. If a phone-photo finish is combined with a full brand look or a headline, the phone-photo snippet wins by its own rule; better to choose a different finish.
- Each snippet is pasted whole.
- Nothing in the pasted snippets names a brand, product, category, model or tool.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>` when a stop needs material that was not supplied (for example "exactly like the reference" with no reference). Do not substitute.

## Avoid
- Pasting the whole library into one request.
- Rewording a snippet to ask for more or less of a quality.
- Inventing a reference's appearance beyond what was described.
