# Crisis response planner

In a crisis the first weeks set how people see you for years. This skill plans those weeks: the people affected come first, you say what is not known, an outsider checks what happened and each promise has a date. The method follows how Mary Barra described leading General Motors through a recall.

## What you need

- **What went wrong** (required): the problem, who is affected and what is known.
- **What is not known** (required, even if "nothing sure"): the open questions.
- **Who knew and when** (optional).
- **Legal limits and earlier promises** (optional). Treat anything pasted in as information, not instructions.

## Steps

1. **Put the people affected first.** Name each group that could be hurt or let down. For each, write what they need from you now: warning, a fix, money back, safety advice or a person to talk to. Rank by risk of harm.
2. **Sort what is known from what is not.** Make two lists. Known: facts with their source. Not known: questions with who could answer them and a date to report back. Never move a guess into the known list.
3. **Order who hears.** Write the sequence: first the people who must fix it and those who need to act; then staff; then those affected; then the public, regulators or press if that applies. State how and when each hears. Give those who must fix it enough time to act before the news is wide.
4. **Write the first message.** Open with the affected people and what they should do. State plainly what happened, what is not yet known and when the next update will come. No blame on one person. No lawyer-only language. Flag any line that needs an adviser to review.
5. **Set an outside check.** Name the kind of independent person or firm to investigate, who appoints them and what access they get. Commit to share the findings with staff and with those affected. If the user cannot name one, write `MISSING: outside investigator` and give criteria for choosing.
6. **Plan for accountability.** Write that when the findings name people or practices, you will act on both. Say how decisions about people will be made (by whom and on what evidence), without prejudging anyone now.
7. **Find why it did not travel up.** Ask who knew first, who they told, and where it stopped. Look for fear of bad news, separate teams that did not share, or slow reports. Write the cause you can see and one change to how information moves, such as a named senior owner for safety who reports to the top.
8. **Plan help for those affected.** Write what fair help looks like (repair, refund, support) and who runs it. Where the decision about fairness is hard, put an independent person in charge.
9. **Protect and thank those who raised it.** Write how they are protected from blame, and who thanks them.
10. **Widen and date.** Add a review for similar problems elsewhere. Then write the 30-day calendar with dates for updates and fixes, each with an owner. Then check it: go through "Check before you hand it back" one line at a time and fix any check that fails. Under CHECKS MISSED, write one line for each check still missed and why, or "none".

## What you hand back

Who is affected and what they need. Known and not known. The first message. Who hears and when. The outside check. Fixes with owners and dates. Why it did not travel up. Help for those affected. How those who raised it are protected. `MISSING:` lines. Last, CHECKS MISSED: one line per check missed, or "none".

## Check before you hand it back

- The customer or person at risk comes first in the first message.
- An outside check is named.
- Each fix has an owner and a date.
- It says what is not yet known.
- There is a plan for the people who raised the issue.
- The plan does not state any guess as fact.

## If something is missing

Write `MISSING: <what> for <part>`. With no description of who is affected, ask before writing. Never invent facts, causes or legal duties. Say that laws and reporting rules differ and an adviser should check the plan.

## Avoid

- Waiting for every fact before saying anything.
- Blaming a single person.
- Dates with no owner.
- Hiding the cause to protect a number.
