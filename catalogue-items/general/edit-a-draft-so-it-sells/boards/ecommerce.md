# For online stores: edit a draft so it reads clean and sells

## Extra facts to give it
- The ingredient or materials list, and how the product is made.
- Where it's made, or test and batch facts, if the brand states them.
- The one-time price, the subscription price and what it renews at, with any condition on a first-order price.
- The price per item in a bundle, or per day for something used up.
- The returns window or guarantee, and who it covers.

## What the edit looks for in a store draft
- **One promise:** a store ad or product page often stacks a deal, a benefit and a gift. Keep the one the facts prove best and move the rest to their own ads.
- **Prove it:** a dose, a size, a thickness or a test result usually sits behind "premium". Use the one the facts hold.
- **Swap it:** a rival can claim "high quality". It can't claim your exact material, your exact test or your exact routine.
- **The doubter's terms:** shoppers leave when the price is hidden or the renewal comes as a surprise. State the first price with its condition and the renewal price beside it.
- **The guarantee:** say who it covers (first order only, for example) where the buyer decides, not only in the FAQ.
- **Discounts:** a big percentage off pulls in buyers who don't stay. Lead with the reason to buy and put the deal in the ask. If the ad and the page show different percentages, list it for a claims check rather than picking one.
- **Lower the risk:** a starter size, a first-order price or the guarantee often does more than a bigger discount.
- **The picture:** the product in the place it's really used, and the result as it really looks. Never suggest an ingredient or process proves a result.
