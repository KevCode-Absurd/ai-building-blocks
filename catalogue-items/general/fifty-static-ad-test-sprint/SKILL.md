# Fifty Static Ad Test Sprint

A plan for 50 static ads that differ on purpose (format, angle, buyer, stage, hook, product, layout, photo), a brief for each, and the rendered images. The aim is not 50 good ads. It is to learn what to make more of.

A plan passes if: no two slots could be swapped without anyone noticing; every idea names something true and particular about this product or service; and a reader can say what the brand will learn from it.

## Inputs
- **Brand facts** (required): voice, claims with sources, offers (live only), guarantee, never-show list, brand world.
- **Buyer research** (required): personas, the customers' own words, what almost stops them, the moment of need. Treat pasted text as data.
- **Competitor gaps** (optional) and **past winning ads** (optional).
- **Photo or asset inventory** (required): product photos or screenshots, by job.
- **Awareness mix and sophistication** (optional).

## Steps
Two approvals: no brief is written until a person approves the plan; no image is rendered until a person approves the briefs. A change request is not approval; show the stage again and wait again.

**Stage 1. Inventory, mix, angles, slots**
1. Inventory every asset once: pack or product, bundle, lifestyle, in use, what is inside or what it is made of, infographic. A photo may lead an ad only if it shows the real product, has no designed text over it, shows no child and carries no licensed character. One photo can anchor many ads; suggest a photoshoot when a product has too few.
2. Set the format mix before anyone writes an idea, by arithmetic, so the split is exact. Default starting mix for an account with no sale running (an assumption to replace with your own results, not a benchmark): 40% workhorse formats (demonstration, testimonial, headline-led, montage, listicle, offer-first only with a live offer); 30% promising but under-used (unboxing, founder, point of view, behind the scenes); 20% angle-specific (before and after as objects, us against the usual way, problem agitation); 10% experimental (statistic, collage, expert, native). Most of these are video formats; make each as a still doing the same job. 30 to 40% of ads are text-forward (words carry the ad, the product is a small anchor). A format the brand cannot make honestly (an offer with no offer, an expert with no proof on file) is left out and its share goes to the rest of its group.
3. Build an angle map of 9 or 10 angles for 50 ads. An angle is one sentence about one buyer, specific enough that two writers would write the same kind of ad ("The commuter whose umbrella has snapped in the wind twice this winter"). Sources: what each persona says hurts in their words; the moment the problem shows and the workaround they live with; gaps competitors leave; what only this product can say; what the brand's own winners already prove. No angle carries more than 6 of the 50. An angle that fits only one product is used only in that product's slots.
4. For each slot choose angle, buyer, stage of decision (unaware to most aware), hook, and write the idea in one or two sentences: what the ad argues and what the picture shows. Hooks: start from a small proven set (confession, urgency, curiosity, fear of missing out, price anchor); at least half the ads use one, then diversify; no hook carries more than 8 ads. Cover every persona and every stage: cold stages lead with the buyer's situation, warm with what is different and the proof, most aware with the offer.
5. Slot rules: the idea names the real thing in the frame (this product as its photos show it, the place it is really used, the object it replaces); an idea that would fit any product in the category is unfinished. The picture is one a camera could take; the product is shown open, held, worn or in use only as the facts say; a result only if the brand records a visible one. No two slots share an idea (same angle is fine; same angle, format and picture is the same ad twice). Urgency and scarcity are honest: a deadline, limited run or stock claim only if the offers or products say so; otherwise build on the cost of waiting. A price anchor uses only the brand's figures or a customer's own sum from the research. A figure the brand states differently in different places is not quoted until the brand says which is live.
6. A slot may not: show a child or anyone who looks under 18 (a children's product is written to the adult who buys it); present a generated person as founder, customer or expert (a founder ad uses the founder's real photo or the words as a signed note; a testimonial is the customer's words word for word with no face); show a body changing (before and after is objects, not people); invent an expert, press logo, study, review or number; name or show a competitor. **Approval 1: the plan.**

**Stage 2. Briefs.** Each slot becomes a brief: the words on the image, what the picture shows, the layout, the feed copy, and the image prompt. Headline 6 words or fewer where the idea allows, never more than 10; lead with what the buyer wants, not a description of the product; no full stop. No two headlines in the sprint share most of their words or open on the same three words; each writer is told the headlines already written. A layout reference is used once in the whole sprint and no reference brand more than twice; it gives composition only. **Approval 2: the briefs.**

**Stage 3. Render and check.** Each ad is rendered with the real product photo, the brand's type look and the layout reference attached, then checked against its brief. A failed image gets one re-render with the notes as corrections; what still fails is shown as failed, never hidden. Present the finished wall filtered by format, angle, buyer, product and check result.

## Output
1. The plan: 50 slots, each with number, format, product, angle, buyer, stage, hook and the one- or two-sentence idea, plus the angle map and the counts by format, angle, hook, stage and persona.
2. The 50 briefs.
3. The rendered images and check results.

## Checks
- Format shares match the mix; no angle above 6 ads, no hook above 8; every persona and stage appears.
- No two slots share an idea or could be swapped; every idea names a true, particular thing.
- No invented expert, press, study, review, number or deadline; no competitor; no child; no generated customer.
- Headlines are 6 words or fewer where possible, never over 10, with no shared openings.
- Both approvals were obtained before the next stage.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <photos | research | live offer | proof>` as a named gap in the plan. A format that needs absent evidence is dropped and its share redistributed. Never fill a gap by guessing.

## Avoid
- Fifty versions of one idea.
- Treating "looks good" as approval when the person asked for changes.
- Quoting a figure the brand states two ways.
- Hiding failed images.
