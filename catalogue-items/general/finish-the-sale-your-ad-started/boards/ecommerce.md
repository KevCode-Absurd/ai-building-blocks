# Landing page that continues your ad: for online stores

## Extra things to give it
- Variants (size, colour) and which one the ad showed.
- What's in the box, item by item.
- Shipping cost and speed, the free-shipping threshold, the returns window and who pays return postage, warranty terms.
- Subscription terms: how often it ships, the saving in money, how to pause or cancel.

## What changes
- **Product page order:** an announcement line (proof, risk removal or the offer), a photo gallery that already works as a mini page (benefit, proof, in use), the buy box, real customer content, a strip of benefits, two or three sections on what the buyer gets, what sets it apart, the guarantee, questions, and a closing block. On a phone the buy box sits right under the gallery, so price and button are in the first screen, and a button follows the visitor once the buy box scrolls away.
- **Buy box, bought again:** pack or bundle rows ("most popular" only if true), one-time against subscribe with the saving in money, price per day only if the facts give it, and "pause or cancel" in the store's words.
- **Buy box, bought once:** one price, a size and colour picker with the size guide one tap away, delivery date, returns window and warranty. Never "3 of the same item".
- **Bundles:** for something bought again, a routine where each step is needed for the result. For something bought once, the set that completes it (a lid and care kit with a pan). The saving is the shipping saved by sending them together.
- **Below about 100 in the store's currency,** a flat bundle price reads stronger than a percentage. Suggest it; the owner confirms.
- **Fold-out sections in the buy box:** bought again: ingredients, how to use, shipping and returns. Bought once: dimensions and materials, what's in the box, care, shipping and returns, warranty.
- **Hero image:** the same product, pack and variant the ad showed.
- **Keep checkout costs in mind when writing the risk line.** Extra costs, slow delivery, unclear returns and forced account sign-ups are the most common reasons people abandon a cart. State cost, delivery time and returns on the page. Keep everything added at checkout under about 10% of the order value (a rule of thumb).
