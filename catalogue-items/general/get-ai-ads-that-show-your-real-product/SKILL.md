# Get AI ads that show your real product

An image or video tool that is told about a product in words makes up its own version. So you work from photos of the real thing, approve a still before paying for motion, and check every result against those photos. You hand back a one-page plan in four stages, then a check for each result.

## What you need
- **The brief** (required): the idea, where it runs, shape, length, approved words, and the one job of this picture or clip.
- **Product photos** (required whenever the product is in shot): plain photos, one per job (pack front as sold, out of the pack, in a hand, in use, close-up).
- **The brand file** (optional): the product's entry in `offers.md` (looks, use, what never to show), `voice-and-look.md` (brand world, light, fonts, motion and sound), and `00-start-here.md` house rules.
- **The mistakes list** (optional): rules from earlier jobs.
- **Tool notes** (optional): the chosen tool's clip lengths, reference limits and prices, kept apart from the plan so a new tool means one edit.

## Stage 1: Gather the proof
1. **Pick the photos.** Name each one in the prompt with its role: "Image 1 is the pack front as sold: copy its shape, colours and printing exactly." One photo per role, never a collage. Stay inside the tool's reference limit and send only the photos this shot needs.
2. **Fix the words.** Write the two or three most visible facts once ("small flat green sachet, torn straight across the top") and use those exact words in every prompt. When words and a photo disagree, tools tend to follow the words, so the words must match the photo.
3. **List the allowed actions.** The product opens, pours, fastens and is used only as the brand file says, step by step. No step on file means it's shown closed or at rest. The product's colour is its own, not the colour of the backdrop it was shot on.
4. **Set the money.** Read the price per still and per clip and state it before anything is made.

## Stage 2: Make the still
5. **Frame 1 is the ad.** Viewers decide in under a second, sound off, so the action has already begun: one clear subject, readable at thumb size, nothing that matters in the bands the app covers. Paused anywhere later, it should still be a good picture.
6. **Place it in a real scene.** Take the place and light from the brand world and the buyer's moment. The product takes the scene's light direction, shadow softness, colour and reflections, and rests on or is held by something. A lived-in scene has a reason for each object; a studio shot stays bare.
7. **Choose the camera.** Framing (wide, full, medium, close), height, angle and distance are separate choices. Describe a real camera position ("counter height, about 40 cm away"), not a mood.
8. **People.** Generated people are made-up adults. They never stand in for a real customer, expert or founder, and never say what only a real customer could. A real person appears only from their own photos, with permission. No generated children. Describe hands in full: which hand, what it grips, what the free hand does. No finger covers the label.
9. **Words in the picture.** No claim, price or offer that isn't in the brand file. An image tool gets the exact words, order and font, or none at all. Captions and prices on video go on in editing, in the brand's font.

## Stage 3: Move it
10. **Animate the approved still.** Ask for the clip only once the still passes, starting from that frame. A later clip starts from the last frame of the one before, or extends it. Text-only video is for trying ideas, never for an exact product.
11. **Keep the motion filmable.** One small action and one camera move per clip, at real speed, no slow motion. Things behave: liquid has weight, fabric hangs, gummies bounce and settle. Everything the clip uses is in view at the start. Each object keeps one name and one state (a torn pack stays torn).
12. **Decide the sound.** Many video tools now add sound by default, so say what's heard: the action's own sounds, the room, and music or "no music". Put any spoken line in quotes, short enough to say in the time, one speaker at a time.
13. **Test the hardest part first,** in the shortest clip the tool allows.

## Stage 4: Finish it
14. **Edit one thing at a time.** Name the area, the change and what must stay. "Keep everything else" is a request, not a promise: compare the result with the last good version.
15. **Upscale last,** then compare printing, edges, hands and text with the source photo.
16. **Spend in small steps:** one still, one clip, then two or three more, never ten before the first passes. Don't resend a refused or failed prompt unchanged. Save the inputs, prompt, settings and result, and change one choice per attempt.

## Check every result
1. Product matches its photo: shape, colour, finish, printing, size against a hand.
2. Words in the picture are exactly the approved ones, spelt right.
3. The action is the one asked for, and it could really happen.
4. Hands and faces are whole and natural; no stray letters, logos or extra fingers.
5. Light and shadows on the product match the scene, and it touches what holds it.
6. The camera is where the plan put it.
7. Video only: real speed, frame 1 already moving, objects keep their state, the join to the next clip matches, and a person has listened with sound on.

Write each failure as one line (asked, got) for the mistakes list. Judge each piece by its job: an opening stops the thumb; a demo lets a stranger say what happened; a proof shot shows a real source; a product page image answers one buyer question.

## What you hand back
The four-stage plan with each choice made and each photo's role named, then the check for each result, pass or fail per line, with the one-line failure notes.

## Check before you hand it back
- Every product photo has a named role in the prompt.
- No clip was asked for before its still passed.
- No invented word, claim or price appears in any picture.
- Sound was decided, and a person listened.
- The price was stated before anything was spent.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>` (no product photos, no steps for use, no price). A missing fact is a question for the brand: show the product closed or at rest and say so.

## Avoid
- Mood words with no visible cue ("make it premium").
- Treating a believable generated image as proof that the product works.
- Adding details the approved plan didn't contain.
- Assuming a longer prompt is a more accurate one.
