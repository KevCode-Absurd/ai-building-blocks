# Script type recipes

Each recipe: person and tense; beat order; what carries it; when the product arrives; what it must not be confused with; what to watch. Examples are made up. "This breaks the usual order on purpose" is marked.

**straight argument.** Second person, present. Hook, problem, promise, mechanism, objection, proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action, identity line; problem is one or two lines and the promise arrives by the third. Carried by whichever beat is strongest; give it the most words. Product at the offer, eased in. Not unaware observation (that stays in the buyer's life a third of the way). Watch for reading like a checklist: escalate, and hand over line to line.

**confession.** First person, past. A confession (hook and problem), the moment it became undeniable, what they found out, what they'd tried, proof from outside themselves, what they bought and why those terms, identity line. Promise implied until the mechanism, then stated. Carried by the problem, with stakes early; over half the words come before the mechanism. Product late. Not failed solutions (what they bought) and not witness (opens after the change). Watch who speaks: a real customer tells it as theirs; a generated presenter tells the problem as a story and stops short of a personal result. Bridge into the offer.

**unaware observation.** Second person present, or third narration. Observation with no problem or product, more of the pattern until they recognise themselves, the reason behind it (problem then mechanism), promise, what people usually try, proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action, identity line. Carried by a long problem, about half the script. Product late (not for the first third). Watch: the observation comes from research; never tell the viewer they have a condition; ask little at the end, no deadline.

**failed solutions.** First or third, past. Fix 1 and fix 2 with costs, the low point, what they had in common (bridge), promise, mechanism as what sits upstream of everything they tried, proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action. Carried by the objection; say why each half worked. Product as what addresses what the others missed. Not price of the problem (which sums the tries). Watch: kinds of solution, never brands; invent none.

**objection first.** Second, present. The objection conceded, why the doubt is fair, what is different (mechanism), the promise this makes safe, proof aimed at that doubt, offer, a matching reversal, call to action. One doubt answered completely. Product early. Not how it's made, not failed solutions. Watch: the doubt must be in the research; honest disclosures (adjustment period, who it doesn't suit, weight) are strong; concede without telling the viewer what they have. Breaks the usual order on purpose.

**honest review.** Second present or narration. What is not perfect, who should not buy it, why it is that way (the shortcoming is usually the price of something), what that buys, outside proof, offer, a reversal for the unsure, call to action. Carried by the concession. Product at once. Not objection first (here the shortcoming stays true). Watch: a real property the material holds; never invented for effect; if none exists, do not use. A generated presenter gives no verdict of their own. Breaks the usual order on purpose.

**discovery.** First or third, past for the finding, present for how it works. The finding as a turn, the problem as it looked before, the mechanism plainly, the promise it makes possible, why earlier tries could not work, proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action, identity line. Carried by one cause taught in two to four plain sentences with one picture. Product after the mechanism. Not three things (parts) or category call-out (blames the category). Watch: a discovery that is really a feature list; a brand's own finding is not independent science.

**three things.** Second, present. The count, one line of problem, the promise, thing 1 (and why most miss it), thing 2, thing 3, the objection answered by one of them, proof, the product as all in one, risk reversal, call to action. Two or three things, never more; each a feature with its "which means". Pay off the open question. Product after the list. Not discovery or timeline. Watch: features with no mechanism; order so they build.

**category call-out.** Second or third, present; "we" only from the offer on. The category's flaw as a fact, why it matters, the promise, the same thing done properly, proof (third party first), "they all say that" answered with a specific, offer, risk reversal, call to action. Carried by a sharper mechanism. Product after the proper version. Not how it's made (told from the shelf, not the workshop). Watch: true and supportable; kinds of product, never a named rival; contrast on a fact, not an adjective.

**timeline.** Second present or future; third narration. The first time point or why timing matters, the problem in a line or two, then for each time point: what is happening (mechanism), what they'd notice or do (modest promise), the doubt then; after the last: proof, offer sized to the timeline, reversal on the same timeline, call to action. Carried by the mechanism in loops; the natural way to lengthen without padding. Product at the first point. Not three things (order is time). Watch: only time points and effects the business supports; honest early effects belong at their point.

**days in.** Second present, or third narration. The day count and where things stand, a glance back at day 1, what the product is doing by now, what a person would notice (modest), what is not settled yet, offer sized to the pack or returns window, call to action. Carried by the plain middle between day one and result. Product in line one. Not timeline (this stays on one day). Watch: use a count the business holds; no effect by a date unless it states one. Breaks the usual order on purpose.

**how it's made.** First person plural, past for the decision, present for how it works. The decision, what it cost the maker, what it does for the buyer, the easy way in a line, outside proof, who it was for, offer, risk reversal, call to action. Carried by specifics of the making: a dose, material, step, test, time, thing rejected. Product present from the start, named at the offer. Not category call-out or demonstration. Watch: only real process facts; never invent a factory story; the maker's word needs a higher rung of proof beside it.

**witness.** First past, or third about a described customer. The witness moment, back to how things were, what changed and why, what they'd tried, wider proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action, identity line. Carried by proof; the witness is a named kind of person and what they said is specific. Product in the middle. Not demonstration. Watch: needs a true account; with none, write a brief for a real customer to film with listed placeholders; a generated presenter cannot claim this.

**demonstration.** Second or narration, present. The demonstration under way, what you saw and why it matters, why it does that, who it is for, the doubt answered, a second proof from outside, offer, risk reversal, call to action. At 15 seconds: demonstration, what you saw, offer, action. Carried by proof, shown; available with no reviews. Product on screen from the first frame. Not witness or how it's made. Watch: only what really happens, filmed with the real product as one continuous action; never an effect on a body. Breaks the usual order on purpose.

**daily routine.** Second present, or narration. A moment of the day, what that part used to take, the product used where it falls, why it is easy, a later moment where it is no longer thought about, offer, call to action. Carried by use in its place; small true details persuade. Product early, name at the offer. Not demonstration (this proves only that it fits). Watch: a day nobody has; show use, never its effect on a body; a real person's routine is a brief they film. Breaks the usual order on purpose.

**several side by side.** Second present or narration. The line-up and criteria, why these criteria, each option with one note, where this one comes out, what it does not win on, offer, risk reversal, call to action. Carried by two or three criteria named before any result that the buyer can check alone. Product in the line-up as one of several. Not category call-out. Watch: a test nobody ran; others are unbranded stand-ins, each given its due; a generated presenter never says "I tested". Breaks the usual order on purpose.

**offer first.** Second present. The offer, why these terms, the price in one frame, the reversal with real terms, one line of proof, the reason for now, call to action; problem and mechanism one line at most. Carried by the offer. Product in line one. Not price of the problem (for people who don't know the product). Watch: every number real; urgency real or omitted; fails on cold audiences.

**price of the problem.** Second present. The tally already paid, what it went on and why it kept being spent, the promise, mechanism in a sentence or two, proof, the offer set against the tally, a reversal removing the fear of wasting money again, call to action. Carried by the offer, reframed. Product after the mechanism. Not failed solutions or offer first. Watch: costs from research or the business's own numbers; no competitor price; leave the sum to the viewer or use a placeholder.

**identity led.** Second or third, present. The identity, what that kind of person does differently (the accommodation turned into a choice), the mechanism that makes their choice right, the promise, proof that others like them chose it, offer in terms that fit, risk reversal, call to action repeating the identity. Carried by identity tied to a mechanism. Product in the middle. Not a straight argument with an identity line at the end. Watch: identity with no mechanism is a lifestyle ad; describe, never flatter or shame; never assert a personal attribute of the viewer.
