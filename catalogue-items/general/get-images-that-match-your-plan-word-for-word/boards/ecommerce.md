# Get images that match your plan, word for word: for online stores

Use this when the subject is a physical product. It adds detail to steps 4, 7 and 9 of the main skill.

**Extra things to give it**
- The product's truth sheet: what it looks like, the pack, how it's used, what never to show, any visible result, and reference photos by job (pack front, out of the pack, in a hand, in use, close-up).
- Measured outside dimensions, if you know them.
- Lessons from past feedback about how the product opens, is held and is used.

**Keep the product true**
- Say which form is in the picture: the pack, the product out of it, or both, and which is the main subject. A form no photo shows (a single sachet from inside a pouch, a pan out of its sleeve, a jacket's lining) is never the main subject.
- Repeat the two or three visible facts word for word in every prompt of a set: "a 30 ml amber glass bottle with a black dropper cap and a white label"; "a 26 cm bare steel pan, dark grey, with a long riveted handle".
- Keep printing exactly: "Keep the label exactly as in Image 1: same words, same typeface, same position. Do not redraw or re-letter it."
- Say in full what must not appear: "There is no box and no second product anywhere in this image." For a frame without people: "No people: no face, no hands."
- Size needs evidence: measured outside dimensions plus a visible object of known size at a similar distance. Capacity (such as 500 ml) tells you nothing about height or width. Palm and wrist comparisons aren't universal measurements. A product close to the camera can look large through perspective while keeping its true size; make it prominent by framing, not by enlarging it.
- Round labels follow the curve and disappear round the side. Clothes keep their cut, pattern size and the way they drape in that pose, without changing other clothing. A macro close-up changes the framing, not the proportions. Several products together need evidence of their relative sizes.
- No invented parts, ingredients, finishes or damage.

**The product in use**
- The top of a pack is set by its printing: the top edge is the one above the name when the name reads upright. A tipped pack still opens at that edge; say how its printing now reads.
- Say the whole action: "The dropper is lifted straight out of the neck of the bottle; three drops fall from its tip into her left palm." "The jacket is zipped to the collar; the storm flap is pressed flat over the zip."
- Hands: which hand, where thumb and fingers sit, which way the front faces, what the other hand does. No hand over the label, and no more hands than people in frame. Fingers join the palm and wrist in a believable way, and fingers that would naturally be hidden stay hidden. Keep the approved nail shape, length, colour and finish, with curved highlights that match the light. Add no manicure, pinch grip or accessory the plan didn't ask for.
- Approved worn details (a hair clip, a stud, a tattoo) need their position, how they attach, what overlaps, where they touch and how the surface responds: a clip grips a named lock of hair with strands passing underneath; a stud follows the ear's shape with a tiny reflection and shadow; matte ink follows the skin with no raised edge. Keep the count, design and size the same across views.

**Placing a product into a scene**
Name the roles of the scene photo, the product photo and any identity photo. Anchor the size. Say what holds the product up. It takes on the scene's light, reflections, perspective and focus. A readable but invented label is still wrong. Condensation, steam, props and luxury styling are choices, not defaults.
