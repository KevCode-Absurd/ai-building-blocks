# For online stores: Get one image ad ready to make

## Also give it
- Product photos, each labelled by what it shows: the pack front, the pack open, the product in a hand, the product in use.
- How the product opens and is used, and anything that must never be shown (a torn pouch, a partner's logo).
- A visible result, only if the store has real, consented photos of one.

## Store offers, said plainly

| Offer | The ad must show | Never write |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription | The price, how often it ships, how to pause or stop, in the store's words | "Cancel anytime" or "easy to cancel" unless the site says exactly that |
| First-order price | Today's price and the price from the second order, side by side | A per-day figure worked out from the first-order price alone |
| Bundle or multi-pack | The price per unit or per use, and why that amount suits how fast it gets used | Savings worked out against a price nobody pays |
| Sale | The sale price in money, not only a percentage, and the real end date | "Lowest ever" or a "was" price that isn't on record |
| Guarantee or returns | How long, what the buyer does, what they get back, every condition | A warranty period written as how long the product lasts |
| Free gift | What it is and how it helps with the product | A gift that has nothing to do with using it |
| A reason to buy now | A real batch, season, stock limit or price change | A countdown with no date behind it |

If the store shows the same offer two ways (two discount figures, two star ratings), use neither in the ad and list it under Check before launch.

## Price, put in terms a buyer feels
- Per day or per use, only when both the price and how long a pack lasts come from the store.
- Next to something they already pay for, named as a kind of thing, never a rival brand.
- As the effort it takes ("one pack, no shaker"), when effort is the real cost.

## Pictures that suit products
- For shoppers who know you (an offer): the product as the picture, one proof as the second read, the price or bundle as the third.
- For shoppers who don't (a problem-led plain post): the thing they put up with as the picture, the product small or left out.
- The material or part that does the work, shot close.
- The pack opening and the product coming out, the way the store says it opens.
- The usual way next to this one, shown with objects (a tub and shaker against a single pack).
- Texture or detail you can almost feel: a pour, a weave, a grain.

## Extra risks for physical products
- Green, safety and origin words ("sustainable", "non-toxic", "made in") need proof on file.
- A before-and-after of a person only from real, consented photos, and never for weight or health.
- Draw the product from its real photos. Never invent a colour, size, flavour or pack.
