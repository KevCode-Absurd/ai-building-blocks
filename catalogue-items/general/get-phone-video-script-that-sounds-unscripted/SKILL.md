# Get a phone video script that sounds unscripted

You write what one person says to their phone, so a stranger takes it for a person talking. People tell stories in three moves: this was going on, and then something got in the way, so I did this. Most bad scripts skip the middle and list features. This one starts on the snag and spends most of its time showing the product in someone's hands.

## What you need
- **Buyer** (required): one buyer, the moment the problem shows, their main doubt, their own words.
- **Product facts** (required): what it is, how it's used step by step, handling rules, claims allowed and banned, the price and offer exactly as stated.
- **Speaker** (required): a real creator who has used it; an AI presenter (a made-up adult); or a real founder, customer or expert on their own footage.
- **Brand file** (optional): voice, banned words, past scripts so you don't repeat them.

## Steps
1. **Settle who says it.** A real creator may tell their own story; leave their true details as `[blanks]` for them to fill or drop. An AI presenter speaks in "you" or as an observer and tells no personal story or result. A founder, customer or expert speaks only on their own footage.
2. **Write the spine in one line.** "[Situation], but [snag], so [what they did]." The snag comes from the buyer's own doubt or hassle. If you can't fill the "but", the script isn't ready. If the draft reads "and, and, and", it has no snag.
3. **Size the length from the hands.** List the real use steps. Allow about 4 seconds a step, plus about 8 for the snag and 12 for noticed and pointer together. That's the length. Don't squeeze it shorter or pad it longer.
4. **Write the four parts.**
   - **Snag** (opens the video): the problem or doubt, mid-thought, no greeting. One line of situation at most.
   - **Hands** (the longest on screen): the product used in its real order, one action per shot, following the handling rules. Include one proof moment the viewer can see: the label held to the lens, a count, a timer. Someone watching with the sound off should follow it.
   - **Noticed**: only what the speaker saw, tasted, timed or did. No effect on a body unless it's a real customer's own result inside the allowed claims.
   - **Pointer**: where to get it, plus one honest thing to know before buying, such as the real price after the first order or which size to start with. Same voice and pace as the rest.
5. **Check the numbers.** At most two product facts, each from the facts list and also visible on screen. Prices and offer terms are said exactly as the business states them.
6. **Add what the maker needs.**
   - How to say it: tone, pace, where they are, what they wear, how the phone is held, what the face does at the snag.
   - Shot list, part by part: time, one action, which hand holds what.
   - Two other snags, each a different buyer doubt or hassle, each read with the first hands line.
7. **For AI video:** start each part on a new clip; the hands part starts with the product ready in frame. If it runs long, cut the situation line first, then any line that repeats what the picture shows. Never cut the proof moment or the noticed line.
8. **Run the say test.** Read it aloud standing up, at speaking pace. Rewrite any line you'd never text to a friend, any hype word (obsessed, life-changing, game-changer) and any line that would suit a polished advert's voice-over.

## What you hand back
```
Script: "<title>" (<N> seconds, from <steps> hand steps; real creator / AI presenter)
Spine: <situation>, but <snag>, so <what they did>
Snag (<times>): "<line>"
Hands (<times>): "<words>"  [proof moment: <what is seen>]
Noticed (<times>): "<observation or [blank]>"
Pointer (<times>): "<where, plus one thing to know>"
How to say it | Shot list (time, action, hand) | 2 other snags
Blanks and gaps: ...
```

## Check before you hand it back
- The spine has a real "but".
- The hands part is the longest on screen and can be followed with the sound off.
- Every spoken fact is in the facts list and also on screen.
- The speaker could truthfully say every first-person line.
- An AI presenter never says "I tried it" or "I didn't believe it".

## If something is missing
Write `[VERIFY: what is needed]` in place of any missing fact and ask. No buyer, no script.

## Avoid
- Telling a demonstration over a talking face instead of showing it.
- An ending louder or pushier than the rest.
- On-screen text that repeats the whole spoken sentence.
