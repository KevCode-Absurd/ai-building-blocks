# Get talking openings that keep viewers watching

You write three spoken openings for an existing ad, then take them through the video process with an AI-made presenter. An opening has one job: keep the right person watching long enough for the ad's first line. Three openings that differ a lot teach you more than ten that differ a little. Stop for approval after stages 1, 3 and 4.

## What you need
- **The ad it goes in front of** (required): its first spoken line, its first frame (who, where, light, what's in hand) and the full script if you have it.
- **The product** (required): photos by what they show (front, in hand, in use), how it's handled, what it replaces, any visible result.
- **Buyer and brand file** (required): the buyer's moment and own words, their main doubt, the brand voice, claims with sources, words never to say, a real deadline only if one exists.
- **Approved presenter** (optional): the ad's avatar card, sheet and lock lines, so the opening and the ad match.
- **Clip length** (optional): 6 seconds by default, 8 if your tool allows it.

## Steps

### Stage 0: Script in (the openings)
1. **Choose three moves:** mirror, gap, promise, proof, flip (the five moves from the openings skill). A visible result allows a promise preview. A sourced number allows proof, unless the ad already says it. A doubt the ad answers allows a gap. Name the ad line that pays each opening off. Skip any move the facts don't support, and say why.
2. **Gate the speaker.** The presenter is a made-up adult who speaks in "you" or as an observer, never "I tried it" or a personal result. A real founder, customer or expert appears only on their own footage. No children.
3. **Size the line to the clip.** About 2.5 words a second, with half a second to settle at the start and one second of stillness at the end: 11 words for 6 seconds, 16 for 8. If a line won't fit, shorten it.
4. **Set two dials for each:** energy (calm, level or lively) and distance (close at arm's length, or propped at mid shot). One opening matches the ad's first frame on both dials, so the cut feels like one video; the other two change at least one dial.
5. **Read each one aloud, then the ad's first line.** It should flow as one video. No greeting, no "so", no brand name in the first three words.
6. **Show the line-up for a yes:** move, line, word count, dials, where the product sits, the touch, and the ad line that pays it off. This yes locks the words.

### Stage 1: Pick the avatar (approval 1)
Reuse the ad's approved presenter so the join doesn't jump: copy its avatar card unchanged. With none, write a new **avatar card** for a made-up adult who fits the buyer (age, look, plain clothes, voice, what never changes). Never an AI look-alike of a real creator or any real person.

**Approval 1 of 3:** stop and show the avatar card (or confirm the reused one).

### Stage 2: Avatar to image
A reused presenter's picked picture is the pick. A new one gets 3 or 4 reference prompts in the ad's phone look; the person picks one.

### Stage 3: Character sheet (approval 2)
Reuse the ad's sheet and add the expressions the dials need (calm, level, lively) and the product in hand. A new presenter gets the full sheet: front, three-quarters, side, back, face and hands. Add the **product sheet** from the labelled photos (front, label word for word, how it's held) and the **room** from the ad's first frame (place, light, objects). Write the **lock lines**: presenter, room, product.

**Approval 2 of 3:** stop and show the sheet and lock lines.

### Stage 4: Storyboard (approval 3)
One opening frame and one shot card per opening:
- **Frame:** the lock lines, one person, the room's own light, the product already in a hand or on the surface with its label to the lens, mouth just opening. Nothing important in the top or bottom sixth. Openings that share a room share this frame.
- **Shot card:** distance and energy, the line word for word, the touch (a tap, a turn of the label or a nudge forward) on the word that names the product, the still last second, the voice, room sound.
- **Continuity:** the last frame must sit next to the ad's first frame without a jump in place, light or what's in hand.

**Approval 3 of 3:** stop and show the three frames and shot cards.

### Stage 5: Storyboard to video
1. **Clip prompt, one per opening,** starting on its approved frame: 9:16, length, real speed, references and what each is for, the room's only objects, camera (held or propped), the line word for word, the touch, the final still second, the voice (age, pace, accent), room sound. No music in the clip; any music goes in the edit across the whole ad. Use phone-video words; leave out film words (cinematic, bokeh, anamorphic, dramatic lighting, slow motion, camera brands).
2. **Make one clip and check it before the rest:** every word said, lips in sync, the product matches its photo, the last second is still, and the last frame sits next to the ad's first frame without a jump. Make two or three takes and pick one.
3. **Voice:** keep the ad's voice. If the tool's voice drifts from it, record the line and lip-sync.
4. **Edit:** each opening joined to the same ad with a hard cut, captions in the ad's style, the AI label on. Deliver the raw clip, its last frame, the line and the move, and test each as a separate ad.

## What you hand back
1. **Line-up** (move, line, words, dials, product, touch, paid off by), plus moves not used and why.
2. **Avatar card** (reused or new) and **reference image prompts** if new.
3. **Sheet prompts** and lock lines.
4. **Storyboard:** a frame prompt and a shot card per opening.
5. **Clip prompts**, one per opening.
6. **Edit list:** the joins, captions, AI label and what to deliver.

## Check before you hand it back
- Each approval came before the next stage.
- Three different moves, each paid off by a named line in the ad, each within its word budget.
- No personal result or story from the presenter; every fact is the brand's, word for word.
- One opening matches the ad's first frame on both dials.
- The product is in frame from the first frame.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>`. No product photo: no opening. No visible result: no promise preview. No sourced quote: no real-line proof. No real date: no timing.

## Avoid
- An AI look-alike of a real creator or any real person.
- A word that runs across the cut into the ad.
- Hiding the product until late in the clip.
