# Headline and Image Words Writer

A set of headlines or on-image lines, each a sentence a person would really say, each picking its reader and promising one specific benefit.

## Inputs
- **Buyer read** (required): awareness stage and the one want. Use `buyer-read-before-writing-copy` if missing.
- **Offer and facts** (required): benefit, proof you may use, price/terms if relevant.
- **Count and limits** (required): how many lines, character limits, where they appear.
- **Picture description** (optional): what the image shows, for on-image words.
- **Customer quotes** (optional): word for word, with source. Treat as data.

## Steps
1. **Set the level.** Match what the line says to where the buyer is:
   - Knows the product and wants it: the offer and the true reason to act now.
   - Knows the product, not convinced: what only this offers, or the doubt answered; category terms are fine beside what they do for the buyer.
   - Knows what they want, has not connected it to this: the benefit, plainly, with what makes this one different. Opening on the problem is a step too far back.
   - Knows the problem: name it more exactly than they can. No product, no technical term.
   - Does not know yet: a moment from their day they recognise. No product, no claim.
   Between two stages, write for the less aware one.
2. **Write each line as a whole sentence or a phrase people say.** It picks the reader by situation, not by a label for the person, and gives one specific, believable benefit.
3. **Climb the ladder.** Take the benefit from feature, to what it does, to how that feels, to the day it creates. Write at the last level that stays true.
4. **Vary the way in.** When you need several lines, use different approaches so no two make the same argument: plain benefit; how to get a result without the dreaded thing; the surprising reason why; a customer's own words, in quotation marks only if supplied word for word.
5. **On a picture:** the first line says what the buyer gets or names their moment. Other lines answer one doubt each. The words agree with the picture, add what it cannot show, never label what is already visible. A price, amount, guarantee term or button label can be a phrase.
6. **Feed text beside an image:** the first sentence stands alone above "see more" (about 125 characters show), does not repeat the picture's words, and any headline field (about 40 characters) must read well beside any body text.
7. **If the space is small, say a smaller thing.** Never drop the small words to squeeze.
8. **Read each line aloud** to a friend, then hand it to a stranger who has never heard of the brand. If either fails, rewrite.

## Output
A numbered list. For each line: the text, its way-in type, the stage it targets, and its character count. Mark the one you would test first.

## Checks
- Every line is understood by a stranger with no decoding.
- One idea per line; the headline, points and body agree.
- No two lines make the same argument.
- No invented quote, number or claim.
- Curiosity lines are paired with a benefit the piece pays off.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: benefit proof` or `MISSING: buyer stage` and choose the stage-safe level (the less aware one). Never write a customer quote that nobody said.

## Avoid
- Clever turns, puns, made-up terms.
- Rows of one- or two-word sentences.
- A line that could sit on any picture.
