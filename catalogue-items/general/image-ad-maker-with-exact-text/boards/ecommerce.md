# Get images that match your plan, word for word: for online stores

Use this when the image shows a physical product you sell. It adds to steps 3, 4 and 8 of the main skill.

**Bring as well**
- Your show-it-right rules: how the product opens, how it is held, what is never shown.
- Product photos by role: front of the pack, the product out of the pack, in a hand, in use, a close-up of the texture.
- Outside measurements, if you have them.
- Notes from past images that went wrong, such as "the pouch keeps coming back torn".

**Pick the main subject**
- Decide what the camera sees: the closed pack, the product taken out, or the two together. Then name the one that leads.
- Only a form you have a photo of can lead. A sachet from inside a pouch, a lid off its jar or the inside of a jacket, with no photo, stays small or out of frame.

**Hold the product steady across a set**
- Write the same visible facts in every prompt. For Sprout: "a glossy deep green stand-up foil pouch, big yellow wordmark, yellow bear face top right, "28 Daily Packs" along the bottom."
- For the printing: "Keep every word printed on the pouch as Image 1 shows it, with matching letter shapes in matching places. Do not redraw it."
- Name what must not be there once, plainly: "No second pouch and no box." For a shot with no people: "No people and no hands."
- No invented flavours, ingredients, finishes, dents or tears.

**Get the size right**
- Size needs proof: measured outside dimensions, or a photo next to a hand or an everyday object. "500 ml" or "28 packs" tells the model nothing about height.
- Make a product stand out by framing it closer, not by making it bigger than it is.
- Labels on round packs bend with the surface and wrap out of sight at the sides.
- Clothes keep their cut, print size and the way they hang in that pose. A close-up changes the crop, never the proportions.
- Several products together need a photo or measurements that show their sizes side by side.

**Opening, holding, using**
- Find the top of a pack from its printing: whichever edge sits over the brand name while that name is the right way up. If the pack is tipped, it still opens at that edge; say which way the printing now faces.
- Describe the whole movement. For Sprout: "Her right hand holds a daily pack torn straight across its top edge; a small handful of green gummy bears tips out into her open left palm. The big pouch stays closed behind them."
- Hands keep the label in view. No more hands than people. Fingers meet the palm naturally, and nails keep the shape and colour of the approved photos, with no manicure added.
- Approved worn items (a hair clip, an earring, a tattoo) stay in the same place, size and number in every view, and sit on the body the way they really would.

**Putting the product into a scene photo**
Say which photo is the scene, which is the product and which is the person. Anchor the size to something in the scene. Say what the product stands on. It takes the scene's light, shadow direction, reflections and focus. A label that reads clearly but says the wrong thing is still wrong. Steam, water drops, extra props and luxury styling are choices the plan makes, never extras you add.
