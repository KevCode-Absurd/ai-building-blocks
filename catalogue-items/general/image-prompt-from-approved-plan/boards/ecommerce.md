# Image Prompt From Approved Plan: add-on for online stores

Use when the subject is a physical product. It sharpens steps 4, 7 and 9 of the main skill.

**Extra inputs**
- Product truth sheet: looks like, pack, how it is used, never show, visible result, reference photos by job (pack front, out of pack, in hand, in use, close-up).
- Verified exterior dimensions, if known.
- Lessons from past feedback about how the product opens, is held and is used.

**Product fidelity**
- Name the form in the picture: the pack, the product out of it, or both, and which is the hero. A form no photo shows (a single sachet from inside a pouch, a pan out of its sleeve, a jacket's lining) is never the hero.
- Repeat the two or three visible facts verbatim in every prompt of a set: "a 30 ml amber glass bottle with a black dropper cap and a white label"; "a 26 cm bare steel pan, dark grey, with a long riveted handle".
- Keep printing exactly: "Keep the label exactly as in Image 1: same words, same typeface, same position. Do not redraw or re-letter it."
- Say what must not appear in full: "There is no box and no second product anywhere in this image." For a frame without people: "No people: no face, no hands."
- Size needs evidence: verified exterior dimensions plus a visible anchor at similar depth. Capacity gives no height or width. Palm and wrist comparisons are not universal measurements. A close product can look large through perspective while keeping its true size; prominence comes from framing, not enlargement.
- Cylindrical labels follow curvature and occlusion. Garments keep cut, pattern scale and pose-dependent drape without changing other clothing. A macro changes framing, not proportions. Multi-product arrangements need relative size evidence per item.
- No invented components, ingredients, finishes or damage.

**Product in use**
- A pack's top is set by its printing: the top edge is the edge above the name when the name reads upright. A tipped pack still opens at that edge; say how its printing now reads.
- Say the whole action: "The dropper is lifted straight out of the neck of the bottle; three drops fall from its tip into her left palm." "The jacket is zipped to the collar; the storm flap is pressed flat over the zip."
- Hands: which hand, where thumb and fingers sit, which way the front faces, what the other hand does. No hand over the label; no more hands than people in frame. Distinct digits join through plausible joints, palm and wrist; naturally hidden digits stay hidden. Keep approved nail shape, length, colour and finish, with curved, source-consistent highlights. Add no manicure, pinch grip or accessory the plan did not ask for.
- Approved worn details (clip, stud, tattoo) need position, attachment, overlap, contact and surface response: a clip grips a named lock with strands passing beneath, a stud follows the contour with a tiny reflection and contact shadow, matte ink follows skin without a raised edge. Preserve count, design and scale across views.

**Placing a product into a scene**
Bind scene, product and identity roles; anchor size; describe the support; inherit the scene's light, reflections, perspective and focus. A readable but invented label is still wrong. Condensation, steam, props and luxury styling are optional choices, not defaults.
