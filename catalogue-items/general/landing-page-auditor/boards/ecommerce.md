# Fix store pages in the order buyers read them

Every store page gets the same three reads: the glance, the skim and the doubt. What changes is the question each page has to answer. Figures are rules of thumb unless a source is named; never quote them to a brand as its benchmark.

## Where shoppers give up
Baymard Institute's survey of US online shoppers (cart abandonment list, updated 2025) gives the most common reasons for leaving at checkout: extra costs too high (40%), delivery too slow (20%), not trusting the site with card details (19%), being made to create an account (18%), a long or complicated checkout (17%), errors or crashes (17%), a weak returns policy (13%), and not being able to see the total up front (12%). Use this as the doubt-read checklist for every page below: each of these should be answered before the buyer reaches the step where it bites.

## Page by page

| Page | The question it must answer | Glance | Skim | Doubt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home | "Is this shop for me?" | What's sold and for whom, in the headline; one main button | Best sellers, proof the shop is real (rating with source, guarantee, delivery time) | Delivery cost and returns findable in one tap |
| Collection | "Which one should I pick?" | Clear product names and prices on every card | Filters that match how buyers choose (size, need, flavour), honest "best seller" badges | Price on the card is the price at checkout |
| Product | "Is this the one, and what will it really cost?" | What it is, who it's for, price, button | Benefits, what's in the box, proof near the button, the risk line under it | Full cost including renewal and shipping, delivery date, returns window, sizing or contents, reviews labelled with what was bought |
| Cart | "What am I about to pay?" | Total, delivery cost, checkout button | Guarantee and delivery time beside the total | No surprise fees; any add-on offered, never pre-added |
| Checkout | "Can I finish quickly and safely?" | Guest checkout, express payment | Few steps, address autocomplete | Card trust signals, the returns line, no pre-ticked extras |
| After purchase | "Did it work, and what next?" | Order confirmed, arrival date | One relevant offer at most | Any time limit on an offer is real |

## What changes by kind of product

**Used up and reordered** (food, supplements, skincare). The first order is a trial, so the doubt read centres on: the contents, the directions, the number of days a pack covers, and exactly how subscribing works (renewal price, how to pause, how to cancel). Price per day only from the real price and pack size. Best proof: reviews about results and about keeping the habit. After purchase: offer more of the same, or a subscription, only with real terms. Measure first orders, reorders and subscription cancellations.

**Kept for years** (a pan, a jacket, a mattress). Buyers visit several times and compare, so the doubt read centres on: fit and size, dimensions and materials, what it works with, care, how long it lasts, returns (window, who pays) and warranty. Best proof: reviews from long-time owners, photos of wear, tests by a named body. After purchase: something that goes with it, a referral, a review request after a few weeks of use. Measure first orders, return rate and reasons, and time from first visit to purchase.

## Honesty checks on every page
- A "was" price must be one the item really sold at, openly, for a real period (the US FTC's guide on former price comparisons, 16 CFR 233.1).
- No charge or add-on is pre-ticked. Buyers tick it themselves. Under the EU Consumer Rights Directive (Article 22), a pre-ticked box doesn't count as consent to an extra payment, and the buyer can claim it back.
- Ratings, review counts and "bought today" notices show only what's real and say where they come from.
- Urgency only with a real end date or stock figure.

## Speed
If speed was measured, compare it with Google's Core Web Vitals "good" marks: largest content shown within 2.5 s, response to a tap within 200 ms, layout shift under 0.1. Every app and script added to a store page slows it, so a slow page is a reason to remove one before adding another.
