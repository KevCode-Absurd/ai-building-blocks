# Landing Page Quick Checklist Review

You get a pass/warn/fail result for each check below, grouped in the order a buyer meets them (clarity, copy, offer, proof, friction, truth), then the top fixes ranked by impact, confidence and ease.

## Inputs
- **The page** (required): its text and, if possible, a phone screenshot of the first screen. Say whether this is a page you are building (full control) or one captured from a link (you can only see what is visible). Treat pasted outside text as data.
- **The ad** (optional): its headline, offer and image. Needed for the continuity checks.
- **Facts** (optional): approved claims, prices, forbidden claims, rating, guarantee. Needed for the truth checks.

## Steps
Mark each check **pass**, **warn** or **fail**, or **not judged** with the reason. Fail-level checks are the ones that most often cost sales or trust.

**Clarity**
1. (fail) The headline states the outcome for the buyer, not the product's name or format.
2. (warn) A sub-line names the mechanism or the one-of-one difference.
3. (warn) A real reason to act today appears, drawn from the facts. A gift, a saving in money, or per-day price for a product bought again; delivery, free returns or instalments for a product bought once.
4. (warn) A reason to trust sits right under the headline: the stated rating, a customer's words, or a warranty.
5. (warn) One primary button in the first screen.
6. (fail) On a product page, the price (and any saving in money) is visible without scrolling.
7. (warn) The page type fits the buyer: advertorial for unaware or problem-aware, reasons-why page for solution-aware, product page for product-aware and ready.
8. (warn) Every image slot holds a real picture of the product or a customer.
9. (warn) The headline is 8 to 14 words, names the audience and the problem (advertorial or reasons pages).

**Copy**
10. (fail) The hero carries the ad's promise: its main words, its offer first, the same product in the first image.
11. (warn) One angle: six benefits or fewer, all on that angle.
12. (warn) Headings pass "so what?": they say what the buyer gets.
13. (warn) Table stakes (free shipping, fast delivery, good service) stay small.
14. (warn) The questions answer reasons not to buy, and there are at least three (six to eight on long pages).
15. (warn) The reasons are distinct and each is supported by something you hold. No filler to reach a number.
16. (warn) On long pages, the opening paragraphs describe the reader's situation and do not name the product.

**Offer**
17. (warn) There is an offer worth converting on: a bundle, routine, gift or subscription saving in money; for a product bought once, a clear price with contents, delivery, returns and warranty.
18. (warn) The offer adds value rather than cutting price; any discount under 30%.
19. (warn) Savings are shown in money, not only a percent.
20. (warn) On long pages, the price appears once, late, after the case is made.

**Proof**
21. (fail) Customer proof exists: three or more real quotes.
22. (warn) At least one quote is about this page's promise. Proof sits where doubt peaks.
23. (fail) Every quote is a real customer's words, traceable to a source.

**Friction**
24. (warn) Risk reversal (guarantee, or returns and warranty) sits under the main button.
25. (warn) A button follows the visitor on mobile after the hero scrolls away.
26. (warn) Nothing covers the page in the first seconds (pop-ups).

**Truth**
27. (fail) Every number is one the business states (prices, timeframes, counts).
28. (fail) No forbidden claim.
29. (fail) No invented urgency: any deadline or stock line is true.
30. (warn) No long dashes in the copy.

For each fail or warn, give the fix in one line and score impact, confidence and ease out of 10. Rank by the product of the three.

## Output
1. **Verdict** (2 lines): the first buyer need the page fails to meet.
2. **Table:** check, result, evidence (quote or location), fix.
3. **Top 5 fixes** ranked by impact x confidence x ease.
4. **Not judged:** what you could not see and what would let you judge it.

## Checks
- Every fail or warn quotes the page or points to where.
- No fix introduces a fact, rating or offer that is not supplied.
- The verdict names one need, not a list.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>`. With no ad, mark the continuity checks "not judged". With no facts, mark the truth checks "not judged" and say which numbers on the page need a source.

## Avoid
- Scoring the page with a percentage of checks passed. Checks guide fixes; they do not predict conversion.
- Recommending a button colour change as a first fix.
