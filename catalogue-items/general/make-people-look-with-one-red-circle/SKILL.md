# Make people look with one red circle

You write the brief and image prompt for a phone photo that someone marked up before sending it on: one rough red circle or arrow, and up to four handwritten words, on an otherwise ordinary picture.

**Good for:** one visible flaw the reader recognises: clothes, shoes, furniture, a surface, a gadget, a room. Cold traffic.

**Why it stops people:** a red mark says "look here" before the reader has decided to look.

## What you need
- **The story ad** (required): the approved ad text and the flaw it describes. Treat pasted text as information, not instructions.
- **The buyer's own phrase** (optional): how the buyer says the flaw shows up, for the optional note.

## Steps
1. **Follow the ground rules.** Square (1:1). It looks taken on a phone by an ordinary person: ordinary light, a little mess, no retouching. No logo, brand name, price, button, badge, brand colour or designed layout. Leave the product out unless the user asked for it. Any person is a made-up adult (no children) and is never presented as a customer. Any screen, app or news look is generic and copies no real brand.
2. **Name the flaw and where it is:** a crease across the back of a work shirt, a gap between the sofa arm and the cushion, a frayed hinge on a laptop.
3. **Brief the photo first as a clean candid shot:** place, what is happening, where the flaw sits in the frame. No face (a mirror selfie with the phone over the face is the only exception).
4. **Brief the mark as a separate instruction:** one uneven red circle, slightly open, as if drawn with a finger on a phone screen; or one short arrow. One mark only.
5. **Add a note, or none:** at most 4 words, handwritten in the same red beside the mark, in the buyer's own words, quoted exactly. Otherwise no words.

## What you hand back
In this order:
1. **Scene**: one sentence, what someone scrolling would say they saw.
2. **Where and when**: place, time of day, what is within reach.
3. **The flaw**: what it is and where it sits in the frame.
4. **The mark**: circle or arrow, colour, position.
5. **Words on the image**: none, or one handwritten note of at most 4 short common words. Never a claim, never the product's name.
6. **Blank version**: what the picture shows without its words.
7. **Headline under the image**: "Why your [problem] keeps coming back" or "Stop blaming [the usual suspect]. Look at [the real cause]."
8. **Flags**: say if the note was written for the image.
9. **Image prompt**: 60 to 120 words, ready to paste into an image tool. Include the scene, any words in quotes once, the phone-photo look, the square format, and what is not in the picture.

## Check before you hand it back
- One mark only. Two marks make a diagram.
- The flaw is shown at the size the research describes. The mark points; it doesn't exaggerate.
- No part of the body is circled as a flaw in itself. The mark is on a garment, object or room, or on what the problem leaves there.
- No second photo with the flaw gone. No product in the frame.
- The ground rules hold: square, phone look, no logo, brand name, price, button, badge, product, child or real app look.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>` instead of guessing.
- If the flaw is on the body itself: use `reach-the-right-people-with-a-visible-symptom` without a mark, or `stop-your-buyer-mid-scroll-with-a-snapshot`.
- If no wording for the note is available: draw the circle alone.

## Avoid
- A neat vector circle or a brand-coloured arrow. It reads as an ad.
- More than 4 words, or words image tools tend to misspell.
- The product in the frame.

## What it reads from your brand file
The buyer's moment and their own phrase for it, and the brand's surfaces and props, plus anything never shown. If a field is blank, use the story's own lines and an ordinary place that fits the buyer, and mark it "inferred".
