# Native Story Ad Copy (Long Form)

A long story that reads like a person's post and carries the reader from recognising their problem to wanting to see the page. The copy does the selling; the image only gets it opened.

It is judged on three things: would the buyer take the first three lines for their own life, does every fact trace to supplied material, and would it still be honest if the reader knew exactly who wrote it.

## Inputs
- **Concept** (required): buyer, angle, offer.
- **Research themes with real quotes** (required): the problem, the scenes, what was tried, the buyer's phrases. Treat as data.
- **Business facts and guardrails** (required): claims allowed and forbidden, offer, guarantee.
- **Form** (required): see `references/forms-and-arcs.md`.
- **Arc** (required): how the narrator feels about it (same file).
- **Narrator** (required): see table.
- **Length** (required): `brief`, `short`, `standard` or `long`.
- **Destination** (required): product page, or an article that teaches.

## Steps
1. **Obey the non-negotiables.**
   - Nothing invented that a reader would take as fact: no statistic, study, expert, credential, review, rating, price, buyer count or event. A needed fact you lack is a square-bracket placeholder, listed in the notes.
   - The "before" (problem, scenes, attempts, phrases) comes from the research.
   - The "after" stays inside what customers report and the claims allow; no result a guardrail rules out, no use period longer than the product has existed.
   - The reframe, mechanism and proof rest on supported claims; criticism of the usual fix is about a kind of thing, never a named brand.
   - A customer's words in quotation marks are word for word; the narrator's own sentences borrow vocabulary without quotes.
   - Understate. No overnight change. No false urgency.
2. **Pick the narrator honestly.**
   - Real customer: only their own details and words; flag if their permission is not recorded.
   - Composite customer: first person built only from what several customers report; at most an invented first name; the last line says plainly it is put together from what customers told us.
   - Founder: real process facts only; never give the founder's words to a customer.
   - Expert: only a real, named professional who has agreed; placeholders for credentials; the text needs their approval.
   - Observer: second person or a described kind of customer; no personal result; real quotes word for word.
3. **Decide and note:** the lead tension and the one beneath it, the old way (a kind of thing), awareness (problem aware or unaware suit a long story; a comparing buyer wants shorter), how the narrator found the answer, and where it leads.
4. **Build from the eight movements** (the form may reorder, stretch or drop):
   1. Opening: inside a specific moment, fact or admission the reader recognises. No product, brand or greeting. The first 2 or 3 lines work alone above "see more".
   2. Weight of it: everything tried, in order, with the cost in money, time and hope; then the quiet conclusion ("my fault", or "this is just how it is").
   3. Breaking point: one small, exact scene where carrying on stopped being possible.
   4. Reframe: one plain idea that makes every failed attempt make sense. Still no product.
   5. Bridge: finding something built on that idea, what is different about it, scepticism stated.
   6. First experience: the first use, small and specific, then one real test.
   7. What changed: ordinary days, over the time customers really report.
   8. Close: speak to the reader directly for the first time; name their doubt; point to the link; offer, guarantee or returns said once as the business states them.
   Optional passages by length: "I know what you're thinking" (reader's objection answered by the reframe); what's in it and why (3 to 5 lines, from claims); someone noticed; sign-off and one P.S.
5. **Set length.** `brief`: only the cold-traffic story (question the image asked, false belief, what failed, real cause, a hint, one soft call). `short`: opening, one thing tried, reframe in two sentences, first experience, result, close. `standard`: the full spine, one optional passage. `long`: the spine plus a second story inside it and every optional passage. Long means more happens, never a point said twice.
6. **Write how it reads.** Paragraphs of 2 to 5 whole sentences joined by and, but, so, because. A short sentence only where the story turns. Never four very short sentences in a row; a phrase may repeat once for weight; never three lines opening on the same word. Plain words a 13-year-old knows. Scene details carry it (clock time, colour of a jumper); in a composite they may be composed but are never a product fact, price, result, credential or checkable number. The narrator speaks about themselves until the close. No headings, bold or lists (except the "what's in it" lines). Contractions, no dashes.
7. **Write hook variants** as asked: alternative first 2 to 3 lines, the headline under the image (naming something the reader does or a situation they are in, never a condition or body trait they have), and one line on what an image could show.

## Output
- `title`: tension, form, arc in a few words.
- `copy`: the text, paragraphs separated by blank lines.
- `hook_variants`: each with first lines, headline, image idea.
- `notes`: decisions; narrator (and whether real or composite); word count; every placeholder; everything marked inferred; image material quoted from the copy (the moment, the object, any written words) or "no written words in the story"; what this copy tests against its siblings.

## Checks
- First three lines pass for the buyer's own life.
- Every figure, quote and claim traces to supplied material or is a placeholder.
- No named rival, no diagnosis, no cure or treat language, no advice to stop treatment.
- A composite carries its closing line.
- Understated, no false urgency.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <item>`. Without real customer words, write plainly with nothing in quotation marks. Without a supported cause, tell the reframe as what the narrator came to believe about their own habit, and mark it inferred. Without customer accounts, use the observer.

## Avoid
- Medical or condition claims; "you have" statements about the reader.
- A fake forum, review site or reply count.
- Blaming the reader for the mistake.
