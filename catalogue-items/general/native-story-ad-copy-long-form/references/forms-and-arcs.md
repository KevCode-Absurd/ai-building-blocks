# Forms and arcs

Examples are made up. Nothing in them goes into a real piece. The form decides structure; where it differs from the eight movements, the form wins.

## Forms

| Form | Opens with | Suits | Care |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal journey (the standard) | How long they have had it, stated flatly ("I've bought six pans since 2015. I counted last week.") | standard, long | With a composite, durations and attempts must be ones the quotes mention; name kinds, never brands; "go read the reviews" only if the page has real ones |
| Number then family story | A real, sourced fact, then the turn to someone close ("My mother was one of them.") | long | Fact must be in the business's material with its source, said as the source says it; if none, use personal journey. No illness attributed to a medicine, doctor or product; never advise stopping treatment; family member is part of the composite unless a real customer's story |
| Letter about someone | The other person in a scene | standard, long | Narrator reports what they saw; the other's results stay inside what customers report; invented first name only; adult unless the product is for children and every word is supported |
| Forum post | A correction to what they believed ("For eight years I thought it was just me.") | long | Addressed to "anyone else who...", never set on a named forum; no claimed replies or votes; costs are plausible totals of things the research mentions; never says a professional was wrong |
| Expert voice | The expert's standing and the pattern they see | standard, long | Only a real, named professional who agreed; no diagnosis, no advice to change treatment; P.S. quotes are real customer words |
| Mistake I was making | The habit, said with the pride they had in it | standard, long | A common well-meant habit found in research; reader never blamed; that the habit makes things worse must rest on the material or be told as what the narrator came to believe; no dramatic harm unless supported |
| Please stop doing this | An instruction to stop the usual fix | short, standard | "Stop" applies to a kind of product or habit, never to medical treatment; the thing to stop is never something the business's product also asks |
| Rant | "I'm so sick of being told to [usual advice]." | short, standard | Anger at generic advice, never at people or brands; every item is in the research; swearing only if the voice allows; pairs with the quietly angry arc |
| Short review | The problem and what they were about to do about it | short | Only from a real customer's review or story; otherwise write as an observer with real quotes and say the form changed; never laid out as a named review site; no star rating |
| Indirect story (cold traffic) | One of five leads: a story, a warning, an expert who changed their mind, a confession, a question the reader cannot answer yet | brief, short | Narrator is the founder or the business summarising customers; no invented expert; hints at the product, never says the price; closes by pointing to a page that teaches, not the product page; no "do you have [condition]?" |

Indirect-story order: the relatable problem in the buyer's words, the false belief everyone holds, what they tried, the real cause, a hint of the answer, one soft call to the page. The headline under its image takes one shape: "Before you [thing they are about to do], read this"; "Why your [problem] keeps happening"; "It wasn't [what they blamed]. It was [real cause]."; "The reason you [behaviour they are embarrassed by]".

## Arcs (how the narrator feels)

| Arc | Stance | The weight leads with | Reframe is framed as | Closing leans on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The searcher (lead arc) | Determined, a little worn | The full run of things tried, each done properly | What they found late at night when they could not stop looking | "The problem was never me." |
| Exhausted surrender | Tired, resigned | The attempt that cost the most time | An explanation that found them after they stopped looking | Not believing ordinary was still on offer |
| Quietly angry | Controlled, precise | The biggest promise that delivered least | The thing nobody selling in this category mentions | "I'm not making a promise. I'm telling you what happened." Anger only at kinds of product |
| Reluctant believer | Guarded | The attempt they had most hope for | The thing they nearly dismissed and came back to | Being glad they were wrong; leans on the real guarantee if there is one |
| The self-blamer | Privately ashamed | The one they worked hardest at, and what they decided about themselves | Evidence it was never their fault: a structural reason effort could not fix it | Being let off the hook; the story never blames the reader |

Tone rules: lift slowly, never end on excitement; numbers and specifics instead of adjectives.
