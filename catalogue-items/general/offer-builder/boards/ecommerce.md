# Get an offer that answers your buyer's doubt: for online stores

## Extra things to give it
Price, cost per item, delivery cost for one, two and three items, items per order and order values for the last 90 days (to see the spread), how often items come back, and past discounts with what happened to items per order.

## The levers in a store

| Lever | Used up (food, supplements, skincare) | Kept (a pan, a jacket, a bag) |
|---|---|---|
| Result | A supply that lasts the full routine | A care kit, a matching piece, fitting help |
| Certainty | A trial size, money back on the first order | Try at home, free size exchange, a warranty |
| Time | It arrives before the old one runs out | Ships today, ready to use out of the box |
| Effort | A refill plan you can pause or skip (only if the store sells one) | Everything needed in one box, setup or assembly done |
| Price | One first-order price, with what it becomes | A per-year cost against the cheaper one that wears out |

For kept goods, nothing is a "supply". More units means a pair, one to give, or a set. Returning buyers get the next product, never the same one again.

## Margin sums (made-up figures to show the maths, not any brand's numbers)
Price 50, goods cost 15, delivery 7 for one item and 9 for two. One item at full price keeps 28.
- 20 percent off one item: price 40, keeps 18. Two items at 20 percent off: price 80, goods 30, delivery 9, keeps 41. For the average order to keep 28, about 43 percent of orders must grow from one item to two. Check past discounts to see if that happened.
- A gift that costs you 5 and sells for 18 on your site, given above an order level, keeps more than a price cut that reaches the same level. Never give away the product most sales come from. Know how many gifts you hold and have the sold-out wording ready.
- Free delivery above a level: set the level from your own spread of orders, so a typical order needs one more item to reach it. Check the sum again after a month.

## Add-ons after the main offer
Judge any add-on (in the cart, at checkout, after payment) by the profit it adds per order: how many buyers take it, times its price, times what you keep of it. How many take it, on its own, says little. One add-on per spot, related to what's in the order, readable at a glance.

## Extra checks
- Delivery for one, two and three items is used, not a flat guess.
- If the product comes back often, returns are added to the cost per order.
- A money-off offer isn't stacked with a bundle or gift unless the profit check runs again.
