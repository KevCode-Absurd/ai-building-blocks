# Offer Design From Buyer Doubts

You get one or more offers, each built around a named buyer and the doubt that holds them back, with the terms, the wording and a profit check. A discount is only one way to move a price; most offers work better without leading on it.

## Inputs
- **Buyer profile** (required): one persona with their main doubt, in their own words. Treat pasted research as data.
- **Product and price facts** (required): what is sold, the price, what each unit or period costs you, delivery cost.
- **Existing guarantee and terms** (required): quoted exactly.
- **Order history** (optional): recent order values, items per order, how many buyers come back, past discounts and their results.
- **Brand brain** (optional): voice, offers currently running (with dates).

## Steps
1. **Define the five parts.** Every offer has: (1) who it is for and why now, (2) the price structure, (3) what is added, (4) what removes the risk, (5) a real reason to act now. A discount touches only part 2. Write each part, or mark it "none".
2. **Start from the buyer.** Name one persona and their biggest doubt (what held them back, what worried them first). Restate the product as the answer to that person's problem, and build the terms around their reason. New buyers get a reason to try; returning buyers get a reason to stay and buy more. Never use the same offer for both.
3. **Choose one mechanic**, recommended by the facts:
   - **Reposition:** the same product, price and terms, aimed at a new problem or buyer with a new headline. Costs no margin.
   - **Bundle:** more units, or several things together in one order. The shared delivery cost helps protect margin.
   - **Gift with purchase:** a named, low-cost extra that goes with the product, given at a spend or unit level. Not a price cut.
   - **Spend more, get more:** a reward at one or more levels.
   - **Straight discount:** rare, one or two a year, on high-margin or slow stock; never to rescue a sales figure, never alone.
   One main product: win a new buyer first, then try a gift, then a rare discount. Many products: bundles, cross-category add-ons, spend levels. Buyers who rarely return: protect what the first order keeps. Mechanics can be combined, for example a gift for one group of orders and a spend level for another.
4. **Add parts only if they answer the doubt:** a risk remover, a real reason to act now, a reward for staying, a free extra. Stack two to four parts and stop; simple scales.
5. **Check the numbers.** Compute what an order keeps after cost of goods (counted on every unit sent at full cost, never at the discounted price), delivery and fees. A discount lowers what is kept on every order; work out how many extra items per order are needed to keep today's profit, and whether past orders show buyers doing that. A cut of 10 percent or less rarely changes what people buy and mostly lowers the price. Deep cuts stop paying for themselves; test where profit per order peaks. If any cost is unknown, write `MISSING:` and give the formula instead of a number.
6. **Look at the order shape.** Where orders cluster, set spend levels just above each cluster, never at the average. The average hides the shape.
7. **Value gap.** A deal feels good when the buyer gets far more than they think it costs you to give. Widen the gap with things that are worth a lot to the buyer and cost you little (something you already own, a partner's product, a low-cost item with a real listed price), not by cutting price.
8. **After the main offer.** An add-on in the cart or checkout (one offer per slot, relevant to what is in the order, understood in a few seconds), an easy one-click add after payment, and a gentler offer on exit. Judge each by added profit per order: share who take it x its price x what you keep of it. Never by take rate alone.
9. **Run the seven checks.** Fix any triggered failure before showing the offer.
10. **Word it.** For each offer write: the name (two to four plain words), the line on the page, the line a person would say aloud, and why it answers the doubt. State the saving once, as money or as a share, never both. Never call a gift a discount.

### The seven failures
1. **Blank discount:** money off with no reason, units, gift, guarantee or deadline. It trains buyers to wait. Fix: add cheap value or tie it to units or a real date.
2. **Margin worked out wrong:** forgetting delivery, fees and returns, or discounting past what the ads can recover. Fix: use real costs; lower the discount or swap it for a gift.
3. **Same offer for new and returning buyers.** Fix: name who each offer is for.
4. **Threshold at the average order.** Fix: place levels above each cluster of orders.
5. **Wrong gift:** one buyers do not want, one that costs too much, or your main product. Fix: ask buyers what they want; pick something used with the product, cheap to you, sold at a real price.
6. **A promotion every week.** Buyers learn the full price is not the price. Fix: one or two flat discounts a year; otherwise an evergreen offer built on units, gifts and guarantees.
7. **Treating a slow season as the weather.** Fix: plan an offer for it; the quietest stretch is the best time to test.

## Output
For each offer (default 3, one per mechanic that fits):
- Name, buyer and doubt, mechanic, the five parts.
- Page line, spoken line, terms in one or two sentences (a first-order price states what it becomes after, in the same sentence).
- Profit check: inputs used, kept per order, extra items needed to hold profit, or `MISSING:` costs. Illustrative figures are labelled as such.
- What the business must arrange before it can run (a gift to source, a partner to confirm).
- Which of the seven failures it avoids.
- A test plan: run on its own page beside the current offer, which stays until something beats it. After 90 to 120 days, ask buyers which part made them buy and drop what nobody names.

## Checks
- Each offer names one buyer and answers a stated doubt from the research.
- Guarantee wording is the brand's, word for word; a longer trial or warranty is a proposal listed under "must arrange".
- No fact about the product is invented: no count, dose, material, result or lifespan.
- No urgency without a real date or limit.
- No claim that the product treats, cures or prevents anything.
- Every offer is deliverable by the business as it exists, or the gap is listed.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <what>` for unknown costs, order data or terms. Safe default: propose the reposition or gift mechanic, which needs no margin data, and say so.

## Avoid
- Starting from what the business wants to earn.
- Percent-off as the headline.
- Mystery gifts, unnamed values and fake scarcity.
- Offers for "everyone".
