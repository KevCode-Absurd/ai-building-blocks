# Offer design from buyer doubts: online store add-on

## Extra inputs
Price, cost per item, delivery cost for one, two and three items, items per order, order values for the last 90 days (to see where orders cluster), past discounts and what happened to items per order.

## Used up versus bought once
- **Used up:** more units means a longer supply. Offer a subscription only if the store already sells one. Risk remover: money back on the first order, or a small size to try. Returning buyers: a longer supply or a reward on the next order.
- **Bought once:** no subscription, and nothing is called a "supply". More units means a pair, one to give, or a set. Risk remover carries the offer: a trial at home, returns, an exchange for size, a warranty. Value to add: care kit, fitting help, a spare part, an accessory. Returning buyers: the next product, never the same one again.

## Margin sums (illustrative figures, not any brand's numbers)
Price 40, goods cost 12, delivery 6 for one item and 7 for two. One item at full price keeps 22 per order.
- 20 percent off one item: price 32, keeps 14. Two items at 20 percent off keep 33. To hold 22 per order, about 42 percent of orders must grow from one item to two. Check past discounts to see whether that happened.
- The break-even sales lift for a discount climbs gently; the lift needed to keep profit per order climbs faster. Work both out; do not stop at break-even.
- A gift costing the store 4 and listed at 15, given over a spend level, keeps more per order than a deeper price cut that reaches the same level. Never give away the product most sales come from. Know how many gifts exist and have the sold-out wording ready.

## Named deals
Each is a shape of a mechanic above, not another mechanic.
| Deal | Shape | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Buy 1 get 1 free, half price, or 3 for 2 | Bundle | It is a share off the whole parcel. The free unit still costs what it costs to make: compare with the same share off one unit, and both with full price |
| Gift over a spend level | Gift | Set the level just above the typical recent order, not at the average. Name the gift and its price |
| Spend ladder (three levels) | Spend more, get more | Levels above each cluster of orders; the cart says how far the next level is; a last-day bonus level needs a real last day and a named gift |
| Credit on the next order | Gift on the second order | It costs the goods the credit buys, not its face value, and only when spent. The share of credit that gets spent is your own number; until you have it, treat any figure as an assumption |
| Early access for the list | Access on any offer | About a week ahead of the public, same price or percentage; the public price is never below the early one; no extension |
| Shipping cut-off sale | Reposition, no money off | Use the carrier's real last-order dates for each service; show dates, not day counts; consider extending returns into the new year |

## After the main offer: slots
- **Cart:** one offer per slot, relevant to the cart, read in two to three seconds. A cross-sell is a different product that goes with the order; an upsell is more of the same.
- **Checkout:** a small, no-thought add-on, not another product.
- **After payment:** a one-click add that keeps the buyer's payment details; lead high and step down if declined.
- **On leaving:** generous while browsing, small with a full basket, no money off at checkout (a reminder, or a risk removed).

## Extra checks
- Cost of goods counts on every unit at full cost.
- Delivery for one, two and three items is used, not a flat guess.
- Returns, if the product is sent back often, are added to delivery cost.
- A flat discount is not stacked with a bundle or gift unless the profit check is rerun.
