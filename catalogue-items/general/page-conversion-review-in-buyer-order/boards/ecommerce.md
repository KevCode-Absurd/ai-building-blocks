# Page conversion review: online store add-on

Store pages reviewed in the same buyer order. Figures are rules of thumb or illustrations of form unless a source is named; never quote them to a brand as benchmarks.

## Homepage: the three-second test
Look for three seconds, then look away. Can you say what the store sells, who it is for, and what to do next? If not, fix the first screen.
- Headline says what is sold and for whom, under about ten words. Not "Welcome" and not a slogan alone.
- One primary action, contrasting, in view on a phone. A still hero beats a carousel.
- Trust within the first scroll: rating and review count as the store states them, press that really ran, order count, secure checkout, guarantee, free-delivery threshold, delivery time.
- Menu under about seven items, search and cart visible. Text readable without zooming, tap targets about 44 px, no sideways scroll, no pop-up over the first screen.
- Remove: slideshow heroes, many equal buttons, huge menus, autoplay video with sound, instant pop-ups.

## Collection pages
Lead with best sellers and "most popular" badges (where true) so first-timers pick a trusted option. Simple filters, bright consistent images, price and star rating on each card.

## Product page
- Buy button in view on a phone; accelerated checkout buttons on.
- First screen shows what the buyer cannot buy without: pack or bundle rows for something bought again, size or colour picker with a size guide one tap away for something bought once.
- Risk removal directly under the buy button. Delivery time, returns window and (for things bought once) the warranty by the button.
- Comparison chart against the alternatives, FAQ for shipping, sizing, safety and guarantee, "what is in the box" in the images, product video of use, care or unboxing.
- Reviews labelled with what the reviewer bought and whether verified.
- Cost per use for things used up, from the real price and pack size ("per day"); cost per year of use for things bought once only if the brand states how long it lasts.
- The image gallery is a mini landing page: many shoppers swipe the images before scrolling. Put benefits, proof, before and after (only with evidence), included gifts and comparisons in the images.

## Cart
Slide-out cart, never a redirect, never a dead end. Reviews, value props, risk removal and add-ons. Free-delivery progress line with convenient, high-margin add-ons that close the gap.

## Checkout
Guest checkout on. Accelerated payment methods and address autocomplete. Tipping off unless intended. About two or three clicks from product page to payment confirmation. Reassurance: returns or guarantee line, delivery time. A "bought today" notice only if wired to real orders. Fewer apps (each slows the site). What can be changed in checkout depends on the store plan; check before recommending a redesign.

## Landing pages and listicle presell pages
- Above the fold: one headline with a single promise, a subheader, proof, one primary button in a standout colour; the first section mirrors the ad's hook.
- Below: why existing solutions fail, one clear advantage or comparison, guarantees; sticky button; offer-code reminder.
- Three kinds of proof: customer reviews, established sources (press, expert quotes that are real), customer-made content.
- A listicle is a short, scannable page that builds interest before the buyer reaches the product page. Start from one angle (key benefit, a problem solved, or an alternative to the usual way). Headline shape "N reasons ..." (borrow the shape, never the claim: "celebrities prefer" or "going viral" only if true and on file). Spend most effort on the hero: announcement bar, headline, subhead, lifestyle image, proof. Each reason supports the one angle; end with a buy box. Traffic flows ad, listicle, product page.
- An advertorial is advertising and marked as such, in the brand's voice, with no invented narrator, expert, publication or rating.

## Post-purchase
One-click add-ons after payment convert well. For things used up, test more of what they just bought at a better price. For things bought once, a second pan or jacket is rarely wanted: test what goes with it (lid, care kit, second colour as a gift). Thank-you page offers only with real time limits; follow-up emails; store credit; order-anniversary offers.

## What changes by kind of product
| | Bought again | Bought once |
|---|---|---|
| Buying pattern | Fast; the first order is a trial | Slower: several visits and comparisons before one purchase |
| The buyer's worry | Will I like it, what is in it, how do I use it, how long does a pack last, when will I notice a change | Will it fit, how big is it, what is it made of, does it work with what I own, how do I look after it, how long will it last |
| Sections to add | Contents and what each part does; usage; pack duration; expectations, only as the brand states them | Fit or size guide; dimensions and materials; what is in the box; why it lasts; care; comparison with the cheaper kind |
| Offer block | Single purchase versus subscribe, with the saving shown in money, the price per day, delivery rhythm and how to pause or cancel; a starter pack | One price, size and colour pickers, delivery time, returns, warranty; the set that completes it; instalments |
| Risk removal | Money-back guarantee, cancel any time | Returns (window, who pays), warranty, repairs |
| Best proof | Reviews about results and about keeping at it | Reviews from long-time owners, wear photos, tests by a named body |
| Next step after buying | More at a saving; a subscription | An item that goes with it; a referral; a review request after weeks of use |
| Measure | First order, reorder rate, subscription churn | First order, return rate and reasons, time from first visit to purchase |

## Price and order value
- Avoid "no-zone" prices that are neither premium nor a clear deal; round to appealing figures. Customers rarely remember exact prices.
- Contribution margin can beat conversion rate: a higher price with lower conversion can earn more.
- A bundle can beat single units on profit per order even when one unit is free; compute it with your real costs (see the offer design block).
- **Free-delivery level:** use the most common cart value, not the average, which big spenders pull up. Chart purchase frequency against cart value, find where orders bunch, and test levels just above it, watching conversion and order value together. Revisit as order value moves. Pair it with high-margin cart fillers.
- Instalment payments over about 100 in the store currency (rule of thumb).

## Quick revenue ideas (opt-in only)
- Rush processing offered after payment, only if rush orders really ship first.
- Shipping protection as an opt-in, never pre-ticked (a pre-ticked charge is a dark pattern, unlawful in some markets).
- Free-delivery level plus cart fillers.

## Retention (things bought again)
Cancel reasons usually start with cadence mismatch: offer several intervals, easy date changes, pause or skip before cancel, pre-shipment reminders, a downsell on cancel attempt, an exit survey. For things bought once, retain through care, repairs, referrals and what goes with them. An SMS opt-in on the thank-you page a few seconds after the order is placed works because goodwill is highest and checkout stays clean.

## Extra checks
- No pre-ticked charges, fake scarcity, typed counters or default ratings.
- Every "was" price is a real former price.
- Giveaways follow local prize rules.
