# Persuasive Body Copy with Proof and Close

The body and the ask of a longer piece: a problem the buyer has, a promise only this offer makes, proof next to each claim, and one easy next step.

## Inputs
- **Opening** (required): the first lines already written (or the lead type).
- **Buyer read** (required): stage, doubts, words.
- **Facts, proof, offer, guarantee** (required): only what the business can show.
- **Length** (required).
- **Brand brain** (optional): voice, forbidden claims.

## Steps
**Body**
1. **Find the one interesting true thing** a rival could not say: a fact, how it is made, how it works, a contrast, an odd detail a customer noticed. Build around it. If nothing exists, build on the buyer's situation and say so.
2. **Cover four things.** A problem this buyer has now; a promise this offer can make; proof beside the claim it supports; an easy next step. The opening decides the order.
3. **Sell against what they do now.** The old way, the category habit or doing nothing is the villain, never the buyer.
4. **Make it seen.** If a line cannot become a picture, a moment, an action, an object or a number, zoom in until it can. Walk each benefit into the buyer's day. Product details come only from the facts; scenes come from research and carry no figure the research lacks.
5. **Place proof.** Proof must prove the promise made. Size the proof to the claim. Put it in the same breath as the claim. Match the kind to the doubt: others like me, is it the rule or luck, is it real, who is behind it, what if it fails. A small real flaw admitted early, with the larger benefit next, helps doubtful buyers; give cold buyers the benefit first.
6. **Give no comfortable place to stop.** The first sentence is short. Do not end a paragraph on a finished thought mid-argument. Answer every question you open. Use true small yeses early. Answer questions in the order the buyer asks them. Cut any line a good salesperson would not say in person.

**Close**
7. State the deal plainly: what they get, price, how soon, every condition.
8. Take away the risk with the real guarantee and its real condition; never sharpen it into a promise of results.
9. Answer the one doubt most likely to stop this buyer just before the ask.
10. Give one action, named by what is behind it (for example "See the three sizes"), not by the button.
11. Add what waiting costs only if true (a real date, count or price change); otherwise the real cost of the problem continuing. Leave it out for small impulse buys or decided buyers.
12. Return to what the opening promised so the buyer can say in one sentence what they get.

## Output
The body and close as finished text within the length. Beneath it: "Proof placed:" (claim -> proof), "Assumed:" and "Placeholders:".

## Checks
- Every claim a doubtful buyer would question has backing within one sentence.
- One action only.
- No invented figure, review, expert, study, date or count.
- The close names the real guarantee terms or none.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: proof for <claim>` and shrink the claim to what is shown. If no deal terms are supplied, name the action and what the buyer gets, with no terms.

## Avoid
- Piling on points where one doubt needed answering.
- Urgency with no real basis.
- Blaming the buyer for earlier choices.
