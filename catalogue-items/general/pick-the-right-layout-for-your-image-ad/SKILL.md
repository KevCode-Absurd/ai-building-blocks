# Pick the right layout for your image ad

You plan one image ad. First the job the image must do, then a layout with a clear reading order, then a visual style chosen for that job, and, if you're planning a batch, what deliberately varies from the other ads.

## What you need
- **Concept brief** (required): the buyer, the argument, the product or service, the format and the shape (aspect ratio).
- **Evidence on hand** (required): proof assets, the real offer, real photos, testimonials, comparison facts. Anything not supplied counts as absent.
- **Fixed constraints** (optional): a chosen layout, a choice of no text or no product in the image, brand rules.
- **Other ads in the batch** (optional): so this one can differ from them.

## Steps
1. **Choose the job before any layout.** Useful kinds of ad: a problem moment; a mirror of who the buyer is; a myth corrected with facts; a real founder portrait; a testimonial with its source; a grid of verified proof; a fair comparison; a labelled feature; price or value; a genuine deadline; a real bundle; a review that answers an objection. If the proof or offer asset is missing, that kind of ad is ruled out. Generated people or staged scenes never prove a customer's results.
2. **Build a reading order someone gets at a glance.** The main idea first, then the support that matters, then the action if one helps. Give zones to the image, the exact text, the attribution and qualifications, and the product. Rearrange for the requested shape. Never stretch a square. Essential qualifications stay with their claim. Respect no-text and no-product choices. A chosen layout or format is a constraint, not a reason to fall back on a selfie.
3. **Choose the style by its job.** Studio for product clarity. Flat lay for what's inside. Close-up for material. Documentary for how it's used. Editorial or fashion for identity. Food or interior for setting the category. Graphic, illustration or comparison for explaining. A phone video look filmed by a customer is one option, not the default.
4. **Keep the product's identity and the brand's stated rules fixed.** For a batch, vary the mood, light, composition, setting, how big the product is, the style or the camera angle on purpose. Ads that are truly different change the argument or the scene, not just the surface styling.
5. **Describe only the chosen version,** in the fields the person asked for.

## What you hand back
For the one chosen version: the kind of ad and why the evidence allows it; the reading order; a zone map (image, text, attribution, product, action) for the requested shape; the style and the job it serves; fixed constraints; what varies against the rest of the batch.

## Check before you hand it back
- The evidence this kind of ad needs is in the inputs.
- The reading order has one main idea.
- Qualifications sit beside their claims.
- No-text and no-product choices are respected.
- In a batch, no two ads share both the argument and the scene.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <proof | offer | photo>` and choose the nearest kind of ad the evidence supports.

## Avoid
- Starting from the layout.
- Using a generated person as proof.
- A square stretched to fit a tall frame.
