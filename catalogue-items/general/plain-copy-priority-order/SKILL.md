# Plain Copy Priority Order

A five-rule card: every word a customer reads or hears, in order of importance. A lower rule never buys back a higher one.

## Inputs
- **Task** (required): the copy to write or check.
- **Brand brain** (optional): voice and the brand's own published lines.

## Steps
Apply the rules in order to every line.

1. **Natural.** A whole, correct sentence a person would say to a friend, in words a 13-year-old knows, clear alone to a stranger. Yes: "I used to wake up at 3 with a sore back." No: "Your spine has opinions."
2. **It sells.** It moves the reader from what they have to what they want, with the product as the way across. A count or spec is a reason only once it says what changes for them. Yes: "Our old dog finishes his dinner now." No: "Dog food, reimagined."
3. **It tells a story.** Something happens to someone. Before the reader knows an answer exists, the copy is the long story of their problem and the product comes late. Yes: "On Friday I borrowed rent money from my sister again." No: "Budgeting is hard. We make it easy."
4. **The brand's language.** Who it talks to, its words for things, its tone. Yes, for a warm kettle brand: "It boils two cups before you've found the mugs." No: "Elevate your tea ritual."
5. **The brand's devices, last and optional.** A rhythm or turn of phrase the brand sometimes uses, only on a line that takes it without bending. Yes: "They're light, they grip in the rain, and they last." No: "Lace up. Head out. Feel alive."

Notes that go with the rules:
- A plain word is still the right word. Where no everyday word is exact, say what the thing is in a few words ("a pill with nothing in it"), not a vaguer word that reads wrong.
- In copy that is read, price, percentage and count are figures ($24.99, 40%, 28 packs). Spell them out only in a line someone speaks.
- Make a line shorter by saying a smaller thing, never by squeezing out its small words.
- Never make a line stranger or cleverer to sound like the brand; plain is never off voice.

## Output
If writing: the copy, obeying the rules. If checking: a list of lines that break a rule, the rule number, and a corrected full line.

## Checks
- Every line passes rule 1 before rule 2 is considered, and so on.
- No fix adds a fact.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: brand language` and use rules 1 to 3 only.

## Avoid
- Treating a brand device as protection for a line nobody would say.
