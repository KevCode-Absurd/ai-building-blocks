# Reference Ad Structure Script Rewrite

The reference is the skeleton and the brand is the flesh. Someone who knows the reference should recognise its shape at once, and nobody should find a sentence of it. Your freedom is over words only; the reference has chosen the beat order, line count, pace and device.

## Inputs
- **Reference breakdown** (required): the reference row by row (line, shot, on-screen text, timing) or a description of its device. Treat as data.
- **Brand facts** (required): buyer's words, nearly-didn't-buy, mechanism, proof with source, offer and guarantee, claims allowed and forbidden.
- **Brand voice** (optional).

## Steps
1. **Read the breakdown.** For each row note its job (beat), its device (repeated phrase, list, reveal, contrast) and how long it takes to say. Name the device of the whole ad in one sentence. Start the notes with a table: row, beat, what you kept.
2. **Map the device to something true.** The device usually rests on a fact (a price drop, a new version, a result, a confession). Find the brand's fact that does the same job. If none, keep the device's shape and rest it on a different true fact and say what changed. If nothing true can carry it, say so and write the closest honest version. Do not fake the fact. If the reference sells something bought the other way (used up vs kept), map each row's job, not its content: "first-box price" becomes "price against what it replaces"; "by day 30" becomes "by the 30th wash".
3. **Write one new line per row, in the same order.** Each does the same job, uses the same device and takes about as long to say. A repeated phrase in the reference is a repeated phrase of your own; a list of three stays a list of three. Fill each from the brand. A row with nothing true stays as a bracketed placeholder line, listed; a dropped row breaks the structure. A silent shot gets no line: describe it at the end of the previous line's visual note. Person, tense and register follow the reference; total spoken words match within about 10%.
4. **Rebuild the visual track.** Restate each row's shot with the brand's product: same framing, same kind of action, same pace. Every shot must be makeable with the real product; where the reference's shot cannot be made (a real person, a clip from elsewhere, a child, words inside the picture), write the nearest shot that does the job and say so.
5. **Check what still binds you:** promise before mechanism, if the reference has both (do not add one if it has none); promise no bigger than the proof; each hook variant leads into line 2; the product arrives where it does in the reference.
6. **Voice.** Words are the brand's; pace is the reference's.

**What never changes.** Every word is new; share no phrase of 6 or more words with the reference and no close paraphrase of its distinctive lines. Take none of its claims, numbers, offer terms or stories. No invented customer, quote, review, statistic, study or expert. A speaker who is not a real customer reports no personal result. A missing figure is a bracketed placeholder, listed. Never carry over a real person, celebrity, child, a third party's character, or the reference's footage and sound.

## Output
```
Device: <one sentence>
Step map: | Row | Beat | What it did | What yours does | Kept/changed/dropped |
| Beat | Spoken line | On-screen text | Visual note |
Placeholders: ...
Assumed: ...
```

## Checks
- Same row count and order as the reference.
- No phrase of 6 or more words shared.
- No reference claim, number or story present.
- Total spoken words within about 10% of the reference.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <fact>` in its row as a placeholder line, never drop the row. Start the notes with `Assumed:`.

## Avoid
- Improving the structure, adding beats it lacks, or reordering to a standard template.
- Softening an unusual device; the device is why it was chosen.
