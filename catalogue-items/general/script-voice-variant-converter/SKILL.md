# Script Voice Variant Converter

The same script, told from a different seat or at a different time. Each version is a different ad to the viewer and the platform, and it is the cheapest way to get more ads out of something that works. You change the voice and nothing else.

## Inputs
- **Script** (required): lines with beats, on-screen text, visual notes and hook variants.
- **Target person and tense** (required).
- **Who is speaking** (required): real customer, founder, creator, or generated presenter/narrator.
- **Persona name and pronoun** (optional): for third person; otherwise use "they".
- **Brand voice** (optional).

## Steps
1. **Know the voices.** First: "I stopped cooking steak at home." Second: "You've stopped cooking steak at home." Third: "She stopped cooking steak at home." Past suits stories; present suits immediacy and how things work; future is a warning or promise, used sparingly.
2. **Find the new subject.** First: the narrator. Second: the viewer. Third: a described person ("she", "he", "they", or a first name if the original has one). Invent no surname or biography.
3. **Convert story lines** (hook, problem, experience) to the new person and tense.
4. **Leave the mechanism in present tense** whatever the target: how something works is true now.
5. **Leave the offer and call to action addressed to the viewer, in present.** In a third-person script the narrator turns to the viewer for the close.
6. **Fix what the change breaks.** A confession ("I never told anyone") in second person becomes recognition ("you've never told anyone"). A witness line ("my brother asked where I got it") in second person becomes "someone will ask where you got it" only if that stays inside the honesty rule; otherwise keep it as a report about others.
7. **Keep the rhythm:** same sentence lengths, fragments, repeated phrases and brand words.
8. **Convert on-screen text and hook variants** the same way so each hook still leads into line 2.
9. **Update visual notes** only where the person on screen changes (a presenter to camera becomes a narrator over footage). The new picture is makeable: an adult, the real product, no unfilmed real person.
10. **Read it aloud.** Rewrite any line that sounds translated without changing what it says.

**Honesty rules in the new voice.** To first person: only if the speaker could truthfully say it; a line that becomes a personal result needs a real customer saying it; a generated presenter or hired voice keeps that line as an observation ("people say...") and notes it. To second person: describe what the viewer does or tried; never tell them they have a condition or promise a result the original only reported. To future: a result becomes what the product is made to do or what customers report, never a prediction about this viewer.

## Output
The same number of lines in the same order, each with converted spoken text, on-screen text and visual note; converted hook variants; notes listing any line kept in its original voice and why, and any line where honesty limited the conversion.

## Checks
- Line-by-line comparison finds the same facts in the same order; no line added, cut, merged or split.
- Every claim, number, price, offer term, guarantee, product name and quoted customer word unchanged (quotes stay word for word in any voice).
- Each line within about 10% of the original length.
- No conversion creates a claim the original did not make.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: speaker` and assume a generated presenter: no personal result. Without a persona pronoun, use "they".

## Avoid
- Rewriting, shortening or changing the angle.
- Sounding translated.
