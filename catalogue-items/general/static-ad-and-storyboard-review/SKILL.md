# Static Ad and Storyboard Review

A review of what was actually produced, with concrete defects and the smallest useful fix for each. Aesthetic judgement is kept separate from evidence failures and from measured performance.

## Inputs
- **Rendered images or storyboard frames** (required) and **their exact words** (headlines, overlays, feed copy variants, scripts, calls to action).
- **Selected evidence and offer** (required): what the ad is allowed to claim, real prices, terms and dates.
- **Brand voice, logo and typeface references** (required).
- **Buyer and awareness stage** (required).
- **The batch** (optional): for a diversity check.

## Steps
1. Judge the image and its exact words together. A good-looking design fails if the product is wrong, a qualification is unreadable or the headline promises a different argument. Inspect at realistic mobile size: dominant idea, reading order, product recognition where intended, contrast, crop, spelling.
2. Read every delivered headline, overlay, feed variant, script and call to action for brand voice and tone, Grade 8 clarity, a logical buyer argument and a useful action. A necessary supported technical term needs an explanation fitted to the awareness stage.
3. Check the actual logo and typeface, not just a matching colour. A generic opaque repair panel, a removed planned logo or a substituted font fails. Suggest placing text in negative space or a restrained glyph contour while keeping the successful art. For each defect give the exact field or region, the reason and the smallest correction.
4. Check factual statements against the selected evidence. A generated portrait, a proof collage or a before and after scene supplies no customer authentication or efficacy evidence. Comparisons need like-for-like support; reviews need real attribution; offers need the correct price, terms and genuine dates. Label unresolved evidence instead of rewarding confident invention.
5. Find the weak link with a four-beat read: the moment of need, how it feels, the reason to believe, the change promised. Context and visuals may carry beats. Awareness fit means the intended buyer has enough explanation, proof or offer clarity to know the next action.
6. For a batch, ask whether persona, argument, archetype and scene really differ. Recolours and interchangeable selfies add little coverage.
7. For storyboards, inspect the selected identity, product and prop continuity, each shot's purpose, script and action match, and pacing across the full sequence.
8. Score only as an editorial assessment. A score never establishes click rate, conversion lift, return or permission to scale spend.

## Output
A defect list, each item as: asset and region or field; the defect; why it fails (evidence, legibility, brand, argument, diversity); the smallest correction. Then a verdict per asset (pass, fix, remake) and, for a batch, a coverage note. Evidence failures are listed before aesthetic notes.

## Checks
- Every defect names a field or region and a fix.
- Evidence failures and aesthetic notes are separated.
- No score is presented as performance.
- Unresolved evidence is labelled, not passed.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <evidence | logo reference | typeface | words>` and mark that asset "unreviewable on that point".

## Avoid
- Praising polish over a wrong product or unsupported claim.
- Fixes that bury art under opaque panels.
- Treating a generated person as authentication.
