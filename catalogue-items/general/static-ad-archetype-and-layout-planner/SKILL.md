# Static Ad Archetype and Layout Planner

A plan for one static ad: the job the image must do, a layout with a clear reading order, a visual style chosen for that job, and deliberate variation if you are planning a batch.

## Inputs
- **Concept brief** (required): buyer, argument, product or service, format and aspect ratio.
- **Evidence on hand** (required): proof assets, real offer, real photos, testimonials, comparison facts. Anything not supplied is treated as absent.
- **Fixed constraints** (optional): chosen layout, text-free or product-free choice, brand rules.
- **Other ads in the batch** (optional): to vary against.

## Steps
1. Choose the persuasive job before any layout. Useful archetypes: a problem moment; an identity mirror; a grounded myth correction; a real founder portrait; a sourced testimonial; a verified proof grid; a fair comparison; a feature annotation; price or value; a genuine deadline; a real bundle; a review that answers an objection. A missing proof or offer asset rules out that execution. Synthetic people or staged scenes never authenticate customer results.
2. Build a glanceable reading order: the dominant idea, the relevant support, then the action if one helps. Assign zones for the image, the exact text, the attribution and qualification, and the product. Reflow to the requested aspect ratio; do not stretch a square. Essential qualifications stay with their claim. Honour text-free and product-free choices. A selected layout or format is a constraint, not a reason to default to a selfie.
3. Choose the style by its job: studio for product clarity; flat lay for contents; macro for material; documentary for context of use; editorial or fashion for identity; food or interior for category setting; graphic, illustration or comparison for explanation. UGC is one option, not the default.
4. Keep the product's identity and explicit brand constraints fixed. For a batch vary mood, lighting, composition, setting, product prominence, style or camera angle on purpose. Distinct ads change the argument or the scene, not just the surface styling.
5. Describe only the selected execution, in the caller's fields.

## Output
For the one selected execution: archetype and why the evidence allows it; reading order; zone map (image, text, attribution, product, action) for the requested aspect; style and the job it serves; fixed constraints; what varies against the batch.

## Checks
- The archetype's evidence exists in the inputs.
- Reading order has one dominant idea.
- Qualifications sit beside their claims.
- Text-free and product-free choices are respected.
- In a batch, no two ads share both argument and scene.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <proof | offer | photo>` and choose the nearest archetype the evidence supports.

## Avoid
- Starting from layout.
- Using a synthetic person as proof.
- A square stretched to fit a tall frame.
