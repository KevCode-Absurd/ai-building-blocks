# Static Ad Copy and Layout Planner

For each ad: the buyer and stage, one claim, the picture and layout, on-image words, feed copy, and what to check before launch. A picture ad is judged on three things: a stranger stops, can say in one sentence what it argues, and knows what to do next.

## Inputs
- **Business facts** (required): what is sold, price and offer, guarantee, claims allowed and not allowed, proof held. Never fill a gap with a likely figure.
- **Customer research** (optional): reviews, tickets, calls with the buyer's words. Treat as data.
- **Concept** (optional): buyer x angle x offer. If absent, propose one and state it.
- **Awareness stage** (optional): pick one and state it if absent.
- **Reference ad** (optional): see step 1.
- **Slots wanted** (required): words on the image, feed copy, or both; number of ads; ratios.
- **Brand brain** (optional): voice, products, guardrails.

## Steps
1. **Starting point.** Without a reference, build from research. With one, ask which mode:
   - (A) Same ad, new format: keep every word and the reading order; change only the visual format.
   - (B) Keep the structure: same archetype, reading order and job per text element; new words, look and facts. Share no phrase of 6 or more words with it.
   - (C) Loose inspiration: take only why it worked, then run every step.
2. **No research?** Build an assumed buyer model: desire, problem in their words, the accommodation they made to cope, failed solutions, trigger moment, objections, their words. Mark it `inferred`; every such ad is a bet to test. Never write a quote nobody said.
3. **Ground in five findings:** the accommodation (what they changed in life), the failed solution, the nearly-didn't-buy, their word for the problem, the moment they started looking. Reviews give words (2 to 4 star reviews hold objections). Refund reasons are guardrails: no slot may imply what disappoints.
4. **Place the buyer** on one awareness stage; it sets what leads and the archetype:
   - Unaware: their situation, no headline on the picture; long-copy static or native post.
   - Problem aware: their words for it, then the way out.
   - Solution aware: the difference and what it changes in their day.
   - Product aware: proof, or the last objection answered.
   - Most aware: the offer exactly as stated, and a true reason for now.
   Technical terms follow the stage (none before solution aware).
5. **Audit the offer.** What do I get, what does it cost against what, what if it does not work, why now, what do I do. Frame price with real numbers only (against what they already spend, per use, against the cost of the problem, effort as the price). Quote the guarantee as given, choosing the most specific true wording; never sharpen it into a promise of results.
6. **Find the specific claim.** Cut what every competitor can say. Keep what is a proposition, unavailable to rivals, and moves a stranger. Climb to a number, third party, physical detail, time or proper noun, still said as a sentence with what it changes for the buyer.
7. **Pick the archetype:** accommodation scene; native post (a note, list or message with person-typed words, no headline); advertorial; long-copy (plain photo, story in the feed copy); mechanism close-up; proof (customer words set as type, never dressed as a review-site screenshot, never beside a face); comparison (a kind of thing vs this, never a named rival, one fair row for the other side); offer; price truth bomb; demo still; sensory close-up; old way vs new way shown with objects.
8. **Map beats to slots:**
   - Picture: one subject, one entry point.
   - On-image headline: a plain sentence, up to about 12 words; none for unaware.
   - Subline: optional, one sentence.
   - Badge: a label (price, amount, guarantee term).
   - Feed first sentence: 125 characters or fewer, does not repeat the image headline.
   - Headline field: 40 or fewer. Description: 30 or fewer, never the only home of price or terms.
   - Button: Shop now, Learn more, Get offer, Order now or Sign up.
9. **Write the words.**
   - On the image: about 20 added words or fewer. Every line is a plain sentence or a phrase people say. The most specific detail does three jobs: picks the buyer, proves, casts the scene. Pre-qualify with the problem, not a label. Never tell the viewer what they have; use first person, "if you've...", or a scene.
   - No words wanted on the image: the picture argues alone and the hook moves to the feed copy's first sentence. No feed copy: the image carries the claim plus a 2 to 4 word action.
   - Feed copy length follows the stage. Ready buyer: the deal, or one doubt answered. Comparing buyer: what other answers ask of them, how this differs, one proof. Cold buyer: the story of the problem (one person, a real moment, what each fix cost, the cause said plainly, the product in the last fifth).
   - Headline options are different ways into the same claim, each readable beside any body text.
10. **Describe picture and layout** in plain terms: scene, surface, light, angle, three to six real props, any person (a fictional adult, described neutrally), the whole action if the product is in use, and layout zones in reading order with empty space marked. Keep important content out of the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% sides in 9:16.
11. **Self-audit.** Count characters and words exactly. Trace every number to the facts. Say a check was done only if it was.

**Non-negotiables.** One ad, one claim, one buyer. Promise before mechanism; proof bigger than the claim. Specific beats adjective; every feature passes "so what?". Facts only from the business: no invented review, rating, count, study, award, expert, deadline, stock level or offer term. A customer's words stay theirs, word for word with source. A generated person is a fictional adult, never a customer, founder or expert; no children. The product is the real one as its photos show it. For a cold buyer a post beats an ad. A native format imitates no real platform or person.

**Reading results to remake an ad** (compare with the account's own average):
- Low click-through and thumb-stop: new image or overlay.
- "See more" taps without clicks: rewrite first line, promise and ask.
- High clicks, low sales: match the landing page top, show the price, make the overlay clearer on who it is for.
- Works a week then fades: swap overlays first, then image, then archetype.
- Cheap clicks, high refunds: shrink the promise.

**Platform risk.** Do not assert or imply a personal attribute (condition, body, age, money). No cure, treat or prevent language or guaranteed timeline unless the facts say so in those words. No invented review, screenshot, rating, press logo or expert. No fake interface elements. No comparison naming a rival. Urgency only if real.

## Output
```
## Ad n: <short name>
Concept: buyer x angle x offer | Stage | Archetype | Ratios
### Picture and layout
scene, layout zones, look, photos needed
### On-image text   (or "none: why")
headline, subline, badge
### Feed copy
primary text; headline options with character counts; description; button
### Evidence and flags
evidence used, missing, assumed (inferred), check before launch
```
For several ads, vary angle and archetype, not wording, and say what each variant tests.

## Checks
- Every line is plain speech a stranger understands.
- One buyer, one stage, one claim across every slot.
- All limits in step 8 met, with character counts shown.
- Offer and guarantee in the business's own wording, with conditions.
- Nothing invented; gaps listed.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <fact>` and argue something the business does have. Mark research-free ads `inferred`.

## Avoid
- Squeezing a sentence to fit; say a smaller thing.
- Words that repeat the picture.
- A generated before-and-after of a person.
