# For online stores: Stop strangers scrolling with a story ad

**Run both kinds.** Story ads find new people. Direct ads (product, benefit, offer, button) close the people who now know the store. Keep both running.

**Pushes a store can show**
- A thing that failed: a collar stained after three washes, a pan that grabs the egg, a seam that splits.
- A workaround: tape on the charger cable, a towel along the window sill, a second fan.
- Fashion bought only because it looks good has no push. Write a direct ad. Clothing that solves something (sweat, chafing, cold, sore feet) fits.

**Used up or kept changes the shape**
- Used up and reordered (a cream, a snack, a supplement): the past is a string of products started and given up. "Second try" and "the slow habit" fit, because buyers reorder and report the weeks.
- Bought once and kept (a pan, a bag, a lamp): the earlier ones they owned are the failed fixes, each with its price and the way it failed. "Down and back up" fits.
- Gift products suit "someone else's change".

**Narrators in a store.** A customer story needs written permission before it runs as their words. Without it, the brand retells it and quotes the review word for word. The founder suits why the product was made, or what they changed after complaints.

**Photo ideas.** The hallway floor with the muddy boots, the kitchen bench after the fifth failed attempt, the bathroom shelf with too many bottles. If a story truly needs the product, show the real one from its photos, small, unposed and off to one side.

**Proof a store can use.** The review count with its source, a verified buyer's quote word for word, a retailer or press mention only if it's real. No "bought today" counters or rating claims without a named source.

**Where the click goes.** A page that opens on the same moment as the picture, teaches the problem, and reaches the product and offer near the end. If the store has none, write that page first. A product page only suits buyers who already know the problem; then the offer, returns terms and guarantee go in the close once, in the store's own words.
