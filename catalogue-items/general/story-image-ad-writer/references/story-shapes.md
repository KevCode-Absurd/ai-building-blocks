# Story shapes

Every story still runs push, habit, doubt, pull. The shape decides how the habit and doubt parts are told and where the turn to the pull comes. Pick the shape the quotes already show. The examples here are made up and never go into a real ad.

| Shape | The quotes that support it | How habit and doubt are told | Where the turn comes | Take care |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Down and back up** | Several fixes tried before this one | Each fix and what it cost (money, time, hope), down to the low point | After the last failed fix | Name kinds of product, never brands. Each fix costs something different |
| **Second try** | A setback: quit, cancelled, a bad first week, then came back | The hopeful start, then the setback told honestly | At the return | Keep the setback in. Cutting it is what makes these stories sound fake |
| **It was never you** | Buyers blamed themselves ("I just don't stick to things") | What they believed about themselves and the evidence they gathered for it | When the blame moves from the person to the method | The reader is never blamed. The new idea rests on the facts, or is told as what the narrator came to believe |
| **Someone else's change** | A partner, child or parent | The person they watched struggle, and what they tried for them | At the first small sign of change | The other person's result stays inside what customers report. An adult, unless the product is for children and every word is supported |
| **The slow habit** | Sticking with it for weeks | The first doubtful day, then small wins in order | Near the end, when they notice they haven't skipped | Time on the product never longer than it has existed. No overnight change |

## Narrators and shapes
- A real customer can tell any shape, using only what they lived.
- The founder suits "down and back up" (why it was made) and "it was never you" (what they learned).
- The brand retelling suits "second try" and "someone else's change", where one real quote carries the turn.
- "You" suits "down and back up" and "the slow habit", told as the reader's own days.

## Tone
Lift slowly and never end on excitement. Use numbers and exact details instead of adjectives. One customer's words are one customer's words, never "people say".
