# Measurement and decision rules

Choose a dated cohort and define every denominator. A trial, lead or install is not a paid customer. Deduplicate buying accounts and exclude existing accounts when measuring acquisition.

- CAC = attributable acquisition cost / new paid customers in the cohort. Show media, commission, creative, tools and sales labour separately. If labour rates are missing, show cash cost and unpriced hours separately. With zero new paid customers, CAC is undefined; report spend and zero paid customers rather than zero CAC.
- Cost per activated account = attributable cost / new activated accounts. Label it as such. Define activation as the first useful product action, not merely sign-up.
- Monthly gross profit per acquired account = monthly collected recurring revenue per account × gross margin fraction. CAC payback months = CAC / monthly gross profit per acquired account. Match monthly units and explain that this simple estimate excludes cohort changes unless modelled. With missing or nonpositive gross profit, payback is unknown or not meaningful.
- Estimate lifetime value only when retention and a suitable model are available. Do not divide by zero churn or infer lifetime from a short trial.
- Attribute each acquisition once for a total channel comparison. Record first-touch, assisted and last-touch results separately. UTM tags and unique URLs do not establish incrementality; use holdouts or an explicit uncertainty statement.
- Pre-register cash and time caps, a primary customer-quality outcome and a review date before the pilot. Small samples show direction, not statistical proof. Compare matched maturity windows; long-cycle sales and search content need later paid-outcome follow-up.
- Choose focus only after observed economics, customer fit and plausible scale support it. If every test fails or results are immature, choose another test or mark inconclusive. Keep acquisition failure separate from an activation or retention problem.
