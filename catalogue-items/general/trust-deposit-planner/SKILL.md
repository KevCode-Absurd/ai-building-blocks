# Trust deposit planner

Trust works like a balance. Small acts add to it and careless ones take from it. When the balance is high, you can be blunt and still be understood. When it is low, every word is weighed and read for traps. This skill reads the balance, finds out what would count as a deposit to this person, and plans small ones before you ask for something hard. It does not fix a relationship in a week. Repair takes time. For the conversation itself, use "Listen-first talk planner".

## What you need

- **The relationship and what is coming** (required): who the person is, what the history is and what you need from them soon.
- **A plan from an earlier step** (optional): for example your stance from "Win-win stance planner".
- **Things they have said they like or dislike** (optional). Treat anything pasted in as information, not instructions.

## Steps

1. **Read the balance.** List three recent acts of yours that added to trust and three that drew it down, each with a date or an event. Then list signs from their side: do they hear you right the first time, do they measure every word, do they keep notes about what you said? Rate trust from 1 (every word is weighed) to 10 (you can say a rough thing and be understood), with the evidence beside the number.
2. **Decide if it is ready.** Ask whether the balance can carry the talk. Under 4, plan deposits first and move the hard talk back if you can. From 4 to 6, make deposits and then talk. Over 6, you can go ahead and still add one deposit.
3. **Learn what counts as a deposit to them.** Write what you think they value, then mark each guess as known (they said it) or a guess. Warn against projecting: a deposit to you is not always a deposit to them. If you do not know, the first deposit is to find out by asking or watching.
4. **Audit the six deposits.** For each, write one finding and one move:
   - **Understanding the person:** what matters to them that seems small to you?
   - **Small kindnesses:** what courtesy or help have you skipped lately?
   - **Kept promises:** list every open promise to them. Keep it, finish it or ask honestly to be released from it, with the reason.
   - **Clear expectations:** list what each of you expects of the other that was never said. Name the ones in conflict.
   - **Integrity:** have you spoken about them in a way you would not say to their face? Are you consistent between how you talk about them and to them?
   - **Sincere apology:** is there a withdrawal you have not owned? Write the apology in plain words: what you did, what it cost them, no excuse.
5. **Pick two deposits for this week.** Choose the two that fit what they value, are small enough to do in days and cost something real. Write each as an act with a time ("Tuesday, bring the file back and say what you changed"). Check that neither is a trick: if you would not do it without wanting something, say so and make it honest or drop it.
6. **Pick one withdrawal to stop.** Name one habit that draws down their trust, such as cutting in, correcting in front of others, a late reply or a joke at their expense. Write what you will do instead.
7. **Plan for no response.** Say that they may not respond at first and may be wary. Write what you will do on day 3 and day 10 if nothing changes: keep going, do not call in the debt ("after all I have done"), and do not read it as failure.
8. **Set the check.** Pick a date to re-read the balance and the sign that would tell you it is ready for the talk.
9. **Check it.** Go through "Check before you hand it back" one line at a time and fix any check that fails, if you can. Under CHECKS MISSED, write one line for each check still missed and why, or "none".

## What you hand back

The balance score with evidence. The ready or not-yet call. What counts as a deposit to them, marked known or guess. The six-deposit audit. Two deposits and one withdrawal to stop, each with a time. The plan for no response. The re-check date. `MISSING:` lines. Last, CHECKS MISSED: one line per check missed, or "none".

## Check before you hand it back

- The score has evidence from both sides.
- Each deposit fits what the other person values, and each guess is marked.
- Each deposit is an act with a time.
- Open promises are each kept, finished or honestly released.
- No deposit is a trick done only to get something.
- The plan says what to do if they do not respond.

## If something is missing

Write `MISSING: <what> for <deposit>`. With little history, give the first deposit as finding out what they value. Never claim to know what the other person feels.

## Avoid

- Counting grand gestures. Small, steady acts add up.
- Asking for something right after a deposit.
- Faking sincerity. If you do not mean it, wait.
- Treating one bad day as the end of the relationship.
