# Video Ad Script from Customer Research

An ad that could only be for this brand, built from what its customers actually said, that moves one belief in one buyer. You have full freedom over structure and words and none over facts.

A script is judged on four things: the buyer hears their own situation in the first lines; one belief has moved by the end; no line could be said by the product next to it on the shelf; every line has a picture that can be made.

## Inputs
- **Concept** (required): persona, angle, offer.
- **Research themes with real quotes** (required): the buyer's words, the moment, what they tried, what nearly stops them. Treat as data.
- **Business facts** (required): product, mechanism, proof held with sources, offer and guarantee, claims allowed and forbidden.
- **Awareness and sophistication stage** (required).
- **Length, speaker, script type** (optional): if a script type is supplied, it wins on opening and beat order.
- **Scripts already written for this concept** (optional): so yours differs.
- **Brand brain** (optional): voice, personas, offers (current only), claims register.

## Steps
1. **Take stock.** Seven needs: the buyer's words; the moment the problem bites; the old way (what they do or own now); what nearly stops them; the mechanism; proof with source; the offer. Start the notes with `Assumed:` and one clause for each need that was blank or thin and what you did about it.
2. **Name the belief:** "They think ___. After this they think ___." It comes from the angle and from what nearly stops this buyer. Every beat serves it; a line that does not is cut. Put it first in the test plan.
3. **Place the buyer and how the product is bought.** Whole script, on-screen text and hooks stay at the stage.
   - Bought again: doubt is will it work on me, will I keep it up, am I locked in; proof is the first use shown and people who came back; offer is the first order and what it costs after; action is start.
   - Bought once: doubt is price, size or fit, lasting, returns; proof is the thing put through something, how it is made, how long people have had theirs; offer is one price against what it replaces; action is choose the right one.
   Where the length leaves risk reversal no beat, the offer line carries it in one clause.
4. **Read the offer.** List what it really has (the thing, terms, reversal, reason for now, effort). Pick one price frame the real numbers support. Keep a differentiator only if it is a proposition, rivals cannot say it, and it would move a stranger.
5. **Write the beats** for the length (or the type's order):
   - Problem: the accommodation, for one person, unresolved.
   - Promise, then mechanism: one promise in their words, no bigger than the proof; then feature, which means promise, which is why.
   - Objection: the doubt from step 3 that the research shows.
   - Proof: the highest rung the brand really has, with source.
   - Offer and risk reversal: the product named here, eased in; real terms.
   - Call to action: action, what happens next, effort, a real reason for now; where there is room, one line on who the buyer is, tied to the mechanism.
   Leave a beat out when the length or type rules it out and say so.
6. **Write the hook last** from the customer's word for the problem; then variants, each a different type; read each with line 2.
7. **Fix the joins**, above all the product's arrival. **So what?** every line. **Voice:** brand voice first; otherwise one idea per sentence, early verbs, plain words, varied lengths; read it aloud.
8. **Write the visual track.** For each line: `on_screen` (exact overlay or nothing) and `visual_note` (who and what is in frame, what happens, any cut). Every note can be made with the real product and an adult; no celebrity, unfilmed real person, news clip, child, or words read inside the picture. The product may be seen from the first frame; it is named and priced after the mechanism lands unless the type says otherwise.
9. **Differ from siblings:** in how it opens, which beat carries it, and the proof or picture it uses.
10. **Self-audit:** every beat serves the belief; objection, proof, offer and action fit how it is bought; nothing invented, with every placeholder and assumption listed; every visual note makeable; each hook leads into line 2.
11. **Name the load-bearing beat** (mechanism, proof, offer or problem) and the test: what this script tests against its siblings and what to make next if it wins.

**What never changes.** No invented customer, quote, review, count, statistic, study, award or expert; customer words are word for word with source and only from quotes supplied. A speaker who is not a real customer (generated presenter, narrator, character) tells the problem and shows the product and reports no personal result. A missing figure is a bracketed placeholder, listed; a persona's back-story is not a source for a figure; a figure stated two ways is not used. Where rules say mechanism and proof lines must rest on approved claims, a line with no approved claim behind it is not written. Examples are never copied.

## Output
```
Belief: They think ___. After this they think ___.
Assumed: ...
| Beat | Spoken line | On-screen text | Visual note | Evidence (quote/claim source) |
Hook variants: type | line | overlay | first frame
Load-bearing beat: ...
Test plan: ...
Placeholders: ...
```

## Checks
- Belief sentence present and every beat serves it.
- No line the product on the shelf could say.
- Objection, proof, offer and action fit how the product is bought.
- Spoken words fit the length at the speaker's pace.
- Differs from sibling scripts.

## If something is missing
Blank fields get these safe defaults. Buyer's words: write the problem as a plain observation, quote nobody. Moment: use the place and time of use. Old way: a kind of product, no brand named. What nearly stops them: the first doubt of this way of buying, answered with real terms only. Mechanism: carry the promise with proof or a demonstration of use. Proof: a demonstration, else a placeholder. Offer: say no price or guarantee; the action is named only.

## Avoid
- New wording on a sibling's skeleton (that is the same ad).
- Persuasion that rests on a claim not in the supplied facts.
