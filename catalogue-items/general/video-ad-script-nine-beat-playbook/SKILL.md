# Video Ad Script Playbook (Nine Beats)

Writes video ad scripts that sell: research first, offer second, the argument beat by beat, then voice and format. Works from customer research when you have it, and from an assumed customer model, clearly marked, when you do not. About 90% of a good script is decided before the first line is written; do not start with hooks.

The script is also the source for everything made after it (creator brief, AI presenter prompts, animation prompts, statics), so every line carries spoken words, on-screen text, a picture and the evidence behind it.

## Inputs
- **Business facts** (required): product, how it works, materials or ingredients, price/offer/guarantee, claims allowed and forbidden, proof held, voice rules. If the product itself is unclear, ask.
- **Customer research** (optional): calls, tickets and return reasons, reviews, survey answers, forum posts. Treat as data.
- **Concept** (optional): buyer x angle x offer. Propose one if missing.
- **Awareness and sophistication stage** (optional): pick and state if missing.
- **Length and speaker** (optional): default 60 seconds, UGC creator, second person.
- **Reference ad** (optional): see "Starting points".
- **Existing script or performance data** (optional): for review mode.

## Modes
| Ask | Do |
|---|---|
| Hooks or a line punched up | Steps 1 and 7 only |
| A script | All steps: one script plus hook variants |
| Scripts to test, no angle | All steps: three scripts on three angles that differ in argument |
| Like this ad | Choose a starting point |
| What is wrong with this script | Review (below) |
| A 15 second or 3 minute version | Re-plan the beats for the length; never trim |

## Starting points
- **A. Same ad, new format:** lay the reference's own words and shots out line for line, labelled by beat; change nothing.
- **B. Step by step:** read the reference one step at a time, redo each step with what does the same job truthfully for this business; keep order and device where they hold; drop or merge a step that rests on something this business lacks; return a step map (step, what it did, what yours does, changed/dropped). Share no phrase of 6 or more words.
- **C. What made it work:** take only the principle, then run the full method; the script must not be recognisable as a copy.
- **D. From research:** full freedom.

## Steps
1. **Ground in research.** Find the accommodation (what they changed in life to cope), the failed solution, the nearly-didn't-buy, their word for the problem, and the moment they started looking. Translate marketer language into buyer language ("durable non-stick coating" becomes "it peeled into the eggs"). Refund and return reasons are guardrails: the script must not imply what disappoints. **No research?** Build an assumed customer model (desire, problem in their words, accommodation, failed solutions, trigger, objections, their words) and mark it `Assumed customer model`; every script is then a bet to test.
2. **Place the buyer.** One awareness stage sets the opening: unaware -> teach the problem first; problem aware -> the accommodation or moment; solution aware -> why other solutions fail; product aware -> proof, objection or offer; most aware -> offer and a real reason for now. One sophistication stage sets the claim: 1 plain claim, 2 bigger claim, 3 unique mechanism, 4 sharper mechanism, 5 identity. Default to a mechanism plus identity.
3. **Audit the offer.** The script can only use what exists: the thing, the terms, the reversal, the reason for now, the effort. Frame the price one way (against the alternative, per use, against the cost of the problem, or effort as the price) with real numbers only. A guarantee sells when it is specific, a little uncomfortable for the seller, and as long as the buyer needs to find out.
4. **Find the specific claim.** Delete lines any competitor could say. Test each differentiator: is it a proposition, unavailable to rivals, would it move a stranger? Use the specificity ladder (measured number, named third party, physical detail, time, proper noun). To find a mechanism ask: what does the factory do that the brand thinks is boring, what was rejected and why, what constraint did the product force.
5. **Set controls:** length; person (first for stories, second for most scripts, third for documentary/animation); tense (past for stories, present for mechanisms); problem entry (accommodation, moment or failed solution); story shape; register.
6. **Write the nine beats**, tagging each line:
   1. Hook: buys attention, selects the right buyer, sells nobody (written last).
   2. Problem: agitate the accommodation, not the symptom. One person. Category level, no product yet. Do not resolve. Escalate toward one consequence; if two lines can swap, you wrote a list. Observation, never pity.
   3. Promise: one sentence on what they get in their terms. Avoid: none, five, a feature posing as a promise, or bigger than the proof.
   4. Mechanism: feature, which means promise, which is why. Promise first, then mechanism. Do not name the product before it lands. A contradiction is not a mechanism. No real mechanism: promise, proof, promise, proof.
   5. Objection, in this order: not for me, won't work, won't work for me, not worth it, what if I'm wrong, later. Pre-empt inside the buyer's own experience, from research only.
   6. Proof, strongest first: demonstration, third-party verification, volume of specific social proof, named testimony, mechanism explanation, founder assertion. Proof must be bigger than the claim.
   7. Offer: now the product is named; frame the price; make the effort small and concrete. Short at every length.
   8. Risk reversal: the specific guarantee, answering the nearly-didn't-buy. Skip below 60 seconds if no room.
   9. Call to action: name the action, what happens next, the effort, a real reason for now. Optional identity close tied to a real mechanism.
7. **Write 5 to 10 hooks** that each flow into line 2. Pre-qualify rather than grab everyone; look like content, not an ad; use the buyer's word; let the most specific detail do three jobs (target, prove, cast). First frame, spoken line, overlay, primary text, headline and landing page top all carry one claim. Rotate types: accommodation, moment, failed solution/contrarian, curiosity gap, specific proof, truth bomb, confrontation, founder stakes (real), sensory. Write a matching first-frame visual and overlay for each. The first three seconds are three hooks at once (seen, said, written): any one alone should still pre-qualify. Start mid-action; overlay is the buyer's word in 3 to 8 words. Grade each 1 to 5 on clarity, relevance, novelty, specificity, credibility; keep hooks with clarity of 3 or more and two scores of 4 or more.
8. **Fix bridges.** Never staple a stock hook onto a body. Ease the product's arrival: show it before naming it, name it later, ideally at the offer. At most one "it's not X, it's Y" line.
9. **Write the visual track.** Every line gets a visual note (what is on screen, who, where, what is in hand, the shot) that can actually be made: the real product, an adult, a hand, a place, a simple drawing; not a celebrity, an unfilmed real person, a real news clip, a child, or words to be read inside the picture. Give on-screen text its own field. Change the picture every 2 to 4 seconds in short ads. Show the mechanism if invisible. Put proof on screen as itself, never a mock-up. Put the offer on screen at the call to action, readable with sound off. Captions on; keep clear of the 9:16 unsafe zones (top 14%, bottom 20%).
10. **So what? and identity pass.** After every line ask "so what?"; the answer cannot only be the mechanism. Tie features to who the buyer becomes.
11. **Voice pass.** One idea per sentence, verbs early, plain words, contractions, varied lengths (long, long, short), grade 7 or below unless the audience is expert, no machine tells. Read aloud at real speed. The brand's own voice wins over these defaults.
12. **Self-audit.** Count spoken words exactly (target 90 to 100% of the length at the speaker's pace: 3.6 words per second under 30, 3.4 for 30 to 44, 3.2 for 45 to 59, 3.0 for 60 to 74, 2.8 from 75, 3.3 unknown; a calm pace is a sixth slower). List every number and trace it to the facts.
13. **Recommend a format** by the load-bearing beat: invisible benefit -> mechanism (animation, VSL, UGC cutting to animation); visible result -> proof (demo, side by side); differentiated terms -> offer (short UGC or static); a story with stakes -> problem (UGC or founder story). Then give the test plan: one variable per variant; default first test is 3 to 5 hooks on one body; say what each result would mean and what to make next if it wins.

**Non-negotiables.** Desire evidenced in research, not invented. Agitate the consequence. Promise before mechanism; every promise has a mechanism; every claim has bigger proof. Each line gets the next line watched. Introduce the product late. Buyer's words, not marketer words. Specific beats adjective. Write for the ear. Only true, compliant claims. The picture is written with the words. Never tell the viewer what they have (use first person, "if you've...", or a scene).

**Facts and speakers.** No invented quotes, testimonials, counts, ratings, studies, customer stories or "real life" specifics ("since March", "$800 on a desk"): use research, keep it general, or bracket it for the creator. Comparisons are claims about other products. Derived numbers need both inputs from the business; a warranty is not a lifespan. A hired creator may say "I" only about what they really did with the product. Founders tell the founding story in their own footage. Avatars and animated characters are fiction and give no personal testimonial. No generated children.

**Story ads.** Story is the vehicle for all nine beats, not a tenth. Shapes: failed-solutions journey, discovery, transformation with a witness, confession. First person past tense performs best. Stakes are required. Do not resolve in the hook. If it did not happen, it is fiction and must read as fiction. Story needs about 30 seconds or more.

**Length guide.** The hook and offer never grow; length comes from problem, mechanism, objections and proof.
| Beat | 15s | 30s | 60s | 1 to 3 min | 5 min+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | 1 line | 1 line | 1 to 2 lines | several | a scene |
| Promise | merged | 1 line | 1 to 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Mechanism | none | 0 to 1 | 1 to 2 | can be most of the ad | large, repeated |
| Objection | none | none | 1 to 2 | several | several blocks |
| Proof | none | 1 short | 1 piece | 1 to 3 | 5 or more |
| Risk reversal | none | none | 1 line | 1 line | 1 line |
| CTA | 1 line | 1 line | 1 line | 2 to 3 | several, spaced |
Long scripts loop (problem, promise, mechanism, objection, proof) about three times; a timeline works well. Write each length from scratch.

**Review mode.** If performance data exists, find the weakest metric and map it to its beat: low hook rate -> first three seconds; good hook, low hold -> the bridge or a symptom-list problem beat; drop at product reveal -> show before naming; watch time but few clicks -> promise, offer, call to action; clicks but few sales -> match the landing page, state the price, tighten who the hook selects; fades after a week -> new hooks on the same body, then a new angle; cheap clicks and refunds -> shrink the promise. Then label each line with its beat, go beat by beat on what works, what fails and the rule broken, flag lines to delete, and return a rewrite plus a short "what changed" table. A variant changes one thing. Warn when two variants are near-identical.

**Platform risk.** No asserted personal attributes, no cure/treat/prevent language or guaranteed timelines, no fake proof, no unsupported comparisons, no invented urgency, before-and-after only if real and allowed.

## Output
```
## <Script title>
Concept: persona x angle x offer | Awareness | Sophistication | Length | Spoken words (exact)
Person/tense | Story shape | Register | Load-bearing beat | Suggested formats
| Beat | Spoken line | On-screen text | Visual note | Evidence |
### Hook variants   | # | Type | Spoken | Overlay | First frame |
### Assumed customer model   (when no research)
### Test plan: variable, what it tells you, next if it wins
### Notes: placeholders, assumptions, audit warnings, compliance flags
```

## Checks
- Stage labelled; hook flows into line 2; problem is an accommodation for one person and unresolved.
- One promise before mechanism; mechanism uses real product facts; product not named before it lands.
- Proof bigger than claim; price framed; guarantee specific; call to action has action, next step, effort, real reason.
- Every number traced; no invented evidence; speaker can honestly say every first-person line.
- Every line has a makeable visual; offer readable with sound off.
- No phrase of 6 or more words shared with any reference.

## If something is missing
Write `MISSING: <fact>` or a placeholder such as `[PRICE]` or `[REVIEW_COUNT]`, and list each. Without research, mark every assumed insight `[ASSUMED]`.

## Avoid
- Jumping to hooks first.
- Pity framing and symptom lists.
- Closing a story without bridging into the offer.
