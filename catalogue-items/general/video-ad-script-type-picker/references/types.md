# Script type recipes

Fourteen types in four groups, sorted by what the viewer is watching: someone **talking** to them, something being **shown**, a **story** being lived, or a **lesson** being taught. Every recipe uses the nine beats (opening, problem, promise, how it works, doubt, proof, offer, guarantee, call to action) and says where it changes their order. Examples are made up.

Each recipe gives: speaker and tense | beat order | the selling beat | when the product appears | needs | watch for.

## Talk: a person to camera

**Straight case.** "You", present. All nine beats in order; the problem is one or two lines and the promise lands by line three. Selling beat: whichever beat has the strongest material; give it the most words. Product seen early, named at the offer. Needs: a Before point. Watch for a checklist feel: each line should hand over to the next.

**Doubt first.** "You", present. Opens on the loudest doubt, said aloud and granted as fair; then how it works, the promise that is now safe to make, proof aimed at that doubt, offer, a guarantee that matches it, call to action. One doubt, answered fully. Product early. Needs: a Last doubt from research. Watch: concede without telling viewers what they have. Changes the order on purpose.

**Flaw first.** "You" or narration, present. Opens on a real shortcoming and who shouldn't buy, then why it's that way (usually the cost of something good), what that buys, outside proof, offer, a guarantee for the unsure, call to action. Selling beat: doubt. Product at once. Needs: a real shortcoming on file. Watch: never invent a flaw for effect. An AI presenter gives no verdict of their own. Changes the order on purpose.

**Offer up front.** "You", present. Opens on the terms, then why they're these terms, the price in one frame, the guarantee, one line of proof, a true reason for now, call to action. Problem and how it works get a line at most. Selling beat: offer. Product in line one. Needs: terms worth leading with. Watch: every number real; weak on buyers who don't know you yet.

## Show: something happening on screen

**Show it working.** "You" or narration, present. Opens mid-demonstration; then what you just saw and why it matters, why it happens, who it's for, the doubt answered, a second proof from outside, offer, guarantee, call to action. At 15 seconds: the demo, what you saw, offer, call to action. Selling beat: proof. Product on screen from the first frame. Needs: a result you can film in one continuous take. Watch: show the product doing something; never an effect on a body.

**Day in use.** "You" or narration, present. Opens on a moment of an ordinary day; then what that moment used to take, the product used where it fits, why it's easy, a later moment where nobody thinks about it, offer, call to action. Selling beat: proof that it fits a life. Product early, named at the offer. Needs: how and when customers use it. Watch: a day nobody has. A real person's routine becomes a brief they film themselves.

**Line-up.** "You" or narration, present. Opens on the options and what they'll be judged on; then why those tests, each option with one fair note, where this one comes out, what it doesn't win on, offer, guarantee, call to action. Selling beat: proof. Product as one of the line-up. Needs: a real test, or facts a viewer can check alone. Watch: rivals are unbranded stand-ins for kinds of product, given their due. An AI presenter never says "I tested".

**Making of.** "We", past for the decision, present for the result. Opens on one decision in how it's made; then what it cost the maker, what it does for the buyer, the easy way rivals take in one line, outside proof, offer, guarantee, call to action. Selling beat: how it works. Product present throughout, named at the offer. Needs: a real process fact (Hopkins's advice: tell the steps rivals take for granted). Watch: never invent a factory story; the maker's word needs outside proof beside it.

## Story: someone lives it

**Mirror.** "You" present, or narration. Opens on a scene from the viewer's life with no problem or product named; more of the pattern until they recognise themselves; then the reason behind it, the promise, what people usually try, proof, offer, guarantee, call to action. Selling beat: problem, about half the words. Product not before the first third. Needs: a habit customers describe from before they knew the cause. For buyers unaware of the problem. Watch: never tell viewers they have a condition; ask little at the end, and use no deadline.

**Turning point.** "I", past. Opens on the Trigger day; then what life was like before, what they found out, what they had tried, proof from someone other than the speaker, the purchase and the reason for it, a closing line on who they are now. Selling beat: problem, with stakes from the first line; more than half the words come before how it works. Product late. Needs: a real Trigger with stakes. Watch: only a real customer can tell it as their own; an AI presenter may act out the problem but never reports a result of its own. Build a clear join into the offer.

**Tries in a row.** "I" or "they", past. Opens on the first try and what it cost; then the second, the low point, what the tries had in common, the promise, how it works as the thing all of them missed, proof, offer, guarantee, call to action. Selling beat: how it works, as the answer to every try. Product as what fixes what the others missed. Needs: two Tries from research. Watch: name kinds of product, never brands; say what each try got right.

**Someone noticed.** "I" past, or "they" about a described customer. Opens on another person noticing the change; then back to how things were, what changed and why, what they had tried, wider proof, offer, guarantee, call to action. Selling beat: proof. Product mid-way. Needs: a real customer's account. With none, write filming notes a real customer can follow, with blanks for their details. Watch: an AI presenter can't claim this.

## Teach: the viewer learns something

**The real cause.** "I" or "they"; the finding in past tense, how it works in present. Opens on what most people don't know; then the problem as it looked before, the cause in two to four plain sentences with one picture, the promise it makes possible, why earlier tries couldn't work, proof, offer, guarantee, call to action. Selling beat: how it works. Product after the cause. Needs: one true cause. Watch: a list of features dressed as a finding; a brand's own finding isn't independent science.

**Time-lapse.** "You", present or future; or narration. Opens on the first point in time; then for each point: what's happening, what they'd notice, the doubt at that point; after the last point: proof, an offer sized to the timeline, a guarantee on the same timeline, call to action. Selling beat: how it works, told in rounds; the natural way to make a long ad. Product at the first point. Needs: time points and effects the business can back. Reordered goods use the first weeks and a pack that covers them; kept goods use the life of the thing (first use, a season, a hundred washes). Watch: honest early effects, including bad ones, belong at their point.
