## What came out

```text
## 1. Show plan

- **Fit:** A camera can film a shelf of vegetables. It can't show that shelf adding up into one small pack, so watercolour suits this idea.
- **Selling idea:** A whole shelf of greens goes into one small Sprout pack.
- **Show job:** Build. Many parts add up.
- **Technique:** Glazing. The see-through green of each ingredient is laid over dry paint. Every overlap reads darker, and the darkest overlap settles into the deep forest green of the pouch.
- **Paper:** Hot-press, cream-toned. It suits crisp label shapes. Bare paper is the brightest light.
- **Texture:** None. No dry brush, salt or granulation, because dusty marks would echo the "gritty/chalky" feel the buyer quit.
- **Colours:** Sunflower yellow #FFCC2E, sap/yellow-green, forest green #00582D and a phthalo blue-green, all close on the wheel. Cream #FFF7DF comes from the paper. No reds or purples anywhere.
- **Label method:** Mask the "Sprout" wordmark and the bear face before any green goes down. Tint them sunflower yellow after the mask is rubbed off, because yellow can't be painted over deep green.
- **What changes:** The shelf greens glaze layer on layer into one deep green, which becomes the pouch.
- **Implied sentence:** "These greens are all in one Sprout pack."
- **Source:** The ingredient list (kale, spinach, spirulina, chlorella, wheatgrass, broccoli) and the approved line "60+ ingredients… in 1 pack."

## 4. Cast note

The product alone carries this ad. It is about what's inside the pack, and the buyer already knows greens are good for them. A painted figure eating the bears would invite viewers to read a result or a texture verdict that isn't approved. The daily pack is shown already torn, so hands aren't needed.
```
