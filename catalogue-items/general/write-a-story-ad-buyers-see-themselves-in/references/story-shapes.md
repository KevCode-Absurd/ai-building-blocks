# Story shapes

Pick the shape the buyer evidence already shows. Don't pick a shape and then look for quotes to fit it. The examples here are made up and never go into a real piece.

| Shape | The evidence that supports it | Its moves | Where the turn comes | Take care |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Down and back up** | Reviews that name several fixes tried before this one | Life with the problem; each fix and what it cost; the low point; the turn; the first use; an ordinary day since | After the last failed fix | Name kinds of product, never brands. Each fix costs something different (money, time, hope) |
| **Second try** | Reviews with a setback: quit, cancelled, a bad first week, then came back | The hopeful start; the setback, told honestly; what brought them back; what's different now | At the return | The setback stays in. Cutting it is what makes these stories sound fake |
| **It was never you** | Reviews where buyers blamed themselves ("I just don't stick to things") | What they believed about themselves; the evidence they gathered for it; the moment they saw the method was the problem; what changed | When the blame moves from the person to the method | The reader is never blamed. The new idea must rest on the facts, or be told as what the narrator came to believe |
| **Someone else's change** | Reviews about a partner, child or parent | The person they watched struggle; what they tried for them; the small sign something changed | At the first sign | The other person's result stays inside what customers report. An adult, unless the product is for children and every word is supported |
| **The slow habit** | Reviews about sticking with it for weeks | The first doubtful day; the small wins, in order; the day they noticed they hadn't skipped | Near the end, when they notice | Time on the product never longer than it has existed. No overnight change |

## Narrators and shapes

- A real customer can tell any shape, using only what they lived.
- The founder suits "down and back up" (why it was made) and "it was never you" (what they learned).
- The brand retelling suits "second try" and "someone else's change", where one real quote carries the turn.
- "You" suits "down and back up" and "the slow habit", told as the reader's own days.

## Tone

Lift slowly and never end on excitement. Use numbers and exact details instead of adjectives. One customer's words are one customer's words, never "people say".
