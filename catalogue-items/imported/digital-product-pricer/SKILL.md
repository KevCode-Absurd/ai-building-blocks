# Digital product pricing

Digital products have no cost signal from materials or shipping, so the price digits carry more of the quality judgment than for a physical good. This skill covers only what changes for digital products. The general pricing, promotion and discount skills it was built beside are not in this library, so every rule you need is written here. Memberships and recurring plans are not covered yet.

## When to use

- Choosing the digits of a one-off digital price (rounded vs just-below)
- Pricing a freemium→paid upgrade or wording a lead magnet
- Designing a pre-order or launch offer, or deciding whether to let buyers pick a price

## When not to use

- Recurring plans and memberships: not covered here. Use a simple tier table of your own.
- Working out *how much* people will pay: ask 5 to 10 likely buyers first. This skill sets digits and wording, not demand.
- Physical products: shipping and materials change the price signals.

## Inputs

Collect, or state your assumption: product and candidate price; individual vs known publisher; whether the buyer has already used a sample or free tier; launch date; results-driven vs pleasure-driven.

> **Classify the product first.** *Hedonic* = bought for enjoyment, pleasure, or the experience (chocolate, fashion, decor, travel, spa, perfume). *Utilitarian* = bought to do a job, judged on function and value (detergent, appliances, insect repellent, tools, accounting software). If it's mixed, decide by what the buyer is optimizing for at the moment of purchase.

## Procedure

Only the rules that change for digital products. These are rules of thumb from published pricing research (named in reference.md), not guarantees. Test any price change on your own audience. Never state an effect size or a percentage gain that you cannot find in reference.md.

1. **Unknown or individual seller → rounded.** No materials or shipping to signal quality, so a rounded price ($100, not $97) is one of the few quality cues available. Just-below reads as price-led.
2. **Buyer already knows the quality → just-below.** After a free sample or a freemium upgrade prompt, quality is already experienced and a low-price ending is fine — and the left digit must actually drop.
3. **Distant pre-orders: gift, not discount.** Launch 3+ weeks out → bonus module or extra pack at no cost, not a percentage off. Within about a week, either works. Results-driven courses tolerate only short pre-launch windows.
4. **Lead magnets: "for $0", never "FREE".**
5. **Pick-your-price, never pay-what-you-want.** 3–4 fixed options; most pick the lowest, so set it deliberately. Pay-what-you-want is for donations.

Shared, unchanged: sale-price just-below, same-left-digit upgrade tiers, deadline not a seat cap, discount ≤ 40% (quality is hard to judge before buying a file).

## Worked example

The prices below are only an example of how the rules change digits. Use the user's own planned price.

Individual creator, LinkedIn-growth course, plan: $97, "FREE download" checklist, pre-orders 6 weeks out at 20% off, pay-what-you-want ebook.

- $97 → **$100** (unknown seller, quality is the decision driver). Keep a just-below figure for launch-week only.
- "FREE download" → **"Download it for $0"**.
- 20% off, 6 weeks out → **bonus module at no cost**; keep the window short for a utilitarian course.
- Pay-what-you-want ebook → **three fixed options**.
- Never "first 50 spots"; use a dated deadline.

## Common mistakes

- $97 "because creators do it" — for an unknown seller it signals price-led; round it.
- Keeping a pre-order discount for a launch weeks away — a gift wins.
- Calling a lead magnet "FREE" — write "for $0".
- Pay-what-you-want with a floor — still pay-what-you-want; use fixed options.

## Quick reference

| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| Individual / unknown seller | Rounded |
| Freemium / sample already used | Just-below |
| Pre-order, 3+ weeks out | Gift, not discount |
| Lead magnet | "For $0" |
| Let buyers choose | 3–4 fixed options |
| Discount / urgency | ≤ 40%; dated deadline |

Evidence and effect sizes: see [reference.md](reference.md). After you set the price, run it for a fixed period, record sales at each price, and keep the one that sold more.
