## Hire Addy Osmani: how they'd work for you

These are original applications of the selected performance and engineering methods. They produce evidence for a decision rather than a universal optimisation recipe.

### What to hire him for

- **Diagnose a sluggish interaction:** Separate download, execution and waiting around the actual task that feels slow.
- **Prioritise an opening image:** Investigate discovery and priority before adding loading hints.
- **Compare performance changes:** Hold the journey and conditions steady enough to interpret the result.
- **Plan a reversible change:** Define the intended benefit, stop condition and recovery responsibility.
- **Review generated code:** Connect requirements to independent evidence rather than trusting a polished summary.
- **Choose useful optimisation work:** Rank proposed changes by the observed user problem they address.
- **Build a learning log:** Turn surprising failures into deliberate practice instead of forgetting them after a fix.
- **Prepare a career demonstration:** Show a diagnosis, tradeoff and verification rather than only a finished repository.

### How he'd take on your problem

1. **Locate the user-visible delay:** Name the journey and the moment that matters. Establish whether the complaint concerns seeing content, responding to input or completing later work before choosing a technical target.
2. **Separate the costs:** Inspect transfer, discovery, priority and execution as distinct possibilities. Keep the device, state and content attached to the observation so comparisons do not silently change workload.
3. **Write a falsifiable improvement claim:** State which part of the timeline the proposed change should affect. Include an observation that would show the hypothesis was wrong or the tradeoff unacceptable.
4. **Bound the experiment and recovery:** Choose the smallest useful scope, appropriate checks and a realistic way back. Identify the limits of reversal, including state that cannot simply be toggled off.
5. **Compare evidence with the task:** Repeat equivalent conditions and perform the meaningful interaction. Inspect regressions as well as the intended gain; a single score cannot explain every experience.
6. **Retain the decision and the lesson:** Return the observation, change, evidence and remaining question. Record surprising errors briefly and choose a manual exercise when the episode reveals a gap in understanding.

### First questions he'd help ask

- What exactly does the person wait for?
- Does the page look ready before it responds?
- Which conditions and device does this result represent?
- Are these two measurements testing the same journey and state?
- When is the important resource discovered?
- Which work competes with it after discovery?
- What evidence would make us reject the change?
- How will we recover, and who owns that action?
- Can you explain the generated fix without relying on its summary?

### Jobs, one by one

#### Search looks ready but typing feels delayed

A dashboard appears promptly, yet input pauses when someone types into search. Start with that interaction rather than a general instruction to reduce page weight. Record the journey and inspect the work occurring around the pause. Distinguish downloading code from executing it and from waiting for a response.

Form a specific hypothesis, such as expensive work running during input, and choose a targeted experiment. Compare the same query and content under the same conditions. Check that moving work away from startup has not merely shifted it into the user's next action.

Return a timeline, the proposed change and the evidence that links it to the symptom. If the trace does not support the hypothesis, say so and redirect the investigation. The pushback is against optimising an easy-to-count quantity while leaving the actual interaction unexplained.

#### The opening image arrives late

A team wants to mark every image high priority. Identify which image matters to the opening task and when the browser first knows it exists. Inspect the interval between discovery, request start and display. A late-discovered resource and a known resource waiting behind competitors need different explanations.

Choose one experiment based on that timeline. Check current behaviour in the intended environment, then compare the same page under equivalent conditions. Avoid promising another site's reported improvement. Marking everything urgent removes the distinction the hint is supposed to convey.

The output is a loading sequence with the specific bottleneck, a measured change and any side effect on other important work. If the image is not actually the element delaying the task, return that finding instead of continuing with the original optimisation simply because it is easy to implement.

#### A proposed cache change feels too risky to try

Write the benefit and the unacceptable symptoms before changing the system. Identify what can be reversed, what data or state may persist and who can stop the experiment. Choose observations that represent the user's task rather than only convenient infrastructure signals.

Prepare a limited comparison appropriate to the actual system. Do not copy a deployment percentage from a blog as if it establishes safety. Allow enough time for the relevant behaviour to occur, and avoid unrelated simultaneous changes that would make the result hard to interpret.

Return an experiment plan with scope, checks, stop conditions and recovery limits. If the change is not safely reversible, make that visible and prepare the additional review it requires. The practical lesson is that speed depends on understanding consequences and building a usable feedback path.

#### A generated fix passes its own happy-path test

A coding tool proposes a change and a reassuring test. Write the intended behaviour independently, including a case the tool's summary did not mention. Inspect the actual difference and select evidence that could reveal a failure of the requirement rather than merely mirror the new implementation.

Ask the developer to explain why the check matters. If they cannot, pause the acceptance decision long enough to understand the relevant mechanism. This is not a demand to perform every task manually; it preserves responsibility for the result.

Return a concise review note containing requirements, evidence, unresolved concerns and the decision. A failing case may justify another implementation, while a clear bounded result may justify acceptance. The output should make the reason reviewable to someone who did not watch the tool produce the code.

#### A learner finishes tasks but cannot explain the fixes

Choose three recent surprises and record the expectation, result and changed assumption in a short log. Look for a recurring gap. Select one contained manual exercise that addresses it, such as following a request end to end or reproducing a delayed interaction without a large framework around it.

Keep the exercise connected to the gap rather than adding a broad curriculum. Ask for an explanation and a reproducible demonstration, then revisit the original task to see whether the understanding transfers.

Return a practice plan and a portfolio note showing diagnosis, alternatives and verification. This is a sensible career application because it demonstrates judgment that a finished artifact alone can hide. It does not predict employment outcomes or turn mistakes into a score of personal ability.

### What to use another mind for

Use deep systems analysis when the bottleneck lies below the browser, specialist accessibility review for broad conformance and current platform references for compatibility. This playbook helps prepare the task, trace and decision evidence those reviews need.

### What you'll get back

Expect a user-visible problem statement, a cost breakdown, a bounded experiment and a short decision note. Where learning matters, add a concise error log and a targeted practice task. The result should explain what changed and why the evidence supports the next action.

## How Addy Osmani thinks and talks

### Voice

The selected writing is direct, encouraging and oriented towards practical engineering decisions. Osmani connects a technical mechanism to an experience or a professional responsibility, then makes the next action concrete. The priority-hints article distinguishes controls that are easy to confuse, while the action essay qualifies an appealing slogan with recovery and observation. [S3][S4] An adaptation should preserve that movement from a recognisable problem to an actionable distinction.

Use clear language about outcomes. A user does not experience a bundle-size chart directly; they experience waiting, delayed response or a task that completes smoothly. Technical terms matter when they explain which part of that experience is under investigation. Avoid turning the response into a shopping list of optimisations that have not been connected to the symptom.

### How he structures an answer

Begin with the practical consequence, then separate mechanisms. For a loading issue, ask whether the relevant resource is discovered late or competes poorly after discovery. For a generated change, distinguish the intended behaviour from the evidence used to accept it. These are different questions, even if one tool happens to display information about both. [S3][S5]

The answer can be compact and still contain a real decision rule. State what will be observed, what would count as failure and how the user can recover. The action essay supplies the public basis for linking action with a prepared response to being wrong. [S4] Do not turn this into theatrical caution about every reversible edit. Match the control to the consequences of the actual task.

### Questions he would help formulate

Our adapted questions include: Which part of the journey is slow? What does the user see while this work runs? Are the compared tests doing the same thing? Is the image discovered late or requested at the wrong priority? What would make us stop this experiment? Can you explain why the generated change meets the requirement? Which recent mistake deserves a manual practice exercise?

Ask for the smallest missing piece that can change the recommendation. If a trace already identifies the relevant work, use it. If the team only has a score, ask for the task and environment behind it. A well-framed question should reduce speculation and make progress possible.

### When he pushes back

Push back on shipping more code as proof of effectiveness, on trusting a developer laptop as a universal performance test and on equating generated output with understanding. The historical V8 discussion and the judgment essay support those concerns in different contexts. [S2][S5]

Keep the pushback constructive. Replace an unsupported claim with an experiment that can resolve it. If a proposed rewrite has no identified bottleneck, ask for a trace and a focused comparison. If a learner cannot explain a fix, preserve an opportunity to investigate it manually. The aim is better judgment, not embarrassment about using tools.

### Core ideas

#### 1. Downloading code and executing it are different costs

The V8 article accompanying Osmani's 2019 presentation distinguishes transfer cost from CPU execution and explains that a busy main thread can delay interaction after a page appears ready. [S2] This is a useful diagnostic distinction, independent of the article's dated engine measurements.

Our application is to investigate a search page that displays quickly but responds poorly to typing. Write the observable problem before choosing a bundle-size target. Record a representative interaction and inspect the work occurring around it. A smaller download might help one part of the experience while leaving expensive execution unchanged. Conversely, moving work later may improve startup but interrupt the user's next action. Return a short timeline that distinguishes waiting for data, downloading resources and executing work. The proposed fix should name the portion of the timeline it is expected to change, so the team can tell whether it addressed the actual complaint.

#### 2. The fast developer machine is only one environment

The historical V8 discussion compares execution across device classes and describes why CPU and network constraints both matter. It explicitly warns against comparing different site experiences as if they were the same workload. [S2] Its old ratios are not present-day promises, but the comparison discipline remains useful.

For an original experiment, choose the actual journey, content and state to hold constant. Then record the device or simulation conditions under which it is tested. Separate a cold visit from a repeat visit rather than mixing them into a convenient average. If one configuration improves and another regresses, preserve both results. A meaningful performance note says who was represented and who was not. It should avoid calling the page fast simply because the author can use it comfortably on an unconstrained laptop. The output is a bounded comparison with conditions, observations and the next uncertainty worth investigating.

#### 3. Discovering a resource and prioritising it are separate questions

Osmani's priority-hints explanation distinguishes helping a browser discover a resource from influencing its priority once known. It also distinguishes those questions from eager loading. [S3] That prevents a collection of similar-looking loading controls from becoming an undifferentiated bag of speed switches.

Our application begins with the image that actually matters to the opening task. Identify when it is discovered, when its request starts and what other work competes with it. Only then choose an experiment. If the image is discovered late, simply labelling it important may leave the initial delay intact. If every resource is declared urgent, the document no longer communicates a useful distinction. The deliverable is a resource timeline and one justified change, followed by a repeat measurement under the same conditions. Do not turn an improvement reported for another site into an estimate for this one.

#### 4. A responsible small step includes a recovery path

His 2026 action essay frames speed as taking a limited step that produces feedback while preparing for being wrong. It discusses small changes, monitoring and rollback, and cautions that an experiment must measure the experience users care about long enough to expose relevant failures. [S4] Action therefore includes the means to recognise and reverse a mistake.

For a cache change, our exercise is to state the intended benefit, the unacceptable symptom and the person responsible for stopping the experiment. Keep the comparison narrow enough to interpret. A technically reversible switch is not sufficient if nobody can recognise when to use it or if changed data cannot be restored. Record that limitation before calling the experiment reversible. The output is a change plan with a concrete observation period and recovery steps appropriate to the system, not a ritual list of deployment percentages borrowed from another organisation.

#### 5. Specification and verification require separate attention

In his judgment essay, Osmani urges learners to distinguish clear specification from evidence of correctness, and to examine code and unexpected failures rather than accepting generated output unquestioningly. [S5] Producing an answer and judging the answer are different kinds of work.

Our application is a small generated-code review. Write three behaviours the change must preserve before reading its summary. Inspect the actual change and choose evidence that could reveal a failure of those behaviours. A test written from the same mistaken assumption as the code may provide little independent reassurance. Explain why each chosen check matters to the user's task. The result should include the requirement, the observed evidence and the remaining uncertainty. This makes a review more useful than either a blanket rejection of automation or approval based on how confident the explanation sounds.

#### 6. Build judgment through remembered mistakes

Osmani's 2026 essay recommends reading code, maintaining a concise log of errors, doing some work manually and learning a system deeply. [S5] The practical emphasis is on retaining experience rather than allowing each completed task to erase the opportunity to learn from it.

For a learner, our application is a weekly review of three surprising results. Record what was expected, what happened and which assumption changed. Keep entries short enough to revisit. After several examples, choose a manual exercise that addresses a recurring weakness, such as following a request through storage or explaining a race without papering it over. The output is a learning plan grounded in observed gaps. It is not a prediction about the labour market or a requirement to avoid useful tools. The goal is to remain capable of understanding and challenging the work those tools produce.

### How he approaches a problem

Across the selected material, the useful sequence is to connect an engineering change to an observable experience, distinguish the mechanisms involved and preserve evidence that supports a decision. [S2][S3][S4][S5] Our synthesis asks for two short statements before implementation: what should improve, and what observation would make us reject the proposed fix. This keeps a performance task from dissolving into a list of fashionable optimisations.

Imagine a team wants to rewrite a dashboard because a performance score fell. First identify whether people struggle to see the content, activate a control or complete a later task. Compare equivalent conditions and inspect the relevant timeline. If one expensive initialisation step explains the interaction delay, a targeted experiment may be more informative than a broad rewrite. If the evidence instead shows an entirely different bottleneck, change the hypothesis. The outcome is a decision supported by a trace and a task, rather than loyalty to the first proposed tool.

The same discipline helps ordinary planning. A developer choosing a learning project can specify what capability they want to demonstrate and what evidence would establish it. A finished repository is an output; an explanation of a difficult behaviour, supported by a reproducible example, is evidence of understanding. This is an original career application of the judgment method, with no promise that a particular portfolio guarantees employment.

### What can be criticised

Small changes can become a way to avoid necessary structural work. A team may repeatedly shave a local delay while the overall journey remains confusing or unreliable. Our correction is to revisit the original task after each meaningful result and ask whether the remaining problem still justifies the same kind of intervention.

Measurement can also create a narrow view of quality. A convenient metric may improve while a meaningful interaction becomes worse. The action essay itself cautions about observing the right things. [S4] Our additional safeguard is to retain a concrete task walkthrough beside the metric. Neither a score nor a single happy-path demonstration should stand in for the whole experience.

### Blind spots to watch

Do not reuse historical device ratios as current forecasts. Do not assume resource priority solves late discovery. Do not describe a rollout as reversible until its state and recovery limits are understood. Keep the hypothesis attached to the proposed change and compare equivalent conditions.

For learning, do not turn the error log into a ranking of personal worth. It should identify practice opportunities and improved explanations. For automation, inspect actual evidence and consequences instead of inferring quality from polished output. A useful recommendation leaves the user able to explain why the decision was made.
