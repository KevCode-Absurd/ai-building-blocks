## Core ideas

### Separate identity and behavior

Kennedy's approach distinguishes internal worth from a moment someone regrets. [S1]

Original everyday application: After yelling, identify an action to repair instead of concluding you are a hopeless parent. Shame about an entire identity can absorb the energy needed for change. Keep the action specific and responsibility intact. The point is neither self-condemnation nor self-excuse, but an adult able to return and do something useful.

### Hold two truths

Her public interviews describe keeping a boundary and acknowledging a child's feeling simultaneously. [S2]

Original everyday application: A child can be angry about leaving the park while you guide departure. The feeling need not be argued away before the action can happen. Explain the boundary simply and remain present. Avoid making the child perform agreement or gratitude to prove that you were empathic enough.

### Lead without emotional agreement

Good Inside presents parents as steady leaders who can hold limits. [S1]

Original everyday application: End screen time while staying available for disappointment. Choose a limit you can actually maintain. If you repeatedly announce rules you cannot carry out, revise the setup. Steadiness includes acknowledging when a plan was unrealistic rather than escalating to preserve an image of infallible authority.

### Teach outside the hardest moment

Her interviews distinguish emotions from the skills needed to handle them. [S2]

Original everyday application: Practice turn-taking during a calm game instead of offering a long lesson during a fight. A skill becomes more available through manageable practice. Pick a realistic next response and demonstrate it. A child's continued difficulty does not prove a lack of goodness, but it can signal a need for more support or a better fit.

### Repair with yourself first

Her TED teaching asks adults to regain groundedness before repairing with a child. [S1]

Original everyday application: Name overload before an outburst while keeping responsibility for the shouting with yourself. Understanding the conditions helps you plan prevention. It should not become an explanation that makes the child responsible for your reaction. Identify a resource such as a pause, a simpler routine or another adult's help that changes the next moment.

### Name and own the rupture

Her repair teaching models acknowledging what happened and taking adult responsibility. [S2]

Original everyday application: Say you shouted, that it was your responsibility and what you plan to do differently. Leave out the sentence beginning with the child's offense. Their behavior can be addressed separately after repair. Do not demand a hug or immediate forgiveness; give them space to respond and make the promised change concrete enough to practice.

### Transfer the principle carefully

Her interviews discuss applying steady collaboration to other relationships. [S1]

Original everyday application: Acknowledge a colleague's disappointment while explaining a real constraint. Treat the colleague as an adult peer rather than importing a parent-child hierarchy. State what is open to discussion and what is decided. The transferable principle is holding care and clarity together, not treating another adult's disagreement as a tantrum.

## An everyday worked application

In this original repair case, a parent shouted over a refusal to put on shoes. First regain enough steadiness to speak responsibly. Return and name the shouting without blaming the child for causing it. Explain that managing your voice was your job and state one change you will practice when rushed. Allow the child to respond without requiring forgiveness. Discuss the shoe routine in a separate conversation. Two problems can both matter without using the child's behavior to cancel the adult's accountability.

Before trying the example, define what a useful result would look like for the people involved. Separate a change in your own behavior from an outcome requiring someone else's participation. An invitation can be declined; a boundary can be maintained without agreement; a practical obligation may need a direct decision. These are different tasks, so do not judge all of them by whether the conversation feels pleasant immediately.

## Where the method was criticised or needs caution

The sources above primarily present the person's own account and institutional record. They should not be mistaken for independent proof that every application works. The cautions here are editorial limits on this educational adaptation, rather than invented reports of named critics or a claim of scientific consensus.

Repair is important and does not make repeated harm acceptable. Do not claim a phrase erases the past, guarantees secure attachment or removes the need for support. A child can remain upset after a responsible apology. The useful measure is whether adult behavior and the conditions around it begin to change.
