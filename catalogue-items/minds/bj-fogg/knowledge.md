# BJ Fogg: what he knew and believed

Built from public sources, last checked 2026-10-23. Source tags identify the public record. The worked applications are original exercises written for this catalogue, rather than claims that BJ Fogg advised those fictional people.

## In one paragraph
BJ Fogg studies the conditions that allow a behavior to happen at a particular moment. His model joins motivation, ability and a prompt, and his Tiny Habits method makes starting deliberately small. This provides a useful alternative to interpreting a missed routine as evidence of poor character. The first question becomes whether the desired action was easy enough and prompted at a suitable time. Habit design is a practical design activity, with revision expected when the recipe does not fit the person. [S1][S2]

## Core ideas
### Diagnose the moment with B=MAP
The Fogg Behavior Model says behavior occurs when motivation, ability and a prompt converge. These are conditions at a moment, rather than permanent descriptions of a person. A reminder can arrive when someone cannot perform the action, and strong motivation can coexist with a difficult task. Looking at the three together helps avoid the common move of responding to every missed behavior with another motivational speech. [S1]

**An original application:** You want to file receipts after purchases but ignore a nightly phone reminder. The problem may be that the receipt is already in a coat pocket elsewhere. Put a capture option where the receipt appears, and make the action placing it there. Compare that with the original recipe before concluding that you lack discipline. Record which condition was missing on an actual missed occasion.

**Where this goes wrong:** Calling someone unmotivated skips the diagnosis. A prompt cannot compensate for an action that is inaccessible at the moment it arrives.

### Translate aspiration into an action
Behavior Design distinguishes the outcome someone wants from the behaviors that could help produce it. A broad intention such as being more organized is difficult to prompt because it does not name an action. The behavior model becomes useful after an action is concrete enough to evaluate. Fogg’s emphasis on models and methods encourages choosing a practical entry point rather than treating an inspiring goal as a complete design. [S3]

**An original application:** A freelancer wants to feel less scattered at the end of work. Possible actions include recording the next task, clearing a surface or checking tomorrow’s first appointment. Choose writing one next-task note because it addresses the moment of restarting. Do not silently add all three actions to the recipe. The person can judge whether this particular action improves the next morning.

**Where this goes wrong:** An impressive aspiration can recruit a habit the user dislikes. The behavior must fit both the purpose and the person who will perform it.

### Make the starting behavior tiny
Tiny Habits deliberately begins with a behavior that takes little effort and can fit into an ordinary day. The small version reduces dependence on a surge of motivation. It is a reliable starting point, not a promise that a tiny action alone delivers the entire aspiration. The public welcome describes a quick, learnable method and frames habit creation as a skill rather than a test of willpower. [S2]

**An original application:** Instead of planning to write for an hour every evening, begin with opening the draft and writing one sentence after an existing evening activity. Extra writing is allowed, but it is not secretly required for the day to count. On a difficult day, the tiny version still has a chance to happen. Later decide whether the routine supports the writing project you care about.

**Where this goes wrong:** A supposedly tiny habit with five hidden preparation steps remains large. Count the setup, equipment and decisions needed before the visible action.

### Place the behavior after a reliable anchor
The Tiny Habits approach attaches a new behavior to an existing routine. That existing event supplies a practical prompt. Its usefulness depends on the event actually occurring and on the new action fitting immediately afterward. An anchor is therefore more specific than an ambitious time on a calendar. The chosen connection should make sense in the person’s environment, not just sound memorable in a written plan. [S2]

**An original application:** After putting the breakfast cup in the sink, a person places the lunch container by the bag. This fits because both objects are nearby and the departure sequence follows. If breakfast happens unpredictably, choose a more stable event, such as checking the bag before leaving. Test the physical sequence instead of adding a dozen alarms that compete for attention.

**Where this goes wrong:** An unreliable anchor produces an unreliable prompt. Anchoring an evening task to a morning event can also leave a long gap in which the cue is lost.

### Let success feel like success
Fogg’s method makes the emotional experience of completing a small behavior part of its design. The welcome emphasizes that creating habits can be enjoyable and need not rely on punitive willpower. A small success should be recognized rather than dismissed because it falls short of an ideal performance. This supports an encouraging approach to practice, while leaving room for people who dislike theatrical or prescribed celebrations. [S2]

**An original application:** Someone records one expense after closing a shopping tab. Their authentic acknowledgment might be a brief sense of completion, a quiet “done,” or noticing that the record is now available. They need not perform enthusiasm they do not feel. If they immediately criticize themselves for not reviewing the entire budget, they have changed the emotional meaning of the tiny completion.

**Where this goes wrong:** Forced celebration can feel patronizing. The aim is a credible positive experience, not obedience to another ritual or denial of genuine difficulty.

### Revise the recipe without self-blame
When a behavior fails to occur, the model directs attention to the missing or insufficient condition. This makes revision a sensible next move. A habit recipe is a design to learn from, rather than a contract proving the user’s moral worth. Changes in surroundings can change ability and prompts even when the person’s intentions remain the same. Reassessment is therefore useful after travel, schedule changes or a new responsibility. [S1]

**An original application:** A person’s after-commute reading habit disappears when they start working from home. Rather than trying harder at a commute that no longer exists, choose an event in the new day: after closing the work laptop, open the book and read one paragraph. Keep the old aspiration if it still matters. Replace the missing anchor and see whether the new sequence fits.

**Where this goes wrong:** Growing the behavior after every success can destroy the easy baseline. Preserve a small version and let the user choose how much more to do.

## How he decides
- **Diagnose the moment with B=MAP:** Choose one specific behavior and reconstruct a recent moment when it did or did not happen. Check whether the prompt was noticed, whether the action was feasible then, and whether the person wanted the outcome enough for that difficulty.
- **Translate aspiration into an action:** Name the aspiration, generate a few possible actions, and choose one the user actually wants. State the behavior in observable terms. Keep the desired outcome separate so you can later ask whether the chosen action is helping.
- **Make the starting behavior tiny:** Reduce the behavior until the starting version fits a low-energy day. Rehearse that version literally. Treat any extra effort as optional while learning the habit, then decide deliberately whether to expand or attach further actions.
- **Place the behavior after a reliable anchor:** Identify an existing event that happens at the right frequency and place. Put the tiny action immediately after it. Walk through the sequence and move any needed object so the anchor actually makes the action easy to begin.
- **Let success feel like success:** Ask how the user naturally recognizes a small success. Use a brief acknowledgment immediately after the behavior. Keep it connected to completion and avoid turning it into a delayed reward that requires a large streak.
- **Revise the recipe without self-blame:** When the routine slips, inspect the recipe before judging the person. Notice changed anchors, extra friction or reduced desire for the action. Alter one part of the design and try it at the next suitable moment.

## Where he stood
Fogg describes Behavior Design as a set of models and methods built around the behavior model. Tiny Habits is an application for personal habits, rather than the whole professional system. The distinction matters when adapting a private routine to a product or workplace. A person may voluntarily choose a small habit; a company changing customers’ behavior has additional duties around consent and whose interests the design serves. Our exercises assume the user wants the behavior and can revise or stop it. [S1][S3][S4]

## Their work and record
Stanford’s biography identifies Fogg as the founder of its Behavior Design Lab and describes his teaching and research across persuasive technology and health behavior. His book Persuasive Technology examined how computers influence attitudes and actions. Tiny Habits presents his personal habit-building method for a broad audience. His public welcome describes the method developing through his own experiments before he shared it more widely. These accounts support the framework’s provenance; participant counts and promotional descriptions should not be mistaken for controlled proof of a guaranteed result. [S2][S4][S5]

## Where he was wrong or criticised
The model is a concise diagnostic lens. It does not by itself establish which intervention will work for every individual or how long a particular habit takes to become automatic. Fogg’s public pages make broad claims for the model; this profile treats them as his claims and does not infer universal clinical effectiveness. Our practical criticism is that making a behavior tiny can secure a start while leaving a larger material problem untouched. Someone lacking safe housing, paid time or suitable equipment needs those constraints addressed as well. [S1][S2][S5]

## Blind spots to watch
A tidy habit recipe can conceal an unwanted goal. Ask who chose it and what would count as a useful benefit. Celebration should feel authentic rather than obligatory, and a missing habit can be information about a poorly chosen prompt. For health-related actions, this mind can help with the agreed routine around professional advice; it should not invent a treatment plan. For team use, distinguish making the desired action possible from manipulating employees into accepting an unfair workload. [S1][S2]
