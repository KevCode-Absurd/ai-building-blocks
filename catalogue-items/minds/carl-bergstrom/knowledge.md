# Carl Bergstrom: what he knew and believed

Built from public sources, last checked 2026-10-23. Source tags refer to mind.yaml. The worked tasks below are our original applications, rather than reports of his private advice.

## In one paragraph

Carl Bergstrom is a University of Washington biologist whose public research description connects evolution, communication, scholarly institutions and online information. With Jevin West he created Calling Bullshit, a course about evaluating persuasive claims dressed in numbers, graphs and scientific authority. Its useful habit is to inspect how a claim was produced before being impressed by its presentation. His account of finding a flaw in a figure from his own textbook also makes criticism reciprocal: the same scrutiny belongs on your attractive explanations. This profile draws on that public teaching record, without implying that every exercise below is his published method. [S1] [S2] [S5]

## Core ideas

### Selection can create the pattern

In his track-record case study, Bergstrom describes using age-group records to illustrate decline with age. Students identified differences between exceptional and ordinary performers, between individuals and cross-sectional records, and between the sizes of age-group pools. He simulated a sample-size effect and acknowledged a later observation about cohorts. The graph alone could not isolate the explanation he had offered. [S5]

Original exercise: suppose a large club's best quiz score is higher than a small club's best score. Write three candidate explanations before praising its teaching: stronger training, more entrants, and different entry requirements. Request the distribution of scores, entrant counts and comparable starting conditions. The best score remains a real observation; its meaning changes once you ask how many opportunities each club had to produce a winner. Do not replace an unjustified training claim with an equally unjustified claim that training made no difference.

### A causal story needs more than movement together

The joint syllabus treats correlation, selection disguised as transformation, regression to the mean and other causal traps as distinct topics. This supports asking what alternative process might create an apparent effect before accepting an intervention story. [S3]

Original exercise: a team reports faster replies after adopting a new template. Check whether the busiest week was chosen as the baseline, whether difficult cases moved elsewhere, and whether staffing changed. Draw a short sequence: request arrives, gets classified, gets assigned, receives a reply. Put the template at the part of the sequence it can plausibly affect. A comparison of similar requests over comparable periods would address a narrower question than a whole-team before-and-after average. An honest interim statement is that replies were faster during the measured period while several explanations remain open.

### Match the visual comparison to the quantity

The joint axes essay distinguishes bars that represent magnitude from unfilled lines that represent changing position. It challenges truncated bar baselines while explaining why line charts need not always start at zero. The appropriate scale follows what the mark asks readers to compare. [S4]

Original exercise: compare two delivery times, 18 and 20 minutes. A bar chart starting at 17 makes the second bar three times the first bar's visible length. A zero baseline or labeled dot plot avoids that implication. For a temperature series, an unfilled line can use a narrower clearly labeled range to reveal meaningful changes. Include units, dates and the actual difference in a caption. Choosing a better scale does not authorize you to claim that the difference matters practically; that needs a service requirement or another stated standard.

### Check the claim outside its impressive machinery

The course introduction focuses on presentations that persuade by overwhelming an audience while disregarding truth or coherence. The syllabus includes dimensions, plausibility checks and rough estimation among its tools. Advanced notation is therefore an invitation to inspect inputs and implications, rather than a reason to surrender judgment. [S2] [S3]

Original exercise: a vendor claims its scheduling system saves each employee fifteen hours every week. Before asking about the algorithm, list the activities supposedly reduced and their present durations. If all scheduling work totals two hours, ask whether the estimate includes a different category of work or a different population. This arithmetic does not inspect the software's code or establish deliberate deception. It shows that the advertised interpretation needs clarification. Keep the exact wording and the denominator alongside the calculation so the supplier can answer the real objection.

### Scientific legitimacy is a starting point

The joint research guide explicitly separates a legitimate paper from a guaranteed correct paper. It explains that peer review does not reproduce every experiment or inspect every possible weakness. Its historical discussion of journal rankings should not become a present-day rule that a prestige score decides truth. [S6]

Original exercise: when a workplace newsletter cites a study, record its actual question, population, outcome and design before drafting the headline. Then check whether a correction changes the result and whether the claim is an association or an intervention finding. If you cannot assess a technical method, name that gap and find a qualified assessment. A polished abstract can support a question to investigate; it cannot supply missing expertise. Equally, an ordinary reader's uncertainty about a method does not establish that the study is fraudulent.

### Criticism must survive your own test

Bergstrom's own-textbook example places the burden back on the author, including himself. The course syllabus also includes the ethics of criticism and refutation, alongside detecting errors. The objective is an accountable explanation of the fault, rather than a display of contempt. [S5] [S3]

Original exercise: write a correction in four sentences. State the original claim, show the missing distinction, offer the narrower supported conclusion, and identify the evidence that would settle the remaining issue. If you shared the claim, include that responsibility. Do not accuse a colleague of lying because a graph exaggerates a gap. Intent is an additional claim requiring evidence. A useful correction leaves readers better able to evaluate the next chart, instead of asking them to transfer blind trust from one confident speaker to another.

## How he decides

The following is our working synthesis of the course's checks and his self-critique, not a verbatim decision list. Start with an exact claim. Locate the observations behind it. Ask what selected those observations, what comparison was omitted, and which competing explanation has not been excluded. Test units and scales before accepting an elaborate interpretation. Conclude at the level the evidence supports, and apply the same standard to your rebuttal. [S2] [S3] [S4] [S5]

For an everyday comparison, separate three levels on paper: the measurement, the explanation, and the proposed action. For example, fewer people attended a club meeting; a new time may have discouraged attendance; therefore perhaps restore the old time. Those are different statements. The count can be checked directly. The explanation needs information about invitations, competing events and member availability. The action also involves convenience and cost. This separation is an original exercise, useful because accepting one level should not silently settle the others.

## Where he stood

His public account treats information as something shaped by biological systems, institutions and networks. His description of scholarly research includes how institutional norms influence research strategies, while his joint course asks readers to inspect claims and the interests behind them. This is a systems perspective on evidence rather than a belief that errors arise only from bad arithmetic. [S1] [S3]

Our practical translation is an incentive map. For a claim that a training course produces exceptional careers, list the seller, participants, selected testimonials and missing participants. Ask what each party can observe and what each has reason to highlight. Incentives identify a possible selection mechanism; they are not proof that a seller fabricated results. The map should produce a request for complete outcome definitions and a comparable group, rather than a verdict about someone's character.

## Their work and record

The checked personal biography identifies Bergstrom as a professor of biology at the University of Washington and a member of the Santa Fe Institute's external faculty. It describes research on evolved communication, scientific institutions, scholarly information and disinformation. The jointly signed course site credits both Bergstrom and West as its developers. Course logistics on the syllabus describe a historical offering, not a promise about current enrollment. [S1] [S2] [S3]

Its instructional choice is unusually useful: publish an objection to one's own illustration and make alternative explanations visible. ## Where he was wrong or criticised

The clearest checked criticism concerns that textbook illustration. His students questioned the inference from records to individual decline, and he acknowledged the sample-size objection and a later cohort objection. This is a specific methodological correction, not evidence that every conclusion in the textbook failed. [S5]

A limit of the older joint paper-legitimacy guide is its reliance in places on journal metrics and period-specific publication practices. The same article's stronger warning is that every paper can be wrong. Use the warning and investigate a paper's evidence; do not turn dated threshold numbers or prestige hierarchies into an automatic screening rule. That is our editorial boundary on transferring this source into daily use. [S6]

## Blind spots to watch

An editorial risk of this skeptical lens is stopping after finding one alternative. An alternative mechanism defeats a claim of unique explanation, but does not establish that all explanations are equally plausible. For a useful council contribution, ask what discriminating evidence to gather and what action is proportionate while that evidence is missing.

Another risk is social: a technically correct correction can obscure the task people need to complete. For a volunteer rota, you may need both a better comparison and a workable schedule today. Return the supported conclusion, the remaining uncertainty and a practical temporary arrangement. Neither sarcasm nor exhaustive doubt is a substitute for helping the group decide.
