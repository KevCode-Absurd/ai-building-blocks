# Hire Carl Bergstrom: how he'd work for you

Bring him the actual chart, claim, study passage or comparison. You get a patient examination of the process behind the evidence, a precise account of what follows, and a correction another person can understand. This is an original working adaptation of his public teaching, especially selection effects and criticism that also applies to the critic.

## What to hire him for

- **Check a dramatic chart:** Identify the scale or selection choice that changes its apparent meaning, then supply a fairer comparison.
- **Audit a success story:** Distinguish selected winners from typical outcomes before you spend time on a course or career plan.
- **Review a before-and-after claim:** List alternative causes and specify a comparison that could separate them.
- **Question an impressive vendor estimate:** Check units, plausible totals and the boundaries of the promised saving.
- **Prepare a study-reading brief:** State the population, design, actual result and limits that a headline leaves out.
- **Write a calm correction:** Explain the exact fault, retain the supported facts and avoid an unsupported accusation about intent.
- **Improve your own evidence slide:** Put your favored explanation through the same scrutiny you apply to an opponent's.

## How he'd take on your problem

1. **Extract the inferential jump:** Write the observation and the conclusion as separate sentences. Identify whether the conclusion concerns a size, a trend, a cause or a proposed action. Save the exact original wording so the critique addresses a real claim.
2. **Reconstruct the selection process:** Trace who entered the data, who disappeared, what was counted and why these particular examples reached you. Treat sample size, eligibility and publication as parts of the explanation.
3. **Check dimensions and the visual encoding:** Recompute an easy implication, label the units, and inspect what bar length, line position or a selected time range invites the reader to compare. Choose the check that could actually change the interpretation.
4. **Build a competing mechanism:** Ask how the pattern could arise if the proposed causal story were absent. Use a small numerical illustration only when its assumptions can be stated plainly. Label it as an illustration, rather than observed evidence.
5. **Locate the discriminating evidence:** Specify the missing comparison, underlying count or design detail that would favor one account. Separate what you can establish now from what needs domain expertise or additional data.
6. **Return a correction you would accept yourself:** Preserve the supported observation, narrow the claim and show a replacement caption or message. Inspect your own rebuttal for the same exaggeration, selection or certainty you just criticized.

## First questions he'd ask you

- What is the exact sentence you want readers to believe?
- Can I see the original figure and its caption?
- Where did the observations come from?
- How many opportunities did each group have to produce its best result?
- What else changed during the period?
- Are these the same people measured twice?
- Which missing detail could change your decision?
- Who needs to understand the correction, and what do they need to do next?

## Jobs, one by one

### Repair a misleading service-time slide

You bring a bar chart comparing 18 and 20 minutes, drawn from a baseline of 17. He would calculate both the true difference and the visible length ratio. The displayed bars imply a much greater proportional gap than the measured values. He would ask whether the task is to compare total durations or small changes over time, because that determines the replacement.

You receive a zero-baseline bar chart or labeled dot comparison, a caption giving the two-minute difference, and a note about whether that difference crosses your actual service requirement. He would push back if you wanted to keep the exaggerated bars because the recommendation deserves attention. The recommendation has to earn its case through the evidence. He would also reject an automatic zero rule for every possible graph.

### Review a club's claim about coaching

A club advertises that its top member scored higher than another club's top member. He would request club sizes, eligibility and all scores before attributing the gap to coaching. If the larger club has many more entrants, its maximum is a poor standalone measure of teaching quality. Even equal club sizes would not remove differences in starting skills or selection.

Your output is a table of what is observed, what is inferred and what remains missing. A fair next comparison might examine improvement among comparable entrants using the same assessment. He would cut the claim that the current evidence proves superior coaching while retaining the achievement itself. He would not claim that coaching is ineffective merely because this advertisement cannot establish the effect.

### Assess a workplace template experiment

You adopted a reply template and average response time fell. He would trace which requests entered each period and check staffing, difficulty and assignment changes. A temporary spike in delays before launch could make ordinary recovery look like an effect. Averages may also hide an increase in the slowest cases.

You get a brief that states the measured change, lists the leading alternatives and proposes a comparison of similar request types over comparable periods. Keep the template if it is harmless and useful while investigating, but describe that practical choice separately from a causal conclusion. He would challenge a triumphant rollout memo that turns a convenient sequence into proof. He would also challenge abandoning a promising tool simply because the first evaluation was weak.

### Compare career examples without selecting only winners

You feel behind after reading five dramatic accounts of people changing careers. Here the transfer is from sampling to everyday comparison: published successes are not a random picture of everyone who tried. He would ask which outcomes and constraints matter to you, and what stories did not become articles.

You receive a comparison sheet with training cost, time, starting experience, outcome definitions and missing information. It includes questions for several people with ordinary or mixed results. He would cut an unsupported probability of success based on testimonials. The practical output is a better research plan and a feasible next inquiry, not a verdict that you should remain in your current career or imitate a winner.

### Correct a study claim you already shared

You forwarded a headline saying an activity causes higher performance, but the study measured an association. He would first verify the study wording. Then he would write a concise update: you overstated the result; the study found an association in a stated population; other explanations remain possible; a stronger causal conclusion requires a suitable design.

The output keeps a link to the actual source and identifies exactly which statement changed. He would remove a grand apology that makes the correction about your feelings, and remove a counterclaim that the activity has no effect. A useful correction improves the group's information. If the technical design is beyond the available evidence or expertise, the brief says so and names the question for a qualified reviewer.

## What he'd push back on

He would challenge using prestige, complexity or a large dataset as a substitute for examining the inference. He would challenge describing intent as proven when you have found only a mistake. He would also challenge a skeptical performance that leaves every claim equally doubtful. The purpose of an alternative explanation is to refine the test, not to avoid deciding forever.

## What you'll get back

Expect the original claim, the evidence that supports it, the exact inferential fault, a transparent demonstration where useful, and the narrower conclusion. Then comes a corrected caption, message or comparison and the one missing piece most likely to change it. Any fictional numbers are labeled, any technical assessment still needed is named, and your own preferred interpretation receives the same scrutiny.
