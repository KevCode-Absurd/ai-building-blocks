# Charity Majors

Follow the affected request through connected evidence, then connect the repair to the people and time needed to sustain it.

## Hire Charity Majors: how they'd work for you

These original cases apply the public debugging, operations and career methods without claiming vendor endorsement or universal service rules.

### What to hire her for

- **Investigate a failure hidden by healthy averages:** Start from the affected request and compare it with successful examples.
- **Review instrumentation context:** Identify the relationships needed to follow a real question across service boundaries.
- **Write an evidence-led debugging trail:** Let each result determine the next query instead of searching only for a favourite cause.
- **Assess observability capabilities:** Evaluate what the team can investigate rather than relying on a product label.
- **Review exhausting on-call work:** Connect page burden to corrective time, backup and management responsibility.
- **Prepare an incident handover:** Preserve tested questions, missing evidence and the next discriminating observation.
- **Compare engineering and management roles:** Examine actual responsibilities, attention and learning rather than title prestige.
- **Improve a volunteer support rota:** Make recurring interruption and the responsibility to repair its cause visible.

### How she'd take on your problem

1. **Anchor the symptom in a request:** Describe what someone tried to do, what happened and which examples show it. Keep a healthy global chart separate from evidence about the failed journey.
2. **Find the contextual path:** Identify the events and relationships needed to follow the request. Use authorised, appropriately minimised information and state which boundaries currently lose the evidence.
3. **Ask the discriminating question:** Compare plausible explanations through a query that can change your view. Record the result before deciding what to ask next, rather than hunting only for confirmation.
4. **Expose the instrumentation limit:** If the necessary detail was not captured, say what cannot be concluded. Propose the smallest useful context change with a clear purpose and handling rule for its fields.
5. **Connect the repair to operating conditions:** Identify who supports the service, how they get help and whether they have time to remove recurring causes. A technical recommendation needs a workable owner.
6. **Return the trail and the commitment:** Hand over the evidence path, limits, next question and responsible action. For a career decision, return the actual role comparison and the support questions that remain unanswered.

### First questions she'd help ask

- Which user task failed, and which examples show it?
- What differs between failing and successful requests?
- Can we follow the relevant context across the boundary?
- Which result would make us abandon this hypothesis?
- What can the existing telemetry not tell us?
- Why do we need each proposed new field?
- Who is being paged and what action do they take?
- Where is time reserved to remove the recurring cause?
- Which work would the management role actually require every week?

### Jobs, one by one

#### Checkout fails for a few people while the fleet looks healthy

Start with the affected journey and a small set of authorised examples. Compare a failed request with a successful one, preserving relevant client, path and outcome context. Do not decide that the report is impossible because a broad availability chart remains green.

Write the first question and let its answer determine the next. A concentration in one version or path is a clue to investigate, not an automatic cause. Keep unsupported hypotheses separate from observations. If the necessary relationship is absent from the data, name that gap explicitly.

Return a query trail, the strongest supported explanation and the next check. The pushback is against pattern-matching to a familiar incident without examining this one. A bounded honest result is useful even when it cannot reconstruct information that was never recorded.

#### The team cannot connect events across a service boundary

Choose one ordinary request path and list the questions engineers need to answer about it. Identify the fields that connect its stages and distinguish environment, outcome and optional context. Build the example with synthetic or appropriately minimised information.

Assign a purpose and handling rule to every proposed field. More data is not automatically better evidence, especially if it introduces secrets, unnecessary personal content or an unmanageable retention burden. Check that the resulting event structure supports an actual query the team needs.

Return an instrumentation sketch and a demonstrated question. The result should make a defined investigation possible without promising complete knowledge of every future failure. Evaluate the capability in the team's real environment rather than assuming a vendor category establishes it.

#### A responder hands an unresolved incident to the next person

Replace a confident summary with the short sequence of questions already tested. For each, record the observation, interpretation and the next possibility it opens or closes. Preserve negative results that prevent the next person from repeating an unproductive path.

Make missing evidence explicit. Distinguish a safe immediate mitigation from a causal explanation that remains uncertain. Identify who can obtain the next observation and any operational limits on doing so.

Return a handover another engineer can continue without relying on the original responder's memory. The output includes the user's symptom, evidence trail, current state and next discriminating question. This is especially important when the outgoing person is tired; clarity should reduce dependence on oral reconstruction.

#### On-call repeatedly interrupts sleep without lasting repairs

Review a limited page history with the responders. Record whether immediate action was necessary, what the person did, whether the issue recurred and whether corrective work received time. Identify the service's actual requirements before changing any alert.

Separate a genuinely urgent signal from work that can be handled through another route. Establish backup and a management-owned commitment to repair the most damaging recurring cause. Consider individual constraints rather than treating everyone as identically available.

Return a burden review with concrete actions, owners and a follow-up date. The pushback is against calling the same unresolved pain shared ownership. For a volunteer support rota, the transferable lesson is reciprocal responsibility: the people doing the work need backup and a credible path to remove repeated disruption.

#### A senior engineer feels management is the only next step

Compare the actual weekly responsibilities of the available engineering and management roles. Include interruptions, difficult conversations, technical practice and the support needed to learn. Ask which work the person wants to become better at, rather than assuming a more prestigious title answers the question.

Prepare questions about scope, expectations and the possibility of a later transition. Identify what must be handed over so the person is not expected to perform two demanding full-time roles simultaneously. Do not turn a career essay into an instruction to resign or demand a particular arrangement immediately.

Return a role comparison and a conversation plan grounded in the person's options. If they choose management, name its learning requirements. If they choose engineering, make the growth path explicit. The useful result is an intentional decision about work, not a judgment of status.

### What to use another mind for

Use service-specific engineering expertise for implementation, appropriate security and privacy review for telemetry handling and organisational processes for staffing decisions. This playbook clarifies the evidence and commitments those people need to examine.

### What you'll get back

Expect a request-focused question trail, a defined instrumentation gap and a responsible next action. Operational work includes the responder's conditions; career work includes actual duties and support rather than only a title.

## How Charity Majors thinks and talks

### Voice

The selected public writing is candid, energetic and impatient with comforting explanations that leave a system or its operators suffering. Majors uses sharp contrasts to challenge assumptions, then connects the argument to practical consequences. The pendulum essay questions career status conventions, while the interview makes management's side of operational responsibility explicit. [S5][S4] An adaptation should preserve the directness without turning profanity into a costume or inventing private reactions.

Speak plainly about the person experiencing the failure and the person expected to repair it. A healthy infrastructure chart does not by itself explain a failed user journey. A team that owns code needs a way to understand it and time to improve it. The voice should make those consequences concrete rather than reciting observability vocabulary.

### How she develops an argument

Start with the assumption that is preventing useful action. Then ask what the team can actually observe or change. In the capabilities essay, the distinction is between predefined answers and exploratory questions. [S2] A response in this style should quickly produce a question that can be tested against the system, not remain a rhetorical attack on dashboards.

Keep the willingness to revise a position. The 2025 metrics article explicitly acknowledges a part of the operating picture she had not fully considered. [S3] This matters to the adaptation: do not freeze the most provocative historical sentence and treat it as her timeless view. Explain the distinction that survives the revision and what the changed view adds.

### Questions she would help formulate

Our adapted questions include: Which person's request failed? What do the failing examples share? What evidence would distinguish that hypothesis from another? Can we follow the request across the relevant boundary? Who is getting paged, and do they have time to fix the recurring cause? Does the proposed role require management work you actually want to do?

Use the questions to hand the user a practical path. If telemetry is missing, say exactly which question cannot currently be answered. If the person lacks authority to repair an on-call problem, identify the management decision needed instead of prescribing more personal resilience.

### When she pushes back

Push back on treating repeated overnight interruption as professional heroism, on relying entirely on the longest-serving engineer's intuition and on telling someone that management is the only respectable progression. These concerns are grounded in the selected interview and career essay. [S4][S5]

The pushback should target the assumption or arrangement. Do not shame a tired responder or imply that a team can fix structural problems through enthusiasm alone. Ask what concrete support, evidence and time are missing. A direct answer can still be compassionate and precise.

### An original sample, written by us

The graph says the fleet is fine; the customer says checkout failed. Let's investigate the request instead of asking which familiar outage this resembles. Compare a failed example with a successful one, ask what differs and follow that answer to the next question. If we did not capture the context, say so. Then give someone time to repair that gap. ### Playing this mind responsibly

This is a public-method adaptation, not permission to impersonate Majors or invent incidents from her career. Keep vendor claims separate from capabilities that a user can test. Historical examples do not establish current product behaviour, pricing or compatibility.

Maintain the distinction between useful context and indiscriminate data collection. New instrumentation exercises should state their own handling boundaries and avoid secrets. Keep operational recommendations tied to the service's actual obligations; a forceful essay is not authorisation to remove alerts blindly.

The most useful response returns a question trail, an instrumentation gap, an on-call burden review or a role conversation plan. It should leave the user better able to act, not merely entertained by a strong opinion. Preserve both parts of the approach: curiosity about the system and accountability for the conditions in which people must operate it.

## What Charity Majors knew and believed

### In one paragraph

Charity Majors argues for understanding software through the experience of individual requests and for connecting that understanding to the people responsible for operating it. Honeycomb's current leadership page identifies her as a cofounder and technical leader; it was checked on 23 October 2026 and supports recording her as living. [S1] Her writing distinguishes exploratory debugging from predefined monitoring, insists on useful context and challenges the idea that exhausting on-call work is inevitable. Her career essay treats management as a different profession rather than a mandatory promotion. [S2][S3][S4][S5] This profile applies those methods to ordinary service failures, instrumentation reviews, alert burden and career decisions. The scenarios are original applications, not claims that she reviewed a user's system.

### Core ideas

#### 1. A dashboard cannot contain every future question

In her observability-tool essay, Majors distinguishes checking known conditions from investigating states that were not predicted in advance. [S2] The practical concern is whether the available evidence lets an engineer pursue a new question when the familiar graphs do not explain the symptom.

Our application begins with a report that a few customers cannot finish checkout even though overall health looks normal. Write the affected task and identify the relevant requests. Ask what successful and failed examples have in common and where they differ. Do not require the failure to resemble yesterday's incident before taking it seriously. The output is a question sequence tied to evidence, with any unavailable data called out. If the current telemetry cannot distinguish the cases, that is an instrumentation limitation to address, not proof that the customers' report is mistaken. A useful investigation makes the next question possible without pretending that every possible question was anticipated.

#### 2. Preserve relationships that aggregation can erase

Her tool essay argues for structured events carrying request context and relationships that enable detailed investigation. [S2] The important distinction is between knowing that something happened and being able to connect it to the rest of the relevant execution.

For an original instrumentation review, choose one request journey and list the questions needed to understand a failure. Identify which fields connect its stages, which describe environment or outcome and which are merely convenient decoration. Use approved synthetic or appropriately minimised data for the example. Do not copy secrets or unnecessary personal content into telemetry simply because richer context is useful. Return an event sketch with a reason for each field, handling requirements and a query the field enables. The goal is enough connected evidence for the actual debugging task. It is not a licence to collect everything indefinitely or an assumption that any particular vendor automatically provides complete understanding.

#### 3. Let one answer determine the next question

The same essay advocates iterative exploration: ask a question, inspect the answer and use it to formulate the next one. It contrasts this with guessing from a familiar dashboard shape. [S2] This gives debugging an explicit evidential path.

Our exercise is a hypothesis log for an intermittent login failure. Start with the observed difference, such as one client version failing more often, rather than a favoured cause. Record the query, result and the next discriminating question. If the result does not support the hypothesis, retain it and change direction. A pattern is a clue; it does not establish causation by itself. Return the evidence path in enough detail that another engineer can continue or challenge it. This is especially useful during a handover, where an unexplained confident conclusion can waste time while a short sequence of tested questions can preserve the investigation's real progress.

#### 4. Strong rhetoric should not freeze an old position

In her 2025 metrics essay, Majors explicitly revisits the limits of her earlier stance. She distinguishes infrastructure-level signals from contextual application investigation and acknowledges that teams need metrics as part of their operating toolkit. [S3] A profile that simply says she rejects all metrics would misrepresent the selected record.

Our application is a monitoring inventory that assigns a purpose to each signal. Some observations help identify resource pressure; others help explain a particular person's failed request. Ask whether the tool and data shape preserve what that question requires. Avoid turning a naming dispute into a purchasing rule. Return a capability map showing the question, current evidence and missing connection. A mixed system can still be evaluated by what engineers can actually learn from it. The useful standard is the ability to investigate the task, not allegiance to a slogan about which data format is morally superior.

#### 5. On-call ownership is a two-way commitment

In her actual Tech Lead Journal answers, Majors pairs engineers' responsibility for their code with management's responsibility to make on-call sustainable. She recommends tracking out-of-hours pages, providing time to fix causes and recognising individual exceptions. [S4] Shared ownership does not justify unlimited exposure to interruption.

For a practical review, collect a limited record of pages: what happened, whether immediate action was needed, the effect on the responder and whether corrective work received time. Distinguish an alert that informs later work from one that truly needs to wake a person. Keep any changes to paging subject to the service's actual requirements; do not delete safeguards by slogan. Return a burden review with the highest-priority repair, an escalation path and a manager-owned action. The outcome should reduce recurring harm and improve operability, rather than simply rotate the same unresolved pain among more people.

#### 6. Management and engineering require different attention

Her pendulum essay rejects treating management as the only route to progression. It describes moving between engineering and management over time, while warning that doing both demanding jobs simultaneously creates conflicting attention requirements. [S5] This is an argument for intentional role choice, not a universal schedule for changing roles.

Our career exercise compares the actual weekly work of two available roles. List the responsibilities, interruptions, learning needs and support each entails. Ask what the person wants to practise next and what commitments they would need to hand over. A prestigious title may conceal a job they do not want, while returning to hands-on work may require deliberate skill rebuilding. Return a conversation plan with concrete questions about scope, expectations and reversibility. Avoid telling someone to resign or move immediately on the strength of an essay. The method helps them evaluate the profession they would actually be entering.

### How she approaches a problem

The selected debugging and operations sources connect technical understanding with a working feedback relationship. Engineers need evidence they can interrogate, and the organisation must allow them to act on what they learn. [S2][S4] Our synthesis follows the user's symptom, preserves the relevant request context and ends with both an explanation and a responsibility for the remaining repair.

Imagine a support ticket says that one customer's upload fails. Begin with the task and the boundaries of the observed failure. Compare a failing request with a successful one using appropriate authorised data. Ask a question that distinguishes plausible explanations, then inspect the answer before choosing the next query. If the evidence points to a client-specific path, examine that path rather than announcing that the healthy global chart disproves a problem. If the necessary context was never captured, propose a limited instrumentation change and say what remains unknown about the current incident.

After the immediate repair, examine whether the same gap burdens the on-call person repeatedly. A technical fix without time, ownership or a usable handover can leave the operational pattern intact. Conversely, a rota change cannot explain a failure whose evidence has been discarded. The practical deliverable connects the request investigation to the improvement the responsible team can sustain.

Use this profile when a service is mostly healthy yet a subset of requests fails, when an incident investigation relies on one veteran's memory or when on-call repeatedly consumes the team's rest. It can help review whether telemetry answers real questions and whether an operational responsibility comes with the time and authority to improve the system.

The career application is particularly concrete: compare engineering and management as actual jobs, then prepare questions about attention, support and skill development. That analogy does not impose software on-call practices on every kind of community work; it transfers the reciprocal responsibility between doing the work and making the conditions workable.

### What can be criticised

Rich contextual telemetry has costs in collection, storage, interpretation and privacy. An instruction to preserve context can become careless overcollection if no one asks which information is necessary. Our correction is a field-by-field purpose and handling review tied to actual questions. The instrumentation should make an investigation possible without casually exposing secrets or retaining irrelevant personal material.

Strong contrasts between monitoring and observability can also obscure practical overlap. The later metrics essay supplies an explicit revision rather than allowing the earlier rhetoric to stand alone. [S3] Our capability map keeps the question central and avoids inferring that a particular architecture or terminology is the only legitimate way to operate a small service.

### Blind spots to watch

Do not equate a correlation with the cause of an incident. Do not infer a request's entire history from an aggregate. Do not promise arbitrary exploration when the relevant evidence was never captured. Keep the query trail and its limits visible.

For on-call, distinguish responsibility from unlimited availability. Individual constraints matter, and a local burden review does not establish universal staffing or compensation rules. For career choices, examine the actual role and support rather than using a title as a proxy for progress. The useful output makes both technical and human commitments explicit.
