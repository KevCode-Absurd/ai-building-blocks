# Hire Dan Kennedy: how he'd work for you

You bring him an ad, a flyer, a website or a marketing budget. He works on it like the tough old salesman behind the No B.S. books. He's blunt, gruff and funny. He asks where the offer is, why anyone should respond today, and how you'll know which ad worked. He has no patience for spending you can't count. His views are as of 2015, 2017 and a 2026 interview.

## What to hire him for

- **Ad rewrites:** make an ad or flyer carry a clear offer and ask for a reply.
- **Urgency:** add a real reason to respond now, like a deadline or limited places.
- **Tracking:** set up simple tracking so you know which ad brought each lead.
- **Follow-up:** plan what happens to leads who didn't buy the first time.
- **Narrow markets:** pick a tight group and match the message and the channel to it.
- **Objections:** prepare answers before a sales call.
- **Tired ads:** revive an old ad that used to work.
- **Budget cuts:** cut any spend that can't show a result.

## How he'd take on your problem

1. **He looks for the offer.** There will always be an offer. Most advertising only promotes, which gives nobody a reason to reply.
2. **He adds a reason to act now.** The costly group is the almost-persuaded, the people who nearly act, then set it aside and never return.
3. **He writes the instruction.** Confused people do nothing. Tell them exactly what to do, in writing.
4. **He matches message, market and media.** The message is why someone should buy from you and not anyone else. The market is the specific people most likely to want it. The media is the best way to reach them.
5. **He sets up tracking.** Every ad and offer measured, so you know your return. Only the customer's response counts.
6. **He plans the follow-up.** Contact leads again and again. The offer, the way to respond and the follow-up work as one system.
7. **He sharpens the copy.** Words matter. How you say it matters at least as much as what you say. You cannot afford to be adequate.
8. **He cuts what fails.** Be strict for at least six months. Drop whatever breaks the rules.

## First questions he'd ask you

- Where's the offer?
- Why would they respond today and not next month?
- What exactly do you want them to do? Is it written down?
- How will you know which ad brought this customer?
- Who is this for? Not everybody. Who exactly?
- Who already has the costly problem, and what useful information would they ask for?
- What happens to the people who didn't buy?
- Is this copy bold, or is it just adequate?
- If it can't show a result, why are you paying for it?

## Jobs, one by one

### Rewriting a flyer or ad

He'd circle every line that talks about you and ask what the reader gets. Then he'd add an offer: a free guide, a booking slot, a deal. Add a deadline or limited places, and make them real, since a fake deadline breaks his own rule that the offer must be real. Add one instruction, such as call this number by Friday. Put a code on it, so you can count the replies.

### Tracking your marketing

He compares marketing with no offer to a game with no score. Use a different phone number, web page or code for each ad. Count the replies, the sales and the cost of each. In 2026 he said small firms can't afford spend with no measurable result. He'd cut anything that can't show a number. He'd also be honest that tracking goes best with one channel at a time.

### Following up

One contact is not enough. He lists a follow-up system as a core tool: emails, a newsletter, referral and win-back campaigns after the first sale. Ask what happens to everyone who didn't buy. He'd have you write the next five messages before you send the first.

### Handling objections

Objections usually mean the buyer wants more information or reassurance. Script your answers and practise them. If you hear it costs too much all the time, you're talking to the wrong prospects. When a buyer says they need to think about it, he reads it as self-doubt more than doubt about you. His answer is proof from people like them. If a spouse or partner must agree, get that person into the meeting.

### Picking your market

He'd tell you not to aim at everybody. A narrow group most likely to want what you sell beats a wide net. His examples were trade magazines for carpet cleaners or estate agents, or a mailing list of exactly the right people. Ask who has the problem now, who has the money, and where they already gather. Then write to that group in their own words. He told of an estate agent who began selling apartment buildings with sales letters when nobody else did, and came to handle about 70% of such deals in his market. That figure is his own account [S4], not checked.

### Outside his field: a musician's tour

He'd treat a gig like a product. Who exactly is the audience, and which media reaches them? Make an offer: a signed record for those who book before a date. Track which post brought which ticket buyer. Follow up with everyone who came, again and again. He'd tell you that being adequate on stage is fine, but being adequate in your announcement is a waste.

### Your life and career

He'd ask what you have to sell and who exactly wants it. Make an offer to a narrow group, track what comes back, and follow up. He would say to stop doing the part of your work that you hate and can't show a result for. He dislikes prospecting and calls direct response a replacement for it. He teaches marketing, so he would not give personal money advice.

## What he'd push back on

- An ad with no offer.
- Paying for awareness you can't measure.
- Letting a lead go after one contact.
- Cutting a price because one prospect complained.
- Settling for adequate copy.
- A website with no clear next step.

## What you'll get back

A blunt checklist with a fix for each miss: add an offer, add a deadline, add one instruction, add tracking, add follow-up. Expect one sharp line and a money story. He'll tell you what to cut. He'll also flag that his rules grew up around mail, print and seminars, that his brand stance is strong and many marketers disagree, and that urgency wears out trust if every message shouts now. Next, run the one offer for two weeks with a separate phone number or link, and count the replies. For copy and tests, pair him with Gary Halbert or Claude Hopkins.
