## Core ideas

### Separate parts of experience

The Whole-Brain Child introduces sensations, images, feelings and thoughts through SIFT. [S1]

Original everyday application: Before sending an angry message, notice a tight jaw, a mental replay, anger and a prediction of rejection. The categories help you observe rather than collapse every reaction into one story. You need not identify every part correctly. Use what you notice to create a little room for choice instead of claiming the exercise reveals an exact brain process.

### Connect before redirecting

The coauthored parenting materials emphasize connection before correction. [S2]

Original everyday application: Acknowledge frustration before explaining a rule about taking turns. Connection can be brief and must fit the child. It does not require a particular touch, gaze or emotional disclosure. Protect safety and state the limit, then use the available calm to make instruction easier to hear.

### Name an experience

Their practical strategies use words and narrative to make sense of a difficult event. [S1]

Original everyday application: When ready, help a child tell what happened before, during and after a frightening moment. Let the child decide the pace and avoid pressing for detail. A simple account can distinguish the event from the frightening predictions that followed it. Persistent distress or a serious event needs appropriate personal support beyond a general reflection exercise.

### Distinguish state from identity

The public framework treats feelings as changing experiences rather than the whole person. [S2]

Original everyday application: Describe feeling angry now rather than being an angry person unable to choose. This makes responsibility more specific: you can acknowledge anger and still choose what to do with a message. It does not make a harmful action harmless. Notice the state, own the action and plan a more useful response.

### Use development as context

The books ask adults to consider skills a child is still developing. [S1]

Original everyday application: Break getting ready into visible steps and practice outside the morning rush. Development is a reason to adjust teaching, not to assume that a child cannot learn. Observe what is manageable for this individual. Use a smaller next step and suitable help rather than making a sweeping claim about age or ability.

### Treat discipline as teaching

No-Drama Discipline frames discipline around instruction and skills alongside limits. [S2]

Original everyday application: After stopping unsafe throwing, practice a safe place and way to use a ball. Decide the lesson before choosing the response. If the response teaches only fear of adult anger, it may miss the practical skill. Keep instruction brief enough to use and return to it when the child can actually participate.

### Practice reflection ordinarily

The materials bring reflection into everyday family experience. [S1]

Original everyday application: Invite willing family members to mention something difficult or interesting at dinner. Participation should remain voluntary rather than a required public report on feelings. Model a small example from your own day and listen without grading the answer. The practice is attention and understanding, not producing a family that always talks emotionally.

## An everyday worked application

In this original message example, a parent receives a school note and immediately wants to accuse the child of not caring. Before writing, identify a physical sensation, a mental image, a feeling and a thought, using only what is actually noticeable. The thought may be a prediction rather than a fact. Ask the school what happened and speak to the child when you can listen. Choose one practical skill or routine to support. This pause does not establish a brain state; it creates room between reaction and action.

Before trying the example, define what a useful result would look like for the people involved. Separate a change in your own behavior from an outcome requiring someone else's participation. An invitation can be declined; a boundary can be maintained without agreement; a practical obligation may need a direct decision. These are different tasks, so do not judge all of them by whether the conversation feels pleasant immediately.

## Where the method was criticised or needs caution

The sources above primarily present the person's own account and institutional record. They should not be mistaken for independent proof that every application works. The cautions here are editorial limits on this educational adaptation, rather than invented reports of named critics or a claim of scientific consensus.

Popular brain metaphors simplify complex science. Do not use upstairs and downstairs language to diagnose someone, guarantee a developmental outcome or dismiss behavior as something no one can influence. The educational value lies in observing experience, choosing teaching over humiliation and keeping real-world skills and support in view.

## Blind spots to watch

For a presentation you find stressful, write the sensations and predictions you notice, then separate preparation tasks from emotional responses. Practice one manageable part and ask for feedback on the work. The framework is an attention aid. It should not become a claim that you can locate a feeling in a particular brain region from self-report.

For a council, pair this lens with someone who checks facts, options and practical constraints. Bring the relevant schedule, commitments or task itself into the discussion. A relationship interpretation can clarify how people speak while leaving a shortage of time, money, space or support untouched. Keep those limits visible and do not turn every material difficulty into a communication failure.
