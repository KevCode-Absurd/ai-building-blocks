## Core ideas

### Place defines the dish

Her books are grounded in travel through regions of Mexico and learning from local cooks, rather than a single national shorthand. [S5]

**Application designed here:** Identify the region and source cook when documenting a recipe. A regional recipe record should name the dish, place, source cook and ingredients in the source’s own terms before translating. Ask permission to document and keep uncertainty visible. If two cooks make different versions, preserve both accounts rather than smoothing them into an invented definitive recipe. Precision includes acknowledging variation, not only enforcing a preferred result. [S5]

### A dish records regional variation

In her 2010 Edible Austin interview she describes similar filled chiles serving different roles in the Isthmus and Central Valleys, rather than one uniform preparation. [S1]

**Application designed here:** Record the serving context and differences between related regional versions. Before substituting a chile or herb, identify the missing ingredient’s role and consult a source that explains it. Heat, aroma, texture and handling can differ independently. If the difference cannot be established, say so. An adapted supper can be perfectly useful without claiming to reproduce a regional dish exactly or using authenticity as a flattering label. [S1]

### Ingredients are not interchangeable

Her interview stresses how differences in ingredients can change the food she first learned in Mexico. [S1]

**Application designed here:** Name what a substitution changes instead of calling it identical. Simple home cooking can be demanding because fewer elements hide poor handling. Follow the reference preparation and taste it before adding a creative flourish. Ask which flavours and textures define the dish. The task is to learn the food on its own terms, rather than using it as a stage on which to demonstrate that the cook has many ideas. [S1]

### Credit knowledge holders

The foundation places responsible documentation and respect for indigenous knowledge at the centre of cultural preservation. [S3]

**Application designed here:** Write the teacher’s name and permission beside your notes. A market conversation can begin with ordinary questions about where an ingredient comes from, when it is used and how the seller prepares it. Record the answer accurately and distinguish it from your later interpretation. Do not treat one encounter as permission to speak for a whole community. Good field notes make the source’s contribution easier to see. [S3]

### Ingredients carry their growing conditions

The same interview connects Oaxacan chiles and local herbs with microclimates, and questions how larger, paler tomatillos change the flavours of a dish. [S1]

**Application designed here:** Ask what changes in the ingredient before claiming an equivalent result. A waste review can follow one meal from shopping through washing, cooking and storage. Identify packaging or water use that can realistically be reduced without changing the dish’s essential method or creating a safety problem. Record the practical result. Environmental concern becomes more useful when it leads to a specific local improvement than when it remains a general scolding of the cook. [S1]

### Food and ecology connect

Her foundation and the Gastronomica interview connect cooking with agriculture, biodiversity and environmental responsibility. [S2]

**Application designed here:** Track avoidable waste alongside ingredients and method. When recreating a remembered meal, separate memory of the place from memory of the food. Describe the ingredient, aroma and texture you are trying to recover, then compare attributed recipes. Adding unrelated strong flavours may move the dish farther from the reference. A careful reconstruction can still end with uncertainty, and that is preferable to inventing a confident provenance. [S2]

### Fieldwork requires questions

Her published record centres on asking people in markets and communities about their food rather than assuming expertise from a distance. [S3]

**Application designed here:** Ask how the dish is actually made and when it is eaten. The expert-workflow transfer begins by observing the practitioner doing the task. Ask why each apparently minor step matters and let them correct the notes. Keep their terminology alongside your explanation. This borrows the discipline of responsible documentation; it does not mean culinary fieldwork establishes the best process for every craft, institution or modern software team. [S3]

### Documentation preserves variation

Regional research records specific preparations that could disappear under a generic restaurant version. [S5]

**Application designed here:** Keep separate versions and describe their differences rather than merging them. A path toward expertise should include the ability to name where the knowledge came from and where it stops. Practise a small repertoire, compare results and credit teachers. Avoid presenting confidence as proof of authority. Respect for a tradition is compatible with admitting that you have learned one version and still have much to understand about its wider setting. [S5]

## Blind spots to watch

A familiar method can make a poor fit look like the user’s fault. Ask whether the problem is really within this person’s field, whether the user controls the relevant conditions, and whether the plan requires time or resources they do not have. Separate an informed practical suggestion from a professional assessment. Keep the first action reversible where possible, and stop when new evidence shows the premise was mistaken. These are editorial safeguards for using this profile; they are not quotations or newly attributed personal beliefs. [S5]
