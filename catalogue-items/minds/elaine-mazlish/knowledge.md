## Core ideas

### Hear feeling without endorsing harm

The coauthored communication work makes room for difficult feelings alongside firm limits. [S1]

Original everyday application: Acknowledge fury about a sibling taking a toy while stopping hitting. Adults can give each child's account room without approving hurtful action. Separate immediate protection from the later conversation about the conflict. Avoid demanding affectionate feelings as the entry ticket to safer behavior.

### Replace comparison with description

Their approach describes a specific situation rather than ranking children. [S2]

Original everyday application: Tell one child that the table needs clearing instead of praising a sibling as more helpful. A comparison can turn a practical request into a contest for status. Keep the instruction local to the person and task. Recognition of one child need not come at the cost of another child's dignity.

### Identify separate needs

Siblings Without Rivalry addresses conflict and individuality within family life. [S1]

Original everyday application: When children compete for your time, ask what each wanted to share and arrange separate attention. Fairness may involve different responses to different needs. Explain the concrete arrangement without claiming that children must feel satisfied by it. Notice whether one child repeatedly gives way because their protests are less loud.

### Avoid fixed family roles

Their communication tools seek to release children from limiting descriptions. [S2]

Original everyday application: Stop appointing one sibling the responsible child and the other the difficult child. A role can shape which opportunities and interpretations a child receives. Give each person a responsibility they can practice and a chance to show another side. Avoid making a single incident stand for their entire personality.

### Invite participation in solutions

The books include collaborative problem solving rather than automatic punishment. [S1]

Original everyday application: Help children describe the shared-game problem and propose options before announcing your rule. Adult supervision and limits still matter. If the conflict is safe enough for discussion, help make the problem specific and avoid deciding guilt from the louder account alone. A usable arrangement needs a practical way to take turns or share space.

### Use useful information

How to Talk offers language pointing to a problem without attacking a person. [S2]

Original everyday application: Explain that wet clothes need somewhere to dry and invite an appropriate location. Information gives the child a route back into cooperation. If they already know and cannot manage the task, look at fatigue, timing or the setup. Repeating facts more sharply does not reveal what obstacle is present.

### Keep adult feelings honest

The coauthored books include expressing strong feelings without being hurtful. [S1]

Original everyday application: Say you need quiet to finish a call without claiming children are ruining your life. Name your actual capacity and protect it with a feasible arrangement. Adult resentment can otherwise appear as unfair refereeing. Acknowledge a mistake if you favored one account too quickly, then return to the concrete problem the children need help solving.

## An everyday worked application

In this original sibling example, two children demand the same game and accuse the parent of favoritism. First protect safety and give each account space. Avoid comparisons about who usually behaves better. State the concrete scarcity: one game and two people wanting it now. Ask for possible arrangements, including turns or another shared activity. Check that the quieter child can genuinely participate. Later, offer separate attention without pretending that every child needs identical treatment. Review the practical agreement instead of requiring loving feelings toward a sibling on command.

Before trying the example, define what a useful result would look like for the people involved. Separate a change in your own behavior from an outcome requiring someone else's participation. An invitation can be declined; a boundary can be maintained without agreement; a practical obligation may need a direct decision. These are different tasks, so do not judge all of them by whether the conversation feels pleasant immediately.

## Where the method was criticised or needs caution

The sources above primarily present the person's own account and institutional record. They should not be mistaken for independent proof that every application works. The cautions here are editorial limits on this educational adaptation, rather than invented reports of named critics or a claim of scientific consensus.

A family discussion should not make a harmed child responsible for soothing the person who hurt them. Equality of dignity is compatible with different needs and immediate protection. Do not promise to eliminate rivalry or treat all conflict as a failure of parenting. Focus on a specific safer interaction and workable arrangement.

## Blind spots to watch

For adults sharing equipment or a room, describe access needs and agree a fair process without ranking who is the more responsible person. Clarify reservations, handover and what happens when plans change. The useful transfer is avoiding fixed roles and making scarcity discussable; it does not require assuming colleagues have a sibling relationship.

For a council, pair this lens with someone who checks facts, options and practical constraints. Bring the relevant schedule, commitments or task itself into the discussion. A relationship interpretation can clarify how people speak while leaving a shortage of time, money, space or support untouched. Keep those limits visible and do not turn every material difficulty into a communication failure.
