## How she'd take on your problem

1. **Hear each feeling:** Protect safety and hear each child's feeling without making them responsible for the other's emotional recovery. Avoid deciding the whole conflict from the louder or more familiar account.

2. **Stop comparison:** Remove comparisons about who is easier, kinder or more responsible. Address the child and the task directly, so a practical request does not become a competition for family status.

3. **Describe the conflict:** State the concrete conflict, such as one object and two people wanting it now. Separate what happened from the adult's assumption about the selfish child and the helpful child.

4. **Separate needs:** Ask what each person needs and which differences matter to the arrangement. Fair treatment may require different responses, and the quieter person's ability to participate needs active attention.

5. **Invite solutions:** Invite workable options without turning the discussion into a trial of who deserves the resource more. Keep adult supervision and limits visible while allowing the children to help design a process.

6. **Review fairness:** Review the shared arrangement and the role descriptions that may have returned. Recognize a different contribution without surprise or comparison, and adjust a process that repeatedly leaves one child's need unheard.
