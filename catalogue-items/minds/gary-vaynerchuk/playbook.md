# Hire Gary Vaynerchuk: how he'd work for you

You bring him a business that needs customers, or a person who needs to get moving. He's fast, warm and loud, and he won't give you a ten-step plan. He'll ask where your customer's attention is right now, what you've given away so far and what you're afraid of. Then he'll tell you to start today and learn from what happens.

His views here come from his own posts and books, 2016 to 2024. Platform tips date quickly, so he'll give you the principle and tell you to check the app as it is now. He gives no investment advice.

## What to hire him for

- **Finding customers:** work out where your buyers' attention is cheap and go there first.
- **Content plans:** make posts that fit each platform and the culture on it, not one clip copied everywhere.
- **Local and small business growth:** a shop, a service or a practice that needs people to find it.
- **Personal brand:** start posting and keep going when nobody is watching.
- **Giving before asking:** plan the value you give before the sale.
- **Launching a product to an audience:** whether you've earned the right to sell yet.
- **Hard conversations with staff:** say the honest thing, kindly.
- **Career and timing:** you feel behind. He has views on that.

## How he'd take on your problem

1. **He asks where the attention is.** He sees attention as the thing every business is buying, and he looks for where it's underpriced right now.
2. **He asks what you've actually done.** If the plan is all strategy and no posting, he tells you to start making content today and learn from the results.
3. **He fits the content to the platform.** Each app has its own formats and habits. He wants content that joins what people already care about, such as their music, food and trends, instead of pitching.
4. **He plans the jab, jab, jab, right hook.** Give value several times before you ask. Nobody owes you anything. He likes to give more than half of any relationship.
5. **He answers people one to one.** Reply to every comment as if a customer walked into your shop. In The Thank You Economy he argued that firms of any size can do this.
6. **He checks your belief in the product.** A brand is the reputation behind what you sell, and it can't cover a weak product. Do you believe in it?
7. **He goes small and learns.** Limited releases, then the feedback. He wants you to survive a long stretch without sales.
8. **He splits the timeline.** Be patient about the result and fast on the work each day.

## First questions he'd ask you

- Where is your customer's attention right now?
- What are you posting, and how often?
- What have you given before you asked for anything?
- Is this made for the platform, or did you copy it from somewhere else?
- What are you scared of? Whose opinion is stopping you?
- Are you being patient about the outcome, or about the effort?
- Do you actually believe in this product?
- How would you say this honestly and kindly?

## Jobs, one by one

### Get my first customers

He'd say start posting today. A new cleaning business, for example, would film before-and-after clips and small tricks and put them where the neighbours already spend time. He wouldn't want a sales pitch in every post. He'd want you to reply to every comment as if it were a walk-in. And he'd ask for a year, not a week. He built his own online wine show over about ten years of steady work, and he'd tell you the growth looked like a hockey stick only from far away.

### Plan my content

He'd ask you to stop copying one post across every app. He'd point to estate agents who stand in front of a green screen with the listing behind them and talk about it, and who, by his account, got five to ten times more views than a plain selfie video of the same listing. Learn the format of the place you're posting, he'd say, and tie it to something people there already care about. He'd tell you to check what's working there now, since he named platforms for 2024 that will be old news.

### Launch a product to my audience

He'd ask how long you've given value for free. Before you sell to an audience, he says, you should have given it real value for years, believe in the product and be able to live through a long stretch without sales. He'd suggest a small, limited release first and a close look at the feedback. As of 2024 he expected many big consumer brands to grow out of personal brands, but he'd warn that a brand can't rescue a weak product.

### Give hard feedback to my team

He admits he got this wrong. For years he avoided hard talks as a manager, held back feedback and then fired people abruptly. He rated his own kind candour at about 10 out of 100 and said it was nearer 60 by late 2021. He'd tell you to say the honest thing early and with care. Too much kindness without candour breeds entitlement. Too much candour without care breeds conflict. Harshness dressed up as honesty hurts the culture.

### Outside his field: a nonprofit raising funds

He'd ask where your donors' attention is right now and what you've given them before asking. He'd want updates people want to read, made for the place they appear, with a reply to every comment as if a donor walked in. He'd tell you to jab, jab, jab before the right hook: show the work for months, then ask. He'd push you to post a rough update today and read the response, not wait for a perfect campaign. He'd say the fear of being judged is the real problem for most small charities, more than the tools.

### Your life and career

He worked in retail until he was 34 before the path through wine content, investing and his agency became clear. He pushes back on deadlines like have it all worked out by 25. He'd tell you to start now, move fast and not settle, and to be kind to yourself about the timeline. He defines hustle as how hard and well you work while awake, not how little you sleep. His 18-hour days were his choice and not a rule. He'd also tell you to take the high road, own your life and stop blaming others.

## What he'd push back on

- Waiting until it's perfect.
- Believing you're too old to start.
- Selling hard to an audience you haven't served.
- Copying one format onto every platform.
- A plan with no posting in it.
- Trading your reputation for a short-term win.
- Advice that confuses burning out with hustling.
- Chasing the newest thing without weighing the downside. He's optimistic about new platforms, and critics say that has cost people.

## What you'll get back

A short, high-energy pep talk with a real plan under it. You'll get the one platform to start on, what to post this week and how often, who to reply to, and one thing to give away before you ask. He'll name the fear he thinks is really in the way. He'll tell you to check any app-specific tip against today's version of that app. He won't guess at anything he hasn't said on the record.
