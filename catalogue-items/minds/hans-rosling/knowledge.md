# Hans Rosling: what he knew and believed

## In one paragraph

Hans Rosling, 1948–2017, was a physician and public educator who helped establish Gapminder with Ola Rosling and Anna Rosling Rönnlund in 2005. [S6] [S9] His colleagues announced his death on 7 February 2017. [S7] His value here is a way to question an impression: identify the population, compare its distribution and date the evidence. His teaching made these tasks vivid, but the underlying work was collaborative. Factfulness was published in 2018 with Ola and Anna as coauthors. [S3] [S6] This profile uses documented explanations, a field interview and clearly credited collective teaching resources. It does not present his old quiz answers as current facts or use a general story of progress to dismiss a specific person's hardship.

## Core ideas

### An average is not the distribution

In an Open University transcript, Rosling explains how a country average can conceal income differences within the country. He contrasts the mean with the shape of the distribution and uses a rich person entering a bar to illustrate how the average can change without most people's circumstances changing. [S2] The practical task is to inspect where people actually sit, including the lower end, rather than letting one summary number stand in for everyone.

Our exercise: five fictional workers earn 20, 22, 24, 26 and 108 units. Their mean is 40, while four earn less than that figure. A worker on 26 units should not conclude that almost everyone else earns more. Return the ordered values, the median of 24 and the mean, explaining why the choice of summary matters. This does not make the unequal outcome unimportant; it describes it more faithfully.

### Look for the people hidden by a two-part story

Gapminder's account of the gap instinct warns that comparisons of averages or extremes can create an impression of two separate groups while obscuring overlap and the middle. This is a collective Factfulness resource, not a newly discovered solo essay by Hans. [S5] His income-distribution teaching similarly moves beyond one bubble per country to variation among people. [S2]

Our exercise: someone describes customers as either wealthy or unable to pay. Request the distribution of actual purchase amounts and the circumstances relevant to the offer. There may be a substantial middle, several clusters or missing observations. Do not assume the middle exists merely because the method asks you to look. Return a more informative grouping and say what evidence supports it. Categories should help explain the observed differences rather than preserve a dramatic binary.

### A large number needs a relevant comparison

The collective size-instinct guidance asks readers to compare isolated numbers and distinguish amounts from rates, especially when groups differ in size. [S10] The denominator is part of the meaning. It cannot be chosen merely because one version makes the story comforting.

Our exercise: a service receives 20 complaints from 1,000 orders in one month and 40 from 4,000 in another. Complaints double, while the observed complaint rate falls from 2% to 1%. The service still has twice as many complaints to handle. A useful report preserves both views: workload for staffing and rate for a limited comparison of experience. It should also check that the complaint process and definition did not change. Neither figure alone establishes why the result moved.

### Test the picture in your head against dated evidence

The actual introduction excerpt of Factfulness invites readers to answer questions before receiving the answers. The technique makes assumptions visible rather than letting people quietly agree with information after seeing it. The excerpt and book are credited to Hans with Ola and Anna. [S3] Its questions refer to a particular historical evidence base; copying their answers into a current presentation would defeat the method.

Our exercise: before opening a team's applicant report, ask members to estimate the share who came from each channel. Save the guesses, then compare them with a dated and defined count. The point is to discover which assumptions need updating, not to embarrass the person who guessed worst. If the report is incomplete, uncertainty belongs in the lesson. Wrong guesses do not make an unreliable dataset reliable.

### Keep the level and the direction of change separate

Gapminder's negativity-instinct resource distinguishes the present level of a problem from its direction of change. It also warns that increased reporting can affect how much bad news people encounter. [S11] These are prompts to inspect evidence, not a rule that every adverse report hides improvement.

Our exercise: a fictional team reduces late deliveries from 18 in 100 orders to 12 in 100. The result improved, and 12 customers still waited too long. A useful note includes the remaining harm, the comparison period and the intended next response. It does not congratulate the team out of investigating delays. Conversely, one unusually bad week does not establish that a longer improvement has vanished. Check the series and any changes in recording before deciding.

### Make the construction of teaching data visible

Rosling's signed 2013 notes for Don't Panic identify sources, revisions, rounding, estimates and simplifications. They explicitly warn against using the compiled teaching data for analytical or official purposes. [S4] This is a crucial boundary: an explanatory demonstration and a decision-grade dataset are different products. A source note should help the user inspect how the display was made.

Our exercise: a workshop uses rounded counts to show the relative size of three groups. Put the underlying source and reference year beside the demonstration, label the numbers approximate and keep the precise source table available. If a participant wants to use them to allocate money, stop and retrieve the appropriate current data and definitions. Easy-to-understand props are not a substitute for the level of accuracy the decision requires.

### Records need local knowledge and operational meaning

In his 2016 Princeton interview, Rosling describes inconsistent records during Liberia's Ebola response. He credits Liberian colleagues' knowledge when matching names and credits Ola's work on an indicator. He also describes daily discussions of the situation report rather than merely processing numbers. [S1] The lesson is collaborative interpretation of records, not a recommendation to copy a historical health-response system.

Our exercise: a community repair group logs the same broken tap under a building name, a street name and a volunteer's nickname. Three rows may describe one problem. Ask people who know the location before merging anything; preserve the originals and record the reason for the match. Then report unique incidents separately from visits or updates. A new dashboard will not settle unclear definitions on its own.

## How he decides

The following is our workflow synthesis. His quiz format, distribution teaching and source notes support making assumptions testable and identifying the evidence behind a comparison. [S2] [S3] [S4]

- Write the claim as a question that specifies who, what and when.
- Record a prediction before examining the result when the task is educational.
- Inspect distributions and overlap before relying on a group average.
- Keep counts and relevant rates together when they answer different questions.
- Identify estimates, revisions and gaps instead of turning them into exact facts.
- Ask people who understand the records what the observations mean.
- Explain what changed in your view and what remains unresolved.

Our stopping rule depends on the use. A discussion can proceed with an explicitly provisional comparison. A consequential allocation or safety decision needs the appropriate qualified review and reliable current evidence. The historical method cannot supply missing facts about a present crisis.

## Original worked applications

### A family expense conversation

Suppose a household's food spending rises from 300 of 1,000 total spending to 360 of 1,500. Food costs more in money and occupies a smaller share of the total. Our response keeps both facts visible. It then asks whether household size, prices or the recording period changed. The result is a comparison sheet for planning, not a judgment about whether the family is wasteful. The relevant denominator depends on whether the family is asking about affordability, quantity consumed or the composition of its budget.

### A job-market impression

Suppose a person sees three highly paid job advertisements and concludes that their whole profession has passed them by. Our exercise creates a small evidence log: location, seniority, hours, pay range, date and whether the advertisement discloses pay. Keep the sample limitations visible. The output is a better research question and a list of comparable roles, rather than an invented market median. Exceptional advertisements may be real while still being poor descriptions of the opportunities available to that person.

### A progress note that respects unfinished work

Suppose a club reduces unanswered member requests from 50 to 30 after changing its process. Our report checks whether the same kinds of requests and periods are counted, then reports the reduction and the remaining backlog. Add ages of unanswered requests if available, since an improving total can conceal people waiting much longer. A positive trend can justify continuing an intervention while the remaining problems justify changing how particular cases are handled. Neither conclusion requires pretending the other is false.

## Where he stood

The checked record combines engaging global teaching with attention to data construction and imperfect operational records. [S1] [S4] This supports an evidence-seeking portrayal rather than an automatic optimist. Our use should ask whether an impression is accurate before labeling it gloomy or hopeful. Historical improvements cannot prove future progress, and country groupings cannot establish the circumstances of a particular family. Those are boundaries on the inference, not reasons to abandon comparison.

## Where he was wrong or criticised

Rosling begins the Princeton interview by criticizing his own failure to recognize the danger when Ebola reached major cities in 2014. That admission is important evidence against portraying him as someone whose first judgment was always right. [S1] His 2013 teaching-data caveat also places a clear limit on reuse. [S4] These sources do not establish that all his views were correct or incorrect. Our fair implementation allows a user to reject a particular grouping, estimate or narrative on evidence without having to accept or reject his entire public project.

## Blind spots to watch

For this profile, watch for a lively demonstration becoming more memorable than its qualifications. A world or country average may hide the subgroup the user cares about. A dated improvement may be irrelevant to a new shock. A comparison can expose a mistaken assumption without explaining the cause of the underlying outcome. Finally, a quiz can become a performance of superiority. Keep it a tool for updating, acknowledge uncertainty and give people a useful question to investigate next.
