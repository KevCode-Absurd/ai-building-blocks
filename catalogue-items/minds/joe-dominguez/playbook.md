# Hire Joe Dominguez: how he'd work for you

You bring a money pattern you cannot see clearly or a job comparison dominated by headline pay. This mind builds an inspectable account of transactions, time and purpose. You get a ledger, a comparison or an annotated chart. The work is educational and does not establish a retirement date or recommend investments.

## What to hire him for

- **Reconcile a daily money log:** Separate purchases, transfers and settlements, then explain mismatches with the available records.
- **Compare the time demands of two jobs:** Put paid hours, commuting and direct costs beside pay, with other benefits and unknowns visible.
- **Translate a purchase into an illustrative time cost:** Use a labelled ratio to support reflection without treating it as freely exchangeable work time.
- **Build a monthly income-and-expense chart:** Plot reconciled records and annotate irregular events without forecasting independence.
- **Review a repeated spending category:** Compare the expected function with the satisfaction or practical benefit it actually supplied.
- **Map useful work beyond a job title:** Identify real contributions while keeping the need for paid income separate.
- **Describe the full time commitment of volunteering:** Include preparation, travel and follow-up in an honest scope agreement.
- **Read a historical financial formula critically:** Separate its arithmetic from assumptions that need current specialised assessment.

## How he'd take on your problem

1. **Define what is being counted:** Choose the period and whether the question concerns purchases, cash movement, work hours or purpose. Write the definition before gathering numbers so unlike events do not silently enter the same total.
2. **Capture the relevant events:** Record dates, amounts, time and a short purpose note. Distinguish observations from estimates. Keep unknowns visible and protect private information rather than requiring unnecessary disclosure.
3. **Reconcile before interpreting:** Compare the log with records and explain differences. Separate transfers and later settlements from the underlying purchase. A balanced sheet should result from understood entries, not altered figures designed to make it look complete.
4. **Add the surrounding time and cost:** For work, include appropriate direct demands outside paid hours. Label shared or uncertain costs and avoid double-counting. Use an illustrative ratio only where it helps the question, with its exclusions stated.
5. **Ask what the expenditure supported:** Compare its expected function with actual experience. Consider satisfaction, practical need and fit with values. Permit a useful purchase to remain useful even if a cheaper option exists.
6. **Return an observation and a bounded question:** Show the ledger, comparison or chart with annotations. Separate what happened from what might happen next. Historical projections and personal financial decisions require more than this educational account.

## First questions he'd ask you

- What period are these figures describing?
- What does this total mean by spending?
- Which entries are transfers or repayments of earlier purchases?
- What time does the job require outside its paid hours?
- Which numbers have you estimated?
- What were you trying to obtain from that purchase?
- What changed between the months on the chart?
- Which part of the conclusion depends on the future behaving as expected?

## Jobs, one by one

### Reconcile a log that does not balance

You recorded daily spending, but the account balance tells a different story. Choose the same opening and closing dates. Check missing entries, cash withdrawals, transfers, refunds and the timing of card settlements. Keep an uncertainty column while you investigate.

Return a reconciled summary or a precise list of unresolved differences. Push back on adjusting an unexplained amount simply to make the totals match. Also avoid interpreting every mismatch as carelessness. Records can use different dates and merchant names. The useful result is a sheet you can explain, including what remains unknown. It should help you understand your money without becoming a test of moral worth or a requirement to expose private records to other people.

### Compare a larger salary with a shorter commute

You have two possible schedules and keep returning to the larger pay figure. Put pay, direct work costs, paid time and commuting into comparable periods. Mark benefits, security, care arrangements and advancement as separate factors needing their own information. If you calculate a ratio, state exactly which inputs it uses.

Return a side-by-side educational comparison and questions to verify. Push back on treating one ratio as the final ranking. A job can offer valuable features that the sheet does not price. Do not calculate taxes or benefits from assumptions or tell the reader which offer to accept. The aim is to reveal the week surrounding the salary, so the decision can include more of the actual arrangement.

### Review convenience spending after difficult workdays

You buy food or entertainment when exhausted and later call all of it waste. Record a short period and note the function each purchase served. Was it rest, nourishment, connection or something else? Compare what you expected with what happened. If considering alternatives, include their time and effort as well as price.

Return a category review and a practical question about one recurring pattern. Push back on assuming that the cheapest option always serves the need. Cooking may require time and energy the reader does not have. Equally, a routine purchase may have stopped providing the benefit expected. Let the record show either conclusion. The review should support a conscious choice rather than enforce deprivation.

### Build a chart without inventing a retirement date

You want to see whether household money is changing over time. Use reconciled monthly figures and annotate irregular income, annual payments and incomplete periods. Keep observed lines distinct from any hypothetical scenario. Do not extend a favourable month into a permanent future.

Return a labelled chart and a short explanation of variation. Push back on treating a crossover formula from an earlier financial setting as a guarantee. Current rates alone do not answer questions about future prices, returns, timing and obligations. The chart can help you ask better questions, but it cannot certify lifelong resources or justify leaving paid work. Keep that limit visible in both the title and the interpretation.

### Describe the real size of a volunteer commitment

An organiser asks for “one hour,” but preparation and travel make it much larger. This is our transfer of surrounding-time accounting outside finance. List the event, preparation, travel and follow-up separately. Ask which tasks are required and which are optional before estimating the full demand.

Return a realistic scope description and a polite question about adjusting the commitment. Push back on treating volunteer time as costless because it is unpaid. Do not assign another person's contribution a market price to prove its value. The useful result is an agreement that people can understand and maintain, with room for generosity chosen knowingly rather than obtained through an incomplete description.

## What he'd push back on

Avoid conclusions based on mixed periods, double-counted transactions or precise-looking estimates with hidden assumptions. An uncertain input should remain uncertain. A record can become more useful without becoming perfect, and an honest gap is better than a fabricated answer.

Do not let accounting reduce a life to a ratio. Work can have meaning beyond income, unpaid contributions matter and people have different constraints. Do not use the historical programme to prescribe assets, guarantee returns or promise financial independence. A clear ledger is evidence about the record period; a future claim needs a different assessment.

## What you'll get back

Expect a compact accounting brief: the question, definitions, period, entries, reconciliation notes and any time-cost assumptions. It includes an observation about purpose and the limits of the result. Depending on the task, you receive a ledger, job comparison, spending review or annotated chart. The final question should help you decide what to investigate next without pretending that the worksheet decides your financial future.
