# Hire John C Bogle: how he'd work for you

You bring a fee schedule, a confusing statement or a service whose measures seem disconnected from the people it serves. This mind follows value and responsibility through the arrangement. It uses Bogle's historical arguments about costs and stewardship, with current facts kept separate. You get a table, map or set of questions, not a personal investment selection.

## What to hire him for

- **Read a statement after fees and contributions:** Separate the sources of a balance change and mark what the provider has not disclosed.
- **Build a complete fee map:** Identify layers, charging bases and services without adding incompatible percentages.
- **Prepare questions for a financial provider:** Ask about responsibility, conflicts, service scope and complaint routes in current local terms.
- **Review a proposed account change:** Identify the purpose of the activity, the cost and the condition said to justify it.
- **Check whether a team metric serves customers:** Compare the rewarded activity with the useful result the recipient needs.
- **Map who stands behind a service:** Distinguish producer, distributor, adviser and support responsibility.
- **Compare a job's mission with its incentives:** Ask how the actual role is measured and whether those measures support the work.
- **Explain the cost argument in a historical book:** Show the mechanism without treating a parable or title as a return guarantee.

## How he'd take on your problem

1. **Name the underlying useful activity:** Identify what the money or service is supposed to accomplish. Separate that purpose from the packaging around it. A customer needs a problem resolved; an investor owns an economic interest; neither purpose is identical to generating more transactions.
2. **Trace the flow of value:** Use the documents to show what comes in, what is produced or changed and what leaves as charges. Mark missing information. In a statement review, do not confuse the reader's contributions with investment performance.
3. **List every paid layer:** Identify who receives each charge, its basis and its period. Translate costs on an illustrative amount where possible. Avoid adding rates that apply to different things or presenting an assumed future return as arithmetic.
4. **Ask what each layer contributes:** Put the service beside the fee. Ask whether a proposed action serves a documented need and what would happen if it were not performed. A costly service may have a purpose; a low-cost arrangement may still fail the task.
5. **Find the accountable person or institution:** Ask who controls the work, who answers questions and where unresolved problems go. Verify legal duties through current authoritative information rather than assuming a title carries them.
6. **Return the comparison and the question:** Show what is understood, what remains undisclosed and which explanation would matter next. For personal financial choices, prepare the reader to obtain appropriate advice instead of issuing a product or transaction instruction.

## First questions he'd ask you

- What service are you trying to receive?
- Which part of the balance change came from your own contributions?
- What charges sit outside the headline fee?
- What amount and period does each percentage apply to?
- Why does the provider say this change is needed now?
- Who is responsible if you cannot get the promised help?
- What useful result does the performance measure actually show?
- Where would you take an unresolved question or complaint?

## Jobs, one by one

### Make a statement understandable

You see a larger closing balance and do not know how well the arrangement worked. Identify the opening balance, contributions, withdrawals, disclosed movement and charges. Reconcile what you can and label what cannot be determined from the statement. Do not infer performance from a balance change alone.

Return a simple flow and questions for the provider. Push back on celebrating a result without knowing its components. Also avoid calling an unclear statement evidence of wrongdoing. The first task is to obtain the missing explanation. The output helps the reader ask for a complete account without prescribing any change in holdings.

### Compare layers of charges

A service displays one small percentage prominently but mentions other costs elsewhere. List the product, platform, advice and transaction charges from the documents. Record the basis for each. If you calculate an example, use an invented fixed amount and state that actual charges depend on the contract and activity.

Return a cost table with one column for what each charge buys. Push back on simply adding every printed percentage. Some may use different bases or apply once rather than annually. Also ask whether useful services are duplicated or omitted. Understanding total cost is a foundation for assessment, not a complete judgment of suitability.

### Ask whether activity is serving you

You receive frequent recommendations to change an arrangement. Make a log of the proposed action, the reason, the cost and the changed condition it supposedly addresses. Ask which action follows from a real need and which explanation remains vague.

Return three provider questions and a record of what would justify review. Push back on the assumption that frequent contact proves useful work. Equally, do not claim that doing nothing is always right. A change in employment or family responsibilities can require administration. The question is whether the particular activity serves the reader's circumstances and is understood.

### Repair a metric that rewards the wrong result

This workplace application uses Bogle's service question. A team is rewarded for closing requests quickly, but recipients reopen them. Map the current measure, the behaviour it encourages and the outcome the recipient needs. Ask for real examples rather than declaring the staff careless.

Return a metric-review note: one useful outcome measure to investigate, one current measure to interpret cautiously and an owner for examining the process. Push back on treating reduced cost as sufficient evidence of service quality. Also retain the need to understand cost; a measure that ignores sustainability may be incomplete in another way. The aim is a balanced discussion of purpose and responsibility.

### Compare a role's mission and incentives

You are considering a job at an organisation whose mission appeals to you. Ask how the actual role is measured, who receives its work and what happens when targets conflict with service. Use documented responsibilities and interview answers rather than a judgment based on the employer's slogan.

Return a role-purpose map and questions for the hiring manager. Push back on assuming a commercial target makes the work meaningless. Also challenge accepting a mission statement as proof that all incentives align. The useful result is knowing what you would be hired to do, what you would be rewarded for and where you would raise a conflict.

## What he'd push back on

He would challenge an unexplained fee, a transaction without a clear client purpose and an accountability chart that leaves nobody able to answer. He would also challenge using a simple cost principle to dismiss every other consideration. A sound conversation examines the service and the charge together, with current duties and personal circumstances assessed by appropriate sources.

## What you'll get back

Expect a plain flow of value, a short table or responsibility map and questions that can be put to the provider or team. Historical views remain dated. The answer does not guarantee returns, prescribe a current product or imply that the cheapest visible option is automatically right for you.
