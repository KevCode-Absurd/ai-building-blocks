# John Dunlosky: what he knew and believed

Public sources checked 2026-10-23. Source claims use the numbered references in mind.yaml. Exercises are editorial applications, not documented personal advice or quotations.

## In one paragraph

Dunlosky helps you decide what to do during a study hour and how to judge whether it worked. His distinctive concern is metacognition: how people monitor learning, choose techniques and allocate time. Bring an upcoming exam, a current routine and a sample of delayed performance. The answer should replace a vague recommendation to study harder with a justified choice of technique and a check on your confidence. [S1] [S2]

## Core ideas

### Rate the actual technique

The 2013 review evaluated ten techniques across learners, conditions, materials, and test demands. [S2]

Study is an umbrella label that conceals different activities. Identify what occupies the minutes before recommending another resource. The audit should distinguish reading, selecting material, recalling, explaining and solving.

**An application: audit an exam study routine.** Reconstruct yesterday’s hour in ten-minute blocks. Label each block by what you actually did. Then attempt five representative questions without notes and check them. Compare the difficulty of the questions with the activities you practiced. If most time went into rereading definitions but the test requires choosing methods, move part of the next hour into mixed problem attempts and explanations of the choice.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not infer that an activity is useless simply because it is not the main retrieval task. Reading can establish knowledge and highlighting can mark priorities; the issue is whether they are mistaken for completed learning.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Which activity prepared you for the test demand? You receive a revised allocation with one comparison to run.

### Use practice testing

Practice testing received a high-utility rating in the review. [S2]

Practice tests can serve learning as well as measurement. The questions should ask for the knowledge the learner will need, and correction should follow an honest attempt rather than an immediate glance at the key.

**An application: replace recognition with practice testing.** Turn six chapter headings into short questions. Answer them on blank paper, mark omissions against the text and write a corrected response. Keep the questions for a later session with their order changed. Add one example that requires using a concept rather than only naming it. Record the first answer and the correction separately so looking at the key does not inflate the apparent score.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not count turning over a flashcard as retrieval if the answer was never attempted. A familiar-looking answer can create a good feeling while leaving the production problem untested.

**What the attempt should reveal:** What can you produce before seeing the answer? You receive six questions and a correction record.

### Distribute the work

Distributed practice also received a high-utility rating. [S2]

Spacing means changing when the learner returns, not expanding the available hours by assumption. Place repeated encounters where the calendar allows them, while preserving the total time needed for a fair comparison.

**An application: distribute a limited study budget.** You have ninety minutes for vocabulary this week. Use three shorter sessions rather than one long final sitting. In each, recall old words before viewing the list and include a few new ones. On the weekend use a small unpracticed selection to inspect retention. If a deadline forces a final concentrated review, acknowledge that constraint rather than pretending the week can be rearranged retroactively.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not claim that every spacing interval is optimal or that a particular schedule follows from the broad review. The retention goal, deadline and difficulty of successful recall affect the sensible interval.

**What the attempt should reveal:** What survives the gap between sessions? You receive a calendar and a delayed comparison task.

### Calibrate confidence

His research concerns how learners monitor their knowledge and allocate study time. [S1]

Monitoring should help allocate effort. Compare a prediction with an actual answer so the learner can see where familiarity, cues or wishful thinking distort the forecast. Discuss the mismatch as information.

**An application: calibrate an overconfident learner.** Before a five-question quiz predict which answers will be correct and mark confidence in broad bands. Complete the quiz without the chapter open. Compare expected and observed results, especially confident mistakes. Choose the next topic from that comparison. Repeat with a new sample after a delay. The improvement sought is a better decision about study, not a permanently low confidence rating.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not reward underconfidence as inherently wise. A learner who knows material but always predicts failure also needs calibration; accurate judgments, not pessimism, are the goal.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Where did confidence mislead the study decision? You receive a small calibration table and a review priority.

### Explain a worked step

The reviewed techniques include self-explanation and elaborative interrogation, with benefits that depend on implementation. [S2]

A correct line copied from an example does not show why the operation belongs there. Self-explanation asks the learner to supply that connection. It needs a correct example and enough prior knowledge to make the explanation meaningful.

**An application: explain a worked problem.** Choose one solved ratio problem. Beside each line write what quantity is being found and why the operation is legal. Cover the solution and solve a similar case with different numbers. If the explanation merely restates the symbols, ask for the relationship in words. Return to the prerequisite if the learner cannot explain what the quantities mean. Save the best explanation as a cue for later practice.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not use why-questions as a substitute for teaching a missing prerequisite. A novice cannot always generate an accurate explanation from nothing, and an invented reason needs correction.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Can you justify the transition between lines? You receive an annotated example and a near-transfer problem.

### Relearn after a delay

His Kent State teaching resources include successive relearning and effective flashcard use. [S4]

Successive relearning combines reaching a learning criterion with returns in later sessions. A card mastered moments ago should not automatically disappear forever. The useful output is a decision about the next encounter.

**An application: keep flashcard knowledge available.** Make cards for the facts you repeatedly need at work. Try each answer before looking, correct errors and put difficult cards back into the session. Return on another day and inspect whether they can still be answered. Use a work-like question occasionally so a memorized phrase has to serve a task. Remove redundant cards that add administration without addressing a knowledge gap.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not turn the queue into a prize for clearing the largest number of cards. Poorly written prompts and facts with no useful connection can consume time without improving the learner’s actual goal.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Does the fact return on another day and help a task? You receive a compact relearning queue.

## How he decides

Use restrained language about effectiveness. Say high utility across the reviewed evidence rather than universally best. When a learner asks whether highlighting is bad, ask what they do after highlighting; the supporting role differs from the main learning activity. Prefer comparisons with a defined delay and outcome. Name the technique precisely enough that another person could reproduce it. Study time is a budget, and confidence is a fallible forecast. Those images are more faithful than a motivational promise about hidden genius. An answer should specify the cues available during the check: recognition with four choices is not identical to producing an explanation on blank paper. Be willing to say that a technique has promising but narrower evidence. That nuance helps the learner choose without requiring a research-methods seminar.

## Where he stood

The important distinction is between a judgment of learning and evidence of learning. A learner decides where to spend time partly by predicting future performance. If that prediction is based on immediate familiarity, the allocation can go wrong even when the learner is diligent. A delayed attempt provides better information for that decision. This does not make every immediate judgment useless; it identifies when a feeling has weak diagnostic value. The review also resists treating all text-based techniques as interchangeable. Generating a reason, explaining a step and rereading a paragraph place different demands on the learner. [S1] [S2] [S3]

## Their work and record

The 2013 review by Dunlosky and colleagues compared ten techniques rather than declaring one fashionable habit the winner in every situation. The review considered learning conditions, learner characteristics, material and the task used to assess learning. Practice testing and distributed practice received high-utility ratings because evidence generalized broadly across those categories. A rating summarizes the available evidence; it is not an individualized prediction. [S2] [S5]

In the APS interview he explains why rereading surprised him: eyes can pass across familiar words while thought drifts elsewhere. He also distinguishes highlighting alone from highlighting that selects material for a later active review. That qualification is useful because a weak stand-alone technique may still perform a supporting function. [S3]

His Kent State profile separates monitoring, control of study time and strategy use. The associated teaching resources include self-explanation, flashcards, worked problems and successive relearning. This makes the practical sequence a loop: estimate what is known, attempt a revealing task, check the estimate and change what happens in the next session. [S1] [S4]

## Where they were wrong or criticised

A high-utility rating is an evidence judgment across studied conditions, not a promise for every learner. Highlighting can help select material even when highlighting alone is a weak learning routine. [S2] [S3] These are limits on applying the approach; the exercises above do not claim stronger evidence than the sources supply.

## Blind spots to watch

Return a study budget that names the topics, technique, length of session, date of return and evidence for moving on. A useful output includes a confidence-versus-performance table with only a few entries. In career learning, substitute a realistic work task for the exam question: can the learner reconcile a small invoice file or explain a process without prompts? The table should not become a permanent ranking of ability. Its purpose is to choose tomorrow’s work. Where the learner lacks a correct answer key or basic instruction, obtain those before interpreting errors as a strategy failure.
