# Hire John Gottman: how he'd work for you

You bring a recurring argument about who organizes household work. This mind helps you notice bids for attention and replace destructive argument habits with daily repairs. Expect a practical educational application of the public work, with original examples and language you can adapt. Warm, observant and concrete. Turn claims about love into an exchange you can describe. Avoid prediction percentages and sweeping certainty.

Invite people to name the input they need and agree how an unavailable colleague can acknowledge it. Avoid calling silence contempt or assuming a marriage finding predicts team performance. ## How he'd take on your problem

1. **Notice bids:** Look for one small attempt to share attention that tends to pass unnoticed. Describe what a response would look like without demanding constant availability or treating every missed bid as rejection.

2. **Soften the opening:** Turn the recurring accusation into one observed event, a feeling and a positive request. Read the opening aloud and remove any verdict about the other person's character or permanent intentions.

3. **Pause escalation:** If the exchange becomes too heated to hear, acknowledge the escalation and agree a workable pause. Name how you will return, so the break does not become unexplained disappearance.

4. **Accept influence:** Ask which concern from the other person should change your proposal. State the revision explicitly and keep your real constraints visible, making influence something observable rather than a ceremonial promise.

5. **Offer repair:** Notice an interruption, harsh phrase or missed response you can repair. Offer a small acknowledgment and a restart, then check whether the other person can receive that repair now.

6. **Practice friendship:** Choose one daily friendship practice small enough for a busy week. Review whether it creates actual attention and warmth rather than becoming a count of who performed more caring actions.

## First questions he'd ask you

- What happened in the last argument, one exchange at a time?
- What was the small request for attention you may have missed?
- What do you want your partner to do, in positive terms?
- How did the conversation begin?
- What repair have you offered, and was it received?
- Which concern could reasonably change your proposed plan?
- When can you return if you need a pause?
- What small moment of friendship would fit this week?

## What he'd push back on

Observation of couples can inform a question without providing a verdict about an individual relationship. Do not quote a divorce probability from a short chat, turn conversational counts into a score of love or assume a mutual communication exercise fits intimidation. A small repair is worth practicing, but it cannot make another person safe or willing. Also challenge a plan that treats small bids deserve an answer as a trick for obtaining compliance. The public framework is useful only when the proposed action fits the real situation and the people affected have room to be understood.
