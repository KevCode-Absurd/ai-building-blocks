# Joseph Grenny: what he knew and believed

Built from public sources, last checked 2026-10-23. Source tags identify the public record. The worked applications are original exercises written for this catalogue, rather than claims that Joseph Grenny advised those fictional people.

## In one paragraph
Joseph Grenny examines the behaviors that produce a result and the conditions that keep those behaviors in place. His influence work uses personal, social and structural sources, each considered through motivation and ability. This makes a change effort more specific than asking people to care more. The practical use is a team routine that keeps failing despite repeated reminders, or a personal plan that works only in one environment. A good diagnosis asks what people need to do at the crucial moment and what makes that action attractive, possible or difficult. [S1][S2][S3]

## Core ideas
### Choose the result and the vital behavior
Grenny’s ATD essay asks for a few high-leverage behaviors that materially affect the desired result. A result such as better quality is not itself a behavior, and a broad value such as accountability does not specify what someone should do. The distinction narrows the change effort to an action at a recognizable moment. It also makes it possible to inspect whether that action is connected to the result. [S1]

**An original application:** A team wants fewer last-minute surprises. Instead of asking everyone to communicate better, choose a behavior: when a dependency will miss its date, the owner flags it in the shared handoff before the next planning check. Define what information the flag contains and who responds. The team can now examine whether earlier flags actually improve coordination, rather than merely count messages.

**Where this goes wrong:** An easily counted behavior can still be irrelevant or harmful. Do not reward activity without checking whether it improves the intended outcome.

### Separate motivation from ability
The influence model asks whether someone wants to act and whether they can. Grenny’s change essay uses resistance to cross-training as an example: a person may fear not being competent rather than reject the goal itself. Responding to an ability barrier with persuasion misses the problem. A useful diagnosis asks about both dimensions before deciding which intervention fits. [S2]

**An original application:** A colleague avoids a new reporting tool. Ask them to complete the task and explain where it becomes difficult. They may support the change but not understand how to correct an import error. Provide a worked example and practice on a safe sample. Another speech about the importance of data would leave the skill gap intact and may make the colleague less willing to reveal it.

**Where this goes wrong:** A claim of motivation can conceal pressure to agree. A claim of ability can conceal fear of looking incompetent. Make the diagnosis safe enough to be honest.

### Inspect the social conditions
The six-source model includes social motivation and social ability. Other people can encourage or discourage an action, and they can provide or withhold practical help. Grenny’s change essay points to peer pressure and lack of backup as different barriers. This makes a team habit more than an individual resolution. The group’s norms and actual cooperation can contradict the official request. [S2]

**An original application:** A team says it values careful reviews but praises whoever ships fastest and treats requests for help as weakness. Identify the peer messages and the missing backup. Agree that a reviewer can request a second check for a defined risky case and assign someone to provide it. The desired behavior now has social support instead of requiring one person to resist the room every time.

**Where this goes wrong:** Peer influence can become policing or humiliation. Keep support and shared standards visible rather than making conformity the only route to belonging.

### Inspect rewards and the environment
Structural motivation and structural ability concern incentives and the environment around the behavior. Grenny’s model asks what the system makes rewarding and what it makes feasible. A poorly designed reward can favor the opposite of the official goal, while a missing tool or awkward workflow can block a willing, skilled person. The practical response includes changing the structure rather than repeatedly appealing to character. [S1]

**An original application:** A small service business wants accurate estimates but pays attention only to the number of quotes sent. Staff rush the checks to keep the visible count high. Review the incentive and put the essential information into the quoting workflow. Measure whether estimates become more reliable and whether delays create another burden. The intervention changes the conditions instead of blaming staff for following the signal.

**Where this goes wrong:** A reward can produce gaming when the measure replaces the purpose. Pair counts with the quality and consequences of the action.

### Use a specific story to make the issue discussable
In the feedback-to-leaders essay, Grenny recommends a story that illustrates the effect of behavior and points toward a useful change. The account can connect with experience where abstractions such as low morale do not. The story should remain accurate and specific. Its purpose is to help another person understand and act, rather than manipulate emotion or make an isolated event prove a universal character judgment. [S4]

**An original application:** You want a manager to notice that abrupt changes unsettle the team. Describe one recent handoff, what people did afterward and a moment when a clear explanation helped them recover. Ask for a brief reason and next step when priorities change. The story supports a concrete request. Avoid claiming everyone feels the same or inventing an uplifting incident to make the message persuasive.

**Where this goes wrong:** A vivid anecdote can overpower contrary information. Use it to open understanding and inquiry, not to close the case before the other person responds.

### Redesign the plan when circumstances change
Grenny’s essay on disrupted change plans argues that a new situation can remove old supports and introduce new pressures. A plan that worked at home may fail on a trip without proving the person has lost all progress. The six sources can be inspected again for the changed setting. The useful question becomes what version of the behavior and support is appropriate now, including a modest temporary target. [S3]

**An original application:** A person reliably prepares the next day’s materials at home but loses the routine during travel. The usual shelf, timing and partner reminder are absent. Choose a smaller travel version, put the needed items together before bedtime and use an event that actually occurs in the new setting. Return to the home version afterward. The changed conditions receive a changed plan.

**Where this goes wrong:** A temporary adjustment can become an indefinite lowering of the goal if it is never revisited. State when the context-specific plan ends and what will be reviewed.

## How he decides
- **Choose the result and the vital behavior:** State the result in observable terms and identify the few actions most likely to change it. Specify who acts, at what moment and what they do. Check the link between that behavior and the result before designing incentives.
- **Separate motivation from ability:** Ask whether the person sees value in the behavior and whether they can perform it in the real setting. Observe or rehearse the task where possible. Match the response to the barrier: meaning and choice for motivation, instruction and practice for ability.
- **Inspect the social conditions:** Identify who influences the behavior and who can make it possible. Examine the messages people actually send through praise, criticism and availability. Arrange practical support and a norm that fits the desired action.
- **Inspect rewards and the environment:** List the rewards, penalties, tools and workflow steps surrounding the behavior. Look for a signal or obstacle that contradicts the goal. Change the structure carefully and check for unintended effects on the result and the people doing the work.
- **Use a specific story to make the issue discussable:** Choose a real, relevant episode and describe the behavior and effect without exaggeration. Connect it to a specific request the listener can act on. Keep room for their context and avoid treating the story as the entire evidence base.
- **Redesign the plan when circumstances change:** Before an unusual period, identify which supports disappear and what new pressures appear. Choose a realistic version of the behavior and rebuild the relevant personal, social and structural supports. Review a lapse as data about the new context.

## Where he stood
Grenny’s change essay warns against assuming resistance is simply a bad attitude. A person may be worried about ability, receiving social pressure or being asked to add work without backup. His writing about disrupted personal plans similarly examines changed conditions rather than treating a lapse as a return to zero. The article on giving feedback to leaders adds a communication angle: a specific story can make an experience and a useful request easier to grasp than a general accusation. These methods share an interest in observable behavior and the context around it. [S2][S3][S4]

## Their work and record
Grenny is identified by Crucial Learning as an author and cofounder whose coauthored books include Crucial Conversations and Crucial Influence, previously titled Influencer. The 2023 announcement describes the third edition of Crucial Influence and its personal, social and structural model. His ATD essay gives a compact account of vital behaviors and the six sources. His public advice archive applies these ideas to organizational change and everyday plans. The sources sometimes make strong promotional claims about improved odds of change; this profile explains the framework without converting those claims into an individual guarantee. [S1][S4][S5]

## Where he was wrong or criticised
The influence model is a diagnostic framework rather than proof that every resistant person can or should be changed. Our criticism concerns applying multiple levers without consent or a clear legitimate goal. Rewards, peer pressure and environmental design can become coercive if people cannot question the intended behavior. They can also reward compliance with a poor process. Check whether the target action serves a real shared result, whether the person has a voice and whether the plan preserves relevant standards and autonomy. [S1][S2][S5]

## Blind spots to watch
A team may need a different policy or more resources rather than a stronger influence campaign. The framework should be able to reveal that conclusion. Keep measurement tied to the actual outcome and check for gaming or burden shifted elsewhere. In personal use, avoid treating a habit plan as treatment for a clinical condition. The original applications here concern ordinary routines and work practices, where the user can inspect the conditions and revise the plan. Success means a useful behavior under real conditions, not merely obedience to the designer’s preferred action. [S1][S2][S3]
