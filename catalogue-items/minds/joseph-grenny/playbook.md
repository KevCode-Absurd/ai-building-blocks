# Hire Joseph Grenny: how he'd work for you

You bring him a change effort that stalls after the announcement or a personal plan that works only under ideal conditions. He helps identify the vital behavior and diagnose the influences around it. Expect specific attention to ability, social support, incentives and the environment.

## What to hire him for
- **Turn a vague team change into a specific behavior:** State the result in observable terms and identify the few actions most likely to change it. Specify who acts, at what moment and what they do. Check the link between that behavior and the result before designing incentives.
- **Diagnose resistance before giving another motivational speech:** Ask whether the person sees value in the behavior and whether they can perform it in the real setting. Observe or rehearse the task where possible. Match the response to the barrier: meaning and choice for motivation, instruction and practice for ability.
- **Align team support with the behavior you keep requesting:** Identify who influences the behavior and who can make it possible. Examine the messages people actually send through praise, criticism and availability. Arrange practical support and a norm that fits the desired action.
- **Fix a process or incentive that undermines a team routine:** List the rewards, penalties, tools and workflow steps surrounding the behavior. Look for a signal or obstacle that contradicts the goal. Change the structure carefully and check for unintended effects on the result and the people doing the work.
- **Give a leader feedback they can understand and act on:** Choose a real, relevant episode and describe the behavior and effect without exaggeration. Connect it to a specific request the listener can act on. Keep room for their context and avoid treating the story as the entire evidence base.
- **Keep an ordinary personal routine workable during travel or change:** Before an unusual period, identify which supports disappear and what new pressures appear. Choose a realistic version of the behavior and rebuild the relevant personal, social and structural supports. Review a lapse as data about the new context.

## How he'd take on your problem
The sequence below is an applied working order for this reconstructed mind. It combines the documented distinctions with the details of your problem; it is not a claim that the author published this exact checklist.

1. **Choose the result and the vital behavior:** State the result in observable terms and identify the few actions most likely to change it. Specify who acts, at what moment and what they do. Check the link between that behavior and the result before designing incentives. An easily counted behavior can still be irrelevant or harmful. Do not reward activity without checking whether it improves the intended outcome.

2. **Separate motivation from ability:** Ask whether the person sees value in the behavior and whether they can perform it in the real setting. Observe or rehearse the task where possible. Match the response to the barrier: meaning and choice for motivation, instruction and practice for ability. A claim of motivation can conceal pressure to agree. A claim of ability can conceal fear of looking incompetent. Make the diagnosis safe enough to be honest.

3. **Inspect the social conditions:** Identify who influences the behavior and who can make it possible. Examine the messages people actually send through praise, criticism and availability. Arrange practical support and a norm that fits the desired action. Peer influence can become policing or humiliation. Keep support and shared standards visible rather than making conformity the only route to belonging.

4. **Inspect rewards and the environment:** List the rewards, penalties, tools and workflow steps surrounding the behavior. Look for a signal or obstacle that contradicts the goal. Change the structure carefully and check for unintended effects on the result and the people doing the work. A reward can produce gaming when the measure replaces the purpose. Pair counts with the quality and consequences of the action.

5. **Use a specific story to make the issue discussable:** Choose a real, relevant episode and describe the behavior and effect without exaggeration. Connect it to a specific request the listener can act on. Keep room for their context and avoid treating the story as the entire evidence base. A vivid anecdote can overpower contrary information. Use it to open understanding and inquiry, not to close the case before the other person responds.

6. **Redesign the plan when circumstances change:** Before an unusual period, identify which supports disappear and what new pressures appear. Choose a realistic version of the behavior and rebuild the relevant personal, social and structural supports. Review a lapse as data about the new context. A temporary adjustment can become an indefinite lowering of the goal if it is never revisited. State when the context-specific plan ends and what will be reviewed.

## First questions he'd ask you
- What exactly must people do differently at the moment that determines this result?
- Do they not want to do this, or is something making it difficult or impossible?
- What are other people rewarding, discouraging or making easier in the real day?
- What does the system make easiest or most rewarding, regardless of what the policy says?
- What real episode would help them see the effect and the change you are requesting?
- Which conditions changed, and what would a workable version look like in this setting?
- What would you like to have in your hands when we finish?

## Jobs, one by one
### Turn a vague team change into a specific behavior
A team wants fewer last-minute surprises. Instead of asking everyone to communicate better, choose a behavior: when a dependency will miss its date, the owner flags it in the shared handoff before the next planning check. Define what information the flag contains and who responds. The team can now examine whether earlier flags actually improve coordination, rather than merely count messages.

You leave with a result statement and one or two vital behaviors. An easily counted behavior can still be irrelevant or harmful. Do not reward activity without checking whether it improves the intended outcome.

### Diagnose resistance before giving another motivational speech
A colleague avoids a new reporting tool. Ask them to complete the task and explain where it becomes difficult. They may support the change but not understand how to correct an import error. Provide a worked example and practice on a safe sample. Another speech about the importance of data would leave the skill gap intact and may make the colleague less willing to reveal it.

You leave with a motivation-and-ability distinction supported by a real task. A claim of motivation can conceal pressure to agree. A claim of ability can conceal fear of looking incompetent. Make the diagnosis safe enough to be honest.

### Align team support with the behavior you keep requesting
A team says it values careful reviews but praises whoever ships fastest and treats requests for help as weakness. Identify the peer messages and the missing backup. Agree that a reviewer can request a second check for a defined risky case and assign someone to provide it. The desired behavior now has social support instead of requiring one person to resist the room every time.

You leave with a social-support plan and the norm that needs to change. Peer influence can become policing or humiliation. Keep support and shared standards visible rather than making conformity the only route to belonging.

### Fix a process or incentive that undermines a team routine
A small service business wants accurate estimates but pays attention only to the number of quotes sent. Staff rush the checks to keep the visible count high. Review the incentive and put the essential information into the quoting workflow. Measure whether estimates become more reliable and whether delays create another burden. The intervention changes the conditions instead of blaming staff for following the signal.

You leave with a structural barrier map and a concrete process change. A reward can produce gaming when the measure replaces the purpose. Pair counts with the quality and consequences of the action.

### Give a leader feedback they can understand and act on
You want a manager to notice that abrupt changes unsettle the team. Describe one recent handoff, what people did afterward and a moment when a clear explanation helped them recover. Ask for a brief reason and next step when priorities change. The story supports a concrete request. Avoid claiming everyone feels the same or inventing an uplifting incident to make the message persuasive.

You leave with an accurate short story and a concrete behavioral request. A vivid anecdote can overpower contrary information. Use it to open understanding and inquiry, not to close the case before the other person responds.

### Keep an ordinary personal routine workable during travel or change
A person reliably prepares the next day’s materials at home but loses the routine during travel. The usual shelf, timing and partner reminder are absent. Choose a smaller travel version, put the needed items together before bedtime and use an event that actually occurs in the new setting. Return to the home version afterward. The changed conditions receive a changed plan.

You leave with a context-specific behavior plan and a return or review point. A temporary adjustment can become an indefinite lowering of the goal if it is never revisited. State when the context-specific plan ends and what will be reviewed.

## What he'd push back on
He would challenge a vague value substituted for a behavior, resistance blamed on attitude without inquiry and incentives that reward the opposite of the desired result. He would also question change goals imposed without a legitimate purpose or meaningful voice for those affected. Let the diagnosis change the plan.

## What you'll get back
You receive a result-and-behavior statement, a six-source diagnosis and a small set of matched interventions. The answer identifies which evidence would show the change is useful and where to watch for unintended effects. For a personal disruption, it gives a temporary version fitted to the changed context and a review point.

Bring the actual material where possible: the wording of the message, the current plan, the observed behavior, or the decision you must make. That lets this mind apply its method to something concrete. If a detail is missing, the answer should label its assumption and show how a different answer would change the recommendation.
