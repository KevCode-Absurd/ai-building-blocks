# Kelly Starrett: what he knew and believed

Public sources checked 2026-10-23. Numbered tags refer to mind.yaml. Worked applications below are written by us, not reports of advice the person gave a particular reader.

## In one paragraph

Starrett is a physical therapist, author and co-founder of The Ready State. His public writing emphasizes readiness within real constraints and questions why good intentions fail to become sustainable routines. The everyday applications here concern scheduling, environment and practical self-observation. They do not reproduce his clinical work, prescribe mobility drills or offer treatment for pain. ## Core ideas

### Start from real-world load

His welcome essay defines readiness in relation to real lives and practical constraints. The point is an approach that improves life without adding an impossible list of obligations. [S1]

**Original everyday application: make a movement-friendly desk-day plan.** Look at the day you will actually have, including calls, travel and care responsibilities. Identify a transition where a comfortable familiar movement option can fit. Keep an accessible alternative for days when the setting changes. Do not create a separate ideal schedule that competes with the real one. Review whether the change made the day easier to navigate.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** A feasible routine is not the same as an optimal clinical program. Do not assign a movement dose or imply that fitting a break into work treats pain.

**Make it visible:** A realistic workday plan with a comfortable movement option and fallback. Ask: What does tomorrow really demand of you?

### Measure the thing you mean to change

Built to Move organizes practices with assessments and retests. For this educational adaptation, the useful principle is defining an ordinary observable outcome rather than administering physical tests. [S2]

**Original everyday application: check whether a routine solves its practical problem.** You want fewer frantic starts to an outing. Define the target as having shoes, bag and route ready, not passing a mobility test. Try arranging those items beforehand. On the next outing, note where the delay actually occurred. Revise the arrangement based on that observation. A clear practical check keeps the plan connected to the problem it was meant to solve.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Do not administer the book's physical assessments or turn a score into a diagnosis. Here the check concerns organization and access, not an estimate of health.

**Make it visible:** A before-and-after routine check focused on one practical outcome. Ask: What would tell you that this change helped the task?

### Look for barriers to adherence

Starrett's welcome essay explicitly discusses the gap between theoretical best practice and what real people can do. It describes learning from implementations that did not work. [S1]

**Original everyday application: find why a good intention keeps falling apart.** You intend to take a comfortable break but forget it during calls. Walk through the sequence: the calendar has no gaps, water is beside the desk and the next meeting launches automatically. Change one scheduling condition, such as agreeing a meeting finish time that leaves a transition. Ask whether the environment now permits the intended action.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Do not interpret every missed routine as laziness. Workload, authority, disability and caregiving can prevent participation even when motivation is strong.

**Make it visible:** A barrier map and a change to the setting that removes one obstacle. Ask: Where exactly does the plan collide with your day?

### Treat winding down as a transition

His evening essay looks beyond morning optimization to the back half of the day. It describes routines, technology and room conditions through his own experience. [S3]

**Original everyday application: create a simple evening wind-down cue.** Choose the part of the evening where work or browsing tends to spill onward. Select a cue that fits your home, such as putting work materials away and setting out tomorrow's essentials. Choose a familiar relaxing activity you actually like. Agree any shared-space changes with the household. Keep the cue simple enough to use during travel or a busy evening.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** A personal routine does not establish a treatment for insomnia. Do not copy his timing, products, supplement claims or physical techniques as a prescription.

**Make it visible:** A short evening transition with one household agreement and a travel version. Ask: What signals that the working part of your day has ended?

### Use foundations before buying complexity

The public Built to Move framing emphasizes basic practices alongside broader daily habits. The daily-life transfer is checking whether a proposed tool solves an actual obstacle before purchasing it. [S2]

**Original everyday application: decide whether you need another wellbeing gadget.** Name the practical problem you expect the device to solve. Is it missing time, an uncomfortable setup, unclear instructions or a need for professional guidance? Try the non-purchase change that addresses that problem if one exists. If the device remains relevant, identify access, usability and cost questions. Keep marketing claims separate from the reason you would buy it.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Owning equipment is not evidence that a routine fits. Product testimonials and athlete stories do not establish benefits for a particular reader.

**Make it visible:** A buy-or-wait note tied to a specific obstacle and practical alternatives. Ask: Which problem would this tool solve that your current setup cannot?

### Keep readiness tied to life

His writing presents readiness as being prepared for an unpredictable life. This provides a useful career transfer: make routines support participation rather than consume attention through constant optimization. [S1]

**Original everyday application: plan a sustainable routine while travelling for work.** List the essentials that travel disrupts: familiar activities, meals, work boundaries and contact with home. Choose a few arrangements you can control without a rigid timetable. Find an accessible activity option and a clear way to end work. Keep the plan short enough to survive a delayed journey. When travel changes, preserve the purpose instead of chasing a perfect streak.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Do not turn travel into a compulsory wellness challenge. Rest, assistance and reduced expectations may be the appropriate way to make the trip workable.

**Make it visible:** A compact travel routine organized around access, transitions and recovery time. Ask: What do you want this routine to make possible in your actual life?

## How he decides

Our practical decision sequence, adapted from the distinctions above:

Start with tomorrow's calls, travel, care responsibilities and available resources. Use the actual day as the design brief rather than compare it with an ideal routine. Define what should become easier, such as transitioning between tasks or ending work. Use a visible everyday result instead of administering a physical assessment. Walk through where the intention fails. Look for consecutive calls, inaccessible space, scattered materials or an unclear cue before attributing the problem to effort. Choose a comfortable familiar option and a clear transition that permits it. Favor an environmental change over buying complexity or adding an elaborate checklist. Prepare a travel, busy-day or resting alternative that preserves the purpose. The routine should support participation without demanding that every circumstance become ideal. Inspect the practical outcome you chose. Keep what helped and remove ceremonial steps; the finished day should become more workable, not more occupied with maintenance.

## Where he was wrong or criticised

Commercial program pages describe intended benefits and products; they are not independent validation of every advertised claim. This profile adapts scheduling and environmental reasoning, not clinical assessments, pain protocols, exercise doses or rehabilitation instructions.

## Blind spots to watch

A readiness narrative can make someone feel responsible for conditions they cannot change. Employer demands, inaccessible spaces and limited resources require collective or practical solutions, not simply more personal maintenance.
