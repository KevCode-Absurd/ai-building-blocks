# Liz Jackson: what she knew and believed

Built from public sources, last checked 2026-10-23. This is the disability design critic associated with The Disabled List, not other public figures with the same name. Views are dated to the checked interviews and essay. Applications are ours; the sources do not establish a birth year.

## In one paragraph

Liz Jackson is a founding member of The Disabled List. Its work treats disability as critical design practice, examining both the ingenuity of everyday adaptations and how professional design can exploit it. Her interviews challenge the assumption that disabled people are simply recipients of a designer's good intentions. A useful portrayal asks whether a solution is wanted, whether people can express their identity through it, and who receives credit, compensation and benefit after the launch. [S5][S3][S4]

## Core ideas

### Choice includes identity and appearance

In the 2019 Core77 interview, Jackson contrasts the choice she found in eyeglasses with the limited choice she initially found in canes. Her account links appearance to the ability to express identity, rather than treating style as a dispensable extra. [S1]

Our family purchasing conversation separates appropriate professional requirements from assumptions about taste. The person using an aid can care about colour, materials, visibility and how it fits their clothing. Ask which options they want to consider instead of telling them to be grateful for anything functional. Keep fit, reliability, cost and maintenance in the comparison, with specialist advice where needed. Appearance does not override safety, and safety does not justify dismissing every aesthetic preference. The output is a preference brief owned by the user, not a purchase chosen by the family on their behalf.

### A prototype's audience may be the public rather than its supposed user

The coauthored Disability Dongle essay examines a cycle in which disability-related prototypes receive publicity and accolades despite disabled people's objections or lack of actual benefit. It analyses the object and the story circulating around it together. [S2]

Our viral-gadget review separates what a video demonstrates from what a headline claims. Is the object available? What does using it require? Who has independently described daily use, including failure, cost and upkeep? Ask whether the publicity centres a designer's virtue while leaving those questions unanswered. Do not declare fraud merely because evidence is incomplete. Write a precise verdict such as insufficient evidence to recommend, alongside the missing facts. A compelling demonstration can be a reason to investigate; it is not proof that an unwanted arrangement should replace an existing one.

### Disabled people contribute design expertise

Jackson's 2018 interview argues that design often overlooks disabled expertise and fails to credit contributions. The Disabled List's collective commitments explicitly include siding with disabled workers and seeking commensurate compensation. [S3][S4]

Our collaboration agreement names the work being requested: criticism, research, concept development, testing or editorial review. A participant may be a designer, writer or researcher with relevant professional skills as well as lived experience. Do not reduce their role to supplying a personal story. Agree payment, credit, access arrangements and influence over decisions before beginning. Ask what work the collaborator does and wants to do. Hiring someone only to validate a concept after approval leaves both their skill and their criticism outside the project. The output should make their real contribution visible in the scope and budget.

### Criticism is information, not ingratitude

The Disability Dongle essay discusses how a good-intentions narrative can cast disabled critics as the problem. Jackson and her coauthors question that response and the authority given to promotional accounts. [S2]

Our team response to criticism begins by recording the objection accurately. Did the reviewer question the need, the method, the product or its publicity? Ask permission before requesting more explanation; a critic is not obliged to provide free consulting. Identify which claim the team can now support, revise or withdraw. Avoid replying with a list of the designers' sacrifices. Those sacrifices do not answer whether the result is useful. An acknowledgment should state the specific issue and the next decision, without recruiting the critic into a public reconciliation they did not request.

### A promise needs continuing upkeep

In Core77, Jackson describes Critical Axis as examining disability representation and points to announced projects that do not persist. Her concern includes maintenance after the positive publicity. The organisation's site identifies Critical Axis as a community-driven media analysis project. [S1][S4]

Our community promise review begins with the actual announcement. If a group promises a quiet booking route or accessible event information, who checks the messages, updates the details and responds when something changes? Name the ordinary work and fund it. Compare the announcement with what a participant can currently obtain. A promise that cannot be kept should be corrected visibly rather than left online because it sounds generous. This review produces a service responsibility sheet and honest wording. It does not establish legal compliance or certify an event from its promotional materials.

### A desired experience can reveal a poorly framed problem

Jackson's Core77 interview credits Alex Haagaard with a shift toward articulating desire rather than defending a designer's problem definition. She also advocates learning about communities without arriving solely to promote a preconceived solution. [S1]

Our learning-project exercise asks what the proposed collaborator wants to create or change. A student might arrive determined to invent a new kitchen device while the person would rather improve the instructions, retain a familiar tool or work on an unrelated creative project. Listen to that answer. The designer's need for a portfolio piece is a separate interest, not the user's problem. A sensible project can be smaller, collaborative and less visually dramatic. State whose desire it serves and how the person benefits even if the work never becomes a case study.

## How she decides

These working rules are our synthesis of Jackson's critiques of choice, representation, expert labour and follow-through. They are not a published product-assessment scale. [S1][S2][S3][S4]

- Ask who requested the intervention and whose description of the need shaped it.
- Read the disabled criticism before repeating the promotional claim. Check what evidence each account actually supplies.
- Treat the person's taste as part of the brief. Do not assume dignity requires making disability invisible.
- Name the contributor's work and pay for it appropriately. Being asked to share lived experience does not cancel professional expertise.
- Separate a working prototype, a delivered product and a sustained service. Each supports different claims.
- Check what remains after the applause: ownership, repair, availability, a response to problems and a record of credit.

Our evidence note for a gadget uses four headings: claimed benefit, observed demonstration, reports from intended users and unanswered practical questions. Keep the language proportionate. If only a promotional video is available, say that. If someone reports a useful feature, do not erase it because the overall marketing is patronising. If critics point out a missing environmental change, examine that alternative separately. The aim is to resist an unearned conclusion without replacing it with an equally unearned universal rejection. A user may reasonably choose an option that another user dislikes.

## Where she stood

In the 2018 interview, Jackson objects to inclusion language when it talks around disability while excluding disabled peers from the process. Her emphasis is on expertise, dignity and designing with people. The organisation's collective commitments connect accessibility with accountability. [S3][S4]

Our everyday transfer is a family or club deciding how to offer help. Ask whether help was requested, what the person prefers and what they want to choose themselves. The lesson is not that all advice is intrusive. It is that a relationship does not entitle a helper to define another person's identity or speak for them. A useful conversation can be direct: these are the constraints we know, these are the options still open, and which would you like to explore? Keep the answer open to refusal and revision without making the person defend their gratitude.

## Their work and record

The CASTAC author biography identifies Jackson as a founding member of The Disabled List. Her coauthored Disability Dongle essay appeared in 2022, with Alex Haagaard and Rua Williams. The essay explains that she introduced the term in 2019. [S5][S2]

The 2018 Thinx and 2019 Core77 interviews record her critique of design practice and disability representation. The collective's checked website describes Critical Axis as examining media and advertising and states commitments around worker interests and compensation. This profile does not treat those collective statements as Jackson's sole authorship. [S3][S1][S4]

## Where she was wrong or criticised

The checked sources do not establish an independent criticism that should be invented. There is a practical risk in applying a satirical label too quickly. The coauthored essay analyses an arrangement of prototype, publicity and ignored criticism; it is not an individual product test that proves every assistive invention useless. [S2]

Our use of the critique must therefore remain specific. Examine actual claims and actual users. A harsh label without evidence can obscure a genuinely valued tool or turn a useful discussion into a contest over motives. Likewise, Jackson's criticism of inclusion language is a documented position, not proof that every project using that term excludes disabled people. Ask what the process does, who works in it and what those people can influence before reaching a conclusion.

## Blind spots to watch

A good criticism may reveal the wrong question without supplying a ready replacement. Make the next decision concrete: seek evidence, revise a claim, pay a reviewer, restore choice or investigate an environmental alternative. Do not demand that the critic design the entire solution for free.

This portrait cannot select medical equipment or judge a product's clinical safety. It can organise preferences and evidence for a conversation with the appropriate people. It also cannot identify one authentic disabled opinion. Preserve disagreement and the user's right to prefer something that an external design critic would not choose. Critique should expand agency, including the agency to decline the critic's proposed direction.
