## Hire Liz Wiseman: how they'd work for you

You bring her a team that waits for rescue, a delegation that never sticks or effort that does not create useful impact. She helps inspect the effect of your behavior and redesign the handover. Expect an emphasis on real capability, clear standards and ownership that survives the first difficulty.

### What to hire her for
- **Find the management habit making your team dependent:** Choose one recurring helping habit and ask how others respond to it. Identify the responsibility or thinking it may be replacing. Change the habit in one bounded situation and inspect what the other person becomes able to do.
- **Assign work around people’s demonstrated strengths:** Identify a person’s demonstrated strength and the work where it would matter. Give that capability a meaningful assignment with a clear result. Check whether the role and surrounding process allow the person to use it.
- **Raise the quality bar without making review sessions frightening:** Clarify the standard and remove behavior that intimidates people out of thinking. Give space for questions and revisions while asking the owner to meet the agreed result. Make the challenge about the work and the learning it requires.
- **Run a decision meeting with real debate and a clear finish:** Frame the decision and decision rights, gather relevant perspectives and hold a real debate on the tradeoffs. Make the call after hearing them. Explain what was chosen, why and what information would justify reconsideration.
- **Delegate a project so the other person truly owns it:** Agree the result, boundaries and available support. Release the decisions that belong to the owner and set useful check-ins. When difficulty appears, help them reason through it before taking the work back.
- **Make your work more useful to colleagues and your career:** Ask what contribution is most useful in the current situation. Adjust the deliverable to that need and make it easy for others to act on. Step back when someone else should lead, and close the handoff rather than merely send your part.

### How she'd take on your problem

2. **Find useful capability beyond the job title:** Identify a person’s demonstrated strength and the work where it would matter. Give that capability a meaningful assignment with a clear result. 3. **Create intensity without intimidation:** Clarify the standard and remove behavior that intimidates people out of thinking. 4. **Debate before the decision:** Frame the decision and decision rights, gather relevant perspectives and hold a real debate on the tradeoffs. Make the call after hearing them. Explain what was chosen, why and what information would justify reconsideration. Debate becomes theater when the leader has already decided and hides it. State whether input can change the choice or only its implementation.

Agree priorities and keep the cost of the contribution visible.

#### Raise the quality bar without making review sessions frightening
A team must improve a difficult presentation. State the audience need and quality standard, then let each owner revise their section. Invite concerns about what is not yet understood. During review, discuss the material rather than humiliating the presenter. The pressure stays on making the presentation useful, and people can admit a weak argument before it reaches the audience.

#### Run a decision meeting with real debate and a clear finish
A small team is choosing between two suppliers. Ask each relevant person to examine cost, reliability and switching effort before the meeting. Debate the tradeoffs for a fixed period, then have the owner decide and explain the reasoning. Someone whose preference loses can still see that their evidence shaped the choice or the risk plan. Avoid reopening the whole issue at every later handoff.

You leave with a decision brief, debated tradeoffs and an explicit call. Debate becomes theater when the leader has already decided and hides it. State whether input can change the choice or only its implementation.

#### Delegate a project so the other person truly owns it
You ask a colleague to run a community event. Agree the budget, purpose, date and conditions requiring consultation. Let them choose the working plan and suppliers within those limits. Set a check-in where they bring risks and requests for help. If you quietly rebook everything yourself, you have restored the old dependency. Offer support that leaves the event’s decisions with its owner.

You leave with a result agreement, decision boundaries and a support rhythm. Letting go without resources is abandonment. Ownership needs enough authority, information and access to help to be credible.

You leave with a relevant deliverable with a lighter handoff. Becoming the person who absorbs every loose task is not sustainable impact. Agree priorities and keep the cost of the contribution visible.

## What Liz Wiseman knew and believed

### In one paragraph
Liz Wiseman examines the effect a leader has on the thinking and contribution of other people. Multipliers use their own intelligence to draw out and develop capability around them; diminishers can crowd it out, sometimes with good intentions. The useful daily question is what people become able to do in your presence. Her framework combines space to think with challenge and ownership. It is useful for a manager whose help has become a bottleneck and for an individual contributor trying to make their effort matter to the team. [S1][S2]

### Core ideas
#### Notice the effect of your help
Wiseman describes accidental diminishers whose positive intentions still suppress contribution. An expert who always supplies the answer may make people feel they need not think. A rescuer may prevent ownership from developing. The diagnostic target is the effect of a repeated behavior, rather than the leader’s self-description as helpful. This makes it possible to revise a well-meant habit without deciding the person is malicious. [S2]

**An original application:** A manager rewrites every report before sharing it. The team learns to send rough work and wait for the manager’s version. Ask the authors what happens when they know the rewrite is coming. Try returning the next draft with a clear standard and two questions, leaving revision with its owner. Compare whether the next submission shows more judgment rather than merely fewer manager edits.

**Where this goes wrong:** Good intentions do not settle the effect, but one frustrating interaction does not prove a permanent identity. Work with a specific pattern and its consequences.

#### Find useful capability beyond the job title
The talent-magnet discipline looks for the capability people bring and uses it where it can contribute. The leader’s own expertise is only one resource among many. This directs attention to what someone does naturally and well, including strengths obscured by a narrow role. It is an allocation question: give relevant capability a real task and room to operate, rather than merely complimenting it. [S1]

**An original application:** A quiet operations colleague is unusually good at spotting missing steps in a plan. Invite them to review the onboarding handoff before launch, with authority to identify gaps and propose repairs. Do not make them sit through every unrelated brainstorm. The assignment uses a specific capability where it helps the team, and their contribution becomes visible through the improved handoff.

**Where this goes wrong:** Labeling someone as the detail person can trap them in one kind of work. Treat observed capability as useful information, not a permanent ceiling.

#### Create intensity without intimidation
The liberator discipline joins room to think with a demand for strong work. Wiseman’s manifesto distinguishes a tense environment from an intense one. A leader can ask people to stretch while reducing fear that makes them conceal uncertainty. This is neither permissiveness nor constant reassurance. The useful combination is a clear standard, permission to think and a response to mistakes that keeps learning available. [S1]

**An original application:** A team must improve a difficult presentation. State the audience need and quality standard, then let each owner revise their section. Invite concerns about what is not yet understood. During review, discuss the material rather than humiliating the presenter. The pressure stays on making the presentation useful, and people can admit a weak argument before it reaches the audience.

**Where this goes wrong:** Calling pressure healthy does not make it so. Check whether people can question the plan, disclose mistakes and obtain the support needed for the standard.

#### Debate before the decision
Wiseman’s debate-maker discipline seeks rigorous input before a decision. Her interview stresses that multipliers can be decisive and need not govern by consensus. This separates broad contribution from indefinite agreement-seeking. People should understand the question, the evidence needed and who will decide. A good debate improves the choice and commitment without implying that every participant has a veto. [S2]

**An original application:** A small team is choosing between two suppliers. Ask each relevant person to examine cost, reliability and switching effort before the meeting. Debate the tradeoffs for a fixed period, then have the owner decide and explain the reasoning. Someone whose preference loses can still see that their evidence shaped the choice or the risk plan. Avoid reopening the whole issue at every later handoff.

**Where this goes wrong:** Debate becomes theater when the leader has already decided and hides it. State whether input can change the choice or only its implementation.

#### Hand over ownership and stay available
The investor discipline gives people ownership of results while investing in their success. In the interview, Wiseman uses the image of a baton that must actually be released. A leader who gives an assignment but keeps making every meaningful choice has not handed over ownership. The practical work is to define done, identify support and allow the owner to choose and learn within agreed boundaries. [S2]

**An original application:** You ask a colleague to run a community event. Agree the budget, purpose, date and conditions requiring consultation. **Where this goes wrong:** Letting go without resources is abandonment. Ownership needs enough authority, information and access to help to be credible.

#### Aim effort at the work that is needed
Impact Players distinguishes working hard from creating relevant value. Its five practices include doing the job needed, stepping up and back, finishing stronger, asking and adjusting, and making work light. The emphasis is responsiveness to the real situation. This can help an individual contributor understand why a polished piece of work is not landing if it addresses the wrong need or adds coordination burden for everyone else. [S4]

### Where she stood
Wiseman’s public explanation emphasizes behavior rather than a permanent division between good and bad people. Her interview describes accidental diminishing and distinguishes decisive leadership from a need for consensus. The manifesto gives five disciplines: talent magnet, liberator, challenger, debate maker and investor. The intent is to put capability to use while asking for strong work. Impact Players extends the concern to contributors who notice what is needed, adapt and make the work lighter for others. [S1][S2][S4]

### Their work and record
The Multipliers manifesto describes the question growing out of Wiseman’s management experience at Oracle and subsequent research with Greg McKeown. It reports studying 150 leaders in 35 companies across four continents. Her later books include Rookie Smarts and Impact Players. The Wiseman Group’s current team page identifies her as its CEO and author. These materials establish a public research and consulting program. Its reported capability measures should be distinguished from an independently established promise that a particular manager will double output. [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5]

### Where she was wrong or criticised
In the checked interview, Wiseman explicitly says she cannot claim the reported capability difference means twice the productivity. That limit deserves preservation. The research relies in part on people’s accounts of how much of their capability a leader draws out, which differs from a direct measure of intelligence. Our practical criticism concerns using labels as weapons. Calling a colleague a diminisher can shut down the very inquiry the framework supports. Describe the observable habit, its effect and a different behavior to try. [S1][S2]

### Blind spots to watch
A stronger challenge can become another unfair demand if the team lacks capacity or authority. Let ownership include appropriate resources and access to help. Some people need instruction before they can own a new task; asking questions should not withhold essential knowledge. For contributors, doing the job that is needed should not become an expectation to absorb every unassigned task. Keep boundaries visible and agree which contribution matters now. [S2][S4]
