## Core ideas

### Shopping is part of cooking

Ingredienti frames ingredient selection as a skill that precedes preparation, giving attention to condition and suitability rather than labels alone. [S4]

**Application designed here:** Choose produce for tonight’s dish, not for an attractive name. The tomato sauce is a useful restraint exercise precisely because the ingredient list is short. Make the reference version before adding a favourite herb, garlic or another fat. Observe the simmer and taste it with the pasta. Only then decide whether a variation serves your own aim. The lesson is to understand an existing balance before claiming it needs improvement. [S4]

### Restraint preserves character

Her tomato, butter and onion sauce demonstrates how a small set of ingredients can produce a complete result without extra decoration. [S2]

**Application designed here:** Make the reference version before adding herbs or garlic. Shopping for a vegetable dish begins with the food’s condition and intended use. Choose what appears suitable for the meal rather than relying only on a fashionable label or price. Protect delicate produce on the journey home. A carefully chosen ingredient can lose quality through careless handling before any decision at the stove has a chance to matter. [S2]

### Simple requires care

The sauce depends on an unhurried simmer and a recognizable finished state, showing that few ingredients do not remove the need for judgment. [S2]

**Application designed here:** Observe the sauce rather than relying only on elapsed time. When a short sauce recipe disappoints, ask about time, heat, ingredient condition and the final consistency. A short list does not mean every version of the process is equivalent. Resist adding unrelated flavourings to hide an undiagnosed failure. The first useful comparison is between the chosen reference method and the steps that actually happened in the kitchen. [S2]

### Ingredients have individual roles

**Application designed here:** Ask what each ingredient is doing at this stage. Removing the onion in the published sauce is a prompt to examine purpose. ### Market senses are practical

Her tomato excerpt recommends checking scent, appearance and handling, connecting shopping choices to the food that reaches the table. [S3]

**Application designed here:** Inspect ripeness and protect delicate produce during transport. A pasta meal should be judged at the table as a combined dish. Consider whether the sauce coats and suits the pasta you selected and whether the portions make sense together. Do not let the photograph of a garnish become the standard. The point of learning the preparation is a satisfying mouthful, not a display of how many ingredients can fit on it. [S3]

### Regional differences matter

Her work helped English-language readers encounter a broader Italian repertoire than a single red-sauce shorthand. [S5]

**Application designed here:** Name the dish and its regional reference before generalising. A market-led dinner can start with one good vegetable and a known preparation rather than a rigid shopping list. Keep a short set of dependable pantry accompaniments and decide what the ingredient wants to become. If it is not suitable for the planned dish, change the dish. Paying more attention is often more useful than buying more things. [S5]

### Substitution changes a result

Ingredienti challenges assumptions such as fresh pasta always being better than boxed pasta, directing attention to fitness for the dish. [S4]

**Application designed here:** Choose the form that suits the intended preparation. For a life decision, distinguish an essential commitment from something retained because it is familiar. Choose one obligation to review and name the benefit it still provides. Do not assume that fewer commitments are always better.
