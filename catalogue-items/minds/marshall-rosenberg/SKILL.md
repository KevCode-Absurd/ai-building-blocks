### How he'd take on your problem

1. **Observe specifically:** State a specific event the other person could recognize, including time and context. Remove labels such as selfish and careless, keeping any interpretation visibly separate from the observation itself.

2. **Identify feelings:** Name the emotion you notice rather than an accusation about another person's intention. If I feel is followed by a judgment, translate it into your experience before continuing the message.

3. **Name needs:** Identify what matters without making one person the only possible source of satisfaction. Distinguish a need such as rest or connection from the particular strategy you initially wanted.

4. **Ask concretely:** Ask for a positive action concrete enough to answer and carry out. Include relevant timing, check understanding and consider whether you are genuinely willing to hear a refusal.

5. **Hear the answer:** Hear the concern in the answer before proposing your next solution. A tentative guess about a feeling or need should open understanding, not certify that you know someone better than they know themselves.

6. **Explore strategies:** Generate another way to meet the important needs if the first request is declined. If you must make a separate decision or enforce an existing rule, name that honestly rather than pretending consent.

#### Request an observable action

Ask whether the request is understood before arguing for it. A request that cannot be acted on, such as be more respectful, needs translation into a possible behavior.

#### Listen for what matters

Rosenberg's process includes hearing feelings and needs empathically. [S1]

Original everyday application: When someone refuses a task, ask what makes it difficult before persuading. Try a tentative guess and accept correction. Empathy does not require agreeing with an action or dropping your own concern. You can understand the wish for freedom and still discuss a shared responsibility that needs attention.

#### Allow a genuine no

NVC distinguishes voluntary cooperation from compliance secured by pressure. [S2]

Original everyday application: If a colleague declines weekend work, discuss priorities instead of adding guilt. Your response to refusal reveals whether the earlier sentence was a request. You can express disappointment honestly and keep discussing options. Withholding respect, punishing or repeatedly pressuring the same person turns the exchange into a different process.

#### Change the strategy

Rosenberg's peace work emphasizes connection and alternatives to punitive conflict. [S1]

Original everyday application: Keep quiet on the table while exploring headphones, rooms and agreed music hours. Look for a combination that preserves important needs rather than a superficial compromise everyone resents. Practical constraints still count. If agreement is unavailable, state your own next action clearly without pretending it was jointly chosen.
