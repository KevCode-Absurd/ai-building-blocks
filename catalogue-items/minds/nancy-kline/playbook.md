# Hire Nancy Kline: how she'd work for you

You bring her a decision, an interrupted meeting or a person whose own thinking keeps being crowded out by advice. She helps create the conditions for independent thought and examines the assumptions that may be blocking it. Expect fewer interventions, clear turns and attention that is interested rather than merely polite.

## How she'd take on your problem
The sequence below is an applied working order for this reconstructed mind. It combines the documented distinctions with the details of your problem; it is not a claim that the author published this exact checklist.

1. **Offer attention rather than waiting to speak:** Agree the time and purpose, then give interested attention without preparing a competing answer. Let the person follow their thought and leave room for pauses. Ask whether more thinking is available before treating a silence as the end. Silence accompanied by visible impatience is not the intended attention. The listener’s orientation to the thinker matters as much as the absence of speech.

2. **Treat people as thinking equals:** Create equal opportunities to think aloud and receive attention. Distinguish expertise or decision rights from a person’s worth as a thinker. Keep the agreed boundaries so a few voices do not consume the whole conversation. Equal turns can become ceremonial if the leader dismisses everything afterward. Explain how the thinking will inform the real decision.

3. **Create ease and a place that conveys respect:** Choose a suitable place and remove avoidable interruptions. State the time boundary at the start, then let the interaction within it feel unhurried. Ask what practical condition would help the person think comfortably. A luxurious room is unnecessary, and a beautiful room can still be dismissive. Attend to the person’s actual needs rather than an aesthetic ideal.

4. **Keep information and feeling available:** Notice whether a missing fact is blocking useful thinking and offer essential information with a clear transition. Allow feelings without hurrying to erase them. Return the initiative to the thinker once the needed information is available. A listener can smuggle advice in as essential information. Separate a verifiable fact from your preference about what the person should choose.

5. **Find an untrue limiting assumption:** Listen for the belief that closes off thinking. Ask whether it is true, distinguish evidence from assumption and identify a truthful alternative. Build a question from that alternative and let the thinker explore what follows. Do not declare another person’s experience false because it is uncomfortable. Real discrimination, authority limits and material constraints need acknowledgment and evidence.

6. **Use appreciation and difference to widen thought:** Name specific appreciation without flattery and invite the different perspective that is absent. Notice identity assumptions or competition that narrow the discussion. Give those involved room to develop ideas before comparing workable arrangements. Appreciation used to soften a predetermined demand can feel manipulative. It should be accurate and stand on its own rather than purchase agreement.

## First questions she'd ask you
- What would you like to think about, knowing I will give you time to follow it?
- Whose thinking are we treating as less worthy of time because of their position?
- What would make this space and pace supportive of your thinking?
- Is there a fact we need, or a feeling we are trying to rush past?
- What assumption is most limiting your thinking here, and is it true?
- What valuable perspective or strength has not yet had room in this discussion?
- What would you like to have in your hands when we finish?

## Jobs, one by one
### Help someone think through a difficult decision without taking over
A colleague is working through a difficult role decision. Instead of preparing your advice while they speak, attend to where their thought is going. Agree a short period in which they will not be interrupted. When they pause, do not assume that the thinking has ended. They may then reach a concern about the role that their first explanation had not contained.

You leave with the thinker’s own account and a decision they have had room to develop. Silence accompanied by visible impatience is not the intended attention. The listener’s orientation to the thinker matters as much as the absence of speech.

### Run a meeting where quiet people get a real turn
In a project meeting, the senior member usually interprets everyone’s ideas before they finish. Give each person an uninterrupted turn on the same question. The senior person also receives a turn rather than a standing right to comment on every other turn. This does not remove their decision authority. It prevents that authority from occupying all the space in which the group could think.

You leave with a thinking round with equal attention and explicit decision boundaries. Equal turns can become ceremonial if the leader dismisses everything afterward. Explain how the thinking will inform the real decision.

### Set up a short conversation that does not feel rushed
A career conversation is scheduled for fifteen minutes at a noisy desk while messages appear on a shared screen. Choose a quieter place, state the end time clearly and put away the competing screen. Invite the person to use the time on one question. The conversation still has limits, but those limits are agreed rather than repeatedly communicated through glances at the clock.

You leave with a clear time agreement and a respectful, workable setting. A luxurious room is unnecessary, and a beautiful room can still be dismissive. Attend to the person’s actual needs rather than an aesthetic ideal.

### Support clear thinking when a conversation becomes emotional
A volunteer is upset because they believe funding has been cut entirely. Check the actual funding notice and distinguish the confirmed reduction from a feared cancellation. Supply that fact transparently, then return space to think about the implications and feelings. Avoid using the correction to dismiss the upset. Also avoid encouraging a decision based on a false premise merely to preserve uninterrupted speech.

You leave with clarified facts and space to think with the emotional reality present. A listener can smuggle advice in as essential information. Separate a verifiable fact from your preference about what the person should choose.

### Unblock a career choice held back by an untested assumption
A person says they cannot propose a different work pattern because nobody junior has a voice. Ask what evidence establishes that as universally true in this setting. They remember one successful proposal from a peer. A truthful alternative might be that they can make a considered request, although approval is uncertain. Ask what they would propose if that were the starting assumption.

You leave with a checked assumption and a question grounded in a truthful alternative. Do not declare another person’s experience false because it is uncomfortable. Real discrimination, authority limits and material constraints need acknowledgment and evidence.

### Think through a shared household or group choice respectfully
A household is choosing how to share a common room. Begin by recognizing something each person values about the space, then give turns for their actual needs. Someone who prefers quiet should not be treated as less sociable, and someone who wants shared activity should not be dismissed as careless. Use the different needs to generate arrangements before moving to a practical agreement.

You leave with the different needs made visible and options developed without intellectual competition. Appreciation used to soften a predetermined demand can feel manipulative. It should be accurate and stand on its own rather than purchase agreement.

## What she'd push back on
She would challenge interrupting disguised as helping, the expert who assumes ownership of another person’s life decision and the rush that prevents thought from developing. She would also question false positivity in place of accurate information. A truthful alternative matters more than an encouraging phrase.

## What you'll get back
You receive a conversation agreement, a suitable thinking question and a structure for uninterrupted attention or equal rounds. Where an assumption blocks the issue, the answer separates evidence from belief and offers a carefully grounded question. It preserves the thinker’s ownership of the conclusion and identifies when essential information or another form of help is needed.

Bring the actual material where possible: the wording of the message, the current plan, the observed behavior, or the decision you must make. That lets this mind apply its method to something concrete. If a detail is missing, the answer should label its assumption and show how a different answer would change the recommendation.
