## In one paragraph

Winfrey grew up poor in Mississippi, Milwaukee and Nashville, worked in radio as a teenager and later anchored local news [S4]. Struggling as a news reporter in Baltimore led her into talk shows [S2]. In 1984 she took over a Chicago morning show that became *The Oprah Winfrey Show*, which ran nationally from 1986 to 2011 and was the highest-rated daytime talk show in US history [S6]. She built Harpo Productions, a magazine, a book club that could turn any book into a bestseller, and her own TV network, OWN [S4][S5]. Her method was connection: she believed people most want to feel seen and understood [S1], and she wanted viewers to leave believing they were responsible for their own lives and could triumph [S2]. Critics faulted her for giving airtime to guests with weak health claims and for backing a memoir that turned out to be invented [S4][S5].

## Core ideas

### Everyone wants to be seen

In her 2013 Harvard speech, Winfrey said that across thousands of interviews, the common thread was that people want to be validated and understood [S1]. After almost every interview, she said, guests would ask her if it had been okay [S1]. Even the famous and powerful asked [S1].

How she applied it: in 1991 she said she wanted guests to keep their dignity on the show, even when she disagreed with them [S2]. Her style made the talk show more personal and confessional, a shift critics called "Oprahfication" [S4].

### Failure is a change of direction

Winfrey told Harvard graduates there is no such thing as failure. In her view, failure is life trying to move you in another direction [S1]. Her advice was to feel the loss, learn from the mistake, and then take the next right step [S1].

How she applied it: in Baltimore she was nearly fired as a news reporter and moved to early-morning slots. She said that failure led her to the talk show [S2]. When OWN was widely called a flop in its first year, she called that one of the hardest times of her career, and said she later turned it around [S1].

**Treat a setback as a teacher.** In her 2008 Stanford speech she gave graduates three lessons: trust your feelings, treat failure as information, and find happiness through service [S7]. Her test for a hard moment was one question: what is this here to teach me [S7]. She said that hard times are signals to pay attention to, and that whatever you resist persists [S7]. How she applied it: she built each lesson on a story, such as being fired from her first anchor job, her father's insistence on a college degree, and a crisis at her school for girls in South Africa [S7]. She closed by speaking straight to the graduates [S7].

### Build an inner compass

Winfrey argued that the key to life is to develop an internal moral and emotional guide, like a GPS, that tells you what to do [S1]. She urged graduates to ask what makes them come alive, and to build their story around who they want to be and why [S1].

How she applied it: she linked her own sense of purpose to her charity work through the Angel Network [S1].

### You are responsible for your life

How she applied it: she said she had stopped giving airtime to guests whose message did no good [S2].

### Reading changes lives

Winfrey said that being able to read saved her life [S2]. Her grandmother taught her to read early, and books showed her that other ways of living were possible [S2]. She named books such as *A Tree Grows in Brooklyn* and Maya Angelou's memoir as shaping her [S2].

How she applied it: she started Oprah's Book Club in September 1996, with *The Deep End of the Ocean* [S5]. ### Education is freedom

### Practice until you're at ease
