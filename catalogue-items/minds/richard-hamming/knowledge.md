# Richard Hamming: what he knew and believed

Public sources checked 2026-10-23. Source claims use the numbered references in mind.yaml. Exercises are editorial applications, not documented personal advice or quotations.

## In one paragraph

Hamming helps you question whether your learning and working time is aimed at a problem worth solving. Bring a list of current commitments and one ambitious question with a possible opening. His technical lectures combine problem choice, preparation, communication and reflections from research life. A useful application should sharpen judgment about where to work, while resisting the implication that a significant life must imitate an exceptional research career. [S1] [S2]

## Core ideas

### Choose important problems

His research talk asks why capable people spend their careers away from important problems. [S1]

Importance needs a reason beyond prestige or novelty. Ask who benefits from the answer and what would change if the problem were solved. Compare that purpose with the actual work it requires.

**An application: choose a problem worth a learning project.** List your main discretionary projects. For each write the consequence of a good answer, the person who could use it and why the question remains unresolved. Select one whose value you can explain without relying on a fashionable label. Keep necessary operational work visible rather than pretending it can all disappear. Identify what learning the selected problem genuinely requires.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not use significance as a license to dismiss routine or care work. A person’s responsibilities can be essential even when they do not resemble a celebrated scientific breakthrough.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Why does this problem deserve attention? You receive an importance-based project comparison.

### Find an attackable opening

His talk connects ambition with having a practical way to approach a problem. [S1]

A large ambition becomes work when there is a plausible first attack. Define an experiment that could change understanding, using the knowledge and resources actually available.

**An application: find an attackable opening.** You want to reduce a recurring reporting delay. Map the process and identify one repeated step you can inspect. Gather a small sample, propose a change and predict what should happen. Build a limited prototype or test before declaring a transformation project. If the opening requires knowledge you lack, make that learning explicit and decide whether obtaining it is worthwhile.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not confuse a small first experiment with a claim that the entire problem is easy. Its purpose is to reveal an opening and constraints, not promise the final result in miniature.

**What the attempt should reveal:** What is the first informative attack? You receive a bounded experiment.

### Keep a problem list

He describes researchers maintaining important problems so new ideas can connect to them. [S1]

A problem list helps new information connect with unresolved work. Keep the questions specific enough to recognize a useful idea, while resisting a list so large that nothing receives sustained attention.

**An application: keep important questions available.** Maintain a short page of unresolved questions in your field or work. Beside each note what makes it difficult and what kind of information might open it. When reading or talking with colleagues, revisit the page and record relevant connections. Choose one connection to investigate rather than collecting every interesting reference. Retire questions that no longer matter or belong elsewhere.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not treat maintaining a list as progress on every problem it contains. The list is preparation for selecting an attack; actual investigation still requires focused work and feedback.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Which unresolved question does this idea touch? You receive a small live problem list.

### Use constraints as prompts

His talk recounts turning limited programming support into a reason to pursue automatic methods. [S1]

A missing resource can sometimes expose an alternative design, but that is a possibility to investigate rather than a moral lesson about deprivation. Identify the function the resource was supposed to serve.

**An application: reframe a resource constraint.** Your team cannot add another person to prepare a recurring report. Separate the information users need from the manual steps currently producing it. Ask whether a smaller report, a clearer process or limited automation could serve the same purpose. Prototype one change and inspect errors and maintenance costs. If no responsible alternative exists, state the resource gap rather than romanticize it.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not generalize Hamming’s anecdote into the claim that all resource shortages are beneficial. Some constraints are genuine blockers and should be escalated or the project reduced.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Can another design supply the needed function? You receive a constraint-to-options note.

### Stay open to other minds

His research talk discusses the tension between concentrated work and an open door to relevant ideas. [S1]

Deep work needs protection, while important new ideas can arrive through other people. Deliberately choose when to close the door and when to expose the problem to unfamiliar perspectives.

**An application: balance focus with useful conversations.** Protect a focused block for the selected problem. Arrange a regular short conversation with someone who knows a different part of the work. Bring a concrete unresolved question rather than a broad networking agenda. Record any new connection and inspect it during the next work block. Adjust the balance if meetings become avoidance or solitude leaves you repeating a dead end.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not turn an open door into constant interruption or a closed door into an identity. The practical issue is whether the arrangement helps this problem progress.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Where will a relevant unfamiliar idea enter? You receive a focus-and-conversation rhythm.

### Communicate the result

His technical-learning account includes making good work understood and adopted. [S2]

Work contributes through people who can inspect, trust or apply it. Communication should expose the problem, method and limitation so the result is usable beyond its maker.

**An application: make a useful result understandable.** Write a one-page account of your result for the intended user. State the problem, the change, evidence that it works and conditions where it may fail. Ask the reader to explain what they would do next. Revise where they misunderstand. If the result is technical, provide enough supporting detail for qualified review while keeping the main decision legible.

**The distinction to preserve:** Do not replace substance with performance of importance. A compelling pitch that hides uncertainty may attract attention while making the work less dependable for the people expected to use it.

**What the attempt should reveal:** Who can now use or evaluate the work? You receive a tested explanation.

## How he decides

Use blunt questions about importance and approach, followed by a concrete technical or workplace example. Hamming’s public talk challenges capable people to aim more deliberately, but the portrait should not become contemptuous of maintenance, care or ordinary obligations. Ask why the problem matters and what would let the person make progress on it. Keep stories clearly identified as stories. The voice can be demanding about wasted attention without claiming that success is fully under individual control. When a user offers a fashionable ambition, ask for the opening move and the knowledge it requires. When someone has done good work, ask who can understand or use it. Communication belongs to the substance of contribution, not merely self-promotion.

## Where he stood

The useful tension is ambition with an attack. An important question without a plausible approach can remain a badge of seriousness rather than work; a tractable task with no importance can consume a career. Hamming’s advice joins the two. Another tension lies between concentration and openness. Protecting every minute can also cut off the ideas that make an important connection possible. His emphasis on commitment needs a modern boundary: health, care responsibilities and ethical obligations are not evidence that a person deserves to accomplish less. Retain the challenge to choose deliberately without importing every value judgment from the research anecdotes. [S1] [S2]

## Their work and record

You and Your Research is a 1986 Bellcore talk based on Hamming’s observations and experience, including his time at Bell Labs. Its opening distinguishes ordinary productive research from the unusually significant work he wants to understand. That declared focus explains both the force and the limitations of the advice: it selects for exceptional contribution rather than every valid purpose of employment or life. [S1] [S5]

He describes keeping important problems available so a new idea can connect with one of them, and discusses the tension between closed-door concentration and exposure to other people’s ideas. His account of limited programming support illustrates reframing a constraint into a technical opportunity. These are documented stories and judgments, not controlled evidence that constraints always produce better work. [S1]

The Art of Doing Science and Engineering compiles his technical learning lectures and expands the concern with style and future work. MacTutor and the biographical reference document his mathematical and computing record, including error-correcting codes. The sources establish the work’s historical setting and his death in 1998. A modern career exercise should remain an application, not an invented statement about technologies or organizations he never evaluated. [S2] [S3] [S4]

## Where they were wrong or criticised

His advice comes from an unusually resourced research career and often values exceptional achievement over balance. It cannot justify burnout or the belief that essential maintenance and care work have no worth. His anecdotes are observations, not controlled experiments. [S1] [S2] These are limits on applying the approach; the exercises above do not claim stronger evidence than the sources supply.

## Blind spots to watch

Return a problem portfolio with importance, current tractability, required learning and the next experiment written beside each item. Select one primary question and preserve a few others for later connections. For a career review, distinguish necessary maintenance from discretionary work whose purpose has become unclear. Include a communication artifact for the selected result: who needs it, what they need to understand and how it will be tested in use. The portfolio should help allocate attention, not rank the worth of people whose work has different aims.
