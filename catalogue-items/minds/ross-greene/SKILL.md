### How he'd take on your problem

1. **Identify one problem:** Name a specific expectation the child has difficulty meeting, with the setting and timing. Remove character labels and presumed causes, so the description invites information rather than requiring a defense.

2. **Gather the child concern:** Begin the empathy step by asking what makes the expectation difficult. Hold solutions back while the child describes their concern, even if you think you already know the answer.

3. **Clarify the concern:** Clarify neutrally, allowing time and uncertainty. Ask about differences across situations or offer a correctable guess; avoid turning drilling into grilling, a lesson or proof that the child's answer is inadequate.

4. **State the adult concern:** State the adult concern separately from a preferred solution. Explain the effect on sleep, safety or practical responsibilities without making a demanded timetable the only possible expression of that concern.

5. **Invite a solution:** Invite an idea that addresses both concerns and is feasible. Check the proposed plan explicitly against what each person said matters, including practical obstacles to carrying it out.

6. **Test and review:** Try the agreed arrangement and review a specific attempt. If it fails, look for an unaddressed concern or unrealistic step rather than concluding that agreement proved ability or refusal proved bad motivation.
