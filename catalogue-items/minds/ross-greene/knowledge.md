## Core ideas

### Name an unsolved problem

CPS focuses on specific expectations children find difficult rather than broad behavior labels. [S1]

Original everyday application: Replace defiant with difficulty starting the maths worksheet after dinner on school nights. Remove the presumed cause from the description. A well-worded problem tells you when and where the difficulty occurs without diagnosing why. That creates a question the child can answer rather than an accusation they must defend.

### Plan before the hard moment

Greene distinguishes proactive problem solving from emergency discussion. [S2]

Original everyday application: Talk about getting into the car on a quiet afternoon instead of when everyone is late. Choose a recurring problem that can be discussed safely. Immediate danger requires immediate protection. Proactive conversation gives time for understanding and options that are hard to find under pressure, and it should not become a surprise punishment meeting.

### Start with the child's concern

The empathy step gathers perspective before proposing solutions. [S1]

Original everyday application: Ask what is hard about homework and explore the answer without recommending a timetable. Hold your preferred plan back while gathering information. A child may mention task difficulty, confusion, an unfinished activity or something you did not predict. Understanding the concern is work in its own right, not a formality before the adult's solution.

### Drill without grilling

His training stresses neutral clarifying questions instead of cross-examination. [S2]

Original everyday application: If the child does not know, allow time and ask whether every assignment is equally difficult. Use observation and curiosity without implying the answer should be obvious. A tentative guess can help if it remains open to correction. If trust is low, respect a pause and avoid doing anything that makes the next conversation less likely.

### State the adult concern

CPS includes adult needs rather than treating collaboration as permissiveness. [S1]

Original everyday application: Explain that late starts leave too little time for sleep. Do not disguise a solution as a concern. Sleep, safety and workable family logistics can be concerns; a demanded start time is already one proposed strategy. Stating the concern clearly leaves room for several different solutions.

### Invite a workable solution

The invitation seeks an idea addressing both concerns that can actually be carried out. [S2]

Original everyday application: Choose a small starting task and an available help time together. Check the child's concern and the adult concern explicitly against the proposal. A plan that sounds cooperative but ignores one concern is likely to disappoint. Ask about practical obstacles before committing, and keep the first trial manageable.

### Review without blame

The process treats a failed solution as information about concerns and feasibility. [S1]

Original everyday application: Ask which homework obstacle was missed and revise one part. Do not treat agreement as proof the child can now meet the expectation under all conditions. Look at the particular attempt. A missed concern, an unclear step or a difficult setting can point to a useful revision without a verdict about motivation.

## An everyday worked application

In this original homework case, the problem is difficulty starting the maths worksheet after dinner. Ask the child what is hard and explore whether the difficulty changes with the type of question. Keep the discussion free of proposed solutions until the concern is understood. Then state the adult concern about sufficient time and sleep. Invite an idea addressing both. The plan might include an earlier opportunity to ask for help, but do not prescribe that before hearing the concern. Review feasibility after a trial, and reopen the problem if something important was missed.

Before trying the example, define what a useful result would look like for the people involved. Separate a change in your own behavior from an outcome requiring someone else's participation. An invitation can be declined; a boundary can be maintained without agreement; a practical obligation may need a direct decision. These are different tasks, so do not judge all of them by whether the conversation feels pleasant immediately.

## Where the method was criticised or needs caution

The sources above primarily present the person's own account and institutional record. They should not be mistaken for independent proof that every application works. The cautions here are editorial limits on this educational adaptation, rather than invented reports of named critics or a claim of scientific consensus.

A short conversation cannot establish why a child struggles or provide clinical assessment. Serious safety concerns require suitable immediate support. Proactive problem solving should not become an interrogation, and a child's silence should not be treated as defiance. The public training emphasizes curiosity, specific problems and revising incomplete solutions.

## Blind spots to watch

For a small adult project, adapt the distinction between an unmet expectation and a character accusation. Ask what obstructs a specific handover, state the downstream concern and generate a feasible plan. Label this an ordinary collaboration adaptation, not a validated extension of CPS to every workplace. Keep role authority and fixed commitments visible.

For a council, pair this lens with someone who checks facts, options and practical constraints. Bring the relevant schedule, commitments or task itself into the discussion. A relationship interpretation can clarify how people speak while leaving a shortage of time, money, space or support untouched. Keep those limits visible and do not turn every material difficulty into a communication failure.
