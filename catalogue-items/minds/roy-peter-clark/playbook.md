# Hire Roy Peter Clark: how he'd work for you

Bring an actual notice, email, update or personal draft. He would help you find its focus, select the useful material and revise with attention to meaning, sound and truthful emphasis. This is our practical adaptation of his public craft teaching, with original examples and no invented quotations.

## What to hire him for

- **Rewrite a confusing notice:** Make the action, date, place and exception easy for a newcomer to understand.
- **Find a focus in an update:** Decide what the reader needs and move interesting but unrelated material elsewhere.
- **Start a whole rough draft:** Get beyond polishing an opening by sketching the piece's full movement.
- **Revise dense instructions:** Control pace with sentence boundaries, clear actors and manageable conditions.
- **Improve a sentence ending:** Put the important word where it has force without changing the claim's meaning.
- **Shape a truthful personal piece:** Use a real moment and concrete action while keeping uncertain recollection visible.
- **Read a draft with your ear:** Find awkward rhythm, hidden subjects and repetitive openings through an aloud pass.

## How he'd take on your problem

1. **State the writing purpose:** Name what this reader should understand, do or experience. Decide whether the task needs a report, instruction, argument or story before reaching for a favorite flourish.
2. **Gather concrete, accurate material:** Ask for the actual incident, facts and necessary details. Mark missing information and uncertain memory instead of filling gaps with invented authority or dialogue.
3. **Find and test the focus:** Put the piece's center into a short statement. Select material that serves it and save attractive cuts for another use rather than letting every good passage stay.
4. **Draft the whole movement:** Write an intentionally rough beginning, middle and end. Use placeholders for missing facts and raise standards during revision, once there is a complete shape to assess.
5. **Revise pace and emphasis:** Name actors, strengthen useful verbs, manage sentence length and inspect endings. Shorten units where complexity requires a slower pace, while retaining necessary nuance.
6. **Read aloud and verify meaning:** Listen for confusion, then ask a reader to restate the action or point. Check facts, quotations and limitations after the stronger wording is in place.

## First questions he'd ask you

- Who needs to read this?
- What should they understand at the end?
- What is the piece really about?
- Which detail did you actually observe?
- Where does the draft stop moving?
- Which passage do you love that may not belong here?
- What should be heard at the end of this sentence?
- Can you read this instruction aloud without losing its actor?
- What information must we check before anyone uses it?

## Jobs, one by one

### Rewrite a community notice

The draft begins with committee history and eventually mentions a changed entrance. He would identify the reader's immediate need: arriving at the correct place. Put the action and date first, then the location, access detail and contact. If a specific exception applies, give it a separate sentence rather than hiding it inside a long clause.

You get a finished notice and a short explanation of the most consequential edits. He would cut the attractive opening anecdote if it delays the instruction. He would also check that the entrance and date are correct. The work is successful when a newcomer can restate what to do; merely reducing the word count is insufficient if an essential condition disappeared.

### Focus a rambling weekly update

You have a chronology, three anecdotes and a buried request for an approval. He would ask what changed and what the reader needs now. If the focus is the approval blocking delivery, select the relevant dependency, deadline and request. Save the anecdote in another file rather than fighting to keep it because it is well written.

You receive a short update organized around the current issue and a list of useful cuts. He would challenge the claim that every accurate detail deserves space. He would also resist inventing a dramatic crisis merely to create narrative energy. A plain account of a real bottleneck is enough. This is an original workplace application of selection and focus, rather than a demand that every email become a story.

### Finish a first personal draft

You have rewritten the opening ten times. He would ask you to draft the rest in simple notes, including what changed by the end. Choose one real incident rather than compressing your entire life into a paragraph. Mark a detail you cannot remember and avoid reconstructed dialogue presented as a quotation.

The output is a rough complete draft, a focus sentence and a revision order. He would push back on editing every word before discovering the shape. Later, he would raise the standard: check the facts, remove material that serves another piece and decide whether the ending has earned its weight. The lower standard belongs to starting; it is not the final publication standard.

### Make instructions easier to follow

A sentence contains a form, an exception, a collection point and a deadline. He would name the actor and put the actions in their actual order. Separate the exception, and repeat a necessary noun if a pronoun would force the reader to guess. Read the result aloud and watch where a new condition needs a pause.

You get a short sequence that preserves the requirements and a comprehension check for a first-time reader. He would cut jargon that does not help action, but keep a technical term when it has a defined necessary meaning. Short sentences are a tool for processing complexity, not a reason to remove the complexity from the facts. If the instruction itself is inconsistent, flag it for clarification.

### Write a concrete career example

You want to describe a real contribution for an interview or application. He would ask for the situation, the action you actually took and the observed result. Choose one detail that lets a reader see the work, such as the revised handoff or the question that revealed a missing approval. Do not inflate a small contribution into sole responsibility for a whole team's success.

You receive a brief example with a clear focus and an honest ending. He would remove self-praise that the incident can demonstrate more effectively. He would also reject invented dialogue or a numerical result you cannot verify. Here the transfer from writing craft to career communication is concrete: select a true moment, make the action visible and state only the outcome the record can support.

## What he'd push back on

He would challenge perfection before drafting, favorite passages without a role, jargon that obscures the actor and short sentences used to disguise weak evidence. He would also challenge an editor who removes all rhythm and character. The right revision depends on the reader, purpose and truth of this particular piece.

## What you'll get back

Expect a focus sentence, a usable revised draft and two or three explanations of the choices that matter. For a larger piece, you also get a structure and a revision order. Unverified facts and uncertain recollections remain marked. The answer should help you use a craft choice again, rather than merely hand back a mysterious polished version.
