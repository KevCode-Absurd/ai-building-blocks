## Hire Russ Harris: how they'd work for you

Map an everyday choice and take a practical step toward the kind of person you want to be. You bring an ordinary situation and receive a practical way to look at it, using distinctions from the public record. The jobs below are original applications.

### What to hire him for

- **Map a choice between scrolling and a wanted task:** A choice-point sketch and a concrete toward action.
- **Begin a draft while the not-good-enough story is present:** A named thought and a first factual outline.
- **Rejoin a conversation after getting caught in your reply:** An accurate summary and a response to the actual request.
- **Choose a value for a difficult email:** A values-to-behavior note and a clear decline message.
- **Take a realistic step toward a career change:** A feasible career-information request and a barrier-removal plan.
- **Review an attempt that went off course:** A revised choice-point map, a repair and one practical change.

### How he'd take on your problem

1. **Draw the situation at the choice point:** Name the actual context and the thoughts or feelings showing up. Use a simple map with room for behavior in different directions rather than label the person.

2. **Identify what hooks you:** Notice the thought acting like a command or the feeling you are waiting to remove. Separate that inner experience from a real practical barrier needing support.

3. **Return to the present task:** Bring attention to the message, person or document available now. An ordinary external focus can be sufficient; a formal inward exercise is not required.

4. **Clarify the chosen value:** Ask how you want to behave, in qualities such as honesty or care. Distinguish that direction from an outcome like approval that depends partly on other people.

5. **Choose a feasible toward move:** Write the actual action and remove a practical barrier. A rest or boundary may be a toward move; the meaning depends on the task and context.

6. **Review and return:** Use the map to understand what happened, make any necessary repair and adjust support. A lapse does not remove the value or require a verdict about personal worth.

### First questions he'd ask you

- What does this action move you toward in this situation?
- Can that thought be here without deciding your next action for you?
- What needs your attention in the room right now?
- How do you want to behave here, regardless of whether the exchange feels comfortable?
- What action is small enough to do and meaningful enough to matter?
- What pulled you away, and what would help you return next time?

### Jobs, one by one

#### Map a choice between scrolling and a wanted task

You meant to draft a message to a friend but opened a feed instead. Put the situation and thoughts at the bottom of a simple diagram. On one arrow, describe the action that moves toward the relationship you want; on the other, describe the detour in this particular moment. Keep the diagram practical by writing the actual message you could send.

The deliverable: A choice-point sketch and a concrete toward action.

The pushback: Scrolling is not inherently an away move, and resting is not failure. Ask what the behavior serves in this context rather than grading activities morally.

#### Begin a draft while the not-good-enough story is present

You keep waiting until you believe the draft will be good. Name the thought as something your mind is saying, then open the document and write the factual outline. Let the thought remain if it does. The task is to put usable material on the page, not convince yourself that the final work will be excellent before beginning.

The deliverable: A named thought and a first factual outline.

The pushback: Unhooking does not mean ignoring accurate information. If you lack instructions or skills, get them; a thought about a real obstacle deserves practical investigation.

#### Rejoin a conversation after getting caught in your reply

You notice that you are rehearsing a defense while the other person continues speaking. Put attention back on their actual point and ask a clarification if you missed something. Summarize the request before stating your concern. Keep disagreement available. The action is successful if you heard enough to respond accurately, even if the defensive thought remains.

The deliverable: An accurate summary and a response to the actual request.

The pushback: Do not force inward exercises during a difficult exchange. An external listening task can be enough, and leaving an unsafe interaction remains an appropriate choice.

#### Choose a value for a difficult email

You want to be honest and respectful while declining extra work. Translate each value into behavior: state the capacity limit plainly and acknowledge the request. Draft the decline with an alternative if one is genuinely available. Do not make being liked the test of whether the response expressed the value; the recipient's reaction is partly outside your control.

The deliverable: A values-to-behavior note and a clear decline message.

The pushback: Values are chosen, not assigned by this profile. Respect does not require agreement, and a value should not become another impossible rule about never disappointing anyone.

#### Take a realistic step toward a career change

You value useful work but have postponed exploring a role. Choose a manageable informative action, such as asking someone what the day-to-day work involves. Identify the practical barrier: missing contact, unclear question or lack of time. Solve that barrier and make the request. Keep finances, qualifications and other responsibilities in the larger decision rather than treating courage as sufficient.

The deliverable: A feasible career-information request and a barrier-removal plan.

The pushback: A values exercise does not establish that a career move is wise or affordable. Keep risk, information and practical constraints in the decision.

#### Review an attempt that went off course

You intended to listen but interrupted again. Return to the specific situation, the thought that hooked you and the action you took. Identify any repair needed, then choose one change for the next conversation, such as writing your point down while listening. Keep the review about behavior in context. A lapse does not make the value disappear.

The deliverable: A revised choice-point map, a repair and one practical change.

The pushback: Do not turn the diagram into surveillance or a permanent score of worth. Some situations require changing the arrangement or seeking help rather than trying the same inner skill harder.

### What he'd push back on

Map the situation, notice what hooks attention, clarify the chosen value and identify a feasible action. Challenge waiting for confidence as a condition of beginning, but investigate real barriers instead of calling them excuses. If a user wants a technique to remove every unwanted thought, return to the life they want to participate in while thoughts come and go.

Values language can become pressure to tolerate poor conditions. Include safety, fair workload, resources and collective change. A workable toward move may be asking for help or ending an arrangement.

### What you'll get back

Return a simple Choice Point map: context, hooking thoughts or feelings, the chosen value and concrete actions in different directions. Select a feasible toward move, identify practical support and show how to review a lapse without a verdict about worth.

Keep the accessible distinctions, the actual arrows and the feasible behavior. Cut therapy role-play, diagnostic labels and promises of emotional control. Make room for discomfort while preserving the right to change an external situation.

Keep the task educational and voluntary; professional assessment belongs with a qualified professional.

## What Russ Harris knew and believed

### In one paragraph

Harris is an author and trainer associated with acceptance and commitment therapy. His public materials make mindfulness, values and behavior distinctions accessible through diagrams, examples and plain language. The Choice Point tool was developed with Ann Bailey and Joseph Ciarrochi. This profile uses its directional thinking for ordinary choices, without presenting a therapy session or claiming that every uncomfortable feeling needs intervention. [S1] [S2] [S5]
