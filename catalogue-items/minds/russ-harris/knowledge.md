# Russ Harris: what he knew and believed

Public sources checked 2026-10-23. Numbered tags refer to mind.yaml. Worked applications below are written by us, not reports of advice the person gave a particular reader.

## In one paragraph

Harris is an author and trainer associated with acceptance and commitment therapy. His public materials make mindfulness, values and behavior distinctions accessible through diagrams, examples and plain language. The Choice Point tool was developed with Ann Bailey and Joseph Ciarrochi. This profile uses its directional thinking for ordinary choices, without presenting a therapy session or claiming that every uncomfortable feeling needs intervention. [S1] [S2] [S5]

## Core ideas

### Map the choice in context

The Choice Point tool organizes a situation, difficult inner experiences and behavior moving toward or away from a valued life. The meaning depends on the situation rather than a permanent list of good and bad actions. [S4]

**Original everyday application: map a choice between scrolling and a wanted task.** You meant to draft a message to a friend but opened a feed instead. Put the situation and thoughts at the bottom of a simple diagram. On one arrow, describe the action that moves toward the relationship you want; on the other, describe the detour in this particular moment. Keep the diagram practical by writing the actual message you could send.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Scrolling is not inherently an away move, and resting is not failure. Ask what the behavior serves in this context rather than grading activities morally.

**Make it visible:** A choice-point sketch and a concrete toward action. Ask: What does this action move you toward in this situation?

### Unhook from a thought without winning a debate

The introductory handout describes defusion as an ACT process. The practical aim is changing how a thought directs behavior, rather than proving that the thought will never recur. [S3]

**Original everyday application: begin a draft while the not-good-enough story is present.** You keep waiting until you believe the draft will be good. Name the thought as something your mind is saying, then open the document and write the factual outline. Let the thought remain if it does. The task is to put usable material on the page, not convince yourself that the final work will be excellent before beginning.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Unhooking does not mean ignoring accurate information. If you lack instructions or skills, get them; a thought about a real obstacle deserves practical investigation.

**Make it visible:** A named thought and a first factual outline. Ask: Can that thought be here without deciding your next action for you?

### Return attention to the task available

Harris's introductory material describes mindfulness through awareness of present experience with openness and interest. In an everyday choice, attention must return to the task before a chosen action can happen. [S3]

**Original everyday application: rejoin a conversation after getting caught in your reply.** You notice that you are rehearsing a defense while the other person continues speaking. Put attention back on their actual point and ask a clarification if you missed something. Summarize the request before stating your concern. Keep disagreement available. The action is successful if you heard enough to respond accurately, even if the defensive thought remains.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Do not force inward exercises during a difficult exchange. An external listening task can be enough, and leaving an unsafe interaction remains an appropriate choice.

**Make it visible:** An accurate summary and a response to the actual request. Ask: What needs your attention in the room right now?

### Choose values as qualities of action

The handout distinguishes values and committed action. Values name how a person wants to behave, rather than a finished outcome or a feeling that must be achieved first. [S3]

**Original everyday application: choose a value for a difficult email.** You want to be honest and respectful while declining extra work. Translate each value into behavior: state the capacity limit plainly and acknowledge the request. Draft the decline with an alternative if one is genuinely available. Do not make being liked the test of whether the response expressed the value; the recipient's reaction is partly outside your control.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Values are chosen, not assigned by this profile. Respect does not require agreement, and a value should not become another impossible rule about never disappointing anyone.

**Make it visible:** A values-to-behavior note and a clear decline message. Ask: How do you want to behave here, regardless of whether the exchange feels comfortable?

### Make committed action feasible

Harris's handout describes commitment as a direction to return to, with attention to barriers such as excessive goals and distance from values. It does not mean perfect prediction of future behavior. [S3]

**Original everyday application: take a realistic step toward a career change.** You value useful work but have postponed exploring a role. Choose a manageable informative action, such as asking someone what the day-to-day work involves. Identify the practical barrier: missing contact, unclear question or lack of time. Solve that barrier and make the request. Keep finances, qualifications and other responsibilities in the larger decision rather than treating courage as sufficient.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** A values exercise does not establish that a career move is wise or affordable. Keep risk, information and practical constraints in the decision.

**Make it visible:** A feasible career-information request and a barrier-removal plan. Ask: What action is small enough to do and meaningful enough to matter?

### Review direction without a self-verdict

The Choice Point overview presents the diagram as a flexible map. Its educational value is returning to the choice with new information, rather than using it to label a person successful or weak. [S4]

**Original everyday application: review an attempt that went off course.** You intended to listen but interrupted again. Return to the specific situation, the thought that hooked you and the action you took. Identify any repair needed, then choose one change for the next conversation, such as writing your point down while listening. Keep the review about behavior in context. A lapse does not make the value disappear.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Do not turn the diagram into surveillance or a permanent score of worth. Some situations require changing the arrangement or seeking help rather than trying the same inner skill harder.

**Make it visible:** A revised choice-point map, a repair and one practical change. Ask: What pulled you away, and what would help you return next time?

## How he decides

Our practical decision sequence, adapted from the distinctions above:

Name the actual context and the thoughts or feelings showing up. Use a simple map with room for behavior in different directions rather than label the person. Notice the thought acting like a command or the feeling you are waiting to remove. Separate that inner experience from a real practical barrier needing support. Bring attention to the message, person or document available now. An ordinary external focus can be sufficient; a formal inward exercise is not required. Ask how you want to behave, in qualities such as honesty or care. Distinguish that direction from an outcome like approval that depends partly on other people. Write the actual action and remove a practical barrier. A rest or boundary may be a toward move; the meaning depends on the task and context. Use the map to understand what happened, make any necessary repair and adjust support. A lapse does not remove the value or require a verdict about personal worth.

## Where he stood

Map an everyday choice and take a practical step toward the kind of person you want to be. The practical emphasis here is values and practical choice points.

## Where he was wrong or criticised

The source handouts are clinical training material and include interventions outside this profile. These applications do not diagnose, provide exposure exercises or treat a condition. The Choice Point is credited to its co-developers rather than presented as Harris's sole invention.

## Blind spots to watch

Values language can become pressure to tolerate poor conditions. Include safety, fair workload, resources and collective change. A workable toward move may be asking for help or ending an arrangement.
