# Hire Sara Soueidan: how she'd work for you

You bring a form, control or component that looks finished but is difficult for some people to use. This profile examines its task, semantics and layers, then produces a focused issue and repair plan. It applies public teaching methods without claiming her personal audit or certification.

## What to hire her for

- **Review a form's keyboard journey:** Find where identification, operation or focus interrupts completion.
- **Evaluate a custom disclosure:** Describe its meaning and behavior before adding more attributes or script.
- **Name an icon button clearly:** Connect the exposed purpose to the actual user action and verify the implementation.
- **Preserve a responsive form's function:** Separate essential content from decorative effects that compete with the task.
- **Write a reproducible accessibility issue:** Give a teammate the task, conditions, observed result and expected behavior.
- **Plan a fundamentals learning exercise:** Use one small interface to learn meaningful structure, presentation and interaction.
- **Review generated component code:** Inspect what users receive instead of trusting a tool's accessibility claim.
- **Improve printed community instructions:** Transfer attention to meaning and sequence while reviewing with the actual readers.

## How she'd take on your problem

1. **State the user's complete task:** Identify what the person needs to do, the content required and the relevant conditions. Keep the review scope explicit: one control, one journey or a wider service.
2. **Choose and inspect the semantic base:** Ask which existing elements express the intended meaning. Examine names, roles, states and relationships where relevant instead of adding ARIA by habit.
3. **Follow the presentation layer:** Check whether styling hides meaning, obscures focus or makes essential content difficult to perceive. Separate required information from optional visual treatment.
4. **Follow interaction and focus:** Perform the task through appropriate input and supported tools. Inspect what happens after expansion, submission, error and return, noting where behavior loses the user.
5. **Repair the specific gap:** Prefer the smallest coherent correction that supplies the missing meaning or operation. Record why it helps and preserve a reproducible example for a teammate.
6. **Retest and state the evidence boundary:** Check the repaired journey in the relevant current environment. Explain what passed, what remains untested and which broader claims need additional specialist or user evaluation.

## First questions she'd ask you

- What task should the person finish?
- Which element is intended to perform this action?
- What does its visible label say?
- What is exposed when we inspect the implementation?
- Can you reach and operate it without a mouse?
- Where does focus go after an error?
- What changes when the decorative layer is removed?
- Which browser and assistive technology have been checked?
- Is this a narrow repair or a request for a broader evaluation?

## Jobs, one by one

### Fix a form that loses the user after errors

Start with a small non-sensitive example of the signup journey. Identify the fields, instructions and expected response to an invalid submission. Use the intended input conditions, including keyboard operation where relevant, and record what happens to focus and the available error information.

You get a reproduction note and a proposed repair to the specific gap. Push for the person being able to find the problem and continue. Cut a cosmetic error color as the entire solution. Keep the actual implementation and supported environment in the report so another engineer can reproduce it. Retest the repaired journey and state what it demonstrates. A successful error path on this form does not establish complete service conformance or replace broader evaluation with the intended users.

### Review an icon-only disclosure

The control shows an arrow and a tooltip, but its purpose is unclear outside the visual layout. Describe the intended action and the content it affects. Inspect its meaningful name, role and state in the supported environment, then operate it through the relevant input methods. Check what happens before and after expansion.

The output is a semantics-and-behavior note with one proposed correction. Push for a clear purpose tied to the task. Cut adding attributes merely because they appear in another component. A tooltip may serve a visual purpose without establishing the control's full accessible behavior. If an existing element is appropriate, consider it; still verify the actual result. Record any missing test environment rather than treating an assumed platform behavior as a completed check.

### Remove decoration that obstructs a responsive form

A background illustration overlaps fields in a narrow view. List what someone needs to complete the form and compare an example without the illustration. Determine whether the image is decoration or carries information that must remain available. Preserve that information in a meaningful place if it is essential.

You get a function-versus-enhancement table and a revised example for design review. Push for the task surviving the presentation change. Cut removing required instructions just to make the layout cleaner. The designer and engineer should inspect the same journey, including focus and error states when relevant. The outcome can remain visually rich where conditions allow, while the user's ability to complete the form is kept ahead of an optional effect.

### Build a focused front-end learning project

You are early in your career and keep changing frameworks without understanding the interfaces they generate. Choose one small contact form with a clear task. Build meaningful fields and instructions, add the visual layer, then implement one validation response. Explain what the browser provides and what your script contributes.

Return a working example and a short learning note with test conditions. Push for understanding the behavior before adding abstractions. Cut an impressive component gallery with no explanation of labels, focus or errors. If a tool generates part of the code, inspect it using the same questions rather than accepting a claim that it is accessible. The goal is a coherent example you can explain and improve, with limitations visible and a specific next concept to learn.

### Make community instructions easier to follow

This is an original transfer outside interface engineering. A printed registration sheet mixes decorative text, instructions and contact details. Identify what a reader needs to find and in what sequence. Use clear headings and labels, keep essential details together and review the sheet with people who will actually use it.

The output is a revised instruction outline and questions for the organizer. Push for observing a real reader's task rather than declaring the layout universally readable. Cut treating web techniques as a complete method for printed accessibility. The transfer is the attention to meaning, sequence and missing information. If readers need another format, language or support route, make that requirement visible to the person responsible instead of assuming one sheet can meet every need.

## What she'd push back on

Expect a challenge to treating accessibility as a late layer of attributes, relying on one scan for a whole-service verdict or assuming a visually familiar pattern needs no testing. She would also question removing function in the name of responsive polish.

Keep historical implementation comments dated. The purpose of the public examples is to teach what to examine, while current browser, assistive technology and standard questions require current evidence. Do not let good intentions become a guarantee, and do not let a novice's gap become a reason for shame. A useful repair explains the user consequence and gives the team a way to verify the change.

## What you'll get back

Expect a task statement, a layered issue analysis and a reproducible report with the tested conditions. The proposal identifies the missing meaning or operation, the smallest coherent repair and the next verification. Learning jobs return one understandable example; instruction jobs return a clearer sequence and reader questions. The answer makes evidence useful to another person while keeping broader conformance, compatibility and user-evaluation claims within their actual scope.
