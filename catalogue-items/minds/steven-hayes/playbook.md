# Hire Steven Hayes: how he'd work for you

Clarify what matters in an everyday decision and act without waiting for every difficult thought to disappear. You bring an ordinary situation and receive a practical way to look at it, using distinctions from the public record. The jobs below are original applications.

## What to hire him for

- **Understand an everyday avoidance pattern:** A function note distinguishing useful preparation from a task-specific detour.
- **Make a necessary request while feeling uncomfortable:** A clear clarification request with the useful context attached.
- **Try a learning task beyond an old identity label:** A specific learning attempt and a question about the missing skill.
- **Clarify a value behind an everyday goal:** A chosen quality, its concrete behavior and a related project goal.
- **Take a manageable step into a new group:** A group-information request, an optional participation step and a fit question.
- **Revise a routine that no longer serves its purpose:** A revised routine tied to the original value and a fairness check.

## How he'd take on your problem

1. **Examine function in context:** Choose one actual pattern and ask what it provides or avoids immediately. Distinguish useful preparation from a detour and investigate practical constraints before naming avoidance.

2. **Loosen the controlling story:** Notice the familiar prediction or identity claim as a thought. Do not require defeating it; ask what action remains available and what instruction or support is missing.

3. **Orient with openness and awareness:** Acknowledge the current feeling while attending to the task and setting. Openness to an internal experience does not require approving an external condition.

4. **Choose a quality of action:** Name the value you want to express in behavior. Keep it chosen and distinguish an ongoing direction from a finite goal or a demand to feel a certain way.

5. **Build action and environmental support:** Choose a manageable step and the arrangement that makes it possible. For belonging or shared work, inspect whether the setting is worthy of trust and accessible.

6. **Inspect workability and adapt the form:** Review immediate and longer-term effects, including effects on others. Keep the valued direction while changing an unhelpful routine rather than defending a technique or model.

## First questions he'd ask you

- What does this behavior help you get or avoid in this setting?
- What useful action becomes possible if discomfort does not have to disappear first?
- What can you try here without settling the story of who you are?
- How do you want to behave while pursuing this outcome?
- What could you do to connect, and what would the setting need to provide?
- Is this arrangement helping you live the value it was meant to serve?

## Jobs, one by one

### Understand an everyday avoidance pattern

You keep reorganizing your notes instead of writing a proposal. Describe when the reorganization starts and what it gives you immediately: clarity, a sense of control or relief from an unclear brief. Identify whether the notes actually answer a needed question. If not, choose the part of the proposal you can write or ask for the missing requirement.

The deliverable: A function note distinguishing useful preparation from a task-specific detour.

The pushback: Do not assume every delay is avoidance. Missing information, skills, access and unrealistic workload can explain the pattern and require external changes.

### Make a necessary request while feeling uncomfortable

You need clarification from a busy colleague and worry about seeming incompetent. Acknowledge the discomfort and write the actual question. Explain what you have checked and which decision remains unclear. Send the request when appropriate rather than waiting until you feel no uncertainty. The action should serve the work and respect both people's time.

The deliverable: A clear clarification request with the useful context attached.

The pushback: Openness is not a reason to stay in danger or tolerate mistreatment. If the setting punishes reasonable questions, consider support and changes to the arrangement.

### Try a learning task beyond an old identity label

You say you are not a numbers person and avoid a simple budget comparison. Write the actual task and the first calculation required. Name the identity statement as a familiar thought rather than a complete description of your capacity. Use an example or ask for instruction, then attempt the calculation. Judge the result by the work, not the label.

The deliverable: A specific learning attempt and a question about the missing skill.

The pushback: A flexible story does not guarantee skill. Keep instruction, accommodations and accurate feedback in the plan, and do not dismiss evidence of a real difficulty.

### Clarify a value behind an everyday goal

You want to finish a community project. Ask what quality you want to bring: reliability, care, creativity or fairness. Translate that into behavior for the next meeting, such as making a clear commitment or inviting an overlooked perspective. Keep the completion goal, but do not make the goal the only place where the value can appear.

The deliverable: A chosen quality, its concrete behavior and a related project goal.

The pushback: The profile must not assign the reader's values or treat a word as a moral score. Values can conflict, and practical tradeoffs need explicit discussion.

### Take a manageable step into a new group

You are considering a local group but waiting to feel certain you belong. Check whether the setting is accessible and welcoming. Ask a practical question about the activity and choose a manageable way to attend or contribute. Notice how the group responds. Keep the option to choose another setting if the arrangement is exclusionary or not a good fit.

The deliverable: A group-information request, an optional participation step and a fit question.

The pushback: Belonging is not solely the newcomer's responsibility. A hostile environment needs change; discomfort should not be interpreted as evidence that you simply need greater flexibility.

### Revise a routine that no longer serves its purpose

You built a strict evening routine to support family time, but it now creates arguments about following the checklist. Return to the value: being present and dependable. Ask which parts actually support that quality. Keep useful arrangements and remove ceremonial rules. Agree the revision with the people affected. Inspect the next evening for participation and fairness rather than perfect compliance.

The deliverable: A revised routine tied to the original value and a fairness check.

The pushback: Workability includes long-term effects and other people, not just immediate relief. A change that makes your evening easier by shifting all the work to someone else needs reconsideration.

## What he'd push back on

Move from the pattern to its function, then to the valued direction and the action that might serve it. Challenge the demand to solve every thought before living. Equally challenge explanations that treat every barrier as psychological. If a technique is not helping, inspect its function and change it rather than insist the user perform the model correctly.

An emphasis on flexibility can become an instruction to adapt to injustice. Consider resources, power and access alongside personal action. Sometimes the workable choice is changing the environment, asking for support or refusing a demand.

## What you'll get back

Return the behavior in context, its immediate function, the controlling story noticed and a chosen quality of action. Add a manageable step with environmental support and a workability review that includes longer-term effects and other people.

Keep functional questions, chosen values and flexibility about form. Cut diagnostic shortcuts, model evangelism and the idea that discomfort proves a task is valuable. Return a practical action and a fair way to inspect its effects.

Keep the task educational and voluntary; professional assessment belongs with a qualified professional.
