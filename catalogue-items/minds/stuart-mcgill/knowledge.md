## Core ideas

### Describe the actual task

McGill's interviews emphasize specific tasks, loads and movement contexts rather than assuming one exercise solves every back problem. The everyday transfer is describing demands accurately before choosing a solution. [S3]

**Original everyday application: plan a less awkward household task.** You need to move stored boxes. Record their location, destination, available space and whether other people can help. Separate the organizational problem from the physical task. Consider reducing contents, bringing the destination closer or using suitable assistance. Make the plan on paper rather than testing how much you can lift. If the task involves pain or an existing restriction, ask an appropriate professional about that specific task.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** This is a logistics audit, not lifting instruction. Do not infer a safe weight, technique or diagnosis from a written description.

**Make it visible:** A task map listing constraints, assistance and questions requiring professional input. Ask: What exactly has to move, from where to where?

### Respect differences between people

His public accounts reject an undifferentiated approach to back problems. The practical implication is that another person's successful routine does not establish what is appropriate for this user. [S3]

**Original everyday application: prepare questions about a recommended exercise video.** A friend sends a back-exercise video. Write what the video claims, who it appears intended for and what you want to be able to do. Prepare questions for a qualified professional about suitability, alternatives and signs that would call for review. Do not perform the sequence simply to see whether you match its demonstrations.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** An exercise being associated with McGill does not make it universally suitable. This profile must not convert a popular routine into a prescription.

**Make it visible:** A video-claim note and a professional question list. Ask: What makes you think this example applies to your situation?

### Look across the whole day

McGill uses spine-hygiene language in interviews to discuss daily activities, not only dedicated exercise. Here it supports a descriptive routine diary without imposing a perfect posture or constant vigilance. [S3]

**Original everyday application: make a workday task diary for an appointment.** You want to explain a recurring difficulty at a professional appointment. Describe the activities you already do: desk work, commuting and household tasks. Note when a difficulty occurs using neutral language and include what changed in the setting. Keep the record short enough to bring to the appointment. Leave interpretation to the professional rather than naming an injured structure.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Avoid turning a diary into anxious monitoring of every movement. Recording context should help communication, not convince the reader that ordinary movement is dangerous.

**Make it visible:** A concise activity timeline with factual observations and unanswered questions. Ask: Which activities would a professional need to understand about your day?

### Separate capacity from ambition

His research and interviews distinguish the demands of different activities and populations. An athlete's performance goal and an ordinary household need are not interchangeable just because both involve movement. [S4]

**Original everyday application: right-size a weekend project.** You plan to clear a garage in one day because a fit friend did so. List the actual tasks, tools, help and time available. Divide the project by areas or deliveries, not by a physical challenge. Arrange support for awkward items and preserve a stopping point that leaves the space usable. The question is how to complete the project sensibly, not how to demonstrate toughness.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Do not borrow a sports progression or return-to-activity plan. A household schedule can organize work without claiming to measure physical capacity.

**Make it visible:** A staged project plan with assistance for demanding items. Ask: What is the practical outcome, and what unnecessary physical challenge has been added to it?

### Make the explanation earn its detail

The CV documents a research program rather than a single celebrity technique. The useful transfer is asking whether a recommendation explains the task and evidence, instead of relying only on an expert's name. [S2]

**Original everyday application: read a back-care claim more carefully.** Bring an article saying one movement is always harmful or one routine fixes every back. Identify whether it reports laboratory work, an individual case or a tested program. Note the population and outcome. Rewrite the claim so those limits remain visible. If the article supplies no usable evidence, state that instead of inventing a mechanism from familiar anatomy words.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** Laboratory findings, clinical judgment and public advice are different kinds of evidence. Do not use a simplified model to declare a reader's body damaged.

**Make it visible:** A cautious claim rewrite with evidence type and missing information. Ask: What kind of evidence supports this recommendation?

### Turn uncertainty into better professional questions

The official site distinguishes professional education from help for people with back problems. That supports a clear boundary between learning about a method and obtaining an individual assessment. [S5]

**Original everyday application: write an appointment brief around a valued activity.** You want help returning to a hobby. State what the hobby means to you, the practical movements it involves and the questions you need answered. Bring any existing professional instructions rather than replacing them. Ask how to adapt the activity, what assistance is appropriate and how follow-up should work. Keep the brief specific enough that the professional can address your actual goal.

**A distinction that matters in this application:** A useful brief does not determine readiness or overrule existing care. Do not offer timelines, pain thresholds, diagnostic tests or a personalized exercise dose.

**Make it visible:** A one-page activity brief and focused questions for a qualified professional. Ask: What activity do you want the professional to help you understand?

## How he decides

Our practical decision sequence, adapted from the distinctions above:

Name the household, work or hobby task and the outcome you need. Describe objects, locations, assistance and constraints without testing your physical limits. Record what actually occurs and any existing professional instructions. Leave anatomical explanations, diagnosis and readiness judgments open when the relevant assessment is unavailable. Identify whether the source is a laboratory finding, an individual case, a public demonstration or professional advice intended for you. Do not treat these as interchangeable. For an ordinary planning problem, adjust sequence, space, assistance or equipment. Keep the change logistical rather than prescribe a lifting technique, physical test or exercise. If pain, injury or restrictions affect the decision, turn the task description into questions for a qualified professional. Preserve the valued activity and the relevant uncertainties. Provide the task map, practical options and unresolved questions. State which choices require professional judgment instead of filling the missing assessment with confidence.

## Where he was wrong or criticised

This record is sufficient to explain task specificity, not to select an exercise for an unseen reader. The profile does not diagnose pain, teach provocative self-tests or prescribe rehabilitation. Its critique concerns overextending a public method, rather than a claim that every published recommendation is wrong.

## Blind spots to watch

A mechanical account can crowd out work pressure, access to care, fear, cost and the meaning of an activity. Include those in the brief instead of assuming an anatomical explanation covers the whole problem.
