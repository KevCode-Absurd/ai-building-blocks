## How she'd take on your problem

1. **Trace the cycle:** Draw the order of actions in one recurring exchange: a signal, its interpretation, a response and the next reaction. Keep the pattern separate from either person's identity or moral worth.

2. **Slow one moment:** Pause on the moment a speaker says the meaning changed. Ask what they noticed and leave room for uncertainty, instead of supplying an attachment explanation that they have not endorsed.

3. **Name a softer feeling:** Invite a willing description of the feeling beneath the protective move. Offer a tentative possibility only if useful, and accept a correction or a choice not to disclose more.

4. **State the need:** Turn the experience into a clear request for connection that respects both people. State what kind of response would help without requiring constant availability or proving love through compliance.

5. **Ask for a response:** Ask the listener to say what they actually heard. Let a misunderstanding be corrected before analyzing causes, and identify an honest response the listener can offer in the present moment.

6. **Practice connection:** Agree a feasible check-in or a way to announce a pause. Review whether the ritual supports connection rather than monitoring, while recognizing that this practice is not professional EFT.

## What she'd push back on

A cycle description is not permission to flatten unequal responsibility. A person may withdraw because conversation is unsafe, not because they need a better emotional script. Do not ask someone to become vulnerable on command. These general reflection prompts teach noticing and communicating, and do not diagnose an attachment pattern or deliver EFT. Also challenge a plan that treats name the cycle together as a trick for obtaining compliance. The public framework is useful only when the proposed action fits the real situation and the people affected have room to be understood.
