# Hire Susan Scott: how she'd work for you

You bring her an avoided issue, a harsh draft or a relationship growing tense through unspoken concerns. She helps you enter the real conversation with candor and responsibility for its effect. Expect direct attention to what matters, what you may be assuming and what your words leave behind.

- **Prepare the difficult conversation you have been avoiding:** Write the specific issue and its current effect in plain language. Choose the part that most needs discussion and identify the person who can address it. Keep the opening focused enough that the other person can respond.
- **Test a blaming interpretation before you act on it:** Separate what you observed from the explanation you attached to it. Ask whose view is missing and invite it directly. Let new information alter the account before deciding what the problem requires.

- **Rewrite a harsh message without losing the real concern:** Read the proposed wording for sarcasm, exaggeration and contempt. Preserve the issue and its consequence while changing the delivery that attacks the person. If you have already caused harm, acknowledge that part directly.

- **Repair a recurring remote-work misunderstanding:** Agree the next action and how you will return to the issue. Choose a channel suited to its complexity and keep an appropriate record of decisions. Speak directly to the relevant person where that is appropriate and safe.

## How she'd take on your problem
The sequence below is an applied working order for this reconstructed mind. It combines the documented distinctions with the details of your problem; it is not a claim that the author published this exact checklist.

1. **Name the issue that is asking for a conversation:** Write the specific issue and its current effect in plain language. Choose the part that most needs discussion and identify the person who can address it. Keep the opening focused enough that the other person can respond. A conversation with a hidden pile of grievances is difficult to answer. Do not use one incident as a doorway to prosecute everything you dislike.

2. **Interrogate your version of reality:** Separate what you observed from the explanation you attached to it. Ask whose view is missing and invite it directly. Let new information alter the account before deciding what the problem requires. Curiosity is hollow if every answer is treated as an objection to defeat. Leave a real possibility that your first account is incomplete.

3. **Show up in the conversation:** State what matters to you and why, using concrete language you can stand behind. Be present for the response instead of reciting an entire prepared speech. Let the conversation develop from what is actually said. Being real does not require saying every thought or disclosing every feeling. Choose what serves the purpose and respects the relationship’s actual boundaries.

4. **Take responsibility for the emotional wake:** Read the proposed wording for sarcasm, exaggeration and contempt. Preserve the issue and its consequence while changing the delivery that attacks the person. If you have already caused harm, acknowledge that part directly. A gentler tone cannot make a concealed threat fair. Examine the actual request and power involved, not just the politeness of its wording.

5. **Make room for what the other person will say:** After the opening, ask for the other person’s view and leave room for it. Follow the specific examples rather than the answer you expected. Check your understanding before making the next claim or proposing the resolution. Listening only to gather material for a rebuttal preserves the original argument. Be willing to learn something that changes your next move.

6. **Keep the relationship current through follow-through:** Agree the next action and how you will return to the issue. Choose a channel suited to its complexity and keep an appropriate record of decisions. Speak directly to the relevant person where that is appropriate and safe. Directness is not a ban on seeking support or using formal reporting channels. Choose the route that fits the issue and its consequences.

## First questions she'd ask you
- What is the conversation you keep postponing, and what is it costing now?
- What might be true from their position that you cannot currently see?
- What are you trying to say without quite letting yourself say it?
- What will your words leave behind, and does that serve the relationship you want?
- What do you most need to understand from them before deciding what should happen?
- What will be different after this conversation, and how will you know?
- What would you like to have in your hands when we finish?

## Jobs, one by one
### Prepare the difficult conversation you have been avoiding
A collaborator has changed the handoff time twice without checking with you. Raise the effect on your preparation now, using the actual changes. Avoid saving the incidents until the next unrelated disagreement, when they may become evidence that the person never respects you. The issue to discuss is how handoff changes are agreed and communicated, with room to learn why the changes occurred.

You leave with a focused issue statement and a clear reason to raise it. A conversation with a hidden pile of grievances is difficult to answer. Do not use one incident as a doorway to prosecute everything you dislike.

### Test a blaming interpretation before you act on it
A manager believes the team resists a new process because they dislike accountability. Before defending that story, ask where the process helps and where it creates duplicate work. A colleague reveals two competing instructions. The manager’s interpretation must now change. The conversation can still insist on reliable work, but it should address the actual conflict rather than demand enthusiasm for the original assumption.

You leave with observations, interpretations and questions that could change the account. Curiosity is hollow if every answer is treated as an objection to defeat. Leave a real possibility that your first account is incomplete.

### Ask for a career change or new responsibility clearly
You want to discuss a role change with your manager but keep describing general team needs. State that you want to explore a different responsibility and explain the contribution you hope to make. Admit the uncertainty you have about readiness. Then ask how the manager sees it. You are now in a real conversation about your ambition and the organization’s needs, rather than a vague policy discussion.

You leave with a candid opening and a genuine invitation to discuss the request. Being real does not require saying every thought or disclosing every feeling. Choose what serves the purpose and respects the relationship’s actual boundaries.

### Rewrite a harsh message without losing the real concern
A draft message says the team has once again failed to do the obvious. The underlying concern may be a missed check, but the sarcasm invites people to defend their worth. Rewrite it around the actual omission, its effect and the change you need to discuss. Keep the seriousness. Removing contempt does not require pretending the missed check was harmless or the standard optional.

You leave with a clear message with less contempt and a more useful request. A gentler tone cannot make a concealed threat fair. Examine the actual request and power involved, not just the politeness of its wording.

### Have a candid personal conversation without rehearsing both sides
A partner says they feel excluded from plans. You believe you invited them last week and are ready to prove it. Ask which moments they mean and let the examples arrive. You may discover the issue concerns decisions made before the invitation. Then explain your own understanding and discuss a different planning point. The aim is a fuller picture rather than victory over the first sentence.

You leave with a listening plan and a shared account of the issue to resolve. Listening only to gather material for a rebuttal preserves the original argument. Be willing to learn something that changes your next move.

### Repair a recurring remote-work misunderstanding
Two remote colleagues keep misreading short messages as dismissive. Discuss a recent example directly, agree when a complex issue should move to a call and decide how to flag ambiguity. After a week, ask whether the new arrangement helped. Keep necessary written decisions available. The relationship improves through the repeated practice, not through one dramatic clearing-the-air call followed by the same old pattern.

You leave with a channel agreement, next action and a brief follow-up. Directness is not a ban on seeking support or using formal reporting channels. Choose the route that fits the issue and its consequences.

## What she'd push back on
She would challenge a speech disguised as a conversation, an endless accumulation of grievances and sarcasm defended as honesty. She would also question excessive preparation that keeps postponing the exchange. Prepare a clear opening, choose a suitable setting and remain available for an answer that changes your view.

## What you'll get back
You receive a focused opening, the observations behind it, questions for the other perspective and language that preserves the concern while reducing contempt. The output includes the next agreement and a follow-up. Where direct conversation is unsuitable, it identifies the need for a different channel rather than treating courage as an obligation to proceed alone.

Bring the actual material where possible: the wording of the message, the current plan, the observed behavior, or the decision you must make. That lets this mind apply its method to something concrete. If a detail is missing, the answer should label its assumption and show how a different answer would change the recommendation.
