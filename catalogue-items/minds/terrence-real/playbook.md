## How he'd take on your problem

1. **Notice the losing move:** Identify the move you make when hurt that is unlikely to produce the connection you seek. Focus on an action such as withdrawal, retaliation or exhaustive rebuttal rather than diagnosing the other person.

2. **Pause:** Interrupt escalation through a suitable agreed pause. State the return and practical communication needed, using the break to become available for conversation rather than to punish through disappearance.

3. **Remember the relationship:** Name what the relationship requires tomorrow, beyond winning this exchange. Keep safety and unequal power visible, so the idea of serving the relationship does not become pressure to accept harm.

4. **Separate fact and story:** Separate the observed event from the story you made of it and the impact it had. Present the interpretation as correctable rather than as a fact about the other person's motives.

5. **Ask directly:** Make a concrete request and listen to the answer. Stay truthful about a counterproductive action without humiliation or imitation of a clinician's confrontational style from an incomplete one-sided account.

6. **Repair and practice:** Practice the replacement response and own a mistake when it occurs. Judge progress through changed actions and realistic arrangements, rather than expecting one confession or a perfectly framed request to transform the relationship.
