## Core ideas

### Vegetables can lead

Books such as Plenty helped make vegetables the organising centre of a meal rather than treating them only as supporting sides. [S5]

**Application designed here:** Choose the vegetable first and build the rest around it. A vegetable-centred meal needs substance as well as colour. Choose a vegetable you want to eat, add a satisfying base and decide which topping or dressing creates a useful contrast. Avoid adding five strong finishing elements out of habit. The goal is a coherent plate with a few distinct experiences, not the largest possible collection of fashionable pantry ingredients. [S5]

### Simple has several meanings

Simple distinguishes time, ingredient count, pantry use, make-ahead work and low intervention, rather than promising that one recipe solves every constraint. [S4]

**Application designed here:** Name the kind of easy you need before choosing dinner. Short on time and easy to make ahead are different promises. If dinner is in thirty minutes, a recipe requiring yesterday’s work is not the answer. Mark active time, waiting time and advance preparation separately. Choose the form of simplicity that solves tonight’s problem and tell the user which tradeoff remains, such as a longer roast or an extra finishing bowl. [S4]

### Flavor combinations create surprise

His newsletter invites new uses for familiar ingredients and describes the ongoing process of refining combinations. [S2]

**Application designed here:** Add one contrasting element with a clear purpose. An unfamiliar ingredient should earn more than one outing. Select a small number of dishes where its role is clear and learn its flavour with familiar companions. Describe the difference if a substitute is proposed. A pantry becomes useful through repeated combinations, not through buying every condiment mentioned in an attractive cookbook before knowing where any of them belongs. [S2]

### Acid and oil make a useful pair

In his Simple interview he names olive oil, lemon and garlic as dependable ingredients and flavor partners. [S1]

**Application designed here:** Try a small finishing adjustment before rebuilding a dish. For a rich vegetable dish, consider whether a bright dressing or a fresh element would create contrast. Test a small portion and evaluate it as part of the whole plate. Do not judge the dressing in isolation or assume that sharpness is always the missing feature. Sometimes the dish needs a textural change or a different cooking treatment instead. [S1]

### Texture is part of generosity

His recipes and index combine vegetables with dressings, toppings and herbs that give different experiences within one plate. [S3]

**Application designed here:** Choose a contrast that survives until the food is served. A make-ahead gathering plan should separate food that holds well from toppings that lose their texture. Finish the stable components early and leave a short assembly list. Explain what must happen near service so the host is not left discovering it while guests wait. Abundance should feel generous to the people eating and manageable to the person cooking. [S3]

### Season and occasion belong together

His Simple interview connects the right dish with the day and encourages cooking food the eater actually wants. [S1]

**Application designed here:** Match a warm or cool meal to the conditions and appetite. The regional name of an ingredient and the oven convention can change a reader’s interpretation. Check whether the recipe uses a fan oven and which measurement system it follows. Translate aubergine or coriander where needed without rewriting the dish’s identity. A small clarification before cooking can prevent a more substantial error that no amount of garnish will solve. [S1]

### Good food can require little cooking

He explicitly includes an excellent salad or ripe fruit with cheese as a valid meal experience. [S1]

**Application designed here:** Use a no-cook component to reduce last-minute pressure. A no-cook component can be a deliberate part of dinner rather than an admission of defeat. Start with ingredients whose condition and flavour make that treatment appealing. Give it a role beside the rest of the meal and season it appropriately. Removing heat from one part of the menu can reduce pressure while adding a contrasting experience at the table. [S1]

### Measurements enable transfer

His newsletter explains metric quantities and regional ingredient names so readers can reproduce dishes in different kitchens. [S2]

**Application designed here:** Check oven setting and measurement system before you begin. For a workshop outside cooking, build around a reliable core activity and one contrasting mode of participation. Ask what the contrast contributes before adding it. A quiet individual observation followed by shared discussion might be useful; a theatrical surprise without a purpose may not be. This is a design analogy from combining experiences, not a documented Ottolenghi management method. [S2]

## Blind spots to watch
