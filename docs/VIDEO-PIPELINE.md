# Video and image blocks

Kevin, 19 Oct 2026: "Blocks have to be different for image outputs and video outputs." And for video, "the entire process should be followed on the skill": pick an avatar, turn it into an image, make a character sheet with all angles, storyboard, then turn the storyboard into video.

## Rule 1: one block, one kind of ad

- A block makes **either an image ad or a video ad**, never both.
- A video block still makes images on the way: reference images, character sheets, storyboard frames. Those are working files for the video, not a finished image ad.
- Where a video look also works as a still (a watercolor, paper or comic still, for example), the still is **its own image block** in the same look, with its own page.

## Rule 2: every video block runs the whole process

Every block that turns a script into AI video follows these stages, in this order. **Stop for approval after stages 1, 3 and 4.** Never skip a stage, and never move on from an unapproved one.

| Stage | What happens | What you approve |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Script | The locked script from the script block: lines, shots, timing, claims. | (already approved) |
| 1. Pick the avatar | Choose who or what carries the ad: a presenter, a character, a mascot or a talking object. Choose it from the buyer and the script. Write an **avatar card**: role, age range and look in plain words, clothes, props, voice, and what must never change. | The avatar card |
| 2. Avatar to image | Make 3 or 4 reference images of the avatar in the ad's look. Pick one. | (picked within stage 2) |
| 3. Character sheet | From the picked image, make the sheet with every angle: front, three-quarter left and right, side, back. Add the expressions and poses the script needs, and the avatar's size next to the product. Also make a **product sheet** from the real photos (front, side, top, label word for word) and a **set sheet** for each place. Write the lock lines that every later prompt repeats word for word. | The sheets and lock lines |
| 4. Storyboard | One frame per shot, built from the sheets as references. Each shot gets a card: framing, camera, the action from start to end, the line spoken, length and sound. Check continuity: the 180° line, eyelines, which hand holds what, and the product's state. | The whole storyboard |
| 5. Storyboard to video | Use each approved frame as the clip's start frame, plus an end frame where the tool takes one. The clip prompt describes only the motion. Make takes and pick one. Add the voice and lip-sync. Then cut the edit: order, cuts, captions, music, end card and safe zones. Finish with a check against the script and claims. | The finished cut |

When there's no character: if the product alone carries the ad (motion graphics, satisfying motion, a product reel), stages 1 to 3 become the **product sheet** and **set sheet**. The product is the avatar.

Which blocks this covers:
- Real footage blocks, which only edit what was filmed (turn-footage-into-ad-people-follow, turn-raw-footage-into-a-clear-social-post), skip stages 1 to 4.
- Script blocks end by handing over the script and a cast note (who should carry it). The video block for that look runs stages 1 to 5.

## People and honesty

- Avatars are invented people. Never use a real person's face or voice, and never clone a voice (RULES.md §9).
- An AI presenter never says "I tried it" or tells a customer's story as their own (FTC 16 CFR 255 and 465). Label AI video where the platform asks for it.
- The product is never redrawn. It comes from real photos and the product sheet, and its label text is exact.
