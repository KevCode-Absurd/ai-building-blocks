# How we write

The headline and copy rules are in `brand/COPYWRITING.md` and win where this file differs. This file covers plain language and our internal jargon.

Everything on the site, and every skill file, follows these rules. The reader is a busy owner or marketer who has never heard our terms. They are hiring a skill to get one job done. Tell them what they get, then show them.

## The test for every page

A stranger lands on the page. Within ten seconds they should be able to answer:
1. What will I get? (the headline)
2. How does it get there, and what do I need to give it? (the subhead)
3. What does the result look like? (the example)

If any answer is unclear, rewrite.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the outcome.** The headline says what the reader walks away with, in their words. "Ads people believe, because every line rests on a real product fact." Not the method's name.
2. **The subhead explains the headline.** One to three sentences: what you give it, what it does, what you get. Concrete nouns.
3. **Plain words.** Write like you're explaining it to a smart friend who doesn't work in marketing. If a term is unavoidable (CRO, hook, landing page), explain it the first time in a few words, and add it to `terms`.
4. **Be specific.** "Finds the three reasons people leave your page" beats "improves conversion." Use numbers when you have them. Never invent numbers, results or time saved.
5. **Active voice, direct verbs.** "It checks every line against your facts." Not "Lines are validated."
6. **Short sentences, but not choppy.** Vary them. No stacked fragments.
7. **Say it once.** If two sentences say the same thing, delete one.
8. **Persuade with facts.** Show what it catches, what it saves you from, what the output looks like. No hype.
9. **Same word for the same thing.** A "skill" is always a skill. Don't rotate synonyms.

## Words and patterns we never use

Banned words: delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, game changer, unlock, supercharge, harness, elevate, seamless, effortless, transformative, revolutionize, paradigm, synergy, holistic, journey, ecosystem, best-in-class, world-class, next-level, powerful (as filler), magic.

Banned patterns:
- "It's not X, it's Y." Say Y.
- "Here's the thing", "The secret is", "What most people get wrong".
- A colon followed by a dramatic reveal ("The result: more sales").
- Trailing "-ing" clauses that pretend to explain ("..., ensuring quality").
- "In today's world", "When it comes to", "At the end of the day".
- Em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma or brackets.
- Emoji, ALL CAPS labels, bold scattered mid-sentence.
- Ending on a cute one-liner or a recap.

## Our own jargon, and what to say instead

The old skill names used our internal terms. Replace them on the page, or explain them once in `terms`.

| Internal term | Say instead |
|---|---|
| brand brain | your brand file (one folder with your facts, voice, offers and what you can claim) |
| angle | the reason someone buys, in their words |
| awareness stage | how much the buyer already knows about the problem and about you |
| lever, lever snippet | a single setting you can change in an image or video (light, camera, mood) |
| native ad | an ad made to look like a normal post, photo or message, not like an ad |
| advertorial | an ad written like an article |
| static ad | an image ad |
| UGC | a video that looks filmed by a customer on their phone |
| hook | the first line or first two seconds that make someone stop scrolling |
| PDP | product page |
| CRO | getting more of your visitors to buy |
| port, input type | what you give it, what you get |
| orchestrator | workflow |
| render | make the image or video |
| format rules | the rules for writing that kind of ad |
| director | turns a script into shot-by-shot instructions for a video tool |

## Names

A skill's or prompt's name is its job title, 2 to 6 words in sentence case: "Meta ads compliance checker", "Landing page auditor", "Customer buying reasons finder". Read it and you know what you'd hire it for. No colons, no internal terms. Platform names are fine when the job is tied to one. Workflows keep verb-first names for now. Full rule: `brand/COPYWRITING.md`, "Names and headlines of blocks".
