People don't lack AI tools. They lack the tested, explained, ready-to-use pieces that make those tools do real work. We build the library of those pieces, and the community around it.

A free library of prompts, skills, routines, workflows, mini tools and agents. Each one is tested on four AI labs, shown with a real before and after, and comes with a plain "set it up yourself" guide. One list, split by type and narrowed by filters: business type, job function and platform. Owners, operators, marketers, freelancers and agencies in digital businesses who already use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini but get little real work out of them. The beachhead is ecommerce, where our own tested work is strongest. Then SaaS, agencies, and job roles.

Everything else is untested, technical or sold as packs. We test every listing, show the result first, give the next step only, keep it free, and answer questions in the comments. The problem

Almost everyone has AI. Few get real work out of it.

The good material exists: skills on GitHub, workflows in YouTube videos, prompts in newsletters. But it's scattered, untested, written for developers, and out of date within months. A busy owner can't tell which piece works, how to set it up, or what to do next.

1

Can't find it

Good skills sit in GitHub repos, long videos and threads. There's no single place that's organised by the job you're trying to do.

2

Can't trust it

Nobody shows the output, the cost or which model it needs. Some downloads carry hidden instructions (prompt injection) or scripts that do things you didn't ask for.

3

Can't set it up

Instructions assume a terminal, an API key and a developer's vocabulary. Non-developers give up at "clone the repo".

4

Can't keep up

Models change every few months. Last spring's best prompt is worse than today's, and nobody re-tests it.

The gap isn't access. It's know-how. People have the tools. What they lack is the tested, explained, next-step piece that turns "I have ChatGPT" into "this job now takes 5 minutes".

1 · Use is wide but shallow

Most people try AI; few use it daily, and only a few organisations get value at scale. The gap between "has ChatGPT" and "gets work done with it" is the market. In the US, 19.8% of businesses used AI in a two-week window in May 2026, and only about 14% in retail (Census).

2 · Skills became portable

The SKILL.md format behind Claude's skills became an open standard (agentskills.io, December 2025), adopted by dozens of tools. One well-made skill can now run in many AIs. That makes a cross-tool library possible for the first time.

3 · Supply is exploding, unchecked

Anthropic's own skills repo has 180k+ GitHub stars, and one directory claims to index 3M+ skills. Volume isn't the problem any more. Nobody tests, explains or safety-checks it for non-developers. That's the job.

Models change every few months. A library that re-tests its listings when they do, and shows the cost on each lab, stays useful in a way a static pack or video can't.

The growth loop

Every source and every question makes the library better.

Why it compounds

  • Questions become content. Every answer becomes a FAQ entry, and every repeat problem becomes the next version of the listing.
  • Requests steer the intake. What people ask for is what we look for next.
  • Credit brings creators. Creators we credit share their listing with their audience.
  • Embeds bring links. Mini tools on other sites send people and search ranking back to us.
  • Re-tests bring people back. Following a listing means an email when it improves, which feeds the north star: people who come back.
  • More business types, same cores. Each new industry pack reuses every shared core, so growth costs less each time.
The loop runs without paid ads. Its speed depends on two things we control: how many good sources go through intake each week, and how fast we answer.

The public site never calls a model lab. The factory's keys stay in your .env outside the repo, so they can't be committed. Only the four lab keys are loaded.

Comments can't attack readers

Comments are plain text with limited formatting. No raw HTML and no image uploads at launch. A strict content security policy is in place.

Abuse and spam

Bot check, rate limits, link review for new accounts, a report button and a moderation queue with a person deciding.

Privacy law

Cookie-free analytics, a plain privacy page, export and delete in one click, and a 48-hour take-down for creators. A lawyer reads it once before anything paid.

We reduce risk; we never promise zero risk. A security check of the community service (an outside reviewer, paid per review) happens before launch and again before memberships.
