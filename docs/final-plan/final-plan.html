What we're building

A global platform of tested AI building blocks and expert implementation for digital businesses. It covers prompts, skills, routines, workflows, agents and complete systems. Each block does one exact job and shows a real, labelled example. It says which AI model to use and what it costs, and runs in the AI tools people already pay for. When setup gets hard, we do it for them.

Who it's for

Digital businesses that sell online: brands, dropshippers, agencies, software companies, creators and service businesses. Agencies first, because one agency covers many industries and many brands.

Where we start

Exact marketing jobs, where our tested methods are deepest. We then earn the right to cover sales, support and operations. Ecommerce is our first industry pack, not the product.

How we earn

Subscriptions ($99 / $299 / $899 a month), implementation ($5,000), consulting ($1,500), affiliates, and advertising last. A creator marketplace (70/30) comes later.

Part 0 · My calls

Eight calls that shape everything.

You asked for my own plan. These are the choices I'd make, and the rest of this document follows from them.

  1. One catalogue for every digital business, organised by job.We don't build an "ecommerce platform" or one app per industry. Each job has one core part, and industry packs add the brain fields, examples and thresholds for that industry. This keeps one quality bar and one test system, so a fix reaches every industry at once.
  2. Marketing first, because that's where our proof is.Our 110 tested items are almost all marketing: creative, scripts, pages, research. A new function (sales, support, operations) opens only when its first three jobs pass testing and at least 10 customers have asked for it.
  3. Agencies are the first buyer.One agency sale covers many brands in many industries. Agencies feel the pain of repeat work most, prove the parts work outside one industry, and become partners who resell us.
  4. Prove payment before we build the platform.Paid holiday sprints and a founding-member pre-sale come before accounts and payments. If nobody pays by Gate A (11 Dec), we change the offer, not the code.
  5. Testing is the moat, so we publish it.Every part shows real model tests: quality, cost per run and speed, cheap model vs top model. "Which model for which job" becomes our most useful content, and it's hard to copy.
  6. Downloads first, hosted runs later.Site v0 is a catalogue of parts that run in Claude, ChatGPT, Codex or any AI via a router. Running parts on our own servers comes only when customers ask for it, because it adds cost, security work and support.
  7. Agents and systems are sold as implementation before they're products.We sell bounded workflows first, then routines (from May) and agents (from June), each with budgets, approvals and red-team tests. Complex systems stay paid services until we've built the same thing twice.
  8. Small team, hiring on measured load.You, Claude with helper agents, and specialist reviewers. The first hires (onboarding, delivery) come when tracked hours say so, not on a calendar.

Part 1 · The idea

AI answers. Teams still do the setup.

Anyone can ask an AI for an ad, an audit or a sales email. Getting a result you'd actually use means collecting your business's facts, choosing a method, picking the right model, checking the output and doing it again next week. That gap is the business.

1 · The method is too broad

"Write me an ad" has no exact checks, inputs or standard for what good looks like.

2 · The context is scattered

Products or plans, prices, offers, claims, voice and reviews live in different places. The AI gets none of them.

3 · A draft isn't finished work

Someone has to catch the wrong price, the made-up claim and the off-brand line before it ships.

4 · One success is hard to repeat

Models change, prompts drift and nobody owns it. What worked in March breaks in June.

  1. Readiness form. Goal, business type, tools, data access, budget and deadline. Many requests turn into a standard plan with no custom work.

  6. Move to subscription. We keep the parts updated and supported.

Quality rules that never bend

Zero wrong prices, zero invented claims

Checked by code against the brain's current offer and claims register, before any judge.

The judge never wrote the work

A different model grades blind, against the same checklist the part's page shows.

Proven on more than one business type

A launch part passes on at least two kinds of business before it's marked tested.

Knowledge must earn its place

A rule stays in a prompt only if a with/without test shows it improves the output.

Risky actions need a human

No automatic ad pausing or budget changes. Agents propose; people approve.

Content is data, not orders

Instructions hidden in reviews, pages or course files are ignored, and we test for them with red-team cases.

How Claude works on this, safely

  • One section at a time. Parts inside a section are built in parallel batches of about 4 by helper agents, each writing only to its own part folder.
  • Helpers draft; tools and reviews decide. Model tests run through the tools, so costs and logs stay in one place, with hard spend caps.
  • Sources stay read-only. No secrets are ever handled, no course code is run, and instructions found inside files are treated as data.
  • You review per section, not per part. Every section report lists each part's scores, cost and changes.

Appendix

Glossary and where things live.

WordPlain meaning
Part / itemOne building block in the catalogue: a prompt, skill, routine, workflow, agent or system
JobOne exact task a customer wants done, such as "audit my Meta ads"
FunctionAn area of work, such as creative and content or paid media
Industry packThe brain fields, examples, thresholds and wording that fit a part to one industry. It never copies the part.
Brand brainOne file holding a business's facts, what it sells, its offers, claims, voice and buyers, which any AI can load
TierHow deep a part goes: Starter, Standard, Advanced, Production
StackA ready-made chain of parts for a goal, e.g. "holiday sale stack"
Golden setA fixed set of test inputs with known good answers, used to grade every version
Blind judgeA different AI model that grades outputs without knowing which version made them
Costliest mistakeThe error that would hurt most, e.g. a wrong price or an invented claim; it must be zero
Caching orderPutting the parts of a prompt that don't change first, so AI providers charge less for repeats
Lean modeA shorter version of a part (short brain card, fewer references) to save tokens
RouterA service such as OpenRouter that lets one setup reach many AI models
GateA measured check that must pass before the next phase starts
