> Our mapping of one chapter of Chip Huyen's *AI Engineering* (O'Reilly, 2025) onto our stack, written 2026-10-18.
> The reader's section-by-section summary of the book (section 2) is left out on purpose, so sections run 1, 3, 4…; these notes keep only how the ideas apply to us.
> Book section names are given so the original can be found. The master is `../10_AI_ENGINEERING_FRAMEWORK.md`; where they differ, it wins.

## 1. The chapter in brief

The chapter is about making models faster and cheaper to run, where the rest of the book is about making them better. It works on three levels: the **model** (compression, faster decoding, cheaper attention), the **hardware** (accelerators), and the **service** that runs the model (batching, splitting prefill from decode, prompt caching, parallelism). Before any of that it sets out a measurement vocabulary: latency split into time to first token (TTFT) and time per output token (TPOT), percentiles instead of averages, throughput and **goodput**, and utilization (MFU/MBU). Its core technical point is that reading the input (prefill) and writing the output (decode) behave differently. Prefill is limited by compute, and decode is limited by memory bandwidth. Output tokens are therefore the slow, expensive part of every call.

Huyen says plainly that people who use model APIs will implement almost none of this themselves. For them it is a way to judge providers and to know which levers they actually hold. That describes us exactly: API only, four labs, no self-hosting. For us the chapter does three jobs. (a) It gives `run-test` and the listing pages a precise way to measure and state **speed and cost**. (b) It identifies the levers an API user really holds: prompt order for caching, batch APIs, output length, model and effort choice, parallel calls, streaming. (c) It lets us say clearly, on pages and in Learn, which techniques are for self-hosters and which we deliberately ignore.

**AI run cost mini tool: proposed formula file (one formula, five ways, per FINAL_PLAN §6):**
- **Inputs:**
  - words in: the stable part and the changing part, entered separately
  - words out, including an "it thinks first" option that adds the class's measured reasoning share
  - runs per month
  - "can it wait?" (batch yes/no)
  - redo rate
  - lab and class
- **Lines shown:**
  1. tokens = words × that lab's measured tokens-per-word
  2. stable tokens × cached price (after the first run in a window) + changing tokens × input price + one cache write per window
  3. output tokens × output price
  4. × 0.5 if batch
  5. × (1 + redo rate)
  6. = cost per run, then × runs per month
  7. time ≈ first-words wait (from our measured TTFT for that input size) + output tokens ÷ measured tokens per second
- **Prices:** from dated `_models.yaml`. Labelled example numbers come from our real listing runs.
- **Rule:** it never quotes a saving we haven't measured on that lab.

**Worked example for the lesson** (Claude Sonnet 5.5 list prices as cached 2026-10-06: $2 in, $10 out, $0.20 cache read). A C2 skill with 5,000 stable plus 1,000 changing input tokens and 800 output tokens:
- **Uncached:** 6,000 × $2/M + 800 × $10/M = $0.012 + $0.008 = **$0.020**.
- **Cached** (repeat within the TTL): 5,000 × $0.20/M + 1,000 × $2/M + $0.008 = **$0.011**.
- **Cached and batched:** about **$0.0055**.
- After caching, **output is about 73% of the bill**, which is the chapter's point in one line.

**The numbers every listing page shows** (fills `04` §3.6 "cost per run and speed", which is currently undefined):

*Normal view (simple by default): three numbers on the "Which model" card.*
1. **Cost per run** with the recommended model, e.g. "about 1¢ a run". This is the median of our runs, billed with caching on a repeat run.
2. **Time per run**, e.g. "about 20 seconds". This is the median wall-clock time from the speed probe, rounded.
3. **Tested on** date, with the model names.

*Advanced view (the "Run it efficiently" box).*
4. Recommended / budget / not-worth-it rows, each with quality %, cost per run, **cost per good run** and median and slowest time ("of 10 runs").
5. **First words after** (median TTFT), shown only for listings used interactively in chat.
6. **Tokens:** input (and how much of it is cacheable), output, and hidden thinking share.
7. **Caching saving**, measured on a second run ("−45% cost, −30% wait on Claude"), or "too short to cache".
8. **If it can wait:** the batch price, for listings marked `batch_ok: true` (data prep, bulk variants, reports).
9. A link to **AI run cost** prefilled with this listing's words in and out: "what would this cost me per month?"
10. For workflows: time per step and the critical path. For agents: time to first visible output, time to outcome, and median step count.

All numbers are labelled "measured on our runs, [date], from our machine". Latency varies with time of day and region, and we don't claim more than we measured (the zero-invented-claims rule).

## 4. What it changes in what we already wrote

**Agreements (the book confirms):**
- `05` §5 and §13: stable first, changing last, exact-prefix caching, measure the hit rate. The book's Table 9-3 is the evidence. Keep as is.
- `05` §13 "cost per finished outcome, not per call" is the same idea as goodput and the book's "compare cost per request".
- `05` §14 / 09 step 10: compression and distillation are model-level, can change behaviour, and are a late resort. The chapter adds that the techniques with the most impact are all self-hoster levers, which supports "almost never" for us.
- `06` §5.9: the cached-share and lean-mode tests are exactly the right measurements.
- `06` §10: monthly re-tests are justified by Figure 9-8 (serving changes behaviour).

**Corrections:**
- **Stale router references.** `06` §2.3 still makes OpenRouter the default. `_models.yaml` says prices come "from the router's live price list" and lists `routers_ok`. `04` §3.7 and `05` §10 talk about routing through OpenRouter, LiteLLM or Portkey. DECISIONS (2026-10-18) says **no router, four direct lab accounts**. That matters here because caching and batch mechanics are **per lab**: explicit breakpoints on Anthropic, automatic prefix caching on OpenAI and DeepSeek, implicit plus explicit with a storage fee on Gemini [check], and four separate batch APIs. `run-test` needs four thin lab adapters, not one router call. Recommend fixing those four places when Phase 1 starts.
- **`_models.yaml` is missing the fields this chapter needs.** Per model: `price_per_mtok {input, cached_input, cache_write_5m, cache_write_1h, output}`; `cache {mode: explicit|automatic, min_prefix_tokens, ttl}`; `batch {available, discount, max_turnaround}`; `speed_tier {name, price_multiplier}` (e.g. Anthropic fast mode); and measured `tokens_per_word`.
- **C4 isn't free money.** FINAL_PLAN §12 and `04` §6 present "top plans, cheap repeats" as the efficient default. It forfeits cross-model cache reuse and adds retry risk. Test it against "top model at low effort" (§3, Workflow).

## 5. What we should not adopt (and why)

- **Self-hosting any model** (including DeepSeek's open weights), plus quantization, pruning, kernels, compilers, KV-cache tuning, speculative or parallel decoding, batching strategies, prefill/decode splitting and parallelism. Every one needs our own GPUs and an inference team. We're one founder plus Claude, API-only, free-first, with no hosted product until v3. Revisit only if a hosted product's volume makes a self-hosted C1 model cheaper than DeepSeek or Haiku API prices, which is unlikely in 2027.
- **MFU, MBU, GPU utilization and accelerator selection.** We can't see the providers' chips. One sentence in an Advanced lesson, nothing more.
- **p95/p99 latency dashboards and formal SLOs.** We run around 10 calls per model per listing, where tail percentiles are noise. Median and slowest, labelled with n, are honest. Formal SLOs and goodput-per-second belong to a hosted service with traffic (site v3), not to a library.
- **Paid speed tiers in the factory** (e.g. fast mode at 2× price, priority tiers). The factory isn't latency-sensitive, and the caps are better spent on coverage. Mention them in the tool guide as an option for users with live chat products.
- **Latency as a publish gate.** A slow-but-correct listing is still useful. Speed is displayed and can break ties (question 1), but it never blocks "tested ✓". The one exception is the time cap, as a safety stop.
- **Streaming unchecked price- or claim-bearing text to people.** It conflicts with "zero wrong prices". See the `stream_ok` rule.
- **Chasing raw tokens per second across labs.** Tokenizers differ, so we compare dollars per run and seconds per run only.
- **Third-party hosts of the same models** (for testing or as recommendations). Figure 9-8 shows that serving changes scores, and our tested numbers wouldn't transfer.

## 6. What we teach users (Learn / Make it yours)

A lesson sequence for **Make it yours step 9, "Cheaper and faster"**, from beginner to expert. Each lesson has a listing-style page, a before and after from our own runs, and the AI run cost tool.

1. **Beginner: "Why long answers cost more and take longer."** Reading your prompt is fast and done all at once. Writing the answer happens one word at a time. One extra word of answer costs about as much waiting time as a hundred words of prompt, and output is priced several times higher. *Do this:* tell the AI the exact shape and length you want and say "no intro, no recap". Before and after: the same hook prompt with and without a length rule, showing cost and seconds.
2. **Beginner: "Two waits: until the first words, and until the end."** First words depend on how much you sent. The end depends on how much it writes. Thinking models make you wait longer for first words because they write hidden notes first, and you pay for those notes.
3. **Beginner (chat app users): "Keep chats short and fresh."** Long chats get slower, cost more of your usage allowance and get worse. Start a fresh chat after N runs. This is the long-chat test from our listings, with our measured numbers.
4. **Intermediate: "Put what never changes first."** Caching explained with the cost tool: the same brand brain card at the top of every run gets reused at a fraction of the price and comes back faster. Things that break it: today's date at the top, a changing order of items, the customer's name early. It only works above a minimum prompt length. *Tool:* the "cache or not?" check.
5. **Intermediate: "If it can wait, batch it."** Half price for anything you don't need this minute: tagging 5,000 reviews, a weekly report, 50 ad variants for next week. Shown with the review tagger's `--batch` switch and the "run at 6, read at 9" routine pattern.
6. **Intermediate: "Ask for changes, not a rewrite."** When fixing a long document, ask for a list of edits. It's faster, cheaper and easier to review.
7. **Advanced: "The cheapest model that passes your test."** Use your test set from steps 2–3. Try cheap, mid and top. Measure **cost per good result**, not cost per run, since a cheap model that fails three times isn't cheap. Then try the top model on low effort before you build a cheap-then-top chain.
8. **Advanced: "Run independent steps at the same time."** In a workflow, steps that don't depend on each other can run together. Mind your account's rate limits and retry politely.
9. **Advanced (app builders): "Streaming makes it feel fast, but check before you show."** Stream progress, and show the final text after your checks if it contains prices or claims. Comes with the starter app template.
10. **Expert: "What the providers do that you can't."** A one-page tour of caching, batching, speculative decoding and quantization on the provider side. It explains why the same model can score differently on different hosts, and when self-hosting starts to make sense (high volume, open model, an engineer to run it). It links to step 10 for fine-tuning and distillation.

**Glossary entries for Learn:** first-words wait (TTFT), time per word (TPOT), total time, cached tokens, batch, streaming, reasoning/thinking tokens, effort, cost per good result.

## 7. Questions for Kevin

1. **Should speed count in the "Recommended" pick?** My proposal: no change to the quality rule ("cheapest within 5 points of best"), but measure cost **per good run** instead of per run. When two picks are within 10% on cost, the faster one wins. The page also gets a "fastest" note when a qualifying model is at least 2× faster.
2. **Batch by default for factory test rounds?** It halves model-test spend, so $25 a round goes about twice as far. The cost is that results come back in minutes to hours instead of seconds. Recommendation: yes for golden-set and judge rounds; online only for the 3-run speed probe and for debugging.
3. **Normal-view numbers.** I propose exactly three on the listing card: cost per run, time per run, tested date. Everything else goes in Advanced. OK?
4. **Caps on billed or list price?** Proposal: caps count billed spend, the pre-flight estimate uses list price with no cache, and we add time caps (120 s per call, 15 min per run). Confirm the time caps.
