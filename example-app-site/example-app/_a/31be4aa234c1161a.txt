## Do and don't

- Do: Casual internet cadence, like a funny friend who is into wellness but not preachy
- Do: Say the gross thing plainly: 'better poops', 'Fix the Hot Girl Tummy'
- Do: Short 3-beat rhythms: 'Rip. Tip. Enjoy.'
- Do: Lead with numbers: 60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins and minerals, 6g fiber, 1 pack
- Do: Name the enemy by how it feels: chalky powders, gagging on pills, a cobbled-together stack
- Do: Tie every benefit to ease: 1 pack, grab and go
- Don't: Clinical or lab-coat tone in hooks (science goes in the proof beat)
- Don't: Guilt or fear about health, or shaming parents
- Don't: Disease claims: cures, treats, prevents
- Don't: Long paragraphs
- Don't: Em dashes
- Don't: Generating Shrek, Minions or Popsicle characters (licensed IP): use flavor names only
- Don't: Quoting a single discount % as fact, or promising the guarantee on repeat orders
- Never say: "cures", "treats", "prevents", "cure IBS", "treats bloating", "heals", "guarantee on every order", "clinically proven to"

## Guardrails (from refunds and complaints)

- Never claim Sugar-Free is healthier or better than Low Sugar. Ads must warn that Sugar-Free may cause stomach upset (gas, cramping, bloating) in sensitive stomachs, and recommend Low Sugar as the gentler option for digestive sensitivity. Banned phrases: "sugar-free healthier", "sugar-free better", "sugar-free gentler".
- Do not promise instant comfort or immediate results. Ads must disclose that some customers experience gas, bloating, or gurgles in the first few days as the body adjusts, and that this is normal. Banned phrases: "instant comfort", "immediate relief", "no adjustment period".
- Emphasize the 30-day money-back guarantee as a risk-free trial. Ads should frame the guarantee as protection against wasted money, not as a promise of fast refund processing.
- Do not claim 'easy cancel' or 'cancel anytime' unless the cancellation flow is genuinely simple and does not loop or redirect to pause options. If claiming easy cancellation, be prepared to back it with frictionless UX. Banned phrases: "easy cancel", "cancel anytime", "one-click cancel".
- The 30-day guarantee applies to the first order only. Ads and all checkout pages must state 'first order only' clearly and upfront to prevent refund disputes on subsequent orders. Banned phrases: "30-day guarantee all orders", "money-back guarantee every order".
- Limited-time flavors are available for one month only, then the subscription reverts to Original. Ads and cart must state this swap-back rule clearly before purchase so customers are not surprised when their second box arrives. Banned phrases: "lto flavor continues", "limited flavor permanent".
- Do not overpromise taste or claim every customer will love the flavor. Ads should acknowledge that taste is personal and lean on the 30-day guarantee as the safety net for flavor mismatches. Banned phrases: "tastes like candy", "everyone loves the taste", "delicious snack".
- Never promise fast results, energy boosts, or digestion improvements within a specific timeframe (e.g., 2 weeks). Ads should focus on the daily habit and nutrient content, not on speed of benefit. Banned phrases: "energy in two weeks", "better digestion fast", "results in 14 days", "energy boost guaranteed".
- Do not promise that picky eaters or all children will enjoy the gummies. Ads should acknowledge that taste and texture are personal, especially for kids, and recommend the 30-day guarantee for families trying them. Banned phrases: "kids love them", "picky eaters will enjoy", "all children love".
- Do not promise constant availability or guarantee that customers can always swap flavors or reorder their preferred variant. Ads should note that popular flavors may sell out and encourage early reorder. Banned phrases: "always in stock", "guaranteed availability", "swap anytime".
- Do not promise that limited-time flavors will work for the whole family. Ads should acknowledge that flavor preference is personal and remind customers of the 30-day guarantee if a family member dislikes the LTO variant. Banned phrases: "whole family loves", "kids will love this", "family favorite flavor".
