Learning / Model guide

Which model should write your copy?

We gave 6 models the same brief and had 2 other models judge the results without knowing who wrote what. This guide shows what each one wrote, how it scored and what it cost, so you can pick the model that suits your brand for each step of the work.

Choose your models Read what they wrote

The test

Run on 7 October 2026 for 1 supplements brand, Grüns. 5 buyers, 1 at each stage of awareness. For each buyer every model wrote 5 feed headlines, 4 primary texts and 2 video scripts, and planned 1 of the scripts as a video. In all: 150 headlines, 120 primary texts, 60 scripts and 30 video plans. Same instructions, same checks, same day: only the writer changed.

The copy below is test work for a brand we study. It was not made by or for Grüns and none of it has run as an ad. It is 1 test on 1 brand: use it as a starting point, then try the model you pick on a few pieces of your own and read them.

What the test found

  1. 1

    Claude Opus 5.5 wrote the best copy

    Each judge ranked it first in 11 of its 20 readings and scored it 6.9 out of 10. It led on every kind of copy: headlines, primary texts, video scripts and video plans.

  2. 2

    No model reached 8

    8 is the mark for copy a judge would run as it stands. A better model helps, and the copy still needs a person's eye before it goes out.

  3. 3

    The dearer model of a pair was not the better one

    Gemini 3.1 Pro scored below Gemini 3.8 Flash with both judges, at nearly twice the cost. DeepSeek V4 Pro scored below DeepSeek Flash with both judges.

  4. 4

    DeepSeek Flash is the value pick

    It came second with 1 judge and third with the other, at under a third of what Claude Opus cost.

  5. 5

    GPT-6 Sol was the most accurate and the least varied

    The judges disagree most about it: one put it second and the other last. The judge that put it second is a model of the same line, so read that row with care.

  6. 6

    Every model was weakest on variety and accuracy

    All 6 were handed the same buyer and the same customer quotes, and all 6 told much the same story. That is the brief more than the writer: no model made the copy varied on its own.

Pick by the job

A starting point from this test, not a rule. Every step of the work can be put on its own model, and 1 choice sets every step that writes words a customer reads.

Problem aware buyer. The parent of a picky eater, for the kids' product. No offer.

Gemini 3.8 Flash

6.1 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 3 of 6. Chalky vitamins is a vivid, parent-voiced pain point. But 'Kids eat these greens without a fight' states a guaranteed outcome and 'greens' is not in the facts, so accuracy suffers.

Best line: My kid won't chew chalky vitamins.

Weakest line: Kids eat these greens without a fight.

6.0 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 4 of 6. “Dinner shouldn't be a veggie fight” recognizes the parent's frustration without blame. But “A month of kids' packs is just $24.99” obscures both the 28-pack quantity and the introductory subscription condition.

Best line: Dinner shouldn't be a veggie fight.

Weakest line: A month of kids' packs is just $24.99.

Gemini 3.1 Pro

5.7 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 6 of 6. Line 3 borrows a personal first-person claim the brand cannot own and line 1 hedges with 'may'. Line 5 says one pack ends the fight, an overpromise. Little story.

Best line: Your first 28 packs cost just $24.99.

Weakest line: My kid eats these strawberry gummies.

5.9 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 5 of 6. “Your kid may ask” becomes “1 pack ends the daily veggie fight,” replacing a believable possibility with a hard sell. “Skip the nightly dinner fight” repeats that same payoff rather than adding another reason.

Best line: My kid eats these strawberry gummies.

Weakest line: 1 pack ends the daily veggie fight.

DeepSeek Flash

5.9 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 4 of 6. Good number-led price line, but 'Kids can't taste the 6g of fiber' is an unsupported taste claim and 'ends the veggie fight' overpromises. 'Yummy gummy' is cutesy.

Best line: A pack they'll eat costs 89 cents a day.

Weakest line: Kids can't taste the 6g of fiber.

4.3 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 6 of 6. “1 yummy gummy pack your picky kid eats” sounds forced, while “they'll eat” and “He asks” repeat acceptance without earning it. “89 cents a day” leaves out the introductory subscription condition.

Best line: Kids can't taste the 6g of fiber.

Weakest line: 1 yummy gummy pack your picky kid eats.

Weakest line: 6g of fiber in a pack kids ask for

6.0 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 3 of 6. “Veggie fight over,” “kids ask for,” and “Now they ask” keep repeating the promised outcome. “First pack $24.99” also blurs one daily pack with the 28-pack subscription pouch.

Best line: They refused the chewable. Now they ask.

Weakest line: First pack $24.99. Veggie fight over.

Claude Opus 5.5

7.1 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 1 of 6. Opens on a concrete, relatable image (peas under the napkin) and the trash line, so the set feels like real dinner scenes. Offer line is clear. Weaker on pushing the product, and quote marks on line 4 are odd.

Best line: She hides her peas under the napkin.

Weakest line: "He asks for his pack after breakfast."

7.1 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 2 of 6. “She hides her peas under the napkin” and “The chewable vitamin went in the trash” create distinct, recognizable scenes. But “He asks for his pack after breakfast” presents an unsourced testimonial, and no headline names gummies.

Best line: She hides her peas under the napkin.

Weakest line: "He asks for his pack after breakfast."

GPT-6 Sol

5.9 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 5 of 6. Leans on 'gummy pack' and '1 pack' repeatedly, so lines feel like one idea retold. Ease ties are on brand, but the lunchbox line is off the dinner problem and 'kids' gummies' reads stiff.

Best line: Chewable refused? Grab 1 gummy pack.

Weakest line: 1 pack of gummies fits in a lunchbox.

7.3 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 1 of 6. “1 pack of gummies fits in a lunchbox” gives a concrete use beyond “Skip the veggie fight.” But “28 days of kids' gummies for $24.99” needs the first-subscription-order qualification.

Best line: Skip the veggie fight with 1 gummy pack.

Weakest line: 28 days of kids' gummies for $24.99.

How it was made. Each model wrote through the same steps the app uses for real work. Every text was then read by a second model as a stranger would read it, and the lines it failed were said again by a third, the same for every column. So a column is its writer plus the same safety net.

The writing cost $22.96 in all and the judging $1.72. 4 kinds of copy were judged; the video plans are scored above and not printed here.
