Brand overview

Brand story
Chad Janis came up with Grüns while doing his MBA at Stanford (2022 to 2023). He spent about a year on formulation and spoke to 20 co-manufacturers before launch in 2023. His core insight, in Unilever's words: the supplement industry's biggest barrier "wasn't awareness, but adherence." People know they should take greens. They quit because powders taste bad, pills are a chore, and stacks are expensive. Grüns turns the daily dose into a snack people want.
Origin framing on site
"Like you, we were disappointed. There's an infinite number of 'nutrition' companies, all focused on what's best for them, not what's best for you." Then: "No more choking on and popping pills like a pharmacy. No more clean-up and disgust from gross powders. And no more cobbling together handfuls to dozens of supplements."
Mission
"Our mission is to make daily nutrition simple and enjoyable."
Elevator pitch
Grüns is a daily pack of green gummy bears with 60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins and minerals, and 6g of prebiotic fiber. It replaces greens powders and a shelf of separate supplements. It costs about $1.78 a day on subscription, tastes like fruit snacks, and fits in a pocket. There is an adult line and a kids line (ages 2 to 13).
Brand promise
You will actually take it every day, and you will feel it.

Brand voice and tone

Personality
playful, self-aware, casual, confident, a bit cheeky
Writes like
a funny friend who is into wellness but refuses to be preachy about it

Voice do's

  • Use casual internet cadence. Real example: "We made daily nutrition, like, ridiculously easy."
  • Make fun of the brand's own perks. Real example: "Savings, exclusive flavors, and a minor sense of superiority."
  • Say the gross thing plainly. Real examples: "better poops", "Fix the Hot Girl Tummy", "One pack a day keeps the gut happy 💩"
  • Use short 3-beat rhythms. Real examples: "Rip. Tip. Enjoy." and "No mixing, no mess, no excuses."
  • Pun on the umlaut. Real examples: Exclüsives, Hüdie, Ü Snacks, Nütrops, Immün, Jüced, "Grüns Care Bears" (support team)
  • Name the enemy (powders, pills, stacks) by how they feel: "gagging on pills, sloshing chalky powders, or cleaning messy counters"
  • Lead with numbers: 60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins and minerals, 6g fiber, 1 pack

Voice don'ts

  • No clinical or lab-coat tone in headlines. Science lives in the proof section, not the hook.
  • No guilt or fear about health. The frame is "easy and fun", never "you're failing your body".
  • No long paragraphs in ads. Site copy blocks rarely pass 3 lines.
  • No disease claims. Every benefit is "supports" or "promotes".

Brand world

Places
A home kitchen counter in the morning. A desk. A sofa. Outdoors in the sun: a garden table, a picnic. For Kids, the hallway or the breakfast table before the school run.
Surfaces and props
A plain backdrop in a 2-colour gradient (yellow to green for Original, pink to yellow for Kids, red to cream for Raspberry Lemonade). A backpack, a tote or a gym bag. Fruit that matches the flavour: strawberries, lemons, raspberries.
Light and time of day
Bright daylight, mostly morning. Sunny and candy-like. Never moody or dim.
People
Everyday adults of about 25 to 50 in casual clothes, smiling, caught mid-action. Groups of friends for limited flavours. Children appear only in the brand's own photos, never generated.
People on file
Nobody. No founder, customer or expert photo has been uploaded with that person's say, so none can appear.
Never in frame
Pill bottles. Shaker cups or powder tubs, except as the "before". Lab coats and clinics. Muted tones. Shrek, Minions or Popsicle characters. Another brand's pack.

Unique selling propositions

Ranked by how often they appear across site, ads, and reviews:

Taste that drives adherence.
"Tastes like a treat, works like a supplement." 95% of surveyed users take it 4 to 6 times a week, 80% daily (survey of 3,000+ customers).
Replaces a shelf of products.
60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins and minerals, 6g prebiotic fiber, adaptogens, super mushrooms in 1 pack. "Our hope is this tasty daily habit can replace a big shelf in your medicine cabinet."
Portable single-serve pack.
"Toss it in your bag. Pop it at your desk. No shaker, no water, no routine overhaul required."
Proof it absorbs.
2025 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study: +40.1% vitamin C (2.7x placebo), +20.5% folate (2.9x placebo). 3rd-party tested for 70 pesticides, 4 heavy metals, 16 contaminants, 9 microbial contaminants.
Cheaper than the powder it replaces.
$1.78/day on subscription vs AG1 Next Gen at $2.63/serving. HSA/FSA eligible through Truemed.

Target audience summary

Adults who have tried and quit greens powders, pill stacks, or multivitamins, and want one easy habit they will keep. The emotional peak sits with buyers fixing gut and regularity problems. A second funnel sells parents on Grüns Kids with cartoon collabs. Full personas are in the Customers tab.

Brand guardrails

Never say
"cures", "treats", "prevents" any disease. Keep "supports" language. No FDA disclaimer line in ad copy for now (decided 2026-10-04: the brand's own ads do not carry one).
Never show
pill bottles or powders as the hero product, clinical settings, sad or sick people
Always
tie the benefit to ease ("1 pack", "grab and go"), show the gummy, give a number
Never use em dashes
in any copy written for this brand. Use commas, periods, or hyphens.
Licensed IP
Shrek, Minions, and Popsicle are licensed collabs. Do not generate those characters in AI ads. Refer to the flavor name only.
