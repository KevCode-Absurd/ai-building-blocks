What every writer is told

These few lines go with every script and every image ad, beside the brand's facts and its voice. They are cut from the documents, so change a part here and the document changes with it.

What the brand wins on

  • Taste that drives adherence. "Tastes like a treat, works like a supplement." 95% of surveyed users take it 4 to 6 times a week, 80% daily (survey of 3,000+ customers).
  • Replaces a shelf of products. 60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins and minerals, 6g prebiotic fiber, adaptogens, super mushrooms in 1 pack.
  • Portable single-serve pack. "Toss it in your bag.
  • Proof it absorbs. 2025 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study: +40.1% vitamin C (2.7x placebo), +20.5% folate (2.9x placebo).
  • Cheaper than the powder it replaces. $1.78/day on subscription vs AG1 Next Gen at $2.63/serving.

Guardrails

  • Never say: "cures", "treats", "prevents" any disease. Keep "supports" language. No FDA disclaimer line in ad copy for now (decided 2026-10-04: the brand's own ads do not carry one).
  • Never show: pill bottles or powders as the hero product, clinical settings, sad or sick people
  • Always: tie the benefit to ease ("1 pack", "grab and go"), show the gummy, give a number

What we know so far

What has been learned: the diagnosis, what works, what to avoid, decisions and open questions. It grows with every piece of work. Last updated 2026-10-06.

About this brand

Grüns sells daily greens gummies in single-serve sachets: 60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins and minerals, 6g fiber. Founded in 2023 by Chad Janis, it hit a $300M run rate in 24 months and sold 80% to Unilever on 1 June 2026 (reported $1.2B). The whole brand is built on 1 idea: people quit supplements because they are unpleasant, so make one they want to eat.

Brand diagnosis

Re-diagnose roughly every quarter as new learnings come in.

Grüns won on adherence ("the one thing I've been able to stick to"), but 80% of its live Meta headlines now push a 61% discount at price objectors. Its highest-intensity buyers, gut-fix seekers and powder quitters, get only 5 live ads (Hot Girl Tummy), so transformation and "I finally stuck with it" scripts are the biggest open lane.

Market awareness level
Solution Aware to Product Aware. Buyers know greens supplements. Many know Grüns from retail shelves.
Market sophistication
high. AG1, Bloom, Lemme, 8Greens, and MaryRuth's all sell greens gummies now.
Creative diversity level
low on the adult page (offer-led), medium on the Kids page (collabs, ingredient hooks, humor)
Highest-intensity persona
The Gut-Fix Seeker (about 25% est., high intensity)
Biggest opportunity
ads target price objectors. Reviews are full of people who quit powders and fixed their gut. Script for them.

What we know works

  • Taste as adherence: 95% of surveyed users take it 4 to 6 times a week, 80% daily (brand survey, 3,000+ customers)
  • Objection flip with offer: "'Grüns is too expensive': here's 61% off to prove otherwise" has 15+ live variants, the most in the account
  • Pain in slang: "Fix the Hot Girl Tummy" is the only pain-led angle with real volume
  • Licensed LTO every 4 to 8 weeks: Shrek, Minions, Popsicle, Olipop. Each one gives the Kids page a new launch hook.
  • Stacked numbers: "21 Vitamins. 4 Free Gifts. 61% Off." / "60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins, 6g fiber, 1 pack"
  • Enemy framing: powders are "chalky", pills make you "gag", stacks are "cobbled together"
  • Proof: 2025 RCT (+40.1% vitamin C, +20.5% folate), 1M+ members, 4.8 stars from 100K+ reviews
  • Scale: Unilever deal validates the category and the format
  • Benefit-led beauty angle now live at volume: "Nutrition for Healthy Hair" (6 variants created 2026-10-02). Inferred from variant count, not from results.

What to avoid

  • The gummies come out of the torn top of the daily pack: the pack is tipped and they pour out like cereal, into a palm or a bowl. Gummies never come out of the side or the bottom, and never fall from a sealed pack. [learned 2026-10-04, your feedback]
  • A daily pack (the small single-serve sachet) is torn open straight across its top edge, the edge above the Grüns name when the name reads upright. Never torn from the side, the bottom or a corner, and never cut open. [learned 2026-10-04, your feedback]
  • Disease claims ("cures IBS", "treats bloating"). Keep "supports".
  • Recommending Sugar-Free to gut-sensitive buyers without a note. Sugar alcohols upset some stomachs.
  • Promising a guarantee on repeat orders. The 30-day guarantee covers the first order only.
  • Quoting a single discount number as fact. It changes by surface and by week.
  • Generating Shrek, Minions, or Popsicle characters in AI ads. They are licensed IP.
  • More pure discount hooks. The account is already 80% offer-led, so another one adds little signal.

Product notes

  • 1 pouch = 28 sachets, a "small handful" of gummies each
  • Low Sugar vs Sugar-Free on every SKU. Sugar-Free costs about $5 to $7 more.
  • LTO subscriptions swap back to Original from month 2
  • Sold out on 2026-09-30: Raspberry Lemonade Adults Sugar-Free (28 and 84), Firecracker Adults Sugar-Free 84
  • Walmart sells 12-pack sizes ($19.97 Adults, $14.97 Kids)
