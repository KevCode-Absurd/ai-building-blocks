Set up a brand

Guide, chapter 2 of 20

The brand brain is everything the app knows about a brand. Every word it writes and every image it plans comes from here, so an hour spent on this screen is worth more than an hour anywhere else.

The brand brain: brand facts, offers, personas, claims, the look and the creative kit

Adding a new brand

Any brand that sells online can be added, in any category and any currency. Nothing in the app is written for 1 brand.

  1. Open Manage brands (top left) and add the brand by name. You land on its Brand brain screen.
  2. Give the app the brand, in any of 3 ways. 1 is enough; more gives a fuller brain.
    • The website. Type the address and press Read the website. The app opens the homepage and up to 8 pages about what the brand sells, once. About 1 minute, and nothing is paid for. Each page is kept as its own file; the lines that stand on every page (the menu, the footer, the notices) are kept once, apart from the pages; website.md lists the pages. Every later step that needs the site (building the brain, reading its claims, the style card) reads these files. To read the site again after it changes, press Read the website again.
    • What you have written down. Paste a product sheet, a price list or a brand guide. Or put documents straight into the brand's folder.
    • The founder's answers. The questionnaire, typed or spoken.
  3. Press Build the brand brain. About half a minute and under 10 cents.
  4. Read it through and correct it: the products, the prices and every claim.

The dashboard then shows a setup path, 1 step at a time, until the first ads are made: the brand's look, product photos, what customers say, the insights, the first concept, the first ads. Each step has 1 button.

To take a brand off the list, press Remove on the Brands screen. Nothing is deleted, and it can be restored there.

The 6 things to get right

1. Products and real photos

Every image is built on a real photo of the real product. Upload them on the Brand brain screen, in the Product photos card: add photos for each product and choose the main one. (They are kept in assets/<product>/, and files copied there by hand work too.) Use clean photos of each product: the pack front on, the pack at an angle, the product out of its pack, the product in a hand. The app picks a main photo and up to 3 more; you can change the pick each time you make a set.

Pick plain product shots. An infographic or a lifestyle photo as a product reference teaches the image model the wrong thing.

The pictures on the site's own pages. A store's gallery is 10 pictures a product at most. Its pages hold many more: people, places, the product in a hand and in use. On the Brand brain, Products tab, press Pull pictures. The app reads each product's page, the homepage and the main pages it links to, and saves the pictures on them. A picture on 1 product's page is kept with that product. A picture several product pages share, or one from the rest of the site, is kept for the whole brand. Logos, icons, the header, the footer and another product's gallery are left out. Reading the site is free. The pictures are then looked at, 1 by 1, so the app knows what each shows (about 0.2 cents a picture, once); one kept for the brand that turns out to show a product is moved to that product. A second pull adds only what is new, and a picture you removed stays removed. Measured on gruns.co: 26 pages, 182 pictures saved (79 with a product, 103 for the brand), about 18 seconds.

2. Claims

The claims table is the list of things the brand may say as fact. Each has a status:

  • substantiated: the writers may use it, and must cite it.
  • pending: not usable yet.
  • rejected: never used.

Each claim also shows where it came from: the brand's documents, its website, or one of its ads.

Forbidden claims are phrases that are never written, in any wording. A plan that contains one cannot be rendered.

3. Claims from the website and the ads

Claims the brand already makes in public, each with its exact words and where it was found

The brand already says a lot in public. Press "Read the site and the ads" and the app reads the website pages it keeps in the brand's folder (the ones Read the website stored; when none are there yet it reads the site first: the homepage and up to 8 more pages, products, science, reviews), reads the brand's own ads, and lists every claim it finds. 2 or 3 pages are read in 1 go, and the lines that stand on every page are read once. Merch, careers, policies and gift cards are never read.

  • Each claim shows the exact words and a link to the page. A claim whose words are not on the page word for word is thrown away.
  • Can be checked means it has a number, a named study or a named certifier. General promise means it does not.
  • Same as means a claim you already have says this.
  • With claim checks off (the default), these claims are used as the brand published them: you do not have to approve them. Press Set aside on any you do not want used. The card says which mode the brand is in.
  • With claim checks on (Settings), nothing is used until you press Approve. An approved claim joins the claims table, marked "Website" or "Ad".
  • "Approve the N with a number or a named proof" approves the checkable ones in 1 click. It leaves out prices and offers: those belong under offers.

A claim on the website is something the brand says, not proof that it is true. With claim checks off the app takes the brand at its word. If the brand needs every claim looked at before it is in an ad, turn claim checks on.

For ads, the app reads a file called winning-ads.md in the brand folder (an ad library pull), and any of the brand's own ads you added under References.

Figures that disagree. If the same thing is stated with different numbers in different places (55% off on the site, 61% in the ads), a box above the list shows each figure and where it was seen. Press This is live on the right one, or It differs by place: quote no figure. Until you do, any ad that quotes one of the figures carries a warning; after, an ad that quotes a different figure fails its checks. The dashboard lists open ones under "Needs attention". It compares discounts, guarantee lengths and counts of free gifts.

4. Voice

Every step that writes, edits or judges a word a customer reads is sent the same block about the brand's voice: how it sounds, its do and don't, its guardrails on words, the tone for the kind of words being written, how the brand itself writes that kind, and the buyer the piece is for. The brand's voice comes first: where one of the app's own writing rules differs from how the brand writes, the brand's way wins.

How the brand writes. On Brand, the Guidelines tab has a section of that name. Paste the brand's own published work into it, 1 piece to a line: its ad primary text, its ad headlines, its website copy (in a piece of more than 1 line, write " // " where a line breaks). From 3 pieces up, the app counts their shape (how long, how many short blocks, how many open on a question, how many use a tick list or emoji) and sends the writer the counts and a few of the pieces. This is what makes the copy take the brand's own shape and not the app's. Prices and percentages in a piece are hidden from the writer, so an old offer can't be copied.

The brand's video ads. Add each of the brand's own video ads on References (Add reference). The app transcribes it as it analyses it, and the script writer is then shown what the brand's own videos say, line by line, as the model for a script. Nothing to paste. A competitor's video saved as a reference is not used for this.

A product with a smaller pack inside. The image model draws the product from its photo. If the product has an inner pack (a daily sachet, a refill) and the folder holds no photo of that pack on its own, the app keeps the product as photographed as the main thing in every picture and shows the inner pack only small. Add a clean photo of the inner pack to the product's folder to have it drawn right.

The voice rules (do and don't) and the voice sections of the brand's documents are read by the writer and by the voice check. The persona language lists are the customers' own phrases: the writers borrow from them and may quote them.

5. The look: fonts, colours and logo

The brand look, read from the live website

Press "Scan the site". The app opens the brand's website in a browser and reads the real typefaces, their weights and case, the colours by how much of the page they cover, and the logo. It saves a type specimen: a picture of the brand's own fonts and colours.

  • The colours go into every image prompt as hex values with plain names.
  • The typefaces go in as a description an image model can act on ("bold neo-grotesque sans-serif, uppercase"), with the name only when it is a well-known one.
  • Up to 4 typefaces are read, each with where the site uses it: the headline face, a second headline face the site sets big statement lines in (for Grüns, Retail Display beside Work Sans), the body face, and the face of its small tags with a count ("1 small tag on the homepage"). A face found on a few tags is told to the image prompt as a tag face only, never as the type of every label. The main button's own face is read off the button.
  • The specimen is attached to every image that has words on it.
  • An image model cannot load a font file. It draws from the specimen picture and the description, so the result is close to the brand's typeface and not the font itself.

If the scan picks a wrong colour or misses the logo, replace the file in look/ or scan again.

6. The creative kit

The kit decides what production steps are given: copy gets voice, claims and offers; images get colours, type and the product guide; video gets the presenter, the setting and a style line. It also holds, per product, how the pack looks, what is inside and how it is handled. Build it once, read it, and correct anything wrong: a wrong line here repeats in every image.

The brand brain, tab by tab

The Brand screen is the brand's 8 documents and its facts. Every section has an Edit button that opens a form: text, lists, named fields and tables. Nothing needs markdown.

The brain check on the Brain tab runs by rule on every visit and costs nothing: 29 checks, among them that every avatar is backed by real quotes or says it is inferred, that the hero product and the prices agree across documents, and that nothing is blank that a writer depends on. It reports; it never blocks.

What a camera needs (added 2026-10-05). A picture or a clip made from a description of a product gets the product wrong: in a test of 2 clips both came back in the wrong colour. So each product in the catalogue now has 7 fields for the camera, filled by someone who has looked at the product: Looks like (the thing itself, out of its pack), Pack, How it is used (the steps, in order), Visible result, Moves and sounds, Never show, and Reference photos, which names 1 photo for each job as pack front: file; single unit: file; out of pack: file; in hand: file; in use: file; close up: file. The photos are then attached in that order and each is called what it is. single unit is the 1 serving or piece a customer uses at a time, where the product is sold as a box, pouch or bottle of them; out of pack is the product itself with no packaging on it. Name both where the product has them: a video clip about the single serving is then sent that serving's own photo, and the pack it is sold in only when that pack is on screen (see Production). On the Guidelines tab, Brand world says where the brand's pictures happen and what is never in frame, and Motion and sound says how its video moves and sounds. A product also has 7 fields for the writers (Kind, How it works, Replaces, Made of, Size and fit, In the box, Lasts and care), and the Products tab holds Delivery, returns and guarantee and Questions buyers ask. Fill the ones that fit the product; the brain check lists the blanks.

Before you make a format, its page lists what that format reads from the brain for the product you picked, what is blank, and what goes wrong without it. It never stops you. pnpm brain:check <brand> prints the same for every format at once, with the brain check, and costs nothing.

What every writer is sent is shown at the bottom of the Brain tab: a short brief built from these documents. Edit a document and the brief follows. An ad that shows a customer's words is also sent the quotes under each avatar's "Grounded in", and may show no other: keep real, sourced quotes there.

The brand guide (the link at the top of the Brand screen) is 5 printable pages in the brand's own colours and type, with a tokens file to download. Open it, press Ctrl+P and set margins to none.

The brain keeps itself up to date. The card on the Brain tab reads 3 things: the ad account (what returns more or less than the account's average, and what has never been tried), your feedback and verdicts (a point made twice, or named as a reason a piece was approved), and the winners you add. Each becomes 1 line under "What we know works", "What to avoid" or "Ideas to explore", with its date and what it rests on. When the numbers move, the line is replaced. The newest lines are the first every writer is sent.

  • A learning from real data is added on its own. Switch that off with Add what it learns on its own and each one waits for you.
  • A learning that rests on a simulated account is never added on its own. It waits, with a label, and you can add it or set it aside.
  • Take it back removes a line from the brain. It does not come back unless you let it.
  • Every line added, replaced or taken back is written in the Changes tab.

Add what is working on the Winning ads tab: your best ad, landing page, email or line, with how it did and why you think it works. With a result and a reason, it becomes a line under "What we know works" at the next check.

Real people: the founder, customers, experts

A generated face is never shown as the founder, a customer or an expert. To show a real one, upload a photo or a video of them under "Real people" on the Brand screen: give the name, the role, a title if an ad may state it, and tick They agreed to be in ads. A video gets a still, taken 1 second in, for image ads.

With a founder uploaded, a founder ad in a sprint is given that photo. Without one, a founder ad is the founder's words as a signed note, and a testimonial is the customer's words with no face. The files stay on this machine and are never uploaded to the code repository.

The founder questionnaire

The founder questionnaire: answers can be typed or spoken

Optional, and worth doing for a real brand. It asks what the data cannot answer: why the product exists, what the founder would never say, who it is not for. Answers can be typed or spoken. They are saved as a file in the brand folder and read with the brand's other documents.

Checklist before you make anything

  • Each product has clean photos, and the main one is a plain pack shot
  • Claims are marked substantiated only where the brand can prove them
  • Forbidden claims are listed
  • Offers and the guarantee are exact, with every condition
  • No figure is open under "Figures that disagree"
  • The site is scanned, and the fonts and colours look right
  • The creative kit is built and read
