Bring in research

Guide, chapter 3 of 20

Good ads use the customer's own words. This chapter covers getting those words in (Sources), reading what they add up to (Insights) and turning them into something to make ads from (Concepts).

Sources

Sources: 7 kinds, each with its own job

There are 7 kinds of source in 2 groups. 5 carry what customers say, and each is best at 1 thing. 2 carry what the brand says.

SourceIts main job
Call recordingsThe last objection before buying ("I nearly didn't...")
Support ticketsWhat goes wrong: the failure modes that become guardrails
ReviewsProof points and the buyer's own words
Post-purchase surveyWhy they bought, and what they tried before
CommunityThe language people use among themselves
Website (the brand)What the brand already claims in public, with the page each claim stands on. Where a claim is checked
Founder (the brand)What the data can't show: why the product is made as it is, what the founder hears buyers say

What the brand says: the Website and Founder tabs

These 2 are kept apart from the 5 customer sources, on purpose.

  • Website. Give the address and press Read. The app opens the homepage and the pages about what the brand sells, keeps their text, and lists every claim they make with the exact words and the page. You approve the ones the ads may use. Below that, every claim in the brand brain is marked as stated on the site or not on the pages read. A claim the site does not state is not wrong: it needs its own source. No insights are taken from the website, because a brand's own copy says nothing about what its buyers want. About 4 cents to read a site.
  • Founder. The answers to the founder questionnaire, 1 item for each section answered. Press Bring in the answers and they are read for insights like any other source (about 1 cent a section). Each insight is kept as the founder's words: it is never quoted in an ad as a customer's, it adds nothing to a theme's source count, and a concept cannot rest on the founder's account alone.

Importing

Press Import on a tab and give it a file: a spreadsheet or CSV for reviews, tickets and surveys (you map the columns), a document or transcript for calls. Then:

  1. Cleaning. Names, emails, phone numbers, addresses and order numbers are removed before any text is sent to a model. This is not optional and cannot be switched off.
  2. Extraction. A model reads each item and pulls out insights: a quote, copied word for word, with its type and how strongly it was felt (1 to 5). A quote that is not in the text is thrown away.
  3. Open an item to see its quotes highlighted in place.

"Extract all pending" runs extraction on everything waiting. It costs well under 1 cent an item.

Insights

Insights: themes ranked by how strongly, how often and how widely they are said

Insights groups the quotes into themes and ranks them.

  • Score. Intensity counts for half, frequency for about a third, and how many kinds of source it shows up in for the rest. The weights are in Settings.
  • Type. Each theme is 1 of 10 kinds: an accommodation, a failed solution, a final objection, a desire, a proof point, a buyer word, a failure mode and so on. The type decides which part of a script the theme feeds.
  • The letters C T R S M show which sources a theme appears in. A theme said on calls, in tickets and in reviews is stronger than one said in 1 place.
  • Click a theme to read its quotes. Every quote links back to where it was said.

Things you can do with a theme: rename it, merge it with another, split it, or press Use in concept.

Guardrails

A theme of type failure mode (something that caused refunds or complaints) becomes a guardrail: a rule the writers must keep and the checks enforce. For Grüns, "Sugar-Free causes stomach upset" became a rule that no ad may promise a calm stomach on the Sugar-Free product. The Guardrails tab lists them.

Concepts

Concepts: a persona, an angle and an offer

A concept is 1 persona, 1 angle and 1 offer. It maps to 1 Meta ad set, and its name is built for that.

  • Suggest concepts proposes concepts from your top themes. Accept the ones worth making.
  • New concept builds one by hand. The builder will not let you save a concept whose evidence is all pain and no desire: an ad needs somewhere to go.
  • The differentiator test warns when the main claim is something a competitor could say too.
  • Stage is where the buyer is: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware. It changes how every script for the concept opens.
  • Change 1 variable copies a concept with 1 thing changed, so a test changes only that.

From a concept: Write scripts (chapter 4) or Make assets.

How much research is enough

  • Under about 30 items, themes are thin and the ranking moves a lot. Treat the output as a first read.
  • The most useful single import is call recordings: they carry the final objection, which no other source gives.
  • Re-run clustering after a large import. Themes you renamed or locked are kept.
