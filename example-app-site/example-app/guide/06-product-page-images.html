Product page images

Guide, chapter 6 of 20

The images a shopper swipes through on a product page. The app builds them from a 12-frame template: each frame answers 1 question a shopper has.

Go to Create, then Product images for your landing pages.

The 12 frames: each wireframe beside a real example you can swap

The 12 frames

#FrameThe question it answers
1What does your product do?What is this, and what does it do for me?
2Product benefits at a glanceWhat do I get out of it?
3Benefit-driven headlineWhy does it work?
4How your product worksHow do I use it, and is it easy?
5Reviews and customer contentDo people like me use it?

2 more kinds can be added as extra images: a product shot with no words, and facts and specs (a nutrition or ingredients panel).

Setting up a set

Each row shows the template's wireframe on the left and a real example from the library beside it.

  • Swap opens the library on that frame's examples. Pick the one whose style you want.
  • None builds the image from the wireframe alone.
  • The switch turns a frame off. A page rarely needs all 12; 6 to 8 is common.
  • The direction box takes anything specific to that image.
  • Add from the library adds an image outside the 12.

Choose the sizes (1:1 for most store themes, 4:5 for portrait themes) and press Write the plan.

How a frame is used

The wireframe is a starting structure, not a design to copy.

  1. The job is kept. Each frame answers its question.
  2. The rest bends to what the product can truthfully fill. A supplement cannot show a body before and after, so that frame becomes the old routine against the new one.
  3. The frame sets the structure; the example shows the craft. Where they disagree on structure, the frame wins.
  4. Nothing of the wireframe is drawn. Its grey boxes become photographs and its placeholder words become your words.
  5. A frame the brand cannot fill truthfully is changed or left out. No invented review, rating or comparison.

Reviewing the plan

The plan screen works the same way as for ads (chapter 5), without the ad review and the feed copy. For each image you see the frame, the example, the argument, the words with their sources, and the read before rendering.

Things to watch for on this kind of set:

  • Reviews and testimonial frames need real reviews. If the research has no usable quote, the plan writes [REAL REVIEW NEEDED]. Fill it before publishing.
  • No 2 images answer the same question.
  • Words must read on a phone. About 30 words an image; a facts panel may run to 45.
  • Certifications and press appear only if they are in the brand's claims or proof assets.

Rendering

The same as ads, with the wireframe attached too as "structure only". About 15 cents an image and 2 automatic looks at each one.

Tips

  • Start with 6 frames: 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 11 cover most pages.
  • Use examples from brands whose look is close to yours. The example drives the styling.
  • Thin buckets (occasions, reviews) have few examples. "None" often gives a cleaner result there.
