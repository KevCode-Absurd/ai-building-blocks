References

Guide, chapter 10 of 20

Ads saved from other brands, analysed, to learn from and build on. Saving an ad does not prove it sold well. There are 2 collections.

When the app picks examples for pictures, it considers the buyer's stage and your kind of product. Among equally good matches, it prefers examples your brand has been shown less often. Automatic sets avoid using the same advertiser twice. Your own reference choice still takes priority.

Chat uses the saved notes about how an ad persuades. Scripts can now study a small selection of real ads: how they open, develop an idea, turn and close. The brand's own voice and facts stay in charge. Image planning also reads the linked persuasion notes, and its words pass can study the source's headline and supporting lines. Your reference controls still apply.

Photo and PDP suggestions rotate among equally suitable examples. Photoshoots use the reference's set and styling notes alongside its camera and light. These connections make more of the existing library available; better outputs still need to be confirmed by comparing the same briefs before and after.

The reference library: filter by awareness, sophistication, category and format

The reference ads (this screen)

About 1,000 image and video ads, each analysed for its awareness stage, the market's sophistication, its hook, its structure and why it works. They are shared by every brand.

Find one. Filter by image or video, awareness stage, sophistication, category and format, or search hooks, advertisers and transcripts.

Open one to read its analysis: what the ad does beat by beat, what transfers to another brand and what does not.

Make an ad from it. Press "Make an ad from this". A screen opens with the ad on the left and your choices on the right.

On the left is what the app found in the ad: what it is, why it works, its best parts, what did not work, and for a video everything that is said.

On the right, 4 steps:

  1. How close should it stay?
    • Recreate it for my brand: the ad rebuilt part by part. For a video, it is read step by step (what happens, and what each step does for the buyer), and each step is redone with what does that job for your brand. A step that does not fit your brand is changed or dropped. For an image, the picture's layout is kept and the product, words and colours become yours.
    • Keep what makes it work: why it worked, applied your own way. Its order of steps is not followed. This is where it starts.
    • Loose inspiration: only the idea of the ad. Everything else is yours.
  2. What it is about, and who it is for.
    • A product, or The whole brand (an ad about the brand, tied to no one product).
    • A persona, or All buyers.
    • An offer, the offer that fits the buyer, or No offer (no price, no deal, no ask for the sale).
    • Where the buyer is. Left alone, it follows the persona.
  3. What to make. Tick Static ads, Video, or both.
    • Each shows up to 3 formats the app suggests for this ad, with a reason. The first is marked Recommended. Any other format is in the menu below them.
    • Static ads: how many, and the sizes. Every ad is made in every size you pick.
    • Video: how many, the length, the shape, and how many openings for each. The script is written first; its plan, its pictures and the prompt for every clip follow on the Production board.
  4. Anything else to change? Type it, or press Speak and say it. "Make it for mornings." "Leave out the doctor."

Then press Make. Static ads open as a set on the Create screen. Scripts appear on the Scripts screen, and each one's plan on the Production board.

You do not pick a concept. One is made of your answers and filed on the Concepts screen as a draft.

For your own ad made in a new format with its words unchanged, tick "This is my own ad" under Video.

Add your own. "Add reference" takes an image or a video. You can also drop a file anywhere on the screen, or paste a picture you copied. It is analysed (about 1 cent) and joins the library with an "Added by you" badge. Add:

  • ads you admire from other brands,
  • your own brand's ads. These are also read for claims the brand makes (chapter 2).

How a reference is used

A reference is a recipe, not a script. The app takes its layout, its device and its light. It never takes its words, its claims, its brand, its product or its people. The finished image is checked for anything from the reference leaking in, and fails if another brand's name or product appears.

Tick references and build

You can build on exactly the ads you choose.

  1. Press the small box at the top right of a reference to tick it. A note box opens at the bottom of the screen.
  2. Say what this one should do: type it, or press Speak and say it. Press Send (or Enter). Leave it empty if the reference speaks for itself.
  3. In the same box: How many to make from it (1 to 4), the product it is for, and the buyer it is written for.
  4. Tick as many as you want, up to 24. Each ticked reference sits in the bar at the bottom; press its picture to open its note again.
  5. Pick the product for the whole build, open Options for the 3 choices about words (see the image ads chapter) and a note for all of them, and press Build.

An image reference becomes a static ad built on that reference: its structure, its reading order and the job each piece of text does, with your product, your words and your colours. A video reference becomes a script that keeps its structure. The plan is written and the images are made straight after it.

Organise for your brand

Organise for (your brand) puts the references that suit your brand best first, and says why on each card. A reference moves up when:

  • it is from your industry (read from the brand's category),
  • it speaks to buyers your ads do not reach (the gaps on the Coverage screen),
  • it argues the way your market still listens to (your market's stage, and above it),
  • it is a kind of ad you have never run,
  • it looks like what the Brain tab says works. It moves down when it looks like what you avoid.

The panel under the button says what the order rests on. Until an ad account is connected the gaps come from a simulated account, and the panel says so.

Filters: industry (yours, or any), stage of awareness, market stage, type of ad (problem and solution, proof, offer, us against them, product, lifestyle), and Only where (your brand) is lacking. The weights behind the order are the app's judgement. They have not been measured against results.

Tips

  • When an ad of yours works, add it as a reference. The next scripts can then be built on something proven for your own buyer.
  • A reference at the same awareness stage as your concept transfers best.
  • The analysis lists what not to transfer. Read it before using an ad from a different category.
