Costs, caps and settings

Guide, chapter 11 of 20

Every model call is logged with its tokens and its cost. This chapter shows where to see the spend, what the caps are, and what each thing costs to make.

The 3 caps

CapSet byWhat it limits
Text modelsLLM_BUDGET_USD in .envEvery call to a text model, all brands, all time
ImagesMEDIA_IMAGE_CAPThe number of images rendered
VideoMEDIA_VIDEO_BUDGET_USDDollars spent on video clips

The caps are changed in Settings, in the Spend caps card: 3 fields and Save. The values in .env are where an installation starts. A cap is changed 1 way, by typing it and saving. The text cap can't go above ABSOLUTE_BUDGET_USD, the limit of the installation.

When a cap is reached the next paid step refuses to start and says which cap to raise. Raise it in Settings. The dashboard's "Spend and settings" tile shows all 3 as meters, and the sidebar shows the text spend on every screen.

The cost register

Costs in the sidebar is the register of everything paid for. It groups every call into runs: 1 set of images, 1 production job, 1 script or 1 sprint is 1 run. A sprint's sets are counted inside it. Research, the reference libraries and brand set-up are each 1 line.

The top of the screen shows the brand's total, split into text models, images and video, the 3 caps, and a table by model: calls, tokens in, tokens read from cache, tokens out, the price the cost was worked out at, the cost and its share.

Open a run to see its own ledger:

  • The total, and the cost of each finished image counting the text that planned and checked it.
  • By model: the same table, for this run only.
  • Text: each step: planning, prompts, the read before rendering, the claims read, each check on an image. Calls, failed calls, tokens and seconds for each.
  • Images and video: what was rendered, at what quality, the cost of each, second attempts included.
  • Every call, in order, with a running total. A failed call is shown in red with the reason: it was still paid for.

A set's own screen shows what it has cost so far at the bottom, with a link to this ledger. A production job has the same link under its steps.

A model marked est. has no price on file, so its dollars are worked out at a fallback rate. Its tokens are exact.

Where else to see what was spent

Settings: every kind of call, how many, the tokens and the cost

Settings lists every kind of call for the brand: the model, the number of calls, input and output tokens, cache hits and cost. pnpm usage:report prints the same for all brands, with the average tokens and cost of each step.

Inspect: what every step was sent

Inspect, under Costs in the sidebar, shows what went into a model and what came out, read from the log of real calls. Nothing on it costs anything.

  • Steps lists every step that calls a text model: the model it ran on last, how many calls, how many failed, the tokens sent, the share read from cache, the tokens billed out, and the share of that output that was the model's own thinking and not the answer.
  • Open a step for its record: what it is for, what it is meant to be sent, and what it really was sent, cut into blocks (each skill, each part of the brand, the lessons, the work itself) with the tokens of each and the file it came from. Below that: how the step has failed, and its latest calls.
  • Open a call (from a step, or from "Every call, in order" on a run's cost page) for its exact words: each block of what was sent, and the answer. For an image or a clip: the prompt, the references attached, and the result.
  • Skills and prompts lists every skill folder and every prompt file with the steps that are sent it, how much of it, and when last. A file counts as sent when its own lines are found in a logged call, so the list shows what happened, not what the code intends. Open a file to read it.

Changing a part, or saying something about it

Every part shown on Inspect can be changed or commented on from where you are reading it.

  • A block that comes from a file has an Edit link beside it. It opens that skill or prompt file at the part the block came from. Change the text and press Save the file. The file on disk is what the next call reads, so the change takes effect at once, for every brand. Say why in the box beside the button: the reason is kept with the change.
  • Every save is kept. Under the editor is the list of changes saved there. Open one to read the lines that went and the lines that came. Put back the text before this returns the file to what it was before that change.
  • A change that would stop the step is refused, and nothing is written: a prompt file that lost its === USER === line, a new word in curly brackets (a place the app does not fill), a skill file with its opening lines gone, an empty file. A change that is allowed but worth knowing is said after the save: a filled place taken out, or one moved into the standing part of the prompt, which costs the cache.
  • A block the app builds (the brand, lessons, offers, buyers) has no file. Its link goes to the screen where that is changed.
  • Feedback is on every step, every block, every call, every answer, every image or clip, and every file. Write what you saw or what should change and save it. Nothing is sent to a model: a note is work for a person. A note on a block shows on that block in every call of the step.
  • Notes and changes, the third tab, lists every open note with a link to what it is about, the notes that are done with what was done about them, and every change made to a file. A number on the tab is the count of open notes.

This is not the same as feedback on a finished ad, which is on the piece itself and becomes a lesson (chapter 9). Notes here are about the machinery: the prompts, the skills and the steps.

The same in a terminal: pnpm inspect (every step), pnpm inspect script shot_plan (the record of those steps), pnpm inspect --skills (the skill map), pnpm inspect --notes (the notes and the changes).

What 1 image costs to draw (measured over 251 renders, 2026-10-04). Choosing the model and the context of a step

Settings opens on a list of every step the app runs: cleaning a transcript, writing an ad, the voice pass, building personas, and so on. In the Normal view the steps are folded by area and each row has the model and its version. The Advanced view adds what the step measured on its last calls (tokens in, how much came from cache, tokens out, cents), its thinking effort and what it is sent.

Each row has up to 4 choices.

ChoiceWhat it doesThe default
ModelWhich model runs the step: Claude Opus, Sonnet, Haiku or Fable, GPT Sol, Luna or Astra, Gemini Pro, Flash or Flash-Lite, DeepSeek Pro or Flash. The list shows each one's tier (frontier, mid, small) and, where the step was compared, its blind score; the Advanced view adds its price. A model that also suits the step is marked "also suggested"The recommended model. For most steps that is the cheapest whose output held up when compared blind; for the jobs under "Recommended, and also suggested" below it is the one we chose after reading the copy ourselves
VersionLatest keeps the step on the newest version of the model you picked, and moves it when a newer one comes out. Or hold it at 1 versionLatest, once you pick a model
Thinking effortLow, medium or high. More effort is more tokens out, which is most of the cost on the cheap modelsWhat the comparison found enough
What it is sentFocused (only what this step needs), Full (the whole brand brain and whole skills) or Minimal (bare facts and rules)Focused

Writer, at the top of the list, sets 1 model for every step that writes words a customer reads. To help you choose, Learning has a model guide ("Which model should write your copy?"): 6 models wrote the same brief, 2 judges scored it blind, and the guide prints the scores, the cost and everything they wrote side by side.

Recommended, and also suggested (2026-10-08). A step starts on its recommended model, shown as "(recommended)" in its list. A step can also name other models that suit it: a line under the step says "Also suggested", and those models are marked in the list. Nothing runs on a suggested model until you pick it, and you can still choose any model for any step.

What you are makingStarts onAlso suggested

Video scriptsGemini 3.1 Pro

The judges' scores and our own read did not agree everywhere, so these starts are our read: we found the video scripts of Gemini 3.1 Pro the better ones, and little between the models on headlines. The model guide in Learning has the same table, under "Where each job starts", with a third row for video plans. It shows where each job starts, not what you changed it to.

The long primary text of an image ad (the story told to an unaware or problem aware buyer) is its own step in the list, "Primary text of an image ad, where it is a long story", so you can give it its own model. The ad's other words stay with the step that writes the ad.

An answer cut off for length is asked again. A model that thinks before it writes can run out of room part-way through an answer. When that happens the app asks again, as asked, with twice the room. You do nothing.

In the Advanced view, click a step's name to see exactly what it is sent, why it runs on its model, and the scores. A changed step is marked "Changed". "Reset" puts every step back on its recommendation. A change applies from the next run, for every brand.

A step that is sent 1 thing (a transcript, an image, the lines to grade) starts on "Only its own input". You can add the brand in short, or the whole brand brain, to it. It is added as background at the end of the step's instructions; the task and the shape of the answer stay as they were. Nothing compared these additions, so the recommended setting is still the step's own input: more is more tokens on every call, and on a mechanical step it can pull brand words into an answer that should have none.

The 2 checks you switch on

Scripts and image ads are written freely unless you ask for a check. This is on purpose: a check costs tokens, slows the work, and makes the writer hedge. A brand in a regulated category switches claim checks on once and leaves them on.

Settings, "Checks for <brand>" sets what is on when a piece of work starts. Both are off until you switch them on there, and the setting is kept for each brand. Every screen that makes a creative (Write scripts, a new set of ads, an ad from a concept, a sprint, References, Coverage, Make assets) has the same switch for that 1 piece; in the chat you say it ("check the claims"). Each check can also be run on a finished piece with 1 press. While a check is off it does not run: nothing is read and nothing is charged for it. A script or a set from before the switch existed counts as off.

Ad review

A second model reads each image ad as a stranger would and lists what it would fix. It advises and changes nothing, so it is off by default and costs about 0.7 cents an ad when you ask for it: Review these ads on the plan.

Pause, stop and resume

Whenever something is running, waiting or paused, a yellow bar sits at the bottom of every screen. Each line says what it is, how long it has run and what it has spent, with 3 buttons.

ButtonWhat it does
PauseStops at once and keeps the job. Nothing more is spent. What was finished is kept
ResumePicks the job up where it stopped. Finished ads are not written again
StopEnds the job. What was finished is kept; start it again from its own screen if you want the rest

A request already sent to a text model is cut off. An image already being rendered is left to finish, because it is billed either way.

Closing the app never costs money later. If the app is closed while a script, a set, a sprint or a production job is running, that job is paused when the app next starts. It waits for you to press Resume.

Spending less

  • Review the plan before rendering. A plan is text; a wrong image is paid for twice.
  • Render 1 size first. Add the second size once the first is right.
  • Give direction up front. A second planning pass costs as much as the first.
  • Fix the brand brain, not the output. A wrong fact in the creative kit is paid for on every image.
  • Video: approve the look frame carefully. It costs 3 cents and decides every clip.

Settings

  • Every step: its model, its effort, what it is sent. See "Choosing the model and the context of a step" above. The comparisons behind the recommendations are in docs/efficiency.
  • Ranking weights. How much intensity, frequency and source diversity count in a theme's score, with a live preview.
  • Skills. The skill files the app has found.
  • Naming. How Meta ad set and ad names are built.
  • Market. The currency and the accent for talent. Both are read from the brand brain and are changed there. The card no longer has a "Spoken words / second" number (from 2026-10-08). It says in 1 sentence that how fast an ad is spoken follows the age of the person speaking: faster for a younger speaker, a little slower for an older one. A number saved before is kept, and nothing reads it. The paces are in Write scripts, under "How fast a script is said".

The planner

The planner: how many ads to make at your spend, and whether each format pays for itself

Enter monthly ad spend, the revenue target, the average spend per ad, ROAS and margin. The planner gives:

  • how many new ads a month the spend calls for (1 per $1,000 of spend, as a simple rule),
  • how many ads the revenue target needs,
  • a production budget,
  • for each output type, whether an ad of that type earns more than it costs to make,
  • your concept and script mix against the targets (unaware and problem aware share, pain-led share, and the split of replication, iteration and net new).
