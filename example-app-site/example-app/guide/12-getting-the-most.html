Getting the most out of it

Guide, chapter 12 of 20

What separates good output from average output in this app, and what to do when something comes out wrong.

The 8 habits

  1. Spend your time on the brand brain. The writers can only be as specific as the claims, offers and customer language they are given. A brand with 12 sharp claims gets better ads than one with 40 vague ones.
  2. Approve claims from the website. It is the quickest way to widen what the writers may say, and every one carries its source.
  3. Bring in calls. The final objection only shows up in calls, and it is the line most ads are missing.
  4. Use the direction box. Say who the ad is for, where they are in the decision, and the 1 thing it should argue.
  5. Read the plan, not just the images. Most problems in an image are visible in its plan: too many words, 2 arguments, a claim with no source.
  6. Give feedback in full sentences. What is wrong, and what right looks like. Twice is enough for a rule to hold.
  7. Change 1 thing per test. Use "Change 1 variable" on a concept, hook variants on a script, and the similar-pairs filter to avoid testing the same script twice.
  8. Add your winners as references. They become structures the next scripts can follow, and a source of claims.

What the app will not do

These are deliberate.

  • It will not invent a number, a study, a certification, a review or an offer term. Where a fact is missing it writes a placeholder or argues something else.
  • It will not render a plan that contains a forbidden claim or breaks a guardrail.
  • It will not name or show a competitor.
  • It will not present a generated person as a customer or an expert.
  • It will not send names, emails, phone numbers or addresses from your research to a model.
  • It will not start a paid step on its own.

The craft behind the words

Every step that writes, edits or judges words is sent 3 things about your brand: its voice, its facts, and the rules learned from your feedback. Since October 2026 some steps are also sent a short piece of general craft: how a senior direct-response writer would go about that 1 job. It comes from a library of 15 parts (skills/copy-craft), distilled from The Copy Machine.

StepThe craft it is sent
Image ads: the pictureNothing. The scene and the layout are planned the way they always were
Image ads: the words on the image and the feed copyRead the buyer first (where they are, the 1 thing they doubt); what a headline has to do; what makes a point on an image sell
Headlines, primary text and hooks asked for in the chatRead the buyer; then headlines, or the 6 ways into a sale, or where a hook comes from
The copy passName what is interesting in the draft before fixing anything. A fix stays only if it beats the line it replaces
The voice pass14 shapes that read as written by a machine, and what to write instead
The judgesWhat sinks a piece, and what a piece is never marked down for (being bold is not a fault)
Concepts, personas, offers, the sprint plan, a landing pageThe parts their work is
ScriptsRead the buyer; where a hook comes from

Your brand comes first: the block that carries the craft opens by saying that the step's own limits, your brand's voice and your brand's facts outrank it, and that it never makes a piece longer than your brand writes.

Why an ad is written in 2 goes. The craft was tested on Grüns: the same 5 image ads, 10 primary texts, 20 headlines and 3 scripts were made with it and without it. 3 judges read each pair blind and split 43 to 40. Then Kevin read them side by side and chose: the pictures made without the craft, the words written with it. So the app plans an ad's picture first, with no craft in sight, and then writes the words into the places that picture left for them. The second go cannot touch the picture. If its words do not fit a place, that ad keeps the first go's words and the set's log says so. It adds under 1 cent an ad.

Why most steps get little. Sent the full craft, a writer made a more complete argument and a longer piece than the brand writes, and it sounded less like the brand. So the parts that do that (the body, the close, the feed copy as a package) go only to a landing page and an offer. The full account is in DECISIONS.md, phases 41 and 42.

To run the test yourself (it costs about $1 of text and 6 to 10 pictures a run, and about $2.50 to judge a pair of runs): pnpm copy:test before with COPY_CRAFT=off set, pnpm copy:test after, pnpm copy:judge before after, pnpm copy:report before after. The page it writes is in data/copy-test.

Where to look when you want to know why

  • "How this set was made" at the bottom of a set: the models, the skills and the brand fields each step was given, and a log.
  • "Brand context this step was given" on each production step.
  • The Pipeline tab on a source item: every step from raw text to verified insight.
  • The architecture chapter: what is sent to which model, how much, and what it costs.
