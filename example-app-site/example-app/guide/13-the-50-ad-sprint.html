Get 100 ads

Guide, chapter 13 of 20

A sprint makes up to 100 static ads, with 100 selected by default. Choose products and buyers, adjust the seven visual families if needed, then press Make the 100 ads. The default runs the plan, character references, briefs and renders in one go. Turn off automatic generation to review the plan and briefs separately.

Find it under Create, as the wide card below the 3 ways to create, or from the dashboard.

The 100-ad default (9 October 2026)

The default includes 20 ads per awareness stage, with formats matched to those stages. It starts with 40 classic statics, 20 product shoots and 8 each of comic, clay/3D, hand-drawn, post-like and surreal executions. Each buyer keeps its saved product eligibility. Smaller or restricted mixes may not support an equal stage split; the plan shows the actual counts.

Turn on distinct realistic characters to use different fictional library identities where the concept needs a person (up to 24 extra image calls). Nano Banana Pro matches each identity to a scene; GPT Sunburst makes the final ads. The characters are saved to the brand avatar library, so they also remain available for videos. Open a brief's Character reference to inspect the result. These characters are actors, never fabricated customers or experts.

Brand font roles and the typography specimen travel with branded images. Native notes and illustrated formats can intentionally use their own lettering. Inspect rendered copy and font fidelity before publishing. The previous 50-ad examples and costs below describe smaller runs; actual costs are in the run ledger, including character generation.

Before you start

  • Decide on claim checks. The sprint follows the brand's setting (Settings, "Checks"). Off, the ads are written freely and you verify the facts. On, read the next point.
  • Claims. With claim checks off, the sprint uses what the brand publishes on its site and in its ads, and nothing waits on you except a figure stated 2 ways. With claim checks on, the screen lists the claims that still wait and the figures that disagree. You can approve all the same: the ads are written without them. With claim checks on, the sprint can only say what the brand's approved claims, offers and research let it say. More approved claims gives 50 ads more to argue. See "Claims from the website and the ads" in the brand chapter.
  • Settle figures that disagree. If the site says 55% off and the ads say 61%, the Brand screen lists it under "Figures that disagree". Until you say which is live, the sprint quotes none of them.
  • Add the founder, if you want to show them. A generated face is never shown as the founder. Upload a photo or a video under "Real people" on the Brand screen and tick that they agreed; founder ads then show that person. Without one, a founder ad is a signed note.
  • Check the product photos. Each ad is anchored on a real photo of the real pack. A product with 1 photo still works, but more photos give more range.

Start a sprint

The start screen asks 4 things, and the panel on the right adds them up as you go:

  1. How many ads: 4 to 100; 100 is the default.
  2. Which products: a slider each. Move 1 and the others make room, so they always add up.
  3. Types of ads: how the ads are drawn. Tick 1 or more and the ads are shared between them; with 2 or more ticked, a slider each sets the share.
  4. Who the ads are for: every buyer, or the ones you choose.

Then any direction for the whole sprint: a season, an offer to lead with, something to stay away from.

The types of ads

TypeWhat it is
Classic staticBuilt from a reference layout: a headline, product and suitable evidence. This is 40 of the default 100 ads; adjust its share as needed
Product shootYour product as the hero of a photograph: in the studio, up close, in a real place. Few words
Comic bookInked panels, halftone dots and a speech bubble
Clay and 3DSoft clay shapes, tiny dioramas and 3D characters
Hand-drawnA marker sketch, a flat cartoon, a labelled diagram
Looks like a postA message between 2 people, a note, a photo of a billboard
Scroll-stoppers1 picture that cannot be true, played straight: an impossible scene, an X-ray, a game screen

A type is how an ad is drawn; its format is still what the ad does. A comic book testimonial is a customer's words in a speech bubble; a clay demo is the product in use, made of clay. So every type gets the full mix of formats, angles, buyers and hooks.

A classic static is built on a library layout. An ad of any other type is drawn from its idea alone, with no layout: a layout is a photograph of another brand's ad, and a picture built on one stays a photograph. Your real product photo is still attached, so the pack keeps its shape, colours and label. Write the plan costs about 60 cents (and 15 cents the first time, to file the photos) and is the only thing it starts.

Stage 1: the plan

The app does 4 things, in about 3 minutes:

  1. Files every product photo. Each image in the brand's assets folder is looked at once and filed: a packshot, a bundle, a lifestyle photo, the product in use, an infographic. A photo can lead an ad only if it shows the real pack, has no designed text over it, shows no child and carries no licensed character.
  2. Sets the format of every ad. The split comes from the Motion 2026 benchmarks and is exact, because the app does the arithmetic:
ShareGroupFormats
40%ScaleDemo, Testimonial, Headline, Montage, Listicle, Offer-first
30%High hit rate, underusedUnboxing, Founder, POV, Behind the scenes
20%Angle-specificBefore and after, Us vs them, Problem agitation
10%ExperimentalStatistic, Collage, Expert, Native format
  1. Writes the angle map and an idea for every ad. 9 or 10 angles from the personas, the research, competitor gaps and the brand's own winning ads. For each ad: the angle, the buyer, where that buyer is in the decision, the hook, and 1 or 2 sentences on what it argues and shows.
  2. Picks a layout and a photo for each ad. The best free example of the right layout type from the static ad library. No layout is used twice in a sprint, and no reference brand more than twice. An ad of a type other than classic static gets no layout: it gets 1 of the looks of its type (clay has 5, comic book has 1), taken in turn.

The plan screen shows the mix of formats, the angle map, how the ads spread across hooks, stages, buyers and products, the product photos with the number of ads each anchors, and all 50 ads in 1 table.

Points to look at sit above the plan: an angle that carries too many ads, a stage with too few, 2 ads with nearly the same idea. The plan is sent back to the planner once on its own if it misses its limits; what is listed is what is left.

To change the plan:

  • the pencil on a row changes that ad's idea, angle, buyer, stage or hook,
  • the shuffle button gives it another layout (or, for an ad of another type, the next look of that type),
  • Plan again with a note rewrites the whole plan (about 60 cents).

Approve the plan and write the briefs is the first approval.

Stage 2: the briefs

The 50 ads are sorted into sets by product, 10 at most in each, and the studio's ad planner writes each one. A brief is the words on the image, the picture, the layout, the feed copy and the image prompt. Every brief gets what any studio ad gets: the voice check, the claims read and the ad review by a model that did not write it, 1 automatic rewrite, and a read of the prompt before rendering.

2 things are specific to the sprint:

  • each set is told the headlines the sprint already has, so it does not repeat them,
  • when all are written, headlines that still share most of their words are written again, once.

This takes about 3 minutes a set and costs about 2 cents an ad (about 1.5 cents more when the brand has claim checks on). The page updates itself.

The briefs screen groups the ads by format. Each row shows the headline, the other words, the first lines of the feed copy and its checks. Open the set takes you to the studio screen for that ad, where every word and the prompt can be changed. What a claim check flagged is shown on the ad in amber, and the ad is made all the same. A brief that failed another check (a fact about the product that is wrong, a person who is not real) is left out until it is fixed, or until you tick "Make the N with a failed check too".

Approve the briefs and render is the second approval.

Stage 3: the images

Each ad is rendered at 4:5 with the product photo, the brand's type specimen and its layout attached, then looked at twice. A failed image is made once more with the notes as corrections. About half an hour for 50, about 16 cents an image.

The wall shows all 50. The dot on each is the check result: green passed, amber has something to look at, red failed. Filter by type of ad (when the sprint has more than 1), format, angle, buyer, product or check result. Click an ad to see it large with its plan and its check notes; from there, open it in its set to give feedback or make it again. Feedback on a sprint ad becomes a lesson like any other.

The top of the sprint screen shows the cost so far, and Every call opens its ledger. A sprint is 1 line in the cost register; its sets are counted inside it.

If it stops

A stage that stops (the spend cap, a provider error) says why at the top, with Start it again. What was already written or rendered is kept and not paid for twice.
