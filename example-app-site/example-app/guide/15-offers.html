Offers

Guide, chapter 15 of 20

Most brands that think they have an ad problem have an offer problem, and most that think they have an offer problem have a discounting problem. The Offers screen (in the Brand group of the sidebar) is where you see what the brand offers now, design better offers, test each against the ways offers fail, and put the ones you choose in front of the writers.

An offer is not a discount

A discount is a percentage off. An offer has 5 parts, and the discount touches only the second:

PartThe question it answers
1. PositioningWhat problem does this solve, for whom, and why now?
2. Price architectureHow many units, at what price, on what billing cycle, above what threshold?
3. Value mechanicWhat does the buyer get that costs the brand little: a gift, access, a download, something done for them?
4. Risk reversalWhat removes the risk: a guarantee, a trial, returns?
5. UrgencyWhy now: a real window, a season, a limit on stock?

Every offer on the screen is laid out in these 5 parts.

Build an offer (phase 47)

The screen now opens on Build an offer: 5 short choices, each with a 1-line example, and 1 option in each marked Recommended by a rule in code.

  1. Who is it for? A persona and their biggest doubt, picked from the doubts on their profile or typed. New buyers and returning buyers get different offers.
  2. How does it work? 1 of 5 ways: the same product for a new buyer, a bundle, a gift with purchase, spend more and get more, a straight discount used rarely. The recommendation comes from 4 questions (how much of an order you keep, whether order sizes bunch in 1 group or 2, 1 hero product or many, whether buyers come back) and from the kind of doubt. A discount is never the recommendation.
  3. What do you add? Tick boxes: take the risk away, a real reason to act now, a reward for staying, a free extra.
  4. Do the numbers work? Price, cost per item, shipping for 1, 2 and 3 items, items per order. A small table shows 1 order today beside 1 order with the offer: what the customer pays, what you keep per order before ads, the return on ads that breaks even, and the return needed to keep today's profit per order. Hover the question mark for the term behind each plain label. Under the table, the 7 ways offers fail are tested as you type. They are notes, never a block.
  5. After the main offer. Optional: an add-on in the cart, at checkout, after payment, or when someone leaves. Added profit per order = the share who take it x its price x what you keep of it.

Then Write the wording: 1 small call (about a fifth of a cent and 3 seconds, measured on 3 Grüns offers) writes the name, the line for the page, the line a person says in a script, and why it answers the doubt. It is sent only your choices and the brand's voice, about 2,000 tokens. Where a gift is part of the offer it also says whether this buyer would likely want it, which is a guess to check with real buyers. In mock mode you get plain wording to edit. Save the offer adds it to the offers in use with its parts, its buyer and its sums, so a script for that persona can state it.

The strip at the top shows which of the 5 parts your offers in use already state. A part no offer states is shown as a suggestion. For a new brand this is the first read of the offers found on its site.

1 list of offers. Every screen reads the brand's offers through 1 reader (the offer book). It joins the prices in brand.json, how each offer is said and who it is for (offer-set.json), and what you build here. Offers in use at the top of the screen shows each with its price, whether it is running today, its mechanic, where it came from, the doubt it answers and the line a person says. 2 offers that state the same deal are shown as 1; nothing is deleted from the files.

The offer a script states. An offer you pick always stands. With none picked, a script states the running offer built for its persona and product; with none built for them, the brand's first offer. The Script builder, the chat, Formats and Coverage all choose this way. How much of the deal is said out loud follows the buyer's stage: none for a buyer who does not see the problem or is only problem aware, 1 term for one comparing answers or who knows the product, the whole deal for one ready to buy. A script that follows a reference ad (keep the structure, loose inspiration) now follows the same rule; with claim checks on, the full terms are still sent.

On the script page. Under the title, the script shows the offer it states and 1 line on why: built for this persona, the brand's default, or picked for this script. A list holds the brand's running offers, with the one built for the script's persona marked Recommended. Pick another and press Rewrite with this offer: the script and its primary text are written again as a version beside the first one, which stays as it is. That costs 1 script. Pictures are not made again.

What the method says, in short (from the offer talk kept in data/audit/phase47/offer-transcript.txt; the working copy is skills/offer-building/SKILL.md):

  • The buyer judges the gap between what they get and what they think it costs the brand. Add things worth a lot to them that cost the brand little; do not cut the price.
  • Start from the buyer's main objection, asked of real buyers. The product is said again for each persona, and the terms follow that persona's reason.
  • Keep it simple. After 90 to 120 days ask which part made people buy, and take out what nobody names.
  • A discount of 5 to 10% loses money. The return on ads needed to keep today's profit climbs faster with every point off.
  • A cross-sell is a different product that goes with the order; an upsell is more of the same. Judge either by the profit it adds per order.
  • When someone leaves: generous while browsing, small with a full basket, no money off at checkout.
  • Only a test says which offer is best: a new one runs on its own page beside the current one.

The rest of this chapter describes the Advanced view's tabs, which are as they were. Offers designed there before are listed under Earlier designs. Cross-sells and post-purchase offers are no longer counted as mechanics: they come after the main offer.

Where it stands

  • What runs now. Each price point in the brand brain as 1 order: what the customer pays, what is kept before ads, the return on ad spend at which it breaks even, and what it leaves after ads today.
  • The right offer for this brand. 4 questions answered from the numbers: the margin floor, how order values are spread, 1 hero product or many, and whether buyers come back. A chart shows where order values bunch and where a spend threshold belongs.
  • The mechanics, ranked. All 7, best fit first, each with the reason. Press Design beside one to have offers of that kind designed.
  • The 7 ways offers fail. Tested against what runs now and every offer in use.
  • The numbers. What a unit costs to make, what an order of 1, 2 and 3 units costs to ship, payment fees, and the return the ads run at. Enter the brand's own here.

Offers

1 section for each mechanic:

MechanicWhat it changes
Product repositioningThe buyer, the problem and the headline. The product stays the same.
BundlesUnits per order, and the cost to ship each unit
Gift with purchaseUnits per order, with almost no change in margin
Tiered offersOrder value: spend more, get more
Disciplined discountingThe price. 1 or 2 times a year
Cross-sells and upsellsExtra contribution on an order already decided, in the cart or at checkout
Post-purchase offersA 1-click add after payment is taken

Each offer is a card: its 1-line statement, what the customer gets and pays, the buyer it is for, and 3 figures (what the customer pays, what is kept before ads, and the most it can cost to win the order and still leave what an order leaves today). Any failure it triggers is shown on the card.

Open a card to edit every part. The figures and the failure checks update as you type.

Other industries

A brand usually knows what its own category offers, and so does everyone else in it. An offer that is ordinary in another industry can be new in yours.

Pick an industry (46 are listed, from car dealers to video games), read what it is known for, then press Design borrowed offers. Each offer takes 1 pattern and rebuilds it as something an online store can deliver: something shipped, something downloaded, access to something, or a service done for the buyer.

Research on the web searches for what the industry is offering now and adds named examples with their sources. It costs about 20 cents, is done once for an industry, and is kept for every brand. Until an industry is researched, its patterns are written from general knowledge and say so.

Discount calculator

For every discount from 0 to 60% it shows the units per order, what the customer pays, what is kept, the return needed to break even, the return needed to match today, the return a sale that size has reached, and what an order leaves. 1 discount leaves the most; the screen names it.

The inputs can all be changed, and the chart and table follow as you type:

InputWhat it is
Price of 1 unitBefore any discount. Starts from the price point you pick.
What 1 unit costs to makeStarts from the product you pick.
Pick, pack and postWhat an order of 1, 2, 3, 4 units costs to send.
Payment feesA share of what the customer pays.
Return on ad spend todayRevenue for each 1 spent on ads, on first orders.
What a sale doesAt each size of sale: the units in the average order, and how much better the ads did than at full price. A discount between 2 rows is read off a straight line between them.

They start from the brand's numbers. What you type is a what-if and is not saved; Put back the brand's numbers undoes it. To change the costs every offer is priced with, save them under Where it stands, The numbers.

What a sale does is the 1 thing saved from here. Enter what the brand's own sales did (for each size of sale: the units in the average order, and how much better the ads did), then press Save as the brand's record. From then on the calculator, the failure checks and the offer designer all read those rows, and the note at the top of the screen stops calling the record simulated. A brand that has never run a sale removes every row and saves an empty record.

How a row is worked out. Click a row of the table. Under it, each column is worked out with that row's own numbers:

  1. Units an order: from what a sale does.
  2. Pays: price x (100% less the discount) x units.
  3. Kept: pays, less the goods, less pick, pack and post, less payment fees. What is left before ads.
  4. Breaks even: pays divided by kept. The return on ad spend at which the order makes nothing.
  5. Today: what a full-price order leaves after ads at today's return. Every row is compared with this.
  6. Needed: the return the ads must reach for the discount to leave what today leaves. Pays divided by (kept less today).
  7. Reached: today's return, raised by how much better the ads did at a sale that size.
  8. Leaves: kept, less what the ads cost at the return reached. The highest row is the discount that leaves the most.

Designing offers

Press Design offers, pick the mechanics, and say how many. 1 call is sent:

  1. the method, as a short card,
  2. the brand in a dozen lines: products, price points, guarantee,
  3. its numbers, already worked out,
  4. the buyers, by name,
  5. what buyers want and fear, as the labels of the research themes,
  6. and, when borrowing, 1 industry's patterns.

About 4,000 tokens in. It costs about 2 cents and takes about a minute. The app then prices each offer, works out its margin and tests it against the 7 failures. Every offer arrives as a draft.

Product repositioning is the mechanic to start with for a brand with 1 hero product. Ask for 6 and you get 6 different buyers, problems and headlines for the same product, each with terms built around that buyer.

The 7 ways offers fail

The list is a file you can read and edit: skills/offer-building/failures.md. The tests are arithmetic, run every time an offer changes, and cost nothing.

FailureThe app flags it when
1A discount called an offerThere is money off and no other part
2The margin is worked out wrongCosts are missing, or the order would have to be won more cheaply than orders have been
3The same offer for new and returning buyersAn offer cuts the price for everyone, or nothing is for returning buyers
4A threshold set at the average order valueA tier sits at the average or inside a peak
5The wrong giftThe gift has no price to the buyer, costs over a quarter of the margin, or nobody asked for it
6A promotion every weekMore than 2 flat discounts in 12 months
7Treating a quiet quarter as the weatherSales dip from January to March and no offer is planned for it

An offer with a failure triggered can still be put in use: the app asks first and says which.

Putting an offer in use

A draft reaches nobody. Save and put in use does this:

The offer isWhat happens
A bundle, a gift, a tiered offer or a discountIt is written into the brand brain as an offer. It can be picked as the offer of a concept (so its scripts state it), and on the new-set screen under "Which offer?" (so every ad in the set states it)
A repositioningAs above, when it changes the terms. Its buyer becomes a persona in use. A draft concept is filed with that buyer, the positioning as its angle, and the research themes that show the want, ready to write scripts from
A cross-sell, upsell or post-purchase offerIt is marked as running. It is shown in the store, not in ads, so the writers are sent nothing new

A writer is sent the full terms of an offer only for the piece that states it. Every other piece is told the offer exists, in 1 line.

Six repositionings put in use are 6 concepts, and each concept can be scripted as many times as you like.

An offer stays in use while a concept states it.

Where the numbers come from

For now, a file in the brand's folder: sample-data/performance/economics.json. It says whether it is simulated, and the screen says so at the top. The Grüns figures are simulated: they test the arithmetic and are no record of the brand's costs.

Enter the brand's own costs under The numbers and they are kept in brand-brain/economics.json. Order values, the repeat rate and the record of past sales stay as they were until the store is connected.
