Coverage: what to make next

Guide, chapter 18 of 20

Most accounts spend nearly all their money on buyers who already know the product, in 2 or 3 kinds of ad, for the buyers who are easiest to reach. The Coverage screen reads the account, shows what the ads cover and what they miss, and plans the scripts that fill it. It also shows which ads are working and writes more of them.

Until an ad account is connected, the figures on this screen are simulated: made up from the brand's own buyers and offers so the screen can be used. The screen says so at the top. The scripts it plans are real.

What the screen reads

Every ad is counted 6 ways. A bar is the share of spend.

LineThe questionWhat counts as a gap
AwarenessHow much does the buyer know when the ad reaches them?A stage with no ad, or under 5% of spend. The plan for new ads is 70 to 90% for unaware and problem-aware buyers
Market sophisticationHow do the ads argue, against how tired the market is of claims?A way of arguing above the market's stage that the ads don't use
FormatsWhich kinds of ad have run?A kind that has never run
BuyersWhich of your personas does an ad speak to?A persona in use with no ad, or under 5% of spend
Age and genderWho does the money reach, against who buys?A group whose share of buyers is 5 points or more above its share of spend
OffersWhich offers do the ads carry?An offer in use that no ad carries

Market sophistication, in plain words

A market gets tired. First a plain claim works, then a bigger claim, then only an explanation of why it works (a mechanism), then a sharper mechanism with proof, and at the end only identity: who the buyer is and becomes.

StageThe marketWhat still works
1First to make the claimA plain claim
2Claims are commonA bigger claim
3Tired of claimsA mechanism
4Everyone has a mechanismA sharper mechanism
5Tired of mechanismsIdentity

The rule the screen follows: write at the market's stage and above it. If the market is at stage 2, scripts are planned at 3, 4 and 5 as well as 2. Money still spent on a stage below the market is marked "Behind the market".

The market's stage comes from the brand brain today. It will come from reading competitors' ads once that is connected.

Scripts that fill the gaps

Pick 6, 12 or 24. Each script in the list is 1 choice on every line: a buyer, a stage, a way of arguing and a format. They are picked so that:

  • more of them go where the account is short (cold buyers first while the account has too few ads for them),
  • no 2 are the same ad,
  • each format suits the stage it is written for,
  • a gap a script has taken counts for less on the next, so the set spreads out.

Under each script is why it was picked, in the account's own numbers. Untick any you don't want, then press Write scripts. They appear on the Scripts screen as they finish, each written for its format with that format's own rules.

Tick Put each one on the Production board too to have every script made into its job at once. The plan of each job is written and then waits for your approval. Nothing is rendered before that.

A script that was written here and is not running yet makes its gap count for less. It shows as "written here" on the line.

What is working

An ad is working when it is active, returns at least 1.3 times the account's return, and takes at least 1.5% of spend. For each, Make more of this keeps its argument and changes 1 thing a script:

ChangeWhat you get
Other formatsThe same argument as a different kind of ad
Other buyersThe same argument told for another of your personas
A colder or warmer buyerThe same argument for people one step earlier or later
A stronger argumentThe same ad argued one stage above
New openingsThe same ad with a different way in

Pick the changes and how many scripts (3, 6 or 9). The kinds of change take turns, so 6 scripts on 3 kinds of change are 2 of each.

Nearly working

Some video ads have 1 weak part. The screen finds them from 2 numbers the ad platform gives:

  • How many start it: of the people who see the ad, the share who watch 3 seconds.
  • How many of those stay: of the people who watch 3 seconds, the share who watch 15.
What the numbers sayWhat is wrongWhat the app writes
Under 20% start it, and 25% or more of those stayThe body works. The opening does notNew openings on the same body
30% or more start it, and under 15% of those stayThe opening works. What follows loses themThe same opening idea, and a new body

An ad with both numbers strong is working: it is under "What is working" when it also returns well. An ad with both weak is not worth fixing. Pick 3, 6 or 9 and press the button on its card.

How a script is written for its stage

Every script planned here names a stage of awareness and a way of arguing. The writer is told both, and is also told what to do about them: where the ad opens, what its work is, when the product arrives, how hard it asks, and the usual way an ad at that stage fails. A script for an unaware buyer opens on their life and names the product late, with no price. A script for a buyer who is ready opens on the offer.

These briefs are 10 short files you can read and edit: Inspect, Skills and prompts, buyer-stage.

What it costs

Reading the account and planning costs nothing: no model is called. A script costs about $0.04 to write. 12 scripts are about $0.50.

When real data arrives

The screen reads 1 file per brand until the ad account is connected: brands/<brand>/sample-data/performance/account.json. With no file, the simulated account is used. The file holds the ads (each with its format, stage, way of arguing, buyer, offer, opening line, spend, purchases and revenue), the age and gender breakdown, the share of customers in each group, and the market's stage. The Advanced view shows every rule and every gap with its weight.
