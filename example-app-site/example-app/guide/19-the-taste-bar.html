The taste bar

Guide, chapter 19 of 20

Every screen that makes something has the same small set of choices. Together they are the taste bar. It is how you tell the app how close to stay to your brand, whether the product is in the picture, and how much brand design to put on it.

The full table, use case by use case, is docs/TASTE-DECISIONS.md. It is printed from the code, so it is always what the app does.

The 1 rule behind it

A lever changes what is sent to the model. It never adds adjectives.

Moving a lever does not add "make it bolder" to a prompt. It changes which pictures are attached, which blocks of text go in, and which short note is loaded. A model that was never sent your past ads cannot copy them. A model that was sent only the reference picture cannot drift from it.

The 3 main levers

How close should it stay? (the Look)

4 stops, in this order on the screen:

StopWhat is sent for a pictureWhat is sent for words
Close to my brandUp to 2 of your designed images and up to 2 of your past ads, attached. Your brand guidelines in full. Of a reference, only what kind of ad it isYour own published ads and lines, as the model to write like. Of a reference, only why it worked
In between (where it starts)With a reference: 1 of your images, and the reference described in words. The reference picture is not sent. With none: your look in wordsYour brand voice. With a reference: a description of how it is built, not its words
Exactly like this referenceThe reference picture itself, with only your colours, fonts and logo. None of your past adsThe reference beat by beat. Each beat is rewritten with your facts
Try something newOnly your colours, fonts and logo. No past ads, no reference, no descriptionOnly the basics of your voice and your facts. No past ads

Exactly like this reference is offered only when a reference is picked. Ask for it with none and the app uses In between and says so.

The model is also run cooler or warmer by stop: 0.5 at Close to my brand and at Exactly like this reference, 0.9 In between, 1.2 at Try something new.

The product in the picture

StopWhat is sent
Not shownNo product photo and no logo. The picture is the buyer's own moment
In the sceneThe product photos, with the role: present in the scene, small, never the subject
Clearly shownThe product photos, with the role: this is what the product is (shape, colours, label), easy to see. How it is posed is the picture's own
The heroThe product photos, with the role: the subject of the picture

This lever starts where the buyer is:

Buyer's stageThe product starts at
UnawareNot shown
Problem awareNot shown
Solution awareIn the scene
Product awareClearly shown
Most awareThe hero

A native ad starts at Not shown at every stage. A product page image and a photoshoot frame start at The hero. If you never move the lever, it keeps following the stage: change the stage on the screen and the product changes with it.

Brand styling

StopWhat is sent
NoneNothing of the brand's design. Where a native ad starts
Colours and fonts onlyThe colours and fonts, and the logo. Where an image ad for an unaware buyer starts, because it has to stop the scroll before it looks like an ad
Full brand lookThe style card, the logo, and the look of your designed images as far as the Look lever allows. Where everything else starts

A product page image always uses the full brand look. The lever is not shown there.

Not every screen shows every lever. A script has no product lever. A cast picture has no words and no product, whatever is asked.

4 things no lever moves

It reads aloud. It makes sense. It tells 1 story. The facts are true.

Where you find it

  • Every dialog that writes scripts, the Script builder, Coverage, and concept suggestions.
  • The new image set screen, a concept ad, a format's flow, the 50-ad sprint, and each image of a set.
  • Make assets and the video settings of a job.
  • The References screen: the build tray, new hooks for a winning ad, and writing a script from a reference.
  • The landing page editor.
  • The chat. Say it in words: "exactly like this reference", "no product in it", "try something new", "phone photo", "soft sell". A message that names no lever leaves every lever at its start.
  • The terminal: --look=brand|between|reference|new, --product=none|scene|clear|hero, --styling=none|basics|full, or --taste='<json>' on pnpm scripts:generate, pnpm studio and pnpm produce.

Under each lever a line says what will be sent at the stop you chose. A warning appears when the app could not do what was asked (no reference picked, no product photo on file, no designed images to match). A warning never blocks.

What the tests showed

All on Grüns, on 2026-10-05, 1 image a stop. These are single pictures, not a score: look at your own first few. The pictures and the exact request sent for each are in data/audit/phase47/images, images2 and images3.

  • The Look. 1 ad was made at each of the 4 stops from the same reference (images2/four-stops.png). The requests differ as the table above says: the reference picture was attached only at Exactly like this reference.
  • Try something new did change the kind of ad, on the second try. In the first round an ad at the far stop was "still a pack-hero ad, not a new form". After the forms menu (chapter 5) the same test gave a type poster where the other 2 stops gave a pack shot (images2/variety-trio.png).
  • The product lever. 4 images, 1 a stop (images3/sheet-product-4-stops.png). 3 passed their check; In the scene failed on a detail of the words, not on the product.
  • Known weak spot: Clearly shown and The hero come out alike. Both were planned as the same kind of picture: the pack centred on the same background. The 2 stops ask for different shares of the frame (15 to 30% and 45 to 65%). Those shares are a first estimate, not measured.
  • Brand styling on a native ad. None and Full brand look were each made once (images3/sheet-native-styling.png).
  • The logo. With the product Not shown, no logo was attached, as intended.

Good to know

  • An older set, script or job that was made with the earlier controls (the 1 to 5 variety lever, the closeness picker, the product select) still opens. Its stored choices are read as the matching stops.
  • The shot counts behind Video pace are a first estimate, not measured. Each stop now gives a range, and the planner chooses inside it by what the clip's idea needs. For a filmed person, Normal is a shot every 1.2 to 2 seconds (3 to 5 shots in a 6-second clip, 5 or 6 in 10) and Fast cuts every 1 to 1.6 seconds (4 to 6 in 6 seconds, 6 in 10), with no more than 6 shots in a clip. For a made world (claymation, a cartoon, an explainer, 3D and the like), Normal is a shot every 0.75 to 1.25 seconds (5 to 8 shots in 6 seconds) and Fast cuts every 0.6 to 1 second (6 to 10 in 6 seconds), with no more than 10 shots in a clip. At Calm there is about 1 shot every 5 seconds in either kind: about 2 in 10 seconds. A slower speaker's clip is cut a little slower. Shots are timed to a tenth of a second, and Calm keeps whole seconds. Calm also slows the voice to 0.85 of the speaker's pace, a starting choice, not measured. The counts were raised 3 times on 2026-10-08. For a 10-second clip, Normal went from 3 to 5 shots, to 4 or 5, to 5 or 6, and Fast cuts from 4 to 6, to 5 or 6, to 6; the third change widened the ranges and added the made world. Nothing cut this fast had been rendered when this was written. A plan of shots made before every shot said how it is filmed keeps the pace it was written to. Most clips are now about 6 seconds and not 10 (see Production). At Calm the camera may stay where it is and a shot may run past 3 seconds (2.5 in a made world); at the other 2 stops the plan is checked on both (see Production).
