# Image ad copy (statics and carousels)

This skill writes Meta image ads and carousels that sell, for e-commerce brands. It applies the 9-beat ad script playbook to formats with no sound, where the viewer's eye, not a timeline, decides the order things are read.

About 90% of a good ad is decided before writing. Do the steps in order.

Match effort to the ask
## Match effort to the ask

| Request | Do |
|---|---|
| "5 headlines", "punch up this primary text", "1 more overlay" | Steps 1 and 7 to 9 only. Return the lines with exact character counts. |
| "Make an ad" | All steps. 1 ad with variants. |
| "Make a few ads", or no angle given | All steps. 3 ads on 3 angles and archetypes that differ in the argument. |
| "Make a carousel" | All steps, plus the carousel section. |
| "Sale", "Black Friday", "launch offer" | All steps, leaning on the offer archetypes and the sale rules in step 3. |
| "Make something like this ad" | Ask which reference mode (below): same ad in a new format, keep the structure, or loose inspiration. |
| "Turn this script into statics" | From a script (below). |

**No customer research yet?** Use the `image-ad-copywriter` skill's method: build an assumed customer model, mark it `Assumed customer model`, and treat every ad as a bet to test.

**Starting points.** Every ad starts from 1 of 4 places. Work out which first, because each gives you a different amount of freedom.

| Starting point | What the user gave | Your freedom |
|---|---|---|
| A. Same ad, new format | A reference ad, to be remade exactly in another format | None over the words. Keep every text element and its order; only the visual format changes. |
| B. Keep the structure | A reference ad, as the skeleton | Words and look. Same archetype, same reading order, same job for each text element. |
| C. Loose inspiration | A reference ad, for why it worked | Everything. Only the principle carries over. |
| D. From research | No reference | Full. Run every step from the customer research. |

**Reference modes.** Rules for A, B and C.

- **Mode A, same ad, new format.** Keep the reference's copy word for word and in the same reading order. Move each element to the slot that does the same job in the new format (a video's hook line becomes card 1 of a carousel; its offer line becomes the last card). Do not rewrite, shorten or add. The claim and guardrail checks still run and report; they do not rewrite.
- **Mode B, keep the structure.** Break the reference down first: its archetype; where the eye lands first and what each text element does; which beats sit on the image and which in the copy; how and when the product appears. Then build on the same skeleton with this product, this research, new words and the brand's own look. Generic native formats (a notes-app screenshot, a text thread) are fair to reuse. The reference's specific design and distinctive lines are not. Share no phrase of 6 or more words with it. Show the breakdown as a short table before the ad.
- **Mode C, loose inspiration.** Take only why it worked, then run every step as normal. Name the principle you built on in the flags.

After writing, run the brand voice pass: every word on the image and in the primary text must be one this brand's customer would say or expect from this brand. Swap anything else for the customer's own word. Wording only, never claims, numbers or offer terms.

**From a script.** When a video script already exists for the concept, cut the image ads from it so every asset carries the same claim:

| Script part | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Hook line and its overlay | On-image headline, and overlay variants from the other hook variants |
| Problem line (the accommodation) | The image scene |
| Promise and mechanism | Subline, or the mechanism static |
| Objection (failed solution) | Comparison static, or carousel cards 2 to 4 |
| Proof | Badge, or the proof static |
| Offer, risk reversal, CTA | Offer static, final carousel card, description field |
| The whole script | Long primary text, rewritten for reading |

Keep the script's evidence refs (theme and claim ids) on every slot they feed.

Inputs
## Inputs

Gather these first. If a brand-brain skill or brand folder exists, read it first.

| Input | Why it matters | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Brand brain: products, materials or ingredients, offers, prices, guarantee, approved claims, forbidden claims, proof assets, visual identity, voice rules | Mechanism, proof, offer, compliance and look | Ask. For a draft, use placeholders like `[PRICE]` |
| Customer research: calls, support tickets and return reasons, reviews, post-purchase surveys, Reddit and forum posts | The words on the image come from here | Ask. If none exists, mark assumed insights `[ASSUMED]` |
| Concept: persona × angle × offer | 1 concept per ad | Propose 1 and state it |
| Awareness stage | Decides the archetype and copy length | Pick 1 and state it |
| Placement (feed, stories and reels, both) | Decides aspect ratio and safe zones | Default: 4:5 feed plus a 9:16 version |
| Product images available (packshots, lifestyle, UGC frames, customer photos) | Decides what the designer can use | List what is needed |

**Never invent customer evidence.** No made-up quotes, review screenshots, star ratings, review counts, customer numbers, study results, press logos or before and after images. When a slot needs one, write a placeholder such as `[REAL_REVIEW_QUOTE]` and flag it. Fake reviews and fake press are also against Meta's ad rules.

- **Small specifics read as facts.** "Since July", "$800 on a desk", "after 3 weeks". Take them from research, keep them general, or bracket them for the brand to confirm.
- **Don't invent offer terms** (free replacements, extra gifts, longer trials) beyond the brand brain.
- **Comparisons are claims about other products.** Use them only with support. Don't name a competitor brand.
- **Derived numbers need a stated basis.** "About 25 cents a night" needs the price and how long the product lasts, both from the brand. A warranty period is not a lifespan. Without the basis, put the line in the "to check" list, not on the image.
- **Sale claims.** Say the sale price, not only the %. "Was" prices and "lowest price ever" only if the brand confirms them. Real end dates only.

How image ads differ from video
## How image ads differ from video

- **The eye decides the order.** You cannot control sequence the way a script does. Build 1 clear entry point.
- **No sound.** Every word must be read.
- **The hook is wherever the eye lands first.** Usually the biggest text or the most surprising image.
- **1 frame must carry 1 idea.** If the value prop cannot land in 1 frame, move the argument to the primary text, a carousel or a landing page.
- **The 9 beats still apply.** They are spread across slots: image, on-image text, primary text, headline, description, CTA and the landing page.
- **Statics are not only bottom of funnel.** That belief is a skill issue. Long-copy statics, native posts and advertorials hold large spend on cold audiences.
- **Statics are cheap.** Use them to test angles fast. Develop the winners into video later.

Non-negotiable rules
## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Channel a desire that already exists.** Point to research for it.
2. **1 ad, 1 claim.** Every slot (image, overlay, primary text, headline, landing page top) agrees on it.
3. **Agitate the consequence (the accommodation), never the symptom.**
4. **Promise before mechanism. Proof bigger than the claim.**
5. **Use the buyer's words**, not package language.
6. **Specific beats adjective.** Numbers, names, physical details, times.
7. **Pile B over pile A.** Native-looking ads beat ads that look like ads. Marketing language is what makes an ad look like an ad.
8. **Each element sells the next.** The image sells the overlay. The overlay sells the primary text. Card 1 sells card 2.
9. **Readable at thumb size.** If it needs zooming, it fails.
10. **Only true, compliant claims.**
11. **Never tell the viewer what they have.** Meta rejects ads that assert a personal attribute ("Are you bloated?"). Use 1st person, "if you've...", or a scene. See `references/performance-playbook.md`.

Workflow
## Workflow

### Step 1: ground in research

Find the 5 things:

| Find | What it is | Best image use |
|---|---|---|
| The accommodation | What they changed in their life to cope | Image subject or on-image headline |
| The failed solution | What they tried first and why it failed | Comparison static, objection card |
| The nearly-didn't-buy | Last thing between them and buying | Guarantee badge, offer static, FAQ card |
| The word | Their noun for the problem | On-image headline |
| The moment | What made them start looking | Image scene, story card 1 |

Each source has a job:

| Source | What only it gives you |
|---|---|
| Calls | Final objection before purchase |
| Support tickets and return reasons | Failure modes: angles and claims to avoid |
| Reviews | The words. 2 to 4 star reviews hold objections. Short real review lines are strong on-image text. |
| Post-purchase surveys | The moment of purchase |
| Reddit, Facebook groups, forums | Language of people who have not bought yet |

**Translate marketer language to buyer language:**

| Marketer says | Buyer says |
|---|---|
| Do you suffer from bloating? | I look 4 months pregnant by 4pm. |
| Is your hair thinning? | I can see my scalp. |
| Joint support | I can't get off the floor without holding onto something. |

Treat failure modes from tickets as guardrails. If refunds come from people expecting an outcome the product does not give, no slot may imply it.

### Step 2: place the buyer

Pick 1 awareness stage and 1 sophistication stage.

| Awareness stage | What the ad must do | Archetypes that fit | Primary text length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Teach the problem first | Long-copy static, native post, advertorial, educational carousel | Long |
| Problem aware | Name the accommodation, then the way out | Accommodation static, native post, story carousel | Medium to long |
| Solution aware | Show why other solutions fail | Comparison static, mechanism static, myth-busting carousel | Medium |
| Product aware | Prove it and handle objections | Proof static, review static, objection carousel | Short to medium |
| Most aware | Give the offer and a reason for now | Offer static, truth-bomb price static | Short |

| Sophistication stage | Write |
|---|---|
| 1 | The claim |
| 2 | A bigger claim (watch compliance) |
| 3 | A unique mechanism |
| 4 | A bigger mechanism |
| 5 | Identity and transformation |

Default to a unique mechanism plus identity. It usually beats the market's stage.

Funnel mix for a healthy account: most new image ads (70 to 90%) should target unaware and problem-aware stages, not only retargeting.

### Step 3: audit the offer

| Element | Buyer's question | Image slot |
|---|---|---|
| The thing | What do I get? | On-image headline or packshot |
| The terms | What does it cost, framed against what? | Price callout, primary text |
| The reversal | What if it doesn't work? | Badge, description field |
| The reason for now | Why this week? | Badge, description, primary text end |
| The effort | What do I have to do? | Subline, primary text |

**Price framing for image text:**

| Tactic | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor to the alternative | Was $89, now $69 | 1 specialist visit: $200. 90 days of this: $69. |
| Reduce to the ridiculous | $69 for 90 days | 77 cents a day |
| Price the problem | Only $68 | Less than the 2 things that didn't work |
| Make effort the price | Free shipping over $50 | 1 capsule. Every night. That's it. |

**E-commerce terms to check:** one-time vs subscription price and how to cancel, bundle price per unit, first-order discount and the price after it, free gift, shipping date. A discount alone is a weak reason: pair it with why the product is worth it and a real end date.

**Guarantee badge rules:** specific, a little uncomfortable for the seller, matched to the product's timeline, in the buyer's words. "90 days. If your part hasn't changed, we refund all 3 tubs." beats "30-day money back".

### Step 4: find the specific, unique claim

- **Delete before refining.** Cut claims every competitor can make (vegan, cruelty-free, made locally, premium quality, free shipping) unless research shows buyers care.
- **3-question test** for each differentiator: is it a proposition, is it unavailable to competitors, will it move a stranger?
- **Specificity ladder:** measured numbers, a named 3rd party, a physical detail, a specific time, proper nouns.
- **3 factory questions:** what does the factory do that seems boring, what was rejected in formulation, what constraint was never mentioned?

| Adjective | Specific (good on an image) |
|---|---|
| Fast absorbing | Dry in 40 seconds |
| Handcrafted | 1 person. 4 hours. Every pair. |
| Better absorption | Glycinate, not oxide. Your body uses it. |

### Step 5: pick the archetype

| Archetype | Best stage | Beats it carries | Layout | Copy formula |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accommodation static | Problem aware | Hook, problem | Real-life scene showing the workaround, short overlay | Overlay: the accommodation in their words. Primary text: problem, promise, mechanism, proof, offer. |
| Native post (tweet, notes app, text message, screenshot, Reddit-style post) | Unaware to problem aware | Hook, problem, education | Looks like organic content. Brand kept out of the image. | The post itself teaches or confesses. The sell sits in the primary text. Can run through a creator or expert profile as a partnership ad. |
| Advertorial static | Unaware to solution aware | Hook, curiosity, objection | Article or listicle look ("The best X of 2026, and the ones to avoid") | Must click through to an education page or long primary text. Only make honest comparisons you can back up. |
| Long-copy static | Unaware (strong with 50+ on Facebook feed) | All 9 beats | Plain native photo, a long story in the primary text | Story walks the reader from unaware to ready. Write it like a full video script. |
| Mechanism static | Solution aware | Promise, mechanism | Simple diagram or close-up of the ingredient, material or process | Overlay: promise. Subline: "because [mechanism]". |
| Proof static | Product aware | Proof | Real review screenshot, real customer photo, count plus repeated word | Overlay: the strongest real quote. Badge: volume of reviews. |
| Comparison static | Solution aware | Objection, mechanism | 2 columns: what they tried vs this, or tick and cross table | Use the failed solution from research. Facts only. No named competitor you cannot back up. |
| Offer static | Most aware | Offer, risk reversal, CTA | Packshot, framed price, guarantee badge | Price framed per day or against the alternative. Reason for now. |
| Truth-bomb price static | Product to most aware, high price | Objection (price), proof | Product plus a blunt price line | "This costs $120. It's our best seller. Here's why." Then 3 reasons. |
| Demo still | Product aware | Proof | The product mid-use, result visible | Overlay says what you're seeing. |
| Sensory or texture static | Problem to product aware | Hook, proof | Extreme close-up of texture, pour, fabric | Very short overlay, or none. |
| Before and after | Product aware | Proof | Split image | Strongest proof, highest compliance risk. Real, consented images only. Check Meta policy for the category. Never for weight loss or health claims that break rules. |

### Step 6: map the beats to the slots

| Slot | Beats it usually carries | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Image (the visual) | Hook, problem, proof | The first thing the eye lands on. 1 subject. Native beats polished for cold audiences. |
| On-image headline | Hook or promise | 3 to 8 words. The buyer's word. Biggest text on the image. |
| On-image subline | Mechanism or proof | Up to about 12 words. Optional. |
| Badge or sticker | Risk reversal, offer, proof volume | 2 to 5 words |
| Primary text, first line | Hook continuation | About the first 125 characters show before "See more". This line must make them tap. |
| Primary text, body | Problem, mechanism, objection, proof, offer, risk reversal, CTA | Short version: 2 to 4 lines. Long version: as long as the story needs. |
| Headline field | Promise or offer | About 40 characters show. Plain and specific. |
| Description field | Risk reversal or reason for now | About 30 characters. May not show in all placements. |
| CTA button | Action | Shop now, Learn more, Get offer, Order now. Match the landing page. |
| Landing page top | Same claim as the hook | Must match the ad's promise word for word where possible |

Character counts are approximate. Placements truncate differently. Keep the key message in the first line.

**All hooks must agree.** The image, overlay, primary text, headline and landing page top all carry the same 1 claim. A luxury image with a discount overlay and a science headline confuses the buyer and the algorithm.

### Step 7: write the visual hook and on-image text

- **Write 5 to 10 overlay variants** per image. Text swaps are the cheapest way to make new ads.
- **Pile B:** native look, plain fonts, real settings, no centred logo and tagline for cold audiences.
- **The most specific detail does 3 jobs:** targeting, proof and casting. Put it in the overlay.
- **Pre-qualify.** The words decide who the ad gets shown to. Name the problem, not a persona label. "If you've started parting it on the side" beats "Women over 35, listen up".
- **Native posts** (notes app, tweet, text thread) can carry more text than a headline, but keep the whole post to 25 words or fewer and make its first line do the hook's job.
- **Keep text short and large.** Aim for 20 words or fewer on the main frame. High contrast. Test that it reads at phone size.
- **Leave safe zones** in 9:16: keep text out of roughly the top 14% and bottom 20%, where the interface sits.

Overlay hook types:

| Type | Example (hair thinning) |
|---|---|
| Accommodation | You stopped parting it in the middle. You didn't decide to. |
| Moment | Why the photo from behind always gets you |
| Contrarian | Rosemary oil was never going to fix your part |
| Curiosity gap | Why hair thins at the part line first |
| Specific proof | (A real, measured result) |
| Truth bomb | Yes, it's $69. Here's why it's our best seller. |
| Confrontation | That drawer of products you never use? That's the problem. |

### Step 8: write the primary text

Primary text is where a static does most of its selling.

**Short version (2 to 4 lines)** for warmer audiences:

1. Hook continuation (the accommodation or moment)
2. Promise plus mechanism in 1 line
3. Offer or proof
4. Risk reversal plus CTA

**Long version** for cold audiences: write it like a video script on the page.

1. Hook line that earns "See more"
2. Problem: escalate toward 1 consequence. Do not list symptoms. Do not resolve it.
3. Bridge
4. Promise, then mechanism (feature, which means, which is why)
5. Objection: the failed solution, in their experience
6. Proof bigger than the claim
7. Offer with framed price and stated effort
8. Specific guarantee
9. CTA: action, what happens next, effort, reason for now
10. Identity close tied to a real mechanism

Rules:

- Introduce the product late. Name it after the mechanism lands.
- 1 idea per sentence. Short paragraphs, 1 to 2 sentences each.
- Contractions. Grade 7 reading level or below.
- Vary sentence length. All-short staccato reads as AI.
- Emojis only if the brand's voice uses them. Never as bullet decoration.
- No em dashes, no "it's not X, it's Y" chains, no hype words.
- Story-led long copy works well in 1st person past tense. Only real stories. If it didn't happen, it must read as fiction.

### Step 9: write headlines, description and CTA

- **3 headline options**, each a different angle on the same claim: promise, offer, proof.
- Headlines are plain and specific. "Keep the hair you've still got" beats "Transform your hair journey".
- Description: the guarantee or the reason for now.
- CTA button: match the intent. Cold education ads often suit "Learn more". Offer ads suit "Shop now" or "Get offer".

### Step 10: write the designer brief

For each ad:

- Aspect ratios: 4:5 (feed) and 9:16 (stories, reels). 1:1 only if asked.
- Image: subject, setting, lighting, who is in it (age and look matching the persona), what is in hand.
- Layout: where each text element sits, hierarchy, safe zones.
- Text: exact on-image copy, no rewriting by the designer.
- Style: native or branded, fonts, colours from the brand brain.
- Assets needed: packshots, real reviews, real customer photos.
- Do not: things that make it pile A, forbidden claims, guardrails.

When an image model will make the visual, write the image prompt as a separate field. Keep text overlays out of the prompt and add them in design, unless the model renders text reliably. Follow the image prompt rules in `references/performance-playbook.md`: real product image as the reference, no invented label text, native camera look for cold audiences.

### Step 11: self-audit

Count characters and words exactly, with code if you can run it, and report them. Character limits are hard limits in the ad manager. Then list every number in the copy and trace each to the brand brain or the research. Only say a check was done if you did it.

- [ ] Desire is evidenced in research
- [ ] Awareness stage is labelled and the archetype fits it
- [ ] 1 claim, and every slot agrees on it
- [ ] The eye has 1 clear entry point
- [ ] On-image headline uses the buyer's word and reads at thumb size
- [ ] Problem is an accommodation, not a symptom list
- [ ] Promise comes before mechanism
- [ ] Proof is real and bigger than the claim
- [ ] Price is framed. Effort is stated.
- [ ] Guarantee is specific and timed to the product
- [ ] First line of primary text earns "See more"
- [ ] CTA has action, next step, effort and a reason for now
- [ ] Headline is under about 40 characters
- [ ] Text clears the 9:16 safe zones
- [ ] Looks native (pile B) for cold audiences
- [ ] No invented reviews, numbers, logos, images, offer terms or small specifics. Derived numbers have a basis. Placeholders flagged.
- [ ] Native post text is 25 words or fewer. Final carousel card is 20 words or fewer and carries on-card CTA text.
- [ ] No personal-attribute assertions, no cure or guaranteed-result claims
- [ ] Nothing borrowed from a reference ad's wording or design (no shared phrase of 6 or more words)
- [ ] No forbidden claims. No guardrail broken. Before and afters checked against policy.
- [ ] Contractions, grade 7, varied sentences, no AI tells

Carousels
## Carousels

A carousel is a script in cards. **Card 1 sells the swipe to card 2**, the way line 1 sells line 2.

### Rules

- 3 to 10 cards. Most selling carousels use 5 to 7.
- Card 1 is the hook. It must open a gap that only swiping closes.
- 1 idea per card. About 15 words or fewer per card.
- Keep a visual thread across cards: same layout, colour or character, so it reads as 1 story.
- Introduce the product late. Name it on a later card, not card 1, for cold audiences.
- **The last card carries on-card CTA text** ("Tap Shop now to get yours by Friday"), plus the offer and guarantee, in 20 words or fewer. The shared button alone is easy to miss on the final swipe.
- Each card can have its own headline field. Keep them consistent with the card.
- Primary text carries the overall hook and a short version of the argument, for people who do not swipe.

### Carousel structures

| Structure | Best stage | Card flow |
|---|---|---|
| Story (confession) | Problem aware | 1 the accommodation → 2 the moment → 3 what I didn't know (mechanism) → 4 what I tried (objection) → 5 what changed (proof) → 6 the offer and guarantee |
| Myth-busting | Solution aware | 1 the contrarian claim → 2 to 4 myth and truth pairs → 5 the mechanism → 6 the product and offer |
| Failed solutions | Solution aware | 1 "You've probably tried..." → 2 to 4 each failed solution and why → 5 what works and why → 6 offer |
| How it works | Solution to product aware | 1 the promise → 2 to 4 mechanism steps or ingredients, each with its job → 5 proof → 6 offer |
| Proof stack | Product aware | 1 the boldest real quote → 2 to 5 real reviews, each handling 1 objection → 6 guarantee and CTA |
| Timeline | Product aware | 1 "What to expect" → day 7, day 30, day 90 cards (only what the product reliably does) → offer |
| Objection FAQ | Product to most aware | 1 "Before you buy" → 1 card per top objection from research → guarantee → CTA |
| Product range or bundle | Most aware | 1 the bundle value → 1 card per item with its job → framed price → CTA |

Dynamic catalogue carousels (DPAs) pull products automatically. They need no beat writing, but their primary text and headline should still follow the offer and objection rules. Check DPA results with existing customers excluded and on incremental attribution, because their reported ROAS is often inflated.

Variants
## Variants

From 1 concept, make many ads by changing 1 thing at a time:

1. **Overlay swaps** on the same image (cheapest)
2. **Image swaps** with the same copy (new persona on screen, new setting)
3. **Archetype rotation:** accommodation static to native post to long-copy static to carousel
4. **Primary text length:** short and long versions of the same ad
5. **Persona swap:** same angle, new person and scene

**Say what each variant tests** and what to make next if it wins (the winning overlay as a video hook, the winning angle as a carousel).

Warn when 2 variants are near-identical. Meta's Andromeda groups similar ads and shows them to the same people, so near-duplicates add little new reach.
