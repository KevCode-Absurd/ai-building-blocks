Plan an ad cut from a script: the picture, with the script's own words

For a static made from a script that has its own primary text: the scene, the layout and a first set of words on the picture, picked or shortened from the script; the craft writer (studio_words) then writes the words from the script's lines. No feed copy: the script's own runs with the ad

What it is meant to be sent

Only the chosen path: what an image ad is and its layouts; the script with its hooks and headlines; the buyer and the stage in a line each; the product; the brand's name, market and look; the do and don't of its voice; lessons; the format's own piece. Not sent: offers, claims, buyers, research, the brand's published lines, the copy method, the style skills of a reference.

What it runs on, and why

deepseek-flash, low effort is the recommended setting. Phase 47. Not judged blind: read by me on 1 script, 1 run at each effort. The facts and the words are the script's, so the planner has less to weigh, and the script writer itself runs on this model at low effort. Before, a static from a script went through the full ad writer twice (the plan, then the words again), the copy pass, the voice pass and the ad review, on nearly the whole brand: 12 to 19 cents and 2.5 to 4 minutes. A script with no primary text of its own, or a job held to the claim register, still takes that path.

What goes in, block by block

Each prompt is cut at its tags and its headings. Tokens are the provider's count for the whole call, shared out by length. A block that is not in every call is this step's own to 1 piece of work (a format, a reference, a note). A block that comes from a file is changed in that file; what the app builds is changed on the brand's own screens. Feedback is kept on the Notes tab.
