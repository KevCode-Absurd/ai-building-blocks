Learning / Model guide

Which model should write your copy?

We gave 6 models the same brief and had 2 other models judge the results without knowing who wrote what. This guide shows what each one wrote, how it scored and what it cost, so you can pick the model that suits your brand for each step of the work.

Choose your models Read what they wrote

The test

Run on 7 October 2026 for 1 supplements brand, Grüns. 5 buyers, 1 at each stage of awareness. For each buyer every model wrote 5 feed headlines, 4 primary texts and 2 video scripts, and planned 1 of the scripts as a video. In all: 150 headlines, 120 primary texts, 60 scripts and 30 video plans. Same instructions, same checks, same day: only the writer changed.

The copy below is test work for a brand we study. It was not made by or for Grüns and none of it has run as an ad. It is 1 test on 1 brand: use it as a starting point, then try the model you pick on a few pieces of your own and read them.

What the test found

  1. 1

    Claude Opus 5.5 wrote the best copy

    Each judge ranked it first in 11 of its 20 readings and scored it 6.9 out of 10. It led on every kind of copy: headlines, primary texts, video scripts and video plans.

  2. 2

    No model reached 8

    8 is the mark for copy a judge would run as it stands. A better model helps, and the copy still needs a person's eye before it goes out.

  3. 3

    The dearer model of a pair was not the better one

    Gemini 3.1 Pro scored below Gemini 3.8 Flash with both judges, at nearly twice the cost. DeepSeek V4 Pro scored below DeepSeek Flash with both judges.

  4. 4

    DeepSeek Flash is the value pick

    It came second with 1 judge and third with the other, at under a third of what Claude Opus cost.

  5. 5

    GPT-6 Sol was the most accurate and the least varied

    The judges disagree most about it: one put it second and the other last. The judge that put it second is a model of the same line, so read that row with care.

  6. 6

    Every model was weakest on variety and accuracy

    All 6 were handed the same buyer and the same customer quotes, and all 6 told much the same story. That is the brief more than the writer: no model made the copy varied on its own.

Pick by the job

A starting point from this test, not a rule. Every step of the work can be put on its own model, and 1 choice sets every step that writes words a customer reads.

Unaware buyer. Someone who has never been regular and thinks that is how they are built. No offer.

Gemini 3.8 Flash

6.7 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 3 of 6. Strong first-person story that mirrors the buyer's life and holds attention. But it never mentions the product and borrows a personal fact (15 years) as the narrator's own, so it sells weakly.

Best line: I planned outfits around my stomach.

Weakest line: I finally feel normal after 15 years.

6.0 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 2 of 6. "Days between poops used to be my normal." captures the buyer's belief precisely. "I finally feel normal after 15 years." invents the evidence needed to overturn it. None of the headlines shows a gummy or pack.

Best line: Days between poops used to be my normal.

Weakest line: I finally feel normal after 15 years.

Gemini 3.1 Pro

6.7 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 1 of 6. Mixes the buyer's desire, a testimonial, the gross-plain brand voice, and the product (a pack, no gritty drink). It is the most balanced for an unaware buyer, though the 15-year quote is invented and 'fix' overclaims.

Best line: Go every day without thinking about it.

Weakest line: "Normal for the 1st time in 15 years."

4.3 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 6 of 6. "Normal for the 1st time in 15 years." invents a quantified testimonial. "Fix the heavy feeling, get better poops." and "1 pack a day, and your gut feels lighter" compound that with unsupported outcomes.

Best line: Go every day without thinking about it.

Weakest line: "Normal for the 1st time in 15 years."

DeepSeek Flash

5.4 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 5 of 6. Lines 3 and 4 assume powders and a pile of pills this buyer never mentioned, and line 5 promises 'every day', which is an overreach. The opening lines name the problem well but sit flat as a set.

Best line: Days pass between bathroom trips.

Weakest line: The powder was chalky, so you stopped.

5.7 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 3 of 6. "Days pass between bathroom trips." meets the buyer where they are. "The powder was chalky, so you stopped." shifts to a different buyer, while "Fiber that helps you poop every day." exceeds the supplied regularity claim.

Best line: Days pass between bathroom trips.

Weakest line: Fiber that helps you poop every day.

DeepSeek V4 Pro

5.3 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 4 of 6. Direct, brand-like and plain, but 'I finally go every day' and 'gives you better poops' overclaim, and the $1.78 line is clumsy and repeats itself. A repetitive 'one pack' pattern flattens the set.

Best line: Days between poops? Try a gummy pack.

Weakest line: Going every day costs $1.78 a day.

5.4 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 5 of 6. "Going every day costs $1.78 a day." turns a subscription price into a promised health outcome. "I finally go every day, without trying." adds an unsupported testimonial; "Try a gummy pack." asks for action before building awareness.

Best line: Days between poops? Try a gummy pack.

Weakest line: Going every day costs $1.78 a day.

Claude Opus 5.5

6.7 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 2 of 6. Funny, varied testimonial-style lines with a real arc and plain gross-out honesty. No product or next step appears, and it has a jeans detail that's vivid but invented, so conversion is weak.

Best line: "Is this what it's like to be a person?"

Weakest line: I only pooped once every few days.

7.4 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 1 of 6. "Turns out some people poop every day." challenges the buyer's accepted normal without pitching prematurely. "Is this what it's like to be a person?" loses that specificity, and none of the headlines shows the gummy.

Best line: Turns out some people poop every day.

Weakest line: "Is this what it's like to be a person?"

GPT-6 Sol

4.4 from Claude Sonnet 5.5, ranked 6 of 6. Five near-identical lines on '1 gummy pack helps you go', stiff phrasing and no story. It names the product, but the repetition kills variety, and 'helps you poop daily' overclaims.

Best line: 1 gummy pack a day for better poops

Weakest line: Days pass. 1 gummy pack helps you go.

5.7 from GPT-6.1 Sol, ranked 4 of 6. "1 gummy pack" appears in four headlines; "1 pack of gummies" supplies the fifth variation. "Days pass." recognizes the buyer, but "helps you poop daily" goes beyond support for regularity.

Best line: Days pass. 1 gummy pack helps you go.

Weakest line: 1 pack of gummies helps you poop daily.

How it was made. Each model wrote through the same steps the app uses for real work. Every text was then read by a second model as a stranger would read it, and the lines it failed were said again by a third, the same for every column. So a column is its writer plus the same safety net.

The writing cost $22.96 in all and the judging $1.72. 4 kinds of copy were judged; the video plans are scored above and not printed here.
