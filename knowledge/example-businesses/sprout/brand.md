# Sprout: brand file

A real US brand with the name, people and some details changed. Built from its public website, ads and reviews, captured Sept to Oct 2026. Last updated 2026-10-18.

## What it is

Sprout sells daily greens gummies in single-serve packs. One pouch holds 28 small daily packs. You tear one open and eat the small handful of green gummy bears inside. No water, no mixing, no shaker.

The whole brand rests on one idea: people quit supplements because they are unpleasant (chalky powders, pills that make you gag, a shelf of bottles), so make one people want to eat.

Sold on its own site (sprout.example) and in big-box retailers, warehouse clubs and grocery chains. There is an adult line and a kids line (ages 2 to 13).

## Claims it makes

**Fine to repeat** (on the label or backed by the brand's own study, kept to "supports"):
- "60+ ingredients, 21 vitamins & minerals, and 6g fiber" in 1 pack
- "Clinically tested for nutrient absorption", with the study named and the 2 numbers (vitamin C +40.1%, folate +20.5%)
- "Supports digestion and gut health"
- Third-party tested for 70 pesticides, 4 heavy metals, 16 contaminants, 9 microbial contaminants
- Vegan, pectin base, free from gluten, dairy, nuts, synthetic dyes and sweeteners

## Who buys it

Shares are rough estimates from the mix of public reviews, not sales data.

1. **The quitter who wants to stick** (about 40%). Ages 28 to 45, office jobs, slight male lean among powder switchers. Has quit greens powders, pill stacks and multivitamins. Wants: one habit that lasts. Doubts: "A gummy can't hold enough to replace my powder." "They are expensive." Says: "Sprout has been the one thing I've been able to stick to."
2. **The gut-fix seeker** (about 25%, strongest feelings). Ages 25 to 55, mostly women. Bloating, irregularity, sometimes IBS. Has tried fiber powders and probiotics. Wants: to feel normal. Doubts: "Will this upset my stomach more?" "Will the sugar-free one cause problems?" Says: "My bowel movements are normal for the 1st time in 15 years!"
3. **The busy parent** (about 25%). Ages 30 to 45, mostly mums, buys Adults for themselves and Kids for the children. Picky eaters at home. Wants: no fight at the table, and something easy for themselves. Doubts: "Is this just candy?" "Is the sugar okay for kids?" "Two subscriptions is too much money." Says: "So easy to grab and eat on my way to the billion things I have to do as a mom."
4. **The price doubter** (share unknown, but most ads aim here). Knows the brand, maybe from a store shelf, and thinks it costs too much. Wants: proof it is worth it, or a real deal. Doubts: "Which discount is real?" "Will the price jump after the first order?" "I hate subscriptions because I feel like they are a trap."

## How the brand writes

Playful, cheeky, casual internet voice. A funny friend who is into wellness but refuses to be preachy. Short lines, 3-beat rhythms, numbers up front, says the gross thing plainly ("better poops"). Names the enemy by how it feels: chalky powders, pills you gag on, a cobbled-together shelf. No guilt, no lab-coat tone in headlines.

Lines the brand already uses:
- "We made daily nutrition, like, ridiculously easy."
- "Rip. Tip. Enjoy."
- "Tastes like a treat, works like a supplement."
- "No more gagging on pills, sloshing chalky powders, or cleaning messy counters."
- "Savings, exclusive flavors, and a minor sense of superiority." (about its VIP program)
- "Sachet is a fancy word for our gummy pack."

**Show it right** (the brand's own product rules):
- The daily pack is torn straight across its top edge. Never the side, bottom or a corner.
- Gummies pour out of the torn top into a palm or bowl.
- The big pouch opens only at its resealable top. It is never shown torn.
- Never show the cartoon tie-in characters or any partner brand's marks. Use the flavour description only.

## Competitors (described, not named)

- **A big-name greens powder**, about $79 a month ($2.63 a serving). Science-first, podcast-famous, 90-day guarantee. Now also sells its own gummies at $49 a month. Sprout reviewers name it as the thing they quit ("gritty/chalky", "$94 a month").
- **A TikTok-famous women's wellness brand**: greens gummies at $19.99 for 12 servings, bloat relief angle, creator videos. Reviews mention a "stringy, gooey texture".
- **A celebrity-founded women's supplement line**: greens at $30, frequent sitewide sales up to 45% off. Greens is a side product.
- **A doctor-backed greens tablet and gummy brand**: about $25 to $30 for a 50-count bottle in grocery stores. Some buyers report stomach cramps.
- **A vegan family liquid-vitamin brand**: greens gummies from $19.95 for 60. Trust badges, gentle tone, no fun.
- **The old way**: a half-used tub of powder and a shaker, a shelf of separate pill bottles, drugstore multivitamin gummies.

## What is known to work (hypotheses)

The public ad library shows no spend or results. "Working" here means an angle has many live copies or keeps being relaunched. Treat each line as a hypothesis.

- **Price objection, then a discount.** "'Sprout is too expensive': here's 61% off to prove otherwise" has 15+ live copies, the most in the account.
- **Offer-led ads dominate**: about 80% of a 50-ad sample on 30 Sept, falling to 48% of the 40 newest ads on 4 Oct. The account is widening.
- **Gut pain in slang.** "Fix the Hot Girl Tummy" is the only pain-led angle with real volume, and it speaks to the buyer with the strongest feelings.
- **A new limited flavour every 4 to 8 weeks** gives a fresh reason to buy, especially on the Kids ads.
- **Stacked numbers**: "21 Vitamins. 4 Free Gifts. 61% Off."
- **New in Oct**: hair (6 copies) and membership perks (7 copies).
- **The open lane**: the top review theme ("I finally stuck with a supplement") and the powder-quitter story barely appear in ads. Only 2 of the 40 newest ads use the habit idea ("One Step I Never Skip").
- **Kids ads are more creative** than adult ads: picky-eater pain, ingredient hooks ("Methylfolate, Not Folic Acid"), poop humour.
- Taste can be an acquired thing for some ("after the third day they started tasting better"), and Sugar-Free upsets some stomachs. Point gut-sensitive buyers to Low Sugar.
- Cancelling is the loudest complaint on public review sites. Do not promise an easy cancel beyond the site's own words.
