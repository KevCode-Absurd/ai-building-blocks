# Ad creative strategy and testing: craft principles

This book is for the people who decide what paid social ads a store makes, how many, for whom, and how to read what they did: creative strategists, creative directors, ad designers, video editors, creator leads and the media buyers who launch and judge the work. Read it before you plan a batch, write your briefs and concepts to it, and run the review checklist on every draft before it goes to production or launch.

## What great looks like

- Every ad carries a written address: persona, angle, offer, awareness stage and format, and a new ad differs from the last on at least one of the first three.
- The hook names or shows the buyer's own problem in the buyer's own words, so the platform learns to find buyers, not scrollers.
- Monthly ad volume is worked out from winner churn and hit rate, and every ad in it clears a written quality bar.
- The account's live ads spread across awareness stages, with most aimed at people who do not yet know the brand.
- Every claim is smaller than the proof shown, and every proof is a checkable specific: a number, a name, a timeframe or a demonstration.
- Tests are called on store orders and gross profit after ad cost, at ad set level, once spend reaches about 3 times target cost per purchase.
- Fatigue is diagnosed before it is treated: frequency, audience size and the offer are checked before a hero ad is replaced.
- Winners are scaled sideways into new personas, formats and landing pages, and no single ad carries the account.
- Nothing ships that breaks platform ad rules or endorsement law, however well it might convert.

## The principles

### 1. Define every ad as persona plus angle plus offer before you think about format, and count it as new only if one of the three changes

**Why it works:** Meta chooses who sees an ad by reading the ad itself, so a new persona, angle or offer reaches new buyers, while a new colour on the same idea reaches the same people.

**How to apply:** Before any brief, write the three cells in one line each. Make the persona a person with a specific problem (a consultant who flies weekly and needs one carry-on for work and evenings), not a demographic. Pick the format only after that. Plan in concepts, not ads: two personas times three angles is six concepts, and about ten ads per concept gives 60 ads from one research pass. For a US collagen brand, a menopause angle and an injury angle for women over 40 are two concepts; three new backgrounds on either are not.

**Judge it by:** Each brief names all three cells and a reviewer can say which cell changed from the last batch. Weak work lists five new ads that share one persona, angle and offer.

**Breaks when:** A single-product brand with one narrow buyer has few real angles; then new formats carry more of the diversity.

**From:** MCR-6847, MCR-6649, MCR-6872, MCR-6782, MCR-6726, MCR-6877, MCR-0022

### 2. Write the hook to filter for the buyer, not to stop everyone, because the creative is now the targeting

**Why it works:** Meta reads each ad's transcript, copy and visuals to choose who sees it, then learns from who keeps watching. A broad meme or bait opener teaches delivery to find people who enjoy the ad but never buy, so a high hook rate can lower sales.

**How to apply:** Open on the buyer's problem, belief or situation, in the words they use. Prefer a problem-led opener to a label call-out, which reads as an ad and gets skipped. Ask of every hook: if only the people who stop for this line saw the ad, would they be buyers? After launch, check the drop from click-through rate to link click-through and on to purchase; an ad at 10.9% CTR with almost no purchases lost to a plain demo at 6.3% that sold.

**Judge it by:** A stranger can tell within 3 seconds who the ad is for. Weak work opens on a random satisfying clip or an unnamed product with no buyer cue.

**Breaks when:** The product has very broad impulse appeal or the entertainment audience is the buyer; accounts still on tight interest targeting set the audience elsewhere.

**From:** MCR-6675, MCR-0026, MCR-6736, MCR-6812, MCR-0029, MCR-6855, ADC-6169

### 3. Treat near-duplicate ads as one ad and make real differences, because the platform groups lookalikes and serves them to the same people

**Why it works:** Meta's retrieval system narrows tens of millions of ads to a few thousand per impression and groups near-identical creatives under one entity. Two hundred similar ads may behave like twenty, so frequency climbs, reach shrinks and return falls while the team thinks it is testing.

**How to apply:** Before launch, count unique ideas in the batch once variants are removed. Skip micro-variations such as a background colour or a pose: a 1% to 2% effect cannot be read without large split budgets anyway. Vary persona, angle or offer, or change format so the ad looks, sounds and reads differently. Run 10 to 15 distinct formats across the account rather than iterating one winner into dozens of copies. If you can, ask the Meta rep for the account's creative similarity score, where higher is worse.

**Judge it by:** The batch sheet counts unique concepts, not files. Weak work is eight text-overlay swaps of one video called eight tests.

**Breaks when:** Small accounts with a handful of ads, where finding one working concept matters more; retargeting refreshes meant only to fight frequency.

**From:** MCR-0078, MCR-6631, MCR-6669, MCR-6885, MCR-0036, MCR-6617, ADC-6167, Meta Engineering on Andromeda (https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/)

### 4. Write down what a 7 out of 10 ad looks like, then launch as many ads above that bar as you can fund

**Why it works:** Once ads clear a basic quality bar, nobody can reliably pick which will win. Volume above the bar buys more draws with known odds; volume below it only buys noise.

**How to apply:** Fix creative in this order: ad quality, then concept and format diversity, then volume. Write the bar as checks a reviewer can tick: clear to a stranger in 3 seconds, one persona, a promise in one sentence, proof as big as the claim, a working bridge into the product, on brand and policy-safe. Then rank effort: volume above the bar, then concept quality, hook strength, storytelling, editing and talent. Accounts launching 2,000 ads a month on $250,000 of spend stayed unprofitable because the ads sat below the bar.

**Judge it by:** The team can show its written bar and every ad in a batch passes it. Weak work is either a few polished sure things or a flood of unchecked template ads.

**Breaks when:** Very early brands should first study their own and rivals' winners to learn what good looks like; tiny budgets give each extra ad too little spend.

**From:** MCR-6618, MCR-6661, MCR-6792, MCR-6841, MCR-6658

### 5. Set monthly ad volume from winner churn and hit rate, with about one new ad per $1,000 of monthly Meta spend as the check

**Why it works:** Every ad has a limited lifetime spend (about $700 to $1,500 on average) and a normal hit rate is about 5%. Holding spend steady therefore needs a steady flow of new winners, and that flow can be calculated.

**How to apply:** Divide winners lost per month by hit rate: 10 winners at 20% monthly churn means 2 to replace, and at a 5% hit rate that is 40 new ads. Cross-check with the new-customer revenue goal divided by average revenue per ad ($300,000 at $3,000 per ad is 100 ads), and with one ad per $1,000 of monthly spend. Measure hit rate as ads that spent over 5% of campaign budget at target cost per purchase, divided by ads launched. Give each new concept at least 10 ads before you abandon it.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows churn, hit rate and the result. Weak work picks 20 ads a month from habit.

**Breaks when:** New accounts have no reliable hit rate yet; above about $1M to $3M a month the per-$1,000 rule overshoots; tiny accounts cannot give each ad enough spend.

**From:** MCR-0025, MCR-6880, MCR-6659, MCR-6790, MCR-6663, MCR-0059, MCR-6784, ADC-0017

### 6. Judge creative at ad set level on spend and 7-day click store results, and do not switch off low-return openers inside an ad set that hits target

**Why it works:** Meta shows people a sequence of ads but credits the last click, so an unaware opener can show 1.4x while the closer it fed shows 6x. One-day view credit is often 30% to 50% of reported return.

**How to apply:** First check the numbers tie to store orders; in over 1,000 audits, broken measurement was the main bottleneck in about 30% of stores. Rank metrics: amount spent (where Meta, not your caps, chose it), then return on 7-day click or the incremental column, then cost per click and click-through rate. Report on 7-day click, or 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view, never the 1-day view default, with existing customers excluded. Never add ads to an ad set hitting target or pause a winner to relaunch it; put fresh ads in a new ad set.

**Judge it by:** Reviews name the attribution setting and level used, and cuts happen only in ad sets missing target. Weak work kills the worst ad by ad-level ROAS in a winning ad set.

**Breaks when:** An ad takes real spend and is weak on every signal; one-ad ad sets; brands whose lift tests show different ad-level effects.

**From:** ADC-0059, ADC-6239, MCR-6650, ADC-6294, ADC-6207, ADC-6204, MEA-6320, MEA-6314, MEA-6303

### 7. Call a creative test only after about 3 times target cost per purchase in spend, over about a week, on store orders and profit

**Why it works:** Small samples crown false winners. A fixed data rule stops the team calling tests on taste or a lucky day.

**How to apply:** Judge a new concept after spend of about 3 times target cost per purchase: 3 or more purchases keep, 2 wait, 0 or 1 cut; 5 times target gives a firm call. Run about a week to cover day-of-week swings and the 3 to 4 days buyers take; small slow accounts need 10 to 14 days. Set test budget as target conversions times expected cost per purchase divided by days: 20 conversions at $100 over 14 days is about $143 a day. Check offer, page, price, stock and tracking stayed unchanged. Drop an angle with a high return rate even if its ROAS looks great. In a sale, ad ROAS mostly shows which product was discounted, so read ad sets and clicks.

**Judge it by:** Each verdict shows spend against target and gross profit after ad cost. Weak work calls a winner after two days at $30 a day.

**Breaks when:** High order value accounts with few daily orders need a longer window or a higher-funnel event.

**From:** ADC-0039, ADC-6209, ADC-6211, MCR-6727, MCR-0068, MCR-0029

### 8. Tag every ad on one fixed set of fields, and read each loser by the step where it broke

**Why it works:** "This ad works" teaches nothing; "this opener works" can be reused across fifty ads. Hook and hold rates do not predict sales, but they show which part failed, so a loser becomes the next brief.

**How to apply:** Name every ad on the same fields: persona, angle, mechanism, hook, format, and for video the actor, setting and edit style. Change one field at a time. Read a loser in order: did they stop (3-second view rate), stay (15-second hold rate; weak hold usually means the bridge failed), click, buy? A gap of more than 3 points between all CTR and link CTR means people engaged but never went to the site; under 2 points means clickers arrive primed. Many viewers reaching the end card with few clicks means the middle is not converting. Ridge puts the opening frame in each ad's name to compare one opener across every ad.

**Judge it by:** Each loser note names the broken step and the field to change next. Weak work says "did not perform" and moves on.

**Breaks when:** Accounts with a handful of ads, where patterns are noise; tags applied inconsistently; high order value products with too few purchases.

**From:** MCR-0051, ADC-6298, MCR-0029, MCR-0071, MCR-6743

### 9. Build angles only from desire you can find written down outside the brand's own marketing, in the buyer's own words

**Why it works:** An ad persuades when the viewer thinks "this is me", and that only happens when the problem is described the way the buyer lives it. If the desire exists only in the brand's copy, you have a product description, not a concept.

**How to apply:** Mine reviews, support tickets, return reasons, surveys and forums for five things: the accommodation the buyer has made around the problem, the failed solution they tried, what nearly stopped them buying, the exact word they use, and the moment they decided. Each feeds one beat. Write the problem in their words: "I can see my scalp" beats "hair thinning". Use survey answers to find the persona behind your top 20% of customers. Mine creator videos for angles the brand never used; one creator's label comparison took an account from 1.7x to 4x ROAS once rebuilt as statics.

**Judge it by:** Each concept cites at least one real buyer phrase and its source. Weak work uses category clichés like game changer or busy moms.

**Breaks when:** A brand-new product has no customers yet; use competitor reviews and forums as the outside evidence instead.

**From:** MCR-6700, MCR-6699, MCR-6731, MCR-6686, MCR-6810, MCR-0035, AVT-0010

### 10. When ads stop working, change angle and offer before persona, and pick format last by the beat that carries the sale

**Why it works:** Angle and offer change what the buyer believes and gets, which moves results most; format only changes how it looks. Most teams do the reverse and burn months swapping UGC styles on a tired argument.

**How to apply:** Test angle first, then offer (the biggest lever, but it runs out of options when the product cannot change), then new personas for breadth, with format fourth. Choose format after the script exists: find the load-bearing beat and pick a format that can carry it. Invisible mechanisms need animation, not a talking head. Visible benefits need a demonstration. Unusual terms need the offer on screen. A mechanism-led supplement like IM8 runs about 78% video because it must explain; a fashion brand may run mostly statics and catalogue.

**Judge it by:** The brief names the load-bearing beat and why the format suits it. Weak work starts with "let's do a UGC video" before any argument exists.

**Breaks when:** The team can only make one format, so it writes to that format's limits; new products with no proven buyer must test persona early.

**From:** MCR-6678, MCR-6870, MCR-6791, MCR-6708, MCR-6705, MCR-6859, MCR-6611

### 11. Aim most of the account at people who do not yet know the brand, with only about 20% of ads at product-aware and most-aware buyers

**Why it works:** Product-aware buyers are few and already warm, so an account built only on offer, product and brand-aware ads hits a ceiling and its frequency climbs. Colder stages cost more per result but hold far more people.

**How to apply:** Tag each ad by the stage its hook speaks to: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware. Put most volume in the first three and about 20% in the last two. Read the category first: where buyers already understand the product, like creatine, solution-aware ads can carry scale. Before copying a famous brand, count how many of its hooks only work if you already know the brand; Alo Yoga's was 96% because its demand comes from outside Meta, while Meta-built brands ran 3% to 18%.

**Judge it by:** Each ad has a stage tag and the live spread is reported. Weak work is all product shots, discounts and shop now.

**Breaks when:** Brands with large outside demand (stores, celebrities, search) can run more product-aware ads; small accounts may start low in the funnel and move colder as spend grows.

**From:** MCR-0057, MCR-0011, MCR-6772, MCR-6620, MCR-6751, MCR-6692, ADC-6181

### 12. Keep each ad on one awareness stage and one claim from first frame to landing page

**Why it works:** Every element of an ad is read together as one message. When they speak to different stages or claims, the cold viewer is confused, the warm one is bored, and delivery cannot tell who the ad is for.

**How to apply:** Make the stage a required line on the brief, so copywriter, designer and editor write to one stage. Before launch, lay the first frame, first spoken line, overlay, primary text, headline and page headline side by side; any mismatch sends the ad back. Read the stage from where the product first appears: named about 9 seconds into a short ad puts it near solution aware. When a hook changes to reach a new persona or stage, rewrite the body too; two Gorilla Mind ads had different hooks and the same product-dump body. Move down the stages steadily inside an ad; do not jump.

**Judge it by:** A reviewer reading only the hook, the headline and the page top finds one claim at one stage. Weak work says luxury in the image, sale in the headline and science on the page.

**Breaks when:** A deliberate test of different headlines on the same creative, where the mismatch is the variable.

**From:** MCR-0056, MCR-6719, ADC-6218, MCR-6626, MCR-0015

### 13. Put more variation into hooks than bodies, but only on bodies that already sell

**Why it works:** About 80% of viewers leave during the hook, so the opening decides how many people ever hear the argument, and a new hook gets its own creative ID and fresh reach. But hooks multiply a body; they cannot rescue a weak one.

**How to apply:** Once a body is proven, test 3 to 5 genuinely different hooks on it (up to about 8); for the same hour, two solid bodies with 15 hooks usually beat five bodies with two each. Treat each hook as three levers: the spoken line, the on-screen text and the first visual. In direct-response video the words carry most of the weight. Score hooks on clear to a stranger in 3 seconds, relevant and novel, then specific and credible. Do not launch an unproven ad as eight near-identical text versions.

**Judge it by:** Each hook in a set differs on line, visual or voice, and its body has a sales record. Weak work stacks 20 hooks on an untested body.

**Breaks when:** Static ads, where the whole image is the hook; budgets too small to spread across hooks; music-led formats where audio carries the hook.

**From:** MCR-6863, MCR-0013, MCR-0028, MCR-6743, MCR-0063, MCR-6668, MCR-6744, MCR-6652, MCR-6617

### 14. In cold video, keep the feeling of being sold near zero through the hook, put the promise before the mechanism, and name the product late

**Why it works:** Viewers leave when an ad turns into a pitch, and only about 15% reach the second half. Revealing the product late keeps fewer viewers but usually sells more, because those who stay are now problem and solution aware.

**How to apply:** Script the bridges as carefully as the hook: each line's only job is to get the next line watched. State the promise in plain words before any jargon; leading with "lowers ghrelin" loses viewers before the point. Add 4 to 6 seconds of mechanism education after a strong problem hook before naming the product. Show the product before naming it, and name it at the offer. Still give the why early; a curiosity gap left open for 24 seconds loses most viewers.

**Judge it by:** The retention curve has no cliff at the product reveal. Weak work opens on powder poured into a bottle with the brand named in line one.

**Breaks when:** Retargeting and product-aware audiences, and cheap gadgets whose visible mechanism is the hook, which should show it in the first 2 seconds.

**From:** MCR-0027, MCR-6738, MCR-6715, MCR-6852, MCR-0077, MCR-6711, MCR-0014, MCR-6737, MCR-6638, MCR-6805

### 15. Build every direct-response script from nine beats sized to its length, and write each length as its own script

**Why it works:** A buyer asks the same questions in order, from is this about me to what do I do now. Missing beats leave a question open; beats that do not fit the length get rushed.

**How to apply:** Use hook, problem, promise, mechanism, objection, proof, offer, risk reversal, call to action. At 15 seconds only hook, a problem line and a call to action fit; the mechanism needs 45 to 60 seconds; risk reversal needs over 60; ads over 4 minutes need several calls to action. Keep the problem beat to about 10 to 15 seconds and make it escalate: if two lines can swap without changing the argument, it is a list. Write a 30-second cut as its own script rather than chopping a long one. Do not spend tests on call-to-action wording in video; almost nobody reaches it.

**Judge it by:** Each beat is labelled and fits the stated length. Weak work lists ingredients without saying why they work.

**Breaks when:** Pure brand films, impulse gadgets where a short demo sells, and short bottom-funnel ads where hook, offer and call to action are enough.

**From:** MCR-6721, MCR-6614, MCR-6718, MCR-6703, MCR-6729, MCR-6775, MCR-0032

### 16. Make every static either fully clear or a deliberate curiosity gap, and send curiosity statics to a teaching page, never straight to the product page

**Why it works:** A static has one frame, and statics that sit in the middle, half intriguing and half explaining, fail both ways. A product shot on white only reaches the most-aware buyer.

**How to apply:** Decide the end of the line before designing. A clear static states product, benefit and offer and can go to the product page. A curiosity static pairs a persona-matched native image, a curiosity headline and long story-led primary text, and lands on a listicle, advertorial or quiz whose offer matches. Pair primary text and headline like a thumbnail and title: each raises a question the other answers. For buyers over about 50 on Facebook feeds, long-copy statics can carry cold traffic.

**Judge it by:** A reviewer can name which end the static chose and where it lands. Weak work sends a vague clever headline to a product page, then decides statics do not work.

**Breaks when:** Retargeting statics, where product and offer are the message; impulse products a clear image sells; Stories and Reels placements that hide long text.

**From:** MCR-6879, MCR-0024, MCR-6873, MCR-6874, MCR-6884, MCR-6690, MCR-6882, MCR-6886, LPG-6632, MCR-6848

### 17. Keep every claim smaller than its proof, and use the strongest proof type the claim can carry

**Why it works:** Buyers discount adjectives and believe what they can check. A claim bigger than its proof makes them doubt everything else in the ad, and it is also where legal and platform risk sits.

**How to apply:** Rank proof from strongest to weakest: demonstration, third-party verification, a counted volume of specific social proof, named individual testimony, mechanism explanation, founder assertion. Replace adjectives with checkables: a number, a named third party, a physical detail, a timeframe ("dry in 40 seconds, we timed it"). Count reviews instead of saying customers love it. Five reviews about the exact advertised benefit beat 400 generic ones. Match the messenger to the claim: no risky claims, creator demos; big claims, real experts. Keep proof numbers the same across all ads; IM8 ads launched the same day claimed different customer counts.

**Judge it by:** Every claim has its proof beside it, and every number traces to a source. Weak work says clinically proven with no study, or invents a statistic.

**Breaks when:** You lack verified data; an invented number is worse than an adjective. In premium services named testimony can outrank volume.

**From:** MCR-6712, MCR-6713, MCR-6714, MCR-6706, MCR-6754, MCR-6609, LPG-6620, MCR-6022

### 18. Diagnose fatigue before you treat it: check ad-level frequency, audience size and the offer before blaming the creative

**Why it works:** Purchase odds fall sharply after a second view of the identical ad, so frequency is the clearest sign. But results also fall when the persona pool is too small or the offer changed, and new creative fixes neither.

**How to apply:** On cold campaigns, flag any ad whose frequency passes 2 over 30 to 60 days; the ads are too similar or too few. Then name the cause: the ad reached its lifetime spend or repeats other ads, or the persona is too small (a narrow call-out can saturate after about $10,000 of spend, and a small market cannot hold $10,000 a day on a niche persona). Check the change log for offer, price, page and stock changes first. Pushing an ad harder burns its capacity sooner; held near $100 a day, a winner can last months.

**Judge it by:** The fatigue note names the cause with a number. Weak work replaces a hero ad because it has been running a while.

**Breaks when:** Retargeting and existing-customer ads run higher frequency by design; very large markets let niche personas hold big spend; sale peaks rarely exhaust reach, so a stall there is usually offer fatigue.

**From:** MCR-6858, MCR-0040, MCR-6641, MCR-6630, MCR-6790, ADC-6197, ADC-0044

### 19. Revive a fatigued winner before you replace it: new hooks from a new person, a new setting, then a new format

**Why it works:** A body that sold still holds a working argument; often only the face, scene or opening has worn out. Cosmetic changes are grouped as the same ad, but a new speaker or setting reads as new.

**How to apply:** Work down a ladder: new hooks on the same body, shot by a different person rather than recut; the same script reshot in a new setting (one founder ad moved from inside the gym to outside absorbed about $40,000 more spend); the same message in another format; its post ID in a scaling campaign; a short cost-cap squeeze. Skip duplicating ad sets. When live ads are too few, relaunch past winners: one account spending $350,000 a month on 15 ads cut cost per customer by about 20% to 30% after relaunching to reach about 60.

**Judge it by:** The fatigue plan says which rung it uses and why. Weak work abandons a proven argument, or reposts the same ad in a new colour.

**Breaks when:** The body or offer has stopped persuading, or the whole market has seen the story; relaunching only buys time if new production stops.

**From:** ADC-6300, ADC-6217, MCR-0047, MCR-0012, MCR-6777, MCR-6652, ADC-6176

### 20. Split new creative about 50 to 60% remaking winners, 20 to 30% fixing near-misses and 20 to 30% net new, and keep spend spread across many ads

**Why it works:** Remakes of winners have the highest hit rate, but every concept fatigues. Accounts that ride a few hero ads fall hard when those tire; one agency reports brands arriving 40% down on new customers after three ads holding most spend fatigued at once.

**How to apply:** Plan each batch by the split and never drop net new to zero. Under campaign budget optimisation about 4% of ads can hold about 64% of spend; manage that risk. Three ads sharing $1,000 a day at 4x are safer than one ad holding it all, because when one tires the other two absorb its spend. When one concept takes about 80% of spend, shift output toward it or cap it near 40% and rotate new concepts in. Apply new formats to ideas that already won first; teams abandon winning ideas long before the market tires of them.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows the split and the top three ads' share of spend. Weak work is all new ideas, or 90% copies of one winner.

**Breaks when:** New accounts with no winners must lean on net new; short drops where longevity does not matter.

**From:** MCR-0042, MCR-6670, MCR-6796, MCR-6671, MCR-6853, ADC-0014, ADC-0061, MCR-0052, MCR-6774

### 21. Scale a winning script sideways into new personas and angles, each with its own landing page, before you look for a new product

**Why it works:** Every angle hits a spend ceiling because it only speaks to people who share that problem. The same product and script reach new people when the persona or benefit changes.

**How to apply:** Keep angle and offer fixed and swap the persona, or keep the persona and reposition the product into a new benefit. IM8 swaps only the persona (joints, menopause, alcohol, gut) on one timeline script, each routed to its own page; Grüns sells one gummy under several benefit angles. When a concept wins, build a named bundle, a dedicated landing page and a matched retention flow around it, which can scale that concept 2x to 3x. When a scaled account plateaus, open avatars defined by a different problem rather than new formats for the same buyer.

**Judge it by:** Each winner has a written list of next personas and angles, checked against claims the product can support. Weak work makes another format for the same tired buyer.

**Breaks when:** Single-purpose products; angles that stray into claims you cannot back, especially health claims; personas too small to hold spend.

**From:** MCR-6612, MCR-0073, MCR-6838, MCR-6734, MCR-6807, MCR-6867, MCR-0038, MCR-6756

### 22. Compare formats by expected gross profit per ad after production cost, and fund production as a share of media by spend tier

**Why it works:** A video can win on return and still lose money once its making cost is counted. Statics cost a fraction of video and usually get the lowest CPMs. Creative is also usually underfunded next to the media it feeds.

**How to apply:** Work out gross profit per ad by format minus production cost: at $1,000 revenue per ad and 10x return, a $20 static nets $880 and a $200 video $700; at 2x, $480 versus $300. Test angles and offers as statics first, then move winners into video. Fund production by tier: under about $30,000 a month on Meta, founder-made phone content and about 3 concepts; $30,000 to $100,000, about 25% of media into production and 5 to 6 concepts; above $100,000, about 10% and 8 to 10 concepts. Moving $10,000 of $350,000 into production pays at a 5% efficiency gain.

**Judge it by:** Format choices cite cost per asset and margin per ad. Weak work spends months on one polished video when twenty statics would have taught more.

**Breaks when:** Products that need motion to be understood; brands whose limit is offer, margin or stock, not creative.

**From:** MCR-6878, MCR-0023, MCR-6846, MCR-6888, MCR-6887, MCR-6862, MCR-6788, MCR-6660

### 23. Make retargeting and bottom-funnel creative a small, objection-led layer, and distrust its high reported return

**Why it works:** Warm buyers have seen the pitch; what stops them is one specific doubt. Bottom-funnel and catalogue ads also take credit for buyers who would have bought anyway, so their reported return is often double the true one, and feeding them starves the prospecting that fills them.

**How to apply:** Split non-buyers by reason: price, trust, quality doubt, not ready. Give each its own ad: cost per use for price, counted reviews for trust, a demo for quality, education for timing. Find real objections by talking to people who abandoned checkout; HexClad's lifetime warranty against rivals' 1 to 3 years is an objection ad. Write offer ads that also carry value and objection handling, so a cold viewer past the hook can still buy. Check catalogue and retargeting return with existing customers excluded and on the incremental column; one showing 7x was 1.9x.

**Judge it by:** Every warm ad names the one objection it answers. Weak work retargets with the same cold ad, or a bare discount.

**Breaks when:** Very small retargeting pools where only one or two ads deliver; fashion and frequent-drop brands where holdout tests show customer spend is incremental.

**From:** MCR-6674, MCR-6845, MCR-0043, MCR-6759, MCR-6632, ADC-0010, ADC-6201, ADC-6150

### 24. In sale periods, keep evergreen winners running and add offer creative on top, built and loaded before the sale starts

**Why it works:** Offer creative absorbs far more spend in a sale, but evergreen ads hold the persuasion that makes the offer worth taking. Accounts with more, and more varied, creative also suffered less CPM inflation in one peak auction that ran 72% more expensive.

**How to apply:** Keep evergreen campaigns at about 50% to 60% of spend and add offer-badged copies; in one agency's accounts offer creative took 4 to 5 times more spend at similar 1-day return. Build five types: offer statics, the offer overlaid on evergreen winners, creator videos, product or VSL videos, and early access or VIP ads. Size the batch from per-ad spend ceilings: if a winner holds about $10,000 a day, a $150,000 peak day needs 12 to 18 such ads. Pre-build an offer ladder (deeper discount, gift, free shipping, bundle) with creative and pages loaded.

**Judge it by:** The sale plan shows evergreen share, the five types and a per-ad ceiling sum. Weak work switches everything to one discount banner the night before.

**Breaks when:** New accounts with no evergreen history; aspirational brands that keep price out of creative; budgets too small for five types.

**From:** MCR-0069, MCR-6778, MCR-0070, MCR-6781, MCR-6779, OFF-0068

### 25. Use founders and creators for the argument only they can make, and run creator ads from the creator's own handle

**Why it works:** A real face lends trust, but only when the script carries a real case. Partnership ads use the audience data of both the brand's and the creator's accounts and feel organic, and in some agency accounts they carry up to 40% of spend.

**How to apply:** Give founders the three claims only a founder can credibly make: why the business started, why the mechanism works, and what was wrong with rival products they tested; leave product experience to customers. The founder need not be on camera: vary along face, voice only, founder evidence, a proxy (staff, supplier, expert), then animation. Run cold creator ads as partnership ads through the creator's page, and do not pay creators to front hard-sell warm ads. Cast creators native to the market; UK and Australian accents underperform in the US.

**Judge it by:** Each founder script states its founder-only claim, and each creator matches the buyer and market. Weak work films the founder reading product features.

**Breaks when:** Founders who read stiffly or do not match the buyer; creators with mismatched or fake audiences; a business preparing for sale.

**From:** MCR-6761, MCR-6763, MCR-6766, MCR-6771, MCR-6815, ADC-0003, ADC-6299, MCR-6629, MCR-6677

### 26. Study competitor ads from brands one or two steps bigger, live for 60 days or more, and check what feeds an account before you copy it

**Why it works:** A long-running ad proves it works for that brand, at that spend, with its fame. A much bigger brand is often harvesting demand made elsewhere, so its ads fail where demand must be created.

**How to apply:** Pick brands one or two steps bigger (a $1M brand studies a $10M one), not category giants. Pull only ads live for at least 60 days, and treat several versions of one angle as a sign it is scaling. Count how often a brand's ads frame a problem and how many hooks need prior brand knowledge; Alo Yoga's ran 0% problem framing because celebrity seeding and stores fill its demand. Audit a library by sorting ads into pillars and counting unique ideas; Lovevery's 86 ads held 39 ideas in 7 pillars. Swipe formats from outside your niche; never copy claims or footage.

**Judge it by:** Swipe notes name the source's size, run length and demand source. Weak work copies a famous brand's lifestyle ads into a cold-start account.

**Breaks when:** Few rivals advertise; long-running ads are evergreen retargeting rather than prospecting winners; bulk edits reset start dates.

**From:** MCR-0034, MCR-6758, MCR-6682, MCR-6747, MCR-6823, MCR-6634, MCR-0055

### 27. Re-cut every concept for each platform's native grammar: frame, safe zones, length, sound and voice

**Why it works:** Each feed trains its users to expect a look and a pace. An ad that breaks the grammar reads as an ad and gets skipped, and text in the wrong place is covered by the interface. The concept travels; the cut does not.

**How to apply:** For Meta Stories and Reels, build 9:16 at 1440 x 2560 and keep about 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side free of text and logos. TikTok prefers 9:16, treats sound as part of the story, and recommends 21 to 34 seconds for in-feed ads, though 5 to 60 is allowed. Pinterest favours 2:3 vertical images with keywords and a matching landing page. Use trending audio only where you hold paid-use rights. Recheck each platform's live spec before a large batch, since these pages change.

**Judge it by:** Each placement has its own export, with text inside the safe zones and length set for that feed. Weak work posts one landscape cut everywhere with captions under the buttons.

**Breaks when:** Story or testimonial ads where trust comes from one person speaking; premium films where slow pace is the positioning.

**From:** MCR-6040, MCR-6837, ORG-6401, TikTok creative best practices (https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)

### 28. Build the brand into the ad with its own cues from the start, even while you hold the product pitch back

**Why it works:** Attention that does not attach to the brand is wasted: viewers remember the ad but not whose it was. Cues the brand owns (a colour, character, sound or pack shape) make that link without feeling like a pitch.

**How to apply:** Keep the pitch and product name late in cold direct-response video, but place one or two distinctive cues in the first seconds: the brand colour in the set, the pack in the background, the usual narrator or sound. Google's ABCD guidance, built with a Kantar study of 11,000 ads, advises showing the brand or product in the first 5 seconds and keeping it present; Ipsos found brand assets linked to branded attention more than simply showing or talking about the brand. On-brand comedy statics build recall while reaching colder buyers.

**Judge it by:** A viewer shown only the first 5 seconds can name the brand from a cue. Weak work could belong to any rival in the category.

**Breaks when:** A new brand with no cues yet must first build them; native creator ads where heavy branding breaks the organic feel.

**From:** MCR-6622, MCR-6728, Google ABCDs of effective video ads (https://business.google.com/us/resources/articles/abcds-of-effective-video-ads/), Ipsos Power of You (https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/2022-03/power-of-you-ipsos.pdf)

### 29. Stay inside ad policy and endorsement law on every ad, even when a riskier version would convert better

**Why it works:** Outrageous claims can buy a lower cost per customer for a while, but they bring account bans, regulator risk and a brand nobody will buy.

**How to apply:** On Meta, never assert or imply a viewer's personal attributes: a line about the viewer's own hair loss fails, a line on how common hair loss is after 40 may pass. Weight-loss and cosmetic ads target 18 and over, and side-by-side weight-loss transformations are barred; check the live standard first. In the US, have a reasonable basis for every objective claim before it runs. Endorsements must reflect honest opinion and real use, show typical results when a result is exceptional, and disclose paid or free-product ties on screen and spoken, early, inside the video. Fake or AI-made reviews and testimonials are banned. Comparisons must be clear and true.

**Judge it by:** Every claim maps to an approved claim, and every endorsement carries its disclosure. Weak work invents a testimonial or implies the viewer has a condition.

**Breaks when:** Rules differ by country and change; check the market's own regulator and the platform's live standards before launch.

**From:** MCR-6023, MCR-6044, MCR-6799, MCR-6022, MCR-6025, MCR-6060, MCR-6045, MCR-6063, MCR-6020, MCR-6608

### 30. Use AI for briefs, scripts and production, keep humans on ideas and final analysis, and never use AI to fake a demonstration or a customer

**Why it works:** Language models drift to the average answer, so AI-led ideation gives every brand the same concepts, while production is where AI saves the most time. Faked demos and AI customers break trust and law, and turn into refunds.

**How to apply:** Write 30 to 40 strong personas by hand from research; then give the model wide context, ask for 20 more, keep the best 4 or 5 and repeat. Use AI video to dramatise the problem or the story, never to show the product working or a person who did not use it. In fashion, avoid AI renders of garments; they drift from the real item and returns rise. Strip AI tells from scripts, such as it's not this, it's that lines. At scale, transcribe live ads and check for clashing claims.

**Judge it by:** Concepts trace to human research, and AI footage never stands in for product proof. Weak work ships a model's list of ten ad ideas as the strategy.

**Breaks when:** Newer models may close some of the ideation gap; tiny teams with no strategist may still gain from AI-led work with careful review.

**From:** MCR-6646, MCR-6676, MCR-6789, MCR-6876, MCR-6809, MCR-6833, MCR-0016, MCR-6750

## Working from the org context

- Read the buyers' own words first: the phrases, the accommodations they describe, the failed fixes they name. Hooks and problem beats come from there, not from the tagline. If the brain holds only a handful of real buyer quotes, pull reviews and tickets before writing.
- Read what ran and what won: list past winners by persona, angle, offer and format, with spend and store results. Remake or relaunch proven winners before inventing new ideas, and note which cells have never been tried.
- Keep the voice and the claims list exactly: use only approved claims, word for word, at the brand's reading level. Native and lo-fi formats still speak in the brand's voice; change the register, never the personality.
- Keep the design system's distinctive cues (colours, type, pack, characters, sound) in every ad, even native-looking ones, so the attention lands on this brand.
- Check offers and stock before you brief: an ad must carry the live offer and push products with stock depth and margin, not ones about to sell out.
- Respect positioning limits: an aspirational brand that keeps price out of creative, or a premium brand wary of pain-led ads, can still run problem-led work if the agitation is a neutral observation, run through creators or partnership ads, rather than pity.
- Where the data shows the brand is under-weight (all product-aware ads, one format, one persona), propose the change as a small test with a named cell, a budget of about 3 times target cost per purchase and a stop rule, never as a rewrite of the brand.
- Write back what you learn: each test's verdict, the winning phrases and new persona notes go into the brain so the next job starts further ahead.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Does every ad name its persona, angle, offer, awareness stage and format?
- [ ] Does each new ad change at least one of persona, angle or offer from what already runs?
- [ ] Does each hook show or name a real buyer's problem in the buyer's own words, clear to a stranger in 3 seconds?
- [ ] Is the batch size worked out from winner churn and hit rate, with the numbers shown?
- [ ] Does every ad pass the written quality bar?
- [ ] Do most ads speak to unaware, problem-aware or solution-aware buyers, unless the category or outside demand justifies otherwise?
- [ ] Do the first frame, on-screen text, primary text, headline and landing page top make one claim at one stage?
- [ ] Are hook variants written only for bodies with a sales record, and do they differ on line, visual or voice?
- [ ] In cold video, does the promise come before the mechanism and the product name after it?
- [ ] Does every static commit to clear or curious, and does each curious one land on a teaching page?
- [ ] Is every claim on the approved list, smaller than its proof, with each number traced to a source?
- [ ] Are endorsements real, disclosed early on screen and in speech, and free of implied personal attributes?
- [ ] Are results read at ad set level on 7-day click (or the incremental column) against store orders, with no 1-day view?
- [ ] Does each test verdict meet the data rule of about 3 times target cost per purchase over about a week?
- [ ] Does every ad name carry the shared tag fields, and does each loser note name the step that broke (stop, hold, click or buy)?
- [ ] Is fatigue diagnosed with a number (frequency over 2, pool size, or a change log entry) before any winner is replaced?
- [ ] Does the plan keep new concepts at 20% or more and stop any one ad or concept from carrying the account?
- [ ] Is each placement cut to its own frame, length and safe zones?
- [ ] Can a viewer name the brand from its cues in the first seconds?

## Common failures

- Calling eight colour or text swaps of one ad eight tests. Fix: count unique persona, angle and offer combinations, and make each new ad differ on one of them.
- Hooks that stop everyone and sell to no one (a satisfying clip, an unnamed snack, a hey guys opener). Fix: open on the buyer's problem or belief so delivery learns who buys.
- Naming the product and its features in the first line of a cold video. Fix: hook, problem and promise first, mechanism next, product named at the offer.
- Generic copy: game changer, busy moms, customers love it. Fix: buyer phrases from reviews, a specific persona, counted proof.
- Calling winners on ad-level ROAS with 1-day view after two days. Fix: ad set level, 7-day click or incremental column, about 3 times target cost per purchase in spend, about a week.
- Pausing the low-ROAS ads inside an ad set that hits target. Fix: leave working ad sets alone and launch new ads in a new ad set.
- Replacing a hero ad the moment results dip. Fix: check frequency, pool size and the change log, then try new hooks from a new speaker or a new setting before a new concept.
- Writing off a losing ad as "did not perform". Fix: name the step that broke (3-second view, 15-second hold, CTR to link CTR gap, purchase) and change only that field in the next version.
- Copying a famous brand's polished lifestyle ads into a cold-start account. Fix: study brands one or two steps bigger and check how much of their demand comes from outside paid social.
- Pouring budget into retargeting and catalogue ads because their reported return is high. Fix: check with existing customers excluded and the incremental column, and keep warm creative small and objection-led.
- Letting AI pick the ideas and fake the proof. Fix: human research and concepts, AI for scripts and production, real demonstrations and real customers only.
- Clever curiosity statics sent straight to a product page, then giving up on statics. Fix: curious statics go to a listicle or advertorial; clear statics go to the product page.
