# Claims and compliance: craft principles

This book is for everyone who writes, checks or ships the claims an online store makes: compliance and ad policy reviewers, ad copywriters, campaign builders and packaging designers. Read it before the job, write to it, and run the review checklist on your draft before you hand it over. The aim is strong selling words that survive a platform reviewer, a regulator and a rival's lawyer; we flag risk and draft fixes, and a qualified lawyer confirms the hard cases.

## What great looks like

- Every objective claim in an ad, page, email or pack maps to a dated evidence file that supports its exact wording and scope.
- Each asset is checked for every market and platform it reaches, and where one asset serves many markets, the strictest rule wins.
- A risky line comes back as a rewrite that still sells, with its proof named, not as a deletion or a vague hedge.
- Prices, was prices, deadlines and stock counts trace to the store's own records for the moment they ran.
- Testimonials, reviews and creator posts are real, unaltered in meaning, and carry their disclosure up front.
- Labels list every mandatory field per market before design starts, and no listing goes live without its certificate.
- Consent for messages and tracking is asked in plain words beside the button, and every yes is logged.
- Anything close to the line goes to a lawyer as one clear question with the evidence attached; nothing is ever called legally safe.

## The principles

### 1. Build the claims sheet before anyone writes a line: each objective claim, the evidence for it, its date and who checked it

**Why it works:** In the US, an objective claim made without a reasonable basis is deceptive, and an ad that says "tests prove" must hold that level of proof. The UK CAP Code wants the documents in hand before the ad runs. Regulators judge the whole impression an ad leaves, not just its literal words.

**How to apply:** List every claim a buyer could check: a number, a result, a time frame, a ranking, an ingredient amount, including those hidden in product names, image text and alt text. Beside each, record the evidence file, what it measured, the sample, the date and the market. Then write the claim to the evidence: a hydration test at 2 hours supports "more hydrated 2 hours after use", not "24-hour hydration". Copy may use only claims on the sheet, and each script leads with one promise that sits inside its proof, not five.

**Judge it by:** Every objective line maps to a row, and the words match the test's scope. Weak work holds proof for a smaller claim than the one made.

**Breaks when:** The line is pure opinion no buyer reads as measurable ("the comfiest tee you'll own"). Puffery stops being safe once it names a measurable result.

**From:** MCR-6022, MCR-6713, MCR-6706; ASA CAP Code, rule 3.7 (https://www.asa.org.uk/codes-and-rulings/advertising-codes/non-broadcast-code.html)

### 2. Set claim strength by what the business must survive, and drop any angle whose returns show the product does not deliver it

**Why it works:** Bigger claims can lower CAC for a while, but the cost returns as ad account bans, refunds, chargebacks, weak repeat rates and a lower sale price. Blue Sense describes $50M to $100M supplement brands that beat a compliant rival's CAC with wild claims, yet face constant bans and are likely unsellable, since a buyer inherits the risk and may switch off the ads that drive sales.

**How to apply:** Before choosing an angle, note whether the store depends on one ad account, whether the owner plans to sell, and how strict the category is. Then tag orders by the angle that drove them (UTMs or a post-purchase survey) and read returns, refunds, chargebacks and support tickets by angle. If an angle's return rate sits far above the store's norm (one example ran 50%), cut it even if its ROAS is the best in the account. Where buyers repeat, compare 365-day gross profit per buyer by angle, not first-order CAC.

**Judge it by:** The recommendation names each angle's claim risk and its refund or return signal. Weak work ranks angles on CAC alone.

**Breaks when:** The category carries little claim risk, such as basic apparel; or buying is one-off, so read refunds and chargebacks only.

**From:** MCR-6608, OPS-6623, CRO-6609, MCR-6727

### 3. Rewrite a risky claim into a checkable specific or a scene from the buyer's life, never into a vague hedge

**Why it works:** Hedges like 'may help support' lose selling power and still imply the claim. Specifics persuade because a buyer can check them, and a scene sells the outcome without promising a medical result. Blue Sense reports IM8 showing a moment like joining in with the grandkids instead of saying "improves mobility", which converts better and carries less risk.

**How to apply:** For each blocked or risky line, try two rewrites in order. First, a specific the proof supports: a number, a named third party, a physical detail or a timeframe ("dry in 40 seconds, we timed it" instead of "fast absorbing"). Second, a scene of what the buyer can now do, with no disease or body change named. Then test the scene: if a fair reader takes it as the same promise, it is the same claim. Keep proven lines at full strength; cutting a true claim out of fear costs sales for nothing.

**Judge it by:** Each fix keeps a selling point and names its evidence. Weak work swaps "cures" for "may help with", which still targets the disease.

**Breaks when:** The scene still implies a regulated result (a pill ad showing someone running pain free), or the product is technical and buyers want specs.

**From:** MCR-6613, MCR-6713, MCR-6828

### 4. Write for the platform reviewer as well as the law, because it judges the landing page and can stop the whole account

**Why it works:** Platform enforcement is faster and often costs more than a regulator. Meta reviews image, video, text, targeting and landing page, usually within 24 hours, and can restrict the ad account, Page or whole business portfolio. Google can suspend Merchant Center over price mismatches; Amazon can deactivate a seller and hold funds. A suspension stops sales the same day.

**How to apply:** Check the page against the ad before launch: same price, same offer, same claim. Follow Google's editorial rules (no gimmicky capitals or symbols, no phone number in the text, the business named). On Amazon, keep best-seller claims, prices, warranty text and competitor comparisons out of A+ content, and hold order defects under 1%. After a rejection, find the cause in policy terms first; an edited ad is reviewed as new, so fix the cause instead of nudging words. Above about $250k a month on Meta, a second ad account limits the damage of one ban.

**Judge it by:** Each rejection has a named policy cause and a fix or a drafted appeal. Weak work resubmits small edits until one slips through.

**Breaks when:** Special ad categories and regulated goods need extra authorisation first.

**From:** ADC-6082, ADC-6142, GAD-6122, AMZ-6444, AMZ-6446, ADC-6225

### 5. On Meta, describe what the product is for, never what is wrong with the viewer, and keep body results out of the frame

**Why it works:** Meta bans ad content that asserts or implies about 12 kinds of personal attribute, including health, age and finances. "Struggling with hair loss?" gets rejected; "a shampoo made for thinning hair" can pass. Weight-loss and cosmetic ads must target 18+, side-by-side weight-loss transformations are banned, and plain before-and-after shots tend to raise CPMs or get disapproved.

**How to apply:** Search every hook, caption and on-screen line for "you" or "your" next to a sensitive attribute, and rewrite it as a product or third-person line. For sensitive health topics, use an illustration with a five or six word persona-and-problem headline instead of graphic images. Read Meta's live Health and Wellness page on the day: its before-and-after wording changed in December 2024 and July 2026. A small, fast inset is still a breach where the image is banned. Check targeting and the landing page, since review covers both.

**Judge it by:** No line pairs "you" with a condition, 18+ is set where required, and any before-and-after is marked as a risk with the policy date read. Weak work tucks the breach into a corner.

**Breaks when:** The post is organic, under community rules, or the platform is TikTok or Google, which word their limits differently.

**From:** MCR-6023, MCR-6044, MCR-0020, MCR-6799, ADC-6082

### 6. Match the type of proof to the size of the claim: the bigger the claim, the higher up the proof ladder it must sit

**Why it works:** Proof has to answer the doubt the message creates. From strongest to weakest the ladder runs: demonstration, third-party verification, a volume of specific social proof, a named person's testimony, a mechanism explanation, then founder assertion. A big result claim backed only by the founder reads as hype to buyers and as unsupported to regulators.

**How to apply:** For each headline claim, note its rung. A visual promise ("stain gone in one wipe") needs an unedited demo in one take. A health-adjacent result needs third-party data or a real, qualified expert. Five reviews naming the exact benefit beat 400 generic ones, while trust badges and security seals close nobody. Never cast an actor or a borrowed white coat as an expert; that breaks platform policy and the buyer's trust.

**Judge it by:** Each headline claim has proof on a matching rung, shown near the claim. Weak work pairs a big promise with a star rating.

**Breaks when:** The claim is banned whoever says it, or the product makes so few claims that a creator demo is enough.

**From:** MCR-6712, MCR-6754, LPG-6620

### 7. Check whether a word changes the product's legal category before it goes on an ad, page or pack

**Why it works:** Regulators class many products by what the seller says they do. In the US, a cream "to treat acne" can become a drug, a fabric that "kills germs" can become a pesticide needing EPA registration, and a product marketed to children 12 and under becomes a children's product needing third-party lab tests, a certificate and tracking labels. One word moves the product into a regime the store has not met.

**How to apply:** Flag three word families. Disease and body function words (treat, heal, prevent, acne, eczema, pain) on cosmetics and supplements. Germ words (antibacterial, kills 99.9%) on any unregistered product; careful odour or mildew resistance wording on a treated article can stay outside. Child words and images (for kids, ages 3+) on general products. For each hit, drop the word or meet the new category's rules first.

**Judge it by:** No category-changing word appears without its registration, test or certificate on file. Weak work treats wording as style only.

**Breaks when:** The product is already registered for that use, or the market sorts products differently (EU cosmetics follow their own claim criteria).

**From:** COG-6375, COG-6373; FDA, cosmetic or drug (https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap); EPA PRN 2000-1 on treated articles (https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/prn-2000-1-applicability-treated-articles-exemption-antimicrobial-pesticides); EU Regulation 655/2013 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2013/655/oj)

### 8. Write food and supplement health claims to each market's list, not to one global script

**Why it works:** The US and Europe work in opposite ways. A US supplement may make structure or function claims ("supports joint comfort") with the FDA disclaimer and a notice to FDA within 30 days of first marketing, but never a disease claim. The EU and Great Britain allow only health claims authorised on their registers, worded close to the approved text. A US script run in the UK can be unlawful word for word.

**How to apply:** Keep one claims row per market. For the EU and GB, find the authorised claim for the nutrient, confirm the product holds the amount its condition of use requires, and write close to that wording. For the US, write structure or function only, file the notice, and carry the disclaimer. Canada and Australia keep their own lists; flag them for the lawyer. Borrow a rival's format, never its claims.

**Judge it by:** Each market's version cites its register entry or US basis. Weak work translates a US claim into German and runs it.

**Breaks when:** The product is a medicine or medical device, which needs a licence; or it is a botanical whose EU claims are still on hold.

**From:** MCR-6608, MCR-6757; FDA structure/function claims (https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/structurefunction-claims); EU Regulation 1924/2006 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/1924/oj); GB NHC register (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/great-britain-nutrition-and-health-claims-nhc-register)

### 9. Show a was price only if the store really charged it, and in the EU set it from the lowest price of the last 30 days

**Why it works:** A strikethrough lifts conversion because buyers read it as a real saving, so a fake one misleads by design. In the US the former price must have been openly offered in good faith for a reasonably substantial period. In the EU the prior price must be the lowest of the 30 days before the cut, and in 2024 the EU Court of Justice said the percentage off is worked from it. A brand on a permanent headline discount has no real full price.

**How to apply:** Pull 30 to 90 days of price history per SKU and market before any sale. In the EU use the lowest 30-day price; in the US, a price held openly for weeks. Never launch high just to discount. A bundle shown as "$160 for $100" needs the $60 item to have sold at $60. Keep feeds aligned: Google disapproves products whose feed price differs from the page.

**Judge it by:** Every was price links to a dated price record. Weak work uses the compare-at field as a marketing number.

**Breaks when:** The price is introductory or for members, which have their own rules, or a US state sets its own period.

**From:** OFF-6145, OFF-6661, OFF-6684, ADC-6047, GAD-6122; EU Directive 2019/2161 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/2161/oj)

### 10. Make the first price the buyer sees the price they pay, and say plainly who pays duties on cross-border orders

**Why it works:** Surprise extra costs are the top reason buyers leave checkout, and regulators now treat hidden mandatory fees as drip pricing: the UK bans it, with CMA fines up to 10% of global turnover, and California requires all-in prices. Cross-border orders add a second surprise. The US de minimis exemption is suspended, and since 1 July 2026 the EU charges a €3 duty per item on parcels up to €150, so a buyer can face a bill on delivery and refuse the parcel.

**How to apply:** Put every mandatory fee in the headline price; optional extras may be added if shown as optional. Show VAT included for UK and EU buyers. Keep shipping and fees charged at checkout under about 10% of order value. For each foreign market, collect duties at checkout (delivered duty paid, which needs HS codes and a capable carrier) or state on the page that the buyer pays them.

**Judge it by:** The page price equals the checkout total before optional extras. Weak work adds a "service fee" at the last step.

**Breaks when:** US sales tax, which is still usually added at checkout.

**From:** LPG-0063, SET-6204, OPS-6647, OPS-6658; CMA guidance (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207); California hidden fees law (https://oag.ca.gov/hiddenfees)

### 11. Show the real charge, how often it repeats and how to cancel before the buyer enters card details on any subscription

**Why it works:** Subscriptions draw the most enforcement. US federal law requires all material terms before billing details, express consent before charging, and a simple way to stop; it stands though the FTC's click-to-cancel rule was struck down in 2025. California adds online cancelling and renewal notices. A monthly figure that charges quarterly lifts take rate, but Blue Sense thinks it breaks some state rules.

**How to apply:** Beside the subscribe button, show the price after any first-order discount, the frequency, the first charge date and how to cancel, with a separate, unticked consent. Let buyers cancel online the way they joined. Win saves honestly: a one-minute founder video offering pause, delay or swap lifted weak cancel flows from a 5 to 6% save rate to about 15%, with the cancel button still on screen. Remind buyers before a trial turns paid or a yearly plan renews. EU online contracts need a withdrawal function from 19 June 2026.

**Judge it by:** A reviewer finds the total charge and cancel route without opening the cart. Weak work hides the frequency until checkout or the cancel button behind offers.

**Breaks when:** One-off or business sales. These rules move (UK rules phase in later); recheck before each launch.

**From:** OFF-6304, OFF-6610, CXP-0002; California AB 2863 (https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2863); EU Directive 2023/2673 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2673/oj)

### 12. Treat every testimonial as the brand's own claim: real use, honest opinion, and typical results or a clear note of what most buyers get

**Why it works:** US guides say an endorsement must reflect the endorser's honest opinion and real use, and an exceptional result needs proof it is typical or a clear statement of what people generally get. A customer saying "it cured my eczema" is a disease claim the brand now makes. The US rule on fake reviews and testimonials, in force since 21 October 2024, allows civil penalties, and it covers AI-made ones.

**How to apply:** Before a quote runs, confirm the person bought or used the product, the words are theirs and unchanged in meaning, and you hold permission for their name and face. Run the quote through the same claims sheet as brand copy. If a featured result beats what most get, add the generally expected result in plain words next to it, from the store's data. Choose each testimonial to answer one buying objection, so the set does selling work.

**Judge it by:** Each quote has a source record, a permission and a claims check. Weak work lets a reviewer's health claim through because it is "only a quote".

**Breaks when:** The line is the brand speaking in its own voice, which falls under substantiation instead.

**From:** MCR-6025, MCR-6021, MCR-6045, LPG-0053

### 13. Collect reviews without steering sentiment, and publish the bad with the good

**Why it works:** Going from no reviews to about 80 can roughly double conversion, so stores push hard, and that is where breaches happen. The FTC allows a reward for a review only if no sentiment is asked for, and bans suppressing negative reviews. Google bans review incentives and asking only happy customers. The UK lists fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews among practices banned outright.

**How to apply:** Ask every buyer, not just the happy ones, with no star filter in front of the review link. If you reward reviews, reward any honest review and say so; never on Google or Amazon, which ban it. Moderate negative and positive reviews by the same written rules, removing only for set reasons such as abuse or private data. Mark up only reviews visible on the page and written by users. Sorting photo and video reviews to the top is allowed and helps.

**Judge it by:** The request flow has no gate and no sentiment wording, and moderation rules are written down. Weak work sends "loved it? leave us 5 stars" emails.

**Breaks when:** A marketplace runs its own program (such as Amazon Vine), or local rules are stricter.

**From:** LPG-6616, CXP-6301, CXP-6300, SEO-6465, MCR-6045; CMA fake reviews guidance (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208)

### 14. Put every paid or gifted connection in the content itself, up front, in plain words, on screen and spoken

**Why it works:** The FTC wants any money, job, family or free-product link disclosed where viewers cannot miss it; platform tags alone and vague words like "collab" do not count. The UK ASA expects the ad label up front as well. Partnership ads can carry up to 40% of spend in some accounts, so a missing label puts a large share of revenue at risk.

**How to apply:** Use "ad", "sponsored" or "paid partnership with" the brand at the start of the caption, on the image long enough to read, and early in any video, both shown and said, since many people watch with the sound off. Add the platform's branded content tag as well, not instead. Gifted product counts. For affiliate and referral programs, give members the exact words, tell them to disclose, and check a sample each month; the advertiser must train and monitor the people it rewards.

**Judge it by:** Every creator asset shows the label in its first seconds and its caption's first line. Weak work leaves it in a hashtag cluster at the end.

**Breaks when:** The poster is an unpaid customer who was never asked, or the share is private between friends who know about the reward.

**From:** ORG-6405, MCR-6060, CXP-6303, ADC-6210

### 15. Run urgency and scarcity only when they are true at the moment the buyer sees them

**Why it works:** A real deadline shortens the time from first view to purchase, which matters when every impression costs money. A fake one is a named unfair practice: the UK bans false "very limited time" claims, and regulators look for timers that reset and "only 3 left" lines with no stock behind them. True scarcity sells better too: from Robert Cialdini's Influence, stock that drops from plenty to few because others bought it pulls harder than a shortage that never changes.

**How to apply:** Tie every countdown to a fixed end date; when it ends, the price goes back up for everyone. Show stock counts only from live inventory, and say when demand caused the shortage ("restocked Monday, 70% gone"). Retire evergreen timers and permanent "only 3 left" banners. A near-permanent "60% off, ends today" makes a discount brand with no real full price. Earn year-round urgency with real drops, as Last Crumb did with fixed weekly drops of limited stock.

**Judge it by:** Each urgency line has a date or stock source a reviewer can check. Weak work loops a 15-minute timer on every visit.

**Breaks when:** The edition or drop is truly limited, which is both lawful and strong.

**From:** OFF-6661, WLD-0009, CRO-6609; Robert Cialdini, Influence; CMA guidance (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207)

### 16. Use free, guarantee and lifetime only in their legal meaning, and put the conditions in the ad itself

**Why it works:** These words carry promises. In the US, 'money back' or 'satisfaction guarantee' means a full refund on request, with any material condition shown in the ad, and 'lifetime' must say whose or what lifetime it covers. "Free" means the buyer pays nothing beyond stated delivery. A gift that is really store credit must say so.

**How to apply:** Write the guarantee terms first, then the ad line from them, with the conditions beside it (unused, 30 days, original buyer). For a gift paid as credit, as with Lovevery's welcome code worth up to $54, say "credit" on the page. Then test longer terms: one agency reports that on cheap consumables a 180-day guarantee brings fewer claims than a 30-day one, so test both and read refunds per order by cohort. Refund without return, given case by case through support, usually pays under about $20, since a return can cost 20% to 65% of the item's cost.

**Judge it by:** Every free or guarantee line has its terms one tap away and its conditions in view. Weak work says "risk free" over a 14-day, unopened-only policy.

**Breaks when:** UK and EU buyers hold legal withdrawal rights a guarantee cannot reduce, or a US written warranty brings extra federal duties.

**From:** OFF-6501, OFF-6502, OFF-6626, OFF-0060, CXP-0018

### 17. Do not list a product in a market until its tests, certificates and responsible people exist

**Why it works:** Undocumented goods get seized, recalled or blocked, often at peak. One missing customs document got Oodie's stock seized over Black Friday, the window for about 80% of its annual profit. In the US the importer, not the factory, issues the children's product certificate from a CPSC-accepted lab, and from 8 July 2026 certificate data must be filed with customs at entry, even for low-value shipments.

**How to apply:** For each SKU and market, list the rules that apply, the test and lab, the certificate, who holds it, and the retest date. US children's products need third-party tests, lead under 100 ppm, paint under 90 ppm, tracking labels, and retests at least yearly or on a set plan. EU products need a risk assessment and technical file before sale. Name one person who answers a regulator within 24 hours.

**Judge it by:** No listing goes live with a blank certificate cell. Weak work trusts a factory's note that says "CE ok".

**Breaks when:** No specific rule covers the product; general safety duties still apply, and edge cases need legal advice.

**From:** OPS-0001, COG-6373, COG-6375, COG-6392, COG-6313, COG-6385, COG-6312

### 18. Make tracking follow the buyer's consent choice, and still send every signal the buyer allowed

**Why it works:** Missing conversions raise ad costs: if Meta sees 70 of 100 sales, a 3% conversion rate reads as about 2%, which lowers the store's auction value. But in the UK and EU, non-essential cookies and pixels need prior consent, and regulators expect rejecting to be as easy as accepting. The skill is not to dodge consent but to stop losing signals the buyer did allow.

**How to apply:** Make "Reject all" as visible as "Accept all" on the first screen of the banner. Fire the pixel on the consent update, not only on page load, so a visitor who accepts is not lost. Send server-side events for buyers who consented. Consent duties stay with the store even when an app installs the pixel. Health and wellness stores should check how Meta has classed their data source, since restricted classes cannot send purchase events; recheck that setting each quarter.

**Judge it by:** A tag test shows no non-essential tag before consent and full firing after. Weak work loads the pixel before the banner.

**Breaks when:** US states mostly use opt-out models, with stricter rules for health data, and these rules change yearly.

**From:** ADC-6171, MEA-0007, MEA-6227; EDPB cookie banner taskforce report (https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/other/report-work-undertaken-cookie-banner-taskforce_en); ICO cookies guidance (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/cookies-and-similar-technologies/)

### 19. Collect email, SMS and WhatsApp consent with the wording beside the button, never as a condition of buying

**Why it works:** Owned channels are a store's cheapest repeat sales, and a consent breach can wipe out a list or bring damages for each message. US texts need express written consent that says it is not a condition of purchase, no sends before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. local time, and opt-outs by any reasonable means honoured within 10 business days. US email needs a postal address and opt-outs honoured within 10 business days, with penalties up to $53,088 per email. In the UK and EU, marketing email needs prior consent unless the soft opt-in for existing customers applies.

**How to apply:** Put SMS wording above the consent button on every form, unticked and optional. Use double opt-in where carriers require it, such as US cart reminders. Send STOP instructions at least monthly. For Gmail, authenticate with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, offer one-click unsubscribe and keep spam under 0.30%. WhatsApp opt-in must name the business. Log the time, source and wording of every consent.

**Judge it by:** Each form shows its wording, and the consent log exports. Weak work pre-ticks the SMS box at checkout.

**Breaks when:** A US state or another market sets stricter times or consent rules; check each provider's country guide.

**From:** EML-6345, EML-6323, EML-6327, EML-6341, EML-6385, EML-6340, EML-6320; ICO guidance on PECR (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/)

### 20. Compare only on a measurable attribute you win on today, like for like, with dated proof for both sides

**Why it works:** Comparison is one of the strongest selling moves; HexClad's lifetime warranty against rivals' 1 to 3 years answers a real buying objection. The US lets an ad name a rival if the basis is clear and truthful. The EU allows comparison only on material, relevant, verifiable and representative features. A wrong or stale comparison invites a rival's legal letter, the fastest way to lose a winning ad.

**How to apply:** Name the attribute, the unit and the date. Capture the rival's page or label as a dated screenshot plus an archive copy. Compare like with like (price per use, same size). When proof on the rival is thin, compare against the category or the old way instead. "Best-selling" and "#1" need current data for the market and period stated. On Google a rival may bid on your trademark, but its use in ad text can be restricted.

**Judge it by:** Each comparison line links to two dated pieces of evidence. Weak work sets the store's best SKU against a rival's cheapest.

**Breaks when:** The products do not really differ; a mechanism claim will not be believed, so win on a buyer the rival does not serve instead.

**From:** MCR-6020, MCR-0043, GAD-6120, AVT-0014; EU Directive 2006/114 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/114/oj)

### 21. Keep one source of truth for every number the store repeats, and check the whole ad library against it

**Why it works:** At hundreds of live ads, claims drift and clash. Blue Sense found IM8 ads launched the same day claiming over 700,000 and about 395,000 customers, while its filings showed 414,000. Clashing numbers hand a regulator or rival an easy complaint, and buyers who notice stop trusting every claim.

**How to apply:** Hold customer counts, review counts, ratings, ingredient amounts, awards and test results in one dated file in the org context, and pull every ad, page and email from it. When a fact changes (a new formula, more reviews), list every live asset that carries the old fact and update or pause it. Each quarter, transcribe the live ad library and search it for numbers and claims that disagree. Make the first frame, the headline and the top of the landing page carry one claim, not three.

**Judge it by:** Numbers match across ads, pages and pack, and a dated change log exists. Weak work quotes last year's review count.

**Breaks when:** The account runs only a few ads, which can be checked by hand.

**From:** MCR-6609, MCR-6750, ADC-6218

### 22. Use AI to show the idea or the story, never to fake a demo, a customer or an expert

**Why it works:** A fake AI demo misstates what the product does. Buyers who spot it turn on the brand, and buyers fooled by it become refunds and chargebacks. The US fake reviews rule bans AI-made testimonials, and YouTube requires a label on realistic synthetic content, such as a real person shown saying what they never said.

**How to apply:** Allowed: AI illustration of a problem, a mechanism explainer, clearly stylised animation, scripts, captions and upscaling. Not allowed: AI people presented as customers, AI footage of the product doing something, or a cloned voice of a real person without permission. Apply each platform's AI label whenever generated content looks real. Keep a note per asset of what was made with AI, so a reviewer can answer a platform query in minutes.

**Judge it by:** Every realistic AI asset is labelled and none poses as a buyer or a demo. Weak work runs an AI avatar saying it tried the product for 30 days.

**Breaks when:** The piece is plainly illustrated and no viewer reads it as real, or it is a political or issue ad, which carries stricter disclosure.

**From:** MCR-6809, MCR-6045, MCR-6064, MCR-6063

### 23. Put every mandatory label field into the artwork before design starts, one list per market

**Why it works:** A label is a legal document before it is a brand surface. A missed field means relabelling stock, a blocked shipment or a takedown, which costs far more than the print run. EU sales need a responsible economic operator based in the EU, named with a postal address; Great Britain needs the GB importer's address; US apparel needs fibre content, origin and maker ID plus a permanent care label backed by evidence.

**How to apply:** Open each pack brief with a field list per market: product name, net quantity, maker or importer name and address, origin, batch or serial number, safety warnings in the local language, fibre and care for textiles, and CE or UKCA marks where a rule applies. For California, put the Prop 65 warning on the product page or before checkout, naming at least one chemical. Lay these out first, then design around them, and run every claim on the pack through the claims sheet.

**Judge it by:** Each market's list is ticked off against the dieline. Weak work designs the front, then squeezes legal text in at the end.

**Breaks when:** The category has its own regime (food, cosmetics, electricals, toys) and needs a specialist's list.

**From:** COG-6384, COG-6385, COG-6388, COG-6263, COG-6383, COG-6395, COG-6396

### 24. Promise a ship or delivery time only from your own fulfilment data, and tell buyers before a delay

**Why it works:** Fast delivery promises lift conversion, and broken ones turn into refunds, chargebacks and marketplace penalties. The FTC's mail order rule requires a reasonable basis for any ship time stated or implied, shipping within 30 days when none is stated, and a delay notice with a free way to cancel. At peak, a 24 to 48 hour pick promise can slip to 7 days.

**How to apply:** Set the promise from the last 30 days of dispatch and carrier records, split into pick time and courier time, not from the best case. Track delivered-on-time yourself and look into it whenever it falls below 95%. Change the promise for peak season and for each region. When an order will miss it, email the buyer first with the new date and a free cancel with a refund; the FTC expects refunds within about seven working days, and store credit does not count. Write pick-on-time standards with penalties into the 3PL contract so the promise has teeth.

**Judge it by:** The stated time matches recent records, and a delay email with a cancel option exists before peak. Weak work keeps a theme's default "ships in 24 hours".

**Breaks when:** Orders outside the US follow local consumer law, and carrier transit times need their own data.

**From:** CXP-6160, OPS-0015, AMZ-6444

### 25. Say where a product is made only to the standard each market sets, and mark imports so customs agrees

**Why it works:** Origin sells, and it is checked by regulators and at the border. In the US an unqualified 'Made in USA' means all or virtually all made there, and the FTC can seek civil penalties for each violation. Every import must show its origin country in English, and goods that arrive unmarked owe an extra 10% duty. A US place name on a product made abroad needs 'Made in' plus the true country close by.

**How to apply:** Write origin claims from the bill of materials and the factory, not from where the brand is based. Use qualified claims where true ("designed in California, made in Portugal", "assembled in USA from imported parts"). Check apparel labels show the country of origin on the front of the label. A non-US brand can present as local in the US, with a US site and US copy, but must never imply US manufacture.

**Judge it by:** Every origin word matches a supplier record, and pack and page say the same thing. Weak work puts a flag on an imported product.

**Breaks when:** Other markets, food or textiles set their own origin rules; flag those for the lawyer.

**From:** COG-6370, COG-6397, COG-6263, LPG-6612; FTC Made in USA Labeling Rule (https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/made-usa-labeling-rule)

### 26. Make environmental claims specific, about the part that is true, and backed by a recognised method, or leave them out

**Why it works:** Green claims are now a top enforcement target. Since 27 September 2026 EU law bans generic claims such as "eco-friendly" without recognised excellent environmental performance, claims about a whole product when only part qualifies, product claims of climate neutrality based on offsets, and sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme. Canada requires adequate and proper testing and has allowed private cases since June 2025. The US Green Guides and Australia's ACCC push the same way.

**How to apply:** Replace each broad word with the exact fact and its scope ("outer box made from 80% recycled card"), backed by a supplier certificate. In the US, say "recyclable" only where facilities exist for a substantial majority of buyers. Drop offset-based "carbon neutral" product claims for EU buyers. Where one asset serves all markets, use the EU set.

**Judge it by:** No broad green word stands alone; each claim names the part, the number and the proof. Weak work says "sustainable" with nothing behind it.

**Breaks when:** An official label (such as the EU Ecolabel) is used as its scheme allows. These rules are moving, so recheck them every year.

**From:** EU Directive 2024/825 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/825/oj); FTC Green Guides, 16 CFR 260 (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-260); Canada guidance summary (https://www.blakes.com/insights/competition-bureau-releases-guidelines-on-environmental-claims/); ACCC principles (https://accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-releases-eight-principles-to-guide-businesses%E2%80%99-environmental-claims)

### 27. Clear a product or brand name, and every rival mark you use, before it goes on a pack, a domain or an ad

**Why it works:** Renaming after launch throws away packaging, reviews, links and search demand. The USPTO refuses a mark that is confusingly similar to one on related goods, including different spellings said the same way. Amazon Brand Registry needs a pending or registered trademark, so no mark means no brand tools and weaker protection from copycats.

**How to apply:** Before naming, search the free USPTO database, state registers and common-law use on the web, then the UK and EU trademark offices for those markets. Check sound, look and meaning, not just spelling: a name said the same way as a live mark on related goods fails. File in each market you sell in before launch, because a pending application already opens Amazon Brand Registry, and the brand name must be fixed on the product or pack. In ads, use a rival's name only inside a truthful comparison; on Google you can complain when a rival puts your mark in its ad text.

**Judge it by:** The name file shows each search, its date and the result. Weak work picks a name because the domain was free.

**Breaks when:** The name is too descriptive to register, or the call is close; both need a trademark lawyer.

**From:** BRD-6484, BRD-6485, AMZ-6441, GAD-6120

### 28. When you find a breach, pause it, count what ran, and put one clear question to the lawyer before the deadline

**Why it works:** Speed changes the cost. In the US a firm must report to CPSC within 24 hours once information reasonably supports a reportable hazard. A negligent customs misclassification can cost up to twice the lost duty, cut to interest if the importer discloses it first. Even refunds have deadlines: some US tariff refunds are refused once entries pass a set window.

**How to apply:** When a claim, label or classification is wrong: pause the live assets, list every place it ran with dates, spend and units sold, keep the evidence, draft the corrected version, and write the lawyer a one-line question naming the rule and the date it bites. Never delete records or quietly edit the page. Log the fix and the approved wording in the org context so the same breach does not return in the next job.

**Judge it by:** The hand-over names what ran, where, for how long, and the deadline. Weak work edits the page and says nothing.

**Breaks when:** It is a routine platform rejection, which needs a fix, not a report.

**From:** COG-6393, OPS-6686, FIN-6437

### 29. Make product, cart and checkout usable by keyboard and screen reader, because EU law now requires it

**Why it works:** The European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025 to online shops selling to EU consumers; micro businesses with under 10 staff and no more than €2M turnover are exempt from the service duties. In the US, store websites are a frequent target of disability claims. A buyer who cannot finish checkout is a lost sale as well as a legal risk, and specific alt text also helps Google read product images, which it judges with the page.

**How to apply:** Meet WCAG 2.1 level AA on the buying path: alt text that says what each product image shows, a label on every form field, a visible focus outline, body text contrast of at least 4.5 to 1, captions on product videos, and no time limits without a way to extend them. Publish an accessibility statement. Test the checkout with a keyboard only and a free screen reader before each major theme change.

**Judge it by:** Checkout can be finished with the keyboard alone, and images carry useful alt text. Weak work relies on an overlay widget or stuffs alt text with keywords.

**Breaks when:** The store is an EU micro business, though marketplaces and larger partners may still require it.

**From:** SEO-6463; EU Directive 2019/882 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj); W3C WCAG 2.1 (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/)

## Working from the org context

- Read the approved claims and product facts first, and quote them word for word. The voice can change the rhythm of a claim, never its scope.
- Read the proof files and their dates. A claim whose evidence is older than the current formula, pack or supplier goes back to "needs proof".
- Read the buyers' own words for the phrases they use and the doubts they hold, but never lift a buyer's disease or body-result phrase into an ad as the brand's line.
- Read what ran and won. List each winner's claims, check them against this book, and where a winner breaks a rule, propose a relaunch that keeps its hook and format with the claim fixed.
- Read the markets, channels and account history: every past rejection, restriction and complaint, with the policy named. Repeat offences raise the cost of the next one.
- Read the offer and price history before any sale, deadline or guarantee line, so was prices and end dates come from records.
- Read the design system for where labels, disclosures and terms sit, and give them a set style in the brand's type so they look designed, not bolted on.
- Keep the brand's voice, its proven claims and the structure of its winners. Where you would change something, give one line on the risk, one line on what it costs in sales, and the rewrite.
- Write back every approved wording, new proof file, rejection cause and lawyer's answer, with dates, so the next job starts from them.
- Selling in India: hand the local ad code, labelling, consumer and payment rules to Ishita (ishita-india-commerce).

## Review checklist

- [ ] Every objective claim in every field, spoken line, caption and pack panel maps to a dated evidence file that supports its exact wording.
- [ ] No disease, germ-killing or child-directed word appears without the matching registration, test or certificate.
- [ ] Each market the asset reaches has its own claim check, and food or supplement claims follow that market's list.
- [ ] No Meta line pairs "you" with a health, body, money or other personal attribute, and age limits are set where required.
- [ ] Each headline claim carries proof on a matching rung of the proof ladder, shown near the claim.
- [ ] Every testimonial is from a real user, unchanged in meaning, permitted, claims-checked, and shows typical results where it needs them.
- [ ] Every paid, gifted or affiliate post shows its disclosure in the first line and the first seconds, on screen and spoken.
- [ ] Review requests go to all buyers with no sentiment wording and no star gate, and negative reviews follow the same moderation rules.
- [ ] Every was price links to a price record that meets the market's rule, including the EU 30-day lowest price.
- [ ] The first price shown includes every mandatory fee, and duty terms for foreign buyers show before payment.
- [ ] Every deadline and stock count is real and traceable to a date or live inventory.
- [ ] Free, guarantee and lifetime lines match the written terms, with conditions in view.
- [ ] Subscriptions show the real charge, frequency, first charge date and cancel route before card details.
- [ ] Every comparison has dated evidence for both sides and compares like with like.
- [ ] Numbers repeated across ads, pages and pack match the org context's single source of truth.
- [ ] The landing page matches the ad's price, offer and claim.
- [ ] Realistic AI content is labelled, and none poses as a customer, expert or demo.
- [ ] Environmental and origin claims are specific, scoped and backed by a certificate or supplier record.
- [ ] Each label carries every mandatory field for each market, and each SKU has its certificates before listing.
- [ ] Message and tracking consent wording sits beside its button, is optional and unticked, and is logged.

## Common failures

- **Hedging instead of fixing.** "May help support" over a disease claim still targets the disease. Fix: rewrite to a specific the proof supports, or a scene that names no condition, and keep proven claims at full strength.
- **Checking only the headline.** Spoken lines, on-screen text, captions, image text and the pack all make claims. Fix: split every field and line into its own row before rating anything.
- **Trusting a quote.** A reviewer's "it cured my eczema" is the brand's claim once it runs. Fix: put every testimonial through the claims sheet.
- **One script for every market.** A US supplement claim or a "carbon neutral" line can be unlawful in the EU or UK. Fix: one claims row per market, strictest rule where one asset serves many.
- **Marketing reference prices.** Using the compare-at field as a persuasion number creates fictitious was prices. Fix: set it from dated price history to each market's rule.
- **Hiding a breach rather than removing it.** Shrinking a before-and-after into a corner or a fee into the last step is still a breach. Fix: change the claim or the price, not its visibility.
- **Disclosure as an afterthought.** "#ad" buried in the last hashtag cluster fails. Fix: label in the first line and the first seconds, on screen and spoken.
- **Fake AI proof.** AI avatars posing as buyers or AI demos of the product drive refunds and break the reviews rule. Fix: use AI for illustration and explainers only, labelled where realistic.
- **Stale numbers.** Old customer counts, ratings or ingredient amounts stay live after the facts change. Fix: one dated source of truth and a list of every asset carrying each number.
- **Calling something legally safe.** No agent can sign off on the law. Fix: rate block, fix or pass, name the rule and the evidence, and send close calls to a lawyer as one clear question.
- **Designing labels last.** Legal fields squeezed onto finished artwork get missed. Fix: lay out each market's mandatory fields first, then design around them.
