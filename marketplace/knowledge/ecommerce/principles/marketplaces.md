# Marketplaces: craft principles

This book is for anyone who sells a brand's products on someone else's shelf: Amazon managers and Amazon ads specialists, the people who open Walmart, Etsy, eBay, Zalando or bol, and TikTok Shop managers. Read it before the job, write the listing, plan or change to it, and check the draft against the review checklist before hand-over.

## What great looks like

- Every SKU on every marketplace has a profit per unit after every fee, with the fee page and date it came from, and a floor price under which nothing is sold or matched.
- Every bid and target ACoS traces back to profit before ads and the real conversion rate, never to the marketplace's suggested bid or ROI.
- The weekly read splits ad sales from organic sales and shows TACoS and profit per order, so a reviewer can see whether ads are building the listing or renting sales.
- No ranked product runs out of stock, and slow stock has a dated plan to turn into cash.
- Account health metrics sit well inside the marketplace's lines, and every defect is traced to the orders behind it.
- Titles, bullets, images and backend terms pass the marketplace's written rules on the first submission and use the words buyers type and the complaints they write.
- Reviews come only through the marketplace's own routes, and rival reviews are read before the product or listing is changed.
- Each marketplace has its own P&L, and off-marketplace media gets credit for the sales it sends there.
- A new channel opens only when its profit per order, cash timing and legal set-up are worked out for the store's own SKUs.

## The principles

### 1. Work out profit per unit per SKU per marketplace before any listing or ad, and drop SKUs that cannot keep about 30%

**Why it works:** Fees stack on the price the buyer pays, shipping included, and click costs do not shrink with price. In one worked example a $100 item with a $30 factory cost looks like 70% margin, yet keeps 24% once shipping, pick and pack, payment fees, freight, tax and refunds come off. Every later choice (bid, discount, price match) needs that real floor.

**How to apply:** One row per SKU per channel: price, referral fee on the total including shipping, fulfilment, storage, returns at full cost, payment or closing fees, and landed cost. What is left is profit before ads. On Amazon US the referral fee is the greater of a category rate, commonly 15%, or a minimum, usually $0.30. One seller with two US accounts screens for at least 30% net after landed cost and fulfilment. Pull fee pages on the day and log URL and date.

**Judge it by:** Every fee line has a source and date, and each SKU has a floor price per channel. Weak work quotes "15% referral" and stops.

**Breaks when:** Consumables with proven repeat can launch below the floor if repeat buyers return through a channel the brand measures.

**From:** AMZ-6383, AMZ-0004, MEA-0037, MEA-6251, MEA-6276, Etsy fees policy https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/

### 2. Read page one before putting a SKU into a category, and walk away when the top price is under double the bottom

**Why it works:** When near-identical items sit in a narrow price band, buyers choose on price and review count, so a new entrant must undercut. A wide spread ($20, $50, $100 on one page) shows buyers pay for design, quality or brand, leaving room at a higher price. A rival with thousands of reviews cannot be caught.

**How to apply:** Search the SKU's main buyer phrase and two or three variants. Record page one's lowest and highest price; drop the category if the top is under about double the bottom (tongue scrapers at $7 to $13 fail). Look for beatable leaders: at most 300 reviews, listed 8 months or more (not a fad), 4 images or fewer. Note what the top-priced listings do differently and whether the brand can match it. Leaders at $5,000 to $20,000 a month is the band a new listing can usually fund (principle 20).

**Judge it by:** The sheet shows page-one low and high prices per phrase, the top rivals' review and image counts, and a go or walk-away call. Weak work lists a SKU because the category is big.

**Breaks when:** Buyers already search the brand's name, so page one matters less; gated or patent-heavy categories (baby, battery electronics) need checks first.

**From:** AMZ-0001, AMZ-0006, AMZ-0005, AMZ-0004

### 3. List the few SKUs that can win on a marketplace, and leave heavy, cheap or add-on items off it

**Why it works:** Each SKU on a marketplace needs its own ads, stock and reviews, so a full catalogue spreads budget and cover thin. Cheap items cannot carry click costs, and heavy, low-value goods lose money on fulfilment: one supplement brand gives its canned drink only 3.7% of its Meta ads and leaves it mostly to retail. Add-ons that sell only beside a main product cannot be advertised to strangers.

**How to apply:** Rank SKUs by profit per unit after the channel's fees, price (screen out items under about $25 that rely on paid clicks), size tier, return rate and review strength. Start with the few that clear every screen, and put shipping cost as a share of order value beside each. Sell add-ons as bundles. Prune with care: blanket cuts across a range have cut total sales elsewhere, so watch repeat rate on what you remove.

**Judge it by:** The list shows each SKU's profit, size tier and reason to list or skip. Weak work lists the whole catalogue on day one.

**Breaks when:** Consumables with strong repeat recover acquisition cost later; items selling on outside demand need few clicks.

**From:** ADC-6149, AMZ-0004, PRD-0012, PRD-0013, MEA-6400

### 4. Set every bid from profit: break-even click cost is profit before ads times conversion rate, and under dynamic bids up and down enter half

**Why it works:** A click is worth profit per unit times the chance it buys; at $1 a click, an $8 item needing two or three clicks per sale loses money. Amazon's up and down strategy can raise a bid by up to 100%, so the entered bid is not the ceiling.

**How to apply:** Break-even ACoS is profit before ads divided by price. Break-even cost per click is profit before ads times unit session percentage (last 30 days, by child ASIN). A $40 item with $14 profit before ads at 10% conversion gives a $1.40 break-even click and 35% break-even ACoS. Set target ACoS below that, except in a capped launch. Under up and down, enter about half ($0.70 here); or use down only, so the entered bid is the ceiling. Review bids about every two weeks, one change at a time.

**Judge it by:** Each bid traces to profit and a conversion rate. Weak work copies the suggested bid.

**Breaks when:** Top of search placement adjustments stack on top. A child ASIN with few sessions gives a noisy rate; use the parent's until it has traffic.

**From:** AMZ-0004, AMZ-6447, AMZ-6383

### 5. Run Amazon on TACoS and profit per order across ad and organic sales, and use ACoS only as the bid control

**Why it works:** Ad sales on a search term build the sales history that lifts organic rank on it, so some ad spend buys future organic sales. ACoS sees only the ad sale. TACoS, ad spend divided by total sales, shows whether ads build the listing or rent sales it would never get alone.

**How to apply:** Weekly, per ASIN, record total, ad and organic sales, ad spend, TACoS, organic rank on target terms and profit per order. In a launch, accept TACoS above break-even for a period and cap the owner signs off; after it, TACoS should fall as organic share grows. If TACoS rises while organic sales stay flat, fix the listing, price or reviews, not the bids. Test brand-term ads before funding them: one strategist calls them a defensive tax until rivals bid on the brand's name, and one gum brand switched Amazon ads off with no drop in sales. Pause them for a set period and compare.

**Judge it by:** The report splits ad and organic sales, shows profit per order, and the launch has an end date. Weak work celebrates a falling ACoS while total sales fall.

**Breaks when:** Outside demand moves organic sales for other reasons; a handful of orders a week is noise.

**From:** AMZ-0003, MEA-6279, MEA-6326, MEA-0029

### 6. Never let a ranked product run out: keep cover above reorder lead time plus a buffer, and tie each SKU's ad target to its days of stock

**Why it works:** Stock is a Featured Offer factor, and an out-of-stock product stops building the sales history its rank needs. Amazon also charges a low-inventory-level fee on standard-size FBA units shipped when days of supply fall under 28. Ads on a SKU about to run out buy units that would sell anyway.

**How to apply:** Reorder when cover falls to lead time plus receiving time plus a buffer, counting marketplace, inbound and warehouse stock separately, and keep at least 28 days of supply at FBA. When a SKU has fewer days of stock than its reorder lead time (one example: 30 days left, 80 to reorder), raise its target sharply or pause its ads. Grade slow stock by age and plan its route out.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows cover, lead time and reorder date per SKU, and ad targets move with stock. Weak work scales ads on a bestseller the week before it sells out.

**Breaks when:** The hero product's rank matters more than batch margin, so slow sell-through beats going dark.

**From:** ADC-0023, AMZ-6442, MEA-6277, AMZ-0005, Supply Chain Dive on the low-inventory-level fee https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/amazon-fba-low-inventory-level-fee-april-credit/712326/

### 7. Keep every account health metric well inside the marketplace's line, and fix the cause in the orders before writing any appeal

**Why it works:** Breaking selling rules can cost the account, Featured Offer eligibility and withheld funds. The windows are short, so a bad week crosses a line fast, and a metric recovers only as clean orders replace bad ones. Appeals that promise change without showing it rarely work.

**How to apply:** Know Amazon's lines: order defect rate under 1% (negative feedback, A-to-z claims, chargebacks, all fulfilment methods); for seller-fulfilled orders, late shipment under 4%, pre-fulfilment cancellation under 2.5% over 7 days, valid tracking above 95% over 30 days per category. Set alerts inside each line, not at it. When one nears its line, move those SKUs to FBA or lengthen handling time first, then fix the process. For an appeal, pull the orders behind each defect and state the cause, the fix made and how it stays fixed. A US store needs a reasonable basis for any ship time it promises.

**Judge it by:** Each defect links to order numbers and a dated fix. Weak work drafts an apology before finding the cause.

**Breaks when:** All orders ship through FBA, so the shipping metrics do not apply; a programme sets stricter targets.

**From:** AMZ-6443, AMZ-6444, CXP-6160

### 8. When the Featured Offer drops, diagnose it factor by factor before touching price, and never match below the floor

**Why it works:** Amazon picks the Featured Offer on item plus shipping price, delivery speed, free shipping, free returns, seller performance and stock; its staff say reviews, stars, the Prime badge and fulfilment method are not factors. Cutting price first gives away margin when the cause is often a slow delivery promise or a complaint. From July 2026 Amazon drops the separate eligibility gate, so more offers compete on these factors.

**How to apply:** Read Featured Offer percentage by ASIN in Business Reports. Then check in order: Pricing Health for a price that makes the offer ineligible, total price against rival offers, delivery promise and shipping settings, free returns, Voice of the Customer complaints, stock. Fix the first that fails. Only then consider price, and only above the floor. Amazon can also pull offers it judges unfairly priced against recent prices on or off Amazon, so a sudden jump costs the offer too. When an unauthorised seller undercuts the brand, use Brand Registry tools, not a price war.

**Judge it by:** The diagnosis names the failing factor and its evidence. Weak work drops price 10% and hopes.

**Breaks when:** Amazon changes the method; other marketplaces pick the winning offer on their own rules.

**From:** AMZ-6442, AMZ-6441, Amazon Marketplace Fair Pricing Policy (Seller Central help G5TUVJKZHUVMN77V)

### 9. Read the 1 and 2 star reviews, yours and your rivals', before spending more on ads, and fix what they point to

**Why it works:** Ads buy visits; the rating and its reasons decide whether visits convert. A product rated 4.1 to 4.3 that still makes over $5,000 a month proves demand and unmet needs, and its complaints are the cheapest spec for a better version. In one demo, a patio shade at about $5,000 a month had 12% of reviews at one star.

**How to apply:** Pull every 1 and 2 star review for your ASIN and the top three rivals. Group complaints: product faults (size, durability, missing parts), wrong expectations the listing created, and shipping damage. Product faults go to the supplier as a costed fix list before the next order. Wrong expectations get answered in the title, bullets and images. Damage goes to packaging. Long photo and video reviews also show who else buys; note self-describing phrases ("as a nurse") that repeat.

**Judge it by:** The work shows complaint groups with counts and the change that answers each. Weak work raises budget on a listing whose rating is falling.

**Breaks when:** Complaints come from one bad seller, not the product type, or the fix costs more than the margin allows.

**From:** AMZ-0002, AVT-0013, AVT-0016, AVT-0011

### 10. Launch on the long tail: win page one on many cheap descriptive terms first, and reach for the head term once their sales lift rank

**Why it works:** Shoppers describe one item many ways (slim, waterproof, wallet, for men), and each phrase is its own auction. A new listing cannot win the main term early, but it can rank on many small ones, whose combined sales later lift rank on the main term. One- or two-word products have no side doors.

**How to apply:** List descriptive variants with real search volume; favour products with dozens. Build four Sponsored Products campaigns: automatic for discovery (close match, loose match, substitutes, complements, each bid separately), exact on the long-tail set, phrase for discovery, and product targeting on weaker rival ASINs (fewer images, lower rating, fewer reviews). Bid from principle 4. Track organic rank on each long-tail term weekly, and add the head term in exact once several long-tail terms hold page one.

**Judge it by:** The launch sheet lists long-tail terms with bids and ranks to watch. Weak work opens with one broad campaign on the head term.

**Breaks when:** Long-tail terms have almost no volume, the product sells mainly on brand search, or variants need products you do not carry.

**From:** AMZ-0003, AMZ-0006, Amazon Ads targeting guide https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/targeting-with-sponsored-products

### 11. Harvest search terms on a fixed cycle: promote terms that convert to exact, and negate a term only after enough clicks to prove it will not

**Why it works:** Discovery finds buying phrases you did not guess, then keeps paying for ones that never buy. Negate early and you cut future sales; late, and you burn margin. Amazon's guide says judge a keyword after at least 20 clicks and let negatives run two weeks or more before changing course.

**How to apply:** Every week or two, move terms with two or more orders at or under target ACoS into the exact campaign, and add each as negative exact where it came from so the two do not compete. Negate a term with no orders once its clicks pass the larger of 20 or about twice the clicks one sale needs (at 5% conversion, 40 clicks). Negate plainly wrong terms on sight, such as other products or "cheap" for a premium item. Use negative phrase with care: it blocks every query containing the phrase.

**Judge it by:** Each negative and promotion shows clicks, orders and ACoS. Weak work negates after a week, or never.

**Breaks when:** Seasonal terms convert only in season; in low-traffic accounts, judge related terms as a group.

**From:** AMZ-0004, AMZ-6447, Amazon Ads targeting guide https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/targeting-with-sponsored-products

### 12. Write the title from the words buyers type, in the order they decide, inside the marketplace's title rules

**Why it works:** The title is the main ranking text and the first thing a phone shopper reads, so it must match the query and settle the first question: is this the right thing, in my size or count. Since January 2025 most Amazon titles may not exceed 200 characters with spaces, use ! $ ? _ { } ^ ¬ ¦ unless in the brand, or repeat a word more than twice.

**How to apply:** Take the top buyer phrases from the search term report or brand analytics, not the internal product name. Order: brand, product type in the buyer's word, the one difference that wins the click, then size, count, colour or fit. Write from real attributes, count characters, scan for banned characters and triple words, and check the stricter category style guide; brand owners get 14 days to fix flagged titles. A UK kitchen brand leads with "non-stick frying pan" before its range name.

**Judge it by:** The main buyer phrase comes early and the title passes every rule. Weak work stuffs keywords.

**Breaks when:** A category sets a shorter limit; Walmart, Etsy and others have their own rules.

**From:** AMZ-6160, AMZ-0003

### 13. Make the main image pass the marketplace's rules and win the click, and give every other image one buying question to answer

**Why it works:** On a results page the main image is often all a shopper sees beside price and stars, and a non-compliant one can get the listing suppressed. Inside the listing, images do what bullets cannot on a phone: scale, use, contents and proof the common complaint is solved. Rivals with four images or fewer leave a gap.

**How to apply:** Amazon main image: pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255), product filling about 85% of the frame, at least 1,000 pixels on the longest side for zoom, no added text, graphics or watermarks. Then take the top five buying questions from reviews and rival listings and give each one image: size against a hand, the product in use, what is in the box, the fixed complaint, and how it compares within the brand's range. Style those images in the brand's design system.

**Judge it by:** Each secondary image answers a named question from the review read. Weak work repeats one product shot.

**Breaks when:** A category has its own image rules (clothing, media); Amazon updates its standards, so check before shooting.

**From:** AMZ-0006, AMZ-0002, AVT-0016, Amazon staff post on main images https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/13af96ea-6b07-4bf9-8dbe-a13292c2e3b1

### 14. Fill the backend search terms with buyer words the visible copy does not carry, and count bytes, not characters

**Why it works:** Search terms are indexed but unseen, so they hold the synonyms that would make the title read badly. Amazon's limit is 249 bytes, and going over can make it ignore the whole field, so one extra word can switch off every word in it.

**How to apply:** Gather buyer words from the search term report, rival titles and reviews that are not already in your title and bullets: synonyms, alternate names, uses and situations. Write them in lower case, single spaces, no commas, no repeats. Leave out other brands' names (Amazon's too), temporary words such as "new" or "on sale", and subjective words such as "best". Count bytes with a byte counter: accented letters and non-Latin scripts take more than one byte each, which matters on Amazon.de, .fr or .co.jp. Stop at or under 249.

**Judge it by:** The field holds only new buyer words, with a byte count shown. Weak work pastes the title again, adds rival brands, or runs to 300 characters.

**Breaks when:** A marketplace or category sets another limit; check the keyword attributes help page for that store.

**From:** AMZ-6445

### 15. Plan A+ content around the questions that stop the sale, compare only the brand's own products, and check every line against the banned list

**Why it works:** A+ content sits below the fold where serious buyers settle doubts, so it earns its place by answering objections and steering buyers to the right item. Content that breaks Amazon's rules fails validation and never goes live.

**How to apply:** Basic A+ needs a Professional plan and Brand Registry: up to five modules per ASIN (Premium up to seven). Plan one module per objection from the review read: how it works, how to choose a size, what it is made of, care and use. Use a comparison chart of the brand's own range so buyers pick the right one, which also cuts wrong-fit returns. Never include "best-selling" or "top-rated" claims, prices or promotions, shipping, guarantee or warranty text, links or QR codes, contact details or any rival. Awards name the body and year. Images stay under 2 MB at module sizes.

**Judge it by:** Each module answers a named objection and the draft was checked against the banned list. Weak work restates the bullets beside lifestyle photos.

**Breaks when:** The marketplace or category has its own A+ rules, or Premium criteria change.

**From:** AMZ-6446

### 16. Get the first reviews only through the marketplace's own routes: Vine once the product is final, and Request a Review on every order

**Why it works:** Getting caught buying reviews costs the listing or the account. The FTC's 2024 rule (16 CFR Part 465) bans fake reviews, AI-written ones included, and paying for a positive or negative sentiment. Amazon bans incentives, requests in packaging and asking buyers to change reviews.

**How to apply:** Enrol in Vine only when product, listing and stock are final: up to 30 units go to experienced reviewers who report every flaw. It needs fewer than 30 reviews, FBA and, for a brand, Brand Registry. The fee per parent is 0 for up to 2 units, $75 for 3 to 10 and $200 for 11 to 30, charged only after a first review. Count units as launch cost. Send Request a Review (5 to 30 days after delivery) on every eligible order, not a chosen few.

**Judge it by:** Only Vine and the request button are used, and Vine units sit in the launch budget. Weak work suggests inserts asking for five stars.

**Breaks when:** The product is adult, digital or a bundle, has 30 or more reviews, or ships seller-fulfilled; other marketplaces set their own rules.

**From:** AMZ-6440, AMZ-6448, MCR-6045, CXP-6301

### 17. File the trademark in the exact spelling on the pack, enrol in Brand Registry on the application number, and claim the brand's listings before resellers do

**Why it works:** Brand Registry unlocks A+ content, a Brand Store, Sponsored Brands, Brand Analytics, Vine and tools to remove copycats. Without it, whoever lists first controls the detail page. Outside demand turns into branded Amazon searches anyway: one deodorant brand held its category's number one best seller badge before spending on Amazon ads.

**How to apply:** Run a clearance search first: the official trademark database, business registers and unregistered use found online. File a word mark, or a design mark with letters, in a country with an Amazon store, in the exact spelling on the product, and put the logo with the name permanently on product or pack. Enrol with the application number rather than waiting for registration. Then claim and fix every existing listing of the brand.

**Judge it by:** The plan names the mark, office, number and date, and lists ASINs to claim. Weak work leaves resellers' listings in place while running ads to them.

**Breaks when:** The seller is a reseller, not the owner; the mark is a pure image; or the brand on the pack differs from the filed mark.

**From:** AMZ-6441, BRD-6485, AMZ-0007

### 18. Hold one price for the same product across your site, marketplaces and key retailers, and change it as a dated event, never as a split test

**Why it works:** Buyers compare channels, retailers watch marketplace prices, and Amazon can pull offers it judges unfairly priced. Holding the Amazon price level with the main retailer protects that relationship and lets buyers choose a channel for speed, not price. Two prices for one item at once break trust: Amazon's 2000 DVD price test ended in refunds to 6,896 customers.

**How to apply:** Set one list price per SKU per currency and a floor no channel goes below. Run discounts as dated events, logged for all channels. To learn what buyers will pay, test bundles, pack sizes and framing, not two prices on one item. In a new country, price to that market rather than converting: one Australian fashion brand kept 70 AUD as 70 USD in the US and took the margin.

**Judge it by:** The price sheet shows one list price across channels, a floor and a dated event log. Weak work lets a repricer chase rivals below the site price.

**Breaks when:** Channels sell different packs, so prices are not comparable; local rivals are clearly cheaper; tariffs and shipping eat the gain.

**From:** AMZ-0007, CRO-0009, OFF-6671, AMZ-6442

### 19. Give each marketplace its own P&L, and credit it with the sales your other media sends there

**Why it works:** A marketplace has its own fees, returns and payment timing, so its profit cannot be read from a blended number. The same media also sells where the site cannot track: one portfolio estimate puts about 20% of Meta's created value on Amazon and roughly half of YouTube's. A brand judging Meta on site sales alone sees it worsen as Amazon grows, and may cut it.

**How to apply:** Build a P&L per channel (site, each marketplace, wholesale, retail) down to contribution after ads, with payment terms beside it; in one example wholesale earned 10% contribution on 60-day terms against the site's 22.5% paid in days. Track Amazon branded search against outside spend. Tag outside traffic to Amazon with Amazon Attribution: brand-registered sellers can earn the Brand Referral Bonus, a referral fee credit averaging about 10% of those sales.

**Judge it by:** The report shows each channel's contribution and the spend feeding it. Weak work judges site ads on site sales alone.

**Breaks when:** Rivals bid on the brand's terms, so marketplace ads are more incremental; the split is a portfolio average, so test your own.

**From:** MEA-6274, MEA-0029, AMZ-0007, Seller Labs on the Brand Referral Bonus https://www.sellerlabs.com/knowledge-base/brand-referral-bonus-earning-credit-for-the-traffic-you-send-to-amazon/

### 20. Size each launch to cash: fund about three months of stock plus the launch ads before the first unit sells

**Why it works:** On FBA, stock is bought, shipped and received before it sells, and payouts come after sales. Matching a rival making $30,000 a month means funding about three months of stock plus the ads to reach that rate, which most new sellers cannot. Profit is not cash: a brand showing $2M profit with $3M of unsold stock is about $1M short.

**How to apply:** Take the monthly units you aim for, multiply by landed cost and three months of cover, then add the capped launch ad budget and Vine units. If that is beyond the cash the owner can put at risk, lower the target: start against rivals making $5,000 to $20,000 a month and raise the band as cash allows. Work out the cash conversion cycle (inventory days plus receivable days minus payable days) for the channel, and agree supplier terms before ordering.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows the cash needed, when it goes out and when it returns. Weak work plans ad spend with no stock line.

**Breaks when:** The seller has financing; fast-turning products with short restock times need less cover.

**From:** AMZ-0005, MEA-6271, MEA-6312, MEA-6275

### 21. Pick the next marketplace on profit per order for the store's own SKUs and on buyer fit, and open one at a time with a pass line

**Why it works:** Audience size is not profit, and opening several marketplaces at once splits attention. Some large brands stay almost wholly on their own site (one sells 98% there and 2% on Amazon) because focus beats reach at their stage.

**How to apply:** For three to six candidates, work out profit per order on the store's top SKUs after commission, fixed, payment, shipping and return fees, from fee pages pulled on the day. Walmart Marketplace charges no set-up or monthly fee and takes a referral fee by category; Etsy charges listing and transaction fees and, for shops it enrols, an offsite ads fee. Score buyer fit, cash timing, effort and price risk. Pick one, set a pass line in profit per order and units with a decision date, and decide on that date, not on inbound offers.

**Judge it by:** The ranking shows dated profit per order per marketplace, one pick, a runner-up and a pass line. Weak work says "all major marketplaces".

**Breaks when:** Buyers already ask for the brand on a marketplace, so a minimal presence protects demand.

**From:** PRD-0017, PRD-0012, MEA-6274, Walmart Marketplace pricing https://marketplace.walmart.com/pricing

### 22. Count returns at their full cost on each channel, and budget high-return categories at up to double the home rate in a new market

**Why it works:** The returns line understates the real cost. On a $150 order one example showed about $22, but adding the outbound shipping already paid and the unrefunded payment fee made it about $34. Marketplace buyers return easily, and in fashion US return rates have run at double those of some other markets.

**How to apply:** For each SKU and channel, cost a return as the return label if you pay it, outbound shipping, unrefunded fees, handling, and the share of returned units that cannot be resold. Multiply by the return rate and carry it into profit per unit (principle 1). In a new country, model double the home return rate and be glad if it comes in lower. Read the top return reasons per SKU and fix the size guide, images or claim behind them. US buyers expect delivery in about two days; if you cannot meet that, state real times.

**Judge it by:** Each SKU's profit carries a returns line built from parts. Weak work uses the refund total as the whole cost.

**Breaks when:** Low-return categories such as consumables; no-return or store-credit policies.

**From:** MEA-6276, MEA-6266, CXP-6609

### 23. On TikTok Shop, rank videos, creators and products on revenue per 1,000 views and profit after commission, not on views

**Why it works:** Views measure reach, not buying intent. Across seven top TikTok Shop videos, sales per 1,000 views varied more than 100 times: a $29 to $39 drink made about $385 per 1,000 views on 78,000 views, while a 23 million view reaction video for a $14 nasal stick made about $1.65. Cheap impulse buyers, such as $20 TikTok Shop orders, also come back to buy again far less often than full-price buyers spending $200.

**How to apply:** For each video, creator and product, record views, orders, revenue, commission, samples and fees, then revenue per 1,000 views and profit per order after commission. Rank on those. Match format to product: reactions suit an instant felt sensation (taste, cooling, smell); demonstrations suit a visible mechanism. Feed top earners into paid promotion and brief more creators on their angle. Check TikTok Shop buyers' repeat rate before counting their volume as lifetime value.

**Judge it by:** The weekly read ranks on revenue per 1,000 views and profit, with commission shown. Weak work reports total views and follower growth.

**Breaks when:** Paid and organic views mix, so the ratio partly measures ad spend; one video is too small a sample to judge a format.

**From:** MCR-0046, MCR-0045, CXP-0016

### 24. In TikTok's GMV Max, set the ROI target from your break-even, not the suggestion, and switch off the promotion-day boost unless you chose it

**Why it works:** GMV Max promotes the shop automatically with your own and affiliate videos. TikTok often suggests an ROI target around 1.2, below break-even for many stores once fees and commissions count. Left at default, its special promotion days setting can lift spend up to ten times on listed days.

**How to apply:** Break-even ROI per product is 1 divided by profit before ads as a share of price, after referral fee, commission and fulfilment. Set the target at or above it; one practitioner adds a full point to the suggestion (1.2 becomes 2.2) and lowers it if impressions stall for a few days. Start with the top 20% of products by orders in one campaign, since a product sits in only one GMV Max campaign. Set daily budget at about twice target cost per order, and turn off unplanned promotion-day boosts.

**Judge it by:** The set-up shows break-even ROI, target and boost setting. Weak work accepts the defaults.

**Breaks when:** Margins are so thin that 2.2 still loses money; no affiliate videos feed the campaign; TikTok changes defaults often, so check at launch.

**From:** ADC-0062

### 25. Build the TikTok Shop affiliate base from many small creators on a floor-or-commission deal, with disclosure written into every brief

**Why it works:** Small creators who match the buyer post more and cost less than big influencers, and TikTok Shop's direct commission hooks a creator once a video takes off. Seeding at scale works because winners are rare: one programme sent 100,000 invites in 60 days, onboarded about 6,000 creators (about 6% of invites) and got about 5,800 videos.

**How to apply:** Recruit creators often under 10,000 followers, and keen customers. One brand's starter deal: $1,000 a month or 15 to 20% commission, whichever is higher, for one video a day for 30 days on the hero product; once commission passes the floor, go commission only. Aim for two or three videos per sample and cost samples at landed cost. Every brief requires plain disclosure such as "ad" in the video itself, since free product counts, and the brand checks it.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows the deal, sample cost per video, profit after commission and disclosure rules. Weak work pays flat fees to a few big names.

**Breaks when:** Products do not demo on video; margins cannot carry 15 to 20% commission; regulated claims need controlled scripts.

**From:** ORG-0036, ORG-0009, ORG-6405, CXP-6303

### 26. Start live selling with short, frequent shows, and use $1 anchor lots only when the audience is already there

**Why it works:** New sellers who promote one long show for weeks often sell five to ten items and quit, while several short shows find buyers through in-app discovery. Low-start auctions on known, valuable items bring people in and margin comes from the rest, but only when enough viewers bid against each other.

**How to apply:** With no audience, run many short same-day shows (four 15-minute shows beat one hour) with few items. Once you have followers, schedule shows one to three weeks out and promote them about a week, two or three days and a few hours before. Start anchor lots at $1 only when you expect 300 to 500 or more viewers at once; one 250-lot show used about 20% anchors. A buyers-only giveaway breaks even when it lifts the average sale by prize cost divided by lots.

**Judge it by:** The run sheet shows length, lot mix, anchor rule and break-even sum. Weak work plans one two-hour launch show with $1 starts and no audience.

**Breaks when:** A full-price brand cannot survive auction discounts or must protect its price; sell at fixed prices with a live-only bundle instead.

**From:** ORG-0005, ORG-0008, ORG-0007, ORG-0006

### 27. Clear each new country's product safety, VAT and duty rules before the first listing goes live

**Why it works:** Marketplaces enforce local law and often collect tax, so a missing safety contact or VAT set-up blocks goods or removes listings, and low-value import rules are changing fast.

**How to apply:** For the EU, appoint a responsible economic operator in the EU or Northern Ireland under the GPSR, label its details, the maker and a batch number, and carry safety information on each listing. Since 1 July 2026 EU parcels worth €150 or less pay €3 duty per item (goods sharing one tariff code): 7.5% of a €40 single-SKU order but 2.5% of a €120 bundle, so bundles cut it. IOSS collects VAT at checkout up to €150; the Council plans to lift that cap and apply normal duty from 1 July 2028, pending adoption. UK consignments of £135 or less pay no duty today, with VAT charged by seller or marketplace, until March 2029 at the latest. Put each into landed cost. For India's marketplaces, hand the job to Ishita (ishita-india-commerce).

**Judge it by:** The launch file names the operator, VAT route and duty per unit per country. Weak work lists in the EU and finds out at customs.

**Breaks when:** The brand has an EU importer entity or imports in bulk; dates may move.

**From:** COG-6369, OPS-6658, FIN-6438, OPS-6631

## Working from the org context

- Read the buyers' own words first: review phrases, survey answers and chat questions become title words, bullet openings and image captions, in the brand's reading level.
- Take product facts and approved claims word for word from the org context. A marketplace listing that overstates a claim gets suppressed, and it also breaks the brand's own rules.
- Keep the brand's name, product names and voice the same on every marketplace, so a buyer who saw the brand on social or in a shop finds it on Amazon at once.
- Use the design system for every secondary image and A+ module: fonts, colours and photo style. The main image follows the marketplace's rules, not the brand's styling.
- Read the offers the brand runs on its own site before setting marketplace prices, and keep list price and event dates aligned with the site and key retailers.
- Read what ran and won: the ad angles, hooks and creators that worked off the marketplace are the first briefs for Sponsored Brands video, TikTok Shop affiliates and live shows.
- Read the return and complaint reasons the brand already logs; each top reason gets an answer in a bullet, an image or the size chart.
- Where the brand does something the marketplace punishes (a keyword-stuffed title, review requests in the pack, a price below its site), keep the brand's intent, show the rule and the cost, and propose the compliant version as a draft for the owner's yes.
- Write back to the org context what the marketplace taught: buyer phrases from search term reports, new complaint themes, and the price and offer that held the Featured Offer.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Does every SKU have a profit per unit for this channel, with each fee's source page and date?
- [ ] Before a SKU is listed, does the work show page one's price spread and the top rivals' review and image counts, with a go or walk-away call?
- [ ] Does every bid or target trace to profit before ads and a real conversion rate, with the dynamic bidding setting stated?
- [ ] Does the weekly read split ad and organic sales and show TACoS and profit per order?
- [ ] Does every advertised SKU have days of cover above its reorder lead time, and do ad targets change when it does not?
- [ ] Is every account health metric checked against the marketplace's line, with defects traced to orders?
- [ ] If the Featured Offer is down, is the failing factor named before any price change, and is the price above the floor?
- [ ] Were the 1 and 2 star reviews of this ASIN and its top rivals read, grouped and answered in the product, listing or pack?
- [ ] Does the launch start on long-tail terms with a capped spend and an end date?
- [ ] Is each negative and each promotion to exact backed by its clicks, orders and ACoS?
- [ ] Does the title lead with the buyer's main phrase and pass the character, symbol and repeat rules?
- [ ] Is the backend search terms field new words only, with a byte count at or under the limit?
- [ ] Does the main image meet the rules, and does each other image answer one named buying question?
- [ ] Is every A+ line checked against the banned list, with comparisons only within the brand's range?
- [ ] Do reviews come only from Vine and the request button, with no incentive, insert or sentiment ask?
- [ ] Is the list price the same as on the site and key retailers, with discounts logged as dated events?
- [ ] Does the marketplace have its own P&L, with outside media's halo and attribution tags noted?
- [ ] For TikTok Shop, are videos and creators ranked on revenue per 1,000 views and profit after commission, with disclosure in every brief?
- [ ] For a new country, are the safety operator, VAT route and duty per unit in place and in landed cost?

## Common failures

- Using ACoS against product margin as the target. Fix: take every fee, return and storage line into profit before ads, then set break-even ACoS and TACoS from it.
- Entering a category because it is big, when page one is a $7 to $13 price war. Fix: drop it unless the top price is about double the bottom and the leaders are beatable on reviews and images.
- Copying the suggested bid, or the suggested GMV Max ROI. Fix: work bids and ROI targets back from profit and conversion rate, and state the bidding strategy.
- Scaling ads on a bestseller just before it stocks out. Fix: check days of cover against lead time before every budget change and pause or raise targets when cover is short.
- Cutting price the moment the Featured Offer drops. Fix: check pricing health, delivery promise, complaints and stock first, and never go below the floor.
- Keyword-stuffed titles and backend fields that break the repeat or byte limits, and images that repeat one product shot. Fix: write from real attributes, count characters and bytes, move extra words to the backend, and give each image one buying question from the review read.
- Review shortcuts: inserts asking for five stars, free product for reviews, asking unhappy buyers to edit. Fix: Vine when the product is final and the request button on every order.
- Listing the whole catalogue on every marketplace on day one. Fix: start with the few SKUs that clear the profit floor and carry click costs, and add bundles rather than stand-alone add-ons.
- Judging a marketplace or a social channel on its own tracked sales only. Fix: run a P&L per channel and credit the halo with branded search and attribution tags.
- Reporting TikTok Shop views and follower growth as success. Fix: rank on revenue per 1,000 views and profit after commission and samples.
- Writing an account health appeal before finding the cause. Fix: pull the orders behind each defect, fix the process, then write cause, action and prevention.
- Opening a new country on the marketplace before the legal set-up. Fix: appoint the safety operator, choose the VAT route and add duty to landed cost first.
