# Media buying: craft principles

This book is for anyone who runs paid budgets for a store: Meta, Google, TikTok, Snapchat, Pinterest and Amazon buyers, and the lead who splits money across them. Read it before the job, write the plan or change to it, and check the draft against the review checklist before hand-over.

## What great looks like

- Every target (ROAS, CPA, ACOS, CAC) is worked back from delivered gross profit on a new customer's order, with the cost lines shown.
- The headline number is new-customer profit, read as acquisition MER and new-customer CAC from store orders, not platform ROAS.
- Each spend increase is judged on what the next dollar returned, not on the account's average.
- Spend on existing customers and retargeting is set in money from a frequency target, and is small.
- The account has as few campaigns as the business allows, and each extra one has a written reason a reviewer can check.
- Creative tests are funded to reach a decision, read at ad set level, and called on store profit.
- Budget, stock and cash agree: no winner is pushed into a stock-out, and slow stock has a planned route out.
- Every claim that a channel works rests on two reads that agree, and big moves wait for a holdout.
- Changes are few, sized against the gap, and logged with a date so the next read can tell what worked.

## The principles

### 1. Set every target from delivered gross profit on a new customer's first order, then scale inside a 2 to 3 lifetime gross profit to CAC band

**Why it works:** Your bid is your unit economics: a rival with more profit per order wins auctions with weaker ads. Targets built on product margin or store-wide order value overstate what a new buyer is worth.

**How to apply:** Build margin to the door: product, inbound freight, shipping, pick and pack, payment fees and a returns allowance. One store's 70% product margin was 24% once those landed. Use new-customer order value and margin. Break-even ROAS is 1 divided by gross margin; break-even CAC is first-order gross profit. Target CAC is time-bound lifetime gross profit over 2 ($80 of 30-day gross profit gives $40). Read the ratio monthly: under 1 loses, 1 to 2 hold, 2 to 3 scale, above 3 underspends.

**Judge it by:** The plan names the cost lines, the new-customer basis, the window and the break-even line. Weak work quotes "3x ROAS" with no margin.

**Breaks when:** Consumable and subscription brands with proven payback can buy below first-order break-even, but most lack the cohort modelling to do it safely. High-return categories must take returns out of CAC first.

**From:** ADC-0057, MEA-0023, MEA-0037, MEA-6260, OFF-6633, MEA-6371, MEA-6345, MEA-6353, MEA-6269, MEA-6383

### 2. Run paid media on acquisition MER and new-customer CAC from store orders, never on platform ROAS or blended MER

**Why it works:** Blended MER mixes in repeat revenue that ads did not buy, so it can rise while new-customer efficiency falls: one brand went 4x, 5x, 4x blended while acquisition MER slid 2.0, 1.8, 1.6. Platform ROAS adds view-throughs and existing buyers on top.

**How to apply:** Acquisition MER is new-customer revenue divided by total ad spend, taken from a first-order-only P&L. CAC is total ad spend divided by new customers; dividing by all orders gives a blended CPA that looks cheaper. Make new-customer profit contribution the headline, with acquisition MER, new-customer orders and CAC under it. Track CAC beside first-order gross profit monthly: a flat CAC can hide a swing from $70 to $40 per order. Where volume allows, optimise on a first-purchase event or Google's new customer goal.

**Judge it by:** Every verdict cites a store-side number for new customers. Weak work reports "ROAS up 20%" and stops.

**Breaks when:** Omnichannel brands must remove store buyers from new online customers, or CAC reads low. Once several channels win new buyers at scale, no single platform owns the acquisition MER and lift tests must split credit.

**From:** MEA-6235, MEA-0031, MEA-6344, MEA-6294, MEA-6241, MEA-6332, MEA-6343, MEA-6389, MEA-6234, MEA-6330

### 3. Fix the data the account learns from before touching structure, and fix structure before chasing creative

**Why it works:** Platforms bid on what they see. If Meta sees 70 of 100 sales, a 3% conversion rate reads as 2%, which lowers auction value and raises costs. Broken data was the main bottleneck in about 30% of one agency's 1,000+ audits.

**How to apply:** Fix data integrity, then structure, then creative, which matters most but takes longest. Check platform purchases against store orders for the same window. On Google, use one primary purchase action from the native tag; GA4 imports miss 10 to 15%, and two primaries double count. Send real order value, ideally margin-adjusted. Check that the email platform's customer list still syncs to Meta, or existing buyers count as new. Do not expect a paid server-side tool to fix much: measured recovery is often too small to see.

**Judge it by:** The audit opens with a tracking check against store orders, with the gap stated in percent. Weak work rebuilds campaigns on numbers it never checked.

**Breaks when:** An agency or buyer is handed only platform data; then the data pillar cannot be checked, so say so and do not audit on it.

**From:** ADC-6171, MEA-6303, ADC-0028, GAD-6150, MEA-0017, MEA-0016, ADC-6227, MEA-6285, MEA-0001, MEA-6342

### 4. Read Meta on 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view, drop 1-day view, and never compare ROAS across platforms

**Why it works:** In one agency's audits 30 to 50% of reported Meta ROAS came from 1-day view-throughs. Each platform credits itself on its own windows, so the same sale is counted several times. Pinterest can claim a sale when any of 6 to 12 tiles on screen was your ad.

**How to apply:** Set Meta reporting to 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view. Meta moved likes, comments and shares out of click credit in 2026, so click-only ROAS fell overnight; keep engaged view in the read. Know the defaults: Pinterest 30-day click and 1-day view, Snapchat 28-day swipe, Reddit 28-day click. Switch TikTok and Pinterest reads to 7-day click, where they usually fall to acquisition MER or below. Compare channels only on store-side new-customer cost, never on each other's ROAS.

**Judge it by:** Every channel row names its window, and cross-channel ranking uses one store-side basis. Weak work ranks "Pinterest 7x beats Meta 2.5x".

**Breaks when:** Long consideration products may need longer click windows to see value. Platform settings change: recheck the definition of engaged view before each quarter's plan.

**From:** ADC-6154, ADC-6152, ADC-6151, MEA-6100, MEA-6101, MEA-6102, MEA-6348

### 5. Scale on the marginal return of the next dollar, and stop a channel when its marginal acquisition MER reaches break-even

**Why it works:** Returns fall as spend rises; the average hides it. In one account, 25% more spend moved average CAC from $337 to $392, inside a $400 limit, while the extra 76 customers cost about $1,050 each.

**How to apply:** For each step up, divide the extra new-customer revenue by the extra spend. Move money in fixed steps, such as $10,000 a month, to whichever channel returns most on the next step, and stop when its marginal acquisition MER hits break-even. Add a new channel only once the main ones sit at that frontier. To forecast, plot a year of daily acquisition MER against spend, drop sale days and very low spend days, and fit a curve. Accept a lower ROAS when net cash grows: 5x at 100 times the spend can beat 10x.

**Judge it by:** Scale proposals show the step, the extra spend, the extra new customers and the marginal cost. Weak work says "ROAS held at 3x, so double it".

**Breaks when:** Peak weeks with heavy delayed conversion need longer reads. Small accounts with daily swings need at least 7 days per step.

**From:** ADC-0049, MEA-6279, MEA-6289, ADC-6160, MEA-6264, ADC-0034, ADC-0064

### 6. Exclude existing customers from cold campaigns and budget any spend on them in money, from a 3 to 7 monthly frequency

**Why it works:** Repeat buyers clicking a reminder ad would usually have reordered anyway, so they inflate ROAS even on click-only reads. One 12.84 ROAS ad fell to about 5 on new buyers and 1.71 on incremental credit. One practitioner finds this overspend in about 90% of accounts.

**How to apply:** Exclude purchasers of about the last 2 years from cold campaigns. In Advantage+ sales campaigns, cap existing-customer spend with two ad sets: one for them with a daily spend cap, one excluding them. Outside frequent-drop fashion, default near 0% of paid to existing customers. Where you do spend, size it as customers x monthly frequency x CPM / 1,000: 300,000 customers at 3 a month and a $2.50 CPM is about $2,250. Aim for 3 to 6 a month; above 8 is overspend. Hold its CPA to half the new-customer CPA or lower.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows the audience definitions, the exclusions and the frequency maths. Weak work sets "10% to retargeting".

**Breaks when:** Fashion with drops every 1 to 3 weeks is the exception: holdouts found existing-customer spend incremental there. Audits often find 20 to 100 views a month; cut, then test.

**From:** MEA-6297, ADC-0024, MEA-6330, ADC-0025, ADC-6080, ADC-6187, ADC-6190, ADC-6273, ADC-6280, ADC-0042, ADC-6178

### 7. Keep dynamic product ads and retargeting as a capped support layer, because a 2x cold campaign usually beats a 7x retargeting one

**Why it works:** Retargeting and catalogue ads mostly reach people already close to buying, so their ROAS looks high and is rarely incremental. Excluding existing customers and using incremental credit can halve DPA ROAS, from 5 to 2.5. Six fashion brands that moved about 80% of spend into DPAs declined for six months.

**How to apply:** Cap DPAs well below the 70% of spend auditors often find. Merge cart, checkout and visitor audiences into one retargeting ad set and steer it by frequency, not ROAS. Most accounts need no separate visitor retargeting, because cold campaigns already reach engaged visitors. Read retargeting on 7-day click: one furniture brand had 57% of budget in a DPA ad set showing 7x that was 1.9x on 7-day click. Aim 70 to 90% of effort at cold buyers.

**Judge it by:** Retargeting and DPA shares are stated with 7-day click reads. Weak work moves 30% of budget down the funnel because "it has the best ROAS".

**Breaks when:** Cold DPAs can work up to about $1,000 a day, not far beyond. Long-journey goods may justify a 90-day visitor ad set.

**From:** ADC-6196, ADC-6192, ADC-0026, ADC-6214, ADC-6178, ADC-6201, ADC-6181, ADC-6175, ADC-6198

### 8. Recompute break-even ROAS for every discount depth before a sale, and cap promo spend so evergreen keeps running

**Why it works:** A $100 item with $60 of costs breaks even at 2.5 ROAS; at 30% off it needs about 7. The ROAS needed to keep a fixed profit per order rises steeply: 15% off might need 8x where 25% needs 20x.

**How to apply:** For each offer, rebuild gross profit per order with the discount, any units-per-order lift and fixed costs per order, then set new break-even and target ROAS. One store needed 4.5 instead of 2 at 40% off. Even when 25% off lifted units per order from 1 to 1.63, ROAS still had to rise from 6 to about 9. Stacked offers can cut it: a 90-day supply took required ROAS from 6 to 2.9. Where a sale nearly always runs, cap promo campaigns at about 20 to 30% of budget.

**Judge it by:** Each offer has its own break-even line, and targets change the day it starts. Weak work keeps last month's ROAS target through a 30% sale.

**Breaks when:** Clearing slow stock can justify break-even on purpose. Discount-led buyers can carry worse lifetime value, so cohort them by code before scaling.

**From:** OFF-6633, OFF-6632, MEA-6361, OFF-6642, OFF-6630, ADC-6284, MEA-6365

### 9. Start from one cold campaign and add a campaign only for a business reason a reviewer can defend, not for spend

**Why it works:** Each campaign learns on its own data. Split 100 purchases a month over four campaigns and each learns on 25; accounts often improve once a campaign passes 100 to 200 a month. On Google, merging Shopping campaigns at 50, 20 and 10 conversions into one with 90 usually lifts ROAS.

**How to apply:** Write a reason for every campaign beyond the first. Valid reasons: brand versus non-brand, a country with its own stock, site or season, ranges with different margins, personas with their own funnel, or very high order value with few daily orders. Do not split a placement, age band or region because its breakdown ROAS looks higher; Meta spends on marginal return, so that slice has no room left. Accounts at $1M to $5M a month often run 4 or 5 campaigns.

**Judge it by:** Each campaign has one line of purpose; about half in typical audits fail. Weak work runs 25 campaigns on $50,000 a month.

**Breaks when:** Mixed men's and women's ranges in one ad set often serve the wrong gender; check the breakdown and split.

**From:** ADC-6166, ADC-6191, GAD-6151, ADC-6237, ADC-6246, ADC-6194, GAD-6171, ADC-6233, ADC-0030, ADC-6259, ADC-6238

### 10. Give products with different contribution their own campaign or target, and use value bidding, because conversion bidding buys the cheapest sale

**Why it works:** Maximise-conversions bidding sends spend to the lowest-CPA product, not the most profitable. One store's $90 hoodie made $24 contribution at a $45 CAC, its tee $12 at $15; the algorithm preferred the tee. In shared campaigns, spend drifts to discounted slow stock where margin is thinnest.

**How to apply:** Choose value-based bidding about 90% of the time in e-commerce. Rank campaigns on profit contribution: two at 4x on $10,000 can differ in profit through discount codes and margin mix. On Google Shopping, segment by ROAS tier when margins sit within about 10 points, and by margin tier when they spread wider. Check for products with suspiciously low CAC: they may sell without ads, and pulling spend can lift their contribution from $12 to $27 a sale.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows contribution per product beside CPA and puts high-contribution lines where they can win spend. Weak work celebrates the lowest CPA product.

**Breaks when:** A catalogue with similar margins and prices gains little from splits and loses data density (principle 9). Stores with 5 to 10 products should not run tier systems.

**From:** ADC-6159, ADC-6246, GAD-6157, ADC-6156, GAD-0040, ADC-6157, GAD-6172

### 11. Match the account build to the spend tier: test, scale and a tiny customer campaign under $50k a month; add campaigns only above it

**Why it works:** Bid strategy testing is worth about 3 to 4% efficiency, which matters at $1M a month and not at $20,000. Complex builds at low spend starve each model.

**How to apply:** Under about $50,000 a month on Meta, run one testing campaign with one concept per ad set, an optional scaling campaign and existing-customer retargeting; read tests over at least 5 days. From $50,000 to $250,000, add one to three cold campaigns only for a commercial reason, such as a high-LTV category on a looser CPA. Ignore cost and bid caps under about $200,000 a month. Above $250,000, consider a second ad account on the same pixel with a different bid strategy and about 10% of spend, which also guards against a ban. On Google, run at most two campaigns at $10,000 a month, three to five under $50,000.

**Judge it by:** The plan states the spend tier and the build follows it. Weak work copies a nine-figure brand's structure onto a $15,000 account.

**Breaks when:** Many categories, countries or margins can justify more campaigns at lower spend.

**From:** ADC-6200, ADC-6216, ADC-6182, ADC-6185, ADC-6179, GAD-6222, ADC-6231

### 12. Fund each creative test to its decision point: daily budget equals target conversions times expected CPA, divided by test days

**Why it works:** A test that cannot reach enough purchases ends in a guess. At a $100 CPA, 50 conversions in 7 days needs about $714 a day per test; 20 conversions over 14 days needs about $143.

**How to apply:** Launch each batch of 4 to 8 ads for one concept as its own ad set, with a minimum spend of about 1x target CPA for up to 7 days. Call a concept after 3x target CPA in spend: 3 or more purchases keep, 2 wait, 0 or 1 cut; 5x gives a firmer call. Run about one week to cover weekday swings and the 3 to 4 days buyers take; small, slow accounts need 10 to 14. Ads a CBO never spent on can get a second chance in their own ad set; one advertiser recovers about 20% more winners that way.

**Judge it by:** Each cell shows its budget, assumed CPA and call date. Weak work runs ten $30-a-day cells and calls a winner on day 2.

**Breaks when:** High-price products with few sales need an earlier metric, named first. Sale weeks distort reads; do not test then.

**From:** ADC-6211, ADC-0005, ADC-0039, ADC-6209, ADC-0031, MCR-0068

### 13. Judge results at ad set and campaign level, leave a winning ad set alone, and rank ad metrics by reliability with spend first

**Why it works:** Meta sequences several ads to one buyer but credits the last. In one ad set the opener showed 1.4x and the closer 6x, yet the opener made the sale possible. Campaign ROAS on 7-day click with existing customers excluded tracks the P&L far better than ad ROAS.

**How to apply:** In an ad set hitting KPI, do not switch off low-ROAS ads or add new ones; that disturbs learned sequencing. Put new ads in a new ad set and scale winners by budget. Rank ad metrics: amount spent, then ROAS or CPA on 7-day click or incremental credit, then CPC and CTR as early signals, then hook and hold rate for diagnosis only. A 1.7x ad holding twice the spend of a 2.2x ad is usually the better one: Meta funds it because it starts journeys. Keep 3 to 15 ads per ad set.

**Judge it by:** Verdicts name the ad set, its spend and its store-side return; ad-level numbers appear only as diagnosis. Weak work pauses every ad under 2x.

**Breaks when:** An ad set that misses KPI is where you cut or add ads. High-CTR ads with few sales are false winners.

**From:** ADC-6294, MEA-6314, ADC-6204, ADC-6207, ADC-6206, ADC-0009, MCR-0029

### 14. Treat creative volume as a budget line: plan about one new ad per $1,000 of monthly spend, or new-customer revenue target divided by revenue per ad

**Why it works:** Most ads hold little spend, about $700 to $1,500 lifetime on average, and only about 5% become winners. One brand on 15 live ads cut CAC 20 to 30% just by relaunching old winners to reach about 60.

**How to apply:** Size volume two ways and use the higher: one new ad per $1,000 of monthly spend, or the new-customer revenue target divided by revenue per ad ($300,000 at $3,000 needs 100 ads). Check churn: 2 winners lost a month at a 5% hit rate needs about 40 new ads. Fund production: about 25% of media at $30,000 to $100,000 a month, about 10% above that. Moving $10,000 of $350,000 into production pays back at a 5% efficiency gain.

**Judge it by:** The plan includes new ads a month and the production budget. Weak work raises spend with no new creative.

**Breaks when:** Volume below the quality bar fails: 2,000 ads a month on $250,000 stayed unprofitable. Returns on volume fade around $1M to $3M a month.

**From:** MCR-6659, ADC-0017, ADC-0013, MCR-0059, MCR-6663, MCR-0025, MCR-6667, MCR-6660, MCR-6658

### 15. Spread spend across many different ads, and treat cold ad frequency above 2 as a sign the creative is too similar

**Why it works:** Under Meta's retrieval system, near-duplicate ads share one entity and serve to the same people: 200 similar ads may count as about 20. Purchase odds fall sharply after a second view of the same ad. Under CBO about 4% of ads can hold about 64% of spend, which is fragile.

**How to apply:** Check ad-level frequency on cold campaigns over 30 to 60 days; above 2 means too little variety. New ads differ in persona, angle or offer, not colour. When one concept takes about 80% of spend, shift output toward it or cap it near 40% and rotate new concepts in. Three ads holding $1,000 a day at 4x are safer than one; brands leaning on three big ads fell 40% on new customers when they tired. Ask the Meta rep for the creative similarity score.

**Judge it by:** The review shows top-ad spend share and cold ad frequency. Weak work duplicates the winner ten times with new colours.

**Breaks when:** A narrow persona saturates fast; in a small market it cannot hold large daily spend however varied the ads.

**From:** MCR-6631, MCR-0040, ADC-0014, MCR-6666, MCR-0022, ADC-0061, MCR-6671, MCR-6669, MCR-6642

### 16. Pace spend by stock and cash: pull or slow ads on SKUs that would sell out before reorder, and push slow stock at break-even

**Why it works:** Profit is not cash: $2M of profit with $3M in unsold stock is about $1M short. Every ad has a lifetime spend capacity, so pushing a winner hard burns it out in weeks, while about $100 a day can keep it alive for months.

**How to apply:** For each advertised SKU, compare days of cover with reorder lead time; when cover is shorter, raise its ROAS target or pause it. Grade stock A to D by sell-through and run C and D through paid at break-even, in its own funnel or a new market. Do not pour budget into a product already selling fast: 90% of $100,000 behind one viral SKU cut its contribution from $500,000 to about $410,000. On Google, pull a product when its core size sells out.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows days of cover per pushed SKU and a route for slow stock. Weak work pushes the hero into a stock-out.

**Breaks when:** A launch that restocks in about two weeks can sell out safely. Above about $50,000 a month this is core to the job.

**From:** MEA-6253, ADC-6197, ADC-0023, ADC-6199, MEA-6385, GAD-6226, ORG-0038, ADC-6180

### 17. Raise budgets on what the data shows, not on rules of thumb, and expect a purchase lag after every jump

**Why it works:** One agency finds no Meta documentation behind the "never raise more than 20%" rule; the dip after a big raise is a lag while newly reached people take days to buy. Meta's help centre says an ad set leaves learning after about 50 optimisation events in 7 days. Google exits learning after 50 conversions or 3 buying cycles.

**How to apply:** On Meta, raise winners about 20% a day under $50,000 a month, faster with volume, then judge on at least 7 days. On Google, raise about 20% at a time; with 50 or more conversions a week, up to 30% every 3 to 5 days. Raise only when a campaign spends its full budget and hits KPI; under 80% spend and missing KPI, loosen the target instead. Fast cuts, even by half, rarely hurt.

**Judge it by:** Each change names the trigger, step size and read date. Weak work raises 50% Monday and cuts Wednesday.

**Breaks when:** Campaigns under 10 conversions a week swing wildly; 78% of such Google campaigns moved 50% or more in ROAS in one review.

**From:** ADC-6173, GAD-6193, ADC-6216, ADC-0064, GAD-6165, GAD-6187, GAD-0023, GAD-6198, Meta Business Help, About the learning phase: https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700

### 18. On Google, put most e-commerce spend in Shopping and treat the product feed as the main lever

**Why it works:** Shoppers see the image, price and about five rivals before they click, so Shopping pre-qualifies the visitor. Price, title and main image drive most Shopping results, and the feed is the first lever in Performance Max as well.

**How to apply:** Keep about 80 to 90% of e-commerce Google spend in Shopping. Rewrite titles to lead with terms that already convert in the search terms report, brand last; buyers often see only the first 70 characters. Supply 4:5 images, since the image fills about 70% of a listing, and set them apart from rivals' white backgrounds. Keep feed price identical to the page price, or products are disapproved. Run one feed per currency when selling abroad. A sudden CTR drop on a product often means a rival cut price.

**Judge it by:** The plan includes feed fixes ranked by the spend on affected products. Weak work tweaks bids while titles still read like supplier codes.

**Breaks when:** B2B stores and products hard to judge from an image lean more on Search. Under about 30 SKUs, growth comes from titles and bids, not breadth.

**From:** GAD-6152, GAD-0019, GAD-6155, GAD-0054, GAD-6169, GAD-6123, GAD-6174, GAD-6122, GAD-6170, GAD-6217, GAD-0001

### 19. Wall off brand search: exclude brand from every prospecting campaign, run it in its own capped campaign, and keep its spend small

**Why it works:** Brand searchers were coming anyway. Brand often takes about 11% of search spend but 34% of conversions, which inflates blended ROAS. Without brand exclusions, Performance Max is mostly a retargeting campaign.

**How to apply:** Exclude brand from PMax twice, with a brand list exclusion and brand negative keywords, so one catches what the other misses. Add exact brand negatives to every non-brand campaign. Run brand search alone on maximise clicks with a CPC limit; for one brand this halved clicks while value per cost rose. Keep brand at about 3 to 8% of Google spend, never above 10%. Non-brand ROAS should then track acquisition MER; if not, look for brand leakage.

**Judge it by:** Cold campaign search terms show no brand terms, and the brand line is reported apart. Weak work reports a 20x PMax with brand inside.

**Breaks when:** If a rival bids hard on your name, brand defence may need more; Google lets rivals bid on your trademark but can restrict it in their ad text. One brand's holdouts put brand under 1% of spend.

**From:** GAD-0003, GAD-6154, GAD-6121, GAD-0028, GAD-6200, GAD-0024, GAD-6184, GAD-6120, ADC-0051

### 20. Run Performance Max as one layer beside standard Shopping and Search, not as the whole account

**Why it works:** Left alone, PMax drifts toward warm and repeat buyers it did not win; agency owners report half or more of its conversions are repeat or warm. Doubling PMax kept its ROAS near 5 but barely moved revenue; scaling standard Shopping fell to 3 or 4 ROAS but added more revenue.

**How to apply:** New advertisers can grow on PMax, but expect a plateau somewhere between $3,000 and $100,000 a month, then rebuild around standard Shopping. Above about $15,000 to $20,000 a month, keep 20 to 40% of Shopping spend in PMax. Exclude add-to-cart audiences, existing customers and brand. If over 5 to 10% of PMax spend leaves Search and Shopping, switch to feed-only: delete asset groups, turn off URL expansion and auto-created assets. Use one asset group per product type, 15 or more products each.

**Judge it by:** The review shows PMax placements, spend share and new-customer share. Weak work tunes audience signals, worth about 1% after 30 days.

**Breaks when:** Pausing a large PMax can raise Search CPCs; one lifted CPC from $9 to $11. When it slips, check top products for stock-outs first.

**From:** GAD-6148, GAD-6156, GAD-6182, GAD-6163, GAD-6197, GAD-6196, GAD-0048, GAD-6149, GAD-6162, GAD-6199, GAD-6215

### 21. Set target ROAS just under actual, and when spend falls, pull the target back sharply rather than in small steps

**Why it works:** A target set above what a campaign achieves leaves almost no room to explore, so spend shrinks, conversions drop, the model weakens and it spirals. Raising targets step by step lifts reported ROAS by shrinking bids to warm buyers, while the lower target was likely winning more new customers.

**How to apply:** Start new campaigns on maximise conversion value to build data, then switch to target ROAS at 30 to 50 conversions a month, set at or a little under trailing 30-day ROAS. Hold a new target 2 to 3 weeks. When conversions fall from 100 to 80 a month under a target, cut it hard. Bidding weights the last 30 days of a 90-day lookback most. Monthly, sort budget-limited campaigns: actual far above target (500% against 300%), remove the target; within 10 to 20%, leave it.

**Judge it by:** Each target change shows trailing ROAS, conversions per week and the reason. Weak work raises target ROAS to "improve efficiency" while new-customer volume falls.

**Breaks when:** From 17 August 2026, budget-limited target campaigns bid toward the stated target, so a loose legacy target can pull results down; confirm in Google Ads Help. A broken feed or tag needs fixing first.

**From:** GAD-0053, GAD-6166, GAD-0021, GAD-6173, GAD-6206, GAD-6175, GAD-6194, GAD-0004

### 22. Diagnose before you change anything: break the result into its parts, read change history, and start where spend is largest

**Why it works:** CPA is CPC divided by conversion rate, and clicks are impressions times CTR; these two cover about 90% of diagnoses. Rebuilding resets learning and can start a spiral.

**How to apply:** Split revenue into sessions, conversion rate and order value; a 5% dip in order value plus 4% in conversion can be noise. Read change history and use 90 to 180 days in a weekly view, so you do not react to your own edits. Check the platform with the most spend first: if Google is 10 to 30% of spend, a store dip is rarely Google. Look in the top five campaigns by cost; doubling one with 10% of budget moves the account about 5%. Act only when you can state the chain from cause to metric. Change one or a few things at a time.

**Judge it by:** The diagnosis names the chain and the evidence for each link. Weak work restructures after one bad week.

**Breaks when:** A tracking break or lost feed needs an immediate fix. Most setting nudges move results about 2%; spend time on changes worth 20 to 30%.

**From:** GAD-6209, GAD-6211, MEA-0009, GAD-6208, GAD-6210, GAD-6216, GAD-6218, GAD-6177

### 23. Read recent days with their conversion lag in mind, and never judge the last 3 to 4 days on default columns

**Why it works:** Google credits a purchase to the day of the click, so the latest days look weak until late sales land. Meta's gap between 1-day and 30-day click revenue is about 40% all year and doubles to about 80% in November, so pre-sale spend looks weak in real time and pays later.

**How to apply:** On Google, add a conversions by conversion time column before diagnosing recent days, and recheck any week about two weeks later before judging it. For a quick health check, compare the two weeks ending a week ago with the two before; 10% more cost should bring 10% or more conversions. Before a sale, one store's real-time 1.0 ROAS in mid-November became about 4 once value booked. On Black Friday, spend peaks around 5 to 7 a.m. and revenue around 7 to 11 a.m.; pace with lag multipliers.

**Judge it by:** Reads of the last week state their lag allowance. Weak work cuts on Tuesday after a slow weekend.

**Breaks when:** Impulse products have little lag. Batch-processed returns create their own fake dips; track acquisition MER raw and with returns added back.

**From:** GAD-6212, ADC-0046, GAD-6192, GAD-6188, MEA-6357, ADC-0047, MEA-6393

### 24. Before a big cut or raise, prove the channel's lift with a holdout, and match the measurement tool to the store's size

**Why it works:** Platform numbers are fast but least reliable; finance numbers slower and trustworthy; lift tests slowest and closest to truth. Meta's incremental column is not a live holdout; it applies factors from other advertisers' tests, so treat it as a second read.

**How to apply:** Match the tool to revenue: at $1M to $5M use 7-day click, tagged product costs and a daily new-customer dashboard; at $5M to $20M add platform lift studies; above $20M run geo-lift tests and apply incrementality factors. For a geo test, match region groups whose spend, new-customer revenue and orders moved together, change spend in one, and compare: $40,000 extra driving $100,000 is 2.5x incremental. As a cheap second read, a channel with 50% of spend should appear in about 50% of post-purchase survey answers.

**Judge it by:** Any move above about a quarter of a channel's budget cites two reads that agree. Weak work cuts TikTok on its own dashboard.

**Breaks when:** Countries with few states need commuting-zone splits. TikTok lift tests need about $200,000 of spend. Basic hygiene often beats buying an attribution tool.

**From:** MEA-6315, MEA-6309, MEA-6308, MEA-6246, MEA-0033, MEA-0045, MEA-6301, MEA-6304, MEA-6364

### 25. Expect a channel's effect to show up in another channel's numbers, and read them together before cutting one

**Why it works:** When Meta creates demand, branded search rises with Meta spend and Google takes the credit. One TikTok test at about 25% of Meta budget reported 1x to 2x while Meta's mid-funnel campaign jumped from 4x to 12x on retargeted TikTok traffic.

**How to apply:** Before cutting an upper-funnel channel on its own ROAS, check brand search volume, other channels' retargeting returns and acquisition MER over the same weeks. Do not raise Meta budgets on a mid-funnel jump that a new channel caused. Be wary of small tertiary channels: low-spend Snapchat, Pinterest or Reddit budgets drift to warm visitors and report high ROAS. Never run traffic or reach campaigns you cannot measure; a 3% slice launched during Black Friday was simply lost. Brands on Amazon should count it: about 20% of Meta-created value landed there in one strategist's data.

**Judge it by:** Channel verdicts cite cross-channel movement, not one platform. Weak work kills the channel that made the others work.

**Breaks when:** Pop-ups and stores also lift digital returns locally; one pop-up tripled ad results in one city.

**From:** MEA-6356, MEA-6288, ADC-6153, MEA-6319, ADC-6193, MEA-0029, MEA-6311

### 26. Open a new channel with one readable test sized to the target CPA, and only once the main channels have reached their profit frontier

**Why it works:** A small budget spread over several channels leaves every read too thin. Meta usually scales further before YouTube pays, since YouTube needs more spend and its own creative.

**How to apply:** On TikTok, start with 3 creative styles across 2 audiences at $50 a day per ad group for 3 to 4 days, size ad groups near 2x target CPA with a $20 floor, and use 2 to 3 interests at most. In the first day look for CTR above 1%, watch time above 25% and CPM under $15; CPM above $20 is an audience problem. After 3 days cut the bottom half. On Google, start Shopping on 5 to 10 bestsellers and add PMax after about 100 purchases. Step a Google test from about $10,000 to $20,000 a month.

**Judge it by:** The plan names the channel's job, test budget, read date and second read. Weak work spreads $3,000 over four channels.

**Breaks when:** TikTok paid strongly in fashion brands with a native organic engine, while holdouts outside fashion failed; skip it when cash is needed now. In GMV Max, set the ROI target a full point above TikTok's suggestion.

**From:** ADC-6253, ADC-0036, ADC-0063, ADC-0035, ADC-0037, GAD-0030, GAD-0033, GAD-6202, ADC-6286, ADC-6272, ADC-0062, ADC-0063

### 27. Plan peak season as a quarter, not a weekend: build audience in September and October and write the scaling rules in advance

**Why it works:** Underspending before the sale shrinks the audience the sale converts. Geo holdouts ending 3 or more weeks before Black Friday showed about 105% lift after spend stopped.

**How to apply:** Put about 31 to 43% of the September to December budget into September and October, and plan November near 1.8x October. On Google, raise non-brand spend in September and October, since search shifts to branded terms about two weeks before Black Friday. Write scaling rules first: scale on a 3-day rolling read of marginal contribution, cut after two poor reads in a row. Keep evergreen campaigns on, at about 50 to 60% of Black Friday spend, and add offer-badged copies of past winners: offer ads hold 4 to 5 times more spend at similar ROAS. Plan a post-Christmas offer for the cheap auction after 16 December. Set a stricter CAC for November buyers, who repeated less in one sample.

**Judge it by:** The plan shows spend, rules and offer break-evens before October. Weak work goes all-in on Black Friday week.

**Breaks when:** Stores with other peaks shift the calendar but keep the logic. Do not plan from last year's auction; recheck CPM trends.

**From:** MEA-0034, ADC-0048, GAD-6189, ADC-6264, MCR-0069, ADC-6262, MEA-6358, ADC-6261

### 28. On Amazon, bid from break-even ACOS and the real conversion rate, and judge the program on total ad spend over total sales

**Why it works:** Amazon's own guide says ACOS must stay below profit margin to make money. The most a click can cost is price times target ACOS times conversion rate, because cost per sale is cost per click divided by conversion rate. Ads also lift organic rank, which ACOS misses.

**How to apply:** Work break-even ACOS from gross profit after referral, fulfilment and storage fees. If the affordable click is below suggested bids on the main phrases, ads cannot pay at that price. At launch use dynamic bids down only; up and down can raise a bid by up to 100%. Check the offer holds the Featured Offer, which rests on price, delivery speed and seller performance. Products described many ways give cheap long-tail terms. Track TACoS, total ad spend over total sales: if it falls while ads hold, ads are building organic sales.

**Judge it by:** Each bid traces to break-even ACOS and conversion rate. Weak work copies suggested bids.

**Breaks when:** On cheap units, a few clicks wipe out the profit. A launch may run above break-even only inside a capped, approved loss.

**From:** MEA-0019, GAD-0038, AMZ-6447, AMZ-6442, AMZ-0003, Amazon Ads, ACOS guide: https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/acos-advertising-cost-of-sales

### 29. Enter a new country with proven winners, local voices and a price set for its CPMs, modelled on first-purchase profit only

**Why it works:** A new market knows nothing of the brand, so home awareness that propped up ROAS is gone. Auction costs differ by country: US Meta CPMs ran about $22 against about $11 in Australia, so with the same CTR and conversion, CAC doubles.

**How to apply:** Model the launch on first-purchase gross profit against expected CAC, with no lifetime value, so it pays its own way. Take only home ads with large lifetime spend: one brand moved five ads with over $200,000 each and the US became 75% of the business. Re-voice or reshoot winners with local creators and accents. Offset higher CPMs with price, not just better ads. Give the country its own campaign only if it has its own stock, site or season. Budget for higher returns where buyers return more, up to double in US fashion.

**Judge it by:** The country plan shows its own CPM, price, CAC and return rate. Weak work copies home targets and creative.

**Breaks when:** A small budget, strong seasonality or retail in a few regions can justify starting in part of a country instead of nationwide.

**From:** MCR-6628, OFF-6618, MEA-6267, ADC-6163, MCR-6629, ADC-6237, MEA-6266, ADC-6164

### 30. Spend the buyer's hours on the big levers and switch off settings that hide what works

**Why it works:** Growth levers rank from smallest to largest: budget, account structure, creative, offer, brand, product; teams spend most time on the first two. Daily bid and budget tweaks squeeze at most about 5% from a Meta account, while the same hour on creative can double it.

**How to apply:** Under about $200,000 a month, stop daily bid tweaks, interest targeting and lookalikes; spend the time on structure, concept testing, existing-customer allocation, landing page tests and creative. Turn off flexible ads and dynamic creative unless the page ad limit is hit: you cannot see which asset worked, and Meta may crop or stitch assets. Turn off Advantage+ creative enhancements at advertiser level and on each ad. Define new, engaged and existing audiences in Meta's audience segments. Name ad sets by persona, angle, offer, format and date. Heavy launchers should add pages before they hit the live-ad cap.

**Judge it by:** The weekly routine lists time spent on each lever, and the settings audit is dated. Weak work logs twenty bid edits and no new ad.

**Breaks when:** Above about $250,000 a month, bid strategy work becomes worth a dedicated person.

**From:** OFF-6644, ADC-6230, ADC-6212, ADC-6249, ADC-6248, ADC-0015, ADC-6203, ADC-6240, ADC-6200

## Working from the org context

- Read the numbers first: delivered cost per order, new-customer order value and margin, return rate and repeat curve. If any is missing, build the break-even line before planning spend, and name the one assumption.
- Read what ran and won: past winning ads with their lifetime spend, the concepts and personas behind them, and the offers they carried. Past winners get relaunched or extended before net-new spend is planned.
- Read the buyers in their own words and the segments the data ranks. The best segment gets its own concept ad set or campaign only when it has a distinct funnel or margin, never just a new interest.
- Keep the brand's voice, claims and offer rules exactly as approved. A buyer may not invent a discount, change a claim or add a promise to make a campaign work; propose it to the offer and copy owners instead.
- Keep the design system in every ad the buyer launches or resizes; a placement crop that breaks the brand's safe zones is a launch fault, not a creative choice.
- Read stock, lead times and cash with the same care as ads data. Spend plans follow the stock grades and the cash floor the org context records.
- Where the brand's current setup breaks a principle here (view-through reporting, brand inside PMax, retargeting at 30 views a month), propose the change in two lines: what to change and the money it is worth, with one number, then wait for the owner's yes.
- Record each change with its date, reason and read date, and write what the result taught into the org context so the next job starts from it.
- Pay-on-delivery markets, Indian marketplaces and other India-only matters go to Ishita (ishita-india-commerce).

## Review checklist

- [ ] Does every target trace to delivered gross profit on a new customer's order, with the cost lines shown?
- [ ] Is the headline read acquisition MER or new-customer CAC from store orders, not platform ROAS?
- [ ] Were platform purchases checked against store orders before any verdict, with the gap stated?
- [ ] Does every channel row name its attribution window, with Meta on 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view?
- [ ] Are scale decisions made on marginal return per step, not on the account average?
- [ ] Are existing customers excluded from cold, with their budget set in money from a frequency target?
- [ ] Are DPA and retargeting shares stated with their 7-day click or incremental reads?
- [ ] Does each offer have its own recomputed break-even ROAS, applied from the day it starts?
- [ ] Does every campaign beyond the first have a written business reason?
- [ ] Are products with different contribution separated or bid on value, not on conversion count?
- [ ] Is each test cell funded to its decision point, with the call rule and date written in advance?
- [ ] Are creative verdicts made at ad set level, with spend ranked first?
- [ ] Does the plan include new-ad volume and production budget sized to spend or the revenue target?
- [ ] Is cold ad-level frequency checked, and top-ad spend concentration stated?
- [ ] Do pushed SKUs have days of cover longer than their reorder lead time?
- [ ] On Google, is brand excluded from every prospecting campaign and reported apart?
- [ ] Do recent-day reads allow for conversion lag?
- [ ] Does any large cut or raise rest on two reads that agree, or a planned holdout?
- [ ] Does the spend plan add up to the owner's budget, with each change logged and a read date set?

## Common failures

- Setting target ROAS from habit ("3x is good") instead of break-even. Fix: compute 1 divided by delivered gross margin on new-customer orders, then set the target from the lifetime gross profit to CAC band.
- Celebrating platform ROAS that is mostly view-throughs and existing buyers. Fix: switch to 7-day click plus engaged view, exclude customers, and report acquisition MER beside it.
- Moving budget into retargeting and DPAs because they show the best ROAS. Fix: cap them by frequency, read them incrementally and keep 70 to 90% of effort on cold buyers.
- Building a campaign per audience, placement or country on a small budget. Fix: consolidate until each campaign clears 100 to 200 conversions a month, and write a reason for every split.
- Pausing every ad under target inside a winning ad set. Fix: judge at ad set level and leave winning ad sets alone; put new ads in a new ad set.
- Scaling spend with no new creative. Fix: plan new ads from spend or the revenue target and fund production as a media line.
- Keeping last month's targets through a sale. Fix: recompute break-even ROAS for each discount depth before launch.
- Pushing the hero SKU into a stock-out. Fix: check days of cover against lead time for every pushed product, and route slow stock through paid at break-even.
- Raising tROAS step by step to lift reported ROAS. Fix: watch new-customer volume and pull the target back sharply when spend falls.
- Cutting a channel on its own dashboard. Fix: read brand search, other channels and acquisition MER together, and run a holdout before a large cut.
- Judging the last three days on default Google columns. Fix: add conversion-time columns and recheck the week two weeks later.
- Restructuring the account after one bad week. Fix: decompose the drop, read change history over 90 to 180 days and make the smallest fix first.
