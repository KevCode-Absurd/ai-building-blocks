# Organic social, creators and community: craft principles

This book is for everyone who earns a store attention it does not pay for view by view: organic social, creator and affiliate management, influencer outreach, TikTok Shop and live selling, and PR. Read it before the job, write the deliverable to it, and run the review checklist on the draft before hand-over. The skills say what to make; this book says what great looks like and how to tell.

## What great looks like

- Every creator, post series, gift round and pitch has a profit verdict: gross profit from tracked orders against full cost, read at 30 and 60 days.
- Every fee and commission is a number capped by the store's own gross profit per order and its own paid reach costs, never a rate card.
- The organic feed doubles as the ad lab: outliers move into paid within days, with written rights.
- Creators are picked on audience in the selling country and real reach to strangers, not on follower counts.
- Briefs give the concept, the must-say facts and the banned claims, then leave the delivery to the creator.
- Each platform plan is built on that platform's real rules for links, messages, shopping and reposts, checked on the day.
- Every paid, gifted or affiliate post carries a plain label at the start, and every reused asset has a dated rights record.
- Pitches go out only with a true angle, to writers who cover it, on their lead times.
- Community and loyalty work is judged on reorders against a control group, not on members or likes.

## The principles

### 1. Judge every creator, series and gift round on gross profit per 1,000 views

**Why it works:** Views are cheap and uneven. Across seven top TikTok Shop videos, sales per 1,000 views ranged from about $1.65 to $385, so the biggest video is often not the best one (MCR-0046). Clicks also undercount: one firm's AI referrals were 0.5% of visits but 12.1% of sign-ups, found only by a survey (AEO-0020).

**How to apply:** Give each creator a code, a UTM link and a survey line, merge the three, and count new and returning buyers apart, since codes leak to coupon sites. Work out gross profit per 1,000 views after product, postage, fees, discount and commission, at 30 and 60 days. Once creator spend is large beside paid media, split the credit with a lift test (MEA-6234) or a CausalImpact model (MEA-6249). A UK candle brand ranks ten gifted creators this way and rebooks the top three.

**Judge it by:** a table ranked by gross profit per 1,000 views, new and returning split, with a survey line. Weak work ranks by views or followers.

**Breaks when:** a handful of orders, where one buyer swings the rank (judge the reusable content instead); very different price points; paid spend behind some videos (MCR-0046).

**From:** MCR-0046, AEO-0020, AEO-0010, MEA-6234, MEA-6249

### 2. Price creator deals off your own paid reach: aim at half your Meta CPM, then cut for off-target viewers

**Why it works:** A creator post is bought reach plus trust; if 1,000 real viewers cost more than an ad, buy the ad. Bloom set its creator CPM at half its Meta CPM and held it under $2 for months (ORG-0041).

**How to apply:** Take the views of the creator's last 10 videos times your target CPM, divided by 1,000. Bloom uses the average; use the median when one viral hit would inflate the fee. Cut for off-target viewers: a quarter off-target, cut 25%; half outside your selling country, halve it (ORG-0041). Work commission back from gross profit and pay it on new customers only, so creators do not earn on reorders that would happen anyway: Gorilla Mind pays 20% plus 10% off for followers, about 30% of a first order (ORG-6423), which only pays where buyers reorder. A US pet brand at a $20 Meta CPM offers $10: 40,000 views earns $400, or $300 if a quarter of viewers are abroad.

**Judge it by:** each offer shows views basis, CPM, cuts, new-customer-only commission and a profit cap. Weak work quotes "industry rates" or prices by followers.

**Breaks when:** views are bought; views and Meta impressions differ, so the match is rough; cash-tight stores that need click tracking.

**From:** ORG-0041, ORG-6423

### 3. Move posts that beat your normal views by about 5x into paid within a week, and pre-test ad cuts as trial reels

**Why it works:** Organic is the cheapest test bench a store has. A post with about 5x its usual views (4,000 against 800) has proven its hook to strangers for free (ORG-0035). Trial reels show a cut to non-followers first, so one agency trims 20 hook variants to the best 5 in about 24 hours (MCR-6861).

**How to apply:** Keep a baseline from the last 30 posts. When a post clears about 5x, run it as is through the ad code or partnership permission, plus a version with the same hook and a product back half. For new ad cuts, strip the call to action, post as trial reels and send only the top quarter by retention to the media buyer (MCR-0041). Founders who post daily learn what holds past 3 and 15 seconds, which makes their ads good (ORG-6427).

**Judge it by:** a baseline, outliers with their multiples, and a handoff to paid with rights checked. Weak work boosts posts by likes.

**Breaks when:** there is no baseline yet; the post went viral with non-buyers; trial reels change; retention is a filter, not proof of sales.

**From:** ORG-0035, MCR-6861, MCR-0041, ORG-6427

### 4. Run creator content as partnership ads from the creator's handle for cold buyers, not as brand-page ads

**Why it works:** A partnership ad uses both handles' audience data to find buyers and reads as a person, not a pitch. Some Blue Sense accounts put up to 40% of spend through them (ADC-6210); founder ads from the founder's own handle beat the brand page by about 5 to 30% (ADC-0003).

**How to apply:** Ask for account-level partnership permission in the Instagram app, which lets the brand run ads from the creator's handle without a post and revoke it later (ADC-6081). Use creators whose viewers match the buyer, since their data steers delivery. Pay a flat fee for access, never a share of ad spend, which needs per-creator tracking and audits (ORG-0033). Give must-spend partnership ads their own campaign: one audit found a $2,000-a-week creator ad starved inside Advantage+ (ADC-6271). Keep hard-sell scripts for warm buyers on the brand page (MCR-6815). Send a big ambassador's ads to a page built from the same shoot (LPG-0068).

**Judge it by:** which creators run cold, permission status and end date, a flat fee, and protected spend. Weak work hands over raw videos with no permissions.

**Breaks when:** off-target or fake audiences; contract rights narrower than the platform permission; the edge may fade as more brands use it (ADC-6210).

**From:** ADC-6210, ADC-0003, ADC-6081, ORG-0033, ADC-6271, MCR-6815, LPG-0068

### 5. Seed wide to find the few: expect about 2 good creators per 100 gifted products, and set the payback rule before any parcel ships

**Why it works:** Gifting is a search cost. Ridge finds about 2 creators per 100 gifted products who make good content unprompted, and moves only those into paid work (ORG-0033). One agency's seeding army sent 100,000 invites in 60 days; about 6% became creators and returned about 5,800 videos (ORG-0009).

**How to apply:** Cost the round first (units times landed cost, packing and postage) and get the owner's yes on that cash. Value the return as gross profit from tracked orders plus what the videos would cost to buy. Send only to creators who said yes, with no posting obligation and a request for a clear label. Target 2 to 3 videos per sample sent, and move the best two per hundred to paid deals (ORG-0009). A brand with no reviews can gift about 20 people for specific, honest reviews (MCR-6684). A $3 lip balm can seed 100 creators; a $90 blender cannot.

**Judge it by:** an approved cost, the payback rule up front, and a 30 and 60 day verdict per creator. Weak work counts posts.

**Breaks when:** costly or bulky products cannot be seeded in volume; regulated claims (supplements, skincare) need controlled scripts; reposted content lacks rights or labels.

**From:** ORG-0033, ORG-0009, MCR-6684

### 6. Vet the audience, not the follower count: drop creators whose views come from the wrong country, wrong buyer or paid boosts

**Why it works:** The fee buys the viewers, so they must be buyers. Follower counts hide off-target audiences, bought reach and accounts that only reach existing fans.

**How to apply:** Ask for dated insight screenshots of audience country, age and gender. Score fit on median views of the last 10 posts. Flag high-view videos under about 2% engagement as likely paid boosts (ORG-0010). If 70 to 80% of views come from followers, the creator fails the stranger test and adds little new reach (ORG-0029). Use creators native to the market: UK and Australian accents carry between those two but underperform in the US (MCR-6629, MCR-6677). Ask for past work and the briefs behind it to see how tight a brief they need (MCR-6871). Check labels on their past brand posts.

**Judge it by:** each row shows country match, median views, follower share, boost flags and disclosure record. Weak work lists follower counts.

**Breaks when:** creators will not share insights (then cap the deal at a gift or a small test); voiceless videos where accent does not matter; very small creators with too few posts to read.

**From:** ORG-0010, ORG-0029, MCR-6629, MCR-6677, MCR-6871

### 7. Brief creators on the concept and the facts, not a word-for-word script, and audit what comes back against eight beats

**Why it works:** Creators sell because they sound like themselves. The brand must control the concept (persona, angle, offer) and the claims. Gorilla Mind ran 143 unique scripts but only about 35 real concepts, so volume hid sameness (MCR-6615).

**How to apply:** One page: the buyer, one angle, the offer, three must-say facts word for word, banned claims, the label line and the shots. For cold ads, a strong order is persona callout, problem, product, promise, mechanism, before and after, objections, identity payoff, offer; the promise before the mechanism keeps people watching (MCR-6748). Audit returns for hook, problem, mechanism, proof, objection, offer, risk reversal and call to action (MCR-6822). Move a creator's best line up from a minute in and split it into its own ads (MCR-6814). In fashion, ask for a no-talking cut, sizes on screen and a multi-item haul (MCR-6830). When a hook wins, brief every creator to make their own version (MCR-6820).

**Judge it by:** a one-page brief with one concept; returns carry the facts word for word. Weak briefs are full scripts or mood boards.

**Breaks when:** the creator writes direct response well enough to beat the brief; regulated products, where creators need compliance guidance.

**From:** MCR-6615, MCR-6748, MCR-6822, MCR-6814, MCR-6830, MCR-6820

### 8. On TikTok Shop, sign small creators on a floor-or-commission deal for 30 videos, and never set an ROI target below break-even

**Why it works:** Small creators post more and cost less; a floor removes their risk while commission rewards those who sell. Comfrt pays creators under 10,000 followers the higher of $1,000 a month or 15 to 20% commission for 30 videos, then moves winners to commission only (ORG-0036).

**How to apply:** Work out profit per sale after TikTok's fees as read in Seller Center that day, commission and sample cost spread over orders, and set the commission ceiling from it. Send samples only to creators with fit and category sales. Rank creators by revenue per 1,000 views (MCR-0046). In GMV Max, one agency sets the ROI target a point above TikTok's suggestion and trims promotion-day boosts (ADC-0062); never set it below break-even. Gate on stock: selling out is fine only if restock takes two weeks or less (ORG-0038).

**Judge it by:** profit per sale after all TikTok costs, a commission ceiling, a sample cap and a stock gate. Weak work copies a "standard 20%" and celebrates GMV.

**Breaks when:** margins cannot carry 15 to 20%; products that do not demo on video; TikTok Shop is closed in the market; claim-limited categories.

**From:** ORG-0036, ADC-0062, MCR-0046, ORG-0038

### 9. Keep organic posts all give and let paid ads carry the ask, until posts reach steady views of about 5,000

**Why it works:** For most stores organic does not win new customers alone. New customers are almost always rented, 50,000 followers rarely means 50,000 views a post, and owned channels mostly drive returning revenue (ORG-6425). Organic's job is trust: one operator reports that prospects who check a profile of helpful posts after an ad convert better, at roughly 3 organic pieces to 1 ad in their feed (ORG-0003).

**How to apply:** Give each post one job: reach, trust or sale. Keep calls to action off until posts reach steady views of about 5,000 (about 2,000 in narrow niches), then add a lead magnet (ORG-0014). Tell the owner in plain numbers what organic can and cannot do. A US supplement brand plans 12 helpful posts a month beside 4 live ads, so the profile shows proof, not promos.

**Judge it by:** one job per post, and forecasts in reach, trust and returning revenue, not new customers. Weak work promises organic will replace ads, or ends every post with "shop now".

**Breaks when:** there is no paid budget, so a soft call to action may be the only path; established accounts can sell from day one; a large community that does bring new buyers.

**From:** ORG-6425, ORG-0003, ORG-0014

### 10. Pick one hero platform plus email for six months, and post natively from a creator-style account set up in the selling country

**Why it works:** Each platform rewards native, original posts and learns who to show an account to; a small team spread across five platforms trains none. One programme found creator accounts set up in the target country, posting natively, out-reached brand accounts and schedulers (ORG-0013).

**How to apply:** Choose the platform where buyers are densest, plus email to own the audience. Treat platforms as islands: funnel only to owned channels, never platform to platform (ORG-0022). Post natively in the app, 2 to 3 minutes per post, not through a scheduler (ORG-0013). Make each cross-post a real edit: since 2024 Instagram swaps reposts for the original in recommendations unless the content is significantly changed (TechCrunch). A Canadian outdoor brand picks Instagram plus email, posts from its founder's profile, and adds YouTube Shorts after month six.

**Judge it by:** one hero platform with a reason from buyer data, a six-month window and native posting. Weak work sends one file to five platforms by scheduler.

**Breaks when:** teams with proven formats and staff to repurpose cheaply; short launches worth the reach cost; TikTok Shop features that need the brand account; platform changes.

**From:** ORG-0022, ORG-0013, https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators

### 11. Post 30 to 50 videos in a row on one topic for one buyer, and keep 3 or 4 niche pieces for every broad one

**Why it works:** The algorithm learns who to show an account to from what it posts, and broad viral hits muddy that match (ORG-0023). Niche pieces get fewer views but more buyers: one operator's case-study videos drew about a third of the views of his broad ones, yet more of his customers had watched them (ORG-0001).

**How to apply:** Check the last 20 posts: one topic, one buyer? If not, plan the next 30 to 50 on it. In each block of four or five, aim three or four at the buyer's exact problem and one at a wider interest. Judge niche posts on sales and leads, not views. If 70 to 80% of views still come from followers, rewrite hooks for strangers rather than posting more (ORG-0029). An Australian running store posts 30 videos on shin pain for new runners first.

**Judge it by:** one buyer and topic, a niche-to-broad ratio and the follower-share metric. Weak calendars rotate holidays, memes and product shots.

**Breaks when:** mass products whose buyer is nearly everyone; established accounts the platform already classifies well; niches whose topics are used up.

**From:** ORG-0023, ORG-0001, ORG-0029

### 12. Start with 2 or 3 expert formats at 4 to 6 posts a week for a month, then turn the winner into a named series

**Why it works:** Formats compound where single posts do not: viewers know what they will get and the team makes it fast. In one B2B test, one of five formats doubled engagement and, run twice a week, drove over half of inbound leads within two months (ORG-0011).

**How to apply:** Pick 2 or 3 formats (story, ranking, breakdown, scenario) as video or carousel, post 4 to 6 times a week, and judge after a month (ORG-0015). Name the winner as a series; split it to its own account only if both stay active. Show the product in every episode, like daily "training somewhere new" posts for a portable gym (ORG-0018). For a low-interest product, a one-off prop used as a recurring viral vehicle can beat monthly shoots (ORG-0012). Set volume from the team's real hours; an abandoned series looks worse than none.

**Judge it by:** a format list with a test window and a named series showing the product. Weak work has a new idea every day.

**Breaks when:** impulse products where entertainment beats expertise; teams that cannot keep the pace; props with no link to the product.

**From:** ORG-0015, ORG-0011, ORG-0018, ORG-0012

### 13. Show the topic being done in the first frame, and keep on-screen hook text to 5 to 8 plain words

**Why it works:** Viewers decide in a fraction of a second whether a video is for them. In 1,300 viral Reels, hook text averaged 7.7 words, mostly 5 to 8, below fifth-grade reading level, with returns falling past 12 words (ORG-0016). Unrelated B-roll loses people before they read anything (ORG-0017).

**How to apply:** Run the half-second test: can a stranger tell from the first frame what the video is about? Show the lifting, the eating, the problem. On cold content, keep the product and brand name out of the first seconds, then show it before you name it (MCR-6737, MCR-6693). Cut hook text to 8 words, swap any word a newcomer would not know, and test a negative version (stop, avoid, never), which beat positive wording in that set (ORG-0016). Write captions at grade 5 to 8 (ORG-0024). A UK coffee brand opens on a wince at a bitter sip under "Bitter coffee has one cause".

**Judge it by:** first frames that show the subject, hook words counted, a negative variant. Weak work opens on logos, slow pans or a 15-word sentence.

**Breaks when:** expert niches where technical words signal relevance; calm luxury voices that cannot carry negative hooks; retargeting, where naming the product early is right.

**From:** ORG-0016, ORG-0017, MCR-6693, MCR-6737, ORG-0024

### 14. Read each post by its goal: sends and shares for growth, comments and story views for selling, retention for both

**Why it works:** Instagram's head said in January 2025 that watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach matter most, with sends weighing slightly more for reaching non-followers (Social Media Today). Comments and story views show buying interest among existing followers (ORG-0019).

**How to apply:** Tag every post with its job. For growth posts, track sends and shares per reach and the share of views from non-followers. For selling posts, track comments with buying words, story views and code use. For all posts, read the hold past 3 and 15 seconds on the retention graph (ORG-6427), and put about 80% of planning effort on the opening seconds, the visual as well as the words (ORG-0019). Volume helps growth: one creator going from 2.5 to 4 posts a day tripled his half-year follower gain (ORG-0020), but only while the hit rate holds. Compare variants posted close together, so timing does not skew the read (ORG-0011).

**Judge it by:** a report that sorts posts by goal and reads the right metric for each. Weak reports average likes across everything.

**Breaks when:** accounts whose sales come from search or saves (Pinterest, YouTube search) rather than shares; Instagram changes its signals, so recheck each year.

**From:** ORG-0019, ORG-6427, ORG-0020, ORG-0011, https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/

### 15. Re-post every winning story at least four times in new formats, changed enough to count as new

**Why it works:** Most followers never saw the winner, and a proven story is cheaper than a new one. The Oodie's founder re-posts each viral story at least four times with a new hook, voiceover or text carousel (ORG-0026). Proven angles keep working when the format changes: one brand ran the same anti-fake angle as a cartoon, AI UGC and an expert explainer (MCR-6756).

**How to apply:** Keep a winners log with the story, hook, format and result. For each winner plan four remakes: a new hook, a new register (casual, flat documentary, first-person diary), a new format (carousel, talking head, demo) and a new platform cut (MCR-6697). Change the first frame and the edit, since near copies can be treated as duplicates and lose reach. Turn the best ads into organic posts too, with rights checked asset by asset.

**Judge it by:** a winners log and four distinct remakes per winner. Weak work uploads the same file again, or never reuses a winner at all.

**Breaks when:** remakes stay too close to the original; second or fan accounts that look like impersonation or hide that the brand runs them; stories worn out in a small niche.

**From:** ORG-0026, MCR-6756, MCR-6697

### 16. Turn comments into sales with a comment-keyword DM that sells in one message, inside Meta's messaging limits

**Why it works:** A keyword reply shows exactly who opened, clicked and bought, so non-buyers can be followed up by name; one creator's example showed about 98% open and 37% click rates (ORG-0021). Lead-magnet Reels kept collecting emails 6 to 8 months after posting (ORG-0028).

**How to apply:** Through Meta's API a business cannot start an Instagram DM (ORG-6365), and it gets one private reply per comment within 7 days, with more only after the person answers, within 24 hours of that answer (ORG-6364). So write that one reply to work on its own: the answer, the price, the link and one reason to buy now. Use a unique keyword per post so each is tracked. Attach a free resource to one or two Reels a week and connect sign-ups to a welcome flow. Keep prices public on the post.

**Judge it by:** a single reply that stands alone, a unique keyword, a tracking line and a welcome flow. Weak work replies "check your DMs" and then sends a second unasked message.

**Breaks when:** high-ticket buys that need a conversation; audiences that find comment bait spammy; Meta changes the windows, so recheck the live page.

**From:** ORG-0021, ORG-0028, ORG-6364, ORG-6365

### 17. Build each channel's route to the store on its real link and shopping rules, checked on the day

**Why it works:** A plan that ends in a dead link loses every buyer it earned. Links typed in YouTube Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable; a Short can carry one related video link instead (ORG-6402). Product tagging on YouTube needs the Partner Program and a channel not set as made for kids (ORG-6403).

**How to apply:** Write the route per platform: a Short to a related long video with a clickable link, or the channel's profile links (up to 14). Instagram collab posts take up to 5 collaborators on feed posts, Reels and carousels, not Stories (ORG-6404). Pinterest catalogs need a business account, a claimed site with the tag, clear shipping and refund policies, and a feed with 7 required fields plus item_group_id for variants (ORG-6400). Pinterest favours keywords in titles and boards, weekly fresh Pins and 2:3 images, with no set engagement window (ORG-6401). Check each rule on the help page the day you build.

**Judge it by:** a clickable route per channel, with the rule's source and check date. Weak work writes "link in bio" everywhere.

**Breaks when:** the market lacks the feature (catalogs or YouTube Shopping not offered).

**From:** ORG-6402, ORG-6403, ORG-6404, ORG-6400, ORG-6401

### 18. In live selling, run many short shows until a crowd forms, and use $1 starts only with 300 or more viewers at once

**Why it works:** Live auctions price on the crowd. With few viewers, low starts sell stock below value; with a crowd, they pull bidding up. A reseller with 11 followers sold a first item in about 15 minutes by running short same-day shows (ORG-0005).

**How to apply:** Start with several 15-minute shows rather than one hour. Schedule big shows 1 to 3 weeks ahead and promote them three times: a week, 2 to 3 days and a few hours before (ORG-0008). Make about 20% of lots high-value $1 anchors only when you expect 300 to 500 viewers at once (ORG-0007). A buyers-only giveaway breaks even when it lifts the average bid by prize cost divided by lots: a $250 prize over 250 lots needs $1 per lot (ORG-0006). Check each live price for profit, and script the ad label at start and end.

**Judge it by:** show length, promotion dates, anchors tied to expected viewers and a giveaway break-even. Weak plans run one long show with blanket $1 starts.

**Breaks when:** full-price brands whose margins cannot survive auction-level discounts; brands protecting price across channels; local giveaway rules.

**From:** ORG-0005, ORG-0008, ORG-0007, ORG-0006

### 19. Label every post with a material connection, gifts and commission included, with a plain "Ad" at the start

**Why it works:** Hidden ads break the law and the trust. In the US any brand link, free product included, needs plain words in the post; platform tools alone and tags like "collab" are not enough (ORG-6405). The advertiser must train and monitor the people it rewards (CXP-6303). Regulators check: an EU sweep of 576 influencers found 97% posted commercial content but only 20% labelled it consistently (Law Society Gazette).

**How to apply:** Write the label into every brief, gift note and contract. In video it goes on screen and spoken, early, inside the video (MCR-6060). The UK's joint ASA, CAP and CMA guide asks for "Ad" upfront and treats gifts and affiliate links as ads (ASA). Endorsements must reflect honest views and real use (MCR-6025). Check each post when it goes live. Labels for Indian audiences follow their own rules, so send those jobs to Ishita (ishita-india-commerce).

**Judge it by:** label wording in every brief and a check on every live post. Weak work relies on #collab, #sp, the toggle alone, or a buried label.

**Breaks when:** an unasked, unrewarded customer post needs no label; rules differ by country and change.

**From:** ORG-6405, CXP-6303, MCR-6060, MCR-6025, https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/influencers-guide.html, https://www.lawsociety.ie/gazette/top-stories/2024/february/influencers-face-probes-after-eu-wide-sweep/

### 20. Buy each usage right separately with an end date, and keep a register before any creator or customer content runs

**Why it works:** In the US the person who makes a work owns its copyright unless it is a work made for hire (ORG-6421). A platform permission is not a licence: a creator can grant partnership ad access while the contract allows less (ADC-6081). Ads that run past their rights invite takedowns and claims.

**How to apply:** List the uses the plan needs (organic repost, paid ads by platform, website, email) and the period. Price each separately with an end date. Hold a register row per asset: who, what, where, until when, and a screenshot of the yes. For customer photos, ask in exact words and record the exact reply; paid ad use is a separate ask. Unused earned authority is waste: Gorilla Mind used a Joe Rogan clip in only 3 ads and never ran paid on a 5-million-follower creator (MCR-6625).

**Judge it by:** a register with end dates that the media buyer checks before launch. Weak work treats "they tagged us" as permission.

**Breaks when:** content made by employees within their duties; other countries' copyright and publicity rules, where local counsel decides; a creator's face and voice raise separate rights.

**From:** ORG-6421, ADC-6081, MCR-6625

### 21. Sign long authority partners only from people who already use the product daily, and pay so the cost falls as you grow

**Why it works:** Audiences spot a paid face who never uses the product. IM8 signs only athletes already using its product daily, on deals of at least 3 years with equity (ORG-0039). Pay that shrinks with growth protects margin: IM8's ambassador and royalty share fell from 14% of revenue to 6.2% (BRD-6486).

**How to apply:** Split creators by job: a few authority partners for trust, many volume creators for content and codes. For authority partners, use equity, declining royalties or profits interest units, which only share in growth above today's value; Ridge did this with MKBHD (ORG-0034). Take the outline to a lawyer and an accountant. Name the risk: demand that sits on one person carries that person's risk, as when Coty sold a beauty stake for $74M four years after paying $200M (BRD-6492).

**Judge it by:** proof of daily use, a multi-year term, a cost that steps down and a named key-person risk. Weak work signs the biggest name available for one post.

**Breaks when:** early brands whose equity is too valuable to give; slow growth where royalties never step down; regulated health claims made through the partner.

**From:** ORG-0039, BRD-6486, ORG-0034, BRD-6492

### 22. Build launches on a waitlist with product rewards for referrals, and sell out only when restock takes two weeks or less

**Why it works:** Referral rewards that grow with each friend turn early fans into a sales force. Harry's got over 100,000 emails in a week, 77% from referrals, with shave cream and razors as rewards (ORG-0030). A sell-out creates proof and a restock moment: Bloom did $1.36 million in 12 hours on restock day after one email, SMS and Instagram push (WLD-0012).

**How to apply:** Offer product rewards at set tiers, not cash or discount codes, which draw fraud and junk emails. De-duplicate sign-ups. Keep the programme if referrals pass 30% of sign-ups (ORG-0030). Build anticipation with founder posts and a first-come giveaway: David Protein launched after months of founder posting, a 5,000-bar TikTok giveaway and a 40,000-person waitlist (ORG-0038). Check stock and lead time before you push.

**Judge it by:** reward tiers, the 30% keep-or-cut rule, a de-duplication step and a restock date. Weak work offers 10% off per friend and launches with no stock plan.

**Breaks when:** there is no launch moment to gate; long lead times turn a sell-out into weeks of stockout; fake sell-out or restock claims mislead buyers; founders have no existing credibility.

**From:** ORG-0030, WLD-0012, ORG-0038

### 23. Pitch press only with a true angle, in under 200 words, to writers who cover it, on their lead times

**Why it works:** Editors are flooded and block senders who waste their time. In Cision's 2026 survey of over 1,800 journalists, 82% call relevance a must-have and 53% reject pitches that are too promotional (Cision). Muck Rack reports that 69% of journalists prefer pitches under 200 words.

**How to apply:** Name the angle in one line: a launch, a restock with a waiting list, a real milestone, or a clear recipient and price band for a gift guide. Pick each writer by a recent piece on the topic, and say which piece in the first line. Pitch print holiday gift guides from July, since most glossy holiday print issues close by September; start online outreach in October, when guides run until Christmas (Shift). Hold proof for every claim and follow email law. Show each outlet's actual quote under its logo on the site, since bare logos read as fake (LPG-0037).

**Judge it by:** writers with a recent matching piece, a one-angle pitch under 200 words, and a proof sheet. Weak work blasts a release to a bought list.

**Breaks when:** there is no honest news yet, so build some from store data first; trade press norms differ.

**From:** LPG-0037, https://www.cision.com/resources/articles/why-do-journalists-ignore-your-pr-pitch/, https://muckrack.com/blog/2025/07/09/state-of-journalism-2025-webinar-faqs, https://www.shiftcomm.com/insights/gift-guide-pitching-christmas-in-october-and-september-and-august-and-july

### 24. Find stories that are non-obvious but true: a misconception, a frustration or a fake, each with its proof

**Why it works:** Earned attention goes to surprise, and the surprise must be provable. Peter Rahal's test: if an idea is obvious, it is not good; David bars took on boiled cod on protein per calorie (BRD-0021). A misconception, a frustration or a counterfeit problem makes a story: the Oodie's counterfeit callout drove over $1M in sales (ORG-0027).

**How to apply:** Mine reviews, tickets and store data for one true fact rivals miss, and sort it into one of the three types: a misconception buyers hold, a frustration they share, or a fake they could be fooled by. Write the story as one line a stranger would repeat. Stunts work only when they act out the benefit, such as a mattress brand paying people to sleep, which drew 170,000 applications with no ad spend (WLD-0005). Run the same story as an organic post, a pitch and an ad, so one proof pays three times.

**Judge it by:** one story of a named type, with its proof attached and the three uses planned. Weak work invents an award or a "first ever" claim, or pitches a product launch with nothing surprising in it.

**Breaks when:** comparative claims invite legal challenge; strict claims categories; a counterfeit callout where there is no real fake problem.

**From:** BRD-0021, ORG-0027, WLD-0005

### 25. Get talked about where AI answers look: comparison threads in problem subreddits and the "best X for Y" pages assistants cite

**Why it works:** Assistants have no experience of their own, so they repeat what others say, often from listicles, review sites and Reddit (AEO-0029). The one steady pattern in Gamma's tracking was being discussed by others and compared with something else; Reddit moved ChatGPT most, while Google's AI answers followed normal SEO (AEO-0027, AEO-0024).

**How to apply:** Run your five category prompts (for example, best running shoes for flat feet) in each assistant and log the cited pages. Fix those first: review profiles, the roundup editors (make a factual case to be added) and the cited threads (AEO-0014, AEO-0029). In subreddits where the buyer or the problem lives, post as a named, open brand member, framed as a comparison with a rival you chose on purpose, and go narrow ("best X for Y") before broad (AEO-0027). Repeat one corrected fact across site, founder videos and articles; over two years that shifted what ChatGPT said (AEO-0017).

**Judge it by:** a sheet of prompts and cited pages, a fix per page and disclosed posts. Weak work posts "our product is great" or uses fake accounts.

**Breaks when:** no active subreddits; communities hostile to brands; undisclosed posting, which breaks Reddit rules and FTC guidance; engines change sources within months, so recheck.

**From:** AEO-0029, AEO-0027, AEO-0024, AEO-0014, AEO-0017

### 26. Judge community and loyalty work on reorder rate against a control group, not on member count

**Why it works:** Communities look good on members and likes, but the money is in second orders. Huckberry's content-engaged customers show 3.5x the lifetime value of ad or affiliate customers, though much of that is who they are, not what the content did (ORG-0031). Gymshark grew through ambassadors, events and community and runs a single-digit net margin, so the style is costly (ORG-6426).

**How to apply:** Start with the top 50 customers by lifetime spend: a note, a founder video or early access. Expand to the top 200 only if 90-day reorders beat a matched control (WLD-0007). For groups, price every perk against gross profit, keep joining opt-in and phone numbers private, and compare members' reorder rate with similar non-members. Word of mouth spreads when others see the product in use and asking about it feels normal (BRD-0007); for private products, build a referral or review engine instead.

**Judge it by:** a control group and a reorder target named before launch. Weak work reports member growth and engagement only.

**Breaks when:** too few repeat buyers for a control to mean anything; one-off purchases; gestures that turn routine and lose their surprise.

**From:** ORG-0031, ORG-6426, WLD-0007, BRD-0007

### 27. When sales track the founder's posting, turn it into a system before reach fades: hire, centralise footage and track launch views

**Why it works:** Founder content is cheap and credible, and a founder ad needs no audience; one of the best Blue Sense has seen came from a founder with zero followers (MCR-6760). But reach fades and key-person risk lowers the sale value. Gorilla Mind's launch videos fell from about 500,000 views to 300,000, 170,000 and then 100,000 before it moved weight to paid and retail (ORG-6424).

**How to apply:** Track views on each launch video as the fade signal. When sales follow the founder's posts, hire someone who already gets views, centralise all footage and pay editors per view with bonuses (OPS-0005). Batch the founder's time: 10 hooks times 3 bodies gives 30 ads from about 5 minutes of recording (MCR-6773). Tell the founder story through the viewer's own problem, not the founder's biography (MCR-6771). Founders planning an exit should keep the brand from resting on their face (FIN-0018).

**Judge it by:** a fade chart and a handover plan. Weak work assumes the founder will post forever.

**Breaks when:** the founder is the product and audiences reject a substitute; channels still growing fast; per-view pay that rewards reach without sales.

**From:** MCR-6760, ORG-6424, OPS-0005, MCR-6773, MCR-6771, FIN-0018

### 28. Pre-approve a trend budget and a 36-hour turnaround, and use AI only to remix ideas people wrote

**Why it works:** Trends die fast, about 72 hours in cosmetics, so sign-off chains kill them (OPS-0018). Hyper-timely posts pay off for about 24 hours (ORG-0025). Fake or generic AI content costs trust and can break the rules.

**How to apply:** Agree a fixed budget per trend (one brand used about $1,200), a standby creator and a do-not-touch list, so the team can act without leadership sign-off (OPS-0018). Keep a library of first-person ideas and approved language written by people, and use AI to repeat, remix and reformat it (ORG-0025). Use AI video to act out a problem, never to fake a demo or testimonial (MCR-6809). Disclose realistic synthetic content on YouTube (MCR-6064) and expect Meta's AI info label when it detects AI signals (MCR-6063). Fake reviews and testimonials, AI-made ones included, are banned in the US (MCR-6045).

**Judge it by:** a standing trend rule with budget and turnaround, a human idea library and AI disclosures. Weak work chases every trend with off-brand AI clips.

**Breaks when:** regulated categories where every asset needs review; trends whose tone clashes with the brand; brands with no proven format, which trends will not fix.

**From:** OPS-0018, ORG-0025, MCR-6809, MCR-6064, MCR-6063, MCR-6045

### 29. Buy cheap reach only with caps: clip programmes on a capped CPM budget and early sponsor reads with rising creators

**Why it works:** Some reach costs less than ads when the price is capped and paid only for approved work. Clipping marketplaces pay per view against a capped budget (for example $5,000 at about $0.85 to $1 CPM), only for approved posts, even if one goes viral (ORG-0037). Ridge bought 30-second mid-roll reads across thousands of YouTube channels at about $5 to $10 CPM plus product, aiming to be each rising creator's first sponsor (ORG-0032).

**How to apply:** For clip programmes, give clippers the footage, a do-not-say list and the label rule, and approve each clip before it counts. For sponsor reads, pick growing channels whose viewers fit, pay a small flat fee, and track with a code and a survey answer. Compare the cost per 1,000 with your paid CPM and roll out the cheapest that sell.

**Judge it by:** a cap, an approval step and a CPM set against paid. Weak work signs open-ended per-view deals or buys only top-tier creators.

**Breaks when:** regulated categories where third-party claims are a risk; niche products that sell to only one interest group; sponsor markets inflated by a hot category.

**From:** ORG-0037, ORG-0032

## Working from the org context

- Read the buyers' own words first: reviews, tickets and DMs. Every hook, brief angle and pitch line starts from a phrase a buyer used, quoted in the brief.
- Read the voice and reading level. Creators keep their own delivery, but must-say facts and claims come word for word from the approved claims list.
- Read the design system for covers, series templates and text-safe zones. The brand's own posts look like the brand at a glance; creator content does not need to.
- Read what ran and won: past outliers, past creator results by profit, past codes. Relaunch winners before inventing new ones.
- Read the offers and margins. Gross profit per order and landed cost cap every fee, gift and commission; if they are missing, get them before pricing anything.
- Read stock. Never push a creator round, live show or pitch on a product without cover through the coverage or launch window.
- Read the markets. Where the store sells sets creator country, accents, labels and which features are open (TikTok Shop, YouTube Shopping, Pinterest catalogs).
- Where the brand runs everything from its brand page or posts only polished content, propose one change with one number to watch, such as a share of spend on partnership ads or trial reel pre-tests. Never change the voice without the owner's yes.
- Write back what the job learned: formats that won, creators who paid, phrases that hooked, so the next job starts from them.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Does every creator, gift round and series have a profit verdict rule, gross profit against full cost at 30 and 60 days?
- [ ] Is every fee and commission capped by gross profit per order and set against the store's own paid CPM?
- [ ] Are creators shortlisted on audience country, median views and reach to strangers, with boost and follower-share flags?
- [ ] Does each brief fit on one page with one concept, must-say facts word for word, banned claims and the label line?
- [ ] Does each post have one job (reach, trust or sale) and the right metric for that job?
- [ ] Is there one named hero platform, with the reason taken from buyer data?
- [ ] Does every first frame show the topic, with hook text of 5 to 8 words?
- [ ] Are calls to action kept off posts that have not reached steady views?
- [ ] Is there an outlier rule (about 5x normal views) and a handoff to paid with rights checked?
- [ ] Is partnership ad access paid as a flat fee, with must-spend partnership ads in their own campaign?
- [ ] Are the pages AI assistants cite for the category logged, each with a planned fix?
- [ ] Does every route to the store work under that platform's current link and shopping rules?
- [ ] Do DM replies stand alone in one message inside Meta's windows?
- [ ] Is the ad label plain and upfront, spoken and on screen in video, for gifts and affiliates too?
- [ ] Does every reused asset have a register row with use, place and end date?
- [ ] Is stock checked through the coverage, live or launch window?
- [ ] Do pitches carry one true angle, stay under 200 words and go to writers with a recent matching piece?
- [ ] Are community and loyalty results read against a control group?
- [ ] Does every fact, number and claim come from the brief, the org context or a cited source?
- [ ] Does the work read in the brand's voice at its reading level?

## Common failures

- Ranking creators by followers or views. Fix: rank by gross profit per 1,000 views, with new and returning buyers split.
- Paying rate-card fees. Fix: price off the store's own CPM and median views, capped by profit per order.
- Word-for-word scripts that make creators sound like ads. Fix: brief the concept and the facts, then let them talk.
- Posting the same file everywhere through a scheduler. Fix: one hero platform, native posting and real edits for each cross-post.
- "Link in bio" and "shop now" on every post. Fix: give first, build each platform's real route and use one-message keyword DMs.
- Labels missing or buried (#sp, #collab, the toggle alone). Fix: "Ad" upfront, spoken and on screen.
- Treating a tag or a platform toggle as permission. Fix: written rights per use, each with an end date.
- Promising that organic will replace paid. Fix: say what organic does: trust, free tests and returning revenue.
- Mass press releases with no angle. Fix: one true angle, under 200 words, to matched writers on their lead times.
- Generic AI captions and fake-looking demos. Fix: a human idea library, AI only to remix, disclosure where required.
- Judging a community on members. Fix: reorders against a control group.
- Posting "our product is great" on Reddit or from unnamed accounts. Fix: open brand posts framed as comparisons, in the subreddits where the problem lives.
