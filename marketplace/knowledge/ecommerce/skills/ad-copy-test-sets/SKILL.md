# Ad copy test sets

**You get:** three to five complete ad copy sets for one concept, each answering a different buying objection, with a compliance log and a test plan for the first two sets, ready to load into Meta, Google or TikTok.

A copy set is a full argument for buying. This skill writes three to five of them for one concept, each on a different structure, so a split test compares real alternatives and the store learns which argument sells.

## When to use

- A concept or visual is ready and needs copy variants that differ in argument, not word swaps.
- A winning visual has tired copy and the store wants new arguments on the same image.
- A health, beauty or supplement store needs copy that will pass platform review and its own claims check.
- The store wants matched copy for Meta, Google and TikTok from one facts file.

## Not for

- Headlines only: use `headline-angle-set`.
- Picking the concept, buyer group or offer: use `store-meta-concepts`.
- Video scripts with scenes: use `ai-creator-style-video-ad` or `mechanism-explainer-video-ad`.
- Email calendars: use `full-funnel-30-day-plan`.

## What you need

**Required**

- The concept: product SKU, buyer group, angle, offer, destination URL, awareness stage.
- A facts file: approved claims with source and date. If none exists, draft one from the live product, policy and about pages and the label or spec sheet, and ask the owner to approve it once.
- Customer words: reviews CSV (rating, title, body, reviewer name, date, verified flag); survey answers and pre-sale support questions if any.
- Brand voice: five real lines from the site, emails or best ads. Optionally rate the voice on three scales from 1 to 10: formal to casual, quiet to bold, data-led to feeling-led. If missing, use middle values and say voice was not calibrated.
- Gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase.

**Optional**

- Meta export by ad, last 90 days (ad name, primary text, headline, spend, CTR link, purchases, cost per purchase) to see which arguments already ran.

**Access.** Level 1, read. Level 2 only to load paused drafts, with a yes. Never publish.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money known.** Gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase exist; offer copy has its own break-even at the discount. Fail: run `store-meta-math`.
2. **Facts file approved.** Fail: mark every claim "pending approval" and do not hand copy over as ready.
3. **Test cash.** Each set tested needs about 3 times target cost per purchase [ADC-0039]. Fail: test two sets only.
4. **Tracking.** Meta purchases within about 20% of Shopify paid-social orders over 30 days (practitioner default, test it). Fail: give each set its own UTM content value and read Shopify.

## Steps

### 1. Rank the objections before writing

From reviews, survey answers and pre-sale questions, list what nearly stopped buyers: price, doubt it works, taste or feel, sizing, delivery time, trust in a new brand. Copy the exact phrase for each [AVT-0007] and rank by frequency. Each set answers one objection; one ad cannot handle five [MCR-0043].

### 2. Assign a different structure to each set

| Structure | Shape | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Problem to fix | Situation, what they tried, why this works, proof, ask | Buyers who know the problem, not the product |
| Proof first | Dated review count or quote, what it means, product, ask | Stores with strong, dated proof |
| Objection answer | The doubt in the buyer's words, the answer, the guarantee, ask | Considered buys; warm audiences |
| Mechanism | Cause, how this product works, proof, ask | New or unusual products; the why, not an ingredient list [MCR-0032] |
| Versus the usual way | The common option, where it falls short, what this does instead | Crowded categories; compare with "the usual way" unless a named-rival fact is provable |
| Identity | A scene the buyer recognises, little body copy, image carries it | Gifting, fashion, premium |
| Offer | The deal, what it is worth, a deadline only if real, ask | Live, priced offers only |

Pick three to five that fit the facts file and stage. Skip proof first if proof is thin; skip offer if no offer is live.

### 3. Write each set in full

Per set: headline, subhead or link description, body (short and long), call to action, and the objection and facts rows it uses.

- **Headline and subhead work as a pair:** the subhead adds something new. If the headline is feeling-led, the subhead carries the proof; if the headline is proof, the subhead carries the feeling.
- Open with curiosity, proof or a promise, not the pain every rival names [LPG-0045].
- Each testimonial is a real, dated review answering one objection; a generic "love it" earns no space [LPG-0053]. Never label a quote "verified" unless the review app marks it so.
- **Each body leads with its own proof** (the review, mechanism, label fact or scene that answers its objection). A price or spec line appears in at most 2 bodies; headlines and descriptions can carry it. Bodies that share one string test less and can group as one ad [MCR-0078].
- One awareness stage per set [MCR-0056]; the call to action matches it: softer ("See how it works") early, direct ("Shop now") late.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" lines [MCR-0016]; no launch openers ("Introducing", "Meet"); no hype words (game-changer, revolutionary, unlock); no all-caps sentences.
- Match voice by ear: five brand lines beside five of yours; fix any that sound like someone else.
- AI remixes approved language; people own the first-person ideas [ORG-0025].

### 4. Fit to the platform

| Field | Limit to write to |
|---|---|
| Meta primary text | about 125 characters before "See more"; first line carries hook, product and proof |
| Meta headline and description | about 27 characters each recommended, not a hard cap |
| Google responsive search ad | headlines 30, descriptions 90 characters; no exclamation mark in headlines |
| TikTok ad text | up to 100 characters |
| Button | from the platform's list (Shop Now, Learn More, Sign Up); in-copy asks can be freer |

Count with a script and show the count. Check the current spec before handing over.

### 5. Run the compliance pass

A line fails if it:

- Uses a claim not in the approved facts file, or changes its number or date.
- Names or implies a personal trait of the viewer (health condition, weight, money trouble), which Meta's personal attributes policy rejects.
- Claims to cure, treat, prevent or heal a disease, or names a condition the product is not approved for. Fear-based trend claims need evidence the product addresses that cause [MCR-0004].
- Says "clinically proven", "dermatologist tested" or "doctor recommended" without the study or statement on file.
- Uses a countdown, stock count or "selling fast" line that is not true today.
- Uses before and after imagery or wording that the platform restricts for health and weight products.
- India: breaks the ASCI code or the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act list; cosmetics may not make drug claims.
- US: lacks substantiation, or presents an invented or paid testimonial as an ordinary customer's.

Rewrite failing lines to what the source says, or cut them. This is general practice, not a legal ruling; the owner's adviser signs off borderline lines.

### 6. Score and pick the two to test first

Score each set 0 to 2 on: objection rank, proof strength, fit with the destination page, voice match, clean compliance. Test the top scorer against the set whose structure differs most from what already ran. Name the one variable the pair isolates (structure, objection or tone) and keep everything else equal.

### 7. Write the test plan and the learning rule

- Sets run on the same visual in one ad set, or as variations on one body; 2 to 4 at a time at modest budgets [MCR-0013].
- Budget per set: 3 times target cost per purchase over at least 7 days [ADC-0039] [ADC-0064]. Impressions alone cannot settle a purchase test.
- **Size the read bar to the budget.** Purchases a set can reach = its test budget / target cost per purchase. Any bar you write must sit at or under that. If not, read on a cheaper upstream event (cost per add to cart) or pool sets that share the variable, and say so [ADC-0066]; confirm on purchases before rewriting other creative [MCR-0029].
- Read at ad set level on 7-day click [ADC-0059]; check Shopify orders by UTM.
- Most tests are flat; only about 10 to 20% of well-run tests find a clear winner [CRO-0012]. A tie means keep the simpler copy.
- Write what each outcome would mean: if proof wins, lead future creative with proof; if mechanism wins, buyers want the why. Confirm with a second test before rewriting everything.

## Checks before you hand it over

1. Three to five complete sets, each on a different structure and objection.
2. Every claim maps to an approved facts row with source and date.
3. Every set passed the compliance pass or its failing lines were fixed or cut.
4. Every line shows its count and fits its placement; every set has a call to action.
5. Every testimonial is real, dated and answers a named objection.
6. The first pair isolates one stated variable; test cost fits the test cash.
7. Each body opens on its own proof; no price or spec string sits in more than 2 bodies.
8. Every read bar is reachable at its budget, or the plan names the upstream or pooled read.
9. Nothing is live; paused drafts only with a yes.
10. A plain one-pass draft would not beat the top set on proof and clarity.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** concept, the two sets to test first, the variable isolated, test cost, read date, claims struck.
2. **Objection table:** rank, objection, customer phrase, review date, facts row that answers it.
3. **Copy sets:** set ID, structure, objection, proof that opens the body, headline (chars), subhead (chars), body short (chars), body long (chars), call to action, facts rows, score.
4. **Compliance log:** line, rule, verdict, rewrite.
5. **Test plan:** set, ad set, daily budget, purchases reachable (budget / target cost per purchase), read metric, UTM content value, start, read date, keep or cut rule, what a win means.
6. **To confirm:** short list for the owner.

## Common mistakes

- **Five versions of one argument.** Steps 1 and 2 force different objections and structures.
- **Invented proof or a fake "verified" tag.** Steps 3 and 5 allow only approved facts and real reviews.
- **Urgency that is not true.** Step 5 strikes it.
- **A disease word in skincare copy.** Step 5 catches it.
- **Judging on a thousand impressions.** Step 7 reads purchases.
- **Calling a flat test a win.** Step 7 sets the tie rule.
- **One price and spec line in every body.** Step 3 caps it at 2.
- **A read bar the budget cannot reach.** Step 7 sizes it first.
