# Ad opener writer for an online store

**You get:** a ranked table of openers (spoken line, on-screen text, first visual, voice and delivery notes) for an ad body that already sells, with recording sheets per speaker and a test plan, saved as one file.

## When to use

- A video or static has a body that sells but the opening fails to stop people.
- A winner is tiring and needs new openers on the same body.
- Briefing a creator, the founder or a voice artist on what to say first and how.
- The team wants to learn which voice its buyers trust: customer, founder or expert.
- Before a production day, to have openers ready for every body being shot.

This skill covers two jobs that are one store task: written openers by tactic, and spoken openers by voice style.

## Not for

- Designing the first frame itself: use `first-frame-design`.
- A full creator script: use `creator-video-script`.
- Deciding whether the opener or the body is the problem: use `video-ad-retention-fix` first.
- Primary text and headlines for the ad unit: use `store-meta-concepts`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The body** the openers will sit on: script or transcript, length, and its first line after the opener.
2. **Product, angle and buyer** for this ad, from the brief.
3. **Claims allowed and quote bank** from `store-meta-brand-dna`.
4. **Who can speak:** a roster of real people available (customers who agreed to appear, creators with rights, the founder, staff experts), with what each has actually done with the product.

Optional but used for ranking: **opener history**, an ad-level export with Ad name, 3-second video plays, Impressions, Amount spent and store orders by ad ID, with each past opener's tactic and voice tagged (or tagged in step 1).

Missing 4: write lines for the founder and a narrator only, and mark customer lines "needs a real customer". Missing history: rank on the default order in step 5 and say so.

**Access:** level 1 (read). Recording, uploading or launching needs a yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money.** *Pass:* the product has gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase from `store-meta-math`. *Fail:* write openers, but the test plan in step 7 has no budget.
2. **Proven body.** *Pass:* the body is at or under target cost per purchase, or its hold rate is at or above the account median. *Fail:* opener volume only multiplies a weak ad [MCR-0048]; fix the body first.
3. **Truth.** *Pass:* every claim an opener could use is on claims allowed. *Fail:* drop the claim, not the opener.
4. **Capacity.** *Pass:* the team can record and cut the number of openers asked for. *Fail:* deliver the top 5 only.

## Steps

### 1. Learn from the store's own openers

Tag each past opener by tactic (surprise, question, claim, call-out, story) and voice (customer, founder, expert, narrator, friend). For each tactic and voice with at least 3 openers that spent 3x target cost per purchase, record median hook rate and store cost per purchase. Read hook rate within placement groups only.

### 2. Plan the three levers

An opener is three parts that can be tested apart: the spoken line, the on-screen text and the visual [MCR-0028]. For direct-response video the words carry most of the weight [MCR-0063]; for music-led Reels or visual products the picture does, so lead with `first-frame-design` there.

### 3. Write the batch

Write 15 to 18 openers: at least two per tactic, spread across at least four voices. Rules for every line:

- **Pull buyers, not everyone.** The opener should make the right buyer stay and the wrong one leave [MCR-0026].
- **Bridge to the body.** The body's first line must follow naturally; an opener that does not bridge raises stops and lowers sales [MCR-0013].
- **Short.** On-screen text 5 to 8 words; past 12 words performance falls [ORG-0016]. Spoken lines of 2 to 3 seconds, about 6 to 9 words at a normal pace (practitioner default, test it).
- **Plain words** at about a grade 5 to 8 reading level [MCR-0063].
- **No pattern copy.** No "it's not X, it's Y" lines [MCR-0016].
- **No condition claims.** Never imply the viewer has a condition ("your hair loss"); name the situation instead [MCR-0020].
- **Unfamiliar problems** need a fast reason to care: a prevalence line or a list of signs, but only with a real source [MCR-0018].

Tactics, in plain terms:

| Tactic | What it does | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise | Shows or says something unexpected that links to the product | Must connect within a second |
| Question | Asks what the buyer already wonders | Not a yes or no question they can answer and leave |
| Claim | States one specific, provable result | Only from claims allowed |
| Call-out | Names the buyer and situation | Situation, never a condition |
| Story | Starts mid-moment in first person | Only a true story from a real person |

### 4. Match each spoken line to a real voice

| Voice | Who records it | Delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | A real customer, in their words, on a phone | Natural pace, small pauses allowed |
| Founder | The founder, or a proxy if they will not appear [MCR-0005] | Slower, direct to camera, one take |
| Expert | A staff or partner expert with real credentials | Measured, one number or fact |
| Narrator | A voice artist, third person | Even pace, no first-person claims |
| Friend | A creator with rights, casual | Quick, warm, as if texting |

A customer or creator line may only claim what that person has done. For each spoken line give pace (slow, medium, quick), the word to stress, and where the one-beat pause goes. One voice per ad from start to finish.

**Audio previews:** any text-to-speech tool can make rough internal previews so the team hears pace before recording. Mark them "internal scratch". Never run a synthetic voice as a customer or the founder, and follow the platform's AI disclosure rules for any synthetic voice that does run.

### 5. Rank the batch

Rank by the store's history from step 1: tactic and voice with the lowest store cost per purchase first, hook rate as tie-break. With no history, use this default order and test it: call-out and claim first for buyers who know the problem, story and surprise first for those who do not (practitioner default, test it).

### 6. Check the batch for repetition

- No two openers share their first three words.
- No word other than the product name appears in more than a third of openers.
- No two openers make the same promise in different words.

Rewrite any line that fails.

### 7. Write the test plan

New body: 2 to 4 openers live at once, up to 8 [MCR-0013]. Reviving a tired winner: more than 3, cut by a different person [MCR-0048] [MCR-0047]. Each opener variation sits in the body's ad set; read on store cost per purchase at 3x target, not hook rate alone [MCR-0076]. To learn which voice buyers trust, test across voices first, then across lines within the winning voice.

## Checks before you hand it over

1. 15 to 18 openers, every tactic used at least twice, at least four voices.
2. Every claim is on claims allowed; no line implies a condition.
3. Every spoken line is assigned to a real person who can say it truthfully.
4. On-screen text is 8 words or fewer; spoken lines fit about 3 seconds.
5. The repetition check passes.
6. Ranking cites store history or says it used the default order.
7. Every opener bridges into the body's first line.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** the top 5 openers, why they rank there, and the test to run first.
2. **Opener table:** Rank, Tactic, Voice, Spoken line, On-screen text, Visual, Seconds, Pace, Stress, Pause, Speaker (real person), Claim used, History signal.
3. **Recording sheet** per speaker: their lines, delivery notes, takes needed.
4. **Test plan:** Body, Openers live, Ad set, Budget, Read rule.

Save as `OPENERS-<product>.md`.

## Common mistakes

- **Openers that stop everyone and sell to no one.** Step 3 writes for buyers.
- **Lines a speaker cannot honestly say.** Step 4 assigns each to a real person.
- **Ten versions of one sentence.** Step 6 checks repetition.
- **Ranking by gut.** Step 5 ranks by the store's own results.
- **New openers on a body that does not sell.** The proven-body gate stops it.
- **Synthetic voices passed off as customers.** Step 4 keeps previews internal.
