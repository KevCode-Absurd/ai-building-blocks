# Story-led advertorial page

**You get:** a fast, story-led advertorial HTML page in the store's brand, with images, optional Shopify template files, a claims check file and a beliefs table, ready for review and a test against the product page.

Some products need explaining before anyone will buy: a new kind of fix, a problem people have given up on, a product that looks like many others. A product page cannot do that for a cold visitor. A story can: one person's problem, what they tried, what finally worked. This skill builds that page from a true story, labelled honestly.

## When to use

- The product needs about three minutes of explanation before a cold buyer would consider it [LPG-0004].
- The winning ad tells a story or opens a question, and the product page loses those visitors.
- Buyers have tried and failed with other fixes and doubt anything works [AVT-0009].
- An angle has proved itself on a plain product page and now needs a page for colder traffic [LPG-0030].

## Not for

- A short numbered case for the product: use `reasons-to-buy-listicle-page`.
- Fixing the product page: use `store-meta-landing`.
- Writing the ads: use `store-meta-concepts`; ad and page are planned together (step 8).
- Copying a rival's advertorial: never. To rebuild a structure the client likes, use `page-layout-rebuild`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The true story and its teller**, one of: the founder's own account; a real customer's account from an interview or long review; or a real expert who uses or recommends the product. Each with signed consent to use their name, words, photo and results on an advertising page.
2. **The product**: page URL, price, real bundles and gifts, stock, guarantee, delivery and returns.
3. **Brand files**: logo, colours, fonts, voice, from `store-visual-identity-sheet` or `store-meta-brand-dna`, or read from the live site and confirmed once.
4. **Proof**: reviews export, test or lab documents, press quotes with source, numbers with source.
5. **Gross profit per purchase**, from `store-meta-math`.

Optional: `product-claims-profile` output, support tickets, the ad that will send traffic, the fixes buyers tried before.

Access: read (level 1) to build. Upload to a duplicate theme or unpublished page needs draft access (level 2). Publishing needs the client's yes.

Missing input: ask once. With no true story, do not invent one or a persona. Offer two honest routes: interview 3 to 5 customers (30 minutes each, consent form first), or write in the founder's own voice about why the product exists.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Truth gate.** Every person, event, quote, number and result is real and documented. No composite or invented narrator, no stock photo passed off as the person, no expert who has not agreed. Fail: stop; get a real story.
2. **Consent gate.** Written consent from every named person, kept on file.
3. **Labelling gate.** "Advertisement" or "Sponsored by [brand]" shows at the top in plain words. No fake news mastheads, publication names or comment sections. The FTC's native advertising guidance (US), the CAP Code (UK) and ASCI guidelines (India) all require ads to be recognisable as ads, and ad platforms reject misleading landing pages; check the current text for the market.
4. **Typical-results gate.** If the story's result is better than most buyers get, say what typical buyers get, or pick a more typical story.
5. **Money gate.** Gross profit per purchase known, so the page is judged on profit per visit.

## Steps

### 1. Choose the teller by the buyer's obstacle

| Main obstacle | Teller and format |
|---|---|
| Low trust in the category | The founder's story: why it had to exist |
| "Will it work for someone like me?" | A real customer's story |
| Needs permission or expertise | A real expert explains what they see and recommend |
| Too many options | A buyer's guide comparing honest choices |

Choosing by obstacle follows [LPG-0003]. Commit to one editorial identity for the whole page; a sales letter wearing a news headline converts as neither [LPG-0044].

### 2. Find the story's spine

From the interview, in the person's own words: the moment the problem was worst (a scene, not a summary); the fixes they tried and why each failed [AVT-0009]; the cause they finally understood; how they found the product and what happened, doubts included; life now.

Name failed fixes by category by default ("three different shampoos", "a gym membership"). Name a real product only for a factual, fair statement the person made; comparative claims about named rivals carry legal risk.

If the product works on a cause others miss, name it and map each product feature to it, or the page reads as a scam [LPG-0028]. List the 4 to 6 beliefs a reader must hold before buying ("the problem has a cause", "past fixes missed it", "this works on it", "the risk is low"), and check the draft earns each [LPG-0057].

### 3. Write it in order

Adapted from several practitioner structures [LPG-0024] [LPG-0025] [LPG-0052]:

1. **Opener:** a short label line, then a headline built on curiosity, proof or a promise, without the product name; pain alone does not open, because every brand in a category shares the same pains [LPG-0045]. Byline with the real teller.
2. **The problem**, first person, as a scene.
3. **What they tried**, a short list plus why nothing worked. Kept to this one section.
4. **The real cause.**
5. **The discovery**, set apart in a box: the first reaction and 3 or 4 specific things that stood out, signed by the teller.
6. **How it works**: 3 or 4 short callouts on ingredients, materials or process, each tied to the cause.
7. **Proof**: the story's result, then other real reviews with names and photos where consented, each answering one doubt.
8. **Who it is for, and who it is not for**: three honest lines each.
9. **The offer**: single item and real bundles as checkout shows them, savings as money amounts [LPG-0035], with guarantee, delivery and returns under each button.
10. **FAQ**, 6 to 8: time to results, price, safety, delivery, returns, who it suits.
11. **Close**: keep doing what has not worked, or try this with the risk removed [LPG-0054].

Where cards differ on what opens (some structures open on pain [LPG-0025]), curiosity, proof or promise opens and pain comes in section 2 [LPG-0045].

Length follows the ad: an ad that barely sells needs a longer lead before the product appears, about half the page; a long-copy ad that already sold the idea needs a short one [LPG-0029]. Practitioner default, test it: 1,200 to 2,000 words.

### 4. Edit for flow

- Short sentences, plain words; advertorial copy works at a low reading level [LPG-0047].
- Mark every sentence where the story stops and the pitch starts; each is a likely exit; rewrite it inside the story [LPG-0012].
- Buttons: soft wording ("See if it is right for you", "Check today's offer"), at least two, the first after the product is introduced, plus the close [LPG-0011]. A sticky button appears only after the product is introduced, never during the problem [LPG-0013]. A small "skip to the offer" text link near the top is fine for returning visitors.
- Any deadline or scarcity must be true.

### 5. Build the page

One HTML file with inline CSS, brand colours and fonts as variables in one place, an article layout that still shows the label and brand name, simple line icons, and real photos with consent. No scroll animations that keep text hidden until it moves. Images in WebP under about 200 KB; first image eager [SPD-0002] [SPD-0006]. Optional Shopify page template on a duplicate theme.

India and similar markets (practitioner default, test it): cash on delivery and delivery time near the buttons, written in the language of the ad.

### 6. Check against proof and rules

Line by line: each claim, number, quote and result has a source on file; health and beauty claims follow the ad and legal rules; the typical-results line is present where needed; mobile speed meets Core Web Vitals good levels (LCP 2.5 s or less, CLS 0.1 or less).

### 7. Revise

Up to two rounds after the client reads the draft; after that, change only what the client marks as wrong.

### 8. Plan the test

The ad starts the story and the page continues it; advertorials beat product pages for cold traffic only when the two match [LPG-0026]. Run the same ad to the advertorial and to the product page; judge on gross profit per session. Watch the click-on rate to the product page or checkout (30 to 40% is one practitioner benchmark [LPG-0002]). Size it with `store-meta-test`.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every person and event is real and documented, with consent on file.
- "Advertisement" or "Sponsored" shows at the top; no fake mastheads or comments.
- Every claim and number has a source; atypical results carry a typical-results line.
- Named rival products appear only in factual, fair statements.
- One editorial identity; pitch breaks marked and rewritten.
- Offer matches checkout; the page passes the speed levels.
- The test against the product page is planned with a profit metric. Nothing is published without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary first:** teller and format and why, consent status, the beliefs the page earns, the offer, the test plan, and any assumption that changes the page (one line each, at most three).

**Files:** `advertorial-[product].html`, `images/`, optional Shopify template files, and `claims-check.csv` (Line, Claim, Source, Status).

**Table "Beliefs":** Belief, Section that earns it, Proof used.

## Common mistakes

- **Inventing a narrator or expert.** The truth gate stops it.
- **Hiding that it is an ad.** The labelling gate requires a plain label.
- **Opening on pain everyone shares** [LPG-0045].
- **Repeating the failed fixes in every section.** They live in one section.
- **Pitching too early** [LPG-0012].
- **A page that does not match the ad** [LPG-0026].
