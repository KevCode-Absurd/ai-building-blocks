# Collab drop with another brand

**You get:** a partner shortlist with scores and evidence, a pitch message and one-page proposal for the top three, a term sheet checklist, a unit economics table for both brands, and a dated drop plan (stock, page, ads, emails, measurement). Nothing is sent, signed or ordered without the client's yes.

## When to use

- New-customer cost through ads is rising and the store wants a cheaper route to a fitting audience.
- A brand with the same buyer but a different product has suggested working together.
- The store wants to test a new flavour, scent, colour or design without adding it to the core range.
- A season or event needs a fresh, limited reason to buy.

## Not for

- Paying creators to post: use `plan-influencer-gifting-campaign` or `run-creator-partnership-ads`.
- Instagram account collab posts only: use `grow-instagram-with-collabs`.
- Pitching press: use `pitch-store-to-press-and-gift-guides`.
- Selling to a retailer or corporate buyer: use `build-wholesale-price-list-and-terms` or `corporate-gifting-sales-pitch`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Gross profit per unit** for the products that might go in, from `build-sku-profit-sheet`.
2. **Who the store's buyers are:** survey answers, top locations, age range from analytics, what else they buy (post-purchase survey or reviews).
3. **Stock and cash** available for a drop.
4. **Candidate partners:** the team's own ideas plus brands buyers mention in surveys and reviews.

Optional: partner list sizes and follower counts (public), past collab results, minimum order quantity for a custom product from the supplier.

Research needs no access. Sending pitches needs the client's approval of each message. Contracts, purchase orders and money moving between brands stay with the client.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Goal named:** new customers, a product test, or profit. A collab can be worth it for awareness and testing even when profit is small [WLD-0011]; say which before scoring partners.
2. **Margin room.** Both brands must earn something per unit after the split, or the drop needs a stated marketing budget. Fail: change the format or price.
3. **Stock and cash.** The store can fund its share of stock without breaking the cash plan. A custom product's minimum order can make a one-off unaffordable [WLD-0011]. Fail: use a bundle of existing products.
4. **Rules.** Each brand's trademarks are used only with written permission; customer data is shared only where buyers consented; joint claims must be provable by the brand making them.

## Steps

### 1. Build and score the shortlist

List 8 to 15 brands. Score each 1 to 5:

- **Shared buyer, different product:** same person, no competing product.
- **Audience size:** larger than the store's is better; collabs with larger partners tend to sell out [WLD-0011]. Use public follower counts and, later, list size they share.
- **Price band fit:** similar price per item, so the bundle does not look odd.
- **Values and look:** would both sets of buyers see it as natural?
- **Ability to deliver:** stock, a team, a track record of shipping on time; reviews mentioning late delivery lower the score.
- **Evidence buyers want it:** mentions in the store's surveys or reviews.

Keep the top three. Reject any partner with claims or quality problems in public reviews.

### 2. Choose the format, cheapest first

1. **Joint bundle of existing products** sold on one or both sites. Lowest risk.
2. **Gift swap:** each brand gives the other's sample with orders over a line.
3. **Limited co-designed variant** (a colour, scent, print): only if the minimum order fits the cash and the expected demand.
4. **Content or event collab:** no product, shared audience only.

### 3. Work out the money for both sides

For the bundle or variant:

- Price to the buyer; each brand's delivered cost; who fulfils and the shipping cost; payment fees; returns.
- Transfer price: what the selling brand pays the other per unit (often their wholesale price).
- Contribution per unit for each brand, and break-even units if any budget is spent (photos, ads, packaging).
- Cash each brand puts in and when it comes back.

If one side earns nothing, rebalance the transfer price or the format before pitching.

### 4. Pitch

Find the founder or partnerships lead by name. Message under 120 words: why their brand (one specific reason), what the buyer gets, the format, rough numbers ("we would sell the bundle on our site and pay your wholesale price per unit"), what each audience gains, and a simple next step. Then a one-page proposal: format, timing, roles, money, measurement. For US email pitches, follow CAN-SPAM even though it is business to business [EML-6385].

### 5. Agree the terms

Checklist for the client and their lawyer (this is not legal advice):

- Format, products, quantities, price, transfer price and payment timing.
- Who holds stock, ships, handles returns and support.
- Trademark use, artwork approvals, and who approves every asset.
- Customer data: each brand emails only its own list; buyers' details pass to the other brand only with consent.
- Exclusivity (if any), dates, cancellation, unsold stock.
- Tax invoices between the brands (GST in India; sales tax handled by the seller of record in the US).

### 6. Plan the drop

- **Fixed quantity, real end:** a limited run sold until gone works when demand is real [WLD-0009]; any "limited" line must be true, with stock records to prove it [ADC-6147].
- **Page:** a dedicated landing page built from the collab shoot with the story of why the two brands teamed up [LPG-0068].
- **Ads:** run partnership ads through both brands' handles, with permissions granted in the app first [ADC-6081]. Ads from a relevant personal or partner handle can beat the brand page when that profile has fitting content [ADC-0003].
- **Email and social:** each brand sends to its own list on the same day; shared posts.
- **Stock:** if it sells out, decide now whether a second run is possible; selling out is fine when restock is quick, otherwise collect sign-ups for the next drop [ORG-0038].

Dated plan: proposal agreed, samples, shoot, page draft, ads in review, launch day, last day, after-drop read.

### 7. Measure

- First-time buyers from the drop (Shopify customer type), and how many came from the partner's audience (partner code, landing page source, post-purchase survey [MEA-0045]).
- Contribution from the drop, and 60-day repeat of new buyers.
- Decide: repeat with this partner, try the next one, or stop.

### 8. India and US notes

- **India:** check both brands can invoice each other with GST; COD on a limited drop raises RTO risk, so consider prepaid-only for the drop and say so. Festival timing (Diwali, Raksha Bandhan) suits gift bundles.
- **US:** the seller of record collects sales tax; check product insurance and labelling for any new co-made item.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every shortlisted partner has scores with evidence.
- The format chosen is the cheapest one that meets the goal.
- Both brands earn a stated amount per unit, or a budget is agreed.
- Term checklist covers stock, shipping, returns, trademarks, data and approvals.
- Every "limited" claim matches stock records.
- Measurement of new customers from the partner is set before launch.
- Nothing was sent, signed or ordered without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** top partner and why, format, expected units and contribution for both brands, cash needed, launch date, how success is judged, runner-up partner in one line.

**Partner shortlist:** Brand | Product | Shared buyer 1-5 | Audience size 1-5 | Price fit 1-5 | Values 1-5 | Delivery 1-5 | Buyer evidence 1-5 | Total | Evidence links | Contact.

**Unit economics:** Item | Buyer price | Our cost | Their cost | Transfer price | Shipping | Fees | Our contribution | Their contribution | Units planned | Break-even units.

**Pitch message and one-page proposal** for the top three. **Term checklist.** **Drop plan:** Date | Task | Owner | Approval needed.

## Common mistakes

- **Picking a friend's brand, not a shared buyer.** Step 1 scores fit with evidence.
- **Custom product first.** Step 2 starts with a bundle.
- **One side earns nothing.** Step 3 checks both.
- **Swapping customer lists.** Step 5 keeps each brand to its own list.
- **Fake "limited".** Step 6 ties claims to stock.
- **No way to count new customers.** Step 7 sets codes and survey options before launch.
