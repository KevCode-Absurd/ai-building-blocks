# Brand voice guide

**You get:** a voice guide of about four pages: 3 to 4 traits with rules and measurable markers, a use and avoid word list, a tone-by-moment table, and before-and-after examples for six channels, plus a one-page cheat sheet a writer or AI tool can follow on the first try.

## When to use

- Ads, emails and the website sound like different companies.
- A new copywriter, agency or AI tool is starting and needs rules, not adjectives.
- New positioning has been agreed and the copy must follow.
- The store's copy reads like every rival's or like a machine wrote it.

## Not for

- What the brand says (promise, pillars, proof): use `write-brand-positioning-and-messaging` first.
- Cleaning one batch of machine-sounding copy: use `machine-copy-scrubber`, which uses this guide.
- Colours, fonts and image style: use `store-visual-identity-sheet`.
- Writing ads in the other languages your buyers use: write them with a native speaker, with this guide as input.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The store's copy:** home page, two top product pages, five recent emails, ten recent ads (text), recent WhatsApp or SMS messages, support macros. Live URLs or exports.
2. **Five to ten lines the owner loves and five they dislike** from that copy, with why.
3. **Buyer words:** reviews export, interview summaries or support chats, at least 50 comments.
4. **Positioning:** statement and pillars, or the owner's one-paragraph version marked draft.

Optional: two brands (any category) whose voice the owner admires, and one to avoid; rival copy samples.

Access: read only (level 1). Rewriting live copy is a later job, drafted at level 2 with the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Positioning agreed.** Pass: a statement exists. Fail: draft it with the owner, or run `write-brand-positioning-and-messaging`; voice without a message is decoration.
2. **Real samples.** Pass: at least 20 pieces of the store's own copy across three or more channels. Fail: a new brand uses the founder's recorded speech and buyer words as its sample, and the guide is marked "test".
3. **Who owns sign-off** on voice is named. Without an owner, guides drift.
4. **Rules that outrank voice.** Platform rules, claims law and channel limits beat any style choice. Note them now (step 6).

## Steps

### 1. Collect and sort lines

Pull 40 to 60 real lines. Sort into loved, fine and disliked using the owner's picks, and add buyer lines that sound like the brand at its best. Keep buyers' exact words; tidied paraphrases lose the vocabulary copy needs [AVT-0007].

### 2. Measure the loved lines

For the loved set and the disliked set, measure:

- Average sentence length and the spread (short and long mixed, or all the same).
- Reading grade (any free readability checker; aim within grade 6 to 9 for most store copy, practitioner default).
- Person: we and you, or third person.
- Contractions, questions, exclamation marks, emoji per 100 words.
- Local language mix: US, UK or Australian spelling, and any local words or mixed language your buyers use.
- Jargon and claim words.

The gaps between loved and disliked lines become the rules.

### 3. Name 3 to 4 traits as "this, not that"

Each trait gets:

- A name in plain words (for example "straight-talking").
- **This, not that:** "straight-talking, not blunt"; "warm, not gushing".
- A marker someone can check: "no sentence over 20 words in ads"; "one emoji at most per message"; "we and you, never the company".
- One loved line as the model.

Write in the buyer's dominant feeling where it fits the trait (relief, pride, frustration with past fixes), naming the real alternatives buyers tried rather than vague ones [AVT-0010].

Give every trait and rule a confidence label from its evidence, and list the sources beside it:

- **Strong:** three or more lines back it, from at least two kinds of source (for example site copy, emails and buyer reviews).
- **Emerging:** one or two lines, or a pattern you inferred.
- **Weak:** a single source, or sources that disagree.

Weak rules go into the guide as proposals, not rules. Where sources disagree (the site is formal, the founder's emails are playful), do not average them: log it as an open question with what you found, your recommended resolution and the reason, and the decision you need from the owner, so the owner only confirms or overrides. Open questions that block a trait come first.

### 4. Build the word list

| Use | Instead of | Why |
|---|---|---|
| buyer's word for the problem | category jargon | buyers search and speak this way |
| plain verb | "leverage", "elevate", "unlock" | stock phrases |

Add banned words: rival tag lines, clichés of the category, superlatives without proof, and any word the platforms restrict for the category.

### 5. Set tone by moment

Voice stays fixed; tone shifts with the moment.

| Moment | Tone | Example rule |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | warm, brief | one thank-you, one next step |
| Launch or sale | energetic, factual | real end date only, no fake scarcity |
| Order delay | plain, sorry, specific | new date, a way to cancel, no jokes |
| Complaint | calm, owning it | say what happened, what we will do, by when |
| Review ask | light, no pressure | ask everyone, never hint at a rating |

### 6. Fit each channel's limits

Limits outrank voice:

- **Meta ads:** carousel guidance is about 80 characters primary text, 20 headline, 18 description [MCR-6041]; put the voice in the first line.
- **Google search ads:** headlines up to 30 characters, descriptions up to 90 [GAD-6140]; Google rejects gimmicky capitals, repeated punctuation, symbols and phone numbers in ad text [ADC-6142], so a playful voice must stay within that.
- **SMS:** under 160 characters and emoji-free where possible [EML-0028]; some countries need sender or template registration first, and the text must then match it.
- **WhatsApp:** template messages must stay within the approved template; service replies can be looser.
- **Email:** keep a plain-text founder style as part of the mix, about one plain email for every three or four designed ones in one agency's mix [EML-0016].
- **Product pages:** facts and proof first; voice in headings and the short intro.

### 7. Write before and after examples

For each of the six channels, take one real line that misses the voice and rewrite it. Show the trait applied and the character count. Never write a line as if spoken by a customer unless it is a real, permitted quote; AI-written lines must not pose as customer reviews or testimonials [MCR-6045].

### 8. Test the guide

- **Blind match:** mix 10 brand lines written with the guide and 10 rival lines; ask three people outside the team to pick the brand's. Pass: most picks right (practitioner default: 8 of 10, test it). If they cannot tell, the traits are too generic; sharpen step 3.
- **First-try test:** give the cheat sheet to a writer or AI tool with one brief; check the draft against the markers. Fix any rule that was misread.

### 9. Market notes

- Languages: decide which of the languages your buyers use get translated copy versus copy written fresh, and where mixed language is allowed; keep prices, payment and returns terms plain in every language.
- Spelling and tone: decide US, UK or other spelling, and how casual the brand is on sale messages.
- Disclosures: decide how the brand writes them (#ad, paid partnership) so they are clear, not hidden, in each market.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every trait has a this-not-that rule, a checkable marker and a real model line.
- Every trait and rule carries a confidence label with its sources; every open question has a recommended answer.
- Rules come from measured gaps between loved and disliked lines.
- The word list includes buyer words with their source.
- Each channel example fits that channel's limits and rules.
- No example invents a customer quote or review.
- The blind match test was run and its result is reported.
- A named owner signs off voice changes.

## Output

**One-screen cheat sheet:** the traits with this-not-that and markers, top ten use and avoid words, tone by moment in one line each, channel limits.

**Voice guide sections:**

1. Who we are talking to (from positioning).
2. Traits table: Trait, This not that, Marker, Model line.
3. Measurements: Metric, Loved lines, Disliked lines, Rule.
4. Word list: Use, Instead of, Why, Source.
5. Tone by moment table.
6. Channel table: Channel, Limits, Voice notes, Before line, After line, Character count.
7. Test results: blind match score, first-try test notes.
8. Open questions: What was found, Recommended answer and why, Decision needed, Blocks which trait.
9. Owner and review date.

Traits table rows also carry Confidence (strong, emerging, weak) and Sources.

## Common mistakes

- **Adjectives instead of rules.** Step 3 adds a this-not-that and a marker to each trait.
- **Copying an admired brand's voice.** Step 2 builds rules from the store's own best lines.
- **One tone for every moment.** Step 5 shifts tone for delays and complaints.
- **Voice that breaks platform rules.** Step 6 puts limits first.
- **Fake customer voice.** Step 7 bans invented quotes.
- **A guide nobody tests.** Step 8 runs blind and first-try tests.
- **Averaging sources that disagree.** Step 3 logs the clash as an open question with a recommended answer.
