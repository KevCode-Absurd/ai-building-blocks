# Campaign photo set

**You get:** a finished campaign photo set for one product, in folders by ratio for site, email, ads and social, with truth and casting sheets, a shot list, a check log, a review deck and a rights register.

A campaign set is a group of lifestyle stills in one look that the site, emails, ads and social all share for a season or launch. This skill locks one product, decides who appears from customer data, plans shots that differ in ways that matter, makes them by the cheapest safe route, and checks every frame before anyone sees it. A beautiful frame of a product that is not quite the real product is worse than no frame, because it looks finished and ships.

## When to use

- A launch, season or sale needs fresh lifestyle images across channels.
- The store's lifestyle photos look like stock or show people its buyers do not recognise.
- Ads need new situations and people, not new background colours.
- An agency, creator or generator needs a brief that can be checked.

## Not for

- Plain product shots for listings and feeds: use `product-image-library-by-channel`.
- Single product shots for Meta ads and whether AI product images are safe: use `store-meta-product-photos`.
- Ad images with copy, price and logo: use `store-meta-ad-images`.
- Video ads with scripts: use `category-ad-formula-teardown` and `store-meta-concepts`.

## What you need

**Required**

- The store folder from `store-knowledge-folder`: products table (gross profit per purchase, stock cover), customer words, buyer groups with counts, change log.
- Real photos of the exact variant: front, back, sides, label close-up, and the product in use (worn, held, applied). Pull them from the store, its social posts and reviews before asking the owner.
- The visual identity sheet, or at least brand colours and fonts.
- The campaign: dates, channels and placements, ratios wanted, budget cap.

**Optional**

- Meta age and gender breakdowns for purchases, last 90 days; Shopify customers by location.
- Post-purchase answers (who it was bought for, the occasion).
- Reference photos the owner loves and one they dislike.

**When something is missing.** If no photo of the product in use exists, ask the owner for a phone photo of it in use against a plain wall before planning any worn or held shot. Without buyer group counts, cast from review self-descriptions and say so.

**Access.** Level 1, read, to plan. Booking a photographer, paying creators or buying generator credits needs the owner's yes and a cap. Uploading anywhere needs draft access and a yes to publish.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **One product, one exact variant, worth featuring.** Name the variant the way the store names it (size, colour, formula). It must be in the Advertise list, highest gross profit per purchase first, with stock cover above reorder lead time for the campaign's length. Fail: swap the product before planning.
2. **A true anchor exists.** Without a real photo of that exact variant, there is no run. Never stand in a different size or colour, a render, a mockup or a lookalike, not even as a placeholder; placeholders end up shipped. If a look cannot be shown truthfully with the anchors held, change the look, not the product.
3. **The budget fits the spend.** One agency's bands: under about $30k a month of Meta spend, founder-made content; $30k to $100k, about 25% of media on production; above that, at least 10% [MCR-0039]. Compare formats on expected gross profit per creative, where stills often win at low ROAS [MCR-0023]. Fail: cut shots, never checks.
4. **Someone signs off.** A named person approves references, picks and the final set by set dates.

## Steps

### 1. Write the product truth sheet

From the anchors: exact format (gummy cubes with sugar dusting, not capsules; name the likely wrong version and rule it out), container, material and finish, closure, label text word for word, colours, real size in a hand or on a table, two or three texture cues that make it read real, and how it is actually used. Script each use with working mechanics: which hand, what grip, what moves. If you cannot picture the physics, rewrite the action.

### 2. Cast from customer data

Before any mood board: for each buyer group, who (life stage, body types), where they use the product, the moment, and the evidence count ("11 reviews mention buying it for a parent"). Repeated self-naming phrases show who to cast [AVT-0016]. Define each group as outcome, demographic and situation [MCR-0050]. Platform breakdowns only check the mix, since they reflect who was shown ads. A niche group's photos only work if its ads, copy and page match it too [AVT-0015]. Cast from the store's customers, never from the people in a reference photo.

### 3. Choose one look, references first

Pick one signature look, two at most. Show the owner the reference photos and a one-page style board (light, palette from the identity sheet, locations, wardrobe, props, camera height, what never appears) and get approval before anything is generated or booked. The owner's written direction on a reference beats your reading of it; record any conflict. References give the photography recipe only; never rebuild another brand's photo so closely that it reads as theirs.

Products others see in use spread by word of mouth, so show them where people notice them when that fits [BRD-0007]. Premium brands can use rough, phone-style frames if the product stays out of the rough frames [MCR-0010].

### 4. Plan shots that differ where it matters

Shot list as a grid of buyer group, situation and product moment (unboxing, first use, routine, result, gifting). When people appear, mix solo frames (where emotion and casting land hardest), pairs and groups. A new background or pose is not a new ad; change the person, angle or situation [MCR-0022], because near-duplicates get grouped and add no reach [MCR-0078]. Mark which shots the current anchors can support; an overhead shot needs an overhead anchor.

### 5. Pick the production route

- **Real shoot:** photographer or the owner's phone, real customers or models, signed releases covering channels and time.
- **Creators:** seed product to creators who match the casting sheet, brief the look and shot list, buy ad usage rights in writing. Expect low yield: one brand finds about two good creators per hundred products gifted and pays them flat fees [ORG-0033]. A large creator's shoot can feed a matching landing page when rights allow [LPG-0068].
- **Generator:** every frame showing the product is anchored on a real product photo; never describe the product from text alone. Paste the truth sheet's format and label lines into each prompt. Once one frame renders the product right, add it as a further reference for the rest. Made-up people appear only as styled models, never as customers, reviewers or results. Follow each platform's AI labelling rules and the FTC rule against fake testimonials; in the UK the CAP Code, and in the EU the unfair commercial practices rules, also ban misleading ads.

A mix is normal; one agency sources roughly a third each from AI-assisted work, creators and in-house [MCR-0037].

### 6. Build every ratio from a wide master

Frame or generate each look wide and tall enough to compose every ratio needed: 9:16 (Stories, Reels, TikTok), 4:5 (feed), 1:1 (feed, catalogue, email), 3:2 or 16:9 (site banners), 2:3 (Pinterest). Recompose rather than crop: the product sits lower in 9:16 with space above it; props that frame 4:5 move in or drop out for 1:1. Never stretch a crop to hit a size, and never extend a background across the product. Keep the product and faces clear of the top and bottom bands Meta's current 9:16 safe-zone guide reserves for interface and captions.

### 7. Check every frame at full size

Before the deck, each frame gets a logged pass or fail, with the truth photo placed beside it and the product region magnified three to seven times:

- **Product:** label words read correctly, typeface and position, colourway, closure, silhouette, material and finish, no invented variant, scale.
- **Hands and faces:** five fingers, real grip and contact, natural joints, clean eyes and teeth.
- **Set and light:** props with real physics, one light direction, shadows that agree, no garbled signs or other brands' logos.
- **Frame:** product whole in every ratio; no duplicate frames across looks.
- **Claims:** nothing implies a result the store cannot prove.

If you are unsure whether the product is right, it is not. Check every delivered frame in every round, not a sample.

### 8. Review deck and revisions

One page per look: contact sheet of passing frames, then recommended picks with the group and channel each serves. The owner picks and comments. Two revision rounds; a third means the brief was wrong, so fix the brief. Never report a fix as done while the old frame is still in the deck.

### 9. Deliver, clips and write-back

Export every pick at every ratio, compressed for the web, named `campaign_look_group_situation_ratio_YYYYMMDD.jpg` so "redo 4" means the same frame in every ratio. Optional live-photo clips of two to five seconds: one small action, no cuts, the ratio set explicitly, any generated audio replaced, labelled as AI motion if made by a tool. Add the look, the truth sheet and what was learned to the store folder's learnings log.

## Checks before you hand it over

1. One exact variant, anchored on real photos, is in every frame.
2. The product is in the Advertise list with stock for the campaign.
3. Every cast group traces to a count in the casting sheet.
4. Every frame passed the logged check at full size, this round.
5. No made-up person is shown as a customer, reviewer or result.
6. Every pick exists at every ratio requested, recomposed, with product and faces whole.
7. Releases and usage rights cover every person, place and channel.
8. Spend stayed inside the cap; nothing went live without a yes.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary first:** the look, the product with gross profit per purchase, groups cast with counts, frames passed and dropped, budget used, what is ready per channel.
2. **Truth sheet** and **casting sheet** (group, who, situation, evidence count, source).
3. **Style board** and **shot list** (shot ID, group, situation, product moment, ratios, route).
4. **Check log** (frame, product, hands and faces, set and light, frame, claims, pass or fail, fix).
5. **Review deck**, **final set** in folders by ratio, and **rights register** (person or creator, release or licence, channels, end date).

## Common mistakes

- **A near-miss product.** Gates 1 and 2 lock the variant; step 7 kills any frame you have to talk yourself into.
- **Generating before references are approved.** Step 3 approves them first.
- **Casting copied from the reference.** Step 2 casts from customers.
- **Variations that are not different.** Step 4 changes person, situation or angle.
- **9:16 made by cropping 4:5.** Step 6 recomposes from a wide master.
- **Checking a sample.** Step 7 checks every frame, every round.
- **No usage rights for ads.** The rights register is part of the output.
