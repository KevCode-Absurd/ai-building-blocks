# Category ad formula teardown

**You get:** an HTML report and ad log of the category's long-running ad formulas, a formula table with confidence and store fit, and adaptation briefs for the top three, ready for a creator or editor.

An ad formula is a format plus an order of beats plus timing: a creator reacts in the first second, names the problem by second three, shows the product at eight, proof by fifteen, offer at the end. When several advertisers keep the same formula live for months, it probably pays. This skill finds those formulas, checks which ones the store already runs, and turns the best missing ones into briefs it can afford.

## When to use

- Before a video or static production round.
- The store's ads use one or two formats and results have flattened.
- The store is entering a new category or market.
- A creator or agency needs a brief based on the category, not on taste.

## Not for

- Rivals' messages, offers and open lanes across channels: use `cross-channel-competitor-map`.
- A Meta-only read of rivals' angles with tests sized from Meta data: use `store-meta-competitor`.
- Scripts and copy for a chosen formula: use `store-meta-concepts`.
- Judging the store's own live ads: use `store-meta-creative-review`.

## What you need

**Required**

- Category, main country, and a rival list (from `cross-channel-competitor-map`, or three to eight names).
- The store folder from `store-knowledge-folder` and allowed claims from `product-claims-profile`.
- The products table with gross profit per purchase, the monthly test budget, and what the team can produce (phone video, creators, editor, designer, hours per week).
- The brief's purpose (whole range, one product, or a sale with its offer and dates) and its stage (cold, warm, or both). Ask once; the answer sets which clusters get most attention.
- The store's own live ads (Ads Manager export or Ad Library page), so the report never recommends what it already runs.

**Free tools used**

- Meta Ad Library: each rival's page and category terms; note start dates, and in the EU and UK also ended ads.
- TikTok Creative Center Top Ads, filtered by region, industry and period; use its figures as shown.

**Optional**

- A paid ad database if the store has one; nothing depends on it.

**When something is missing.** Without a rival list, search category terms in both tools and take advertisers that recur. Without production details, assume phone video and statics only.

**Access.** Level 1, read. The report links to ads in the public libraries; it does not download or re-host rivals' videos or images.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Gross profit per purchase is known.** It sets target cost per purchase and what a formula can cost to make. Fail: deliver clusters without adaptation budgets.
2. **Cash and capacity.** Each adapted formula needs production cost plus a test of about three times target cost per purchase. Fail: keep only formulas the team can make with what it has.
3. **Pool size.** At least 30 ads across at least four advertisers (practitioner default, test it). Fail: widen to adjacent categories and say so; under 15 ads, report examples, not formulas.

## Steps

### 1. Build the pool and rank by run length

Take ads live seven days or more, video and static, live or ended where the library shows ended ones. Run length ranks; it does not filter, because recent ads show what rivals are testing now. Practitioner bands, test them: 7 to 29 days, testing; 30 to 89, likely working; 90 or more, long runner. Long runs can still be cheap brand or retargeting ads, so label run length a proxy everywhere. Study advertisers one or two steps bigger than the store, choosing their longest-running ads, over category giants [MCR-0034]. Add two or three long-running formats from outside the category; borrowed formats are less crowded [MCR-0055]. They do not count toward category clusters.

Collapse one advertiser's near-copies to its longest-running version. The same pattern from different advertisers stays in as separate rows: that is the strongest signal. Skip videos over about two minutes and note the skip.

### 2. Watch the ad, not the caption

The caption is not the hook; the first seconds of the video often say something else. Log every ad from what is seen and heard:

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Format | talking to camera, hands-on demo, product footage with voice-over, founder to camera, before and after, side-by-side, step-by-step, first-person view, reaction, customer clip montage, animation, text static, photo static, carousel |
| Hook: words, text, picture | the first spoken line, on-screen words with word count, what fills the first second (for analysis only) |
| Beats | each with its start second: hook, problem, cause, mechanism, proof, objection, offer, call to action |
| Reveal and benefit | second the product first appears; second the first benefit is stated |
| Call to action | start second; spoken, text, or none |
| Pace and level | cuts per ten seconds; phone, creator, studio, animation, AI-assisted |
| People and sound | creator, founder, customer, expert, none; voice, music only, captions |
| Destination | product page, collection, article, quiz |
| Days live, status | on the capture date; live or ended |

Tiebreakers for format: a face speaking to the camera is talking to camera (founder to camera if the speaker is the founder); product footage with a voice-over is not; a timeline from before to after wins over other labels; numbered steps make it step-by-step. Treat a hook as three levers, the spoken line, the text and the picture [MCR-0028]; most of its pull comes from the opening copy, except in sound-led formats [MCR-0063].

When there are more ads than time, watch a few from every cluster in proportion to its size, plus the longest runners, rather than only the top of the list.

### 3. Cluster into formulas and rate confidence

A formula is a format plus beat order plus reveal band (product in the first 3 seconds, 4 to 10, after 10). It counts with at least three ads from at least two advertisers (practitioner default, test it). Confidence: **high** with several advertisers and long runners; **medium** with fewer or shorter runs; **open lane** when the data hints at a gap but few ads show it. For each formula: ads, advertisers, median days live, length, beats with median timings, production level, stage it suits. Ended ads that died within a month, where visible, go to an "avoid" list with what they had in common.

### 4. Read formulas against what is known

- Few viewers reach the second half; on cold traffic, reveal the product late and state benefits before features [MCR-0077]. Warm audiences take an early reveal.
- After a strong problem hook, a few seconds of mechanism before the product helps problem-aware cold viewers [MCR-0014].
- Viewers drop at abrupt jumps from hook to pitch [MCR-0027].
- Reaction formats need an effect felt in seconds [MCR-0045].
- Rough, fast statics can beat polished video where fresh creative is needed often, but not for products that need motion [MCR-0060].
- On-screen hook text of five to eight words reads fastest [ORG-0016].
- Spoken calls to action on TikTok are contested; test with and without [MCR-0061].
- On TikTok Shop, rank by revenue per thousand views, not views [MCR-0046].

### 5. Compare with the store and score

Write the store's baseline: formulas it already runs and formulas it does not. For each missing formula answer yes or no: the product can show what it needs; the store's allowed claims cover its proof beat; the team can produce it; it suits the brief's stage. Estimate expected gross profit per creative, revenue minus spend minus production cost; cheap formats win at low ROAS [MCR-0023]. Drop any formula with a no.

### 6. Write the adaptation briefs

For the top three: beat sheet with timings from the log's medians, three to five hooks in the store's and customers' words (never a rival's line), the claim for the proof beat, shot list, people, length cuts (for example 15, 30 and 45 seconds), ratios (9:16 first, then 4:5 and 1:1), production route and cost, test budget at three times target cost per purchase, and the keep or cut rule on store-side purchases. Add one line per example ad on how this store would rework it. Recommend one formula first, and the second to add once the first has its read.

### 7. Build the report and write back

HTML, one page per formula: summary card, beat timeline drawn as a bar, three to five example links with days live, status and capture date, store-fit answers, and the brief. First page: the one-screen summary and the store baseline. Last: the avoid list. Every finding shows its capture date and a recheck 90 days on (practitioner default, test it). Append the top patterns, with evidence and date, to the store folder's learnings log.

## Checks before you hand it over

1. The pool meets gate 3, or the report says it is examples only.
2. Run length is labelled a proxy wherever it is used.
3. Hooks were logged from the video, not the caption.
4. Every formula has three ads from two advertisers, with links, status and capture dates.
5. Every timing in a beat sheet is a median from the log.
6. No hook, line, image or claim is copied from a rival; no recommended formula is one the store already runs.
7. Every proof beat uses an allowed claim; production plus test cost fits the owner's cap.
8. Nothing was produced, published or spent.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary first:** the three formulas worth making, why each likely pays (ads, advertisers, median days live, confidence), the store baseline, the one to make first and its total cost, the recheck date.
2. **HTML report** as in step 7.
3. **Ad log** (CSV) with every field from step 2.
4. **Formula table:** formula, ads, advertisers, median days live, length, beat order, median reveal and call-to-action seconds, production level, stage, confidence, store already runs (yes or no), store fit, expected gross profit per creative.
5. **Adaptation briefs** for the top three, ready for a creator or editor.

## Common mistakes

- **Long-running treated as proven profit.** Step 1 ranks by it and labels it a proxy.
- **Briefing from the caption.** Step 2 logs what the video shows.
- **Watching only the top of the list.** Step 2 samples every cluster.
- **Recommending what the store already runs.** Step 5 starts from its baseline.
- **Copying a rival's hook.** Step 6 writes hooks in the store's words.
- **Polish the budget cannot pay for.** Step 5 compares expected gross profit per creative.
- **Locking in to a paid database.** Every step works with the free libraries.
