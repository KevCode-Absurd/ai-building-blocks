# Comment and DM reply rules

**You get:** a one-page rule card, a triage table for nine message types, about twenty ready answers in the brand's voice, an escalation list and a weekly check sheet, ready for whoever answers the store's comments and DMs once the client approves them.

## When to use

- Comments and DMs pile up, and buyers' questions sit unanswered under posts and ads.
- Different people answer in different tones, or promise different things.
- A complaint or abuse wave hits a post or an ad and nobody knows what to hide or say.
- A launch, sale or influencer post is coming and comment volume will spike.

## Not for

- Turning DMs into orders with comment-to-DM flows: use `sell-in-instagram-dms`.
- Replying to reviews on review sites and marketplaces: use `reply-to-negative-reviews`.
- Email and helpdesk ticket replies: use `build-support-macros-from-top-tickets`.
- Choosing a helpdesk and setting team response targets across all channels: use `set-up-helpdesk-and-response-times`.

## What you need

Required (read access, level 1):

1. **Last 30 days of comments and DMs**: read in Meta Business Suite inbox, TikTok and YouTube Studio comments; export where the tool allows. Aim for at least 100 messages.
2. **Store policies**: shipping, delivery times, returns, refunds, COD rules, warranty, from the live policy pages.
3. **Product facts**: sizes, materials, ingredients, care, safety warnings, from product pages.
4. **Brand voice**: 5 real replies or captions the client likes.
5. **Team and hours**: who answers, which hours, who handles refunds and escalations.

Optional: top support macros, FAQ page, any past public crisis and how it went.

If the message history is thin (new account), build from policies and the FAQ and revise after 30 days. Replying from the brand's accounts needs publish access (level 3) with these rules as the agreed limits; until the client approves the rule card, draft replies only (level 2). Refunds, credits and legal or safety answers always need a fresh client yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Policies are live and current.** Every ready answer quotes the live policy. Fail: flag the gap and leave that answer for the client.
2. **Owner for money and safety.** Someone named approves refunds, credits and any safety or legal reply. Fail: no answer may promise money or safety outcomes.
3. **Messaging window.** On Instagram, a business may message within the standard 24-hour window after a person's action, up to 7 days after a click-to-message ad, and later only with the human agent tag [ORG-6365]. Pass when the team can reply inside the window. Fail: set the hours so it can.
4. **Privacy.** No order numbers, addresses, phone numbers or payment details in public replies. Fail: rewrite to move to private.

## Steps

### 1. Sort the last 30 days

Read the messages and tag each with one of the nine types in step 2. Count them. The top three types get the most ready answers.

### 2. Set the triage table

| Type | Reply time (practitioner default, test it) | Route | Owner | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales question (price, size, stock, delivery) | Within 1 working hour | Public short answer, then private reply with the link | Social | Answer the question in public so others see it |
| Order problem (late, wrong, damaged) | Within 1 working hour | Private reply; never details in public | Support | Acknowledge in public in one line, solve in private |
| Complaint about product or service | Within 2 working hours | Public acknowledgement, private solution | Support plus client | Never delete, never argue |
| Product, safety or health question | Same day | Public if general, private if personal | Client approves | Quote only the label and product page; no medical advice |
| Hate and abuse | Within 4 hours | Hide or delete; block repeat abusers | Social | No reply; report threats |
| Spam and scams (fake giveaways, fake brand accounts, phishing links) | As soon as seen | Delete, report, warn in a story if buyers were targeted | Social | Never click links |
| Troll or bad-faith argument | Once, or not at all | One calm factual line, then stop | Social | Do not feed it |
| Collab, creator or wholesale request | Within 2 working days | Private | Owner named by client | Send to the right person |
| Praise and customer posts | Same day | Public thanks | Social | Ask to reshare only through `repost-customer-content-with-rights` |

### 3. Write the hide and delete rules

Hide or delete only: slurs, threats, harassment, spam, scams, personal data (yours or the buyer's), and illegal content. Use the platform's own comment filters for slurs and spam words. Never hide or delete an honest complaint, a bad experience, or a question you dislike. Hiding honest criticism makes a store look like it hides problems, and in the US the FTC's review rule bans suppressing negative reviews [MCR-6045]. Treat honest complaints as service, not threats.

### 4. Write the ready answers

Two answers per type (three for the top three types), in the brand's voice, each under 50 words, each with a slot for the buyer's name and the one fact that changes. Rules for every answer:

- Answer the actual question in the first sentence.
- Low effort for the buyer: one step, one link, no "please email us" loops. High-effort service experiences drive disloyalty far more than low-effort ones [CXP-0009].
- Move personal details to a private reply using the comment's private reply option [ORG-6365].
- No promises beyond policy; no discount to make a complaint go away unless the client approved a recovery budget [CXP-0022].
- No medical, legal or result claims beyond what the product page states.
- When a buyer replies in a DM, a person takes over; automation hands off at the first real reply [EML-0031].

### 5. Set up keyword replies, carefully

For selling posts, a comment keyword can send the product link by DM, which also shows who opened and clicked [ORG-0021]. Use only Meta-approved automation tools or the platform's own features, send only to people who commented, and stay within the messaging window [ORG-6365]. Keep a human reply for any comment that asks a real question.

### 6. Write the escalation list

Escalate at once to the client: threats or safety issues; a product causing harm; a legal or regulator mention; press or a large creator complaining; a comment wave of 10 or more complaints on one issue in a day (practitioner default); anything about a data breach. The escalation note says what happened, links, screenshots and a suggested holding reply. No one posts a statement without the client's yes.

### 7. India and US notes

- **India:** reply in the language the buyer used: English, Hinglish, Hindi or a regional language; keep a few ready answers translated by a fluent speaker. Common sales questions: COD available, delivery time to the pincode, GST invoice, size exchange, return to origin. For COD order checks, move to WhatsApp or DM, never ask for the address in public. Do not ask buyers to share UPI or payment details in comments or DMs; payments go only through the store's checkout.
- **US:** keep replies short and plain; do not ask for card details anywhere. If a creator or employee answers questions about the product while promoting it, disclose the relationship [ORG-6405].

### 8. Run the weekly check

Each week, sample 20 replies: was the reply time met, was the route right, was policy quoted correctly, was anything hidden that should not be? Add any new question asked three or more times to the ready answers and send it to `write-faq-from-tickets-and-reviews`. Comments and story replies are also a selling signal; posts with many questions point to content the buyer wants [ORG-0019].

## Checks before you hand it over

- All nine types have a reply time, route, owner and rule.
- Hide and delete rules list exactly what may be removed, and honest complaints are protected.
- Every ready answer is under 50 words, answers in its first sentence and quotes live policy.
- No answer contains order details, a refund promise beyond policy, or a medical or legal claim.
- Automation sends only to people who commented and hands off to a person at the first real reply.
- The escalation list names who is called and what they get.
- Language and COD answers are included for India when the store sells there.
- The client has approved the rule card before any reply is posted under it.

## Output

1. **One-page rule card:** reply times, routes, the hide and never-hide list, the five things never said, and the escalation contacts.
2. **Triage table:** Type, Reply time, Route, Owner, Rule (as in step 2), filled for the store.
3. **Ready answers sheet** (`reply-bank.csv`): Type, Situation, Channel, Public reply, Private reply, Language, Policy line quoted, Approved by, Last checked.
4. **Escalation sheet:** Trigger, Who to call, How, Holding reply, Evidence to send.
5. **Weekly check sheet:** Week, Replies sampled, On time, Right route, Policy correct, Wrongly hidden, New answers added.

## Common mistakes

- **Deleting complaints to clean up a post.** Step 3 protects honest criticism.
- **Asking for order details in public.** The privacy gate and step 4 move it private.
- **Copy-paste replies that ignore the question.** Step 4 answers in the first sentence.
- **Automation that keeps talking after the buyer replies.** Step 4 hands off to a person.
- **Replying to trolls again and again.** Step 2 caps it at one line.
- **Offering discounts to quiet complaints.** Step 4 allows only an approved recovery budget.
- **Missing the messaging window.** Gate 3 sets hours to reply inside it.
