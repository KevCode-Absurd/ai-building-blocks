# Corporate gifting sales

**You get:** a one-page gifting offer with three priced boxes and order deadlines, a 40-company target sheet with the buyer role and reason for each, a pitch email and a LinkedIn note, and a quote template, all drafted for the client to approve before anything is sent.

## When to use

- Diwali, Christmas or year-end is 6 to 10 weeks away and the store wants bulk orders.
- Company buyers have already placed large or repeated orders on the site.
- The store wants year-round employee onboarding or client thank-you orders.
- A one-off corporate enquiry came in and the store has no offer or price bands.

## Not for

- Consumer gifting ads and pages: use `seasonal-gifting-creative-plan` and `build-gift-guide-page`.
- Producing the formal quote, proforma and GST invoice for a won order: use `bulk-order-quote-and-invoice`.
- Follow-ups after the first two touches: use `b2b-sales-follow-up-sequence`.
- Selling to shops that will resell: use `find-and-pitch-retail-stockists`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Shopify order export**, last 12 to 24 months: Name, Email, Billing Company, Shipping Company, Lineitem quantity, Subtotal, Created at, Notes, plus any GSTIN or tax ID field the store captures.
2. **Landed cost per SKU** and **stock on hand**, with supplier lead time for a top-up.
3. **Packaging and kitting costs**: gift box, sleeve, filler, card, logo printing setup and per-unit cost, labour minutes per box.
4. **Shipping quotes**: one bulk shipment to an office versus per-address delivery, for 2 or 3 cities.
5. **Season and delivery date** the buyer will want (for Diwali, the date changes each year; check the calendar).

Optional: past corporate orders and their feedback, branded packaging the store already uses.

If kitting costs are unknown, get one quote from a local packer or time the owner packing five boxes. Research needs read access. Drafting emails in the client's mailbox needs draft access (level 2); sending and any binding quote need the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Gross profit per box.** Pass when each box's price covers contents at landed cost, packaging, kitting labour, branding setup spread over the order, and shipping per address, using delivered-cost thinking [MEA-0037]. Fail: reprice or drop the box.
2. **Capacity by deadline.** Pass when stock plus a top-up order can cover the largest likely order, and the team can kit the boxes per day needed before the last ship date. One late corporate order loses the account the way a missed retail order does [OPS-0022]. Fail: cap total boxes for the season and say so in the offer.
3. **Cash.** Corporate orders tie up stock and packaging before payment. Pass when advances cover packaging and stock bought for the order [FIN-0011].
4. **Rules.** US email outreach follows CAN-SPAM: postal address, opt-out, honoured within 10 business days [EML-6385]. WhatsApp only after a buyer shares their number for this [EML-6320]. India: buyers will need a GST tax invoice with their GSTIN [FIN-6380].

## Steps

### 1. Set the deadline calendar

Work back from the delivery date the buyer wants: last order date = delivery date minus shipping days minus kitting days minus branding print days minus a 3-day buffer. Publish one "order by" date per box type. If branding needs 10 days, the branded option closes earlier than plain boxes.

### 2. Build three boxes

From products the store already makes and has in stock:

- **Starter:** one hero product plus card. US client gifts often sit at or under 25 dollars because that is the most a US business can deduct per client per year; packaging and mailing usually do not count toward it [FIN-6387]. Do not give tax advice; just make one box fit that line.
- **Classic:** two or three products, the default and the one the email leads with.
- **Premium:** fuller set, best packaging, room for a client's own item.

Packaging budget: practitioner benchmarks put unboxing materials at about 2 to 5% of product cost, more for premium [CXP-0008]. Each box has: contents, packaging, customisation options (logo sleeve, printed card, ribbon colour), minimum quantity, lead time.

### 3. Price by quantity band

Per box and band (for example 25 to 99, 100 to 499, 500 plus):

Gross profit per box = price minus contents at landed cost minus packaging minus kitting labour minus (branding setup / quantity) minus shipping per address minus payment fees.

- Price shipping separately: one office drop versus per-address delivery. Per-address delivery can cost more than the contents; never bury it in the box price.
- A lower band price must still pass a floor of at least the store's average online gross profit per order minus its cost per new customer (practitioner default, test it).
- Bands lower the price only because setup and packing costs spread; never discount just to win.

### 4. Find the first 40 companies

Own data first:

- In the order export, filter orders with a company name, a GSTIN or tax ID, a work email domain, or 5 or more units of one item. These buyers already know the product.
- Group by company and rank by spend.

Then add, to reach 40:

- Companies of 50 to 1,000 staff in the store's delivery cities (practitioner default: big enough for a budget, small enough to decide fast).
- Recently funded startups, agencies, consulting and CA firms, clinics, real estate offices, coworking spaces, and event agencies that buy on behalf of clients.

For each, the buyer role: HR or people team (employee gifts, onboarding), admin or procurement (bulk), founder's office or sales head (client gifts). Find a named person from the company site or LinkedIn.

### 5. Offer samples to qualified leads only

One sample box per lead who has a headcount and a date, delivered to the decision-maker. Unrequested samples are mostly wasted [OFF-0057]. Cap samples at about 10% of target companies.

### 6. Write the pitch email

Under 130 words:

- Subject: the occasion and the date ("Diwali gifts for your team, delivered by [date]").
- Line 1: why them (they ordered before, or a reason tied to their team or clients).
- Line 2: the three boxes with price from a quantity, and what customisation is possible.
- Line 3: the real "order by" date and how many boxes the store can take this season.
- Line 4: one true proof (past corporate orders, reviews), only if it can be shown.
- One ask: "Reply with your headcount and budget per person and I will send a quote within one working day."
- Footer: name, store, postal address, opt-out line.

LinkedIn note (under 300 characters): reason, occasion, offer to send the three-box sheet.

### 7. Prepare the quote template

Fields: Quote number and date, Valid until, Buyer company, GSTIN or tax ID, Contact, Box type and contents, Customisation, Quantity, Unit price (ex tax), Tax rate and amount, Packaging and branding setup, Shipping (office drop or per address, number of addresses), Total, Advance due, Balance due, Last date to approve artwork, Delivery date, Cancellation rule.

Practitioner default terms, test them: 50% advance with order, balance before dispatch for new clients; artwork approval in writing before printing; no cancellation after printing starts. India: GST shown separately, tax invoice at dispatch [FIN-6380]. Hand the won order to `bulk-order-quote-and-invoice`.

### 8. Approve, send, track

Show the client the offer, list, emails and quote template. Send nothing until they say yes. Two touches here (pitch, then one follow-up 4 or 5 days later with a photo of a packed box); then hand to `b2b-sales-follow-up-sequence`.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every box at every quantity band shows gross profit after packaging, kitting, branding and shipping per address.
- "Order by" dates work back from delivery with a buffer.
- The season cap on boxes matches stock and kitting capacity.
- The target sheet starts with the store's own company buyers.
- Every email has a reason, the real deadline, one ask, postal address and opt-out.
- No proof, client name or volume claim appears without the client's confirmation.
- Samples are capped and go only to qualified leads.
- Nothing was sent or quoted without a client yes.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** the season, three boxes with price from and gross profit per box, order-by dates, season cap, and the top 10 companies to contact first.
2. **Gifting offer sheet:** Box, Contents, Packaging, Customisation, Price per band (25 to 99, 100 to 499, 500 plus), Shipping options, Minimum, Order by.
3. **Target sheet:** Company, City, Staff size, Source (own orders or research), Past spend, Buyer name, Role, Channel, Reason, Status, Next step.
4. **Messages:** pitch email, one follow-up, LinkedIn note.
5. **Quote template** with the fields in step 7.
6. **Cost sheet:** per box and band, each cost line and gross profit.

## Common mistakes

- **Shipping hidden in the box price.** Step 3 prices per-address delivery separately.
- **Pitching too late.** Step 1 works back from the delivery date and closes branded boxes first.
- **Saying yes to more boxes than the team can pack.** The capacity gate sets a season cap.
- **Cold lists before own buyers.** Step 4 mines the store's orders first.
- **Discounting to close.** Step 3 lowers price only as setup costs spread.
- **Tax promises.** Step 2 mentions the US limit as context; the buyer's accountant decides.
