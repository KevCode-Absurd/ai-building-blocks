# Creator video script for an online store

**You get:** a creator video script table with exact lines, on-screen text and shots, a claims check, delivery notes, a shot list, alternate openers, and a creator brief ready to send once approved.

## When to use

- The store is sending product to a creator and needs a script and brief.
- A customer has agreed to film a video and needs a simple guide.
- The account needs talking-to-camera ads and the team has no template.
- A winning creator ad needs a second version with a different person.
- The store wants to run creator content as partnership ads through the creator's handle.

## Not for

- Openers only, for a body that already works: use `ad-opener-writer`.
- How many creators and videos the account needs each month: use `store-meta-plan`, then come back here per video.
- Checking a finished video before launch: use `prelaunch-ad-check`.
- AI-made presenter videos: use `ai-creator-style-video-ad`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Product facts:** product page URL, price and offer today, what it does, how it is used, how long results take.
2. **Angle and buyer** for this video, from the brief or `weekly-creative-sprint`.
3. **Claims allowed** with sources, and the **quote bank**, from `store-meta-brand-dna`.
4. **Brand voice:** 5 real lines from the site, emails or best reviews.
5. **The creator:** name, handle, audience (country, rough age), how long they have used the product, what they noticed, and two or three of their past videos to read their natural style.

Optional: the store's creative record (which stages and buyer doubts are under-served); past winning creator ads; budget per video.

Missing 5: write for "a customer who has used it for at least two weeks", state the casting criteria the script assumes, and mark experience lines "fill from the creator's own words". Missing 3: no product claims beyond what the product page states.

**Access:** level 1 (read) to write. Sending the brief, agreeing terms or paying needs the client's explicit yes. The creator grants partnership ad access in their own app; never ask for their password.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money.** *Pass:* the product's gross profit per purchase (GPP) and target cost per purchase are known from `store-meta-math`, and fee plus product cost fits the test budget. *Fail:* hold the brief. A $600 video on a product that earns $8 per order needs 75 orders to pay back before any ad spend.
2. **Cash and capacity.** *Pass:* the owner approves the fee and someone can review drafts within 48 hours. *Fail:* gifted or customer videos first.
3. **Experience.** *Pass:* the creator has used the product long enough to say what the script says (practitioner default: two weeks for daily-use products, longer for slow results). *Fail:* the script becomes first impressions only.
4. **Rights.** *Pass:* paid usage terms are agreed in writing before filming. *Fail:* organic use only.

## Steps

### 1. Fix the spine before writing a line

| Spine part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Buyer and moment | One person in one real situation, in review words |
| Want | What they want from it, from the quote bank |
| Awareness stage | One stage [MCR-0056]; prefer the stage the creative record shows as under-served |
| Objections | The one or two real doubts; the demo answers them on camera, not in a caption [MCR-0043] |
| Voice | Customer, creator-as-friend, expert or founder, matched to how sceptical the buyer is |
| Story type | Change over time, confession, routine, or first discovery |
| Shooting style | Handheld selfie, propped phone, or set-up shot; name it |
| Delivery style | Talking points in their own words (default) or exact lines |
| Length | What the concept needs; doubter-to-fan stories often need 45 seconds, a routine can land in 20 |

If a part cannot be filled from evidence, the spine is not ready; ask once.

### 2. Pick one claim and one reason it works

One main benefit from claims allowed and one plain reason. One named mechanism beats a stack of adjectives [MCR-0066]. For cold, problem-aware viewers, give a few seconds of how it works before the product appears [MCR-0014].

### 3. Write the timestamped script

Beat order is fixed; the middle beats stretch to fit the length.

| Beat | What happens |
|---|---|
| Opener (0 to 3 s) | One line that pulls the right buyer, with the product's use visible in frame [ORG-0017] |
| Setup | The situation before and the doubt, in the creator's words; the feeling of being sold stays near zero [MCR-0027] |
| Demonstration | The product in use and a real reaction; it answers the spine's objections; cuts every 3 seconds or so, benefits before features [MCR-0077]; usually the longest beat |
| Honest turn | The true result, said modestly, plus one real limit ("took about a week to notice") |
| Call to action (last 5 to 10 s) | A soft ask, and the offer and risk reducer exactly as on the page today |

Rules:

- Talking points plus 2 or 3 exact lines (the claim, the offer). Contractions; short sentences; about grade 5 to 8.
- One concrete detail from the creator's real use (a time of day, a week count) when it is true; never invent one to sound specific.
- No line in a customer's mouth that no real customer said; no lines labelled "illustrative".
- No jabs at an unnamed "big brand"; compare with the usual way or not at all.
- No "it's not X, it's Y" lines [MCR-0016]; no line implying the viewer has a condition [MCR-0020].
- Read it aloud. Any line that sounds like a polished voiceover gets rewritten until it sounds like a message from a friend.

### 4. Run the claims check

| Line | Claim type | On claims allowed? | Creator experienced it? | Keep |
|---|---|---|---|---|

A line stays only if both pass. Results not yet had become "what I'm hoping for" or are cut. Health, weight, skin and money results need substantiation on file.

### 5. Write delivery notes fitted to the creator

From their past videos, note what they do well (fast energy, calm explaining, humour) and bend the beats to it rather than fighting it. Then: pace per beat, the stressed word in the opener, where the pause goes, camera position per beat, wardrobe, setting, props. Ask for one take with talking points and one fully in their own words.

### 6. Shot list (4 to 8 shots, all 9:16)

| # | Shot | Framing | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opener to camera | Chest up | Product in hand or in frame |
| 2 | The situation | Wide or medium | Real setting |
| 3 | First use | Close, hands only fine | Sound on |
| 4 | Product in use | Close and medium | Two angles |
| 5 | Objection answered | As needed | The proof on camera |
| 6 | The turn | Medium | Only what really happened |
| 7 | Call to action | Chest up | Where to buy |
| 8 | B-roll | Mixed | 10 to 15 seconds spare |

### 7. Three alternate openers and an optional cutdown

Three openers on the same body, each a different tactic (question, call-out, bold claim, story), each labelled with the stage it serves, at least one aimed at a shallower stage than the main opener, so the opener test is also a stage test [MCR-0076]. Offer a 15-second cutdown only if the opener and the payoff still land without the build-up; otherwise say why there is none.

### 8. Creator brief logistics

| Item | Default to propose |
|---|---|
| Deliverables | 1 edited video at 9:16, captions burned in, plus the 3 opener takes |
| Raw files | All clips and B-roll, unedited, original resolution |
| Framing | Subject centred so a 4:5 crop works |
| Usage rights | Paid use on Meta and TikTok for a set term (practitioner default: 6 to 12 months), organic reposting, no resale |
| Partnership ads | Creator grants partnership ad access to the store's page and a TikTok ad code if used; they can carry a large share of spend in some accounts [ADC-0011] |
| Fee | Flat per deliverable, not a share of spend [ORG-0033], set against the creator's recent average views [ORG-0041]; shown as orders needed to pay back at GPP |
| Disclosure | Platform paid partnership label plus a clear tag such as #ad; FTC endorsement rules in the US; ASCI influencer guidelines in India |
| Dates | Ship date, draft due, one revision round, final due |
| Do and do not | Claims allowed only; no competitor names; no medical or income claims |

### 9. India notes

- Offer the script in Hindi, Hinglish or the creator's regional language where the store sells; a person translates the exact-claim lines (practitioner default, test it).
- Mention cash on delivery only if the page shows it, and only after `store-meta-math` confirms refused COD orders still leave the product profitable.
- Shoot in real Indian homes and settings when the buyers are Indian.

### 10. Log it

Add the concept key, spine, creator and date to the store's angle log so the next script does not repeat it.

## Checks before you hand it over

1. Every spine part is filled from evidence.
2. Every claim passed both checks; no invented customer line or detail.
3. Offer, price and terms match the live page today, word for word in the exact lines.
4. The demo answers the named objections on camera.
5. Openers span different tactics and stages; any cutdown is justified.
6. Rights, term, raw files, partnership access and disclosure are written.
7. The fee is shown as orders needed to pay back.
8. Nothing is sent or paid without a yes.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** spine in one line, the claim, length, fee and payback orders, due dates.
2. **Script table:** Time, Beat, Talking points, Exact lines, On-screen text, Shot.
3. **Claims check table.**
4. **Delivery notes, shot list, alternate openers, cutdown or reason none.**
5. **Creator brief** ready to send after approval.

Save as `CREATOR-BRIEF-<creator>-<product>.md`.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing before the spine is set.** Step 1 comes first.
- **Forcing a number into every script.** Step 3 uses a real detail or none.
- **Fake customer lines.** Step 3 bans them.
- **Loose offer wording in the call to action.** Step 3 states terms as on the page.
- **One script for every creator.** Step 5 fits it to their style.
- **No rights or raw files, so the video cannot run as an ad.** Step 8 sets them before filming.
