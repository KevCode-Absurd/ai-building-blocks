# Customer interviews

**You get:** a recruiting message (email and WhatsApp versions), a 30-minute interview script, a one-page summary template for each call, and a cross-interview sheet that turns 12 to 20 calls into counted buying reasons with exact buyer phrases, ready for ads, pages and product decisions.

## When to use

- The team argues about why people buy and nobody has asked them.
- Before a product page rewrite, a new ad batch or a brand positioning project.
- A new product is planned and the store needs to know what job it does for buyers.
- Repeat rate or conversion fell and the data does not show why.

## Not for

- Mining reviews for ad angles without talking to anyone: use `mine-ad-angles-from-reviews`.
- Mapping buying reasons across surveys and reviews at scale: use `buyer-decision-map`, which takes this skill's summaries as input.
- Score surveys after delivery or support: use `run-nps-and-csat-surveys`.
- The "how did you hear about us" question: use `post-purchase-how-did-you-hear-survey`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Customer list** from the Shopify customer and order export: Customer ID, first name, email or phone, marketing consent, first order date, number of orders, products bought, refund or return flag, payment method (COD or prepaid).
2. **The research question** in one line, for example "Why do first-time buyers choose us over the marketplace version?"
3. **A thank-you the store can afford** (see step 2).
4. **A calendar link** and a call tool the buyer already has (phone, WhatsApp, Google Meet, Zoom).

Optional: abandoned checkout export, support ticket tags, review CSV.

Access: drafting the messages is level 2. Sending any message to customers needs the client's explicit yes, the final list and the final wording.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Consent to contact.** Pass: each invitee agreed to marketing or service contact on that channel. In India, WhatsApp messages need the buyer's opt-in and an approved template; SMS needs a registered sender and template under TRAI's DLT rules. Fail: invite only those who opted in.
2. **Consent to record.** Ask every person on the call before recording, and keep the yes in the notes. Some US states require all parties to agree; India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 expects clear consent and limited use. Fail: take notes by hand instead.
3. **One research question.** Pass: written and agreed with the owner. Fail: the calls drift into feedback chats.
4. **No selling.** The calls collect facts. No offers, no upsells, no discount codes on the call.

## Steps

### 1. Pick who to invite

Aim for 12 to 20 completed calls; about 20 calls of 30 minutes surface most of what a buyer group thinks, and more add little, so check at scale with a survey after [AVT-0017]. Fill four groups:

| Group | Why | Share of calls |
|---|---|---|
| Recent first-time buyers (last 30 to 60 days) | memory of the decision is fresh | about half |
| Repeat buyers (3 or more orders) | why they stay | about a quarter |
| Refunders, returners or COD refusers | what broke | a few |
| Near-buyers (abandoned checkout, asked a question, did not buy) | what stopped them | a few |

Weight the most loyal and the most unhappy over the middle; they say the most useful things [AVT-0021]. In India, include COD and prepaid buyers, tier 2 and 3 pincodes, and offer the call in Hindi or the buyer's language.

### 2. Recruit

Invites fill fast when they promise no demo and no sales pitch; one company gave a charity donation per 30-minute call to recruit busy buyers who ignore cash [AVT-0018]. For stores, a store credit or a choice of credit or donation works; never make the thank-you depend on saying nice things. Send to 5 to 10 times the calls needed (practitioner default, test it).

Email version:

> Subject: 20 minutes to help us, a thank-you from us
>
> Hi {first name}, you bought {product} from {store} on {date}. We would like to hear how you chose it, in your own words. It is a 20 to 30 minute call, no sales pitch and nothing to buy. As thanks, we will add {credit} to your account (or donate it to {charity} if you prefer). Pick a time here: {link}. {Founder name}

WhatsApp version (approved template, opted-in buyers only):

> Hi {first name}, {founder name} from {store} here. Could we have a 20-minute call about how you chose {product}? No sales, just listening. Thank-you: {credit}. Pick a time: {link}. Reply STOP to opt out.

Send a reminder the day before and one hour before.

### 3. Run the call (script)

Rules: ask about what happened, not what they would do. No "would you buy...?" Ask "tell me about the last time...". Let silence sit. Ask "what else?" before moving on.

**Opening (2 minutes).** Thank them. Explain the purpose. Ask permission to record; note the answer.

**Life before (5 minutes).**
- Before you bought {product}, what were you using or doing instead?
- What was annoying or not working about that?
- Had you tried anything else that did not work? What, and why? (Failed past fixes mark the highest-intent buyers [AVT-0009].)

**First thought (5 minutes).**
- When did you first think you needed something new? What was happening that day?
- Who else was involved? Did anyone suggest it?

**Looking (5 minutes).**
- Where did you look? What else did you consider? Marketplace, shop, another brand?
- How did you first hear of us?

**Deciding (6 minutes).**
- What made you pick us in the end?
- What almost stopped you? What worried you about buying online, the price, the delivery, COD or prepaid?
- What did you check before paying? Reviews, returns, the founder, the ingredients?

**After (5 minutes).**
- What happened when it arrived? First use?
- How would you describe it to a friend, in your words?
- What would make you stop buying?

**Close (2 minutes).** Anything we should have asked? Confirm the thank-you.

For refunders and near-buyers, swap "Deciding" for "What made you stop or send it back?" and keep the rest.

### 4. Summarise each call within a day

Fill the per-call template (Output). Copy exact phrases; do not polish them, because cleaned-up notes lose the words copy needs [AVT-0007]. Note the buyer's dominant feeling (fear, frustration, embarrassment, pride) and the real names of products they tried before [AVT-0010]. Note self-descriptions ("as a new mum") that may mark a buyer group [AVT-0016].

### 5. Cluster across calls

After all calls, sort whole stories, not single answers, into groups that share a trigger and a reason. For each buying reason, count how many calls showed it. Separate:

- **Triggers:** what set the search off.
- **Pull:** what they hoped the product would do.
- **Doubts:** what nearly stopped them.
- **Habits:** what kept them on the old way.

### 6. Check at scale

Turn the top reasons and doubts into a 3 to 5 question survey for the wider list or the post-purchase page, with multiple choice drawn from the call wording plus "other". An everyone-gets-something incentive gets fuller answers [AVT-0025]. Use behaviour tools for what buyers do and interviews for why [AVT-0019].

### 7. Hand over

Send the cross-interview sheet to `buyer-decision-map`, top doubts to `write-faq-from-tickets-and-reviews` and the product page writer, and buyer phrases to the ad copy team.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every invitee had opted in to the channel used.
- Recording consent is noted for every recorded call.
- No call included an offer, upsell or discount.
- Each per-call summary has at least three exact quotes.
- Buying reasons show counts (for example 9 of 16 calls), not adjectives.
- Refunders or near-buyers are in the sample, not only happy buyers.
- Personal data is stored only where the client keeps customer data, and recordings have a delete date.

## Common mistakes

- **Asking what they would do.** The script asks what they did.
- **Only happy buyers.** Step 1 adds refunders and near-buyers.
- **Selling on the call.** Gate 4 bans it.
- **Polished notes.** Step 4 keeps exact phrases.
- **Counting single answers.** Step 5 clusters whole stories.
- **Stopping at 20 calls.** Step 6 checks the findings with a survey.
