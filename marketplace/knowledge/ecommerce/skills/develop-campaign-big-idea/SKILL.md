# Campaign big idea across channels

**You get:** one campaign idea on one page (the line, the visual device, the proof, the offer if any, and what it must never say), how each channel expresses it (ads, email, site banner, social posts, WhatsApp or SMS), and a rollout sheet with every asset by channel, date, owner and approval.

## When to use

- The store wants one main push for a quarter or a cultural moment instead of scattered posts and ads.
- Ads, emails and the site each say something different.
- A relaunch, new positioning or price change needs one clear story everywhere.
- The team has many tactics and no idea holding them together.

## Not for

- The brand's long-term positioning and messaging: use `write-brand-positioning-and-messaging`.
- Finding the habit the brand stands against: use `brand-opponent-statement` (this skill can start from it).
- A month of ads and email by funnel stage with no single idea: use `full-funnel-30-day-plan`.
- A gifting season plan worked back from shipping cutoffs: use `seasonal-gifting-creative-plan`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Buyer words**: the review quote bank or angle table (`mine-ad-angles-from-reviews`), or at least 30 reviews and the top support questions.
2. **Product facts and proof** (`product-claims-profile`).
3. **Campaign window, goal and budget**: dates, the one result wanted (new buyers, repeat orders, a product line), ad budget, and gross profit per purchase for the products in it (`store-meta-math`).
4. **Channels the store really runs**, with list sizes and posting rhythm: Meta and other ads, email, SMS, WhatsApp, Instagram, the site.
5. **Brand voice and look** (`brand-voice-guide`, `store-visual-identity-sheet`), if they exist.

Optional: the brand's opponent statement, a cultural date the store could own, last campaigns and results.

If buyer words are missing, stop and gather them; an idea with no buyer truth in it is a slogan. Drafting needs no access. Building ads and emails needs draft access; publishing, sending and spending need the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Profit line.** Gross profit per purchase known for the campaign's products. Fail: the idea can be written but no offer or budget can be set.
2. **Stock and capacity.** Featured products have stock for the window; the team can make every asset on the rollout sheet. Fail: cut channels before cutting quality.
3. **Truth.** The idea's core line rests on a provable fact or real buyer words. Fail: replace it.
4. **List rules.** Email, SMS and WhatsApp go only to people who opted in; WhatsApp business-started messages in India use approved templates.

## Steps

### 1. Gather the raw material

On one page: the five strongest buyer quotes, three product facts competitors cannot say, the habit in the category the store stands against, and any date or shared worry the campaign could attach to. A "why now" works best when the worry has reached ordinary buyers, not when it is still fringe or already stale [MCR-0004]. Owning one cultural date each quarter can give buyers a reason to act beyond the big sale days [EML-0055].

### 2. Write three candidate ideas

Each candidate is one sentence that says something true and surprising about the buyer's life or the product, plus a one-line visual device (a recurring image, object, setting or format). Make the three truly different: one from buyer words, one from a product fact, one from the habit the store opposes. Borrowing a visual idea from a far-off field gives more tension than copying the category [BRD-0003].

### 3. Test each candidate

Score each yes or no:

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| True | Can every word be proven or traced to buyer words? |
| Non-obvious | Would a rival not say it tomorrow? An obvious idea is not a good one [BRD-0021]. |
| About the buyer | Does it touch why people buy, not only how the brand feels? |
| Stretch | Can you list ten different executions across channels in five minutes? |
| Fits the brand | Does it sound like the store's voice next to five real brand lines? |
| Sells the product | Does it lead to the campaign's products and goal? |

Drop any candidate with a no on True, About the buyer or Stretch.

### 4. Pick one

Where budget allows, run the survivors as plain product images with only the idea line changed for a few days [MCR-0075] and pick on store-matched purchases or add to carts. Without budget, give the candidates unlabelled to a fresh reviewer with the brief and pick what they choose in both orders. Note the runner-up in one line.

### 5. Write the idea page

The line (and two alternates), the visual device, the one proof that carries it, the offer if any, the tone, three example executions, and the must-nots (claims without proof, personal-attribute call-outs on Meta [MCR-6023], fake urgency, AI-made people posed as customers).

### 6. Carry it through each channel

The idea works only if every touch speaks to the same buyer: creative, copy, photos, proof and the landing page together; adding the buyer's name to a hook alone fails [AVT-0015].

- **Ads:** 3 to 5 concepts at different awareness stages, each holding to one stage [MCR-0056]; briefs via `write-one-page-creative-brief`.
- **Landing page or site banner:** the same line and visual device above the fold; ads with a featured person go to a page built from that shoot [LPG-0068]. Banners via `design-sale-banners-and-hero`.
- **Email:** the idea as the subject theme for the window, inside a normal rhythm of about 3 to 4 campaigns a week at most [EML-0063]; plan via `plan-monthly-email-calendar`.
- **SMS:** only for the launch, the offer and its end, about once a week at most [EML-0025].
- **Social posts:** the idea as a series, with each strong post remade in new formats [ORG-0026]; calendar via `plan-30-day-instagram-calendar`.
- **WhatsApp (India):** one approved template for the launch and one for the deadline, in the buyer's language.

India note: write Hindi or regional versions as their own lines, not translations; show prices with GST and say COD if offered. US note: check SMS consent and quiet hours.

### 7. Build the rollout sheet

Date every asset from the window backwards: briefs 3 weeks before, assets 1 week before, approvals 3 days before (practitioner defaults, adjust to the team). Add the readout date and what result counts as success, in store gross profit.

## Checks before you hand it over

- The idea is one sentence, true, non-obvious and about the buyer, with the proof named.
- Three candidates were tested and the choice and runner-up are recorded.
- Every channel's expression uses the same line, visual device and proof.
- Ads hold one awareness stage each and land on a page that matches them.
- Email, SMS and WhatsApp volumes stay within the store's normal rhythm and opt-ins.
- Every asset on the rollout sheet has a date, owner and approval step.
- Featured products have stock for the whole window.
- Nothing is published, sent or spent without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** the idea in one sentence, why it beats the runner-up, the window, the goal and how it will be judged, channels used, and the three biggest dates.

**Idea page:** Line, Alternates, Visual device, Proof (with file), Offer, Tone, Three example executions, Must-nots.

**Channel map:** Channel, How the idea shows up, Asset count, Audience, Frequency limit, Hand-off skill.

**Rollout sheet:**

| Date | Channel | Asset ID | Asset | Message line | Audience | Owner | Brief due | Asset due | Approval | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Readout plan:** date, measure (store gross profit after ad cost, new buyers, repeat orders), and what happens next for each outcome.

## Common mistakes

- **A slogan with no buyer truth.** Step 1 starts from buyer words and product facts.
- **An idea any rival could run.** Step 3 tests for non-obvious.
- **An idea that only works as one ad.** The stretch test asks for ten executions.
- **Different stories by channel.** Step 6 fixes one line, device and proof everywhere.
- **Ad and page that do not match.** Step 6 points ads to a matching page.
- **Flooding the list.** Step 6 keeps email and SMS inside the normal rhythm.
