# Distributor agreement checklist

**You get:** a term sheet of about 30 terms, each with the store's position, walk-away point and risk rating, a red-flag review of any draft received, the margin stack to the shelf, and a short list of questions for the store's lawyer. It is a negotiating tool, not legal advice; a lawyer reviews before anything is signed.

## When to use

- A distributor or reseller wants to carry the brand in a region, a channel or a country.
- A draft agreement has arrived and the owner needs to know what to push back on.
- An existing distributor relationship is drifting (late payment, missed volumes, marketplace leaks) and terms need resetting.
- The store wants a sales agent on commission and needs the terms written down.

## Not for

- Pricing for shops the store sells to directly: use `build-wholesale-price-list-and-terms`.
- Finding and pitching shops: use `find-and-pitch-retail-stockists`.
- Contracts with influencers or creators: use `influencer-contract-and-usage-rights`.
- Supplier and factory terms: use `negotiate-moq-and-price`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The proposal or draft agreement** from the distributor, or notes from the call.
2. **Wholesale price list** with delivered cost and gross profit per unit from `build-wholesale-price-list-and-terms`.
3. **Retail price and any printed or recommended retail price** per SKU, and the shelf price the distributor's retailers would charge.
4. **Trademark status** in each country involved, with registration numbers.
5. **Supplier terms and lead times**, and current stock.
6. **Who the store already sells to** in the proposed territory (stockists, marketplaces, its own site).

Optional: the distributor's other brands, their retailer list, references from two of their brands, a credit report, or the distributor's company and tax registration status (for example a VAT number check in the EU's VIES service or the UK's register).

If the draft is missing, build the term sheet from the store's side first and send that. Review is read only. Sending the term sheet is the client's call; signing is always the client's own act after legal review.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Margin stack works.** Pass when the store's price to the distributor still leaves gross profit per unit above its floor after delivered costs [MEA-0037] [FIN-0007], and the shelf price lands at the store's retail price after the distributor's and retailer's margins. Fail: renegotiate price or carry fewer SKUs.
2. **Cash.** Pass when credit days given are shorter than the supplier float [FIN-0005], or a credit line or purchase order finance covers large orders [FIN-0020].
3. **Capacity.** Pass when the store can fill the distributor's forecast volumes and lead times. A missed order to a channel partner empties shelves it cannot refill [OPS-0022].
4. **Legal review booked.** Pass when a lawyer in the governing-law country will review before signing. Fail: do not sign.

## Steps

### 1. Name the model

- **Distributor:** buys stock, takes title, resells to shops. Most control lost, least cash risk if paid upfront.
- **Reseller or dealer:** buys for its own shops or site.
- **Sales agent:** sells on commission, never owns stock; the store invoices and collects.

The model decides which terms below apply. Write it at the top.

### 2. Build the margin stack

Store price to distributor, distributor margin, retailer margin, shelf price, against the store's retail price. Work net of sales tax or VAT; where the market prints a maximum or recommended retail price on the pack, check it includes all taxes. Practitioner default distributor margin: 10 to 25% depending on country and service (warehousing, credit, sales team); test it against two other brands' terms if they share them.

### 3. Fill the term sheet

For each term: the store's position, walk-away, risk (high, medium, low).

**Commercial**

1. Products covered (list SKUs; new products not automatic).
2. Price to distributor and how it changes (notice period, cost index).
3. Minimum purchase per quarter, with a ramp in year one.
4. Forecast: rolling 3 months, first month binding.
5. Order lead time and minimum order quantity.
6. Payment: advance or letter of credit for export; credit limit and days for domestic; interest on late payment. Many markets set late-payment rules between businesses, for example the EU rule of 60 days unless agreed otherwise, with statutory interest, and the UK's statutory interest on late commercial payments.
7. Retention of title until paid.
8. Delivery terms (who pays freight, insurance, duty; where risk passes).

**Territory and channels**

9. Territory, defined by country, state or city.
10. Channels included (shops, modern trade) and excluded (the store's own site, marketplaces, corporate gifting, existing stockists by name).
11. Exclusivity: only if tied to the minimums in term 3, and lost automatically if two quarters are missed.
12. No active selling outside the territory.

US: territory and customer limits the brand sets on its own are generally lawful [OFF-6384]. The EU, the UK and other markets have their own competition rules on territory and pricing (the EU limits bans on passive sales into other territories); the lawyer checks.

**Price and brand**

13. Suggested retail price; no agreement forcing a minimum resale price. US: an agreed minimum is judged case by case federally and banned in some states [OFF-6384]. UK and EU: fixing a minimum resale price is generally banned. Elsewhere, ask the lawyer.
14. Online sales and marketplaces: only with written permission, per marketplace.
15. Trademark use under a limited, revocable licence. The distributor never registers the brand, domain, social handles or marketplace brand account in its own name.
16. Marketing: who pays, approval of materials, no claims beyond the store's approved list.
17. Customer and retailer data: the store gets the list of retailers served.

**Product and compliance**

18. Labelling and registrations for the territory, and whose name they are in (importer of record, licences).
19. Storage conditions and shelf life; stock rotation.
20. Recalls: who acts, who pays.
21. Quality complaints process and timelines.

**Reporting and performance**

22. Monthly sell-through by retailer and stock on hand. Sell-through, not sell-in, frees cash and predicts reorders [FIN-0028].
23. Quarterly review against minimums.

**Returns and damage**

24. No sale-or-return. Damage claims with photos within a set number of days.
25. Expired or slow stock: distributor's risk unless agreed otherwise.

**Term and exit**

26. Initial term of 12 months, renewal by mutual yes.
27. Termination for non-payment, missed minimums, breach, or change of ownership.
28. On exit: the store may buy back sellable stock at the distributor's cost less damage, retailers transfer, and use of the brand stops on a set date.
29. Governing law and dispute forum (arbitration seat).
30. Liability cap and insurance.

### 4. Run the red-flag check on their draft

Flag in red any of:

- Exclusivity for a large territory or long term with no minimums.
- The distributor registering or "protecting" the trademark locally in its name.
- Sale-or-return on all stock, or price protection on all future cuts.
- Credit days over 60 for a new relationship.
- No sell-through reporting.
- Upfront marketing or listing fees with no volume commitment.
- Automatic renewal with long notice to exit, or compensation owed on termination with no cap.
- Rights to all future products.

### 5. Write the lawyer questions

Five to eight questions, specific to this draft and country: enforceability of exclusivity and pricing terms, compensation on termination under local law, retention of title, tax and invoicing (a VAT invoice with both VAT numbers in the UK and EU; e-invoicing where the country requires it), and dispute forum.

### 6. Hand over for approval

The client decides what to send. Any binding offer needs the client's fresh yes. Signing waits for the lawyer.

## Checks before you hand it over

- The model (distributor, reseller, agent) is named and only matching terms are filled.
- The margin stack ends at the store's retail price.
- Exclusivity, if any, is tied to minimums with automatic loss.
- Credit days are shorter than supplier float, or financing is named.
- Trademark, domain and marketplace accounts stay in the store's name.
- Exit terms include stock buy-back and a date the brand use stops.
- Every red flag in the draft is listed with a counter-position.
- The sheet says it is not legal advice and names the lawyer step.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** the model, margin stack, the 3 biggest risks in their draft, the store's must-haves, walk-aways, and the recommendation (sign after changes, renegotiate, or walk) with the runner-up in a line.
2. **Term sheet table:** #, Term, Store's position, Walk-away, Their draft says, Risk (H, M, L), Note.
3. **Margin stack:** SKU, Store price to distributor, Store gross profit per unit, Distributor margin, Retailer margin, Shelf price, Store retail price, Gap.
4. **Red-flag list:** Clause, What it says, Why it matters, Counter-position.
5. **Lawyer questions:** numbered, with the clause each relates to.

## Common mistakes

- **Exclusivity with no minimums.** Term 11 ties it to term 3.
- **Letting the distributor own the brand locally.** Term 15 keeps every registration with the store.
- **Judging the partner on orders placed.** Term 22 asks for sell-through [FIN-0028].
- **Credit longer than the supplier gives.** Gate 2 caps it [FIN-0005].
- **No exit plan.** Term 28 sets buy-back and a brand-use end date.
- **Treating this as legal advice.** Step 5 and gate 4 send it to a lawyer.

## Sources

- MEA-0037, FIN-0007: delivered cost and landed cost per unit.
- FIN-0005: supplier float caps credit days.
- FIN-0020: credit line or PO finance for big orders.
- OPS-0022: missed partner orders cost the account.
- FIN-0028: sell-through drives cash.
- OFF-6384: US territorial and resale price rules.

What makes this skill ours: every term has a walk-away, not just a wish; exclusivity is always tied to minimums that switch it off; the margin stack is checked down to the shelf price; trademark and marketplace accounts stay with the store; and late-payment rules and tax invoicing sit inside the payment terms.

## Selling in India

- Check the distributor's GST return filing status in the public GST search by GSTIN.
- Work the margin stack net of GST, with MRP including all taxes on the pack [COG-6264].
- If the store is a Udyam-registered micro or small enterprise, buyers must pay within 45 days of accepting goods, with statutory interest after [FIN-6382].
- Indian competition rules on territory and resale pricing: ask the lawyer.
- Invoicing: a GST tax invoice with both GSTINs [FIN-6380]; e-invoicing if turnover is over the threshold [FIN-6381].
