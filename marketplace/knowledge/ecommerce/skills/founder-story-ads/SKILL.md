# Founder story ads

**You get:** three founder ad concepts and their timed scripts (30 to 60 seconds each, with 3 to 5 hook options per script), a story fact sheet with a proof file or a cut for every claim, and a one-page recording plan for a single session.

## When to use

- The store has a real origin story tied to the problem the product solves.
- Ads sound generic and the founder is willing to record, or at least lend their story.
- A launch, price rise or trust problem needs a human face behind the product.
- A past founder ad worked and has now tired.

## Not for

- A creator-style script with a hired presenter: use `creator-video-script`.
- The written brand story for the About page: use `write-about-us-brand-story-page`.
- A plain-text founder email: use `write-founder-plain-text-email`.
- Editing raw footage into many cuts: use `cut-raw-footage-into-ad-variants` after recording.

## What you need

Required:

1. **A 30 to 45 minute founder interview** (recorded call or written answers to the questions in step 1).
2. **Proof the founder can share**: dated photos, early prototypes, first invoices or orders, supplier or lab documents, press, messages from early buyers (with their permission).
3. **The product's claims sheet** (`product-claims-profile`) and live offer and landing page.
4. **Gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase** (`store-meta-math`).

Optional: past founder ads with results, the angle table from `mine-ad-angles-from-reviews`, the founder's social profile if partnership ads are possible.

If the founder will not appear on camera, the skill still works (step 3). If no proof exists for the core story, stop: write a different kind of ad. This job needs no account access. Running ads through the founder's own profile needs the founder's permission inside Meta, and spend needs the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **The story is true and provable.** Pass when the problem, the turning point and the "why we made it" can each be shown with a document, photo or dated record. Fail: cut what cannot be shown; if the core falls, stop.
2. **The story is tied to the buyer's problem.** A founder journey that does not touch why a buyer would purchase is a brand film, not an ad. Fail: find the overlap or stop.
3. **Profit line known.** Pass when target cost per purchase exists. Fail: concepts can be drafted, but no test plan.
4. **Consent.** Founder agrees to face, voice and name use, and any person or customer in the story agrees too. A cloned or AI voice of the founder needs the founder's written yes and the platform's AI label.

## Steps

### 1. Interview for facts, not lines

Ask, and record answers word for word:

- What problem did you or someone close to you have? When? How do you know it was common?
- What did you try first, and what was wrong with each option?
- What did you find out that others in the category miss or get wrong?
- What did the first version look like? What did you change after early buyers used it?
- What do you refuse to do that rivals do?
- What would make you proudest if a buyer said it?

The non-obvious but true answer is the gold; an obvious one is not an idea [BRD-0021].

### 2. Build the fact sheet

List every factual line the scripts might use. For each: proof file, date, who checked. Feelings and opinions in the founder's own voice ("I was furious") need no proof. Facts do: years of work, number of prototypes, lab results, "doctors said". In the US an objective claim needs a reasonable basis before the ad runs [MCR-6022]; the founder is the brand, so the ad is the brand's claim. Cut or narrow anything without proof.

### 3. Choose three concepts

Pick from these parts and change at least two between concepts: buyer, awareness stage, angle, offer, proof type, founder role [MCR-0009]. The founder does not have to be on camera: the same script can run as founder on camera, founder voice over product footage, founder evidence (notebooks, prototypes, documents) or a stand-in such as a team member or supplier, labelled as such [MCR-0005]. Good starting angles: a misconception the founder set out to fix, a frustration every buyer shares, or a copycat problem [ORG-0027].

### 4. Write each script

A structure that has carried strong founder ads in practitioner accounts [MCR-0006]:

1. **Hook (0 to 3 s):** a bold, provable claim against the category's usual way.
2. **Proof of the claim (3 to 10 s):** the document, demonstration or number.
3. **Story as the reason (10 to 30 s):** what the founder went through and found.
4. **Proof the fix works (30 to 45 s):** a buyer result with permission, a test, a demonstration.
5. **Call to action with social proof (last 5 s):** review count only if real and current.

For cold buyers, do not open on product benefits; open on the problem or the claim [MCR-0011]. Lo-fi footage is fine for a premium brand if the product itself is not shown in the lo-fi part [MCR-0010]. Write 3 to 5 hooks per script. Mark the proof file next to every factual line.

India note: record in the language the buyers use; a Hindi or regional version is its own script, not subtitles on English. Mention COD or easy returns in the call to action only if live.

### 5. Plan one recording session

Practitioner pattern: 10 hooks x 3 bodies gives 30 ads from about five minutes of finished recording, and one 1 to 2 hour session can supply three to four months [MCR-0007]. Plan the session: wardrobe changes, two or three settings, all hooks back to back, each body twice, B-roll of the evidence. A second setting matters later: reshooting the same script elsewhere has revived a tired founder ad [MCR-0012].

### 6. Plan how it runs

Where the founder's profile is active and relevant, running the ads as partnership ads through that profile can beat the brand page [ADC-0003]. Keep founder ads as one part of the account, not the whole of it; brands leaning on one founder ad stalled when it tired [MCR-0008]. Hand each concept to `write-one-page-creative-brief` for the test plan.

### 7. Disclosure and AI

The founder speaks as the founder, never as a customer. Customers quoted in the script are real, with permission, and an unusual result is paired with the usual one [MCR-6025]. AI-made footage or voice is labelled per the platform's rule and never shown as real events.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every factual line in every script points to a proof file on the fact sheet.
- Three concepts differ on at least two of buyer, stage, angle, offer, proof type and founder role.
- Each script is timed, with 3 to 5 hooks and a call to action that matches the live offer.
- Cold-audience scripts open on the problem or a claim, not product benefits.
- Founder, staff and stand-ins are labelled as who they are; no one poses as a customer.
- Consent for face, voice, name and any AI voice is recorded.
- The recording plan yields at least 10 hooks and 3 bodies.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** the three concepts in one line each, the founder role in each, the proof that carries each, the recording date needed and what the session yields.

**Story fact sheet:** Line, Type (fact or feeling), Proof file, Date, Checked by, Status (keep, narrow, cut).

## Common mistakes

- **A story nobody can check.** Step 2 cuts every fact without a file.
- **Founder biography instead of a buyer problem.** Gate 2 ties the story to why buyers purchase.
- **Waiting for the founder to be camera-ready.** Step 3 offers voice, evidence or stand-in versions.
- **One long take, one ad.** Step 5 records hooks and bodies separately for many versions.
- **Leaning on one founder ad.** Step 6 keeps it one part of the account.
- **Opening on benefits to a cold audience.** Step 4 opens on the problem or a claim.
