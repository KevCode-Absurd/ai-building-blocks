# Early Amazon reviews within the rules

**You get:** a dated plan for the first 60 days of reviews on one product: Vine enrolment details and cost, a Request a Review routine for every eligible order, a compliant package insert text, a do-not-do list the client signs, and a weekly tracker of reviews per 100 orders with the product and listing fixes the first reviews point to.

## When to use

- A product is about to launch on Amazon with no reviews.
- A listing has sat at zero to a few reviews for weeks.
- Someone (an agency, a supplier, a friend) proposes a review shortcut and the client needs a clear answer.
- Early reviews are critical and the client wants to know what to do.

## Not for

- Reviews on the store's own site, Google or other platforms: use `build-review-collection-system`.
- Replying to bad reviews on the store's site or Google: use `reply-to-negative-reviews`.
- Using reviews in ads: use `review-screenshot-ads` or `write-review-based-ad-copy`.
- Mining rival reviews for listing copy: use `write-amazon-listing`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Brand Registry status** and whether the product is sold through FBA.
2. **Current review count** and rating on the product page.
3. **Orders by day** from Seller Central (Orders report, last 30 days, or forecast for a new product).
4. **Stock and unit cost**: landed cost plus FBA fees per unit, for valuing Vine units.
5. **The package insert** in use now, if any.

Optional: the brand's email list (for telling existing fans the product is on Amazon, without a review ask), customer questions, early return reasons.

Reading is level 1. Enrolling in Vine and pressing Request a Review need draft or publish access in Seller Central and the client's yes for the Vine fee and units. Any insert change needs the client's yes before printing.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **The product is ready to be judged.** Vine reviewers and first buyers will report every flaw. If samples showed defects, fix them first; early bad reviews stay on the page.
2. **Vine fits.** Fewer than 30 reviews, FBA, Brand Registry for a branded product, not adult, digital or a bundle [AMZ-6440]. Fail: skip Vine and lean on Request a Review.
3. **Money.** Vine units at landed cost plus fees, plus the Vine fee, fit the launch budget. Fail: enrol fewer units.
4. **The client signs the do-not-do list** (Output). Fail: stop. Review abuse can cost the account, and in the US fake reviews carry penalties [MCR-6045].

## Steps

1. **Plan Vine.** Fee per parent product: nothing for up to 2 units, 75 US dollars for 3 to 10, 200 US dollars for 11 to 30; charged only after the first review, and not at all if none arrives within 90 days [AMZ-6440]. Enrol the full 30 units when the product and listing are final and stock is checked in. Count unit cost x units + fee as a launch cost. Amazon picks the reviewers and they are asked for honest opinions; you choose nothing about who or what they write [AMZ-6440]. If variations exist, enrol the variant most buyers will choose.
2. **Request a Review on every eligible order.** The button on each order's page sends Amazon's standard request 5 to 30 days after delivery [AMZ-6448]. Press it for every eligible order, not only for buyers who seem happy: asking only happy buyers is selective asking, which Amazon and US regulators treat as manipulation [CXP-6301]. Set a weekly slot (for example every Monday, orders delivered 5 to 12 days ago). If a tool automates this, it must trigger Amazon's own standard request, never a custom message.
3. **Write the package insert.** Allowed content: how to use, care, setup, and how to reach support through Amazon messaging. Leave out links that send buyers off Amazon; Amazon limits them, so check its communication rules before adding any. Not allowed: any request for a review, positive or neutral, any offer in exchange for a review, and any "contact us before you review" that steers unhappy buyers away from Amazon [AMZ-6448]. Write it in the buyer's language, and in India add a Hindi side if the buyer base reads it.
4. **Tell existing fans, without an ask.** The brand may tell its email list or social followers the product is now on Amazon. It may not offer anything for a review or ask them to review it, and staff, family and friends may not review it [MCR-6045].
5. **Track reviews per 100 orders weekly.** Reviews this week / orders delivered 5 to 30 days ago x 100. Do not set a target from someone else's benchmark; set it from the product's first 4 weeks and watch the trend.
6. **Read every early critical review as a fix list.** Sort complaints into product (broke, leaked, smell), expectation (smaller than expected, colour), and delivery (damaged box). A product with a clear, fixable complaint pattern is a supplier brief, not a reputation problem [AMZ-0002]. Expectation complaints go to the listing: fix the size photo, the bullet, the A+ module. Delivery complaints go to packaging (`packaging-and-unboxing-brief`). Note phrases where reviewers name themselves ("as a runner"), since they show who the product really serves [AVT-0016].
7. **Report, do not pressure.** If a review breaks Amazon's community rules (it reviews the seller or delivery rather than the product, or is abusive), report it through the review's report link. Never ask a reviewer to change or remove a review [AMZ-6448].
8. **US notes.** The FTC's rule bans fake reviews, reviews from people who do not exist (AI-written included), undisclosed insider reviews and incentives tied to a sentiment [MCR-6045] [CXP-6301]. Anyone the brand rewards for talking about the product elsewhere (a creator, an ambassador) must disclose it [CXP-6303].
9. **India notes.** Amazon.in follows the same review policy. Indian consumer protection law treats misleading practices as unfair trade practices; follow the same rules here and ask a lawyer for anything unclear. WhatsApp follow-ups to Amazon buyers are off limits: buyer contact goes through Amazon's messaging only.
10. **Review the plan at day 30 and day 60.** Count Vine reviews received, Request a Review results, and the fixes made. If the rating trend is falling, pause ads (`launch-amazon-ppc`) until the cause is fixed.

## Do-not-do list (the client signs this)

- No free product, discount, refund, gift card or entry to a draw in exchange for a review, even an "honest" one.
- No refund or reimbursement after a buyer posts a review.
- No review clubs, review groups, review swaps or paid review services.
- No reviews by the owner, staff, family, friends, suppliers or agencies.
- No asking only happy buyers; no survey that filters who gets asked.
- No review requests of any kind in the package.
- No messages outside Amazon's system to Amazon buyers.
- No asking a reviewer to change or remove a review.
- No merging unrelated products into one listing family to share reviews; check Amazon's variation rules.
- No AI-written or made-up reviews or testimonials anywhere.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Vine eligibility checked against the four conditions, with units and fee written as money.
- Request a Review covers every eligible order on a fixed weekly slot.
- The insert text contains no review request and no offer.
- The do-not-do list is signed by the client.
- The tracker measures reviews per 100 delivered orders, with no borrowed target.
- Every critical review is sorted into product, expectation or delivery with an owner for the fix.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** product, current reviews, Vine units and cost, Request a Review slot, insert status, fixes found so far, and the day 30 review date.

**60-day plan:** Week | Action (Vine enrol, Request a Review batch, insert print, review read) | Owner | Done.

**Insert text:** Front: product name, how to use in 3 steps, care. Back: support route through Amazon messaging. No review ask, no offer, no off-Amazon link.

**Weekly tracker:** Week | Orders delivered 5 to 30 days ago | Requests sent | New reviews | Reviews per 100 orders | Rating | Vine reviews.

**Complaint log:** Review date | Stars | Complaint in buyer's words | Type (product, expectation, delivery) | Fix | Owner | Status.

## Common mistakes

- **"Honest review" swaps for free product.** Any incentive breaks the policy; the do-not-do list rules it out.
- **Asking only happy buyers.** Step 2 sends to every eligible order.
- **Inserts that ask for a review.** Step 3 keeps inserts to use, care and support.
- **Vine before the product is final.** Gate 1 and step 1 wait until defects are fixed.
- **Treating bad reviews as a PR problem.** Step 6 turns them into product and listing fixes.
- **Friends and staff reviews to "get it started".** Banned by Amazon and, in the US, by federal rule.
