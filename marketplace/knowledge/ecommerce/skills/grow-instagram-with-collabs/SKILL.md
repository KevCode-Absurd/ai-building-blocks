# Grow Instagram with collabs

**You get:** a partner list scored for fit, a 60-day collab calendar, first message, follow-up and brief for each partner, and a tracking sheet that shows followers, store visits and gross profit per collab, ready for the client to approve and send.

## When to use

- Follower growth has stalled and the store wants new audiences without paying for ads.
- A launch or seasonal edit needs reach from accounts the buyer already follows.
- The store has natural partners (a coffee brand for a mug store, a yoga studio for an activewear store) and has never asked them.
- Past collabs happened ad hoc and nobody knows if any of them sold.

## Not for

- Paid influencer campaigns, gifting at scale and deal pricing: use `plan-influencer-gifting-campaign`.
- Contracts, usage rights for ads and exclusivity: use `influencer-contract-and-usage-rights`.
- Running ads from a creator's handle: use `run-creator-partnership-ads`.
- Checking a creator's audience for fake followers in depth: use `find-and-vet-influencers`.

## What you need

Required (read access, level 1):

1. **Instagram Insights, last 90 days**: follower count and growth, top posts, audience top cities and countries, age range.
2. **Buyer profile**: who buys, what else they buy, where they live. From `store-knowledge-folder` or the client.
3. **Product list** with gross profit per order and stock, to choose what to feature and what to gift.
4. **Partner candidates** the client already knows, if any.
5. **Approved budget** for gifts or a giveaway prize, in product units and money.

Optional: past collab results, any existing partner discount codes, the store's UTM rule.

If Insights are missing, use the buyer profile alone and mark targets "set after first two collabs". Sending outreach from the brand's account or email needs draft access (level 2) for drafts and a client yes before every send. Accepting a collab invite or posting needs publish access (level 3) and the client's yes on each post.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Gross profit per order** is known for the featured products and gift costs are counted. Fail: compute it in `build-sku-profit-sheet`; until then, gift only low-cost products.
2. **Own account is ready.** At least 9 recent posts that show what the store sells, a clear bio and a working link. Partners check the profile before replying. Fail: fix the profile first.
3. **Capacity.** Each collab takes outreach, a brief, review and posting. Fail: plan fewer collabs.
4. **Disclosure.** If either side gives anything of value (product, money, a code that earns), the post carries a clear label [ORG-6405]; in India use an ASCI label such as Collaboration or Partnership [ORG-6406]. A collab between two brands with no exchange still benefits from naming the partnership plainly.
5. **Giveaway rules.** Any joint giveaway follows Instagram's promotion guidelines and local contest law. Fail: drop the giveaway format; no legal ruling here.

## Steps

### 1. Build the long list

Find 30 to 50 candidates in four groups:

- **Non-competing brands** that sell to the same buyer.
- **Niche creators** whose audience is the buyer.
- **Local or community accounts** (studios, cafes, clubs, event pages) where the buyer lives.
- **Customer accounts** with a real following who already post the product.

Sources: who the store's followers also follow, hashtags the buyer uses, tagged posts, accounts that comment on competitors.

### 2. Score each candidate

Score 1 to 5 on each and keep the top 10 to 15:

- **Audience fit**: the partner's followers match the buyer (location, age, interest).
- **Size fit**: between half and five times the store's own followers works best for a fair trade (practitioner default, test it).
- **Real engagement**: recent posts get comments from real people. Skip accounts whose high-view posts have very low engagement, a sign of paid boosts [ORG-0010].
- **Non-follower reach**: their Reels reach strangers, not only their followers [ORG-0029].
- **Brand fit**: tone and quality the client is happy to sit next to.

For paid creators only, price the deal off Meta: aim for about half the store's Meta CPM, from the creator's last 10 videos' average views, cut by the share of their audience outside the target [ORG-0041]. Most brand-to-brand collabs need no fee.

### 3. Pick the format per partner

| Format | Best for | How it runs |
|---|---|---|
| Co-authored Reel | Brands and creators | One Reel posted as a collab post on both profiles |
| Co-authored carousel | Brands | Pairing guide, gift guide or "how we use it" |
| Live together | Creators, experts | 20 to 30 minutes, Q and A, product in use |
| Account swap for a day | Brands with similar size | Stories on the other's account |
| Joint giveaway | Brands | Prize from both, entry rules that follow Instagram's guidelines |

A collab post can carry up to 5 collaborator accounts and works on feed posts, Reels and carousels, not Stories; it counts only once the invite shows accepted [ORG-6404]. Make the post useful to both audiences: a demo, a pairing or a story, not a joint ad.

### 4. Plan the 60 days

Two to four collabs a month, spaced at least a week apart, so each one can be measured. Put the strongest partner on a launch or offer week, but keep the collab post itself give-only; selling happens in stories and keyword DMs [ORG-0003].

### 5. Write the outreach

Per partner, write three messages:

- **First note** (DM or email): one specific, true thing about their account, what the store sells in one line, the collab idea in one line, what each side gets, and an easy yes ("Would a co-posted Reel in March suit you?"). Under 80 words.
- **Follow-up** after 5 to 7 days: one line and a new detail (a draft concept or a sample offer).
- **Close the loop** if no reply after another week: polite, door open.

No false flattery, no invented numbers about the store, no promise of results. The client approves and sends each message.

### 6. Write the brief

One page per collab: goal, format, who posts and when, concept, shots, caption owner, required disclosure line, product and links, the partner's unique code and UTM link, what each side may reuse afterwards, and the approval step. Reuse of the partner's content in ads needs a separate written grant through `influencer-contract-and-usage-rights`.

### 7. Set tracking before posting

Each collab gets its own:

- Discount or referral code (or a free-gift code if discounts hurt margin).
- UTM link, following the store's rule.
- Comment keyword that sends the link by DM, so you can see who opened and clicked [ORG-0021].

### 8. Measure each collab

At 7 and 30 days, record: reach, share of reach from non-followers, follows from the post (shown in Insights), profile visits, keyword DMs, link clicks, orders on the code or UTM, and gross profit from those orders minus gift and prize cost. Judge growth collabs on shares and follows, and selling collabs on comments, DMs and orders [ORG-0019]. Keep partners whose collab beat the account's median post on its goal; rebook them first.

### 9. India and US notes

- **India:** many partners prefer WhatsApp for coordination after the first DM; get the client's yes before moving off Instagram. Write captions in the language both audiences use. Use an ASCI label and keep it on screen for the required time in video [ORG-6406]. If the store takes COD, codes must work on COD orders too, or tracking will undercount.
- **US:** a label such as "ad" or "Thanks to [brand] for the free product" goes in the post itself; vague tags like "collab" alone are not enough [ORG-6405].

## Checks before you hand it over

- 10 to 15 scored partners, each with a format and a reason.
- Every collab has a date, a brief, a code, a UTM link and a keyword.
- Outreach messages are specific to the partner, under 80 words, and contain no invented facts.
- Disclosure wording is set per market.
- Gift and prize costs are counted against gross profit.
- No message is sent and no post accepted without the client's yes.
- The tracking sheet has 7-day and 30-day columns.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** the 60-day goal, the top 5 partners and formats, the dates, total gift cost, and how success is judged.
2. **Partner sheet**: Partner, Handle, Group, Followers, Audience fit, Size fit, Real engagement, Non-follower reach, Brand fit, Total, Format, Contact route, Status.
3. **Outreach pack**: per partner, First note, Follow-up, Close the loop, filled in.
4. **Collab briefs**: one page each, fields as in step 6.
5. **Tracking sheet**: Collab, Date, Format, Reach, Non-follower share, Follows, Profile visits, Keyword DMs, Clicks, Orders, Gross profit, Gift cost, Net, Rebook (yes or no).

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing big accounts that will not reply.** Step 2 scores size fit near the store's own size.
- **A joint ad instead of a useful post.** Step 3 makes the content serve both audiences.
- **No way to tell which collab sold.** Step 7 gives each its own code, link and keyword.
- **Forgetting disclosure on gifted collabs.** The disclosure gate covers every exchange of value.
- **Collab posts on Stories.** Collaborator tags do not work there [ORG-6404].
- **Reusing a partner's video in ads without a grant.** Step 6 sends that to a contract.
