# Import setup checklist by market

**You get:** one checklist per import market with each item to set up, who files it, how long it takes, the date to start counting back from your first arrival, the documents the broker will ask for, and a short list of what stops goods at the border in that market.

## When to use

- The first container, pallet or air shipment is heading to a new country.
- The store is moving from shipping each order from home to holding stock in the market.
- A marketplace or 3PL in a new country asked for your importer details.
- Goods were held, fined or sent to a bonded warehouse for missing paperwork.
- The owner is choosing between markets and wants the setup work and lead time for each.

## Not for

- The HS code and duty rate per product: use `hs-code-and-duty-research`.
- Which country to enter next: use `choose-next-marketplace`.
- Store tax settings for selling (sales tax, VAT and goods and services tax at checkout): use `set-up-taxes-gst-and-us-sales-tax` and the accountant.
- Booking and tracking freight: use `inbound-shipment-tracker-with-alerts`.
- Product testing and certificates in depth: the compliance agent meenakshi-compliance, with `order-samples-and-quality-check`.

## What you need

Required (read access, level 1):

1. **Where the business is set up:** country of registration, company type, tax numbers already held.
2. **Target market and first arrival date**, plus the mode (ocean, air, express or post) and the expected value of the first shipment and of a year's imports.
3. **Products:** a list with likely HS codes, materials, whether any are for children, contain batteries, are food, cosmetics or electrical.
4. **Who you already work with:** freight forwarder, customs broker, 3PL in the market, accountant.
5. **How you will sell there:** your own store shipping from local stock, a marketplace, or wholesale.

Drafting the checklist is level 2. Applying for numbers, buying a bond or appointing a representative needs the owner's yes, and the owner or the named filer submits.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Importer of record decided.** Pass when the owner has said who will be importer of record: your own company, a local company, or a service acting for you. Fail: decide this first, because every registration below hangs on it, and in the US the importer of record carries the duty of reasonable care [OPS-6686].
2. **A broker named.** Pass when a licensed customs broker in the market has agreed to act. Fail: list two or three to call; the broker files the entry, not the store.
3. **Lead time fits.** Pass when the first arrival is at least 6 weeks away. Fail: warn the owner that some items take 2 to 4 weeks and a missed filing can mean fines or storage charges.

## Steps

### 1. Pick the markets and their checklist

Use the checklist for each market below. Mark each item "have", "need" or "not needed", with the reason.

### 2. United States

- **Importer number:** the company's IRS employer number (EIN), or a CBP-assigned number for a business without one; the broker files the CBP form. Lead time: days to a few weeks.
- **Customs bond:** a continuous import bond, sized at the greater of $50,000 or 10% of the duties, taxes and fees paid in the last 12 months [OPS-6668]. Bought through the broker or a surety.
- **ISF (10+2) for ocean freight:** filed at least 24 hours before the cargo is loaded abroad; a late or wrong ISF can bring $5,000 in liquidated damages per violation [OPS-6667]. It needs a bond: a continuous import bond covers it [OPS-6669]. The broker or forwarder files; the supplier must send its data in time.
- **Entry:** filed within 15 calendar days of landing, or goods can be moved to a general order warehouse at your cost [OPS-6674]. Shipments of $2,500 or less may use informal entry, with limits [OPS-6675]. Duty-free de minimis entry has been suspended for non-postal modes, so low-value parcels need an entry too [OPS-6647].
- **Commercial invoice:** detailed description, quantities, unit price and currency, country of origin, all charges such as freight and packing, and any assists [OPS-6673].
- **Product safety:** where a CPSC rule applies, a certificate per product with a unique ID such as GTIN or SKU [COG-6312]; children's products need a Children's Product Certificate issued by the US importer from a CPSC-accepted lab [COG-6373]; certificate data must be eFiled with CBP at entry from 8 July 2026 [COG-6313]. Food, cosmetics and some devices have FDA rules; ask the broker.
- **Origin marking:** every item must show its origin country in English [COG-6370]; unmarked goods owe an extra 10% duty unless fixed under customs supervision [COG-6397].

### 3. United Kingdom (Great Britain)

- **GB EORI number:** needed to clear goods; it normally needs UK premises, and a business not established in the UK must appoint someone in the UK to handle customs [OPS-6683]. Lead time: often days.
- **VAT:** register for UK VAT when you hold stock in the UK; a VAT-registered importer can use postponed VAT accounting so import VAT is declared and reclaimed on the same return [FIN-6440]. Consignments of £135 or less have VAT charged at checkout by the seller or marketplace [OPS-6631]. Confirm with a UK accountant.
- **Product marking:** CE marking is still accepted alongside or instead of UKCA for most rules, but the GB importer's name and postal address must be on the product or its packaging [COG-6388].
- **Broker documents:** commercial invoice, packing list, commodity codes, origin proof if claiming a preferential rate.

### 4. European Union

- **EU EORI number:** a non-EU business gets one from the member state where it makes its first customs declaration, and uses it in every EU country [OPS-6655].
- **Customs representative:** a business not set up in the EU usually needs a broker acting as indirect representative, who shares liability; ask the broker what they require.
- **Responsible economic operator:** since 13 December 2024 a consumer product sold into the EU needs an operator established in the EU, named with a postal address on the product, packaging, parcel or paperwork [COG-6384]. Goods without one risk being blocked at the border [COG-6369]. The product needs a batch or serial number and a technical file [COG-6385].
- **VAT:** for stock held in an EU country, register for VAT there. For parcels of €150 or less sent from outside the EU, IOSS lets a non-EU seller charge EU VAT at checkout and file in one member state [FIN-6438]. Those parcels also carry a €3 duty per item from 1 July 2026 [OPS-6658] and a €2 handling fee per item expected from 1 November 2026 [OPS-6656]. Confirm with an EU VAT adviser.

### 5. Canada

- **Business number and import account:** an import and export program account with the Canada Revenue Agency, then registration in the CBSA's CARM portal, where importers post financial security if they want goods released before paying.
- **Broker or non-resident importer:** a business outside Canada can import as a non-resident importer; most use a broker.
- **Goods and services tax:** charged at import, with provincial sales tax rules on top in some provinces. Confirm with a Canadian accountant.

### 6. Australia

- **Business identity:** an Australian Business Number if you have an Australian entity, or registration in the Australian Border Force client register for an overseas business; the broker usually arranges it.
- **Import declaration:** needed for goods over A$1,000; goods at or under that value sold to consumers have goods and services tax collected at checkout by a registered seller or marketplace. Confirm thresholds with an Australian broker or accountant.

### 7. Set the dates

For each "need" item, write who files, the lead time, and the latest start date = first arrival date minus lead time minus two weeks of buffer. Put the dates in the shipment tracker.

### 8. Prepare the broker pack

List what the broker will ask for: company and tax numbers, the importer of record's details, power of attorney or client agreement, commercial invoice and packing list for the first shipment, HS codes per SKU, origin per SKU, product certificates, and the bond or security details.

## Checks before you hand it over

- The importer of record is named for each market.
- Every item says who files it, the lead time and the latest start date.
- Every tax point names its country and says which adviser confirms it.
- Product safety and marking duties are listed for each market.
- No application was submitted and no bond bought without the owner's yes.
- The broker pack lists every document the first entry needs.

## Output

1. **Checklist per market:** item, have or need, who files, lead time, latest start date, notes.
2. **Border stoppers:** the three to five things most likely to hold goods in that market.
3. **Broker pack list:** document, who provides it, status.
4. **Questions for the broker and accountant**, one line each.

## Common mistakes

- **No importer of record.** Gate 1 settles it before anything else.
- **ISF filed late.** Step 2 makes the supplier's data and the 24-hour rule a dated item.
- **No EU operator on the product.** Step 4 puts it on the checklist.
- **Assuming low-value parcels are duty-free.** Steps 2 and 4 cover the US and EU changes.
- **Starting the week stock ships.** Step 7 counts back from the arrival date.
