# Influencer contract and usage rights

**You get:** a one-page term sheet per creator deal plus a contract checklist (every point in steps 1 to 7) marked agreed, open or not needed, and a rights register showing for every creator asset where the store may use it and until when.

## When to use

- A creator said yes to a paid deal and terms must be written down.
- The store wants to run a creator's video as a Meta or TikTok ad.
- The team already runs creator content in ads and cannot find what was agreed.
- A creator asks for exclusivity, a kill fee or a usage fee and the owner needs to price it.

## Not for

- Legal advice or a final contract: this produces the business terms; a lawyer drafts or checks the signed version.
- Setting up partnership ads in Meta once rights exist: use `run-creator-partnership-ads`.
- Spark ad codes on TikTok: use `tiktok-spark-ads-with-creators`.
- The creative brief and script: use `creator-video-script`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Deal summary** from outreach: creator, platform, tier, fee or commission, deliverables discussed.
2. **Ad plan**: will the content run as ads, on which platforms, for how long.
3. **Approved claims list** for the product, from `check-ad-copy-for-policy-and-claims`.
4. **Market** of the creator and of the buyers (India, US, both).
5. **Payment route** and tax details the store needs (invoice, GSTIN or PAN in India; W-9 in the US).

Optional: the creator's own contract or rate card; past agreements to bring into the rights register.

Access: none for drafting. Sending terms to a creator, signing and paying are client actions and need a fresh yes each time.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Fee within the profit cap.** The total (fee + usage + exclusivity + product) sits within the cap from `find-and-vet-influencers`. Fail: cut usage scope or switch to commission.
2. **Ownership understood.** In the US the creator owns what they make; a paid video becomes the brand's only through a signed written agreement, so the contract must grant a licence or an assignment [ORG-6421]. Fail: no ad use until signed.
3. **Claims are backed.** Every claim the creator may say is on the approved list. Endorsements must reflect the creator's honest view and real use [MCR-6025]. Fail: remove the claim.
4. **Category rules.** In India, health and finance creators giving advice must hold and state a qualification [ORG-6420]. Fail: limit the brief to showing the product.

## Steps

### 1. Fix the deliverables

For each piece: format (Reel, TikTok, Story, YouTube integration, raw footage), length, number, post date window, platform, caption must-haves (tag, link, code), and whether raw files are included. Raw footage and extra hooks are worth asking for: they let the ad team cut variants without another shoot.

### 2. Set review rounds

One draft review with written feedback within 48 hours, one revision round included, extra rounds priced. The brand checks facts, claims and the label; it does not script the creator's opinion.

### 3. Define usage rights (the costly part)

Write each right as its own line with a duration:

| Right | Typical choice | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Organic repost on brand accounts | Included, perpetual or 12 months | Credit the creator |
| Paid ads from the brand's handle | 30, 60 or 90 days, renewable | Priced separately |
| Partnership ads or Spark ads from the creator's handle | Same term as paid ads | Needs the creator's in-app permission, which they can revoke [ADC-6081] |
| Edits and cutdowns | Allowed: trims, captions, new hooks from their footage | No changes to what they said |
| AI edits of face or voice | Not allowed unless written separately | Disclose any synthetic change where the platform requires it |
| Territory | Countries where ads will run | |
| Website and email use | Included or priced | |

Practitioner default terms, test them with the owner. Price paid usage as a flat fee per term, never a share of ad spend, which is hard to track and audit [ORG-0033].

### 4. Exclusivity

Only if needed, and narrow: named competitor brands or one product category, for the post window plus up to 30 days. Exclusivity costs the creator income, so it is paid for; if the fee cannot carry it, drop it.

### 5. Disclosure clause

- **US:** the relationship stated in the post in plain words, early and on screen and spoken in video; platform labels alone are not enough [ORG-6405].
- **India:** an ASCI label (Ad, Advertisement, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift) upfront, kept on screen for the required time [ORG-6406]; live streams announce it at the start and end [ORG-6420].
- **Both:** the creator turns on the platform's paid partnership tool as well. The store checks every post and asks for fixes; the creator agrees to edit within 24 hours. The advertiser has a duty to train and monitor [CXP-6303].

### 6. Payment terms

- Fee, currency, and split (practitioner default: 50% on signing, 50% on going live, or 100% on going live for small fees).
- Commission rate, which orders count, holding period until the return window closes, and that refunded orders cancel commission.
- Usage renewal price set now.
- **India:** GST invoice if the creator is registered; TDS on fees as the accountant advises; products the creator keeps may need TDS under Section 194R [FIN-6423].
- **US:** W-9 before first payment; the store may need to issue Form 1099. Through Shopify Collabs, a missing requested W-9 caps payouts at 599.99 dollars a year [ORG-6422].

### 7. Cancellation, takedown and conduct

- Kill fee if the store cancels after filming (practitioner default: 50%).
- Store may stop ads and ask for removal of a post that breaks the claims or disclosure terms.
- Creator may withdraw ad permission at the end of the term; the store stops ads within 48 hours.
- A conduct clause allowing the store to end the deal if the creator's conduct seriously harms the brand.
- No fake engagement: the creator does not buy views, likes or comments on the content.

### 8. Fill the rights register

One row per asset, so the ad team can check before launching any ad.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every deliverable has a format, count and date window.
- Every right has a duration and territory; nothing says "forever, all media" by default.
- Paid ad use is priced separately from the post.
- The disclosure clause matches the market where buyers see the post.
- Every product claim in the brief is on the approved list.
- Tax forms and invoice needs are listed for the creator's country.
- The pack says plainly that a lawyer checks the final contract.
- Nothing was sent, signed or paid without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** creator, total cost split into fee, usage and exclusivity, the rights granted with end dates, and the three open points.

**Term sheet** (one page per creator):

```
Creator / handle / country:
Deliverables: [format x count, dates, raw files Y/N]
Review: [rounds, turnaround]
Usage: organic [term] | paid brand handle [term, territory] | partnership or Spark [term] | edits [scope] | AI edits [no / written terms]
Exclusivity: [none / brands or category, dates, fee]
Disclosure: [exact label and placement for the market]
Claims: [approved list attached]
Payment: [fee, split, commission, holding period, tax forms]
Cancellation: [kill fee, takedown, permission withdrawal]
Signed: [no / date]
```

**Checklist:** one line for each point in steps 1 to 7, grouped under Deliverables, Review, Usage, Exclusivity, Disclosure, Claims, Payment, Tax, Cancellation, Conduct, Data, Signatures, each marked agreed, open or not needed.

**Rights register:** Asset ID, Creator, Asset link, Organic until, Paid until, Partnership permission (Y/N, expiry), Territory, Edits allowed, Renewal price, Contract link.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming payment means ownership.** Gate 2 requires a written licence or assignment before ad use.
- **"All rights forever" asks.** Creators price them high or refuse; step 3 buys only the terms the ad plan needs.
- **Usage priced as a share of spend.** Step 3 prices it flat per term.
- **Losing track of expiry dates.** The register flags when ads must stop or renew.
- **Disclosure left to the creator.** Step 5 writes it into the contract and the store checks every post.
- **Scripted opinions.** Step 2 checks facts, not feelings; opinions must be the creator's own.

- MCR-6025: endorsements must be honest and from real use.

- ORG-0033: flat fees for paid use, never a share of spend.

- ORG-6406: ASCI permitted labels and on-screen durations.
- CXP-6303: advertisers must train and monitor rewarded endorsers.
- FIN-6423: India TDS on products creators keep.
- ORG-6422: Collabs W-9 payout cap and tax forms.
