# Lifestyle photos on a small budget

**You get:** a one-day home shoot plan (scenes, a numbered 25-to-30 frame shot list, light and phone settings, props from home, timed schedule, consent forms) and a pick sheet that turns the day into 20 usable, named lifestyle images at 4:5, 1:1 and 9:16.

## When to use

- The store needs lifestyle images for ads, Instagram or product pages and has no photographer budget.
- Ads with only plain product shots are tiring, and the store wants real-life settings.
- A new product needs in-use images this week.
- The founder, team or a friendly customer can model.

## Not for

- A professional studio day: use `art-direct-product-photoshoot`.
- Creator or UGC video at home: use `plan-ugc-shoot-at-home`.
- Plain feed and catalogue images: use `product-image-library-by-channel`.
- AI-generated scenes: use `campaign-photo-set`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The products** in final packaging and the exact variants sold, clean, with a spare of each if possible.
2. **A phone** from the last few years with a clean lens, and enough storage for about 300 photos.
3. **Where buyers use the product**, from reviews and customer photos ("I keep it on my desk", "I take it to the gym").
4. **The moodboard** or 5 reference images the store likes (`brand-moodboard-for-shoots`), and 2 it does not.
5. **People:** who will appear (founder, team, friends, customers), and their written consent.

Optional: a tripod or phone clamp, a white foam board or white sheet as a reflector, a cheap clamp light.

All planning and editing is done without any account access. Posting or running the images is the client's decision.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **The products to shoot earn money.** Shoot the products with the highest gross profit per order times sales first [MEA-0037]. Fail: pick again; a day spent on a slow, low-margin item is wasted.
2. **Consent is signed** for every person whose face or body appears, covering ads, website and social, before the shoot. For minors, a parent or guardian signs. Fail: shoot hands only.
3. **The home is safe to show.** No house number, street view, family photos, documents, screens with personal data or other brands' logos in frame. Fail: move or cover them.
4. **The look is agreed.** The client has picked the reference images. Fail: shoot one test scene and get a yes before the full day.

## Steps

### 1. Pick 4 to 5 scenes from real use

From reviews and customer photos, list where and when the product is used. Choose 4 to 5 scenes the home can stage, for example: kitchen counter in the morning, desk by a window, bathroom shelf, bag being packed at the door, balcony or garden. A niche buyer only feels spoken to when the photos show their world too, not just the headline [AVT-0015].

### 2. Write the shot list (25 to 30 frames)

For each scene, plan 5 to 7 frames that mix:

- **Wide:** the whole setting, product clearly placed, room to add words.
- **Medium:** hands using the product.
- **Close:** the product in the setting, label readable.
- **Moment:** a person mid-action (pouring, applying, packing), product in frame.
- **Vertical:** composed for 9:16 with the product in the middle band, clear of the top 14%, bottom 35% and side 6% where Meta puts its buttons [MCR-6040].

Shoot each frame in both vertical and horizontal so 4:5, 1:1 and 9:16 crops are possible. Twenty keepers from 25 to 30 planned frames allows for misses.

### 3. Light without a studio

- Shoot by a large window with daylight, product side-on to the window, never with the window behind the product.
- Bounce light back with white foam board, a white sheet or a white wall on the far side.
- Avoid direct midday sun; use sheer curtains to soften it. Best light is usually mid-morning or late afternoon, practitioner default.
- Turn off room lights; mixed colours of light turn skin and products odd.
- Check the product's real colour beside the phone screen in that light.

### 4. Phone settings

- Use the main camera (1x), not the ultra-wide, which bends products at the edges.
- Turn off beauty, portrait blur and filters for product frames; portrait mode can blur product edges.
- Tap to focus on the product label and lower exposure slightly so whites do not burn out.
- Turn on the grid. Keep the phone level with the product for true shape.
- Shoot in the highest quality setting. Clean the lens before every scene.

### 5. Props from home

List what each scene needs from the house: mugs, books, towels, plants, a laptop with the screen off, bags. Props show the buyer's life; they never outshine the product. Two to three props per frame, in colours that match the moodboard. No other brand's logo in sight.

### 6. Run the day

Timed plan for one day, practitioner default:

- 08:30 set up scene 1, test frame, check colour.
- 09:00 to 12:00 scenes 1 to 3 (follow the window light).
- 12:00 break, back up the files.
- 13:00 to 15:30 scenes 4 and 5.
- 15:30 to 16:30 reshoot any must-have frame that failed.
- 16:30 to 18:00 pick and export.

### 7. Pick 20 and export

Look at every frame at full size, not as thumbnails. Keep a frame only if:

- the product is sharp, the label reads, and the colour matches the real product;
- the setting looks like the buyer's life, not a staged catalogue;
- no personal data, other brands or mess in the frame;
- the person, if any, has signed consent.

Rough, real images can work as ads; high-volume native statics have beaten polished production for some advertisers [MCR-0060]. For a premium brand, keep the rough look in the setting and keep the product shots themselves clean; lo-fi then reads as contrast, not as a cheap product [MCR-0010].

Edit lightly: straighten, crop, exposure, white balance, the same settings across a scene so the set matches. No retouching that changes the product. Export JPG at about 2,000 px on the long side and compress under 500 KB, ideally 100 to 200 KB, for web [SPD-0008].

### 8. Know where each image can go

Lifestyle images are for ads, social, email and the product gallery. They are not the Google Shopping main image, which must be a clean product shot without props or overlays [GAD-6043]. As an extra Shopping image or a product page image they help; one agency found soft-background images beat plain white on Shopping click-through [GAD-0019].

### Market notes

- **Every market:** show real homes and real use for the buyer's own country: the kitchens, weather, clothing and rooms they know, as fits the product. Seasonal scenes are worth one slot before the store's peak seasons (Black Friday, holidays, local festivals or wedding season). Get consent forms in the language the person reads best.
- **US:** a model release from each adult; check that any shared or rented home allows photography for commercial use.
- **UK and EU:** a signed release as well; under GDPR a person's image is personal data, so record consent and how long you will use the photos.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Twenty images, each named and tagged to a scene and use.
- Every person shown has signed consent covering ads, site and social.
- The product colour and label are true in every image.
- No personal data, house numbers, other brands or screens with private content.
- Each image exists at 4:5 and 1:1, and at least 6 at 9:16 with the safe bands clear.
- Files are compressed for web and the originals are backed up.
- No lifestyle image is set as a Google Shopping main image.

## Common mistakes

- **Shooting everything.** Gate 1 starts with the products that earn the most.
- **Scenes from the owner's taste.** Step 1 picks scenes from where buyers really use the product [AVT-0015].
- **Horizontal only.** Step 2 shoots both, with vertical frames inside Meta's safe bands [MCR-6040].
- **Portrait mode and filters.** Step 4 turns them off so the product stays true.
- **Huge files on the site.** Step 7 compresses them [SPD-0008].
- **Lifestyle as the feed image.** Step 8 keeps it off Google's main image slot [GAD-6043].
