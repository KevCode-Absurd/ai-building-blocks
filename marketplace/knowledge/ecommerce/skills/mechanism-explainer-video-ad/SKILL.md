# Animated mechanism explainer ad

**You get:** a production package for a 30 to 40 second animated explainer ad: a checked source log, an angle table, a timed script, a style card, shot cards, a freelancer brief, a QC log and a test plan.

Some products do not sell until the buyer understands why the problem keeps coming back. A short animated film can show what a camera cannot: inside a pipe, a strand of hair, a mattress under weight. It only works if the science is true and true of this product. This skill checks that first, then builds the film.

## When to use

- Buyers do not understand why the product works, and that caps conversion.
- The category is crowded and a real mechanism is the store's difference.
- The product works at a scale or speed a camera cannot show.
- A founder story exists but the founder will not appear; animation can carry it [MCR-0005].

## Not for

- Products whose benefit is obvious in a quick demo: use `wordless-video-openers` or `ai-creator-style-video-ad`.
- Clay, comic or talking-product formats: use `stylised-animated-ad-test`.
- The landing page the ad points to: use `store-meta-landing`.
- Picking the concept: use `store-meta-concepts`.

## What you need

**Required**

- Product SKU, its label or spec (ingredients and amounts, materials, dimensions), destination URL.
- The store's mechanism claim in one sentence and its evidence (studies, lab tests, supplier data).
- Product photos where every label line is legible at full size; never a crop from a styled ad.
- Brand files (colours, voice lines) and buyer notes.
- Gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase.

**Optional**

- Reviews CSV for how buyers describe the problem; a long-form page or advertorial.

**Access.** Level 1, read. Tools, voice talent or a motion designer need a yes and a cap. Paused upload needs level 2 and a yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money known.** Gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase exist. Fail: run `store-meta-math`.
2. **Mechanism verified (step 1) before any production.** Fail: stop and report the missing evidence.
3. **Claim category clear.** Health, supplements, skincare, weight or children's products: the owner confirms the claims the product may make. Fail: build a non-health angle or stop.
4. **Cash and capacity.** Production plus test cost (3 times target cost per purchase [ADC-0039]) fits the cap. Fail: build a 15-second cut first.
5. **A matching page exists.** Fail: use a clear opening and the product page.

## Steps

### 1. Check the mechanism against a primary source

Write the chain in three links: what causes the problem, what the product does, why that changes the outcome. For each, find a primary source (peer-reviewed study, standards or regulator page, a test report on this product, or the supplier's study at the dose used). Log link, source, date, what it says, and whether it applies to this product's dose, material or design.

- Every link has a source that says what the script will say.
- It is true of this product, not only the ingredient in general; a mechanism claim needs a real product difference [AVT-0014].
- No link becomes a disease, cure or drug claim [MCR-0073]. Check your market's health claim and ad disclosure rules (for example FTC and FDA rules in the US, the ASA's CAP code and MHRA in the UK, the EU health claims rules, TGA in Australia).
- A "why now" or "modern life broke it" story needs evidence the product addresses that cause [MCR-0004]; "natural" or "how people lived long ago" is not proof.

Rewrite a failing link to what the source supports, or drop the angle. General practice, not a legal ruling.

### 2. Offer three to five complete angles

An angle is a whole film, not a reworded hook. Each differs in opening image, mechanism taught, and the everyday cause it names. Read the market stage: unaware buyers need a felt symptom first; buyers who know the usual fixes need to see why those fail; a saturated category needs a genuinely new mechanism [ADC-0001].

Screen each angle; drop any that fails two of these:

1. Would a regular buyer say "obviously"? (fail)
2. Is there one sentence that makes people stop?
3. Is the cause something people currently trust or ignore, rather than a known villain?
4. Could a viewer retell it to a friend?
5. Would it be interesting even if the product did not exist? (the hardest test: education that only sets up a sale feels like a pitch)

Per angle, in under 120 words: name, opening image and line, mechanism, everyday cause, payoff, best-fit buyer, source, any claim risk. Make one angle the plain control. Recommend one; the owner picks.

### 3. Write the script

Shape for about 35 seconds:

1. **Scale opener:** a surprising, sourced fact over an image no camera could take.
2. **Reframe:** the real cause, stated plainly. Not "it's not X, it's Y" [MCR-0016].
3. **Mechanism:** shown inside the body, material or object; educate a few seconds before the product [MCR-0014]; the why, not an ingredient list [MCR-0032]; one key term, repeated so it sticks [MCR-0066].
4. **Why it got worse:** the everyday cause, sourced.
5. **The product as the fix:** named and shown here. For sensitive problems an illustrated style avoids graphic images, but rules still apply [MCR-0020].
6. **Payoff:** a physical result the facts support, ideally a usage line ("two before bed").
7. **Close:** call back to the opening line and image; offer and ask.

Any claim on screen gets an asterisk and a short source footer. A prevalence line ("most people never notice") needs credible data and must not imply the viewer has a condition [MCR-0018].

### 4. Size the script from measured pace

First pass: about 105 to 120 words for 40 seconds (practitioner default). Render the voice, measure words per minute, recompute words = seconds x (measured words per minute / 60), and rewrite to fit. Budget two voice renders. Do not slow a voice down to fill time; it drags.

### 5. Voice, then a listening gate

Use a licensed voice actor or a stock synthetic voice; never clone a real person's voice without written consent. Respell hard brand and ingredient names phonetically in the text sent to the voice engine; captions keep the correct spelling. Check pronunciation with a transcript made without feeding it the script. Then play the voice to the owner and wait for a yes. This is the last checkpoint before real video spend.

### 6. Lock the style and write shots

Style card for every shot: rendered 3D look that is clearly animation, not photo; palette from brand colours; slow continuous camera moves; light; no text, logos or watermarks in renders. People and hands are clearly rendered; the product stays photoreal and locked to the reference photos. Background crowds are fully clothed.

12 to 14 shots of about 3 seconds. Per shot: voice line, visual, image prompt, image-to-video prompt (motion, camera move, duration, end frame). Chain only the two or three joins where a continuous move carries the story (scale opener into one person, travelling inside the mechanism, the closing pull-back); join the rest with short dissolves, so a bad shot can be redone alone. If a person recurs, make one character reference still and attach it to every shot.

**Manual path:** brief a freelance 3D or motion designer with script, style card, shot list and sources, or build simple shots in free tools such as Blender.

### 7. Captions and assembly

One to three words per caption card, one highlighted word (the loaded one), timed to the spoken word, centred in the lower middle, readable with sound off. Dissolves at most joins. Do not digitally zoom low-resolution shots far; use a longer dissolve.

### 8. Check the finished cut

Watch each shot against the source log; visuals can overclaim when words do not (bacteria vanishing when the product only reduces them). Check the product label at three points in every product shot, no third-party logos, same character throughout, ending matches the opening. Re-ask test 5 from step 2 on the finished film. Fix or cut what fails.

### 9. Destination and test

Curious openings need an educational page [MCR-0024]; complex products often convert better with a longer matched page than the product page [MCR-0033]. Own ad set, best current video as control, 2 to 4 opening variants at modest budgets [MCR-0013]. Keep or cut at 3 times target cost per purchase on store orders [ADC-0039].

## Checks before you hand it over

1. Every mechanism link has a primary source that applies to this product.
2. No disease, cure or drug claim in words or visuals; claims carry an asterisk and footer.
3. The voice is licensed or stock, was heard and approved before video spend, and fits the length.
4. People read as rendered; the product matches its photos at three points per shot.
5. The finished film passed the second fact-check and the interest test.
6. The destination page matches the opening.
7. Costs fit the cap; nothing is live without a yes.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** mechanism verdict, chosen angle, length, cost, destination, read date.
2. **Source log:** link, source, date, what it says, applies to this product.
3. **Angle table:** name, opening, mechanism, cause, payoff, buyer, test results, risk.
4. **Script table:** time, voice line (spoken and phonetic), caption, visual, source.
5. **Style card, shot cards, freelancer brief, QC log** and **test plan**.

## Common mistakes

- **Animating a mechanism nobody checked.** Step 1 comes first.
- **True of the ingredient, not the product.** Step 1 checks dose and design.
- **A script too long for its runtime.** Step 4 sizes it from measured pace.
- **Paying for video behind an unheard voice.** Step 5 gates it.
- **Pictures that overclaim.** Step 8 checks visuals.
- **Education that is only a pitch.** Step 2 test 5 catches it.
