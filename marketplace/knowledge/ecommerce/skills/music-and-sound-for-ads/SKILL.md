# Music and sound for ads

**You get:** a sound plan per ad: the sound's job, the chosen track with licence proof, a timed sound effects list, voice and music levels, a sound-off fallback check, and a rights log row, ready for the editor and safe to run as a paid ad.

## When to use

- A video ad is in the edit and needs music or sound.
- A creator's video with a trending song is about to run as a paid or partnership ad.
- An ad was muted, blocked or claimed for copyright.
- The store wants a sound that stays the same across its ads.

## Not for

- Writing or recording the voiceover: use `voiceover-script-and-voice-pick`.
- Captions and text for muted viewing: use `captions-and-text-for-sound-off`.
- Usage rights for a creator's own video: use `influencer-contract-and-usage-rights` or `run-creator-partnership-ads`.
- Organic Reels where the post is not boosted: the in-app library rules for that account type apply; check them in the app.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The cut,** with the voiceover if there is one, and section timings.
2. **Placements:** Meta (Facebook, Instagram), TikTok, YouTube, others, and whether each is paid, boosted or organic.
3. **Account types:** business or personal accounts on each platform.
4. **Any track already chosen,** with where it came from.
5. **Gross profit per order** from `store-meta-math`.

Optional: brand sound notes, past ads with music the client liked, and the raw shoot audio with product sounds.

Access: read only. Buying a music licence or subscription is a purchase: the client buys it or says yes first.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money.** Gross profit per order is known from delivered costs [MEA-0037]. Paid library fees count as production cost spread over the ads that use them [MCR-0023].
2. **Licence covers this use.** For every track: the licence names advertising or commercial use, the platform, paid placements, and the term. Fail: replace the track.
3. **No borrowed fame.** No popular song without a licence from its owners, no sound-alike of a named artist, no "under 30 seconds is fine" assumptions. In India, film and label music trending on Reels still belongs to its label; trending does not mean licensed.
4. **Disclosure.** AI-generated music that is the main focus of a realistic video needs disclosure on YouTube [MCR-6064]; background beds generally do not, but check each platform on the day.

## Steps

### 1. Decide the sound's job

| Ad type | Sound's job | Music |
|---|---|---|
| Voice-led (creator, founder, narration) | Keep the voice clear | Quiet bed or none |
| Music-led (trend, mood, fashion) | Set pace and feeling | Foreground, cut to the beat |
| Product-sound-led (pour, click, crunch, cooling) | Make the product felt | Minimal; product sounds up front |

On most direct-response ads, sound is a small part of the hook and the opening copy does the heavy lifting [MCR-0063]; music-led formats are the exception. Products felt within seconds lean on their own sounds and reactions [MCR-0045]. Satisfying real-time openers work with sound on [MCR-0003].

### 2. Choose the licence path per platform

Free first, then paid:

- **Meta ads:** Meta's own Sound Collection, offered for use in content on Meta's apps. Read its terms in the account before use.
- **TikTok ads and business accounts:** TikTok's Commercial Music Library, the library meant for business and ad use. Business accounts should not use the general music library.
- **YouTube:** the YouTube Audio Library; tracks can be used in monetised videos without Content ID claims, Creative Commons tracks need the artist credited, and YouTube gives no guidance on use off YouTube [MCR-6065].
- **Several platforms or the website:** a paid music library whose plan names paid social ads on each platform you run. Some plans cover only organic creator use; check the plan page and save a copy.
- **AI music tools:** only on a plan whose terms grant commercial use of outputs; never prompt for a named artist's style or voice.
- **Original music:** a commissioned composer with a written buyout or licence for ads.

A track cleared in one platform's library is not cleared on another.

### 3. Pick the track

- Tempo fits the cut: fast cuts want faster beats; calm demos want slower.
- No lyrics under spoken words.
- A clear change (a lift or drop) near the product reveal, so the cut can land on it.
- The first three seconds already sound right; long intros waste the hook.
- Length covers the ad, or loops cleanly.
- Pick two candidates and play each under the cut on a phone speaker; keep the one that makes the voice easier to follow.

### 4. List the sound effects

Write each effect against its timecode: the cap click, the pour, the zip, the box tape, the sizzle. Record the product's own sounds at the shoot where possible (`plan-ugc-shoot-at-home`); real sounds beat library ones. Free effects: the YouTube Audio Library effects, or record them on a phone in a quiet room. One effect per action; too many sound fake.

### 5. Set the levels

- Voice always on top. Music under speech sits well below it; practitioner default about 15 to 20 dB under the voice, then raised between lines (ducking).
- Product sounds at about the level of the voice for their moment.
- Even overall loudness across the store's ads; practitioner default about minus 14 LUFS integrated, since platforms adjust loudness anyway.
- Final check on a phone speaker and on cheap earphones. If any word is lost, drop the music further.

### 6. Check the sound-off fallback

Watch the ad muted. Every point the voice or sound makes must also be in text or picture (`captions-and-text-for-sound-off`). Sound adds feeling; it should never carry the only copy of the message.

### 7. Clear creator and partnership sounds

For a creator post running as a partnership or Spark ad, check the sound in the original post. Trending songs used under the creator's personal account terms are usually not cleared for the brand's paid use. Replace it with a library track before paying to promote, or get the owner's licence.

### 8. Fill the rights log

One row per track per ad: track title, artist or library, source link, licence type, licence ID or a saved screenshot of the terms, date downloaded, platforms covered, paid use allowed, term or expiry, attribution needed. Keep the file with the ad.

### 9. India and US notes

- **India:** many buyers watch with sound on in regional languages; a familiar folk or festive feel can help, but only from a licensed library. Avoid devotional music in sales ads unless the brand and audience fit; it can offend.
- **US:** a copyright claim on a paid ad can stop delivery mid-campaign; the rights log is the fastest way to contest a wrong claim.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every track has a licence that names commercial or ad use on each platform it runs.
- No popular or label-owned song is used without its owner's licence.
- Creator post sounds were checked before any paid promotion.
- The voice is clear on a phone speaker in every line.
- Effects are timed and few.
- The ad makes its point muted.
- The rights log row is complete, with proof saved.
- Any library purchase had the client's yes.

## Common mistakes

- **Trending song on a paid ad.** Gate 3 and step 7 replace it.
- **One platform's licence used on another.** Step 2 clears per platform.
- **Music fighting the voice.** Step 5 ducks it and tests on a phone.
- **Library effects for everything.** Step 4 records the product's real sounds.
- **No proof of licence.** Step 8 saves it with the ad.
- **Sound carrying the message alone.** Step 6 checks the muted version.
