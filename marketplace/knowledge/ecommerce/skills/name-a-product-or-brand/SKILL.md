# Name a product or brand

**You get:** a shortlist of 3 to 5 names, each with its meaning and reasoning, a check table (domain, handles, India and US trademark search, marketplace and search clashes, language meanings), one recommended name and runner-up, and a hand-off note for the trademark attorney.

## When to use

- A new store or brand needs a name before the logo, domain and packaging.
- A new product or line needs its own name.
- The current name clashes with another brand, is hard to say or spell, or limits growth.
- A sub-line is planned and the team must choose between the house name and a new one.

## Not for

- What the brand stands for and how it is different: use `write-brand-positioning-and-messaging` first; names come after positioning.
- How the brand sounds in copy: use `brand-voice-guide`.
- Logo, colours and type: use `store-visual-identity-sheet`.
- Product titles for listings and feeds: use `write-product-titles-for-store-and-marketplaces`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Positioning in one paragraph:** who it is for, what it does, what it stands against. If missing, run `write-brand-positioning-and-messaging` or write a draft with the owner and mark it as draft.
2. **Markets and languages:** where it will sell (India, US, both) and the languages its buyers speak.
3. **Goods it covers now and in three years** (to judge trademark classes and stretch).
4. **Names the owner already likes or rejects**, with why.

Optional: rival brand list; budget for domain purchase; customer phrases from reviews or interviews.

Access: research is read only (level 1). Buying a domain, registering handles or filing a trademark spends money or makes commitments: draft the list and wait for the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Positioning exists.** Pass: one agreed paragraph. Fail: stop and write it; a name cannot fix an unclear promise.
2. **House name or new name.** Pass: decided. Rule of thumb: a refill, companion or variant sits under the house name; a different buyer or job may earn its own name. Fail: decide with the owner first.
3. **Rename risk.** If renaming, the store needs a clear story of why and what buyers gain, and should plan 6 to 9 months of effort; renames launched that way have not been reported to lose momentum [BRD-0020]. Also plan redirects, marketplace brand records and packaging run-out.
4. **Legal limit.** This skill screens names. Registrability is an attorney's or examiner's call. Every survivor goes to one before money is spent.

## Steps

### 1. Write three different briefs

Write three short naming briefs from the positioning, each from a different angle: the feeling the brand must carry, the buyer's world, and the product's main benefit. Start each from one jumping-off word for the feeling; short names of about four letters are the ideal one naming firm aims for [BRD-0002].

### 2. Generate wide

Produce at least 200 candidates across the three briefs before judging any. Professional teams generate around 2,000 per project, while most founders stall at 50 to 100 [BRD-0018]. Use these routes:

- Real words from another world (a borrowed image).
- Coined words built from sounds that carry the right feel. Research across six languages found B the most reliable-sounding English letter, P, K and B read reliable and fast, D, P and Z fast, and X signals newness [BRD-0019]. Use as a nudge, not a rule.
- Compounds and blends.
- Founder or place names, if the story is real.
- Descriptive names, kept only for comparison.

### 3. Cut to 20 on fit

Score each candidate quickly on: says or suggests the promise, easy to say after hearing it once, easy to spell after hearing it once, distinct from rivals, room to grow into the three-year range.

Beware the comfortable descriptive name that tests well; a coined name like Swiffer grew to about $5 billion while a descriptive rival stayed at a few hundred million [BRD-0017]. A name that splits the team has energy; one everybody agrees on often goes unnoticed [BRD-0016]. Keep at least a few names that make someone uneasy.

### 4. Screen the 20

For each, check and record with the date:

- **Trademark, US.** Search the USPTO's free trademark search for the name, sound-alikes, other spellings and same-meaning words in related goods. Marks need not be identical to conflict: similar sound, look, meaning or overall impression on related goods is enough [BRD-6484]. A full clearance also covers state registers and unregistered use found on the web and marketplaces [BRD-6485].
- **Trademark, India.** Search IP India's public trade mark search (wordmark and phonetic) in the classes for the goods; note pending and registered marks.
- **Marketplaces and search.** Search Amazon, Flipkart, Nykaa, Etsy and Google for the name in the category. A seller using it on similar goods is a likely conflict even if unregistered.
- **Domain.** Check the .com, .in (for India) and a clean alternative. Avoid hyphens and numbers. Note premium prices.
- **Handles.** Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, X and, for US stores, TikTok. Close variants are acceptable; note which.
- **Company names.** In India, check the Ministry of Corporate Affairs name search if a company will carry the name; in the US, the state business register.
- **Meaning.** Ask native speakers what it means or sounds like in Hindi and the store's other buyer languages (Tamil, Bengali, Marathi, Spanish for US Hispanic buyers). Check slang and rhymes.

Drop any name with a clear trademark conflict, an existing seller on similar goods, or a bad meaning.

### 5. Test the survivors

Show 5 to 8 survivors to 10 to 20 target buyers (not the team), spoken first, then written:

- Spell it after hearing it.
- What do you think this brand sells?
- Which would you trust most with {product}? Why?

Report counts. Treat this as a check for problems (misspelling, wrong meaning), not a vote for the winner; liking scores favour safe names [BRD-0016].

### 6. Pick and hand over

Recommend one name and a runner-up with the reasoning: promise, sound, distinctness, screen results, test notes. Write the hand-off for the trademark attorney: name, goods and services description, markets, classes to consider, conflicts found and their dates.

## Checks before you hand it over

- At least 200 candidates were generated from three briefs.
- Every shortlisted name has a dated trademark search in each market it will sell in.
- Sound-alikes and same-meaning names were searched, not only exact spellings.
- Marketplace and web use on similar goods was checked.
- Domain and handle status is recorded with alternatives.
- Meanings were checked by native speakers in the buyers' languages.
- The report says plainly that an attorney must clear the name before purchase or filing.
- Nothing was bought or registered without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** the recommended name, what it means and why it fits, its screen results, the runner-up, and the next step (attorney clearance, then domain and handles).

## Common mistakes

- **Naming before positioning.** Gate 1 requires it.
- **Stopping at 50 names.** Step 2 pushes past 200.
- **Choosing the safe name everyone likes.** Step 3 keeps names with energy; step 5 tests for problems, not votes.
- **Exact-match trademark searches only.** Step 4 checks sound, spelling and meaning.
- **Forgetting India's languages.** Step 4 checks meanings with native speakers.
- **Buying the domain first.** Step 6 sends survivors to counsel before spend.
