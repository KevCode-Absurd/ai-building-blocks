# NNN agreement to protect your design

**You get:** a one-page brief for the owner (why an NNN, what it covers, what it does not), a draft NNN in Chinese and English filled for this factory, a share and hold-back list, and a lawyer check list. It is a draft for a lawyer to finish, not legal advice.

## When to use

- The store has its own design, pattern, mould drawing, formula or packaging artwork and is about to send it to a factory in China.
- A factory asked for the full tech pack before quoting.
- A past supplier started selling a close copy of the store's product.
- The store is moving a product from one Chinese factory to another.

## Not for

- Catalogue or stock products with only a logo added: there is little to protect; use the normal RFQ (`write-rfq-and-compare-quotes`).
- Quality, delivery and tooling terms: an NNN does not set these; use `purchase-order-and-supply-terms`.
- Factories outside China: ask a lawyer in that country what works there.
- Registering trademarks or design patents: ask the lawyer; this skill only lists the question.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The factory's legal name in Chinese**, exactly as on its business licence (营业执照), its unified social credit code (统一社会信用代码) and registered address.
2. **What is being shared**: product names, drawings, tech pack version, mould drawings, artwork, formulas.
3. **The store's legal entity** and signer.
4. **The products and designs to cover**, described so a stranger could recognise them.

Optional: the store's trademarks and where they are registered, any past copying evidence, the expected order value.

Wenhao and Maxine draft at level 2. The owner signs only after a lawyer qualified in China has checked it. Nothing confidential is sent to the factory before signing.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Worth protecting.** Pass: the store's design, mould or formula is its own and not public. Fail: skip the NNN and use a normal RFQ.
2. **Right party.** Pass: the Chinese legal name and credit code match the licence and the company that will make the product. Fail: an NNN signed with a trading company or the wrong entity may not bind the factory that makes the goods. Find the real maker first.
3. **Few factories, first.** Pass: the store will share the design with one or two qualified factories only. Fail: cut the list; for a sensitive new design, qualify one or two factories before sharing drawings, rather than sending them to 20.

## Steps

### 1. Explain why NDA is the wrong tool

With a Chinese factory, the bigger risk is not that it tells others your secret. It is that it uses your design itself, sells it to others, or goes round you to your customers. A Western NDA covers disclosure only and is hard to enforce in China. An NNN covers all three:

- **Non-use:** the factory may not use your design or information except to make your goods.
- **Non-disclosure:** it may not share them.
- **Non-circumvention:** it may not go round you to sell your product to your customers or to sell to others.

### 2. Make it enforceable where the factory is

The draft should be written in Chinese (with an English copy for the owner), name Chinese law as governing law, and name a Chinese court with jurisdiction over the factory. A judgment from your home court is hard to enforce against a factory in China; a Chinese court's judgment can be enforced there. The Chinese text controls.

### 3. Name the parties and the products exactly

- The factory's Chinese legal name, credit code and address, and any related companies that could make the goods.
- The store's legal name and address.
- Each product, design and document covered, with drawings or photos attached as a schedule.
- What does not count as confidential (already public, already the factory's own design before you shared, with proof).

### 4. Set a fixed damages figure

Put a fixed sum per breach (liquidated damages), because proving the real loss in court is hard. Keep it in proportion: Chinese courts may cut damages that are more than 30% above the real loss. The lawyer sets the figure.

### 5. Add the practical terms

- Duration: during the relationship and for a set number of years after (the lawyer sets it).
- Return or destroy all materials on request.
- No reverse engineering of samples you send.
- The factory may not file for trademarks, designs or patents on your products.
- Signed with the company chop (公章) on the Chinese copy and by the legal representative.

### 6. Share in stages

Before signing: product type, rough size, materials, quantities, target price. After signing: full drawings and the tech pack. After a sample is approved and a PO is placed: mould drawings and full artwork files. Keep master design files on the store's side; share PDFs where you can.

### 7. List what the lawyer must confirm

- The party is the right legal entity and can sign.
- Governing law, court and language clauses work for this factory's location.
- The damages figure and duration are enforceable.
- Whether to register the store's trademark in China before production (China works on a first-to-file basis).
- Whether a design patent or other filing is worth it for this product.
- How the NNN fits with the manufacturing agreement.

## Checks before you hand it over

- The party's Chinese legal name and credit code match the business licence.
- Non-use, non-disclosure and non-circumvention are all covered.
- Chinese law, a Chinese court and the Chinese text are named.
- A fixed damages figure is in place, marked for the lawyer.
- Products and designs are listed in a schedule a stranger could recognise.
- Nothing confidential was sent before signing.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending a US-style NDA.** Step 1 uses an NNN.
- **Choosing your home court.** Step 2 names a Chinese court.
- **Signing with the trading company.** Gate 2 finds the real maker.
- **Sending drawings to 20 factories.** Gate 3 and step 6 share in stages.
- **No damages figure.** Step 4 fixes one.
- **Thinking the NNN covers quality.** Not for: that is the supply terms.
