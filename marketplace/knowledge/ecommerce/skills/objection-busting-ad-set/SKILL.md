# One ad per top buying objection

**You get:** five ad concepts in one table, one per top objection, each with the proof that answers it (with its file), the customer type who voices it, the hook and beats, the format, and where it runs; plus the ranked objection list with counts, and a matching review or proof for the product page and abandon messages.

## When to use

- Add-to-cart and checkout starts are healthy but purchases are not.
- Cold ads earn clicks and page views, then few sales.
- The store is raising prices, launching a premium version or adding payment or delivery limits.
- Support and chat keep answering the same five pre-sale questions.

## Not for

- Finding new angles to open with: use `mine-ad-angles-from-reviews`.
- Finished copy sets on one concept: use `ad-copy-test-sets`.
- A whole landing page built on reasons and doubts: use `reasons-to-buy-listicle-page`.
- Chat replies to price pushback: use `handle-price-objections-in-chat`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Objection evidence**, at least two of these: post-purchase survey answers to "what almost stopped you from buying" (Shopify post-purchase survey or a form; free path: a one-question email), pre-sale chat, WhatsApp or email questions (last 90 days), reviews export with low-star and "I almost didn't" reviews, return reasons, and where deliveries are often refused, the refusal and failed-delivery reasons from the courier panel.
2. **Shopify funnel numbers** for 30 to 90 days: sessions, add to cart, reached checkout, orders (Shopify Analytics or GA4).
3. **Store policies as live today**: returns, warranty or guarantee, shipping times, payment options, with URLs.
4. **Claims sheet and proof files** for the product (`product-claims-profile`).
5. **Gross profit per purchase** for each product in scope (`store-meta-math`).

Optional: calls or messages to recent checkout abandoners (only with contact consent), competitor policies for comparison.

With only one evidence source, deliver the concepts as a hypothesis set and add a two-week survey plan. Building ads in an account needs draft access (level 2); turning them on needs the client's yes. Messaging abandoners is the client's call and needs their consent rules.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Gross profit per purchase known.** Fail: concepts can be drafted, but the guarantee, shipping or payment answers that cost money cannot be offered until `store-meta-math` prices them.
2. **Each answer is real.** The policy, guarantee or proof that answers an objection must be live and honoured today. Fail: drop that objection from ads and send it to the owner as a fix to make first.
3. **Two evidence sources.** Fail: label the output hypotheses.
4. **Contact consent.** Calling or messaging abandoners only where the buyer agreed to be contacted; use approved templates for business-started WhatsApp or SMS messages, and follow your market's consent rules (for example TCPA in the US, PECR and GDPR in the UK and EU).

## Steps

### 1. Collect what almost stopped buyers

Pull every stated doubt into one sheet with source, date and product. The best bottom-funnel objections come from people who nearly bought: checkout abandoners asked directly turn up the doubts worth one ad each [MCR-0043]; a "what almost stopped you" question after purchase does the same at no cost [LPG-0005]. Refused deliveries and early returns ("ordered by mistake", "found cheaper", "did not trust delivery") are objections too; note them by region.

### 2. Sort into objection types

Code each doubt to one type: price or value, will it work for me, quality or durability, size or fit, trust in the store (delivery, returns, payment), how to use, not needed now. Keep the buyer's wording beside the code.

### 3. Rank and pick five

Score each type: share of all doubts (count over total), closeness to purchase (checkout and abandon evidence weighs 2, chat 1.5, reviews 1; practitioner default, test it), and whether the store has proof to answer it. An objection with no honest answer is not an ad; it is a product or policy fix for the owner. Pick the top five that can be answered.

### 4. Match proof to each objection

| Objection type | Proof that answers it |
|---|---|
| Price or value | cost per use, the bundle maths, what buyers spent on failed fixes before [LPG-0027]; trust before price [LPG-0017] |
| Will it work for me | a review from a buyer like the viewer, a demonstration, before and after where allowed |
| Quality or durability | warranty length against the category norm, material close-ups, a stress demonstration |
| Size or fit | fit guide, a buyer's measurements in their own review, free exchange policy |
| Trust in the store | a specific delivery date [CXP-0010], returns policy, trusted payment options (wallets, buy now pay later, pay on delivery where used), reviewer name and source shown [LPG-0037] |
| How to use | a 10-second use demonstration |

Each concept uses a testimonial from a different customer type, chosen because it answers that exact objection [LPG-0053], not generic praise [EML-0033]. A longer guarantee can reduce refund claims on low-ticket consumables, but only offer it if the margin absorbs it [OFF-0060].

### 5. Write each concept

One objection per ad. Hook names the doubt in buyer words, as a statement about the product or situation, never as a question about the viewer's body, age or money on Meta [MCR-6023]. Beats: the doubt, the proof, what it means for the buyer, the call to action. Use a real customer's words with permission; label staff or gifted creators in the US, and show what buyers generally get if the featured result is unusual [MCR-6025].

### 6. Decide where each one runs

Under broad targeting, single-objection ads reach near-ready buyers on their own; put them in the prospecting campaign as their own ad set rather than a large retargeting budget [MCR-0044]. Keep existing-customer retargeting small [ADC-0040]. Reuse the same proof in three more places: beside add to cart on the product page, in the matching abandon email, and in the WhatsApp or SMS cart message where buyers agreed to it. Write the slot for each in the table.

### 7. Hand off for production

Each concept goes to `write-one-page-creative-brief`; copy to `ad-copy-test-sets`; page placement to the product page owner.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Five objections, each with a count, a share of all doubts and its evidence sources.
- Every objection is answered by a live, honoured policy or a proof file, linked.
- Each concept handles one objection only.
- Each concept uses a different customer type, with permission for the quote.
- No hook asks about or asserts the viewer's personal attributes.
- Each concept names its campaign, ad set and at least one on-site or message slot.
- Unanswerable objections are listed as fixes for the owner, not turned into ads.
- Nothing is switched on or sent to customers without the client's yes.

## Common mistakes

- **Guessing objections in a meeting.** Step 1 uses what almost stopped real buyers.
- **Answering five doubts in one ad.** Step 5 keeps one objection per ad.
- **Generic "love it" reviews as proof.** Step 4 picks a review that answers that doubt.
- **Promising a policy the store does not honour.** Gate 2 drops it.
- **A big retargeting budget for these ads.** Step 6 runs them in prospecting and on site.
- **Question hooks that Meta rejects.** Step 5 writes the doubt as a statement.
