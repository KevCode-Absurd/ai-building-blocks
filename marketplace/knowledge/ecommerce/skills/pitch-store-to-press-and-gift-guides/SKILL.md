# Pitch the store to press and gift guides

**You get:** a press tracker with 20 to 40 named targets (outlet, writer, recent relevant piece and date, price band, lead time, contact source, status), three ready-to-fill pitch emails, a one-page media kit, and a coverage log that links each mention to sessions and orders. Nothing is sent without the client's yes.

## When to use

- A gifting season (Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, Mother's Day, Black Friday, the holidays) is 6 to 20 weeks away.
- A launch, a restock with a waiting list, or a real milestone can honestly be called news.
- The store has a product that fits a clear recipient and price band ("under 50", "for new parents").
- The owner wants earned coverage and has never pitched.

## Not for

- Paid creator posts and seeding: use `plan-influencer-gifting-campaign` or `write-influencer-outreach`.
- The store's own gift guide page: use `build-gift-guide-page`.
- Corporate gifting sales: use `corporate-gifting-sales-pitch`.
- Joint products with another brand: use `brand-collab-drop`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Product facts:** names, prices, sizes, what makes each different, stock for the season.
2. **Images:** high-resolution product shots on plain background plus 2 to 3 lifestyle shots, with rights cleared.
3. **Proof sheet:** the source for every claim you might make (test reports, review counts with date, awards with year).
4. **Founder or brand story** in the owner's own words.
5. **Sample budget:** how many units the store can send, and to which countries.

Optional: an affiliate programme (some guides only feature products with one), past coverage, customer survey data the store can share.

Building the list and drafts needs no access. Sending email from the client's account needs draft access (level 2) at least, and every send or sample shipment needs the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **A true angle exists.** No angle, no pitch. Fail: wait for a launch or milestone, or build one from the store's own data.
2. **Every claim can be proven.** India's ASCI code wants claims substantiated on request and research cited with source and date [ADC-6146]; in the US every objective claim needs a reasonable basis before it is made [MCR-6022]. Fail: cut the claim.
3. **Stock through the coverage window.** A guide can run weeks after the pitch. Fail: pitch only products with cover through the season.
4. **Email law.** Cold pitches to US addresses fall under CAN-SPAM even when business to business: true sender and subject, postal address, an easy opt-out honoured within 10 business days [EML-6385]. Never use bought or scraped lists.

## Steps

### 1. Pick angles

Write up to three, each one sentence, each true:

- **Gift fit:** "a [product] under [price] for [recipient]". Gift guides are built by price band and recipient.
- **Story:** the founder's real reason, a craft or sourcing detail, a local maker angle.
- **News:** a launch, a restock after a sell-out, a milestone, findings from the store's own customer survey.

A paid-to-use stunt that shows the benefit can also earn coverage at small cost [WLD-0005], but it is a separate bet; do not mix it into gift pitches.

### 2. Work out the lead times

Practitioner default, confirm per outlet: print magazines plan seasonal guides 3 to 6 months ahead; online guides and newsletters 4 to 10 weeks ahead; newspapers' festive supplements in India 4 to 8 weeks ahead. Work back from each season and put a "pitch by" date on every row.

### 3. Build the list

Find 20 to 40 targets in four tiers:

1. Online and print gift guides that featured products in the store's price band last year.
2. Category writers and newsletters (beauty, home, parenting, outdoor).
3. Local and regional press near the founder or workshop.
4. Podcasts and curators with an audience that matches the buyer.

For each: outlet, writer, the recent piece and its date, price band featured, whether they need an affiliate link, contact route (public email, contact form, staff page), where you found it, and the "pitch by" date. Prefer the writer who wrote last year's guide over a general inbox.

### 4. Write the media kit

One page (PDF or a page on the site):

- One-line description of the store and who it is for.
- Founder story in three sentences.
- Products with price, sizes, where to buy, and one honest line each.
- Image link (folder with high-resolution files and usage terms).
- Proof lines with their source and date.
- Sample terms and shipping regions.
- Contact name, email, phone.

### 5. Write the pitch emails

Each email under 150 words, plain text, one image link, no attachments:

- **Gift guide pitch.** Subject names the guide and price band. Line 1: why it fits their guide, naming their last piece. Line 2: the product, price, link. Line 3: one proof line. Line 4: sample offer. Sign-off with name, store, postal address and "reply 'no thanks' and I will not email again".
- **Feature pitch.** Subject is the story in under 9 words. Two short paragraphs: the story and why now; what the writer gets (founder interview, data, photos).
- **Follow-up.** One only, 5 to 7 working days later (practitioner default), adding one new fact. Then stop.

Write each pitch for the person; never send the same email to 40 writers in one blast.

### 6. Paid placements

Some guides charge a fee or require an affiliate deal. A paid placement is an ad: it must be labelled as one; content that looks like editorial must say it is paid under ASCI's rules in India [ADC-6147], and in the US any material connection must be clear [MCR-6025]. Log paid placements separately and judge them like ads, on profit.

### 7. Send, track and use coverage

- The client approves the list and each draft; send in small batches from the client's own address.
- Log replies, samples sent (cost), and coverage with link and date.
- Tag links with UTM parameters where the outlet allows, and add the outlet to the post-purchase "how did you hear" options to see orders it drives [MEA-0045].
- A wire press release can be worth it for genuine news, since AI answers sometimes cite syndicated releases; only publish what is true [AEO-0013].
- When using coverage on the site, show the outlet's actual quote with the logo; bare logos now read as fake [LPG-0037]. Use only what was really published.

### 8. India and US notes

- **India:** include regional and city publications and their festive supplements; offer Hindi or regional-language product notes where the outlet publishes in them; quote prices inclusive of GST.
- **US:** check that sample shipping meets customs and carrier rules for the product; many guides need an affiliate link to feature a product.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every target has a named person, a recent relevant piece with date and a contact source.
- Each row has a "pitch by" date worked back from the outlet's lead time.
- Every claim in the kit and pitches has a source and date on the proof sheet.
- Pitches include sender name, postal address and an opt-out line.
- Only one follow-up is planned per contact.
- Paid placements are marked as ads.
- Products pitched have stock through the season.
- Nothing is sent or shipped without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** angles, number of targets by tier, earliest pitch-by dates, samples needed and their cost, what the client must approve.

**Press tracker** (`press-tracker.csv` or Google Sheet):

| Outlet | Tier | Writer | Recent relevant piece (link, date) | Price band | Affiliate needed? | Lead time | Pitch by | Contact route | Found at | Angle | Status | Sample sent (date, cost) | Coverage (link, date) | Sessions | Orders |

**Pitch emails:** gift guide, feature and follow-up templates with the fields marked. **Media kit** one-pager. **Assumptions:** sample budget and sending address, where not given, as the figure or place used, one line each. Claims are only those with proof on file.

## Common mistakes

- **Pitching too late.** Step 2 puts a "pitch by" date on every row.
- **General inboxes and blasts.** Step 3 names the writer and step 5 writes per person.
- **Claims nobody can prove.** The gates and the proof sheet remove them.
- **Paid placements dressed as editorial.** Step 6 labels them.
- **Endless follow-ups.** Step 5 allows one.
- **Coverage that cannot be measured.** Step 7 adds UTM links and the survey option.

## Sources

- ADC-6146, MCR-6022: claims must be substantiated (India, US).
- EML-6385: CAN-SPAM covers business-to-business pitches.
- WLD-0005: a benefit stunt can earn coverage cheaply.
- ADC-6147, MCR-6025: paid placements must be disclosed.
- MEA-0045: post-purchase survey to check channels.
- AEO-0013: syndicated releases can be cited in AI answers.
- LPG-0037: show outlet quotes, not bare logos.

What makes this skill ours: every target is a named writer with a dated piece in the store's price band, and every row carries a pitch-by date worked back from lead time; claims are checked against a proof sheet before any pitch; paid placements are logged and judged as ads; and coverage is tied to orders through UTM links and the post-purchase survey.
