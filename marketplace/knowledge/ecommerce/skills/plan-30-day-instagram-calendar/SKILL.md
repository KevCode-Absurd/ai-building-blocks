# Thirty-day Instagram calendar

**You get:** one sheet with 30 days of posts, Reels and story sets, each with a dated slot, product, goal, hook, first frame, caption, call to action and judging metric, plus a one-screen summary and a mid-month review rule, ready to shoot and schedule once the client approves.

## When to use

- The store posts when someone remembers, with no link to products or offers.
- A launch, restock or sale falls in the next 30 to 45 days and social must support it.
- Reach is flat and the team wants a month of structured tests rather than more random posts.
- A new team member or agency is taking over the account and needs a working plan.

## Not for

- Twenty raw Reel concepts for one product range: use `reels-ideas-from-products`, then drop the best into this calendar.
- Turning paid ads into posts: use `repurpose-ads-into-organic-posts`.
- Partner and creator co-posts: use `grow-instagram-with-collabs`.
- DM sales flows and comment replies: use `sell-in-instagram-dms` and `comment-and-dm-reply-rules`.

## What you need

Required (read access, level 1):

1. **Instagram Insights, last 90 days**, per post: date, format, reach, share of reach from non-followers, 3-second hold or average watch time for Reels, shares, saves, comments, profile visits, link taps. Export from Meta Business Suite or read it in the app.
2. **Product list** with gross profit per order, stock on hand and any restock dates, from `build-sku-profit-sheet` or the Shopify product and inventory export.
3. **Offer calendar** for the month: dates, terms and codes the client has approved.
4. **Team capacity**: hours per week for shooting, editing and posting, and who does each.
5. **Brand voice**: 5 real captions the client likes.

Optional: existing photo and video library, top 30 reviews, the store's UTM rule from `set-up-utm-tracking-for-all-channels`, festival or event dates.

If Insights are missing (new account), plan from capacity and products alone and mark every target "set after week 2". Drafting posts in Meta Business Suite needs draft access (level 2). Scheduling or publishing needs publish access (level 3) and a client yes on the final calendar.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Gross profit per order is known** for the products you will feature. Fail: compute it in `build-sku-profit-sheet` first, or feature only products the client confirms are profitable and say so in the summary.
2. **Stock and delivery.** A product shown in a selling post must be in stock for the whole selling window. Fail: move it to a reach post or a waitlist post.
3. **Capacity.** The plan fits the hours stated. A calendar the team cannot make is not a plan. Fail: cut volume, never quality.
4. **Offers are approved and real.** No countdowns that reset, no "last chance" that is not the last chance. Fail: leave the offer out.
5. **Disclosure.** Any post with a creator or partner who received anything of value carries a plain label in the post [ORG-6405]; in India use an ASCI label [ORG-6406].

## Steps

### 1. Read the last 90 days

Sort posts by format and by goal. For each Reel note the non-follower share of reach. If 70 to 80% of a Reel's views come from followers, it failed the stranger test; rewrite hooks for strangers rather than adding posts [ORG-0029]. Note the 5 posts with the most shares (reach winners) and the 5 with the most comments, profile visits or link taps (selling winners).

### 2. Set one goal for the month and split the weeks

Judge posts by goal: shares when chasing growth, comments and story views when selling, with retention past 3 seconds governing both [ORG-0019]. Pick the month's main goal (grow or sell). Then mark each week: a selling week sits on an offer or launch; the rest are reach weeks. Never more than two selling weeks in a row (practitioner default, test it).

### 3. Pick 3 to 5 hero products

Rank products by gross profit per order, then drop any with low stock. Prefer products whose benefit can be seen on camera. Do not rank by revenue or follower likes.

### 4. Choose 2 or 3 recurring formats

Start brand social with 2 to 3 expert formats (a story, a ranking, a breakdown, a scenario) as video or carousel [ORG-0015]. Make at least one a numbered series that shows the product in every episode [ORG-0018]. When a format wins several times, name it as a series [ORG-0011]. Keep the topic tight for the whole month: one buyer, one problem, so the platform learns who to show you to [ORG-0023].

### 5. Set volume from capacity

- Start at 4 to 6 feed posts a week, run for a month before judging [ORG-0015].
- Make most of them Reels, because Reels reach non-followers; keep carousels and photos for saves and for followers.
- Stories reach mostly followers, so use them for selling: polls, product close-ups, link stickers, offer reminders.
- More posts drive growth on Instagram (one creator tripled half-year follower gains by moving from 2.5 to 4 posts a day), while small brands average about 8% monthly follower growth [ORG-0020]. Use this to set expectations, not as a target the team cannot meet.

### 6. Place the asks

Keep reach posts give-only: no calls to action [ORG-0003]. Leave calls to action out until posts reach about 5,000 views (about 2,000 in narrow niches) [ORG-0014]. Where the account is there, use a comment keyword that sends the link by DM instead of "link in bio" [ORG-0021], and add one or two lead-magnet Reels a week tied to a free guide or quiz [ORG-0028]. Below that threshold, selling lives in stories.

### 7. Write each slot

For every post write:

- **On-screen hook** of 5 to 8 words at a simple reading level; performance falls past 12 words [ORG-0016].
- **First frame** that shows the product being used, not unrelated B-roll [ORG-0017].
- **Caption**: first line restates the hook for people who read; then 2 to 4 short lines; disclosure first if needed.
- **Call to action**: none, keyword, story link or offer code.
- **Asset**: existing file or new shoot, with owner.
- **Metric** that judges it, from step 2.

Post natively in the app where the client can; one practitioner programme found schedulers cut reach [ORG-0013]. Treat that as a test: schedule half for two weeks and compare.

### 8. Tie offers to dates

For each selling week: tease 2 to 3 days before (reach post plus story poll), open on day 1 (Reel plus story set with link sticker), proof mid-week (review or demo), and a true final reminder only if the offer really ends. Each link carries the store's UTM rule.

### 9. India and US notes

- **India:** write captions in the language the buyers comment in; Hinglish often fits, and a regional-language version can go on the product's top state. Show the price in rupees exactly as on the store. If the store takes COD, say so in selling stories, and mention any prepaid saving the client approved. Mark festival dates (Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, regional new years) and plan gifting posts 2 to 3 weeks before. A "Message us on WhatsApp" story can replace a link for buyers who prefer chat.
- **US:** plan around the store's own peak dates (Mother's Day, Black Friday, holiday gifting). Gifted or paid creator posts need a clear "ad" or "sponsored" label in the post itself [ORG-6405].

### 10. Set the mid-month review

On day 15, compare each format against its metric. Keep formats above the account median; replace the weakest format for days 16 to 30; rewrite hooks on any Reel with a follower-heavy view share [ORG-0029].

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every one of 30 days has an entry, or is marked as a planned rest day.
- Each slot has a product, goal, hook of 12 words or fewer, first frame, caption, call to action, asset owner and metric.
- Featured products are ranked by gross profit and in stock for their selling window.
- Total weekly hours fit the stated capacity.
- No reach post carries a call to action; selling sits in selling weeks and stories.
- Every offer, price and date matches what the client approved, with no false urgency.
- Disclosure is planned for every post involving a partner or gift.
- Nothing is scheduled or posted without the client's yes.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** the month's goal, hero products and why (gross profit, stock), the 2 or 3 recurring formats, weekly volume and hours, selling weeks, and the day-15 review rule.
2. **Calendar sheet** (`instagram-calendar-<month>.csv` or a Google Sheet), one row per post or story set:

| Date | Day | Week type | Format | Series | Product | Goal | On-screen hook | First frame | Caption | Call to action | Offer or code | Link with UTM | Audio note | Asset source | Owner | Hours | Metric | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

3. **Story set sheet**: Date, Frame number, Content, Sticker (poll, link, question), Product, Link.
4. **Mid-month review table**: Format, Posts, Median metric, Account median, Keep or replace, New format.
5. **To confirm** list for the client: offers, dates, any creator involved.

## Common mistakes

- **Planning more posts than the team can make.** Step 5 sets volume from hours.
- **Selling in every post.** Step 6 keeps reach posts give-only and moves asks to stories and keywords.
- **Featuring the bestseller by revenue.** Step 3 ranks by gross profit and stock.
- **Judging every post by likes.** Step 2 gives each post one metric tied to its goal.
- **Hooks that only followers understand.** Step 1 and step 10 test the non-follower share.
- **Changing the plan every few days.** Formats run a month, with one review at day 15.
