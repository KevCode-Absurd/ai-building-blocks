# At-home UGC shoot plan

**You get:** one shoot pack a creator can open on their phone and follow: a two-hour run sheet, a numbered shot list of 25 to 40 shots (openers, bodies, product proof, b-roll, second setting), a light and sound setup card, phone settings, and a file hand-in rule, ready to send with the script.

## When to use

- A creator, the founder or a staff member will film product videos at home on a phone.
- Past footage came back dark, echoey, shaky, or without the product close-ups the editor needed.
- One session has to feed several ads: different openers, a short and a long cut, a second setting.
- A new product needs its first video ads and there is no studio budget.

## Not for

- Writing the script itself: use `creator-video-script` or `write-tiktok-ad-scripts` first.
- Still lifestyle photos: use `lifestyle-photos-without-a-studio`.
- Turning the footage into ads: use `cut-raw-footage-into-ad-variants`.
- A creator made with AI instead of a person: use `ai-creator-style-video-ad`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Approved script or scripts** with the openers to test, from `creator-video-script`. Without one, stop and get it: a shoot without a script wastes the two hours.
2. **Approved claims list** for the product (`product-claims-profile`). Every line the creator says and every demo must sit inside it.
3. **The product as buyers get it:** two units in current packaging, plus any accessory shown on the product page.
4. **The creator's setup:** phone model, which rooms they can use, the time of day they can film, and whether they own a clip-on mic.
5. **Signed usage terms:** paid ads allowed, which platforms, how long, and whether partnership or Spark ads are allowed (`influencer-contract-and-usage-rights`).
6. **Placements planned:** Reels, Stories, TikTok, Shorts, feed.

Optional: the brand moodboard (`brand-moodboard-for-shoots`), the store's past winning ads, and reviews with the words buyers use.

Access: read only (level 1) for the product page and ad account. Sending the pack to a creator is a message on the client's behalf: draft it, and the client approves before it goes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money.** Gross profit per order is known from delivered costs: product, shipping, payment fees, pick and pack, returns [MEA-0037]. The shoot fee plus product sent counts as production cost and is charged against the ads it makes [MCR-0023]. Fail: run `store-meta-math` first.
2. **The product is live.** The variant, colour and packaging filmed are what ships today, in stock, at the page price. Fail: wait or film the live variant only.
3. **Rights in writing.** Paid ad use, platforms, term and edit rights are signed before filming. Fail: no shoot. Footage without paid rights cannot run as an ad.
4. **Honest speaker.** The creator says only what they have really experienced. If they got the product free or are paid, the ad will say so [MCR-6025]. A person who has not used the product does not speak as a customer [MCR-6045].
5. **Safe demos.** No demo that is dangerous, misleading, or shows a result the product cannot reliably give.

## Steps

### 1. Size the session from the scripts

Count what the editor needs, not what feels like enough:

- **Bodies:** 2 or 3 scripts, each filmed in full.
- **Openers:** 4 to 5 per body, because the first seconds decide who stops and taste is a poor judge [MCR-0076]. Launch only 2 to 4 per body on small budgets so each gets enough spend [MCR-0013]; the rest wait on the shelf.
- **Proof shots:** one per claim in the script.
- **B-roll:** 8 to 12 short clips.

A short scripted session can feed months of hook and body combinations [MCR-0007]. Two hours is enough for 2 bodies and 10 openers if the run sheet is followed.

### 2. Pick the room and the light

- Face a window, with the phone between the creator and the window. Soft daylight on the face, no window behind them.
- Turn off overhead lights that mix colours with daylight. If the room is dark, one lamp with a white bulb, bounced off a wall, beats a ring light shining straight on.
- Film between mid-morning and mid-afternoon for steady light. Note the time on the run sheet so a reshoot matches.
- Clear the background to two or three objects that fit the buyer's life. No other brands, no screens playing, nothing personal the creator would not want in an ad.

### 3. Set up the sound

- Pick the softest room: curtains, a bed, a rug, a sofa. Bare tiled rooms echo.
- Turn off fans, air conditioning, the fridge if close, and notifications on every phone in the room.
- A clip-on mic is best. Without one, keep the phone about an arm's length away and film talking shots in the quietest corner.
- Record 10 seconds of silent room tone at the start. The editor uses it to cover cuts.
- Busy cities: noise often peaks in the day (traffic, deliveries, construction, neighbours' kitchens). Film talking shots early morning or late evening, and b-roll in the noisy hours.

### 4. Set the phone

- Back camera when someone can frame; front camera only for selfie-style talking.
- Vertical 9:16, 1080p or 4K at 30 frames a second; 60 for any slow-motion b-roll.
- Clean the lens. Turn on the grid. Lock focus and exposure on the face (press and hold on most phones).
- Do Not Disturb on, at least 20 GB free, battery above 80% or plugged in.
- Test: film 15 seconds, play it back with headphones, check face, product and voice. Fix before going on.

### 5. Frame for captions and buttons

Keep the face and the product in the middle band of the frame. Platforms put buttons, names and captions at the top, bottom and right edge, and the editor needs space for text. Leave a hand's width of empty space above the head and keep the product above the bottom third. The editor applies exact safe zones in `captions-and-text-for-sound-off`.

### 6. Write the shot list

Number every shot and give each a code: O for opener, B for body, P for proof, R for b-roll, S for second setting. Rules:

- **The first frame shows the topic.** An opener starts with the product in use or the problem visible, not a random clip [ORG-0017].
- **Proof shots are one continuous take.** Before and after in the same shot, no cut between them, so nobody can say the result was edited in.
- **Hold still.** Each product shot ends with the product held still for 2 seconds, label to camera.
- **Premium products stay out of messy footage.** If an opener is deliberately lo-fi, keep the product out of that part and reveal it in clean light after [MCR-0010].
- **Disclosure line.** One take of the creator saying it plainly in their own words, for example that the brand sent the product or paid for the video [MCR-6060].

### 7. Plan the second setting

Leave 10 minutes for one more room, outfit or outdoor spot. The same script in a new place can extend a tired ad [MCR-0012], and new openers filmed in a fresh look revive a winner better than recutting old footage [MCR-0047].

### 8. Direct the creator

- Talk to one friend, not "guys". The script is a rail, not a teleprompter: say each line two or three ways.
- Pause one second before and after each line. Clean gaps make clean cuts.
- Never read claims from a phone off to the side. Learn one line, film it, next line.
- One honest small doubt or downside is allowed and helps trust.
- Language: film in the language the buyer thinks in. For English-speaking markets, plain conversational English. For each other language your buyers use (for example Spanish in the US, French in Canada, German or Dutch in Europe), film a native speaker's take, with an English take of the openers only if the brand runs English ads there.

### 9. Hand in the files

- Upload original files to a shared drive or a file transfer link. Do not send through WhatsApp or chat apps, which compress video; if WhatsApp is the only option, send as a document, not as a video.
- Name files by shot code and take: `O3_take2`. The editor maps codes to the tag sheet from `creative-naming-and-tagging-system` [MCR-0051].
- The creator keeps the originals until the editor confirms every file opens.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every line in the scripts and every demo matches the approved claims list.
- Signed usage terms cover paid ads on every planned platform for the planned term.
- The run sheet adds up to two hours or less, with setup and the sound test included.
- Each claim has a matching proof shot filmed as one continuous take.
- Openers per body: 4 to 5 filmed, with a note of which 2 to 4 launch first.
- The disclosure take is on the shot list, and gifted or paid status is recorded.
- Light, sound and phone settings fit the creator's real room and phone.
- Nothing is sent to the creator without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** product, creator, date, scripts covered, number of shots by type, who launches first, rights status, production cost to log against the ads.

**Run sheet:**

| Time | Block | Shots | Setting | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:15 | Setup, room tone, test clip | Test | Room A | Check playback with headphones |
| 0:15 to 0:45 | Openers | O1 to O10 | Room A | 3 takes each |
| 0:45 to 1:15 | Bodies | B1 to B3 | Room A | Full take, then line by line |
| 1:15 to 1:40 | Proof and b-roll | P1 to Pn, R1 to R12 | Room A or kitchen | Continuous takes for proof |
| 1:40 to 1:50 | Second setting | S1 to S6 | Room B or outside | New outfit |
| 1:50 to 2:00 | Disclosure, retakes, upload | D1 | Room A | Upload originals |

**Shot list:** Shot code, Type, What is on screen, Line spoken, Must show (product, label, hand, result), Takes, Claim it proves, Done.

**Setup card:** window position, lights off, mic, room tone, phone settings, framing note, language.

**Hand-in rule:** upload link, naming pattern, deadline.

## Common mistakes

- **Shooting without a script.** The editor gets talk with no openers. The gate in What you need stops this.
- **Light behind the creator.** A dark face and blown window. Step 2 turns them to face it.
- **Echo from a bare room.** Step 3 moves talking shots to the softest room and adds room tone.
- **Cut demos.** A before and after split by a cut looks faked. Step 6 makes proof one take.
- **Footage sent over chat apps.** It arrives compressed and unusable in ads. Step 9 names the upload path.
- **One setting for everything.** Step 7 adds a second look so fatigued ads have a refresh ready.
