# Fifteen-second product demo ad

**You get:** one fifteen-second demo script with second-by-second beats, a six-second cut, a shot list of 6 to 10 shots, and a proof sheet showing how each on-screen result was filmed, ready for a creator or staff member to shoot on a phone.

## When to use

- The product's main benefit is visible: it cleans, fits, holds, cools, folds, pours, lights or fixes something.
- Current video ads spend too long talking before the product does anything.
- A TikTok Shop listing or product page needs a short demo video.
- The account needs a short format to sit beside longer creator ads.

## Not for

- Benefits that take weeks to show (skin, hair, sleep): use `creator-video-script` for a story-led ad, or `mechanism-explainer-video-ad`.
- Comparing against a named competitor: use `comparison-ads-that-stay-legal`.
- An unboxing or first-use ad: use `unboxing-video-ad`.
- Openers without a demo: use `wordless-video-openers` or `ad-opener-writer`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Approved claims list** (`product-claims-profile`), with the evidence for the benefit you will show.
2. **The product** in the variant that ships today, plus whatever the demo needs (the mess, the old tool, the timer).
3. **Live product page URL** with price and offer.
4. **Gross profit per order** from `store-meta-math`.
5. **Who films:** creator, founder or staff, with signed paid-use terms if not staff.

Optional: reviews naming the moment buyers were convinced, and TikTok Shop listing data (views and sales per video) if the store sells there.

Access: read only. Sending the script out and uploading the ad need the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money.** Gross profit per order is known from delivered costs [MEA-0037]. Any shoot or creator fee counts as production cost against the ad [MCR-0023].
2. **A showable benefit.** A muted viewer can see the result within ten seconds. Fail: this format is wrong; hand off as above.
3. **The result is real and repeatable.** The demo shows what the product does in normal use, every time. Test it three times off camera first. If it works only sometimes, or only in a special setup, the ad would mislead. Fail: pick a different benefit.
4. **Safe.** No demo that invites copying something dangerous.
5. **Speaker status.** If a creator was paid or gifted, the ad discloses it on screen [MCR-6060].

## Steps

### 1. Pick the one benefit

List the benefits buyers name in reviews. For each, ask: can it be seen, in under ten seconds, by someone with the sound off? Keep only those. Pick the one buyers mention most that also beats the obvious alternative. One benefit per ad; a second benefit becomes a second ad.

### 2. Pick the demo type

| Type | Use when | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Before and after in one take | The change is visible quickly | No cut between before and after |
| Side by side with the old way | Buyers use a common method now | Use a generic old method, not a named brand |
| Real-time timer | Speed is the benefit | Timer visible on screen the whole time; no speed-up |
| Close-up of fit or texture | Feel, fit or finish sells it | Macro shot in natural light, no filter on the product |
| Fair stress test | Strength or durability sells it | Realistic load, said plainly; no trick setups |

A demo that shows how the product is used usually converts better than a striking visual that only draws curious clicks [MCR-0029].

### 3. Write the fifteen seconds

| Seconds | Beat | Screen | Text (5 to 8 words) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Problem or product mid-action | The mess, the struggle, or the product already working | The buyer's problem in their words |
| 2 to 5 | The action | Hands use the product, close and clear | Optional |
| 5 to 10 | The proof | Result in one continuous shot | The result, plainly |
| 10 to 13 | Name it | Product held still, label to camera | Product name and one fact |
| 13 to 15 | Ask | Product and where to buy | Live offer or a soft ask |

The first frame must show the topic, not a logo or unrelated clip [ORG-0017]. On-screen text stays short, five to eight plain words [ORG-0016]. The opening should filter for people who have this problem, not grab everyone [MCR-0026]. Spoken words are optional; if used, one short line per beat.

### 4. Cut the six seconds

Action (2 s), result (3 s), product and name (1 s). No voice needed. This cut serves short slots such as YouTube bumper placements, which are capped at six seconds, and Stories, and it works as a test opener for longer ads.

### 5. Plan the shots

6 to 10 shots: the problem wide, the problem close, hands with product, the result in one take (two angles if possible, both continuous), product still with label, product with packaging, and a spare result take. Film proof shots in daylight with no beauty or colour filter on the product.

### 6. Fill the proof sheet

For each result on screen, write how it was filmed: date, who filmed, takes, any edit made (crop, colour balance only), and confirmation that speed is real time. Keep raw files. If anyone asks whether the demo is real, the sheet answers.

### 7. Platform notes

- **Meta and Instagram:** 9:16 for Reels and Stories, 4:5 re-frame for feed.
- **TikTok and TikTok Shop:** for Shop videos, link the product and keep the demo in the first seconds. Rank Shop demos on sales per thousand views, not view count; these varied over a hundredfold across top videos in one review [MCR-0046]. Listing videos also feed `optimize-tiktok-shop-listings`.
- **Products felt in seconds** (cooling, taste, scent) can add the user's honest reaction as the proof [MCR-0045].

### 8. Market notes

- **Every market:** show prices in the store's currency only if they match the live page today. Where buyers pay on delivery or return freely, a demo that answers "will it really work" lowers refused parcels and returns; write the proof text in the languages your buyers use if that is how they search. Avoid demos with branded competitor products visible.
- **US, UK, EU, Canada and Australia:** speeded-up, retouched or staged demos are misleading claims under advertising law (FTC in the US, ASA in the UK, the EU consumer rules, the Competition Bureau in Canada, the ACCC in Australia). If a speed-up is unavoidable for a long process, show "sped up" on screen the whole time, or choose another demo.

### 9. Hand over and judge

Send the script to `plan-ugc-shoot-at-home` for the shoot, then to `captions-and-text-for-sound-off`. Judge the ad on store cost per purchase against break-even and gross profit after production, not on views or click rate [MCR-0023] [MCR-0029].

## Checks before you hand it over

- One benefit only, visible with the sound off within ten seconds.
- The demo worked three times out of three off camera.
- Before and after, timer and result shots are single continuous takes in real time.
- No colour or beauty filter on the product in proof shots.
- On-screen text per beat is eight words or fewer.
- Price and offer match the live page on the day.
- Disclosure is on screen where a paid or gifted creator appears.
- The six-second cut makes sense on its own.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** product, the one benefit, demo type and why, who films, what the six-second cut shows, production cost to log.

**Script (15 s):** Seconds, Beat, Shot code, Screen action, On-screen text, Spoken line (optional), Sound.

**Six-second cut:** Seconds, Shot code, On-screen text.

**Shot list:** Shot code, What is on screen, Framing, Continuous take (Y or N), Done.

**Proof sheet:** Result shown, Filmed on, Filmed by, Takes, Edits made, Real time (Y or N), Raw file link.

## Common mistakes

- **Two benefits in fifteen seconds.** Neither lands. Step 1 picks one.
- **A demo that works only sometimes.** Gate 3 tests it three times first.
- **Cut or sped-up results.** Step 6 records how each result was filmed.
- **Opening on the logo.** Step 3 opens on the problem or the product working.
- **Judging on views.** Step 9 judges on purchases and gross profit; for TikTok Shop, sales per thousand views.
- **Naming a competitor.** Step 2 uses a generic old method and hands named comparisons on.
