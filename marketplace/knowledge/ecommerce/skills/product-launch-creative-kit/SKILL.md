# New product launch creative kit

**You get:** a dated launch kit with four ad stages (teaser, reveal, proof, last chance), two to four concepts per stage with hooks and proof, the audience and placement for each, the rule that moves the launch to the next stage, and the asset list with due dates.

## When to use

- A new product, or a new flavour, colour or size worth its own push, launches in 4 to 6 weeks.
- A past launch went live to almost no sales.
- The store wants a waitlist before stock lands.
- The owner is unsure which angle to lead with on launch day.

## Not for

- Choosing the launch price and offer: use `design-launch-offer` first.
- The launch emails: use `write-product-launch-email-sequence`.
- Setting up pre-orders in the store: use `set-up-pre-orders-and-backorders`.
- Meta campaign structure for a launch stage: use `store-meta-plan`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Launch facts**: product, launch date, price, launch offer and its real end date, stock arriving and its date, restock lead time.
2. **Gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase** for the new product (`store-meta-math`), built from the supplier quote and shipping costs.
3. **The claims sheet** with proof files (`product-claims-profile`); samples or prototypes for filming.
4. **Audiences the store owns**: email and SMS list size, WhatsApp opted-in list (where buyers use it), Instagram followers, past buyers.
5. **Ad budget for the launch window**, signed off.

Optional: early tester feedback (with permission), creators who have used the product, the angle table from `mine-ad-angles-from-reviews` for related products.

If stock date or restock time is unknown, plan the teaser and reveal only and hold the rest. Building ads needs draft access; turning them on, messaging customers and spending need the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Profit line.** Pass when target cost per purchase exists for the new product. Fail: run `store-meta-math` with planned costs.
2. **Stock and restock.** Pass when stock lands before reveal day. Selling out is fine only if restock is about two weeks or less, in one practitioner's launch [ORG-0038]; longer gaps waste the launch spend. Fail: run pre-orders with honest ship dates, or delay the push.
3. **Price sanity.** A new brand with no reviews launching at a luxury price can stall; one apparel brand sold a tenth of plan until it cut the price [OFF-0070]. Fail: send the price back to `design-launch-offer`.
4. **Real deadline.** A last-chance stage needs a real end: launch price ends, limited run sells through, bonus ends. Fail: no last-chance ads.

## Steps

### 1. Smoke-test angles before launch day

Two to three weeks out, run 3 to 5 angles as plain product images with one bold text line each, changing only the angle, to the store's cold audience at a small budget [MCR-0075]. If the product is not ready to show, test the problem line with a related product or the prototype. The winner leads the reveal. Each new concept goes in its own ad set with about 3 hook variations [MCR-0079].

### 2. Teaser stage (14 to 1 days before)

Job: build a waitlist, not sales. Concepts: the problem the product fixes, the "why now" if a real trend exists [MCR-0004], behind the scenes of making it, the founder's reason. Show enough to explain, keep the full reveal for launch day. Audience: owned lists, followers, past buyers and engaged visitors, plus organic posts. A waitlist with product rewards for referrals, not cash, can make referrals most of the list [ORG-0030]. Track cost per waitlist sign-up. Where buyers use WhatsApp (much of Europe, Latin America, the Middle East and Asia), collect opt-ins with clear consent; it is often the strongest launch-day channel there.

### 3. Reveal stage (launch day to day 3)

Job: first sales from the warmest people. Concepts: the product doing its job, the price and launch offer, the order-by or ship date. Send to the waitlist and owned lists first (launch emails resent to non-openers with new subjects work for real deadlines [EML-0010]), then past buyers, then cold with the smoke-test winner. Move to the proof stage when the reveal has spent about 3 times target cost per purchase on cold [ADC-0039].

### 4. Proof stage (day 3 to day 21)

Job: convince people who need evidence. Swap in proof as it arrives: first real reviews, unboxings by real buyers, demonstrations, creator videos, test results. Until real reviews exist, use demonstrations, specs and the founder; never write or generate reviews. Creators who got free product must be disclosed (FTC in the US, ASA and CMA in the UK, and similar rules in the EU, Canada and Australia), and a standout result needs the usual result beside it [MCR-6025]. Every objective claim needs proof before it runs [MCR-6022].

### 5. Last-chance stage (final 3 days of the real deadline)

Job: convert the undecided. Concepts: the deadline with its date, what ends (price, bonus, limited run), a recap of the strongest proof. Stop these ads the moment the deadline passes and do not restart the same offer the next week; a deadline that is not kept trains buyers to wait. No countdown timers that reset.

### 6. Order and audience sheet

Write each stage's go-live and stop dates, audience, budget, and the switch rule (waitlist size target, cold spend reached, or deadline date). The reveal never goes to cold before the warm audiences have had a day.

### 7. Asset list and due dates

Per concept: ratios (9:16, 4:5, 1:1), video length, static versions, email header, WhatsApp image, site banner. Teaser assets due 3 weeks before launch; reveal assets 1 week before; proof templates ready to drop real reviews in; last-chance assets 1 week before the deadline.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Four stages are dated, with go-live, stop and the switch rule for each.
- Stock lands before reveal day, or the plan uses pre-orders with honest ship dates.
- The reveal leads with the smoke-test winner, or says no test ran.
- The proof stage uses only real reviews, demonstrations and disclosed creators.
- The last-chance stage exists only with a real, dated deadline that is then kept.
- Every claim maps to a proof file.
- Owned audiences get the reveal before cold audiences.
- Nothing is launched, sent or spent without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** launch date, lead angle and why, the four stages with dates, budget per stage, the switch rules, and the top risk.

**Kit table:**

| Stage | Dates | Job | Concept | Hook | Proof | Audience | Placement | Budget | Switch rule | Asset IDs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Asset list:** Asset ID, Stage, Concept, Channel, Ratio or size, Length, Copy line, Owner, Due date, Status.

**Smoke-test sheet:** Angle, Text line, Spend, Clicks, Cost per click, Add to carts or waitlist sign-ups, Pick.

**Assumptions:** the stock landing date, restock time and offer end date used, one line each. Every creator post carries its paid-partnership label.

## Common mistakes

- **Guessing the lead angle.** Step 1 smoke-tests it before launch day.
- **Teasers sold as sales ads.** Step 2 judges them on waitlist sign-ups.
- **Cold audiences first.** Step 3 reveals to owned lists first.
- **Invented early reviews.** Step 4 uses only real proof as it arrives.
- **A last chance that is not the last.** Step 5 needs a real deadline and keeps it.
- **Selling out with a long restock.** Gate 2 checks restock time before the push.

What makes this skill ours: the lead angle is smoke-tested before launch day instead of guessed; each stage has a switch rule in the store's own money, not a fixed day count; the proof stage fills only with real reviews and disclosed creators as they arrive; and the last-chance stage runs only on a real, kept deadline, with stock and restock checked before any spend.
