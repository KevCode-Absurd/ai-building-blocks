# Reasons-to-buy listicle page

**You get:** a fast, mobile-first reasons-to-buy HTML page with five to seven proven reasons, images, an optional Shopify section set, a reasons table, a speed check and a comparison plan.

Some ads earn a click with curiosity but do not sell the product on their own; sending that click straight to a product page wastes it. A short numbered page makes the case in five to seven reasons, then hands a warmer buyer to the product page or cart. This skill builds that page and proves whether it beats the product page.

## When to use

- Curiosity or "here is why" ads send traffic to a product page and convert poorly [MCR-0019] [MCR-0024].
- A product has several strong reasons to buy that the product page cannot show in its first screen.
- A buyer group or angle has proved itself on the product page and needs its own page [LPG-0030] [LPG-0064].
- A gifting moment or a clear switch from an older way of doing something.

## Not for

- A long story-led page for cold traffic: use `advertorial-story-page`.
- Fixing the product page itself: use `store-meta-landing` or `storefront-conversion-audit`.
- Rebuilding a layout the owner likes: use `page-layout-rebuild`.
- Writing the ads that send traffic here: use `store-meta-concepts`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Brand files**: logo file, colours, fonts, voice notes, from `store-visual-identity-sheet` or `store-meta-brand-dna`. If none exist, read them from the live site (button colour, heading and body fonts) and confirm with the client once.
2. **The product**: page URL, price, offer, bundles, variants, stock, delivery and returns policy, guarantee.
3. **The angle**: the ads that will send traffic, and the buyer they speak to.
4. **Real proof**: reviews export (rating, body, name, date), customer photos with permission, press quotes with source, test documents.
5. **Gross profit per purchase**, from `store-meta-math`.

Optional: support tickets and survey answers, `product-claims-profile` output, the product page's conversion rate and revenue per session.

Access: read (level 1) to build. The Shopify files go onto a duplicate, unpublished theme (level 2). Publishing or pointing ads at the page needs the client's yes.

Missing input: ask once. Rating, review count, customer count and offers are never estimated: if a real figure is missing, leave that element out. If images are missing, list them and collect them before handover; nothing ships with placeholders.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Proof gate.** Each reason needs at least one real piece of proof. A reason without proof is cut, not padded; the page carries as many reasons as the proof supports, five to seven.
2. **Offer gate.** Price, bundles, gifts and stock on the page match the product page and checkout today.
3. **Claims gate.** Health, beauty and performance claims follow the ad platform's rules and local law (for example FTC guidance in the US, the CAP Code in the UK, the EU consumer rules, the ACCC in Australia). Unsure: cut the claim.
4. **Money gate.** Gross profit per purchase known, so the page is judged on profit per visit. Fail: build, but no paid traffic until it is known.
5. **Traffic gate.** Enough planned traffic to judge the page; practitioner default: about 1,000 visits in two weeks, test it.

## Steps

### 1. Pick the reasons from buyer doubts

Count the doubts in reviews, tickets and survey answers. Each reason answers one doubt or states one belief the buyer needs. Order them: the reason most different from rivals first; the biggest doubt by reason 2 or 3; proof-heavy reasons in the middle; risk and guarantee last. One page, one buyer, one angle [LPG-0064].

Sort every fact into one of four piles first. Push: what wears buyers down about the old way, in their words. Pull: what is better here, each with a fact. Doubt: what they fear about switching (it won't work, it costs more, they can't cancel). Habit: what keeps them on the old way (they already own one, it is part of the routine). Pick 3 to 7 reasons that each argue one thing, with at least one that answers a habit and one that answers a doubt where the facts allow. Doubt reasons sit last, right before the offer, where the worry peaks. A reason with no proof goes in the middle of the list, never first or last. Describe the old way as a kind of product, never a named rival.

### 2. Choose the headline angle

Draft one headline for each angle that fits, then recommend one:

- **Moving from an old way:** why this buyer is leaving the usual fix for this one.
- **A gift for an occasion:** why it suits a named occasion and recipient.
- **A problem solved:** why buyers with one specific problem pick it.
- **An upgrade on a cheap category:** why it beats the generic version.

The headline carries the number and the promise in the ad's own words, 8 to 14 words, at 3 to 4 times body size [LPG-0010]. No rival brand named; no claim the proof cannot carry (for example "can't stop buying" needs repeat data).

### 3. Write the page

- **First screen:** headline, one line on who it is for, and two kinds of proof, such as rating with count and one short quote [LPG-0036].
- **Each reason:** a bold one-line claim, 40 to 80 words in the buyer's words, one proof item with name and source, one image. The claim is 5 to 9 words and states the fact itself ("Tested for 70 pesticides in every batch"), never a label such as "Quality", so a reader who scans only the titles still gets the whole case.
- **Proof block:** 3 or 4 real customer quotes with first name, initial and place or handle.
- **Offer block:** price, bundles and gifts as the checkout shows them. Show savings as a money amount in each option's title ("3-pack, save $10", in the store's currency) rather than a small percentage [LPG-0008] [LPG-0035].
- **FAQ:** 6 to 8 questions taken from the doubt list.
- **Calls to action:** after reason 2 or 3, after the offer and at the end; risk reducers (returns, guarantee, delivery time) in the line under each button [LPG-0038]. A sticky button appears only after the product is introduced [LPG-0013].
- **Link target:** the product page with the advertised variant chosen, or a Shopify cart link when the reasons settle the choice. Keep the UTM tags.
- **Sign-off:** signed honestly by the business ("The [brand] team") or by a real person who agreed. Nothing passes for a magazine, a review site or an independent writer.
- **Quote audit:** find every pair of quotation marks on the page and match each to a supplied review word for word; remove the marks from any that do not match.

### 4. Pick the design direction

Offer two, recommend one by traffic and offer:

- **Editorial:** article look, large numbers, images in line, quieter buttons. Suits premium, gifting and considered buys.
- **Offer-led:** stacked numbered blocks each with its own button, a bundle panel with a clear money saving, a sticky add-to-cart bar on mobile. Suits paid traffic and bundle offers.

After the client picks, two revision rounds at most; after round two, change only what the client marks as wrong.

### 5. Build it

- One HTML file with inline CSS; brand colours and fonts as variables in one place; fonts loaded with font-display swap; images in WebP or AVIF with alt text.
- Video only where it shows the product working: a short muted clip with a poster image that does not autoplay on mobile, or a light embed that loads a thumbnail until tapped [SPD-0010]. No scrolling tickers or scroll-triggered animations that hide text until it moves.
- **Shopify option:** one section file with its CSS scoped to that section, settings for every text and image field, blocks for reasons, quotes and FAQ, one setting for the button link; a page template that preloads all the copy; the images; and a short install note (add the files to a duplicate theme, create the page, pick the template). First image loads eagerly with high priority; the rest lazy-load. No paid page builder is needed.
- **Delivery, payment and language (where relevant):** delivery time, the returns rule and the payment options buyers look for (pay on delivery, buy now pay later, local wallets) near each button; a light page for slower networks; another language only if the ad runs in it. Practitioner default, test it.

### 6. Check speed and mobile

PageSpeed Insights on mobile plus a real phone. Pass marks (Core Web Vitals "good"): LCP 2.5 s or less, INP 200 ms or less, CLS 0.1 or less, and no sideways scrolling. Fix images first (hero under about 200 KB, others about 100 KB), then scripts [SPD-0002]; load the first image eagerly [SPD-0006]; build bars as part of the page, not app add-ons that shift the layout [SPD-0007].

### 7. Track it against the product page

- UTMs on every ad, so GA4 splits traffic by landing page; GA4 conversion rate set to count purchases only [MEA-0002].
- Run the same ad to the listicle and to the product page as an A/B test (`store-meta-test` sizes it).
- Judge on revenue and gross profit per session, not click-through. Watch the share who click on: a pre-sell page usually sends a good share on (one practitioner benchmark is 30 to 40% from an advertorial [LPG-0002]); far below that means a weak case or a weak link.
- Revenue per visitor needs more traffic than conversion rate to call [CRO-0014]; fix the sample before launch.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Each reason answers a counted doubt and carries sourced proof; no rating, count or offer is estimated.
- Price, bundles, gifts and stock match checkout today; savings show as money amounts.
- Every claim passed the claims gate, or was cut.
- No placeholder text or image remains.
- Every reason title states a fact as a claim; doubt reasons sit right before the offer; every quotation matches a supplied review word for word.
- Mobile LCP, INP and CLS meet the good levels, with the report attached.
- Buttons link to the right variant with UTMs kept; GA4 counts purchases only; the comparison is sized.
- Nothing is published or connected to ads without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary first:** angle and buyer, the reasons in order, the design chosen, speed results, the comparison plan with dates, and any assumption that changes the page (one line each, at most three).

**Files:** `listicle-[product].html`, `images/`, and the optional Shopify set: `sections/listicle-[product].liquid`, `templates/page.listicle-[product].json`, `assets/`, `INSTALL.md`.

**Table "Reasons":** Number, Reason, Doubt it answers, Count, Proof, Source.

**Table "Speed check":** Device, LCP, INP, CLS, Page weight, Pass or fail.

## Common mistakes

- **A fixed number of reasons.** Proof sets the count.
- **Made-up social proof.** A missing rating is left out, never guessed.
- **Reasons from the brand's view.** Step 1 starts from buyer doubts.
- **Every benefit on one page** [LPG-0064].
- **Autoplay video and animations that slow the page.** Step 5 keeps media light [SPD-0010].
- **Judging on click-through.** Step 7 judges on profit per session.
- **Reason titles that are labels.** Step 3 makes each title state the fact, so scanners get the case.
- **Ignoring the habit.** Step 1 gives at least one reason that answers what keeps buyers on the old way.
