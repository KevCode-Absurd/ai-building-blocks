# TikTok Shop affiliate creators

**You get:** a rate card (open and targeted commission per product), a sample policy, a target list of 30 to 100 creators in priority order, a one-page creator brief, invite and follow-up messages, and a weekly scorecard template.

## When to use

- The TikTok Shop is live and fewer than 10 creators post about it each week.
- Samples go out and fewer than half come back as videos.
- Commission rates were copied from a competitor or left at a default.
- The store wants a few top creators on steady monthly deals.

## Not for

- Setting up the shop itself: use `launch-tiktok-shop`.
- Creators outside TikTok Shop (Instagram, YouTube), and all creator work for buyers in countries where TikTok Shop is not open: use `find-and-vet-influencers` and `write-influencer-outreach`.
- Running creator videos as Spark or GMV Max ads: use `tiktok-spark-ads-with-creators` or `tiktok-shop-gmv-max-ads`.
- Scripts for creators: use `write-tiktok-ad-scripts` or `creator-video-script`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Profit per order after TikTok costs** for each product, before commission, from `launch-tiktok-shop`'s profit sheet.
2. **Seller Center access** at read level to see the affiliate center, creator search filters and current collaboration options. The client signs in.
3. **Sample stock** and landed cost per unit.
4. **Approved claims list**, from `check-ad-copy-for-policy-and-claims`.

Optional: past affiliate report (creator, videos, GMV, orders, refunds); the store's best videos.

Access: read (level 1) to research; draft (level 2) to prepare collaborations; publishing a collaboration, approving samples and sending invites or messages each need the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Commission ceiling known.** Ceiling rate = (profit per order before commission minus profit to keep) / price. Commission, discounts and ads share one pool [FIN-0015]. Fail: compute it first.
2. **Rules read on the day.** Collaboration types, how open and targeted rates interact, sample terms and posting deadlines are set in Seller Center and change; read the current help pages before setting anything. Fail: do not publish.
3. **Sample stock.** Enough for the month's cap in step 3. Fail: lower the cap.
4. **Disclosure.** Creators turn on TikTok's commercial content setting and say plainly they earn from sales; the store checks posts [ORG-6405] [CXP-6303].

## Steps

### 1. Set the rate card

| Collaboration | Who | Rate (practitioner default, under the ceiling) |
|---|---|---|
| Open | Any eligible creator | A standard rate that leaves profit after samples |
| Targeted | Hand-picked creators | Standard plus 5 to 10 points |
| Monthly deal | 3 to 10 proven creators | Floor or commission, whichever is higher |

The monthly deal: one brand offered new creators 1,000 dollars a month or 15 to 20% commission, whichever was higher, for one video a day for 30 days, and moved them to commission only once commission passed the floor [ORG-0036]. Offer it only to creators who already sold in the category, and only if the margin carries it.

### 2. Find target creators

In the affiliate center, filter by category, follower range (micro first: under 100,000), recent sales in the category where shown, and content type. Prefer creators whose buyers look like the store's buyers; scouts who share the buyer's taste spot them faster [ORG-0040]. Watch 3 recent videos each: do they show products in use, and do comments ask "where do I get it"?

Priority order: (1) past sales in the category, (2) demo or problem-led style, (3) posting frequency, (4) followers.

### 3. Set the sample policy

- **Free sample:** creators with category sales or a strong fit score; they agree to post within the deadline Seller Center allows or the store sets.
- **Refundable or paid sample:** new creators with no sales history.
- **No sample:** creators who only want product, with no posts in the category.
- **Monthly cap:** practitioner default, samples worth no more than 10% of last month's TikTok gross profit while starting; raise it as videos prove out.
- Target 2 to 3 videos per sample sent [ORG-0009]. A creator who posts nothing after the deadline gets no second sample.

### 4. Write the creator brief (one page)

- The product in one line and who buys it.
- 3 proven hooks and the first-frame shot.
- What to show: the product working, a real reaction only if the effect is instant [MCR-0045].
- Approved claims; banned words; no before-and-after for restricted categories.
- Disclosure: commercial content setting on; plain words.
- Links to 3 of the store's best videos.

### 5. Write invites and follow-ups

- **Invite (in-app):** under 60 words. Their name and one video you liked, the product, the rate, the sample terms, the link to the brief.
- **Follow-up 1, day 3:** one new reason (a top video's result in sales, not views; a new bundle).
- **Follow-up 2, day 7:** short close. Stop after two.
- **Sample approved:** delivery date, brief link, posting deadline, how to reach the team.
- **Post received:** thanks, one specific note, and an offer to Spark the video if it sells.

Send in batches of 20 to 30 a day after the client approves the batch; invite volume at scale is high and conversion is low (one agency saw about 6% of invites become posting creators [ORG-0009]).

### 6. Weekly scorecard

Per creator: samples sent, videos posted, views, orders, refunds, GMV, sales per 1,000 views, commission paid, profit after commission and samples. Rank on sales per 1,000 views, not views; it ranged more than 100 times across top videos [MCR-0046]. Move the top 10% to targeted or monthly deals; stop samples to creators with no posts.

### 7. Watch buyer quality

TikTok Shop buyers at low order values can repeat less [CXP-0016]. Each month, compare refund rate and repeat rate by top creator; a creator who sells a lot with high refunds is moved down.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every rate sits under the ceiling for that product.
- Collaboration and sample rules were read in Seller Center on the day, with the date noted.
- The sample policy has a monthly cap and a posting deadline.
- The brief lists approved claims, banned words and the disclosure rule.
- Messages stop at two follow-ups.
- The scorecard ranks on sales per 1,000 views and profit after samples.
- No collaboration, sample approval or message went out without the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** rate card, sample cap, number of creators targeted this month, expected samples and their cost, and the 10 creators to invite first.

**Rate card:** Product, Price, Profit before commission, Ceiling rate, Open rate, Targeted rate, Monthly deal terms.

**Sample policy:** Tier, Sample type, Who qualifies, Deadline, Cap.

**Target list:** Creator, Followers, Category sales (if shown), Style, Fit notes, Collaboration type, Rate, Sample, Status.

**Brief:** one page, filled.

**Messages:** Invite, Follow-up 1, Follow-up 2, Sample approved, Post received (full text).

**Weekly scorecard:** the fields in step 6, one row per creator per week.

## Common mistakes

- **Rates above the ceiling.** Gate 1 and step 1 keep every rate under it.
- **Samples to anyone who asks.** Step 3 ties free samples to fit and sales history.
- **Old rules.** Gate 2 reads Seller Center on the day.
- **Ranking by views.** Step 6 ranks by sales per 1,000 views.
- **Ignoring refunds.** Step 7 moves high-refund creators down.
- **Missing disclosure.** Gate 4 and the brief require it.
