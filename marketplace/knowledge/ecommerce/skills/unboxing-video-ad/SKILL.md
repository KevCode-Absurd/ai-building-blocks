# Unboxing video ad

**You get:** one unboxing ad plan: a 30 to 45 second script with a 15 second cut, a shot plan of 12 to 16 shots with sound notes, a packaging prep list, and an honesty sheet that lists what must be shown exactly as buyers receive it, ready to film.

## When to use

- The product is a gift, a kit with several parts, or has packaging worth showing.
- Buyers ask in reviews or chat what comes in the box, how big it is, or how it arrives.
- The store improved its packaging and wants an ad that shows it.
- Creator videos keep coming back as unboxings and the store wants one built on purpose.

## Not for

- A fast proof of one benefit: use `product-demo-in-15-seconds`.
- Designing the packaging or inserts themselves: hand to the post-purchase team (`write-post-purchase-emails` covers the message side).
- A general creator script: use `creator-video-script`.
- Planning the shoot day and phone setup: use `plan-ugc-shoot-at-home` with this script.

## What you need

Required:

1. **An order placed through the live store**, paid the way most buyers pay (card, wallet, buy now pay later, or pay on delivery where offered), shipped by the normal courier to the person who will unbox it. Note order date and delivery date.
2. **Packaging details:** what every buyer gets today (mailer, retail box, tissue, inserts, freebies) and what only some get. Ask the warehouse or 3PL, not the marketing team.
3. **Approved claims list** (`product-claims-profile`) and the live product page.
4. **Who unboxes:** a real customer, a creator, or the founder or staff, with signed usage terms for paid ads.
5. **Gross profit per order** including packaging cost (`store-meta-math`).

Optional: reviews mentioning packaging or first use, and photos of the parts list.

Access: read only. Placing the test order spends money; the client approves it or places it. Sending the script to a creator needs the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Money.** Gross profit per order is known from delivered costs [MEA-0037]. The branded retail box counts in product cost, the plain shipping carton in shipping [FIN-0008]. If the ad promises packaging extras, their cost per order is in the sum. A basic branded moment can cost under half a dollar per order and a premium one several dollars [CXP-0008].
2. **Packaging is the same for everyone.** The box on camera is the box every buyer gets this month. A one-off press box is allowed only if the ad says it is a gift set or press kit. Fail: film what ships, or fix packaging first.
3. **Delivery is fit to show.** If the order took far longer than the product page promises, or arrived damaged, do not make this ad yet. Report the problem to the client; it costs more than any ad earns.
4. **Honest speaker.** Gifted or paid creators disclose it [MCR-6025]. No one speaks as a customer unless they bought and used it [MCR-6045].
5. **Labelling.** Packaged goods carry the labels your market's labelling and product safety rules require (for example net quantity, maker or importer details, CE or UKCA marks, warnings). Never cover, blur or edit them out of the shot.

Fail on any gate: stop and say which one, and offer the fallback in step 1.

## Steps

### 1. Decide if unboxing is the right ad

Make an unboxing ad when at least two are true: the packaging looks good on a phone screen; the kit has several parts buyers want to see; the size or scale is a common question; the first use gives a visible or audible moment within seconds. If none are true, make a first-use ad instead: skip the box, open on the product in hand, and go straight to the demo.

### 2. Pick who unboxes

In order of trust: a real customer who bought it (with consent, see `customer-testimonial-videos`), a creator who received it (disclosed), then founder or staff (said plainly, "I run the shop"). Never a staff member acting as a surprised stranger.

### 3. Prep the box, honestly

- Use the actual delivered parcel, courier label visible but address hidden.
- Do not repack, add tissue, or swap in a nicer box.
- Wipe dust; that is all.
- Lay out the parts list from the product page and check every item is there. A missing item is a warehouse problem to report, not to hide.

### 4. Write the script (30 to 45 seconds)

| Beat | Time | What happens | Words (speaker's own) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open | 0 to 3 s | Parcel in hand or at the door, product world visible; first frame shows what this is about [ORG-0017] | One line on why they ordered |
| Open the box | 3 to 10 s | Tape, flap, tissue; real sounds | Little or no talk |
| What is inside | 10 to 20 s | Each part lifted, named, held against a hand for scale | Part names, one useful detail |
| First use | 20 to 35 s | The main benefit shown in one continuous take | What they notice, plainly |
| Verdict and ask | 35 to 45 s | Product held still, label to camera | Honest verdict, one small downside if real, soft ask, disclosure |

Keep the pitch out of the first half and raise it slowly [MCR-0027]. The verdict underclaims; a calm "it does what it says" beats excitement. For premium products, if the opening is messy or lo-fi, keep the product out of that part and reveal it in clean light [MCR-0010]. Products with an instant felt or heard effect (taste, scent, cooling, a click) can lean on the first-use reaction [MCR-0045].

### 5. Build the 15 second cut

Box opens (2 s), hero part lifted (3 s), first use result (6 s), product still with name and ask (4 s). Text carries the story; the voice is optional.

### 6. Plan the shots

12 to 16 numbered shots: parcel at door, label close-up (address hidden), tape pull, box open from above, each part, part in hand for scale, inserts, first use wide, first use close, result, product still, speaker verdict, disclosure take. Record each sound live: tape, cardboard, tissue, cap, click. The product's own sounds beat library effects; add library sounds only to fill gaps (`music-and-sound-for-ads`).

### 7. Write the honesty sheet

List what must appear as is: the courier parcel, every part in the box today, the real size against a hand, the first use without cuts, the required labels, the disclosure. List what must not appear: extras not in every order, a delivery time faster than the store's normal, results the product cannot give on day one.

### 8. Market notes

- **Payment and parcel:** if the order used a payment option (pay later, pay on delivery), the speaker may mention it only if the store still offers it. Show the courier mailer as it arrived; buyers know what tampered parcels look like and trust a real one. Film in the languages your buyers use; mixed-language speech is fine where that is how buyers talk.
- **Disclosure and claims:** the paid or gifted disclosure goes on screen early and is spoken [MCR-6060]; the FTC in the US, the ASA in the UK and EU consumer rules all expect it. A delivery-speed line must match what the store promises at checkout.

## Checks before you hand it over

- The parcel and packaging filmed are what every buyer gets today.
- Every part on the product page appears, and nothing extra does.
- Scale is shown against a hand or a common object.
- First use is one continuous take with no cut inside the result.
- Speaker status is real and disclosed where required.
- No address, phone number or order number is visible.
- Required labels are not hidden.
- The 15 second cut makes sense with the sound off.
- The test order and the creator message had the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** product, who unboxes, order and delivery dates, packaging cost per order, why unboxing (or the first-use fallback), and the main benefit shown.

**Script table:** Beat, Time, Shot code, Action, Spoken line, On-screen text, Sound.

**15 second cut:** Time, Shot code, On-screen text, Sound.

**Shot plan:** Shot code, What is on screen, Framing, Sound to record, Must show, Done.

**Honesty sheet:** Must show as is (list), Must not show (list), Disclosure line, Checked by, Date.

## Common mistakes

- **The press box.** A prettier box than buyers get is a misleading ad. Gate 2 and step 3 stop it.
- **Ignoring a bad delivery.** Gate 3 sends the problem to the client first.
- **Too long before the product appears.** Step 4 opens on the product's world, not a logo.
- **Hidden scale.** Step 6 puts every part against a hand.
- **Cut first-use results.** Step 4 keeps the result in one take.
- **Stock sound effects everywhere.** Step 6 records the product's own sounds.

## Selling in India

- Packaged goods carry an MRP and maker details label under Legal Metrology rules; never hide it.
- If the order was COD, the speaker may say so only if the store still offers it.
- Hinglish is fine on camera.
