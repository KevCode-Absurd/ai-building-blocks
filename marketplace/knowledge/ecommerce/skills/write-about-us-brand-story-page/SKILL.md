# About us and brand story page

**You get:** one about page written in full (headline, opening story, the difference, how it is made, the people, proof, a brand fact box, and a shop-first close), plus an interview log and fact sheet that back every line, ready for the founder's read-through and the client's approval.

## When to use

- The about page is a generic mission statement or a wall of values.
- Buyers ask "who are you?", "where is this made?" or "is this a real company?" before paying, especially for COD or first orders.
- The store is about to run founder ads, pitch press or work with creators, and needs one true story everyone repeats.
- AI assistants or search results repeat wrong facts about the brand.

## Not for

- Founder video or static ad scripts: `founder-story-ads`.
- Positioning, message pillars and proof points for all channels: `write-brand-positioning-and-messaging`.
- Tone and word rules: `brand-voice-guide`.
- The "what we stand against" line: `brand-opponent-statement` (this page can use its result).

## What you need

Required:

1. **A 30 to 45 minute founder interview**, recorded and transcribed (any free recorder and transcript tool works). Questions in step 1.
2. **Fact sheet** the founder or team confirms: founding year, where the team is, where and by whom products are made, materials or ingredient sourcing, certifications with certificate numbers, number of customers or orders (from Shopify), team size.
3. **Reviews export** and any notes from customers about the story.
4. **Current about page** and top 5 products by gross profit and units.

Optional: photos of the founder, team and workshop or supplier (owned or licensed), press links, the brand's opponent statement, early product versions.

If the founder will not be interviewed, use their past posts, emails and talks, and mark every story line "to confirm". Publishing needs draft access (level 2) and the founder's and client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **The story is true and checkable.** Every event, date, number and place in the draft has a source: the interview, a document or a public record. Fail: cut the line. Never invent hardship, a sick relative, a garage start or a "secret recipe".
2. **Origin and making claims are exact.** "Handmade", "made in India", "made in USA", "family-run", "sustainable" are used only if literally true for the products sold now. US "Made in USA" claims follow the FTC's strict rules; ask a professional before using one. Fail: use the exact factual wording ("designed in Pune, made by our partner workshop in Tiruppur").
3. **People shown are real.** Team photos and quotes are of real people who agreed. AI-made people are never presented as staff or customers, and any testimonial follows the FTC review rule [MCR-6021].

## Steps

1. **Interview for scenes, not slogans.** Ask: What were you doing when you first saw the problem? What did you try before, and what went wrong? What was the first version and who bought it? What did a customer say that changed the product? What do you refuse to do that others in the category do? What does a normal work day look like? Who makes it, and have you visited? Ask for one specific moment for each answer.
2. **Mine the transcript.** Pull exact founder lines into a library of approved first-person language; AI may only rearrange and trim that library, never add new "memories" [ORG-0025]. Pull matching buyer words from reviews by hand [AVT-0007].
3. **Find the story arc buyers care about.** Lay the material in this order: the problem in the buyer's life, why the usual options failed, the moment the founder decided to fix it, what the store does differently, proof that it works, and what to try first. A founder story that names a common belief, proves the difference and explains how the product works beat other founder scripts in one agency's top ads [MCR-0006]. The founder does not need to star; team, maker or customer voices can carry the same story [MCR-0005].
4. **Write the headline and opening.** Headline: the problem or promise in buyer words, not "Our story". Opening: one real scene in 80 to 150 words.
5. **Write "what we do differently".** Three points, each a concrete practice (a material choice, a testing step, a return promise, a pricing rule) with proof: a certificate, a photo, a number from Shopify, or a review. If the store has an opponent statement, use it here.
6. **Write "how it is made" and "the people".** Where, by whom, how checked. Name the partner or workshop only with their permission. Real photos with captions saying who and where.
7. **Add proof.** Real counts ("12,400 orders shipped since 2021", from Shopify), dated press quotes with links, two reviews about trust or the founder. Optional 30 to 60 second founder video; one practitioner saw a short founder video that sold the person lift a landing page's form conversion, with no sample size given [LPG-0023], so test it.
8. **Write the brand fact box.** A short, plain block: brand name, founded, founder names, based in, made in, what it sells, price range, shipping regions, returns window, contact, registered business name. An official brand-facts page helped assistants cite correct facts in an Ahrefs test [AEO-0011], and repeating one corrected fact across the site and other channels shifted what an assistant said over time [AEO-0017]. Keep it as text, not an image.
9. **Write the close.** "Where to start": two or three bestsellers with one line each and a button. Then contact routes that work.
10. **India and US notes.** India: show the registered business name, address, customer care contact and grievance officer details where the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 require them; trust lines like "Made in Jaipur by 14 artisans" help first-time and COD buyers when true. Offer a Hindi or regional version only if the brand speaks that way. US: keep origin claims exact.
11. **Founder read-through.** The founder reads the draft aloud. Any line they would not say in person is rewritten from the transcript.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every factual line maps to the interview log or fact sheet.
- No invented scenes, hardships, quotes or numbers.
- Origin and making claims use the exact true wording.
- Photos are of real people and places, with permission noted.
- The fact box is plain text and matches the footer, policies and invoices.
- The page ends with two or three products and a working button.
- The founder approved the read-aloud version.

## Common mistakes

- **Values with no proof.** Step 5 turns each value into a practice with evidence.
- **A story about the founder, not the buyer.** Step 3 starts with the buyer's problem.
- **Polished myths.** Gate 1 cuts every unsourced scene.
- **Loose origin words.** Gate 2 forces exact making and origin claims.
- **A dead end.** Step 9 closes on products.
- **Facts only in images.** Step 8 keeps the fact box as text.
