# Founder plain-text email

**You get:** one plain-text founder email written in full (two subject lines, preview, body of about 120 to 250 words, one text link, P.S.) in a launch version or a win-back version, plus the interview notes it came from, a fact sheet, and a reply plan with the three likely replies and answers, ready for the founder to read, edit and approve.

## When to use

- A launch or restock where a personal note from the founder fits better than another designed email.
- Lapsed buyers who ignored designed win-back emails.
- The last email of a welcome, cart or sale sequence, where a short note from a person closes better than a banner.
- The list has gone quiet and the store wants replies and trust back, not just clicks.

## Not for

- The founder's story for ads, with scripts and filming: use `founder-story-ads`.
- The brand story page on the site: use `write-about-us-brand-story-page`.
- A full win-back series with offers and the sunset step: use `write-win-back-email-series`; this skill can supply its plain-text email.
- An apology for a service failure that needs legal or refund terms: write it with the support lead and `write-store-policy-pages`.

## What you need

Required (read access, level 1):

1. **The job**: launch or win-back, the one ask, the link, any offer with its terms and end.
2. **A 15-minute founder interview** (questions in step 2), recorded or typed.
3. **Founder writing samples**: three or more real messages, posts or emails the founder wrote, so the voice is theirs.
4. **Fact sheet inputs**: dates, numbers and events the story will mention, with where each can be checked.
5. **Reply owner**: who reads replies, their hours, and the inbox address.
6. **For win-back**: the segment definition and when they last bought, from `win-back-lapsed-customers` or the order export.

Optional: past founder emails with click rate, reply count and revenue per recipient.

If the founder cannot give 15 minutes, write from the samples and the brand story page, mark every story line "to confirm", and do not send until they confirm. Drafts need draft access (level 2); sending needs the client's yes and the founder's sign-off.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **The founder signs it.** The email goes out under the founder's name only after they read and approve every line. Never write as a person who has not approved it.
2. **Real sender.** Display name such as "[Founder] from [Brand]", from an address that accepts replies. Gmail requires display names and headers that do not mislead [EML-6340].
3. **Someone answers.** Replies are read and answered within two working days (practitioner default). If nobody can, cut the reply invitation.
4. **Facts.** Every number, date and event in the story is on the fact sheet with a check. Anything unproven is cut.
5. **Offer.** Any offer has passed its gross profit check in the skill that set it.

## Steps

### 1. Choose the job and the one ask

Launch: "see the new [product]". Win-back: "come back and try [what changed]" or "tell me what we got wrong". One ask, one link. Plain-text founder emails with no product link have still driven large revenue at some brands [EML-0057], so a reply-only ask is a real option for win-back.

### 2. Run the interview

Ask five questions and write the answers in the founder's words:

1. What were you doing the day you decided this product had to exist (or had to change)?
2. What did you get wrong at first?
3. What is one small detail in how it is made that most buyers never notice?
4. What do customers tell you that surprised you?
5. If a lapsed customer were in front of you, what would you want to say?

Pull one moment: specific, small, true, with a place or a time. "I was refilling my own bottle at 6 a.m. and the pump broke again" beats "we're passionate about quality".

### 3. Build the fact sheet

List each fact the email uses: the claim, the source, the date checked. Sales numbers, customer counts, awards, sourcing and "first" or "only" claims need proof or are cut.

### 4. Write the email

Shape (not a fixed formula; the founder's voice wins):

- **Greeting** with first name and a plain fallback ("Hi there").
- **Opening line** inside the moment, not "I hope this finds you well". Open with the story or the news, not the category's shared pain [LPG-0045].
- **Two to four short paragraphs**: what happened, what changed, why it matters to the reader.
- **The ask** as one sentence with one text link, not a button.
- **Sign-off** with the founder's first name and role.
- **P.S.** with one extra reason or the real deadline.

Rules: about 120 to 250 words (practitioner default, test it); no images, no banner, no design blocks; short sentences at grade 8 or lower; read it beside the founder's samples and remove anything they would not say. Cut AI tells such as "it's not X, it's Y" [MCR-0016].

### 5. Launch version

Story moment that led to the product, one detail of how it is made, what it does for the reader, the date or link, P.S. with the launch offer end if there is one. Best sent to the waitlist or engaged buyers, not as the first launch email.

### 6. Win-back version

To past buyers lapsed past the store's usual reorder gap; win-backs at 30 to 45 days after the last order convert better than at 90 days or more for repeat products [EML-0050]. The founder notes what changed since they last bought (a fix, a new size, a new product) and asks honestly why they stopped, with a reply invitation. If the segment includes people who complained or returned, they get a separate version that names the problem and the fix, with no sales link. An offer goes only in the P.S., and only if the win-back offer ladder allows it.

### 7. Where it fits in sequences

Plain-text founder notes work as the closing email in abandonment flows [EML-0044] and as the expiry email in a welcome series [EML-0045]. In campaigns, keep them occasional: one practitioner mix runs about three or four designed emails for each plain-text one [EML-0016].

### 8. Write the reply plan

List the three most likely replies (a complaint, a product question, "remove me") and a short answer to each in the founder's voice, plus the rule for passing complaints to support the same day. "Remove me" replies are unsubscribed by hand within the legal window (10 business days in the US [EML-6341]).

### 9. Market notes

- **Other languages:** if the list includes segments in other languages, the founder approves any translation; buyers who paid on delivery get the payment and delivery facts stated plainly in a win-back.
- **UK and EU:** marketing email needs consent (or, in the UK, the soft opt-in for past buyers), a clear sender name and an easy unsubscribe, even in plain text.
- **US:** plain-text still needs the postal address and an unsubscribe link, and must be identifiable as an ad when it sells [EML-6341].

## Checks before you hand it over

- The founder has read and approved every line, in writing.
- Display name and reply address are real, and replies are owned.
- Every fact in the story is on the fact sheet with a check.
- One ask, one text link, no images.
- Word count within about 120 to 250 and grade 8 or lower.
- Win-back version separates buyers with complaints or returns.
- US footer present; offer terms match the offer brief.
- Nothing sent without the client's yes.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** the job, the story moment, the ask, segment and send time.
2. **The email in full:**

```
From: [Founder] from [Brand] <[reply address]>
Subject A: [...]   Subject B: [...]   Preview: [...]

Hi {{ first_name | default: "there" }},

[Opening line inside the moment]
[2 to 4 short paragraphs]
[The ask, with one text link]

[Founder first name]
[Role, Brand]

P.S. [one reason or the real deadline]
[Postal address] [Unsubscribe]
```

3. **Interview notes:** the five answers, the chosen moment.
4. **Fact sheet:** Claim, Source, Date checked, Approved.
5. **Reply plan:** Likely reply, Answer, Owner, Time limit.

## Common mistakes

- **A designed email with the founder's name on it.** Step 4 removes design and keeps one text link.
- **Big words and no moment.** Step 2 finds one small true scene.
- **Ghost-written lines the founder never saw.** Gate 1 requires sign-off.
- **Inviting replies nobody reads.** Gate 3 names an owner.
- **Selling to people who complained.** Step 6 gives them a fix-first version.
- **Using the format every week.** Step 7 keeps it occasional.
