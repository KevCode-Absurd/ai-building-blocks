# Influencer outreach messages

**You get:** a message pack for each creator on the shortlist: one personal first message, follow-up 1 and follow-up 2, the offer terms for their tier, and four reply templates, ready for the client to approve and send.

## When to use

- A vetted shortlist exists and nobody has written to the creators yet.
- Messages have gone out but fewer than 1 in 5 creators reply.
- The store does not know what to offer a nano creator versus a mid-tier one.
- A creator asked "what's the budget?" and the team needs a fair answer.

## Not for

- Choosing who to contact: run `find-and-vet-influencers` first.
- Invites and messages inside TikTok Shop's affiliate center: use `tiktok-shop-creator-affiliate-outreach`.
- The contract once a creator says yes: use `influencer-contract-and-usage-rights`.
- Shipping gifts at scale with no ask: use `plan-influencer-gifting-campaign`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Vetted shortlist** with tier, fit notes, median views and suggested fee, from `find-and-vet-influencers`.
2. **Gross profit per order** for the product being promoted, from `build-sku-profit-sheet`.
3. **Round budget**: cash for fees, units for gifts.
4. **Brand facts**: product, one true reason it is different, price, a discount code rule if any.
5. **Sender**: who the messages come from (founder or a named team member) and from which account.

Optional: the store's past creator results; a one-page media kit.

Access: drafting needs no account access. Sending is a message to people on the client's behalf, so every send needs the client's explicit yes, message by message or as an approved batch.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Profit cap per creator.** Each creator's fee or commission is at or under the profit cap from the shortlist. Fail: lower the offer or switch the deal type in step 3. Commission and gifts come out of the same pool as discounts and ads [FIN-0015].
2. **Stock and shipping.** Enough units for gifts plus the sales a good post brings. Fail: cut the batch.
3. **Disclosure terms ready.** Each offer states the label the creator must use. US: plain words in the post itself [ORG-6405]. UK: "Ad" or "#ad" up front, as the ASA and CMA expect. EU, Australia and other markets: the label the local ad rules ask for, shown for the time they require. Fail: do not send.
4. **Contact route allowed.** Use only a business email or DM route the creator lists publicly. No scraped personal numbers, no bulk WhatsApp blasts.

## Steps

### 1. Pick the deal for each tier

Practitioner defaults; the profit cap overrides them.

| Tier | Default offer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nano (under 10k) | Free product, no posting required, plus a personal code with commission | Low cost; the best ones post unasked |
| Micro (10k to 100k) | Product plus a flat fee per video, or a monthly floor-or-commission deal | Steady output at a known cost |
| Mid (100k to 500k) | Flat fee priced from recent views | Predictable reach |
| Macro (500k+) | Flat fee plus paid usage rights, only if the profit cap allows | High cost; one post rarely pays back |

- **Floor or commission:** guarantee a monthly minimum or a commission rate, whichever is higher, for a set number of videos; one brand used 1,000 dollars a month or 15 to 20% for 30 videos and moved creators to commission only once commission passed the floor [ORG-0036]. Use it only if the margin carries the commission.
- **Flat fee from views:** median views of the last 10 videos x target cost per 1,000 views, cut for audience outside your market [ORG-0041].
- **Paid ad use:** price it as a flat fee, never a share of ad spend, which is hard to track and audit [ORG-0033].
- **First sponsor:** small rising creators often accept a low fee to land a first brand deal; one brand built a long-running program by aiming to be that first sponsor [ORG-0032].

### 2. Find the personal hook

For each creator, write one line that proves you watched: a specific video, what it did well and why it fits the product. If you cannot name a real video, the message is not ready.

### 3. Write the first message

Under 90 words for DM, under 140 for email. Five parts, in order:

1. The personal hook.
2. Who you are, in one line.
3. Why their audience would care, in one line.
4. The offer, with the number (fee, commission %, or "no posting required").
5. One easy question to answer ("Can I send you one to try?").

Rules: no "collab?" with no detail; no fake urgency; no promise of results; no claims outside the approved claims list. For gifts, say plainly that posting is optional and that if they do post, they label it.

### 4. Write the follow-ups

- **Follow-up 1**, 3 to 4 days later: one new reason (a customer review in the creator's niche, a new colour, a launch date). Under 50 words.
- **Follow-up 2**, 7 days after that: a short, polite close ("Happy to leave it here; the offer stands if timing changes"). Stop after this; no third chase.

Practitioner default timing; test it.

### 5. Write the reply templates

- **Yes:** next steps, what you need (address, insight screenshots if a paid deal), and that a short agreement follows.
- **"What's your budget?":** state the offer from step 1 with the number. If they quote higher, offer the floor-or-commission deal or gifting first, not a bigger fee.
- **Rate above the cap:** thank them, explain you set fees from views and fit, give your number once, and leave the door open.
- **No:** thank them and ask if you can stay in touch.

### 6. Disclosure line in every offer

Add to every offer: "If you post, please label it clearly, for example 'Ad' or 'Gifted by [store]' in the first line and on screen, as the rules where you post require." The store must also check posts and ask for fixes [CXP-6303].

### 7. Market notes

- **Any market:** write the first message in the creator's language when they post in a language other than the store's main one; keep the offer numbers in the creator's local currency and say if shipping and any duties are paid by the store. Use WhatsApp only if the creator lists a business number for brand work, and send one message, not a broadcast. Kept products and fees can be taxable income for the creator in many countries; mention that an invoice or tax form may be needed (for example a W-9 in the US).
- **US:** email is the main route above 50,000 followers; many mid and macro creators use managers. Keep the offer in dollars and say if usage rights are included or priced separately.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every first message names a real video the creator made.
- Every offer has a number and sits under that creator's profit cap.
- Every gift offer says posting is optional and asks for a label if they post.
- Follow-ups stop at two.
- No result promises, fake deadlines or unapproved product claims.
- Language matches the creator's own posts.
- Nothing is marked sent; the send list waits for the client's yes.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** number of creators by tier, the deal for each tier, total committed cash if everyone says yes, and the send order (best fit first, in batches of 10).

**Message pack**, one block per creator:

```
Creator: [name, handle, platform, tier]
Channel: [DM / email / WhatsApp business number]
Deal: [type, number, profit cap]
First message: [full text, word count]
Follow-up 1 (day 3 to 4): [full text]
Follow-up 2 (day 10 to 11): [full text]
Disclosure line: [exact wording for their market]
Status: draft, awaiting client yes
```

**Reply templates:** Yes, Budget question, Rate above cap, No (full text, with blanks only for the creator's name).

**Tracking tab:** Creator, Sent date, Follow-up 1 date, Follow-up 2 date, Reply, Outcome, Next step.

## Common mistakes

- **Copy-paste openers.** Step 2 makes every message name a real video.
- **No number in the offer.** Step 3 puts the number in the first message, which saves a round of back and forth.
- **Paying macro creators by habit.** Step 1 sets deals by tier and caps them by profit.
- **Chasing forever.** Step 4 stops at two follow-ups.
- **Gifts with hidden strings.** The gift line says posting is optional and asks for a label; unlabelled gifted posts break the rules in both markets.
- **Revenue-share on ad spend.** Step 1 prices paid use as a flat fee.

## Sources

- FIN-0015: discounts, commission and ads share one acquisition pool.
- ORG-0036: floor-or-commission deal for new creators.
- ORG-0041: fees from recent views, cut for off-market audience.
- ORG-0033: flat fee for paid use, never a share of spend.
- ORG-0032: aim to be a rising creator's first sponsor.
- CXP-6303: the advertiser trains and monitors rewarded creators.
- ORG-6405: FTC disclosure for influencers.

What makes this skill ours: every offer carries a number capped by the store's own profit per order; the deal type changes by tier instead of one rate card; gift messages say posting is optional and ask for a label, which keeps the store inside US, UK and EU disclosure rules; and creators get messages in their own posting language through routes they list for brand work.
