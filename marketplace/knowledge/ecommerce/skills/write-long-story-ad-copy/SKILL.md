# Long story ad copy

**You get:** one long story ad (headline, primary text of about 200 to 500 words, description and call to action) and a short version of 40 to 80 words from the same story, each with the source of every fact, the story's consent record, a seam check and a one-screen note on who it is for and where it should send people.

## When to use

- Short ads get clicks but cold buyers do not buy; the product needs more explaining than a line can give.
- The price is higher than buyers expect and needs a reason.
- The category is new, complex or low-trust, so buyers need a reason to believe [MCR-0033].
- The store has a true story (founder, customer, maker) that nobody has told well in an ad.

## Not for

- A long story landing page or advertorial: use `advertorial-story-page`.
- Founder ad concepts and video scripts: use `founder-story-ads`.
- Short copy sets for testing angles: use `ad-copy-test-sets`.
- Customer video testimonials: use `customer-testimonial-videos`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The story source**, one of: founder interview notes or a recorded call; a customer's own written or recorded account with written consent to use it in ads; the making or sourcing facts (supplier, process, tests) with documents.
2. **Product facts file** with proven claims (from `product-claims-profile`).
3. **Top 3 buying objections** and the customer words for them (reviews, tickets).
4. **Landing page** the ad will send people to, and the offer.
5. **Gross profit per order** for the product.

Optional: past long ads and their results, the brand voice guide, review highlights.

If no true story exists, say so and stop: an invented story presented as real is a fake testimonial. Offer a clearly fictional or illustrative format through `ad-copy-test-sets` instead. Writing is read-only; publishing needs the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **The story is true and consented.** Every event happened, to the named person, and they agreed in writing to its use in ads. A customer's story is a testimonial; it must not be fake or misrepresent their experience [MCR-6021]. Fail: stop.
2. **Long copy fits the problem.** Pass when buyers fail to buy because they do not understand or believe the product, not because of price, stock or a broken page [MCR-0033]. Fail: fix the page or offer first.
3. **Page match.** Long copy in the ad pairs with a short lead on the page; a long ad sent to another long page repeats itself [LPG-0029]. Pass when the destination is the product page or a short, matched page.
4. **Gross profit per order known** [MEA-0037]. Fail: no offer in the copy.

## Steps

### 1. Choose the story type by the buyer's obstacle

Match the story to what stops the buyer [LPG-0003]:

| Obstacle | Story |
|---|---|
| Low trust in the brand | Founder or maker: why it was made, what was refused, what it cost |
| "Will it work for me?" | Customer like them: the doubt, the try, the honest result |
| Too many similar options | Discovery: what the founder or customer learned that others miss |
| Price feels high | Making: materials, process, the cheaper way that was rejected |

A misconception, frustration or counterfeit problem also makes a strong story [ORG-0027].

### 2. Pull the true beats

From the source, write the facts as bullet points before any copy: who, where, when, the moment it went wrong, what they tried, what they found out, what changed, the one specific number or detail. Mark each fact with its source. If a beat is missing, ask the person; never fill the gap.

### 3. Set the awareness stage

Decide how much the buyer already knows [ADC-0001]. Cold buyers who do not know the problem's cause get a story that teaches it; buyers who know the category get a story that shows why this one is different. One ad, one stage.

### 4. Write the opening two lines

On Meta, only the first lines of primary text show before "See more"; check the preview. They must earn the tap. Open on curiosity, a proof point or a promise, not the generic pain every competitor uses [LPG-0045]. Good openings name a specific moment: "The third time my daughter's lunch leaked onto her school books, I cut the tiffin open."

### 5. Write the story body

Order:

1. **The moment:** one person, one scene, one detail.
2. **The want:** what they needed, in their words.
3. **The failed tries:** what they bought or did, and why it did not work. Be fair; no named competitors.
4. **The turn:** what they found out (the cause, the missing piece). Give the reader a few lines of why before the product appears [MCR-0014].
5. **The product enters** as the answer to the cause, with one or two proven facts.
6. **Proof:** a counted fact, a review line with its source, a test.
7. **The ask:** the offer, the risk reversal (returns, guarantee), one clear action.

Write at a simple reading level in full, flowing sentences, with comparisons close to the reader's life [LPG-0047]. Short paragraphs of one to three sentences for phone reading. No "it's not X, it's Y" lines [MCR-0016].

### 6. Check the seams

Read the draft and mark every sentence where the story stops and the pitch starts. Each is a likely exit point [LPG-0012]. Rewrite each inside the story: instead of "Our lunch box has a silicone seal," write "So I made the lid with a silicone seal, the kind used in pressure cookers."

### 7. Hold the claims

Every product claim in the story is either proven or a personal experience the named person had. "It changed my life" from a customer is their experience; "it will change yours" is a claim the store must prove. Remove any implied promise of typical results unless the store's data supports it.

### 8. Write the short version

40 to 80 words from the same story: the opening line, the turn in one sentence, the product in one sentence, the call to action. It runs as its own ad and as the test against the long one.

### 9. Market notes

- **UK, EU and Australia:** prices include VAT or the local sales tax; name pay later or any payment offer only if live. Ads must not mislead by leaving out facts a buyer needs, under UK CAP Code and CMA rules and EU unfair practice rules.
- **Other languages:** a version in another language your buyers use is written or checked by a native speaker, not machine translated alone.
- **US:** a customer story paid for with free product or money needs that relationship disclosed [MCR-6021].

### 10. Plan the read

Run long and short in the same ad set, same image, same audience. Judge on store orders and gross profit per order after enough spend; one practitioner's rule is about 3x the target cost per purchase before calling a concept [ADC-0039].

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every event in the story has a source, and the consent record is attached.
- The opening two lines show a specific moment, proof or promise, not generic pain.
- The product appears only after the turn, and each seam has been rewritten into the story.
- Every product claim is proven or framed as the named person's own experience.
- The short version keeps hook, turn and call to action in 80 words or fewer.
- The destination page matches the story's promise and is not another long page.
- Paragraphs are one to three sentences; reading level checked.
- Nothing published without the client's yes.

## Common mistakes

- **Inventing or "improving" the story.** Gate 1 and step 2 pull only sourced beats.
- **Opening on the pain every rival uses.** Step 4.
- **Product too early.** Step 5 puts the turn first.
- **Hard seams between story and pitch.** Step 6.
- **Long ad to a long page.** Gate 3.
- **No short version to test against.** Step 8.
