# One-page creative brief for a single ad

**You get:** one filled brief of about one page (under 500 words) for one ad, in the template below, that a designer, editor or creator can produce from without a call, plus a three-line note for the media buyer saying where the ad goes and what result decides it.

## When to use

- One ad concept is approved and must go to a designer, editor or creator.
- Briefs keep coming back with the wrong product, wrong claim or wrong size.
- A freelancer or new creator is joining and needs one clear standard.
- An AI image or video tool will make the ad and needs a fixed spec to follow.

## Not for

- A month of briefs with two approval gates: use `monthly-ad-batch-sprint`.
- Choosing which angle to brief: use `mine-ad-angles-from-reviews` or `proof-scored-ad-ideas` first.
- The full creator script, line by line: use `creator-video-script` after this brief.
- The layout grid for a static: use `static-ad-layout-blueprint` after this brief.

## What you need

Required:

1. **The approved angle**: one buyer, one tension, one belief, from the angle table or the owner.
2. **The product's claims sheet**: what can be said and the proof file for each claim. Use `product-claims-profile` if none exists.
3. **The live offer**: price, any code, valid dates, and the landing page URL, checked today on the live site.
4. **Gross profit per purchase and target cost per purchase** for this product, from `store-meta-math`.
5. **Brand basics**: logo files, fonts, colours, voice notes (`store-visual-identity-sheet` if the store has one).

Optional: 2 or 3 reference ads the owner likes, past winners' ad names, raw footage or product photos on hand.

If the offer or landing page cannot be confirmed live, write the brief with no offer line and flag it. If gross profit is unknown, the test section says "judging line not set" and the brief cannot be marked ready. This job needs no access to ad accounts. Sending the brief to an outside creator is the client's call; the agent drafts, the client approves and sends.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Judging line known.** Pass when target cost per purchase comes from gross profit per purchase. Fail: send to `store-meta-math` first; an ad with no line to beat cannot be called a win or a loss later.
2. **Buyer can act.** Product in stock for the test window (at least 4 weeks of cover) and the landing page live at the stated price. Fail: pick another product or hold.
3. **Every claim has proof.** Pass when each claim in the brief maps to a file. In the US an objective claim needs a reasonable basis before it runs, and "tests show" needs the test [MCR-6022]; the UK CAP Code, EU consumer law and Australia's consumer law ask the same. Fail: cut or narrow the claim.
4. **Rights.** Any face, voice, music or customer quote has written permission for paid use, or the brief says how to make the ad without it.

## Steps

### 1. Name the test first

Write the one question this ad answers ("Does the gift-for-mum angle beat our best evergreen ad for new buyers?"), what stays the same (offer, page, audience), and where it runs. A new concept usually goes into its own test ad set; hand placement to `ad-launch-prepublish-check`. Set the readout rule now: practitioner rule, judge after spend of about 3 times target cost per purchase (3 or more purchases keep, 2 wait, 0 or 1 cut), or 5 times for a firm call [ADC-0039]. Small accounts may need longer; say so.

### 2. Fix the buyer and the belief

One buyer in one moment, in plain words ("a daughter in another city choosing a birthday gift on her phone at lunch"). Then the belief they hold now and the belief the ad must leave. Name the awareness stage and keep the whole ad on it; mixing a product-aware image with problem-aware words fails both groups [MCR-0056].

### 3. Set the job by destination

Say where the click lands. An ad to a quiz or article only has to earn the click; an ad to a product page must sell the product itself [MCR-0030]. For a static, choose: fully clear, or a deliberate curiosity gap sent to an article page, never the middle [MCR-0019].

### 4. Write the hook on three levers

Give the first visual, the on-screen text and the first spoken line separately; each is its own lever [MCR-0028]. The hook must pull in buyers, not just stop the scroll: it should name the buyer's situation or the product's world so the wrong people scroll past [MCR-0026]. The body must match the hook's promise; a shock hook with a feature-list body loses the people it stopped [MCR-0015]. Offer 2 to 4 alternate hooks for the same body [MCR-0013].

### 5. List the proof and the beats

Order the middle: problem in the buyer's words, why it happens (if needed), the product as the fix, one proof, the offer, the call to action. For video, the first half matters most because most viewers leave early; put the benefit before features and show the product doing the job [MCR-0077]. Each proof line names its file: review ID, test report, photo, warranty page.

### 6. Fill format and specs

Placements and ratios (9:16 for Reels and Stories, 4:5 for feed, 1:1 if needed), length for video (15, 30 or 45 seconds), safe areas from Meta's current guide for each placement, captions burned in for sound-off, logo and price rules, and the file names. Resizing and safe-area checks can go to `resize-ads-for-every-placement`.

### 7. Write the must-nots

- No claim outside the claims sheet; no "best" or "number one" without proof.
- No line that asks about or asserts the viewer's health, age, money or other personal attribute on Meta [MCR-6023].
- Founder, staff, relatives and gifted creators are labelled as such in the US, and a standout result shows what buyers generally get [MCR-6025].
- AI-made people or voices are never presented as real customers; follow the platform's AI label setting.
- In AI-written scripts, no "it's not X, it's Y" lines; viewers read the pattern as machine copy [MCR-0016].
- UK, EU and Australia: price shown includes VAT or the local sales tax. Anywhere: name a payment option (pay later, pay on delivery) only if offered; if the page is in another language, the ad language matches it.

### 8. Read it as the maker

Read the brief as someone who has never met the store. Every blank they would ask about is a failure; fill it or cut the field.

## Checks before you hand it over

- The brief fits one page and names one buyer, one angle, one product.
- The hook is given on all three levers, with 2 to 4 alternates.
- Every claim and proof line points to a named file.
- The offer, price and landing page were checked live today, with the date written.
- The test question, what stays the same and the judging line in money are filled in.
- Specs list each ratio, length, caption rule and file name.
- Must-nots cover claims, personal attributes, disclosures and AI use.
- Nothing is sent to a creator or uploaded without the client's yes.

## Common mistakes

- **Brief with no test.** Step 1 names the question and the money line before any creative.
- **Hook as one sentence.** Step 4 splits visual, text and voice so the maker can build each.
- **Hook and body that do not match.** Step 4 checks the body keeps the hook's promise.
- **Stale offers.** The offer line carries the date it was checked live.
- **Proof the maker invents.** Step 5 ties every proof to a file.
- **Banned call-outs.** Step 7 rewrites viewer-attribute lines before production, not after a rejection.

## Sources

- ADC-0039: judge a new concept after about 3x target cost per purchase.
- MCR-0013: 2 to 4 hook variants per body.
- MCR-0015: hook and body must match.
- MCR-0016: no "it's not X, it's Y" lines in AI scripts.
- MCR-0019: statics are fully clear or a deliberate curiosity gap.
- MCR-0026: a hook must filter for buyers.
- MCR-0028: three hook levers.
- MCR-0030: the ad's job depends on its destination.
- MCR-0056: one awareness stage per ad.
- MCR-0077: fix the first half of a video first.
- MCR-6022 (proposed): US substantiation before claims run.
- MCR-6023 (proposed): Meta personal attributes policy.
- MCR-6025 (proposed): US endorsement disclosures and expected results.

What makes this skill ours: the brief starts from the test and a money line built on gross profit, not from a mood board; the offer and page are checked live and dated; every proof line points to a file a reviewer can open; and the must-nots carry the real US, UK, EU and Meta rules, so the ad is not rejected after the money is spent making it.
