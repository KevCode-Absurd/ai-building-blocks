# Ad copy from customer reviews

**You get:** six to nine ready ad copy sets (primary text, headline, description, on-screen text), each built on one or two real review quotes and answering one buying objection, plus a quote register with the source of every quote, and the one-screen summary saying which set to test first.

## When to use

- The store has 50 or more reviews and the ads still describe the product in brand language.
- Buyers hesitate on a known doubt (fit, taste, durability, "will it work for me") and reviews answer it.
- A winning ad needs new copy, and customers have already written the angles.
- Retargeting ads need proof, not more claims.

## Not for

- Finding and ranking ad angles from reviews: use `mine-ad-angles-from-reviews` (this skill can take its output).
- Designing review screenshot images: use `review-screenshot-ads`.
- Filming customers: use `customer-testimonial-videos`.
- Getting more reviews: use `build-review-collection-system`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Review export** with: review ID, product, rating, title, body, reviewer display name, date, verified-purchase flag, source (store review app, Google, Amazon, marketplace), and whether the review was incentivised.
2. **Display permission:** the review app's terms or the store's review request wording that lets the store reuse reviews in marketing. For quotes off third-party sites (Amazon, Google), check that site's terms; when unsure, ask the reviewer.
3. **The top 5 buying objections** from support tickets, chats, abandoned-checkout surveys or `buyer-decision-map`.
4. **Product facts file**, so review claims can be checked against what is proven.

Optional: photos and videos attached to reviews, past ads with results, post-purchase survey answers.

If there is no permission basis, write the copy but mark quotes "permission pending" and do not publish them. Reading reviews needs read access. Contacting reviewers is a message on the client's behalf and needs the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Reviews are real.** Use only reviews from real buyers. Fake or false reviews and testimonials, and misrepresenting what a reviewer experienced, are banned in the US, with civil penalties for knowing breaches [MCR-6021]. Fail on any doubt about a review: leave it out.
2. **Permission to refer to the person.** Many markets expect permission before an ad names or shows a real person, and UK and EU data protection rules treat a reviewer's name and photo as personal data. Pass when the review terms or a reply from the reviewer covers it.
3. **Claims inside reviews are checked.** A customer may write "cured my eczema". The ad cannot repeat a health or result claim the store cannot prove, even in a quote. Fail: skip that quote or trim it to the part that is a personal experience the store can stand behind, without changing its meaning.
4. **Gross profit per order known** for any product the copy pushes with an offer [MEA-0037].

## Steps

### 1. Clean and sort the reviews

Keep verified purchases first. Remove staff, family and gifted reviews, or keep them only with a clear label; insiders' reviews need disclosure [MCR-6021]. Split by product. Read them by hand: automated summaries strip out the emotional words copy needs [AVT-0007].

### 2. Tag each useful review

For every review worth quoting, tag:

- **objection answered** (one of the top 5);
- **before state**: what the buyer tried or feared before;
- **who they are**, if they say it ("as a nurse", "I'm a size 18"). Phrases that repeat mark a buyer group worth its own ad [AVT-0016];
- **specific detail**: a time, place, number or sensory word;
- **failed fixes**: "I spent thousands on other creams". These buyers are the highest intent [AVT-0009].

### 3. Pick quotes by objection, not by stars

For each of the top 5 objections, pick the two best quotes that answer it. A review that answers "will it fit my wide feet" beats ten that say "love it" [EML-0033]. Choose quotes from different customer types so the set does not read as one person [LPG-0053]. Include a 4-star review where it answers the doubt honestly; it reads as real.

### 4. Trim without changing meaning

Rules for every quote:

- Keep the buyer's words. Do not swap in nicer words.
- You may cut words at the start or end, and use "..." for a gap, only when the meaning stays the same. "Didn't think it would work but... my feet were fine after a 10-hour shift" is fine if the review said so.
- Never join two reviews into one quote. Never change a number or time.
- Fix nothing but obvious spacing; spelling slips can stay, as they read as real.
- Translated quotes (a Spanish review in an English ad) are labelled "translated from Spanish", with the original in the register.

Record each trim in the register.

### 5. Write the copy sets

Build 6 to 9 sets. Each set has one objection and one structure:

| Structure | Shape |
|---|---|
| Quote lead | Quote as the first line, then one line of product fact, then the offer and call to action |
| Doubt then answer | Name the doubt in the buyer's words ("Worried it won't fit?"), then the quote that answers it |
| Many voices | Three short quotes from three customer types on one theme; at most one plain claim line after them, such as the rating with its review total |
| Before and after in words | The failed fix quote, then the result quote, from the same or different buyers, clearly attributed |

Attribution: first name and last initial, or the display name the reviewer used, plus "verified buyer" only when the flag is true. Show where the review is from when it is not the store's own site. Named, sourced reviews read as real; bare anonymous quotes now read as fake [LPG-0037].

Hooks and headlines: when the brief gives the buyer's own phrases (from interviews, surveys or the persona), open with them; otherwise use the strongest 3 to 6 words of a quote ("Fine after a 10-hour shift"). A count appears only when the count is the claim, as one plain line such as "Helping 2,400 runners with wide feet" from the real review count [LPG-0049]. Count only reviews that exist today.

Customer words, not tallies: never write how many reviewers said something ("N reviewers mention the fit") in the copy. That tally is evidence for the owner, so it goes in the summary beside the set it supports. In the copy, let one customer say it.

### 6. Add typicality where results vary

If a quote describes a result that not every buyer gets (a fast effect, a big change), add "Results vary" or state what is typical, using the store's own data where it exists. Never imply one review is everyone's result.

### 7. Fit each platform

- **Meta:** quote in the first line of primary text so it shows before "See more"; headline from the quote; description with the rating and review total as one plain claim, or left for the offer.
- **Google Search:** short quote fragments of 30 characters or fewer as headlines, rating and count as a description, only if true on the landing page.
- **TikTok and Reels:** on-screen quote text over product footage; never have an actor or AI voice read a review as if they wrote it.

### 8. Choose the test order

Lead with the set answering the objection most often named in abandoned checkouts or tickets [MCR-0043]. Test two sets with different objections, same image, same audience. Judge on store orders and gross profit.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every quote matches the review text exactly, apart from recorded trims marked with "...".
- No quote combines two reviews or changes a number.
- Every quote has a register row: review ID, source, date, rating, reviewer name as shown, verified flag, permission basis.
- No quoted health, result or safety claim the store cannot prove.
- Staff, gifted or incentivised reviews are excluded or labelled.
- Review counts and ratings match the live site today, and appear in the copy at most once per set, as one plain claim.
- No copy line tallies reviewers ("N reviewers say"); tallies sit in the summary.
- No actor or AI voice presents a review as their own.
- Nothing published without the client's yes; "permission pending" quotes are held.

## Output

**1. One-screen summary:** top objections, the set for each and the evidence behind it (how many reviews raise or answer that objection), test order, permission status. The evidence stays here, out of the copy.

**2. Copy sets:**

**3. Quote register:**

**4. Permissions:** each quoted review carries one line beside it: "reviewer's permission before use". A claim with no proof is cut.

## Common mistakes

- **Picking 5-star praise that answers nothing.** Step 3 picks by objection.
- **"Tidying" a quote into ad language.** Step 4 keeps the buyer's words.
- **Stitching two reviews into one testimonial.** Banned in step 4.
- **Quoting a result claim the store cannot make.** Gate 3.
- **Anonymous quotes.** Step 5 attributes every one.
- **Copy that reads like a report.** "N of our reviewers say" belongs in the summary; the ad lets one customer say it.
- **A creator reading a review as their own.** Step 7.
