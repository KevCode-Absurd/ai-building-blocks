# Store policy pages

**You get:** 5 ready-to-paste pages (shipping, returns and refunds, privacy, terms of service, contact), each opening with a 3 to 5 line summary box, plus the operations fact sheet they rest on, a placement list (footer, product page, cart, checkout, emails) and a short list of points for a lawyer to review.

## When to use

- A new store needs its policy pages before launch.
- Couriers, return rules, refund timing or payment methods changed.
- Support tickets show buyers misreading the return window or shipping times.
- The current pages are an untouched template that mentions things the store does not do.

## Not for

- Designing the returns process, portal and exchange-first push: use `set-up-returns-and-exchanges`.
- Setting shipping rates and the delivery promise: use `set-up-shipping-rates-and-delivery-promise`.
- Short reassurance lines in cart and checkout: use `write-cart-checkout-and-shipping-microcopy`.
- Legal advice: a lawyer reviews; this skill writes plain drafts and flags what to check.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Operations facts**, confirmed by the owner: order cut-off time, dispatch days, couriers, delivery ranges by zone, holidays, return window, condition rules, who pays return shipping, refund method and timing per payment type, exchange option, damaged or wrong item process, COD rules.
2. **Business identity**: legal name, registered address, GSTIN or state registrations, support email, phone or WhatsApp, support hours.
3. **Data facts**: apps and tools that receive customer data (email, SMS, WhatsApp, analytics, ads, reviews, helpdesk), where the store sells, whether it sells to the EU or UK.
4. **Current policy pages** and the last 90 days of support tickets tagged shipping, returns or refunds.

Optional: competitor policies (2), courier SLA document, chargeback history.

Writing is level 1 work. Publishing pages is level 2 (draft) then live with the client's yes. If an operations fact is missing, do not invent it: leave the line out and name it in the summary.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Operations facts are confirmed.** Pass when the owner signs the fact sheet. Fail: stop; a policy the warehouse cannot follow is worse than none.
2. **Refund promise is affordable.** Pass when returns and refund terms are costed in gross profit per order [MEA-0037]. Fail: hand to `returns-and-rto-cost-analysis` first.
3. **Legal review route exists.** Pass when the client names who reviews the privacy and terms pages. Fail: publish shipping and returns only, with privacy and terms from Shopify's templates marked for review.

## Steps

### 1. Build the fact sheet

One line per fact, with source and owner: "Cut-off 2 pm IST Mon to Sat (warehouse lead)". Every sentence in every policy must trace to a line here.

### 2. Shipping policy

Write in this order: dispatch time and cut-off; delivery range by zone; shipping charges and free-shipping line; COD availability and fee; how tracking is sent; what happens if an order is late; address errors; international orders and duties.

- US: the shipping time stated needs a reasonable basis; if no time is stated, the default expectation is 30 days. If the store cannot ship on time it must offer the buyer a delay or cancel choice, and refund cancelled orders within 7 working days for most payment methods [CXP-6160]. Write that promise in.
- Use the same delivery range wording as the product page and checkout.

### 3. Returns and refunds policy

Order: the window (counted from delivery, not order), what can and cannot be returned, condition rules, how to start a return, who pays return shipping, exchange option, refund method and timing per payment type, damaged or wrong items, and COD refunds.

- Fast refunds matter: a poor return experience puts most buyers off buying again [CXP-0007], and slow refunds lose intent [CXP-0015]. State a refund time the team can meet.
- For low-value items, refund without return can cost less than processing a return [CXP-0018]; say so only if the owner chose it.
- On low-cost consumables, a longer guarantee can bring fewer claims than a short one [OFF-0060]; offer it only if the owner agrees and it is costed.
- A clear return window of 30 days or more is one of the inputs to Google's store quality badge [GAD-0018].
- India: COD refunds go by bank transfer or UPI after the return is received and checked; say how the buyer shares details safely (a secure form, never by posting them in chat comments).

### 4. Privacy policy

Start from Shopify's privacy policy template, then edit to the truth: what is collected, why, which tools receive it (from the data facts), how long it is kept, how buyers ask for access or deletion, cookies and consent, and contact. EU or UK buyers bring GDPR duties; California and India have their own data laws. The lawyer confirms which apply. Never claim certifications or practices the store does not have.

### 5. Terms of service

Start from Shopify's template and edit: who the seller is, prices and errors, order acceptance and cancellation, payment methods, product information, limits of liability, governing law, and how disputes are handled. Cut every clause about services the store does not offer.

### 6. Contact page

Support email, phone or WhatsApp, hours, expected reply time, order tracking link, and the registered business address. India: name a grievance contact with how and when complaints are acknowledged; the lawyer confirms the current rule. Keep card or bank details out of contact forms; never ask buyers to send card numbers anywhere outside checkout [SEC-0006].

### 7. Write plainly

Grade 8 to 9 reading level. Short sentences, "we" and "you", numbers in digits, days stated as business or calendar. Each page opens with a summary box of 3 to 5 lines. No phrases like "at our sole discretion" unless the lawyer keeps them.

### 8. Check against reality

For each policy line, ask the owner of that step: can you do this every time, including on the busiest day? Compare against the last 90 days of tickets: each common question should be answered in the policy. Run one test return and refund before publishing [SET-0003].

### 9. Place the pages

Footer (all five), checkout policy links (Shopify settings, Policies), product page (one-line shipping and returns summary linking to the page), cart, order confirmation and shipping emails. Mark the date of last update on each page.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Every sentence traces to a line in the signed fact sheet.
- The delivery range matches the product page and checkout wording exactly.
- Refund timing is stated per payment type, including COD.
- The window is counted from delivery and stated in days.
- No template clause describes a service, certification or practice the store does not have.
- Each page opens with a summary box and reads at grade 8 to 9.
- The lawyer review list names each clause to check.

## Output

**One-screen summary:** the 5 pages, what changed from the old versions, any operations fact left out, and the lawyer list.

**Operations fact sheet:** Fact, Value, Source, Owner, Confirmed date.

**Page template** (each page):

1. Title, last updated date.
2. Summary box (3 to 5 lines).
3. Sections with plain headings ("How long delivery takes", "How to return an item", "When you get your money back").
4. Contact line at the end.

**Placement list:** Page, Location, Link text, Done.

**Lawyer review list:** Page, Clause, Question, Reviewer, Date.

## Common mistakes

- **Template copied as is.** It promises things the store does not do. Steps 1 and 8.
- **Window counted from order date.** Buyers who wait for delivery lose days. Step 3 counts from delivery.
- **Vague refund timing.** Step 3 states days per payment type.
- **Policies that differ from the product page.** Step 2 uses the same wording.
- **No late-order promise for US buyers.** Step 2 writes it in [CXP-6160].
- **Asking buyers to send bank or card details in chat.** Steps 3 and 6 prevent it.

- CXP-0015: instant refunds and repurchase; slow refunds lose intent.

- OFF-0060: longer guarantees on low-cost consumables.

- SEC-0006: never take card numbers outside checkout.
- SET-0003: test the flow before going live.
- MEA-0037: gross margin uses delivered cost.
