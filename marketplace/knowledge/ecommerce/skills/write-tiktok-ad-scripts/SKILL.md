# TikTok ad scripts

**You get:** a set of five scripts for one product, each 15 to 45 seconds, each with three spoken openers to test, timed beats, the exact on-screen text per beat, a caption, a call to action, a shot note and the claims it relies on, plus a one-screen summary with the order to test them. Ready to send to creators or to shoot in-house.

## When to use

- A TikTok test or monthly batch needs new concepts for one product.
- Current TikTok ads are Meta ads resized to vertical and they stall.
- Creators keep asking "what do I say?" and come back with off-brand videos.
- A product sells on an instant reaction (taste, texture, smell, a visible fix) that suits TikTok.

## Not for

- One deep creator script with shot list and delivery notes for a single concept: use `creator-video-script`.
- Openers only, on a body that already works: use `ad-opener-writer`.
- Setting up Spark Ads with creator codes: use `tiktok-spark-ads-with-creators`.
- Sizing and reading the TikTok test: use `tiktok-ads-creative-testing`.

## What you need

Required:

1. **Product facts and proof file**: what is proven, what customers report, what is banned (from `product-claims-profile` if it exists).
2. **10 to 30 real customer lines**: reviews, comments, support tickets, with their source. These give the spoken words.
3. **Live offer and page**: price, delivery, returns, payment options, and the page the ad will send people to.
4. **Who will say it**: in-house staff, founder, paid creators, or Spark Ads from existing posts.

Optional: the store's past TikTok ads with 2-second view rate, 6-second view rate, CTR and cost per purchase; top organic posts on the brand or creator accounts.

If customer lines are missing, mine them first with `mine-ad-angles-from-reviews`. Writing is read-only work. Sending briefs to creators is a message on the client's behalf and needs the client's yes; spend needs a separate yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Gross profit per order.** Pass when delivered cost is known [MEA-0037]. Fail: no discount scripts; write the other formats.
2. **Proof for each claim.** Every result a creator will say out loud must be proven or customer-reported with a source. Fail: drop the claim, do not soften it into a hint.
3. **Real people, real experience.** A creator may only say they used the product if they did. Fake or false testimonials are banned in the US [MCR-6021]. AI presenters must never pose as real customers; check TikTok's current AI-generated content label rules and use the label.
4. **Ad labels.** Creator posts used as ads must be marked as ads or paid partnerships; in the US, UK, EU and most other markets, disguised ads are treated as misleading.
5. **Category rules.** Health, weight, skin and supplements need `check-ad-copy-for-policy-and-claims` on the final scripts.

## Steps

### 1. Pick one buyer and one moment per script

From the customer lines, list the 3 to 5 moments when people feel the problem ("my feet hurt by 4pm", "my lunch box leaks in my bag"). Each script gets one buyer in one moment. The opener must speak to that buyer, not just stop the scroll, because the platform learns from who keeps watching [MCR-0026].

### 2. Give the five scripts five different formats

Vary the format, not just the words. Near-copies teach nothing.

| # | Format | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Talking-head story: "I used to... then..." | Product solves a felt daily problem |
| 2 | Demo with voice-over: show it working, close up | The result is visible in seconds |
| 3 | Reply to a real comment or question | There is a common objection in comments or tickets |
| 4 | List: "3 things I wish I knew before buying..." | Buyers compare options |
| 5 | Reaction or first try | The product has an instant felt sensation [MCR-0045] |

Swap in day-in-the-life or before-and-after when it fits the product better, but keep five different formats.

### 3. Write three spoken openers per script

A hook has three levers: the spoken line, the on-screen text and the first visual [MCR-0028]. For each script write three openers that differ in the spoken line, each 3 to 7 words, plus the on-screen text and the first shot. One practitioner weighs the opening words most [MCR-0063]; another brand cuts every ad with 4 to 5 openers because taste is unreliable [MCR-0076]. Three per script is the practitioner default here; test it.

### 4. Write the beats in spoken words

Structure for 15 to 45 seconds:

1. **Opener** (0 to 3 s).
2. **Problem or setup** in the customer's words, with one specific detail (a time, a place, a number from a real review).
3. **Show it:** the product doing the job on camera. State benefits before features; only a minority of viewers reach the second half [MCR-0077].
4. **Turn:** the honest result, understated. Not "life-changing", but "my feet were fine at 6pm".
5. **Call to action:** what to tap and why now, only with a real reason.

Raise the selling gradually; a sudden jump from story to pitch loses viewers [MCR-0027]. After a strong problem opener, give a few seconds of why it happens before naming the product [MCR-0014].

Spoken-word rules: contractions, short sentences, words a person would say aloud. No "it's not X, it's Y" lines, which read as AI copy [MCR-0016]. Read each line aloud; if it sounds written, rewrite it.

### 5. Write on-screen text and the caption

- On-screen text per beat: 3 to 8 words, carrying the point for sound-off viewers. Keep it out of the bottom and right edges where TikTok's buttons and caption sit; `captions-and-text-for-sound-off` has the safe-area rules.
- Caption: one line, keep it short enough to show before "See more" (check the current limit in TikTok Ads Manager), plus one relevant hashtag at most.
- Call to action: spoken, on screen, or both. Whether a spoken CTA hurts retention on TikTok is contested [MCR-0061]; write each script with a soft spoken CTA and mark it as cuttable so the test can tell.

### 6. Add the shot note

Per script: setting, who is on camera, 3 to 6 shots, props, and the one product moment that must be visible (the leak test, the fabric stretch). Phone footage in natural light is the default; polished studio footage often reads as an ad.

### 7. Market notes

- **UK, EU and Australia:** show prices with VAT or the local sales tax included; name pay later or other payment options in the call to action only if live.
- **Other languages:** write versions of the openers in the languages your target buyers speak, written or checked by a native speaker.
- **US:** creators must disclose paid relationships; "#ad" or the platform's paid partnership tag. TikTok Shop scripts should be ranked later on revenue per 1,000 views, not view count [MCR-0046].

### 8. Set the test order

Rank the five by: proof strength, how clearly the opener calls out the buyer, and ease of shooting. Test 3 first. Early read guides from one practitioner: in 24 hours CTR above 1% and watch time above 25%; these are one account's numbers, so compare to the store's own past ads [ADC-0037]. A first test of 3 creative styles across 2 audiences for 3 to 4 days is one practitioner's starting point [ADC-0036]. Judge winners on store orders and gross profit, through `tiktok-ads-creative-testing`.

## Checks before you hand it over

- Five scripts, five different formats, one buyer and moment each.
- Each script has three openers that differ in the spoken line.
- Every claim in a script links to a proof line or a sourced customer quote.
- Read aloud, no line sounds written; no "it's not X, it's Y".
- On-screen text carries the point with sound off and avoids the button area.
- No one claims an experience they did not have; AI presenters are labelled and never posed as customers.
- Offers, prices and delivery lines match the live page.
- Nothing was sent to creators or spent without the client's yes.

## Common mistakes

- **Five versions of one idea.** Step 2 forces five formats.
- **Openers that grab everyone.** Step 1 ties the opener to one buyer [MCR-0026].
- **Scripts that read like ad copy.** Step 4's read-aloud rule.
- **Creators saying results they never had.** Gate 3 and the claims column.
- **Text under TikTok's buttons.** Step 5 keeps it in the safe area.
- **Calling winners on views.** Step 8 reads store orders.
