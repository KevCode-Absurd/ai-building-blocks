# Value newsletter email

**You get:** one newsletter issue written in full (two subject lines, preview, body of about 200 to 350 words, one product tie-in, P.S.), the repeatable format sheet for the series, and a bank of the next eight issue topics with their source and product link, ready to load as a draft for the client to approve.

## When to use

- Almost every campaign is a sale, and unsubscribes or discount waiting are rising.
- The store sends too few campaigns because nobody knows what to write.
- The product needs teaching: how to use it, how to choose, care, recipes, routines.
- The brand has stories (making, sourcing, customers) that never reach the list.

## Not for

- The monthly plan of all sends with dates and segments: use `plan-monthly-email-calendar`, which places this series in it.
- A sale or offer email: use `write-promo-campaign-email`.
- A founder's personal note: use `write-founder-plain-text-email`.
- Organic social posts from the same ideas: use `repurpose-ads-into-organic-posts` or the social team.

## What you need

Required (read access, level 1):

1. **Questions buyers ask**: the last 90 days of support tickets, chat and WhatsApp questions, tagged by topic.
2. **Reviews export**: lines that show how people use the product, what surprised them, what they struggled with.
3. **Product facts**: how it is made, sourcing, care, use steps, and the **approved claims list** from `product-claims-profile`.
4. **Campaign history**, last 12 weeks: subject, type (sale, product, education), recipients, click rate, revenue per recipient, unsubscribe rate.
5. **Brand voice**: five real lines the owner likes.

Optional: customer photos or stories with permission, a team member who can share behind-the-scenes facts, seasonal dates that matter to buyers.

If support data is thin, use the reviews and the product page's questions; never invent customer questions. Drafts need draft access (level 2); sending needs the client's yes.

## Before you start: the gates

1. **Inbox health.** Engaged campaign opens under 30 to 35% point to placement trouble [EML-0059]; send the series to the engaged segment and flag `email-account-health-audit`.
2. **Capacity.** Name who writes, who approves, and the cadence the team can keep for eight weeks. A series that stops after two issues does harm.
3. **Claims.** Health, results, safety and environmental claims come only from the approved list.
4. **Permission.** Customer stories, photos and names only with written permission.

## Steps

### 1. Define the format once

Name the series (a plain name the brand would use, such as "[Brand] Notes" or "The Sunday fix"). Then fix its sections so each issue is fast to write and readers know what they get:

| Section | Length | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Hook line | 1 sentence | The useful thing, stated plainly |
| The one idea | 120 to 200 words | One micro-topic only [EML-0022] |
| Do this today | 2 to 4 steps or 1 tip | Something the reader can try now |
| From the shop | 1 to 3 lines | One product tied to the idea, with a link |
| P.S. | 1 line | A reader question for next time, or a small extra |

About 70% useful or entertaining and at most 30% pitch [LPG-0015]. Grade 5 to 8 reading level [ORG-0024]. Total about 200 to 350 words (practitioner default, test it). Plain or lightly designed; one image at most.

### 2. Build the topic bank

Make eight topics, each from a real source:

- **Questions:** the most asked support questions, answered fully ("Can I use the serum with retinol?").
- **Mistakes:** what reviews show people get wrong.
- **How it is made:** one step of making or sourcing a buyer never sees.
- **Choose well:** how to pick between two of the store's products.
- **Seasonal:** care or use for the season, or a date the buyers care about.
- **Customer story:** one real customer's use, with permission.

Each topic lists its source, its product tie-in and any claim it needs from the approved list. Keep approved wording in one shared file so every issue reuses checked language [ORG-0025].

### 3. Place it in the mix

A balanced campaign month runs roughly equal shares of education, proof, brand or community and product highlights, with one or two sale emails [EML-0016]. Many practitioners send three to four campaigns a week to an engaged list without raising unsubscribes [EML-0017]; one client moving from two to four non-discount sends a week saw opens rise [EML-0022]. Start at one issue a week and add only while clicks and unsubscribes hold.

### 4. Write the issue

- **Hook line:** the benefit or the surprise, in plain words. Open with curiosity, proof or a promise, not the category's shared pain [LPG-0045].
- **The one idea:** answer the question fully. Specific numbers, steps and examples from the product facts. One idea only; if a second idea appears, save it for the next issue.
- **Do this today:** steps the reader can follow without buying anything. This is what makes the email worth opening next week.
- **From the shop:** one product that helps with the idea, its price, a link. Honest framing: "If you want the easy version, this is what we make for it." No discount needed.
- **P.S.:** ask one question readers can reply to, which feeds the topic bank.

Write two subjects: one plain ("How to store fresh coffee for two weeks"), one curiosity that the hook pays off. Preview text adds what the subject leaves out. Cut AI tells such as "it's not X, it's Y" [MCR-0016] and read the issue beside the five brand lines.

### 5. Market notes

- **India:** topics can follow festivals, monsoon or summer care, and regional use (recipes, fabrics, climate). If the list has a Hindi or regional segment, a native speaker writes or checks that version. Prices in rupees as on site.
- **US:** the issue sells, so it carries the postal address and unsubscribe and is identifiable as an ad [EML-6341].

### 6. Judge it right

Read each issue on unique clicks and revenue per recipient, not opens [EML-0014]. Track revenue per recipient by cohort over the eight weeks, as some brands earn well from emails with little or no pitch [EML-0057]. Keep the series if clicks hold and unsubscribes stay at or below the store's normal campaign rate; change the format only after four issues.

## Checks before you hand it over

- One micro-topic, answered fully, with a do-this-today section the reader can use without buying.
- Pitch is about a third of the words or less; one product, one link.
- Every claim matches the approved claims list; customer content has permission.
- Word count about 200 to 350 and grade 8 or lower.
- Eight topics in the bank, each with a real source and product tie-in.
- Cadence fits the team's stated capacity.
- Nothing sent without the client's yes.

## Output

1. **One-screen summary:** series name, cadence, this issue's topic, the product tie-in, the metric.
2. **Format sheet:** Section, Length, Rule (the table in step 1, filled for the brand).
3. **This issue in full:**

```
Series: [name]   Issue: [n]   Send: [date, time]   Segment: engaged 90 days
Subject A / B: [...] / [...]   Preview: [...]
Hook: [...]
The one idea: [120 to 200 words]
Do this today: 1. [...] 2. [...] 3. [...]
From the shop: [product, price, one line] -> [URL with UTM]
P.S. [question or extra]
Sources: [ticket tag / review id / claims list line]
```

4. **Topic bank:** Issue, Topic, Source, Product tie-in, Claim needed, Status.
5. **Read sheet:** Issue, Clicks, Revenue per recipient, Unsubscribes, Replies, Next topic ideas.

## Common mistakes

- **Three ideas in one email.** Step 1 allows one micro-topic.
- **A sale wearing a newsletter costume.** Step 1 caps the pitch.
- **Topics from the writer's head.** Step 2 sources each from tickets, reviews or facts.
- **Unapproved health or results lines.** Gate 3 uses the claims list.
- **Judging by opens.** Step 6 uses clicks and revenue per recipient.
- **A weekly promise the team cannot keep.** Gate 2 sets cadence by capacity.
