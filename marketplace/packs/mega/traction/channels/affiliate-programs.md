# Affiliate and creator commission programmes

Channel 14 of 19. Book chapter it updates: Affiliate programs. Checked 2026-11-06; platform facts carry their dates. The book is Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (Portfolio, 2015) [1], summarised in our own words; the authors have not reviewed or endorsed this kit.

## 1. What this channel is now

An affiliate program pays other people a commission when someone they send buys or signs up, tracked by a link or a code. The book covers it as one of the 19 channels [1]. In 2015 Weinberg said they used unique Amazon affiliate links to see which channels sold copies of the book itself [2].

In 2026 the partners are bloggers, review sites, newsletter writers, YouTubers and other creators, agencies and consultants who recommend tools, coupon and cashback sites, and your own customers. As of November 2026:

- **Networks consolidated.** Awin finished moving ShareASale onto its platform in August 2025; ShareASale accounts closed on 6 October 2025. Awin reported over 9,500 advertisers and 250,000 active publishers [8].
- **Store platforms built it in.** Shopify Collabs is Shopify's own app for brands working with creators [9]. Software companies can run programs through the dedicated affiliate tools listed below.
- **Coupon extensions were reined in.** From 10 June 2025 the Chrome Web Store allows an extension to add affiliate links, codes or cookies only when it gives the user a direct, clear benefit, the user takes an action first, and the program is disclosed [6]. The aim is to stop extensions adding codes when the user gains nothing.
- **Disclosure is the brand's problem too.** In the US, the FTC's Endorsement Guides (revised 2023) expect affiliates to disclose commissions clearly and close to the recommendation; "affiliate link" alone is not enough, and brands should train and monitor their partners [3]. In the UK, the ASA treats affiliate-linked content as advertising, finds labels like "aff" likely insufficient, and holds the brand at least jointly responsible [4]. In the EU, the Commission's Influencer Legal Hub (launched October 2023) says affiliate marketing must be disclosed [5].

## 2. When it works and when it doesn't

It works when a group of people already recommends products like yours and earns money doing it, when the margin or recurring revenue can fund a commission that matters to them, and when the product converts once someone arrives.

It struggles when margins are thin, when the product needs a long sales process, when the business cannot give partners content or proof to share, and when most "affiliate sales" come from coupon or cashback sites catching buyers at checkout who were going to buy anyway. Local service businesses often fit referral partnerships better (see the business development and referral channels).

## 3. Cheap tests

Set the maximum commission from the economics first (next section). The client sets the caps and approves the terms and the action list once. At level 3 the specialist sends pitches within them. Each new paid partner, every payout run and anything outside the caps needs a new yes, because each one commits the client's money.

### Test 1: Ten hand-picked partners

- **How:** List 20 people who already write or talk to the buyers. Invite 10 personally with a simple offer, a tracked link and a short kit (product facts, images, a demo account). No public sign-up yet.
- **Cost:** A tool's trial or entry plan, sample products or accounts, and commissions paid only on sales. Weinberg's guide for a channel test was about a month and around a thousand dollars [2].
- **Time:** Four to six weeks.
- **Go:** New customers from partners at a total cost per customer at or below target CPA, with more than one partner producing sales.
- **Stop:** No partner sends a paying customer by the end, or total cost per customer is above break-even.

### Test 2: Customers as affiliates

- **How:** Where email consent allows, invite current customers to join the program with their own link. Happy customers already recommend products; a commission gives them a reason to write it down [7].
- **Cost:** Commissions on sales plus the tool.
- **Time:** Four weeks.
- **Go:** Partner-sourced customers at or below target CPA.
- **Stop:** Almost nobody joins, or those who join send no sales.

### Test 3: Network listing with filters

- **How:** List the program on a network such as Awin, Impact or CJ with clear terms. Approve content, review and newsletter partners first; hold coupon and cashback partners until you can check whether their sales are new.
- **Cost:** Network fees (paid; check each network's pricing page) plus commissions.
- **Time:** Six to eight weeks, as partners need time to publish.
- **Go:** Cost per new customer at or below target, and a new-customer share at or above the line the client set before launch.
- **Stop:** Above break-even, or most orders come from existing customers or coupon sites.

## 4. The numbers to watch

- **Max commission per sale** = target CPA - other costs per customer (tool, network, payouts, samples, team time).
- **Total cost per customer** = (commissions + bonuses + fees + samples + team time) / new customers from partners.
- **Partner activation rate** = partners with at least one sale / partners who joined.
- **EPC** (earnings per click, which partners compare across programs) = commissions paid / clicks sent.
- **New-customer share** = partner orders from new customers / all partner orders.
- **Reversal rate** = refunded or cancelled commissions / commissions earned.
- **Recurring cost (SaaS)** = commission % x monthly revenue per customer x months paid.

## 5. Tools

- Networks (paid): Awin, Impact.com, CJ, Rakuten Advertising.
- SaaS affiliate tools (paid): PartnerStack, Rewardful, FirstPromoter, Tolt.
- Shopify: Shopify Collabs (Shopify's creator app; check plan availability), UpPromote (free plan; paid).
- Amazon Associates (free to join) when the partner is the one earning from Amazon links.
- Google Analytics 4 (free) to compare partner traffic with other sources.
- A shared document with terms, disclosure rules and brand facts for partners.
- Access: the lead's brief sets a level per tool. Level 1 reads the network or affiliate tool; level 2 drafts the program, terms and partner kit inside it, not yet live; level 3 sends pitches within the client's caps, and every action is logged. Approving a new paid partner and every payout run always need a new yes.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Paying coupon or cashback sites** for checkout-page sales you would have made anyway.
- **No disclosure rules for partners,** which leaves the brand exposed in the US, UK and EU [3][4][5].
- **Recruiting hundreds of random affiliates** instead of a few who reach the buyer.
- **A commission too small to notice** next to rival programs [7].
- **Paying on trials, refunds or self-referrals.** Pay on paid, kept customers.
- **No kit for partners,** so they write vague posts that don't convert.
- **Forgetting tax forms and payout admin** until the first payout run.

## 7. By business type

### General

Start with test 1. The best first partners are people whose audience trusts them and who already link to products in the category. Offer a commission they would notice, a kit that makes writing easy, and a fast reply when they ask questions. Write disclosure rules into the terms for each country you sell in, and check posts before paying [3][4][5]. Pay on a set date each month and send each partner a short note of their clicks and sales, so they know the program is alive.

### SaaS

Recurring commissions suit subscription software, because partners earn while the customer stays. ConvertKit paid 30% recurring, matching a larger rival, and most of its customers joined [7]. Agencies, consultants and educators who set the tool up for clients are natural partners. Pay on paid conversions after the refund window, cap self-referrals, and give partners a demo account and setup guides. Decide up front whether commission lasts for the customer's lifetime or a fixed number of months, and put it in the terms.

### Ecommerce

Creators, review sites and gift guides are the usual partners for products. Shopify Collabs and similar apps handle links, codes and payouts [9]. Work out the max commission from contribution margin after shipping and returns. Hold coupon and cashback partners until you can see whether their orders are new customers. Send samples only to partners who agree to disclose. Give each creator a personal discount code as well as a link, so buyers who watch on one device and buy on another are still credited.

### Local

Classic affiliate programs rarely fit a shop, clinic or trade, because buyers come from a small area and decide by trust. A simpler version works better: a referral fee or reciprocal deal with neighbouring businesses that serve the same customers, such as an estate agent and a removal firm. Check professional body rules first; some regulated professions limit referral fees. Track each partner's referrals with a named code or a form field, and pay on booked jobs, not enquiries.

### Coaching

Partners are other coaches, podcast hosts, course creators and community owners whose audience needs the program. A commission on a high-ticket programme can be large, so pick partners for fit, not reach. Joint webinars, where the partner hosts and the coach teaches, give the partner's audience a low-risk first step. Keep income and results claims provable, and make sure partners disclose [3]. Agree before a joint webinar who owns the sign-ups and how follow-up emails are shared.

## 8. Real examples

- **ConvertKit (now Kit).** In an October 2015 post, founder Nathan Barry described paying affiliates a 30% recurring commission, the same rate as AWeber. Most customers joined the program, and referrals began to arrive. His advice was to pay referrers generously [7].
- ***Traction* itself.** Weinberg said in 2015 that they gave each channel its own Amazon affiliate link, so book sales could be traced to the channel that drove them [2]. An affiliate link doubles as a tracking tool.
- **Google's Chrome Web Store.** Google tightened its extension policy on 11 March 2025 so affiliate codes need a clear user benefit and a user action, and began enforcing it on 10 June 2025 [6]. Program owners should still check which partners claim sales at checkout.
