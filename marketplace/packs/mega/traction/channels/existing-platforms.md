# Existing platforms

Channel 15 of 19. Book chapter it updates: Existing platforms. Checked 2026-11-06; platform facts carry their dates. The book is Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (Portfolio, 2015) [1], summarised in our own words; the authors have not reviewed or endorsed this kit.

## 1. What this channel is now

This channel means growing by putting the product where a large platform's users already look: an app store, a marketplace, a map listing, an integration directory or, now, an AI assistant's directory [1]. You rent the platform's audience and follow its rules.

As of November 2026:

- **App stores.** Apple lets developers test up to three versions of an App Store product page (icon, screenshots, preview video) against the original for up to 90 days, after App Review approves them [2]. Apple's ad product was renamed from Apple Search Ads to Apple Ads in 2025, and Apple said it would add more ads to App Store search results from early 2026 [3]. Google Play runs store listing experiments with up to two variants of graphics and, for localised tests, descriptions; experiments stop after six months [4].
- **Marketplaces.** Amazon Sponsored Products are pay-per-click ads in Amazon search results and product pages; Sponsored Brands need Brand Registry, and Brand Stores are free for Professional sellers enrolled in it [5]. Etsy first matches listings to the search words, then ranks them on relevance, listing quality and engagement, recency, customer service record and shipping [6]. eBay and others work on the same idea: search inside the marketplace decides who gets seen.
- **Shopify App Store.** Listings must be factual, apps may not offer incentives for reviews or show testimonials in the listing, and new public apps have been built only on the GraphQL Admin API since 1 April 2025 [7].
- **Google Business Profile.** Local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence (links, review count and rating), and Google says you cannot pay for a better local ranking [8].
- **AI assistant directories.** OpenAI opened app submissions for ChatGPT on 17 December 2025 [9]. Its current guidelines describe one directory shared by ChatGPT and Codex, accept only verified individuals or organisations, allow commerce for physical goods only, ban selling digital goods and subscriptions inside the app, and list Instant Checkout as a beta for select marketplace partners [10]. Anthropic's Connectors Directory takes remote MCP servers through a developer portal; anyone on a paid Claude plan can submit, submissions are scanned automatically and listed as Community by default, and some get a Verified review [11].

## 2. When it works and when it doesn't

It works when the platform already holds your buyers, when it has a search or browse system you can rank in, when you can meet its rules comfortably, and early in a platform's life, before every competitor arrives.

It struggles when platform fees and ad costs leave no margin, when one algorithm or policy change can remove most of your sales, when the platform owns the customer relationship and hides contact details, and when the product only makes sense with a full sales conversation.

## 3. Cheap tests

Set the go and stop lines first. The client sets the caps and approves the action list once. At level 3 the specialist publishes listing changes within them; prices, ads, submissions and anything outside the caps need a new yes.

### Test 1: Store page test

- **How:** For an app, run Apple product page optimisation or a Google Play store listing experiment: one change at a time (icon, first screenshot, or opening line) against the current page [2][4].
- **Cost:** Design time; no ad spend needed.
- **Time:** Until the store reports a result, up to the platform limits.
- **Go:** A variant beats the original at the confidence the store reports; Apple suggests waiting for at least 90% [2].
- **Stop:** A draw or a loss; keep the original and test the next element.

### Test 2: Marketplace pilot

- **How:** List 5 to 10 best products on Amazon or Etsy with complete titles, attributes, photos and shipping. Add a small Sponsored Products budget on Amazon [5][6].
- **Cost:** Listing and selling fees, a capped ad budget, stock in place.
- **Time:** Four to six weeks.
- **Go:** Profit per order after product cost, fees, fulfilment and ads at or above target, and new buyers you would not have reached.
- **Stop:** Negative profit per order at the end, or sales that only moved from the client's own shop.

### Test 3: Google Business Profile sprint

- **How:** Complete every field, pick the right categories, add photos, set exact hours, ask recent customers for honest reviews (no incentives), and reply to every review [8].
- **Cost:** Staff time.
- **Time:** Four to six weeks.
- **Go:** Calls, direction requests and website clicks from the profile rise against the same weeks last year, or the previous period if no history.
- **Stop:** No change after the sprint; fix the website and reviews before trying again.

### Test 4: AI directory listing (software)

- **How:** Build a small app or MCP connector that does one job the product already does well, and submit it to the ChatGPT directory or Claude's Connectors Directory [10][11].
- **Cost:** Engineering days and review time.
- **Time:** Review times vary; judge four to eight weeks after listing.
- **Go:** Activated users from the listing at a cost per user (engineering time / activated users) at or below target CPA.
- **Stop:** Rejected, or connections that never turn into active users.

## 4. The numbers to watch

- **Store conversion rate** = installs or first-time downloads / product page views.
- **Marketplace profit per order** = price - product cost - fees - fulfilment - ad cost per order.
- **ACoS** (Amazon) = ad spend / ad sales. **TACoS** = ad spend / total marketplace sales.
- **Review velocity** = new reviews per week; also average rating.
- **Profile actions** = calls + direction requests + website clicks from the Business Profile.
- **Platform dependency** = revenue from one platform / total revenue.
- **Directory activation rate** = active users / connections or installs.

## 5. Tools

- App stores: App Store Connect (needs a paid Apple Developer Program membership), Google Play Console (paid one-time registration), Apple Ads (paid ads).
- Marketplaces: Amazon Seller Central (paid selling plans), Etsy Shop Manager (listing and sale fees), eBay Seller Hub (selling fees).
- Research: AppTweak or Sensor Tower (paid) for app stores; Helium 10 or Jungle Scout (paid) for Amazon; eRank (free plan; paid) for Etsy.
- Local: Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect and Bing Places (all free).
- Integrations and AI: Shopify Partner Dashboard (free), OpenAI Developer Platform (verified account needed), Claude developer portal (paid Claude plan) [11], the official MCP Registry (free).
- Access: the lead's brief sets a level per platform. Level 1 reads listings and reports; level 2 drafts listing changes where the platform allows drafts; level 3 publishes listing changes and runs store tests within the client's caps, and every action is logged. Prices, ads and submissions always need a new yes.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Treating platform traffic as free and permanent.** Rules and rankings change; track platform dependency.
- **Buying or rewarding reviews,** which Shopify and others forbid [7], and expecting to pay for a better Business Profile rank [8].
- **Ignoring fees** in the unit economics.
- **Pasting website copy into a store listing** instead of writing for the store's search and screenshots.
- **Unverifiable claims** in directory listings, which OpenAI's guidelines reject [10].
- **Selling subscriptions inside a ChatGPT app,** which the guidelines do not allow [10].
- **Building on a platform without permission.** It breaks terms and can be cut off overnight.

## 7. By business type

### General

Find the one platform where buyers already compare options in your category and get the listing right before paying for ads there. Complete every field, use real photos, answer questions fast and collect honest reviews. Track how much revenue depends on that platform, and keep a way to reach customers directly, such as an email list, where the platform allows it. If the platform offers a listing test, run it before paying for its ads.

### SaaS

A good first platform is an integration directory: the Shopify App Store for merchant tools, or the marketplace of a tool your buyers already use. In 2026 that includes AI assistants: a small, well-made ChatGPT app or Claude connector can put the product in front of people mid-task [10][11]. Keep the first version narrow, read-only where possible, and with honest descriptions. Judge on activated accounts, not installs.

### Ecommerce

Marketplaces bring buyers with intent but take fees and own the customer. Pilot a few products, price after fees, and use Amazon Brand Registry once you have a registered brand, since it opens Sponsored Brands and a free Brand Store [5]. On Etsy, titles, tags, attributes, listing quality and the shop's service record affect visibility [6]. Watch whether marketplace sales add to or simply move sales from the client's own shop.

### Local

Google Business Profile comes first for most local firms, because it feeds Google's map and local results [8]. Add Apple Business Connect and Bing Places, and the booking marketplace the trade uses, such as a restaurant reservation or salon booking app. Reply to every review, keep hours and photos current, and measure calls and direction requests each week. Prominence counts too, so links from local groups, clubs and directories help [8].

### Coaching

The platforms are course and cohort marketplaces, expert directories, LinkedIn's services listing and podcast directories. Pick one where buyers search for the outcome you deliver and list a clear, specific offer, not a general biography. Collect reviews from real clients and keep results claims provable. Treat marketplace income as a way to find clients for higher-value direct work, and check each platform's rules on contacting clients off it first.

## 8. Real examples

- **Peak Brain Training.** Apple reports that Peak tested three new app icons for 44 days; the brain icon raised conversion by 8% at over 98% confidence [2].
- **Simply Piano.** In a 12-day test reported by Apple, putting a preview video first did worse than the original page, contrary to the team's hypothesis [2]. Test, don't assume.
- **Airbnb.** Andrew Chen wrote in 2012 that Airbnb built a "Post to Craigslist" feature without any public Craigslist API [12]. It is a famous growth story and a warning: work only through integrations a platform permits.
