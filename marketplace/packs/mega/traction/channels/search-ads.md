# Search ads

Channel 4 of 19. Book chapter it updates: Search engine marketing. Checked 2026-11-06; platform facts carry their dates. The book is Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (Portfolio, 2015) [1], summarised in our own words; the authors have not reviewed or endorsed this kit.

## 1. What this channel is now

The book's search engine marketing chapter is about paying to appear when people search for what you sell [1]. Matching and placements have changed since.

As of November 2026:

- **Google keywords are looser.** Broad match is the default and can match merely related searches; phrase and exact give more control and less reach. Google calls Smart Bidding critical with broad match [3].
- **Shopping ads have no keywords.** They run from Merchant Center product data, in Shopping campaigns or Performance Max [4].
- **Ads inside AI answers exist on Google.** Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns can show above or below AI Overviews and, in English in markets including the US, Canada, Australia and India, inside AI Overviews and AI Mode. You cannot target or opt out of these spots separately, and their results sit inside normal reports [2]. No UK or EU country was on that list when checked.
- **Microsoft Advertising** serves search ads on Bing, Yahoo, DuckDuckGo and partner sites [6]. In April 2026 it announced Offer Highlights inside Copilot conversations (retail, English-speaking markets, via pilots) and said AI Max for Search can serve on Copilot Search and Copilot answers [7].
- **ChatGPT carries ads.** OpenAI began testing ads for logged-in adults on its Free and Go plans in the US in early 2026 and says ads do not change answers [8]. On 5 May 2026 it opened a beta self-serve Ads Manager and added cost-per-click bidding [9]. Availability depends on the country.
- **Other AI assistants** count only when their own pages confirm they sell ads; we found none on the check date.
- **Local Services Ads** (pay per lead, mostly home services) are moving into Google Ads as Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals: select US home and storefront services from August 2026, service-area businesses in late 2026, other countries in 2027 [5].

## 2. When it works and when it doesn't

It works when people already search for the problem or product in words you can name, and when one customer is worth more than the clicks it takes to win them. Emergency trades, products with a clear category name, and software people compare by name ("X software", "alternative to Y") fit well.

It struggles when:

- The category is new and few people search for it.
- Margins are thin and repeat purchase is rare, so click costs eat the profit.
- Sales close offline and only form fills are counted, so the platform buys cheap, poor leads.
- Brand searches or demand that would come anyway make paid search look better than it is (see eBay and Airbnb below) [10][11].

## 3. Cheap tests

Set the go and stop lines before spending, using the formulas in the next section. The client sets the budget cap and approves the action list once. At level 3 the specialist launches within it; anything outside the cap needs a new yes.

### Test 1: Demand check with no ad spend

- **How:** Put 20 to 50 buyer-intent keywords into Google Keyword Planner; note searches, top-of-page bids and the forecast. Look at the live results: who advertises, and whether an AI Overview or Shopping unit sits on top.
- **Cost:** Time only (Keyword Planner sits inside a Google Ads account).
- **Time:** Two to three hours.
- **Go:** The top-of-page bid range sits at or below your max affordable CPC, and forecast clicks could produce the test's conversion target within four weeks.
- **Stop:** Bids sit well above max affordable CPC on most terms, or forecast volume cannot reach the target. Move search to "rarely first".

### Test 2: Exact and phrase sprint on Google

- **How:** One campaign, 10 to 30 high-intent keywords in exact and phrase match, two responsive search ads per ad group, one primary conversion that counts real outcomes, and a landing page that answers the search. Review search terms twice a week and add negatives. AI Overview and AI Mode placements are read inside this test, as they are not sold separately [2].
- **Cost:** A budget cap the client sets. Weinberg's guide for a channel test: about a month and around a thousand dollars at most [12].
- **Time:** Two to four weeks.
- **Go:** Cost per qualified conversion at or below target CPA.
- **Stop:** At the end, cost per conversion is above break-even CPA, or most spend goes to search terms you cannot exclude.

### Test 3: Second engine (Microsoft or ChatGPT)

- **How:** Copy the winning keywords and ads to Microsoft Advertising, or run a small cost-per-click test in ChatGPT Ads Manager where the client's country is offered [9].
- **Cost:** A smaller cap than test 2.
- **Time:** Three to four weeks, as volume may be lower.
- **Go:** Cost per conversion at or below target CPA with enough impressions to judge.
- **Stop:** Above break-even CPA at the end, too few impressions, or no conversion reporting you trust.

### Test 4: Smoke test for a new offer

- **How:** Search ads to a page that offers a waitlist, a quote or a call before the product is finished.
- **Cost:** A small capped budget.
- **Time:** Two weeks.
- **Go:** Page conversion rate at or above average CPC divided by target CPA.
- **Stop:** Page conversion rate below average CPC divided by break-even CPA.

## 4. The numbers to watch

- **Break-even CPA** = gross profit per new customer over the payback period the client accepts.
- **Target CPA** = break-even CPA x the share of that profit the client will spend to win a customer.
- **Max affordable CPC** = target CPA x conversion rate.
- **Conversion rate** = conversions / clicks.
- **CPA** = spend / conversions. For lead businesses, cost per customer = cost per lead / lead-to-customer rate.
- **Break-even ROAS** = 1 / gross margin. ROAS = conversion value / spend.
- **CTR** = clicks / impressions. **Impression share** = impressions / eligible impressions.
- **Brand share** = brand-search conversions / all conversions. Report brand and non-brand apart.

## 5. Tools

- Google Ads with Keyword Planner, and Microsoft Advertising (free to open; you pay for clicks).
- ChatGPT Ads Manager (beta where offered; paid ads).
- Free: Google Merchant Center (product feeds), Google Ads Transparency Center (rivals' ads), Google Analytics 4, Google Tag Manager, Microsoft Clarity (landing page sessions).
- Paid: CallRail (call tracking); Semrush (keyword and rival research, with a limited free account).
- Access: the lead's brief sets a level per account. Level 1 reads the ad accounts; level 2 builds paused campaigns, keywords and ads; level 3 launches and makes the agreed changes within the client's budget cap, and every action is logged.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Broad match with no Smart Bidding and no negatives** on a small budget [3]: the search terms fill with unrelated queries.
- **Counting brand searches as new demand** [10]: blended results look fine while non-brand loses money.
- **Optimising to form fills** when the business needs customers: leads rise, sales stay flat.
- **Judging in days.** A handful of conversions cannot tell a good campaign from a lucky one.
- **Sending every ad to the home page,** or copying a big rival's keyword list.
- **Assuming AI answer spots can be bought on their own,** or that every assistant sells ads [2].
- **Letting auto-applied recommendations run** without the client's approval.

## 7. By business type

### General

Start where intent is plain: the words a buyer types when ready to act, such as the product name plus "price", "near me" or "buy". Run brand campaigns only when a competitor bids on your name. Do the demand check first: one afternoon can show the category is too small or too costly for search, which saves a month. If test 2 works, widen to phrase match before broad, one step at a time.

### SaaS

Bid on problem terms, "X software", "alternative to [rival]" and "[rival] vs [rival]". Count trials only if you also send back which trials activate and pay, or Google will chase sign-ups that never use the product. Set target CPA from CAC payback the founder accepts, not from a trial cost. Check the platform's trademark rules before writing rival names into ads. If analytics show most buyers arrive on desktop, run test 3 on Microsoft early.

### Ecommerce

Start with Shopping ads: they show the product, price and image, and they run from the product feed rather than keywords [4]. Test 10 to 20 products with the best margin and stock depth. Work out break-even ROAS from gross margin before launch and treat anything below it as a loss, whatever the dashboard says. Fix feed titles and images before raising bids. Shopping ads are also eligible to appear in Google's AI answers [2].

### Local

For trades in the US, Local Services Ads charge per valid lead and are moving into Google Ads from August 2026, so expect setup changes [5]. Otherwise run Search with a tight radius, call tracking and ad schedules that match opening hours. Judge on cost per booked job, not cost per click. Keep the Google Business Profile accurate, because after the move name, address and hours sync one way from it [5].

### Coaching

Check searches for the problem as well as the job title: a career change at 40, a promotion, burnout, pricing a consultancy. Bid on the terms with real volume in exact and phrase match, and send clicks to a free workshop or a short call, not a sales page. Track cost per booked call and show rate. Avoid income or health promises that ad policies and the law forbid.

## 8. Real examples

- **eBay.** In field experiments published in 2014, brand keyword ads showed no measurable short-term benefit, while non-brand ads mostly moved new and infrequent buyers. True returns were a fraction of normal estimates [10].
- **Airbnb.** Its 2020 IPO filing says paid performance marketing brought about 23% of traffic in 2019 and about 9% in the first nine months of 2020, after it suspended nearly all such spend; the rest came direct or unpaid [11]. Test what paid search adds before scaling it.
