# Social and display ads

Channel 5 of 19. Book chapter it updates: Social and display ads. Checked 2026-11-06; platform facts carry their dates. The book is Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (Portfolio, 2015) [1], summarised in our own words; the authors have not reviewed or endorsed this kit.

## 1. What this channel is now

The book covers paying to show ads to people while they browse feeds and websites, before they search for you [1]. In a 2015 interview, Weinberg said a test needs only a handful of ads to learn something, not dozens, and that the Twitter and Facebook ad tests they ran for the book did not work [2]. Treat this channel as a test, not a given.

As of November 2026:

- **Meta (Facebook and Instagram)** picks ads in stages: a retrieval system shortlists ads, ranking models predict who will act, and an auction weighs bid, ad quality and estimated action rate. Meta's October 2026 advice: optimise for the real outcome, send back what each conversion is worth, don't rely on platform-reported results alone, supply varied creative, and run lift tests [3].
- **Learning thresholds shape test budgets.** Meta marks an ad set "Learning limited" when it is unlikely to get about 50 optimisation events in the week after its last significant edit [4]. TikTok says auction ad groups pass learning at about 50 conversions in a week [5].
- **LinkedIn** targets by job title, function, seniority, skills, company, company size and industry, plus uploaded lists. An audience must reach at least 300 members [6].
- **Reddit** targets by community, keyword and interest, with ads in feeds or in the conversation area under a post [7].
- **YouTube and display** run through Google's Demand Gen campaigns (YouTube including Shorts, Discover, Gmail, Maps and the Display Network). With target CPA bidding Google recommends a daily budget of at least ten times the target, and Display campaigns began moving into Demand Gen from June 2026 [8].
- **EU rules:** under the Digital Services Act, platforms may not show profiling-based ads to children or target ads using sensitive data such as religion or sexual orientation, and each ad must show who paid and why you see it [9].

## 2. When it works and when it doesn't

It works when you can describe the buyer in a platform's own terms (a job title on LinkedIn, a community on Reddit, a visible problem a short video can show on Meta or TikTok), when the offer is easy to say yes to, and when the budget can reach the learning thresholds above.

It struggles when the budget cannot buy enough results to leave learning, when there is no one to make new creative every few weeks, when the purchase needs long research and a sales call, and when the only proof is the platform's own report. Large experiments at Facebook found that common non-experimental methods often failed to match what randomised tests measured [10].

## 3. Cheap tests

Work out target and break-even cost per result first (next section). The client sets the budget cap and approves the audiences, ads and action list once. At level 3 the specialist launches within them; anything outside the caps needs a new yes.

### Test 1: Creative angle test on Meta or TikTok

- **How:** One campaign, one ad set, broad audience, three or four clearly different concepts (different problem, promise, proof or person), not colour swaps. If purchases are too rare, optimise for an earlier event such as add to cart or lead, and say so in the report.
- **Cost:** A cap the client sets. To have a chance of leaving learning, weekly spend needs roughly 50 x expected cost per optimisation event [4][5]. If that is too much, test a cheaper event. Weinberg's guide was about a month and around a thousand dollars per channel test [2].
- **Time:** Two to four weeks.
- **Go:** Cost per result at or below target, and at least one concept clearly ahead of the rest.
- **Stop:** At the end, cost per result above break-even for every concept.

### Test 2: LinkedIn job-title test (B2B)

- **How:** One audience by job title or function plus company size, above the 300-member minimum [6]. Compare a lead gen form with a landing page.
- **Cost:** A capped budget.
- **Time:** Three to four weeks.
- **Go:** Cost per sales-accepted lead at or below target.
- **Stop:** Cost per sales-accepted lead above break-even, or leads that sales rejects.

### Test 3: Reddit community test

- **How:** One ad group aimed at the communities where the buyer's problem is discussed, using community and keyword targeting [7]. Read the comments: they are free objection research.
- **Cost:** A small cap.
- **Time:** Two to three weeks.
- **Go:** Cost per conversion at or below target.
- **Stop:** Above break-even, or comments show the community rejects the offer.

### Test 4: YouTube video test through Demand Gen

- **How:** A short video and an image version aimed at lookalikes of the customer list. With target CPA, fund at least ten times the target per day as Google advises; otherwise use Maximize conversions with a cap [8].
- **Cost:** A capped budget sized to that rule.
- **Time:** Three to four weeks.
- **Go:** Cost per conversion at or below target.
- **Stop:** Above break-even at the end.

## 4. The numbers to watch

- **Break-even cost per result** = gross profit per customer x result-to-customer rate. **Target** = the share of that the client will spend.
- **Learning budget check (Meta, TikTok)**: weekly spend / cost per optimisation event; about 50 or more gives the system a chance to learn [4][5].
- **CPM** = spend / impressions x 1,000. **Link CTR** = link clicks / impressions.
- **Conversion rate** = conversions / link clicks. **Cost per result** = spend / results.
- **Frequency** = impressions / reach. Rising frequency with rising cost suggests the audience has seen the ads too often.
- **ROAS** = conversion value / spend; **break-even ROAS** = 1 / gross margin.
- **Incremental cost per conversion** = spend / conversions the ads caused, from a lift test [3][10].

## 5. Tools

- Ad managers, free to open, paid ads: Meta Ads Manager, TikTok Ads Manager, LinkedIn Campaign Manager, Reddit Ads, Google Ads (Demand Gen and YouTube).
- Free research: Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center, Google Ads Transparency Center.
- Creative: Canva (free plan; paid), CapCut (free; paid).
- Measurement: Google Analytics 4 (free), Google's Campaign URL Builder for UTM tags (free).
- Access: the lead's brief sets a level per ad account. Level 1 reads results; level 2 builds paused campaigns and ads; level 3 launches, pauses and adjusts within the client's budget cap, and every action is logged.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Too many ad sets for the budget,** so none leaves learning [4].
- **Ten near-copies of one idea.** Give the system different concepts [3].
- **Optimising for clicks** when the business needs sales [3].
- **Trusting view-through and platform totals** without checking the shop or CRM, or a lift test [3][10].
- **Narrow interest stacks on small budgets,** or a LinkedIn audience so tight it barely clears the minimum [6].
- **Ignoring EU limits** on minors and sensitive data [9].
- **Judging after a day,** or "testing" by switching ad sets on and off.
- **Boosting posts** with no goal and calling it a test.

## 7. By business type

### General

Start with the creative angle test on the platform where the buyer already spends time. Write each concept around one problem the customer names in their own words, taken from reviews, sales calls or support emails. Keep one ad set and a broad audience so the creative does the targeting. If no concept beats break-even after the capped spend, move this channel to "test when ready" and spend the time on a channel with clearer intent.

### SaaS

For B2B, LinkedIn targets by job title and company size [6], so run test 2 first, and judge it on leads sales accepts, not form fills. For tools bought by individuals, Reddit communities and Meta can be cheaper. Optimise to trial starts only if activated or paid trials flow back. Price the test against the CAC payback the founder accepts. Read the Reddit comments from test 3 for the objections the sign-up page must answer.

### Ecommerce

Start with Meta or TikTok if the product can be shown working in a few seconds. Use real customer videos and plain demos, and connect the catalogue when you have more than a few products. Set break-even ROAS from gross margin after shipping and returns, and judge new-customer orders apart from repeat ones. Check the shop's orders for the same dates; platform totals alone mislead [10].

### Local

Use a radius around the shop or service area and an offer people act on now: a first visit, a free quote, a seasonal slot. Test a Meta lead form with a call-back promise against a visit to the website. Small audiences see the same ads often, so refresh creative when frequency and cost rise together. Judge on cost per booked job or visit, not on reach.

### Coaching

Run Meta or YouTube ads to a free workshop, guide or short call, then follow up by email. Track cost per booked call and show rate, not just cost per lead: a cheap lead who never books is not cheap. Test a video of the coach teaching one useful idea, so buyers see what working together is like. Keep claims about income, health or results honest and provable; testimonials need the same care.

## 8. Real examples

- **Facebook.** Brett Gordon and colleagues compared 15 large US advertising experiments run at Facebook with the observational methods marketers usually use. The common methods often failed to reproduce the true effect, even with rich data (published in *Marketing Science*, 2019) [10].
- ***Traction* itself.** Weinberg said in 2015 that the Twitter and Facebook ad tests he and Mares ran to sell the book did not work: a reminder that even the authors' own tests fail, which is why they stay small [2].
