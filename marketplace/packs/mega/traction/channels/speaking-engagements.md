# Speaking, podcast guesting and webinars

Channel 18 of 19 in the First traction kit. It updates the "Speaking engagements" chapter of Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (2015). The book's ideas are summarised in our own words; the authors have not reviewed or endorsed this kit. Facts checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed at the end.

## 1. What this channel is now

Speaking puts the founder or expert in front of a room of likely buyers who have chosen to listen. The book's advice: start with small events, build one strong talk around a story the audience cares about, reuse it many times, and let each appearance lead to the next. In 2026 the stage is wider. It includes conference and meetup talks, podcast guest spots, webinars (your own or a partner's) and live streams.

Weinberg described speaking to the best-fit audience as a cheap test, and said podcast appearances work much like speaking: for the book's launch they did much better than guest blog posts [1].

What is true as of November 2026:

- **Conference talks are applied for through calls for papers.** Sessionize, a call-for-papers tool, says 320,000 speakers have used it to apply to conferences and 12,300 events have used it. It is free for free community events [3].
- **Podcast audiences are large.** Edison Research's Infinite Dial 2026 found 80% of Americans aged 12 and over have listened to or watched a podcast [6].
- **About half of webinar registrants show up live.** Livestorm's 2026 benchmark (published 1 September 2026), covering 33,786 webinar sessions held in 2025, found an average show-up rate of 47.7%, down from 48.9% in 2024. The average webinar had 175 registrants and 83 live attendees, ran 68 minutes, and was watched for 26 minutes on average [2]. This is one platform's data.
- **Live video on LinkedIn has a bar to entry.** LinkedIn considers members and Pages for Live access if they have more than 150 followers or connections, an account at least 30 days old and a good-standing record. There is no application [4].
- **Paid appearances need disclosure.** The US FTC's influencer guidance treats payment or any other benefit tied to a mention as a connection to disclose clearly [7]. Apply the same care if you pay for a guest spot or the host earns a commission from you.

## 2. When it works and when it doesn't

**It works when** the founder has a clear point of view and real stories; buyers attend events, listen to niche shows or join webinars to learn; the purchase is considered, not impulsive; and the buyer is really buying the person (coaching, consulting, services, B2B).

**It struggles when** the product sells on price or impulse; the speaker cannot yet hold a room; the audience is peers rather than buyers; or the slot is pay-to-speak in front of people who will not buy.

## 3. Cheap tests

Weinberg suggests a first test should take no more than about a month or about $1,000 [1].

### Test 1: Podcast guest sprint

- **What:** Find 20 niche shows your buyers listen to. Pitch each host with three specific episode ideas tied to their audience. Give each show a unique link or offer.
- **Cost:** Time only.
- **Time:** Four to six weeks, because recording and release take time.
- **Go:** At least one released episode brings sign-ups or booked calls through its link, at a cost per lead (your hours valued) at or below your other channels.
- **Stop:** No bookings after pitching the full list with one follow-up. Rework the episode ideas before pitching again.

### Test 2: One partner webinar

- **What:** Co-host a webinar with a complementary business whose audience matches yours. Teach one useful thing; make one offer at the end.
- **Cost:** Time, and a webinar tool (free plan or the partner's).
- **Time:** Three weeks, including two weeks of promotion.
- **Go:** Attendees take the next step (a trial, a call, a purchase) at a cost per customer at or below your break-even CAC.
- **Stop:** Attendees take no next step, or almost all registrants come from your own list, so the partner added no reach.

### Test 3: One local or community talk

- **What:** Give a short talk at a local meetup, association lunch or industry group where your buyers gather.
- **Cost:** Time and travel.
- **Time:** Two to four weeks to book, one evening to deliver.
- **Go:** At least one qualified conversation that leads to a next step.
- **Stop:** The room was the wrong audience; change the venue, not the talk.

## 4. The numbers to watch

- **Pitch acceptance rate** = bookings ÷ pitches sent.
- **Audience fit** = share of the audience that matches your buyer (ask the organiser or host for their data).
- **Webinar show-up rate** = live attendees ÷ registrants [2].
- **Watch-time ratio** = average watch time ÷ session length. Livestorm's 2025 averages work out to about 38% (26 of 68 minutes) [2], so put the offer and key point early.
- **Next-step rate** = (calls booked + trials started + purchases) ÷ attendees, or ÷ visits to the unique link.
- **Cost per appearance** = preparation hours × hourly rate + travel + any fees.
- **Cost per customer** = total cost ÷ customers from the appearance.
- **Reuse count** = clips, posts and articles made from one talk. One talk used many times lowers the cost of each.

## 5. Tools

- **Calls for papers:** Sessionize (free for free community events; paid for others) [3]; conference websites and organisers' newsletters (free).
- **Finding podcasts:** Listen Notes (free to search on its site); Apple Podcasts and Spotify search (free); PodMatch, which matches guests with hosts (paid plans).
- **Webinars and live streams:** Zoom Webinars (paid); Livestorm (paid plans, free trial); StreamYard (free plan; paid plans). LinkedIn Live runs through approved streaming tools [4].
- **Slides:** Google Slides (free); Canva (free plan; paid plans).
- **Tracking:** unique links read in Google Analytics (free); a "How did you hear about us?" field (free in any form tool).
- **Access:** the lead's brief sets a level per tool. Level 1 reads past talks and analytics; level 2 drafts pitches and submissions, unsent, and unpublished clips; level 3 sends approved pitches and publishes approved clips within the client's caps, and every action is logged. Accepting a stage, travel or a fee always needs a new yes.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Pitching yourself, not the audience's problem.** Hosts and organisers book what their audience needs.
- **A product demo disguised as a talk.** Audiences and organisers notice. Teach; make one offer at the end.
- **No next step.** No unique link, no offer, no way to count results.
- **Saying yes to every stage.** Check audience fit before agreeing.
- **Long webinars that bury the point.** Average watch time is well under the average length [2].
- **Not reusing the talk.** Turn each appearance into clips, posts and a written version.
- **Undisclosed paid appearances** [7].

## 7. By business type

### General

Write one talk built on a real story and one useful lesson, and give it at small events first. Pitch podcasts with episode ideas, not a biography. Give every appearance a unique link and a simple next step. After each talk, ask the organiser for the attendee mix and note which questions came up; those questions become the next talk and the next round of pitches.

### SaaS

Webinars that teach the job your buyers do, with the product used naturally inside the lesson, are the usual entry point. Partner webinars with complementary tools share audiences. Ask a happy customer to co-present; buyers trust a peer more than a vendor. Industry and developer conferences want practical, technical talks, not pitches. Track trials and demos booked from each session, and how many convert to paid.

### Ecommerce

Speaking is rarely the first channel for a store, but founder stories travel well on business, maker and niche hobby podcasts, and craft or industry events suit makers who can show how products are made. For wholesale, talks at retail association events put you in front of buyers. Measure with a podcast-only discount code and the "How did you hear about us?" question at checkout.

### Local

Local stages are easy to reach: chamber of commerce breakfasts, Rotary and other clubs, schools, libraries, community groups and local radio. A useful 20-minute talk (how to choose a contractor, what to ask your vet) builds trust before anyone needs you. Clinics should keep to general education and avoid personal medical advice from the stage. Bring a simple booking offer and count bookings in the weeks after.

### Coaching

This is often the main channel for coaches and consultants, because buyers are choosing a person. Podcast guesting, partner webinars, workshops and conference talks all let people hear how you think. End each appearance with one invitation, usually a booked call, and track show rate and close rate from each source. Keep any claims about client results honest and typical. A recorded talk can keep finding new people for years.

## 8. Real examples

- **The *Traction* book's launch.** Weinberg said podcast appearances worked far better than guest posts on major blogs for selling the book, because listeners could hear the ideas at length [1].
- **Brené Brown at TEDxHouston (June 2010).** The researcher's talk "The power of vulnerability", drawn from her research on human connection, showed 71,448,402 plays on TED's site on 6 November 2026 [5]. One recorded talk can keep reaching new people long after the event.
