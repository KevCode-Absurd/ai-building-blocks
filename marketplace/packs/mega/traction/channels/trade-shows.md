# Trade shows

Channel 16 of 19 in the First traction kit. It updates the "Trade shows" chapter of Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (2015). The book's ideas are summarised in our own words; the authors have not reviewed or endorsed this kit. Facts checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed at the end.

## 1. What this channel is now

A trade show is an industry event where sellers meet buyers, partners and press in one place over a few days. The book's lessons still hold: pick shows your buyers really attend, decide what a good show looks like before you go, and book meetings in advance rather than waiting at a booth. Weinberg suggested niche industry events as a cheap test, for example a search-marketing conference for a search engine [1].

What is true as of November 2026:

- **US shows are forecast to grow.** IAEE's 2026 CEIR Index Report (4 May 2026) forecasts the CEIR Total Index for US business-to-business shows to grow 2.1% in 2026, helped by rising attendance, exhibitor numbers, floor space and forward bookings [2]. The detailed 2025 figures are in the paid report.
- **Big shows make room for startups.** CES 2026 had 148,392 audited attendees, more than 4,100 exhibitors and about 1,200 startups in its Eureka Park area, according to CTA figures reported in April 2026 [3]. Startup zones, shared pavilions and table-top spaces cost less than a full booth.
- **Wholesale has an online version.** Faire runs free online buying events for retailers twice a year. In its July 2026 market, more than 32,000 brands received orders from more than 89,000 retailers, and Faire counted more than 153,000 new brand-retailer relationships [4]. Product brands can test retail demand there before paying for a physical gift or wholesale show.
- **Badge scans are personal data.** US: CAN-SPAM has no exception for business-to-business email; every marketing email needs a working opt-out, honoured within 10 business days, and a postal address [6]. UK: the ICO says the PECR consent rule for marketing email does not apply to corporate subscribers (companies, LLPs), but sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals [7]. EU: a contact list you receive from an organiser is third-party data. You must be able to show it was collected lawfully, that any consent covered passing it to you, tell people at first contact, honour objections, and follow the ePrivacy rules for email [8].

## 2. When it works and when it doesn't

**It works when** buyers in your industry gather at a few known shows; deals or opening orders are big enough to cover the trip; the product is physical or easier to judge in person; retailers, distributors or partners attend to find new suppliers; and you can book meetings before the show.

**It struggles when** you sell a low-priced product straight to consumers online; your buyers do not attend; you cannot follow up within days; or the only plan is to stand at a booth and hope.

## 3. Cheap tests

Weinberg suggests a first test should take no more than about a month or about $1,000 [1]. A full booth rarely fits; these do.

### Test 1: Walk the floor

- **What:** Attend one well-matched show as a visitor. Two weeks before, book meetings with target buyers and partners through the show's app, LinkedIn and email.
- **Cost:** Visitor pass, travel and time.
- **Time:** One show, plus two weeks of preparation and two weeks of follow-up.
- **Go:** At least one meeting turns into a clear next step (a trial, a sample order, a proposal) within the follow-up window.
- **Stop:** The people you met are not your buyers, or none move to a next step.

### Test 2: A shared or startup space

- **What:** Take a table in a startup zone, a regional pavilion, or a partner's booth.
- **Cost:** The space fee, travel, samples and printed material.
- **Time:** One show, plus four weeks of preparation and follow-up.
- **Go:** Expected gross profit from deals opened at the show (deals × your usual close rate × gross profit per deal) covers the full show cost.
- **Stop:** It does not, even counting deals still open.

### Test 3: An online wholesale market (product brands)

- **What:** Join an online wholesale event such as a Faire Market [4] with a clear opening offer for new retailers.
- **Cost:** Time and the discount you offer.
- **Time:** The market itself, plus the reorder window you set.
- **Go:** New retailers place opening orders whose margin covers the discount, and some reorder.
- **Stop:** Few orders, or orders only at a discount that wipes out margin.

## 4. The numbers to watch

- **Full show cost** = space or pass + travel + staff time + samples + printing + shipping.
- **Qualified conversations** = conversations with people who match your buyer profile. Count these, not badge scans.
- **Meeting show rate** = meetings held ÷ meetings booked.
- **Cost per qualified lead** = full show cost ÷ qualified leads.
- **Expected value of the show** = qualified leads × your usual close rate × gross profit per deal.
- **Show return** = (gross profit from closed deals − full show cost) ÷ full show cost, read at the end of your normal sales cycle.
- **Follow-up speed** = hours from conversation to first personal follow-up.
- **Wholesale:** opening order value, and reorder rate = retailers who reorder ÷ retailers who placed an opening order.

## 5. Tools

- **Finding shows:** industry associations' event calendars (free); 10times event directory (free to search); last year's exhibitor and speaker lists on the show's site (free).
- **Booking meetings:** the show's own app, often run on platforms such as Swapcard or Grip (provided by the organiser); LinkedIn (free; paid plans); Calendly (free plan; paid plans).
- **Capturing leads:** the organiser's lead-retrieval app (usually a paid add-on); Google Forms on a tablet (free); HubSpot's free CRM and its mobile app (free tools; paid tiers).
- **Wholesale:** Faire Markets (free to retailers; check Faire's terms for brands) [4].
- **Booth material:** Canva (free plan; paid plans) for signs and one-page sheets.
- **Access:** the lead's brief sets a level per tool. Level 1 reads the CRM and past results; level 2 drafts meeting requests and follow-ups, unsent, and a lead form not yet live; level 3 sends them and registers visitor passes within the client's caps, and every action is logged. Exhibitor bookings and travel always need a new yes.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Choosing by size.** A huge show where your buyers are a tiny share loses to a small show where they are the majority.
- **No meetings booked.** Waiting at a booth for the right buyer to walk past is a gamble; book meetings before you go.
- **Counting scans as leads.** Qualify each conversation: role, need, timing, budget.
- **Slow follow-up.** Leads cool while you travel home. Follow up personally within days.
- **Emailing scanned lists without checking the rules** for each region [6][7][8].
- **A big booth at the first show.** Test with a pass or a shared space first.
- **Staff on phones, no demo.** Show the product working.

## 7. By business type

### General

Ask your best customers which shows they attend and which they skip; their answers beat any directory. Walk the floor before you ever exhibit. Book meetings two weeks ahead, carry one clear offer, and log every conversation the same day with role, need and next step. Judge the show at the end of your sales cycle, not on the flight home.

### SaaS

Vertical software often finds its buyers at industry shows (dental, construction, logistics, hospitality), where competitors may be few and buyers come ready to compare. Horizontal tools rarely win at a booth first. Walk the floor, book demos in advance, and look for integration partners who exhibit. A partner's booth or a speaking slot can cost less than your own stand. Measure demos booked, trials started and pipeline, not visitors.

### Ecommerce

For product brands, trade shows are mostly about wholesale: retail buyers come to find new lines. Bring samples, a line sheet, wholesale prices, minimum order sizes and lead times. Test retail demand online first through a wholesale market such as Faire's [4], then pick a physical gift or industry show where the retailers who ordered say they shop. Consumer fairs and expos can also work for direct sales of products that need to be touched or tasted.

### Local

For local businesses the trade show is the home show, bridal fair, health fair or chamber of commerce expo. Visitors are local and often planning a purchase. Offer a show-only booking or a free assessment, take bookings on the spot, and follow up the same week. Count jobs booked against the full cost of the stand and your time. Sharing a stand with a partner trade splits the cost.

### Coaching

Coaches and consultants rarely do well behind a booth. Attend the conferences where your clients gather, book coffees in advance, and aim for a speaking slot or a small side event, such as a breakfast for a dozen invited attendees. A sponsor table can make sense only if the event is full of your exact client and you have a clear, low-risk first offer. Count booked calls, not business cards.

## 8. Real examples

- **Fitbit at TechCrunch50 (September 2008).** James Park demonstrated a prototype housed in a balsa-wood box on stage, walking steps that synced to a laptop. About 2,000 preorders came in that day, but the founders missed their Christmas 2008 target; production began in December 2009 [5]. An event demo can prove demand fast; be ready to deliver.
- **Faire's July 2026 market.** More than 32,000 brands received orders from more than 89,000 retailers during the online event [4]: proof that wholesale buyers now also shop at online trade shows.
