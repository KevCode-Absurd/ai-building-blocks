# Bullseye brainstorm and ranking

**When to use:** once the traction goal is agreed, to brainstorm one concrete test idea for every one of the 19 channels and sort them into three rings. Use it again every month, or when the focus channel stops paying back.

```text
You are a growth lead running a Bullseye brainstorm. Cover all 19 channels; do not skip the ones you think are unlikely.

The business: {{business}}
Playbook (general, saas, ecommerce, local, coaching): {{variant}}
Traction goal and date: {{traction_goal}}
Who buys, in their own words (paste reviews, call notes or messages): {{customer_words}}
Allowable cost per customer (or per order, booked job or booked call), from my numbers: {{allowable_cost}}
Channels we have tried, and what happened: {{past_channels}}
What competitors do for customers: {{competitor_channels}}
Hours and cash available for tests: {{resources}}

Channels: creators and newsletters; publicity; unconventional PR; search ads; social and display ads; offline ads; SEO plus AI answers; content marketing; email marketing; viral loops and referral; free tools as marketing; partnerships and business development; sales; affiliate and creator commissions; existing platforms; trade shows; offline events; speaking, podcast guesting and webinars; community building.

Do this:
1. For each of the 19 channels, write one specific test idea for THIS business (who, where, what offer), not a generic description.
2. For each idea, give rough guesses, labelled as guesses: cost per customer (cheaper, similar or dearer than the allowable cost), how many customers it could reach before it tires, and days until a test could be read.
3. Flag channels competitors crowd, and channels they ignore.
4. Sort the 19 into three rings: inner (most promising, cheap to test now), middle (could work later), outer (long shots now). One line of reasoning each.
5. From the inner ring, recommend up to three to test at once. Check that together they fit the hours and cash I have, and that they do not target the same people at the same time.
6. Name the channel specialist file for each pick (agents/<channel-id>.md) and what to ask it for.
7. List the facts that would most change the ranking, and how to get them cheaply.

Rules: no invented benchmarks or statistics. Where you would need a number you do not have, say how I can find it. Present the result as a table, then the recommendation in under 150 words.
```
