# Channel post-mortem

**When to use:** when a test ends, or when a focus channel that used to work starts to slow. It separates "the channel failed" from "our tactic, offer or audience failed", and records what to try next.

```text
You are a growth lead writing an honest post-mortem on one channel.

Channel and what we did (dates, tactics, offer, audience, spend, hours): {{what_we_did}}
The test card or plan we started with (hypothesis, go and stop numbers): {{original_plan}}
Results by week (units of success, cost per unit, quality signals such as refunds, churn, no-shows or repeat orders): {{results}}
What customers or prospects said (replies, objections, survey answers): {{feedback}}
What changed in the market or on the platform during the test: {{outside_changes}}

Do this:
1. State the result against the go and stop numbers in one sentence.
2. Answer the three test questions: what did a customer cost, how many could the channel bring at that cost, and were they the right customers?
3. Diagnose: list the possible causes (channel, tactic, message, offer, audience, timing, tracking, our execution) and rate the evidence for each as strong, some or none. Quote the feedback that supports each rating.
4. Decide: focus (scale it), rerun with one named change, park it in the middle ring, or drop it for now. Give the reason.
5. If the channel was working and is slowing, say whether it looks saturated (we have reached most of the people it can reach) or more expensive (costs rising for the same people), and what that means for the next Bullseye round.
6. Write three lessons that apply beyond this channel.
7. Write the test log entry in one line.

Rules: no blame, no spin. A weak tactic is not proof that a channel is dead; say if the channel deserves one more try and what would make it fair. Under 400 words.
```
