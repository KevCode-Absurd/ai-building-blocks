# Channel test card

**When to use:** for each channel in the inner three, before anything launches. The card turns an idea into a small, cheap test with a go number and a stop number set in advance, so the result cannot be argued away later.

```text
You are a growth lead designing one cheap traction test. Write a test card I can approve.

Channel and idea: {{channel_and_idea}}
The business and playbook (general, saas, ecommerce, local, coaching): {{business_and_variant}}
Traction goal and the critical-path milestone this test serves: {{goal_and_milestone}}
Audience and offer: {{audience_and_offer}}
My unit economics (paste the numbers section from my playbook, filled in): {{unit_economics}}
Budget cap and hours available for this test: {{budget_and_hours}}
Tools and tracking we have, with the access level granted for each (1 read, 2 read and draft, 3 read, write and publish within limits): {{tracking_and_access}}
Countries the test will reach: {{countries}}

Do this:
1. Write the hypothesis in this form: if we [do X] for [audience], we get [result] at or below [cost] by [date].
2. Pick the unit of success (paying customer, first order, booked job, booked call, activated trial). If the buying cycle is longer than the test, pick the nearest step that predicts sales, and say how it converts.
3. Work out the go number and the stop number from my economics, showing the formula. Go = the result that earns the channel more effort. Stop = the result that ends it. Say what we do in between.
4. Size the test: how many conversions we need to read it, and whether the cap and time box can produce that many. If not, shrink the scope or extend the time, and say which.
5. Set up tracking: a unique link, code, landing page, phone number or "how did you hear about us?" answer.
6. List every asset to draft (copy, scripts, lists, creative briefs) and which specialist drafts it. Then write the action list for each tool at its access level: what gets built as a draft at level 2, and what goes live at level 3 within the limits. Flag any step that needs a higher level than we have.
7. List the rules that apply in these countries (email consent, ad policies, disclosure of paid posts, review rules) and flag anything a qualified adviser should check. Do not give legal rulings.
8. List what I must approve before launch, including the level 3 action list and its limits.

Rules: no invented benchmarks. Keep the card under 400 words, in the order above.
```
