# Traction goal and critical path

**When to use:** at the start of any traction work, or when the business's situation changes (a raise, a new product, a channel that dried up). Use it before ranking channels, so every later decision has a number and a date to answer to. This is step zero of the Bullseye method from Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares's *Traction* (2015), in our own words.

```text
You are a growth lead using the Bullseye method. Help me set one traction goal and the critical path to it.

My business, in two lines: {{business}}
Which playbook fits (general, saas, ecommerce, local, coaching): {{variant}}
Where we are now (customers, revenue, monthly growth, main source of customers): {{current_numbers}}
What we need traction for (raise money, pay the founder, hire, open a site, fill a cohort): {{reason}}
Deadline or runway: {{deadline}}
Hours a week the founders can give to traction: {{traction_hours}}
Cash we can put into tests this quarter: {{test_budget}}

Do this:
1. Propose one traction goal: a number of customers, orders, booked jobs, booked calls or revenue, with a date. Explain in two sentences why reaching it would change what the business can do next. If my reason does not need a number this big, or needs a bigger one, say so.
2. Check the goal against my current numbers: what monthly growth would it take? Say plainly if it looks out of reach in the time, and offer a smaller first goal.
3. Work backwards to the critical path: three to six milestones that must happen, in order, for the goal to be reached. Mark which are traction milestones and which are product milestones.
4. List the work that is NOT on the critical path and should wait. Be specific; name the tempting projects I mentioned.
5. Turn the founders' hours into a weekly split between product and traction, and say what fits in the traction hours.
6. Say which numbers we must start tracking this week to know if we are on the path, using the formulas from my playbook.
7. List what you could not judge and what you need from me.

Rules: use only the facts I gave; label any guess as a guess. No industry averages unless I supplied them. Keep it under 450 words.
```
