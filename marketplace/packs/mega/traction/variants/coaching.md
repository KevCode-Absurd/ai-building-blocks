# First traction for coaches, consultants and course creators

Variant playbook for the coaching growth lead. It applies the Bullseye method from Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (2015) [1], in our own words, to experts who sell their time, programmes or courses. Read `orchestrator.md` first: it explains the traction goal, the critical path, cheap tests, the channel team and approvals. The authors have not reviewed or endorsed this kit.

## 1. Who this is for

Solo coaches, consultants, trainers and course creators, and small expert-led firms. They sell one-to-one coaching, group programmes, consulting projects, workshops or online courses.

What makes this kind of business different:

- **The expert is the product and the marketer.** Every hour on marketing is an hour not spent with clients, so time cost matters more than cash. Weinberg and Mares's advice to give traction about half your effort [1][2] is hardest to keep here, and most needed.
- **Trust comes before the sale.** Buyers need to have heard the expert think before they book a call. Channels that show expertise (posts, talks, podcasts, referrals) usually beat channels that only show an offer.
- **Most sales happen on a call or in a launch.** The numbers that matter are calls booked, calls held and clients won, or for courses, sign-ups, webinar attendance and sales.
- **Capacity is small.** A coach with ten client slots needs a steady trickle, not a flood. The traction goal should match delivery capacity.

The goal is a full calendar of good-fit discovery calls, and one or two channels that keep it full in a few hours a week.

## 2. Intake questions

1. What do you sell (one-to-one, group, project, workshop, course), at what price, and what does a client get?
2. Who is your best client: their role, situation and the problem they hire you for?
3. How did your last ten clients find you? Name the person, post, talk or search if you can.
4. How many discovery calls do you hold a month, how many show up, and how many become clients?
5. How many new clients a month can you take on without the work suffering?
6. What traction goal do you need, by when, and why (replace a salary, fill a cohort, launch a course)?
7. How many hours a week can you give to marketing, and which do you enjoy: writing, speaking, video or conversation?
8. Do you have an email list or community? How big, and how was consent collected?
9. Which podcasts, newsletters, associations and events do your best clients follow?
10. Which peers serve the same clients with a different service, and would refer or co-host?
11. Do you have a lead magnet, webinar or challenge already? What were its numbers?
12. How much cash can go into tests this quarter?
13. Are there rules on claims in your field (health, money, careers, therapy) that limit what you can promise?
14. For each tool (LinkedIn, email platform, calendar, community), which access level will you grant: 1 read, 2 read and draft, or 3 read, write and publish within limits you set?

## 3. The numbers that matter for coaching and expert businesses

Count your own hours as a cost in every channel. Agree an hourly value with yourself and use it.

- **Discovery calls booked per week**, tagged by the source the person names when booking.
- **Cost per booked call** = (cash spent + paid help + your hours × your hourly value) ÷ calls booked from that channel.
- **Show rate** = calls held ÷ calls booked.
- **Close rate** = clients won ÷ calls held.
- **Cost per client** = cost per booked call ÷ (show rate × close rate).
- **Gross profit per client** = revenue per client − delivery costs (tools, venue, contractors).
- **Allowable cost per client** = gross profit per client × the share you are willing to spend to win one.
- **Allowable cost per booked call** = allowable cost per client × show rate × close rate.
- **Opt-in rate** = sign-ups ÷ visitors to the lead magnet page.
- **List growth** = new subscribers − unsubscribes, per month.
- **Webinar show-up rate** = live attendees ÷ registrants. **Offer conversion** = buyers or booked calls ÷ attendees.
- **Repeat or upgrade rate** = clients who buy again or move to a bigger programme ÷ clients who finished.
- **Referral share** = new clients from referrals ÷ all new clients.
- **Hours per client won** = hours spent on a channel ÷ clients won from it. This often decides between two channels with similar cash costs.

Worked example (made-up numbers, to show the maths only): a programme brings 3,000 in revenue and costs 300 to deliver, so gross profit is 2,700. If you will spend a fifth of that to win a client, allowable cost per client is 540. With a show rate of 0.8 and a close rate of 0.25, allowable cost per booked call = 540 × 0.8 × 0.25 = 108.

## 4. Channel ranking for coaching and expert businesses

A starting order before the brainstorm. A corporate trainer and a course creator selling to hobbyists will re-rank differently.

1. **Content marketing** (`content-marketing`): *usually inner ring*. Regular posts on the one platform clients read show how you think, and cost time, not cash.
2. **Speaking, podcast guesting and webinars** (`speaking-engagements`): *usually inner ring*. A talk or podcast puts you in front of a room that already trusts the host.
3. **Viral loops and referral** (`viral-and-referral`): *usually inner ring*. Past clients are the warmest source; a ready-to-forward note makes it easy for them.
4. **Partnerships and business development** (`business-development`): *usually inner ring*. Peers who serve the same clients can refer or co-host.
5. **Email marketing** (`email-marketing`): *usually inner ring*. A lead magnet or five-day challenge turns attention into a list you own.
6. **Community building** (`community-building`): *usually inner ring*. Helping in one group where clients gather, or running a small free one.
7. **Sales** (`sales`): *test when ready*. Personal outreach to a warm network is the fastest first step for consultants, but cold outreach suits few coaches.
8. **Creators, newsletters and podcasts** (`creators-and-newsletters`): *test when ready*. Newsletter swaps and guest posts with peers' audiences.
9. **SEO plus AI answers** (`seo-aeo-geo`): *test when ready*. Answering clients' real questions builds slowly but lasts.
10. **Offline events** (`offline-events`): *test when ready*. Small in-person workshops close well when clients are local.
11. **Existing platforms** (`existing-platforms`): *test when ready*. Course marketplaces, coaching directories and professional profiles bring some buyers, at lower prices.
12. **Affiliate and creator commissions** (`affiliate-programs`): *test when ready*. Useful for course launches once the course has sold.
13. **Publicity** (`publicity`): *test when ready*. Expert comment for journalists, when you have a clear view or data.
14. **Free tools as marketing** (`engineering-as-marketing`): *test when ready*. A short scorecard or self-assessment can feed calls.
15. **Social and display ads** (`social-and-display-ads`): *rarely first*. Only once a webinar or lead magnet already converts without ads.
16. **Unconventional PR** (`unconventional-pr`): *rarely first*. Thank-you gestures to clients are worth doing; stunts rarely fit.
17. **Search ads** (`search-ads`): *rarely first*. Search demand for coaching is often thin or vague; check it before spending.
18. **Trade shows** (`trade-shows`): *rarely first*. Only for consultants who sell into one industry.
19. **Offline ads** (`offline-ads`): *rarely first*. Hard to target and track for expert services.

## 5. The first 90 days

**Week 1.** Agree the traction goal in calls or clients per month, matched to capacity. Set up the pipeline tracker and add "how did you find me?" to the booking form. Agree the access level for each tool (level 1 until the client says otherwise).

**Week 2.** Brainstorm all 19 channels. Interview five past clients: why they bought, where they spend time online, whom they listen to. List the warm network and 30 podcasts, newsletters or associations.

**Week 3.** Rank and pick the inner three. Write test cards with go and stop numbers from the allowable cost per booked call. Draft post themes, pitch emails and a referral note. Client approves.

**Week 4.** Launch round one. Typical mix: 30 days of posts with one call to action, a pitch to 15 podcasts with one sharp angle, and a referral ask to ten past clients. At level 3 access the lead launches these within the agreed limits and logs each action; at level 2 it builds them for the client to publish.

**Week 5.** Monday numbers. Reply to every comment and message personally; that is where calls start.

**Week 6.** Midpoint review. Announce a free 45-minute workshop to the list and on your main platform.

**Week 7.** Run the workshop and read round one against go and stop numbers.

**Week 8.** Round two: rerun what was unclear with one change, or bring in a middle-ring channel such as a newsletter swap with two peers or a five-day email challenge.

**Week 9.** Tighten the call itself: booking reminders to lift show rate, a call outline, and a follow-up note.

**Week 10.** Focus decision. The winning channel's specialist writes a 90-day plan.

**Week 11.** Build the weekly routine for the winner: batch the work into fixed hours.

**Week 12.** Capacity check. If the calendar is filling, decide with the client on a waitlist, a group offer or a price review.

**Week 13.** Review against the goal. Recount the referral share. Re-rank all 19 channels.

## 6. A cheap test menu for the top 6

### 1. Thirty days of posts (`content-marketing`)
One platform, one topic, one call to action. Each post teaches one thing a client needs.
- **Cost:** time only, about 30 to 45 minutes a day.
- **Time:** 30 days.
- **Go:** booked calls from posts at or below the allowable cost per booked call, counting your time.
- **Stop:** no conversations or calls started after 30 days; change the topic or platform before dropping the channel.

### 2. Pitch 15 podcasts (`speaking-engagements`)
Choose shows your clients hear. Pitch one sharp angle with three talking points and a free resource for listeners.
- **Cost:** time only.
- **Time:** six to ten weeks, because shows record ahead.
- **Go:** bookings from listeners via a dedicated link or code, and hosts who invite you back.
- **Stop:** no bookings after two pitch rounds with different angles.

### 3. Referral ask to ten past clients (`viral-and-referral`)
A personal message and a ready-to-forward note that says whom you help and how to book.
- **Cost:** time only.
- **Time:** three weeks.
- **Go:** booked calls from referrals that fit your best-client profile.
- **Stop:** no introductions; ask two clients what would make referring easier.

### 4. Co-hosted session with a peer (`business-development`)
Run one workshop with a peer who serves the same clients, each inviting your own list.
- **Cost:** time only.
- **Time:** four weeks.
- **Go:** calls booked from the peer's audience at or below the allowable cost.
- **Stop:** few sign-ups from the peer's side.

### 5. Five-day email challenge (`email-marketing`)
Five short daily lessons ending in a call offer, promoted to your list and main platform.
- **Cost:** time and an email tool on a free plan.
- **Time:** three weeks.
- **Go:** booked calls at or below the allowable cost, and list growth.
- **Stop:** sign-ups too few to read. Collect consent properly: CAN-SPAM in the US [6]; in the UK sole traders count as individuals [7]; EU rules add ePrivacy duties [8].

### 6. Help in one community (`community-building`)
Answer real questions in one group where clients gather, under its rules, for four weeks.
- **Cost:** time only.
- **Time:** four weeks.
- **Go:** calls or list sign-ups traced to the group.
- **Stop:** no conversations, or the group's rules forbid any mention of your work.

## 7. Real early-traction stories

- **Pat Flynn.** He started a blog of his own LEED exam notes in mid-2007. Long-tail exam searches brought most of his search traffic, and his posts in a forum were his top source of referral visits. On 2 October 2008 he put a study guide on sale for $19.99 with no launch, sold 309 copies that month, and reported $7,906.55 gross for October [3]. Channels: search, community, then a product built for an existing audience.
- **Justin Welsh.** He began posting daily practical tips on LinkedIn in late 2018 and had over 300,000 followers by 2022. VICE reported that, by his account, his one-person business made nearly $2 million a year. He described about 200,000 daily impressions turning into 4,000 to 5,000 site visitors and about 30 course buyers [4]. Channel: content on one platform.
- **Brené Brown.** Her talk at TEDxHouston in June 2010 showed more than 71 million plays on TED.com on 6 November 2026 [5]. Channel: speaking, where one talk can keep working for years.

## 8. Budget guidance

- Your hours are the budget. Decide how many a week go to marketing and protect them.
- Value your time honestly in every test. A channel with no cash cost but many hours per client can be the dearest one.
- Use free plans of email, booking and design tools for tests; pay for tools only when a channel passes its go number.
- Do not buy ads to a lead magnet or webinar that has not converted organic traffic. Ads amplify a working funnel; they rarely fix a broken one.
- Set any paid test's cap from the allowable cost per booked call, sized to book enough calls to read.
- For course launches, pay affiliates on sales, and only once the course has sold to your own audience.

## 9. Common traps for expert businesses

- **Posting everywhere.** Five platforms at low effort lose to one done well. Pick one and go deep.
- **No call to action.** Good content with no next step builds fans, not clients.
- **Ignoring referrals.** Many experts never ask. A simple, regular ask is often the best channel.
- **Counting followers.** Judge channels on calls booked and clients won.
- **Overpromising.** Results claims and testimonials need to be true and typical; paid or gifted endorsements need disclosing [9], and US rules ban fake or bought testimonials [10].
- **Webinars with no show-up plan.** Reminders matter as much as registrations.
- **Filling past capacity.** A waitlist is fine; poor delivery kills referrals.
- **Copying a famous coach's channel.** Their channel fits their audience; run your own Bullseye [1].
