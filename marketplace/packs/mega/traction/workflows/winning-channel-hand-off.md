# Winning channel hand-off

**Trigger:** a test has hit its go number and the lead believes the channel can carry the traction goal.

**Time:** about one week to agree the plan, then 90 days of focus with weekly reviews.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the win (prompt: channel-post-mortem).** Check the result on all three test questions: cost per unit within the allowable figure, a ceiling large enough to reach the goal, and customers who stay, pay or come back. Check tracking was sound. If the ceiling looks too small, plan a second channel now.
2. **Brief the channel specialist for a 90-day plan (prompt: specialist-brief-and-merge, part A; file agents/<channel-id>.md).** Pass on the access level for each tool, and ask whether the plan needs a higher level or wider limits. Ask for: weekly volume targets, the steps to raise volume in stages, the assets needed each week, the tracking, the numbers to watch with formulas from the channel file (channels/<channel-id>.md), the stop rules if costs rise, and what the client must approve.
3. **Check the plan against the business (variant playbook, sections 3 and 8).** Does it fit the cash, the hours, stock or capacity? Does each stage keep cost per unit inside the allowable figure? Cut or slow any stage that does not.
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves the 90-day plan, the spend at each stage, the rules for pausing, and the access level and level 3 limits for each stage.
4. **Set the routine (workflow: weekly-traction-rhythm).** Fix weekly owners, the day each task happens, and the dashboard or sheet the lead reviews on Mondays.
5. **Keep one middle-ring test alive (prompt: channel-test-card).** Small and cheap, so the next channel is ready when this one tires.
   **Approval point 2:** the client approves the side test's card and cap.
6. **Raise volume in stages (level 3 to change live budgets; otherwise the client does).** Each stage's budget must sit inside the approved limits; ask again before going above them. At each stage, recheck cost per unit and quality, and log every change. Costs often rise as the channel reaches colder audiences; the plan says in advance what rise triggers a pause.
7. **Watch for saturation or rising cost.** When the channel stops paying back, run a post-mortem and start a new Bullseye cycle (workflow: bullseye-cycle) for the next goal.
   **Approval point 3:** the client approves moving effort away from the channel.

## Outputs

- Confirmed result with the three test answers.
- 90-day channel plan from the specialist, checked against the business's numbers.
- Weekly routine with owners.
- One side test card.
- Stage-by-stage review notes and, at the end, a post-mortem and a new goal.

## Needs the client's approval

- The 90-day plan, spend at each stage and pause rules (point 1).
- The side test (point 2).
- Shifting effort away from the channel (point 3).
- Level 2 drafts and anything outside the level 3 limits, through the weekly approval request.
- Live changes during the 90 days are made by the lead or specialist only with level 3 access and within the limits; anything outside them needs a new yes. Every action is logged and reviewed weekly.
