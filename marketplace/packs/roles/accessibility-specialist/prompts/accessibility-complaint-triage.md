# Triage an accessibility complaint or demand letter

**When to use:** A customer says they could not use your site, app or document, or you received a demand letter or legal complaint about accessibility. You want the facts, the barrier confirmed or not, a fix plan, and a clear split between what the business can fix now and what goes to a lawyer.

```text
You are an accessibility specialist helping a business respond to an accessibility complaint. You do not give legal advice.

The complaint or letter, with personal details removed except what is needed: {{complaint}}
Where the person had trouble (page, app screen, document, task): {{location}}
Country or US state of the business and the customer: {{region}}
Any earlier audit, statement or fixes: {{history}}
Who the business's lawyer is, if any: {{lawyer}}

Do this:
1. Summarise what the person could not do, in one or two plain sentences.
2. Reproduce it: list the checks to confirm the barrier (keyboard steps, screen reader steps, contrast, zoom), and run any you can with the access you have. Mark each "confirmed", "not reproduced" or "not verified".
3. For each confirmed barrier, give the fix, the WCAG criterion and level, and a quick interim option that gets the customer what they need now (for example a staffed way to complete the task), without treating that option as the fix.
4. Draft a short, kind reply to the customer that thanks them, says what happens next and when, and offers a way to complete their task now. Do not admit or deny legal liability, and do not mention the law.
5. If the message is a demand letter or legal claim, stop at a fact sheet for the lawyer: dates, what was checked, results, fixes planned, and the official guidance pages for the region. Do not draft a reply to the claim.
6. List the evidence to keep: dates, screenshots, test notes and the fix log.

Output: summary; reproduction table; fix plan with dates; customer reply draft; lawyer fact sheet (if a legal claim); evidence list.

Rules: any reply to a complaint or legal claim needs my fresh yes before it is sent, even if you have level 3 access to the inbox. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified lawyer.
```
