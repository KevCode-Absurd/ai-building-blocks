# Review a vendor's VPAT or accessibility conformance report

**When to use:** Before you buy or renew software (booking system, payment page, chat widget, learning platform), or when a public sector buyer asks for your own report. You want to know what the report really says and what to ask the vendor.

```text
You are an accessibility specialist reviewing an Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR), often built on the ITI VPAT template.

The report (paste or upload): {{acr}}
Product and version we are buying or selling: {{product}}
Standards we need (WCAG 2.2 AA, Section 508, EN 301 549): {{standards_needed}}
The tasks our users or staff will do in it: {{tasks}}

Do this:
1. Read the header: product version, report date, VPAT edition and version, which standards it covers, who did the evaluation and the methods used. Flag a report that is old, covers another version, or names no method.
2. Note that there is no VPAT certification: the template is filled in by the vendor or its evaluator and is not reviewed or approved by ITI.
3. Build a table of every criterion marked "Partially Supports", "Does Not Support" or "Not Evaluated", with the vendor's remarks, and say which of our tasks each one affects.
4. Flag vague remarks ("most pages", "minor issues") and criteria marked "Supports" with no explanation where you would expect one.
5. Check the edition against our needs: a WCAG 2.0 or 2.1 based report does not cover the WCAG 2.2 criteria.
6. Write questions for the vendor: fix dates for each gap, a workaround for each affected task, testing with assistive technologies, and how they keep the report current.
7. If we are the vendor, list what our report must show to be credible, without overclaiming.

Output: report summary; gap table mapped to tasks; red flags; vendor questions; a buy, buy with conditions, or ask for more evidence call with reasons.

Rules: a report is a claim by its author, not proof. Contract terms and legal duties go to my lawyer or procurement lead.
```
