# Accessible documents and Easy Read

## When to use

- A letter or form causes calls because people do not understand it.
- A report, policy or menu is published as a PDF and screen reader users cannot read it.
- A council, housing provider, care service or clinic needs Easy Read versions.
- An audit flagged untagged PDFs, scanned documents or images of text.

## What you need

1. The source file (Word, Google Docs, PowerPoint, InDesign) if it exists, not only the PDF.
2. Who receives the document and what they must know, decide or do after reading it.
3. Any legal, clinical or regulatory wording that must stay as written, and who can approve changes to it.
4. For Easy Read: who the readers are, whether you can test with some of them, and who will draw or choose the pictures.
5. House style and brand fonts and colours.

**Access:** level 1 to read the documents; level 2 to make a corrected copy in the shared drive or document library; level 3 to replace the published file in the folder you name, keeping the old version. Replacing a file with no backup needs a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Decide the format

If the document is meant to be read on screen, recommend an HTML page: it adapts to the reader's settings and works better with screen readers than PDF. Keep a PDF only when it must be printed, signed or kept as a record, and then make the PDF accessible too. PDF/A is for archiving, not accessibility. A scanned image of a page needs OCR and rebuilding, not just a checker.

### 2. List the facts before changing a word

Write a fact list: every date, amount, deadline, right, contact, condition and legal phrase. This list is the test for the rewrite.

### 3. Measure the reading grade

Run a readability formula on the original and note the hardest sentences and words. Formulas give a rough grade only; they do not prove that readers understand.

### 4. Rewrite in plain language

- Put what the reader must do, and by when, in the first two lines.
- Use "you" and "we", everyday words and the active voice.
- Keep sentences to around 25 words or fewer, one idea per paragraph.
- Use headings written as the questions readers ask ("What happens if I can't pay?").
- Explain unavoidable terms the first time, and collect them in a short glossary.
- Keep legal and clinical wording that must stay; explain it in plain words next to it, and put it on the "please check" list.

### 5. Build the accessible source file

- Title and language set in document properties.
- Real heading styles in order, starting with Heading 1, no skipped levels, no bold paragraphs posing as headings.
- Real lists, not typed dashes.
- Alt text on every informative image; decorative images marked as decorative; no text inside images.
- Tables for data only, simple, with a header row; no merged or split cells.
- Link text that makes sense alone.
- Text contrast that passes 4.5:1, nothing shown by colour alone.
- A sans serif font at 12 point or larger, left aligned, single column where possible; no important information in headers or footers.
- Run the built-in accessibility checker (in Word: Review, then Check Accessibility) and fix what it finds.

### 6. Export and check the PDF

- Export as a tagged PDF from the source.
- Check: tags present, headings tagged H1 to H6 in order, figures with alt text, tables with header cells, reading order matching the visual order, document language and title set, fonts embedded.
- Run a PDF checker (for example PAC or veraPDF) and then check the reading order and headings by hand or with a screen reader's heading list. Checkers cannot judge alt text or reading order sense.

### 7. Make the Easy Read version

- One idea per line, short sentences, everyday words, the most important points only.
- A picture next to each point that shows the idea. Write a picture brief for the designer for each one (what the picture should show, without real people's faces unless agreed).
- Key words in bold, numbers as digits, no abbreviations.
- Larger text than standard print, with plenty of space; if readers need large print, that starts at 16 point.
- Develop it with the audience: test a draft with a few readers with learning disabilities, or commission an organisation that does this, and change what they stumble on.

### 8. Compare and report

Tick every item on the fact list against the rewrite and the Easy Read version. Measure the reading grade again. Anything whose meaning might have shifted goes on the "please check" list for the client's legal or clinical lead.

## Checks

- Every fact on the list survives, unchanged in meaning.
- Legal and clinical wording is flagged, never reinterpreted.
- Headings, lists, tables and alt text are real structure, not formatting.
- The PDF's reading order and headings were checked by hand, not only by a checker.
- Easy Read pictures show ideas, and the plan says who tests it with readers.
- Reading grade before and after is reported with the formula used.
- Nothing claims the document is "fully accessible"; the report says what was checked.

## Output format

1. Format recommendation (HTML, accessible PDF, or both) with the reason.
2. Reading grade before and after, and the formula.
3. The plain language rewrite, with question headings.
4. Easy Read version as a two-column table: words, picture brief.
5. Glossary of terms that cannot be avoided.
6. Document checklist results: structure, alt text, tables, reading order, contrast, PDF checker output.
7. Fact list with ticks, and the "please check" list.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a small housing association and its rent arrears letter. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Reading grade:** about grade 14 before, about grade 7 after (Flesch-Kincaid).
>
> **First lines of the rewrite:** "You owe £312.40 in rent. Please pay it, or call us to agree a plan, by 28 November 2026."
>
> | Easy Read words | Picture brief |
> |---|---|
> | You owe us **£312.40** rent. | A rent book with a small amount circled |
> | Please pay by **28 November 2026**. | A calendar with the date ringed |
> | Can't pay it all? **Call us**. We can make a plan. | A person on the phone, calm, with a notepad |
> | You can ask someone to help you. | Two people reading a letter together |
>
> **Please check (legal lead):** the sentence on the right to apply to court for a delay has been explained in plain words next to the original wording, which is unchanged.
