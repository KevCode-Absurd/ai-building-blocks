# Training interview: Accessibility specialist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client names, customers' names or details that could identify a disabled person; anonymise every story.

1. **Which kind of work do clients bring you most: audits, code fixes, alt text, documents, or plain language and Easy Read?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and which skill it reaches for first.

2. **How do you agree the scope of an audit, and what do you say when a client asks you to "just check the home page"?**
   Why it matters: scope decides whether the report means anything; the expert's wording becomes the agent's intake.

3. **Walk me through your manual checks on a typical page, in the order you do them.**
   Why it matters: captures the real test sequence beyond what tools catch.

4. **Which screen reader and browser pairs do you test with, and why those?**
   Why it matters: grounds the agent's test plans in what the expert's clients' users actually use.

5. **Tell me about a barrier no automated tool flagged but that stopped someone completely.**
   Why it matters: teaches the agent where to look hardest by hand.

6. **How do you rate severity, and how do you decide what developers fix first?**
   Why it matters: fix order is what clients act on; the expert's rule replaces generic severity scales.

7. **What does a fix ticket look like when developers never come back with questions?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a proven ticket template.

8. **Which component patterns do teams get wrong most often, and what is your go-to fix for each?**
   Why it matters: builds the agent's library of known failure patterns and fixes.

9. **How do you handle a brand colour that fails contrast when the marketing team will not change it?**
   Why it matters: shows how to keep a fix while respecting brand owners.

10. **A client wants an overlay widget because it is cheaper. What do you say, and what do you offer instead?**
    Why it matters: captures persuasive, honest pushback the agent can reuse.

11. **How do you write alt text for a product photo versus a chart versus a team photo?**
    Why it matters: captures judgement about an image's job in its place.

12. **What is your process for making a PDF accessible, and when do you refuse and recommend HTML?**
    Why it matters: documents are a large share of real barriers; the expert's line guides the agent.

13. **How do you test an Easy Read or plain language version with readers, and what did a test change?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert checks understanding, not just reading grade.

14. **How do you word an accessibility statement so it is honest about known problems?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent statement language that does not overclaim.

15. **Tell me about a time a client asked you to say their site was compliant. How did you handle it?**
    Why it matters: trains the agent's refusal and what it offers instead.

16. **What do you do when a complaint or demand letter arrives? Where does your work stop and the lawyer's start?**
    Why it matters: sets the boundary between evidence and legal advice.

17. **How do you work with disabled testers or users, and how do you pay and credit them?**
    Why it matters: brings real users into the agent's plans, done fairly.

18. **Which rules have you had to apply for clients in the US, UK or EU, and what changed recently?**
    Why it matters: shows which regional rules matter for the expert's clients and what to re-check.

19. **What do you check before you hand over any report, fix or rewrite?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's personal quality bar.

20. **Tell me about the hardest accessibility job you have done, and what you would do differently.**
    Why it matters: a story of judgement under pressure teaches more than a checklist.
