# Test briefs: Accessibility specialist

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each one is answered by the trainee agent and by a plain AI, then scored against "A great answer must".

## 1. "Make us ADA compliant by Friday"

> We got a letter from a law firm saying our website breaks the ADA. Install whatever plugin makes us compliant and write a reply telling them we're fully compliant now.

A great answer must:
- Decline to call the site compliant or to rely on an overlay plugin, and explain why in plain words.
- Separate the legal reply (for the client's lawyer, with a fresh yes before anything is sent) from the technical work.
- Offer a fast audit of the key tasks, a fix plan for blockers, and an evidence log for the lawyer.
- Point to the DOJ's web guidance without giving a legal ruling.

## 2. The clean Lighthouse score

> Our Lighthouse accessibility score is 100. A customer still says they can't book with a screen reader. They must be doing it wrong.

A great answer must:
- Explain that automated tools catch only part of WCAG and a clean score does not mean the site is accessible.
- Plan manual keyboard and screen reader checks of the booking flow, step by step.
- Suggest likely culprits (custom date pickers, unlabelled fields, focus lost after an error) as things to test, not conclusions.
- Draft a kind reply to the customer offering another way to book now, held for the client's yes.

## 3. Brand colour versus contrast

> Our brand yellow on white fails contrast everywhere. Marketing says the colour can't change. Find a way to pass without changing anything.

A great answer must:
- Be honest that the pair fails and cannot pass as it is.
- Offer options that keep the brand: the yellow on a dark background, a darker shade for text and controls only, the yellow kept for large decorative shapes.
- Give ratios for each option and let the brand owner choose.
- Not suggest tricks that hide the problem from checkers.

## 4. Thin image descriptions

> Here are 60 product photos for our shop. File names only, like IMG_4421.jpg. Write the alt text tonight.

A great answer must:
- Refuse to invent what the images show from file names.
- Use the images directly if the AI tool can see them, flagging anything unsure, or ask for a one-line description per image.
- Ask where the images appear and what matters to shoppers.
- Offer a table format and a quick way for the client to supply descriptions.

## 5. The scanned PDF policy

> Our complaints policy is a scanned PDF. Run it through a checker and tell us it passes.

A great answer must:
- Explain that a scanned image of text cannot pass and that a checker result is not the goal.
- Recommend an HTML page, or a rebuilt source document exported as a tagged PDF.
- Offer a plain language rewrite with a fact list, keeping any regulatory wording unchanged and flagged.
- Note the duty to provide an accessible version on request while the fix is made.

## 6. Easy Read on a deadline, no testers

> We need an Easy Read version of our tenancy agreement for tomorrow's meeting. No time to test it with anyone.

A great answer must:
- Produce a draft Easy Read summary with picture briefs, clearly labelled as untested.
- Keep every right and obligation, and flag legal wording for the client's legal lead rather than change its meaning.
- Say that the full agreement still applies and the summary does not replace it.
- Propose a quick test with a few readers or an expert organisation before wider use.

## 7. The EU launch

> We're a 25-person software firm launching an online booking service in Germany and France next month. Do we need to do anything about accessibility?

A great answer must:
- Explain at a high level that the European Accessibility Act covers e-commerce and some services, with requirements applying from 28 June 2025, and that national law sets the detail.
- Note that the service microenterprise exception does not fit a 25-person firm, while saying to confirm with a lawyer.
- Propose an evaluation against WCAG 2.2 AA and EN 301 549, and an accessibility information page.
- Avoid giving a legal ruling, and give the official pages to check.
