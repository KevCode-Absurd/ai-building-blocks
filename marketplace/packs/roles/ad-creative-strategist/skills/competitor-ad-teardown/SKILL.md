# Competitor ad teardown

Rivals' public ads show what a market has already heard. This skill reads them systematically so the client stops repeating crowded claims and finds true things nobody is saying. It never copies an ad and never states a rival's spend or results as fact.

## When to use

- A new product enters a crowded category.
- The client's ads look like everyone else's.
- The client asks "what are competitors running?"
- Before a quarterly creative plan, to refresh angles.

## What you need

- **Rival ads:** pasted text, screenshots or links from public ad libraries, ideally 15 to 40 ads across 3 to 5 rivals, with start dates and placements where the library shows them. The Meta Ad Library holds all active ads on Meta's apps; EU-delivered ads stay for a year after their last impression, so EU searches show more history.
- **Rivals' landing pages** for the main offers, if available.
- **The client's facts:** prices, terms, delivery times, guarantees, reviews, certifications, what they do differently. These decide which gaps are usable.
- **Access level:** level 1 only. Public libraries need no access from the client. If I cannot browse, I say so and work from what is pasted.

## Steps

1. **State what is in and out.** List rivals, number of ads, date range, countries and placements covered. Note gaps (for example "no TikTok ads collected").
2. **Tag every ad** in one row: rival, start date, format (static, carousel, video, creator-style), offer, angle (pain, desire, proof, comparison, risk removal, timing, identity, mechanism), hook style, main claim, proof used, call to action, landing page type.
3. **Mark long runners.** Ads that have run for many weeks are a clue that they pay, not proof; say so.
4. **Count patterns.** For each angle, hook style and claim, count how many rivals use it. Angles used by most rivals are crowded; angles used by none are possible gaps.
5. **Read sophistication.** If every rival makes the same big claim, the market is tired of it; the opening is a mechanism, a demonstration or a sharper buyer, not a bigger claim.
6. **Test each gap against the client's facts.** Keep only gaps the client can prove today. A gap that needs a claim the client cannot prove is not a gap; it is a risk.
7. **Check rival claims for risk, without ruling on them.** If a rival uses before-and-after shots, personal-attribute call-outs or undisclosed creators, do not follow them; platform policy and consumer law apply to the client too.
8. **Write test briefs** for the best three to six gaps: angle, concept, hook, proof ID, format, what the test will teach.
9. **Rank the briefs** by likely payback (size of the buyer group, strength of proof) and effort (what must be filmed or designed), and name the first to test.
10. **Compare on facts only.** Any comparative line names the fact (price, speed, terms) and where it can be checked, and is true on the day the ad runs.

## Checks

- Every row has a source (library link or screenshot name) and a date.
- No ad text, layout or brand element is copied.
- No rival spend, revenue or results stated as fact.
- Every recommended angle traces to a client fact with a proof ID.
- Comparative claims are factual, current and checkable.
- The coverage gaps are stated.

## Output format

1. **Scope:** rivals, ad count, dates, countries, placements, gaps.
2. **Tag table:** one row per ad.
3. **Pattern summary:** crowded angles, open angles, long runners, sophistication read.
4. **Ranked test briefs:** rank, angle, concept, hook, proof, format, effort, what it teaches.
5. **Do-not-follow list:** rival practices that carry policy or legal risk.

## Example of good output (short)

*Hollow Oak Mattresses is a made-up business used only to show the format: an online mattress seller with a 120-night trial (P1) and a UK workshop it can film (P2). The rivals below are also made up.*

**Pattern summary (24 ads, 4 rivals, UK, Meta only)**

| Angle | Rivals using it | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Discount and urgency ("Biggest sale ever") | 4 of 4 | Crowded; heavy discounting is the norm |
| "Best sleep of your life" outcome claim | 3 of 4 | Crowded and hard to prove |
| How it is made (mechanism) | 0 of 4 | Open; client can film the workshop (P2) |
| Risk removal (trial) | 1 of 4, as small print | Open as a lead message; client's trial is P1 |

**Brief 1 (test first):** Mechanism. Concept: a maker shows the layers being built, one layer per shot. Hook: "This is what's inside a mattress you can return after 120 nights." Proof: P1, P2. Teaches: whether showing how it is made beats discount-led ads for cold buyers.

**Do not follow:** Rival C's "Doctor-approved" line names no doctor or study; the client should not make a similar claim without evidence.
