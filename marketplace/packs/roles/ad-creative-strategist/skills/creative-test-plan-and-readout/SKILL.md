# Creative test plan and readout

Most creative "tests" on small accounts answer nothing: several things change at once, the test stops when one version looks good, or edits muddy the result. This skill sets up tests that can teach something and reads them honestly.

## When to use

- Before launching a new creative batch.
- The client asks "which ad won?" or "why did this test fail?"
- A four-week test calendar is needed for a fixed monthly budget.
- A winning ad needs a remix plan that keeps the win and finds more.

## What you need

- **The question.** "Does X beat Y for Z?" in one line.
- **The ads** to compare, and what differs between them.
- **The budget and the timeframe.**
- **The account's history:** cost per result and volume per week for similar ads, from an export. This sets the baseline.
- **For a readout:** an ad-level export by day with spend, impressions, short video views, quartile views or ThruPlays, link clicks, results, cost per result and value, plus a note of any edits made during the test.
- **Access level:** planning and reading need level 1. Building the test as paused drafts needs level 2. Launching, pausing or ending the test needs level 3, within the spend cap the client sets.

## Steps

**Planning**

1. **Write the question** and the one change: hook, concept, format, offer, or first frame. Everything else stays the same.
2. **Pick the decision metric** (usually cost per purchase or qualified lead) and one guardrail (refunds, lead quality, frequency).
3. **Choose the method.** Use the platform's split test where possible, so audiences do not overlap. Meta documents split tests that keep cells separate and a Creative Test type that compares 2 to 7 creatives with one ad per cell, and says to set the confidence level before starting.
4. **Size it.** From the baseline, estimate how many results each version needs to show the difference you care about, and how much spend and time that takes. If the budget cannot reach it, test bigger differences (concepts, not button colours) or label the test as directional before it starts.
5. **Set the stop rule in writing:** end date, spend per version, and what you will call "no clear winner".
6. **Freeze the setup.** No edits, budget changes or new ads in the test while it runs.
7. **Get approval.** Show the plan; at level 2, build the test as paused drafts; at level 3, launch only after the client confirms the action list and cap.

**Reading**

8. **Check fairness first:** equal delivery and budget, no edits mid-run, no overlapping campaigns, the planned dates and spend reached, tracking working.
9. **Read in order:**
   - Attention: early views divided by impressions, for video.
   - Hold: quartile drop-off or ThruPlays.
   - Click: link click-through rate.
   - Result: cost per result and value.
   - Quality: refunds and lead quality from the client's records.
10. **Diagnose.** Strong attention, weak hold: fix the middle. Strong hold, weak clicks: fix the ask or offer. Good clicks, weak results: check the landing page and offer match.
11. **Decide** against the stop rule written in step 5. If neither version clears it, write "no clear winner" and say what that teaches.
12. **Log the learning** and write the next test from it. For a winner, plan remixes that change one thing each: a new hook, a new first frame, a new format.

## Checks

- One change per test, named in the question.
- Decision metric and stop rule written before launch.
- No peeking-based stop: the test ran to its planned end.
- Fairness checked before any result is read.
- "No clear winner" used when true.
- Every launch, pause or edit at level 3 is logged with time and reason.

## Output format

1. **Test card:** question, versions, the one change, metric, guardrail, method, budget per version, dates, stop rule, access level used.
2. **Readout table:** version, spend, impressions, early-view rate, hold, CTR, results, cost per result, quality note.
3. **Verdict:** winner, loser or no clear winner, with the reason in two sentences.
4. **Learning log line** and **next test**.

## Example of good output (short)

*Fernhill Tutoring is a made-up business used only to show the format: online maths tutoring, booking free assessment calls.*

**Test card:** Does a parent-story hook beat a tutor-demo hook for cold parents? Same body, offer and audience. Metric: cost per booked assessment. Guardrail: show-up rate from the booking system. Method: Meta split test, two cells. Stop rule: both versions reach the planned spend over 14 days; if cost per booking differs by less than the margin set from the baseline, call it no clear winner.

**Readout (made-up numbers)**

| Version | Spend | Early-view rate | CTR | Bookings | Cost per booking | Show-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: parent story | 300 | High | 1.1% | 9 | 33.3 | 7 of 9 |
| B: tutor demo | 300 | Lower | 1.4% | 10 | 30.0 | 5 of 10 |

**Verdict:** No clear winner on cost per booking; ten results per version cannot separate them. B gets more clicks, A brings parents who turn up. **Next test:** keep A's hook, add B's demo as the middle beat.
