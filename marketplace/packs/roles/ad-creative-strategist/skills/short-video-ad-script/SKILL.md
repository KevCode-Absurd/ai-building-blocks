# Short video ad script

A short video ad has to stop the scroll, make sense with the sound off, show the brand early, prove one thing and ask for one action. This skill writes scripts that do those five jobs and can be filmed with what the client really has.

## When to use

- A client wants a 15, 30 or 60 second ad for Reels, TikTok, Shorts or YouTube.
- A founder will film themselves and needs a plan for one afternoon.
- An existing ad needs ten new opening hooks to test on the same body.
- A draft script is too long and needs cutting with reasons.

## What you need

- **The offer and the one action** the viewer should take.
- **What can be shown on camera:** the product in use, the place, the founder, real customers who have agreed, before-and-after only if real and allowed.
- **Proof:** reviews with permission, numbers the client can show.
- **Placement and length:** each changes the rules. YouTube skippable ads can be skipped after 5 seconds and bumpers last up to 6 seconds. Reels and TikTok are vertical 9:16 with safe zones. Check the current spec page before filming.
- **Restricted areas:** health, weight loss, cosmetics, finance and similar have extra platform rules.
- **Access level:** writing needs no access. Uploading drafts to an ad account needs level 2; publishing needs level 3.

## Steps

1. **Pick the format** that suits what can be shown: demo, problem and fix, founder to camera, review read aloud, day-in-the-life story, side-by-side comparison against the old way (not a rival by name unless the facts are public and checked).
2. **Write the hook first, several times.** Offer at least five openings in different styles: a question in the buyer's words, a visible demonstration in the first frame, a provable surprising fact, a story moment, a situation call-out. Each must make sense muted, so pair every spoken hook with on-screen text or an image that carries the same idea.
3. **Get the brand in early.** Show the product, the pack, the logo or a distinctive cue in the opening seconds, and again at the end.
4. **Build the body as beats, one idea per shot:** the problem, the turn, the proof, the offer. Keep only beats that move toward the ask.
5. **Write on-screen text for every key line.** Keep it short and inside the placement's safe zone: on Reels, Meta asks for the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% of each side to stay clear; on TikTok, use TikTok's safe-zone files, because the zone shrinks with longer captions.
6. **End with one ask,** said and shown, matching the destination (shop, booking page, form).
7. **Time it.** Read the spoken lines aloud at a natural pace with a timer, including pauses. If it runs long, cut words, not speed. Leave room for music and breaths.
8. **Write the shot list:** shot number, what we see, who is on camera, props, location, framing (close, medium, wide), and whether it is filmable on a phone. Prefer what the client can film themselves.
9. **Check every claim** against the proof list and against what the camera will really show. No filters or edits that change what the product does. No actor or AI person presented as a real customer.
10. **For cut-downs,** keep the hook and the ask, remove the weakest beat first, and note why each cut was made.

## Checks

- The first seconds work with the sound off and with it on.
- The brand or product is visible early.
- One idea per shot; one ask at the end.
- On-screen text sits inside the safe zone for each placement.
- The timed read fits the length with a spare second.
- Every claim is shown or proved; nothing exaggerated by filters or editing.
- Paid creators or partners disclose the relationship in the video itself, not only in the caption.
- Music is original, royalty-free or licensed for ads.

## Output format

1. **Hook options:** number, style, first frame, spoken line, on-screen text.
2. **Script table:** time, what we see, what we hear, on-screen text.
3. **Shot list table:** shot, framing, who, props, location, phone-filmable (yes or no).
4. **Timing note:** read-aloud time and what was cut.
5. **Flags:** claims to confirm, permissions needed, policy risks.

## Example of good output (short)

*Copperleaf Bikes is a made-up business used only to show the format: a repair shop offering a same-day puncture fix. Proof P1: "Fixed while I had a coffee" (review, permission given).*

**Hook options**

| # | Style | First frame | Spoken | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demonstration | Hands pull a thorn from a tyre, close up | "This is why your tyre keeps going flat." | Flat again? |
| 2 | Story moment | Rider pushing a bike in the rain | "Monday, 8:10, two miles from work." | Two miles. One flat. |
| 3 | Question | Mechanic looks up from the stand | "How long can you go without your bike?" | Same-day puncture fix |

**Script (15 seconds, hook 1)**

| Time | We see | We hear | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 s | Thorn pulled from tyre, Copperleaf apron in frame | "This is why your tyre keeps going flat." | Flat again? |
| 3-8 s | Tyre off, patch on, wheel back on, timer in corner | "We find the cause, not just the hole." | We find the cause |
| 8-12 s | Customer collecting bike, coffee cup in hand | "Fixed while I had a coffee." | Real review, used with permission |
| 12-15 s | Shop front with sign | "Same-day puncture fix. Book on our site." | Book a same-day fix |

**Flag:** the on-screen timer must show real time from the shoot, not a sped-up clip presented as real time.
