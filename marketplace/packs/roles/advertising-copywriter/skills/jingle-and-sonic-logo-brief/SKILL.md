# Jingle and sonic logo brief

A jingle or sonic logo only works if people link it to the brand, and that takes the brand name inside the memorable part and years of consistent use. This skill writes lyrics that sing cleanly and briefs a composer can turn into sound, with nothing borrowed from existing songs.

## When to use

- A local business wants a radio jingle people hum.
- A brand needs a two-to-five-second sonic logo for app start, video end cards or ad stings.
- A podcast needs an intro and outro.
- Ads need a music brief so every video and spot sounds like the same brand.

Not for: composing, recording or producing music, or writing parodies or sound-alikes of real songs.

## What you need

- **The name, phone number, web address or line that must fit in,** and how each is said.
- **The one thing to remember.**
- **Where it plays:** radio, podcasts, video, app, in store, on hold. Phone speakers change what works.
- **Length and versions:** for example 60, 30 and 15 seconds, plus a short tag.
- **The feel:** words from the client, and two or three pieces of music they like for mood only (never to copy).
- **Existing brand sounds,** if any, so the new work fits them.
- **Access level:** level 1. Sending briefs to a composer is done by the client, or at level 3 to a contact the client names.

## Steps

1. **Write the hook first:** the line with the brand name in it. Count its syllables and mark the stressed beats (CAPS for stress). Say it aloud on a steady beat; if the name's natural stress fights the rhythm, rewrite the line, never the name's pronunciation.
2. **Build the lyric around the hook:** verse lines that each carry one fact (what, where, why now), then the hook again. Keep rhymes clean and simple; a forced rhyme is worse than none.
3. **Give a syllable count per line** and the rhyme scheme (AABB, ABAB). Matching counts between paired lines makes the tune easier to write.
4. **Write the melody guide in words:** tempo range in BPM, mood, instruments, where the tune rises and falls, where the hook lands, and how the last note resolves.
5. **Make the versions.** Keep the hook identical in every length. The 15-second version is usually just a short lead-in and the hook. The tag is the hook alone.
6. **For a sonic logo:** two to five seconds; the note shape (for example a three-note rise), instruments, mood, tempo, how it ends, and how it should sound on a phone speaker and in a quiet room. Give two or three different directions, not one.
7. **For a brand music brief:** mood, tempo range, instruments to use and avoid, where the brand sound appears, and rules for voiceover (music ducks under speech).
8. **Originality check:** no reuse of a known song's melody, lyric, title hook or famous slogan, and no brief that asks for a sound-alike of a named song or voice. Music already played on the radio cannot go into an ad without the publisher's rights. Bespoke composition is often cheaper than licensing a famous track.
9. **Fact check:** the name, number and address in the lyric match the brief exactly.

## Checks

- The brand name sits inside the hook.
- The hook appears at least twice in versions of 30 seconds or more, and is the same in every version.
- Every line has a syllable count; stresses fall naturally.
- The melody guide is usable by a composer with no further meeting.
- Nothing is copied, parodied or imitated from a known song, slogan or voice.
- Facts match the brief.

## Output format

1. **Hook line** with syllables and stresses.
2. **Lyric sheet** per version: line, syllables, rhyme letter.
3. **Melody guide:** tempo, mood, instruments, shape, where the hook lands.
4. **Sonic logo directions** (if asked): two or three options.
5. **Originality and fact notes.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a pizza takeaway with delivery until midnight, phone 01234 555 210, retro and fun. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Hook:** "[Business], PIZ-za till MID-night" (the name, stressed as the owner says it, then 5 syllables)

| Line (30-second version) | Syllables | Rhyme |
|---|---|---|
| Hot from the oven, the cheese still sings | 9 | A |
| Crust that crackles, the good old things | 8 | A |
| Call oh-one-two-three-four, five-five-five | 9 | B |
| Two-one-oh, and we'll arrive | 7 | B |
| [Business], pizza till midnight | name + 5 | Hook |

**Melody guide:** 120 to 128 BPM, bright 1960s pop feel, upright bass and hand claps, the tune climbs on the business name and lands on a held note on "midnight". The hook repeats as the final line in every version.

**Tag (5 seconds):** "[Business], pizza till midnight" with the same melody.

**Originality note:** the client asked for "something like a famous mambo hit". The brief keeps the era's instruments only; no melody or lyric is borrowed.
