# Print and outdoor copy

Print and outdoor give one glance. This skill writes copy that makes its point in that glance, then rewards anyone who looks longer, and tells people exactly how to respond in a way the business can count.

## When to use

- A flyer, poster, postcard, press ad, menu insert or shelf sign for one offer or event.
- A tri-fold or multi-page brochure for a clinic, school, trade or venue.
- A billboard, bus shelter poster or digital screen.
- A review of a print piece that "nobody reads".

Not for: graphic design or print-ready files (this skill gives words and layout notes), buying media, or mailing lists.

## What you need

- **What is printed:** size (A5, A4, letter, six-sheet, 48-sheet, digital screen), fold or page count, and where it will be seen (in the hand, on a wall, from a car).
- **The one message and the one action:** call, visit, scan, book.
- **Facts:** offer, prices, dates, address, opening hours, terms, proof with permission.
- **Mandatory lines:** legal copy, licence numbers, disclaimers, word for word.
- **Brand:** name, tagline, tone, any colour or font rules.
- **Access level:** writing needs level 1. Placing copy into a Canva draft needs level 2; exporting final files to a printer needs level 3 within the client's limits.

## Steps

1. **Name the viewing moment.** In the hand for minutes, on a noticeboard for seconds, from a moving car for a moment. The shorter the moment, the fewer the words. For roadside boards, OAAA's guidance is one message and seven words or fewer; digital boards typically rotate every six or eight seconds.
2. **Write the single message** in one sentence, then the one action.
3. **Plan the reading order:** what the eye hits first (picture or headline), second (the supporting line or offer), last (contact and terms). On a fold, plan each panel in the order the piece opens: cover, inside flap, inside spread, back.
4. **Write ten headlines,** then pick two. The headline must make the point on its own and must not mislead if read alone: the FTC notes that accurate body text may not cure a false headline. Prefer a specific benefit or fact to an adjective.
5. **Add the body,** short lines, one fact each, with proof next to the claim it supports. Cut anything that does not help the reader act.
6. **Build the offer block:** what they get, the price or saving with any condition beside it, the real deadline, and the action.
7. **Add the response path,** unique to this piece: a phone number, code, short web address or QR code, so replies can be counted per version and per area.
8. **Add the contact block and terms,** readable at the real distance. Qualifiers sit next to the claim they qualify.
9. **Write image notes** for the designer: what the picture shows, where the brand sits, which line goes where. Keep the brand easy to find; tiny logos are a common reason outdoor ads fail.
10. **Check regulated claims:** environmental, health, price and comparison claims need specifics and proof. In the EU, from 27 September 2026, a generic green claim on a poster is banned unless its specifics fit on the same poster or the trader can show recognised excellent environmental performance; if there is no room to explain it, leave it out.
11. **For reviews of existing pieces,** run the glance test (what does a stranger remember after a few seconds?), mark the reading order as it really happens, list clutter and missing facts, then rewrite.

## Checks

- A stranger can say what is offered and by whom after one glance.
- One message, one action.
- The headline is true on its own.
- Every price, date and claim matches the client's facts.
- Qualifiers sit next to their claims and can be read.
- The response path is unique to this piece or version.
- Word count fits the viewing moment; outdoor copy kept to as few words as the idea allows.
- Image notes say where the brand goes.

## Output format

1. **Brief line:** piece, size, viewing moment, message, action.
2. **Copy deck** by panel or zone in reading order: headline (two options), supporting line, body, offer block, response path, contact block, terms.
3. **Image and layout notes.**
4. **Fact check table:** claim, source, status.
5. For reviews: **glance-test result, problems ranked, marked-up rewrite.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Larchmere Garden Centre is a made-up business used only to show the format: a spring plant sale, 20% off perennials, two weekends in April, A5 flyer for letterboxes within two miles.*

**Brief line:** A5 flyer, read at the door for a few seconds. Message: perennials are 20% off for two weekends. Action: visit.

| Zone | Copy |
|---|---|
| Headline (option 1) | 20% off every perennial. Two weekends only. |
| Headline (option 2) | Fill your borders for less this April. |
| Supporting line | 12-13 and 19-20 April, 9am to 5pm |
| Body | Over 300 varieties grown on site. Staff on hand to help you choose. Free loan trolleys to your car. |
| Offer block | 20% off all perennials. Bring this flyer or say "SPRING20" at the till. Ends 5pm, 20 April. |
| Contact | Larchmere Garden Centre, Mill Lane. Free parking. |

**Image note:** one full-bleed photo of a border in flower; logo bottom right, large enough to read at arm's length.

**Fact check:** "Over 300 varieties" needs the client's stock list before print.
