# Radio and podcast script

Audio ads are heard once, often while people drive or work. This skill writes scripts with one idea, an opening the ear catches, an action people can remember without a pen, and a length that fits when read at a natural pace.

## When to use

- A local business buying radio spots in 30 or 60 seconds.
- A streaming audio ad, often 15 or 30 seconds with a companion banner line.
- A podcast sponsorship read by the host.
- A voiceover for a video, timed to its scenes.
- A cut-down of an existing script.

Not for: recording, voicing, mixing or booking airtime.

## What you need

- **Offer, listener and length.** Lengths are sold by the station or network; confirm them before writing.
- **The one thing to remember and the one action.** Prefer a short name, a search term or a place over a long number or web address.
- **Mandatory lines,** word for word: terms, risk warnings, licence numbers, required disclosures. Their length comes out of the creative time, so get them first.
- **Names and pronunciations:** business, people, streets, products.
- **For host reads:** whether the host has really used the product, and what they think of it.
- **Category:** in the UK, radio ads in special categories (gambling, alcohol, finance, health and beauty, slimming, environmental claims and others) must be cleared by Radiocentre before broadcast.
- **Access level:** writing needs level 1. Preparing a clearance submission needs level 2; sending scripts to a station or submitting to clearance needs level 3 within the client's limits.

## Steps

1. **Set the time budget.** Subtract the legal lines' read time from the spot length. What is left is the creative time.
2. **Choose a style:** straight read, slice of life (overheard conversation), testimonial (real, with permission), humour tied to the selling point, music-led, or sound-effect-led. Humour that the listener cannot link to the product is wasted.
3. **Write the opening as the headline:** a sound, a question or a short scene that the ear catches in the first seconds.
4. **Write for the ear.** Short sentences. One idea per spot. Words people say, not words people write. Paint a picture with sound and description.
5. **Say the key message and the action clearly,** and repeat them in longer spots. Do not make actors repeat the brand name in unnatural conversation; branding through a consistent voice, sound or music works better.
6. **Write numbers and addresses for the ear:** a short web address or search term, a phone number only if it is short or memorable, spelled out as it should be said ("nine-three-seven, five-five-five...").
7. **Add cues in brackets:** [MUSIC: light acoustic, under], [SFX: wrench clink], [PAUSE].
8. **Add voice direction** (age, warmth, pace, accent if relevant) and a **pronunciation guide** for every name.
9. **Time it.** Read the full script aloud at a natural pace with a timer, legal lines included. If it runs over, cut words; never ask the voice to rush. Leave a breath at the end.
10. **For host reads,** give talking points plus a full read in the host's own style. The host speaks only from real experience, and the paid relationship is made clear in the audio.
11. **Run the claim check:** every claim from the brief, no false urgency, regulated lines flagged for the client's compliance check. Note any clearance the ad needs.
12. **Write cut-downs** that keep the opening, the message and the action, dropping the weakest beat first.

## Checks

- Fits the length when read aloud at a natural pace, legal lines included.
- One idea; the action is easy to remember without writing it down.
- Mandatory lines appear exactly as supplied.
- No long phone numbers or web addresses read out where a name or search term would do.
- Every name has a pronunciation note.
- Host reads reflect the host's honest experience, with the paid relationship disclosed audibly.
- Any special-category UK radio ad is marked for Radiocentre clearance; US station ads identify the sponsor.

## Output format

Header: client, title, length, version, voice. Then the script table:

| Time | Voice or sound | Line or cue |
|---|---|---|

Then: word count, timed read, voice direction, pronunciation guide, clearance or disclosure notes, and cut-down versions.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a tyre and brake garage on Station Road offering a free brake check and 10% off brake pads until 30 April. 30-second spot, friendly, not shouty. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

| Time | Voice or sound | Line or cue |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 s | [SFX: brakes squeal, car stops] | |
| 3-12 s | Voice (warm, local) | That squeal is your brakes asking for help. At [Business] on Station Road, we'll check them free, while you wait. |
| 12-21 s | Voice | If they need new pads, you'll get ten percent off until the thirtieth of April. No booking needed. |
| 21-28 s | Voice | Free brake check. [Business], Station Road. |
| 28-30 s | [MUSIC: short sting] | |

**Timed read:** 27 seconds at a relaxed pace, plus the sting. **Pronunciation:** [Business] spelled out by sound, as the owner says it. **Note:** "while you wait" must be true at busy times; confirm with the client.
