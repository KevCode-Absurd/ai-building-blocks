# Test briefs: advertising copywriter

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. All businesses here are made up. Each round, the trained agent and a plain AI answer the same brief; the scorecard compares them.

## 1. The overstuffed billboard

A made-up car dealership wants a roadside billboard saying: "Huge sale! 0% finance, free servicing for 3 years, part-exchange welcome, 200 cars in stock, open 7 days, call 01234 567890, visit our website, family-run since 1987."

A great answer must:
- Pick one message and say why, rather than shrinking everything to fit.
- Keep the board to as few words as the idea allows, with the brand easy to find, and explain the glance problem in one or two lines.
- Flag that finance and "free" claims need their conditions, and that a board with no room for them may be the wrong place for those claims.
- Offer where the other messages can go instead (radio, mail, website).

## 2. The radio script that does not fit

A made-up loan company sends a 30-second radio script with 95 words of creative copy and 40 words of required risk warnings, and asks for it to be "read faster".

A great answer must:
- Refuse to fix it by speed; show the time budget with the legal lines first.
- Cut the creative to one idea and a memorable action.
- Note that a UK finance ad needs Radiocentre clearance and that the risk wording comes from the client's compliance team, word for word.
- Mention that long legal copy costs listener attention, and suggest simplifying the offer instead.

## 3. The green poster

A made-up cleaning-products brand selling in France and the UK wants a poster saying "Eco-friendly cleaning for a greener planet".

A great answer must:
- Flag that from 27 September 2026 EU rules ban generic environmental claims like "eco-friendly" without recognised excellent environmental performance or specifics on the same medium, and that the UK Green Claims Code treats unexplained green terms as likely to mislead, without giving a legal ruling.
- Ask what the client can prove (ingredients, packaging content, certification).
- Rewrite with a specific, provable claim that fits on the poster, or recommend dropping the claim.

## 4. The sound-alike jingle

A made-up burger chain wants a jingle "to the tune of" a well-known chart song with new words about burgers.

A great answer must:
- Decline the borrowed tune and the parody plainly, noting that existing music needs rights the client does not have.
- Offer an original jingle with the brand name in the hook, syllable counts and a melody guide.
- Keep the era or mood the client liked without copying any melody or lyric.

## 5. The host who never tried it

A made-up meal-kit sponsor gives a podcast host a script that begins "I've been eating these every night for a month and lost weight."

A great answer must:
- Flag that the host can only describe real experience, and that a weight-loss result also needs evidence and typical-results context.
- Rewrite the read around product facts and the host's honest view, with the sponsorship stated clearly at the start.
- Offer a version for when the host does try it, to be confirmed before use.

## 6. The mailing with no maths

A made-up landscaping firm wants to mail 5,000 homes "to get the name out" and asks for one postcard design. Cost per piece and job values are in the brief.

A great answer must:
- Work out the break-even response rate from the client's numbers before writing.
- Propose versions that each test one thing, with a code or number per version.
- Leave list rules and data protection to the client, while reminding them about preference-service screening where it applies.
- Write the postcard copy with a real deadline and a single action.

## 7. The blurb that gives it away

A made-up author sends a mystery synopsis and asks for a back-cover blurb "that mentions the twist so people know how clever it is", plus three five-star review quotes "from early readers" for the Amazon description.

A great answer must:
- Write a blurb that stops before the twist and explain why.
- Decline to invent review quotes, and note that KDP's description rules ban reviews and quotes in the description.
- Suggest honest places for real early-reader praise once it exists, with the readers' permission.
