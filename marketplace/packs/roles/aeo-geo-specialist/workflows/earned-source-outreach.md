# Earned source outreach

**Cadence:** a monthly cycle, fed by the cited-domains table from the **ai-answer-visibility-audit** skill and the fortnightly checks.

**Goal:** get the brand accurately included on the independent pages AI answers cite for its category, by honest means. Research on AI search finds a strong lean toward earned, third-party sources, and brand mentions across the web correlate with AI visibility, so these pages often matter as much as the brand's own site.

## Steps

1. **List the cited domains** (input: coded answers). Rank third-party domains by how often AI answers cite them for the brand's priority questions. Mark each: review site, publisher, trade body, directory, marketplace, forum or community, encyclopedia, video.

2. **Check each source.** Does it mention the brand? Is it accurate and current? Who runs it? Is there an honest route in:
   - review sites and testers: product or service submission;
   - publishers and trade press: expert comment, original data, a newsworthy story;
   - directories and marketplaces: a complete, accurate listing;
   - trade bodies: membership listing or case studies;
   - communities and forums: only open, disclosed participation by real staff answering real questions, within the community's rules;
   - encyclopedias: only through their conflict-of-interest processes, with independent sources.

3. **Match assets to sources.** For each route, name what the brand can offer: the latest product for testing, data from its own records, a named expert, updated facts, photos. If it has nothing worth covering, plan the asset first (a small original survey or data cut can work).

4. **Draft the outreach** (tool: the **brand-fact-sheet** prompt for accurate facts; level 2 saves the messages as drafts in the client's email). Short, specific messages: who we are, the fact or asset, why their readers would care. No demands for links, no payment for editorial inclusion; any paid placement is labelled as sponsored.

5. **Fix what is wrong first.** Correction requests for outdated or wrong facts on cited pages usually succeed faster than new coverage. At level 3, send the approved requests to the agreed contacts and log each one.

6. **Track** each source: domain, route, contact, date, status, outcome, and whether it starts appearing with the brand in AI answers at the next checks.

7. **Review monthly.** Drop sources AI stops citing, add new ones from the latest checks.

## Outputs

- A ranked source list with routes in.
- Outreach messages and correction requests: drafted for approval, or at level 3 sent and logged.
- A tracking sheet linked to the fortnightly check results.

## What needs the client's approval

At level 3, after the client approves the message wording and contact list once, I send those messages in the client's name, within that list, and log each one with its reply.

Always a new yes:

- Any message to a contact not on the approved list, or with new wording or claims.
- Any product sent for review, and any paid or sponsored placement.
- Any public post or answer by staff in communities.
- Use of customer data or quotes in pitches.

## What this workflow never does

Fake reviews, sock-puppet accounts, undisclosed paid posts, planted forum answers, or text hidden on pages to influence AI. Google says inauthentic mentions do not help and its spam systems also feed its AI features; communities ban these tactics too.
