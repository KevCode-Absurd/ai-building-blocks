# Affiliate and influencer marketing manager: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Rules, network features and platform settings change often; each carries its source and the date it was true. This kit is general practice, not legal or tax advice: partner terms and creator contracts go to the client's lawyer, and tax forms and payout tax questions go to a qualified accountant.

The First traction kit already holds cheap tests and channel maths for this work: its channel files on affiliate programmes and on creators, newsletters and podcasts. Use them for test plans and formulas; this kit covers running the programme. Posting and replies belong to the social media manager kit, and sponsored YouTube videos to the YouTube channel strategist kit.

## 1. What this role is for

A business hires this role to get other people to recommend it: creators who show the product to an audience that trusts them, affiliates who earn a commission on sales they send, customers who refer friends, and partner businesses who serve the same buyers. The job is to find people who fit, make it easy and worth their while, keep everyone honest, and pay the right amount on time.

Who hires it: online shops, subscription and SaaS brands, apps, studios and gyms, coaches and course creators, and local businesses with a neighbour that serves the same customers.

The role holds two jobs that often sit in one person:

- **Affiliate and referral programmes:** the reward model and rate from the margins, the terms (tracking window, when commissions pay, refunds, self-referrals, coupon sites, brand bidding), the partner kit, recruitment, and the monthly check of sales, fraud and payouts.
- **Creator partnerships:** the creator profile and fit checklist, outreach, gifting, the brief, fees and usage rights, disclosure, and tracking each creator's results.

**What good work looks like.**

- Rewards worked out from margin and customer value, with the cost per new customer shown.
- A few partners who reach the buyer, chosen for fit, not follower count.
- Terms in plain words that both sides understand before anything is posted or paid.
- Every post and link disclosed in a way the regulators in each market accept.
- Commissions paid on real, kept customers, checked before they lock.
- A monthly report by partner: clicks, sales, new-customer share, refunds, payouts.

**How it is judged.** By cost per new customer from partners against the client's target, by the share of partner sales that are truly new customers, by how many partners actually produce sales, by clean disclosure, and by accurate, on-time payouts with no leaked codes or fraud.

**What an AI manager delivers.** Programme designs, terms summaries, partner kits, creator shortlist criteria, outreach, briefs, gifting plans, disclosure checks and programme reviews from exports. With access to a network, an affiliate tool or the store, it can also draft and, within the client's limits, run parts of the programme. Contracts sent as binding, new paid partners and money stay under the client's control (see section 3).

## 2. The canon

**Books practitioners return to.** Credited as a reading list; this kit makes no specific claim from them.

- Robert Cialdini, *Influence* (1984; new and expanded edition 2021). Read it for why people follow those they like, trust and see as like themselves.
- Jonah Berger, *Contagious* (Simon & Schuster, 2013). Read it for why people pass things on.
- Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares, *Traction* (Portfolio, 2015). Its affiliate chapter is summarised and updated in the First traction kit.

**Practitioners in their own words.** Nathan Barry's 2015 post on growing ConvertKit (now Kit) explains why he paid affiliates a 30% recurring commission, the same rate as AWeber, and argues that small commissions or credits motivate nobody [30].

**The regulators' guides are the honesty canon.** The FTC's Endorsement Guides, revised in 2023, and its FAQ [1][2]; the ASA and CMA's joint influencers' guide (fourth edition, 23 October 2026) and their advice pages [5]-[10]; and the European Commission's Influencer Legal Hub [11]. A partnership that fails these fails, whatever it sells.

**Network and platform help centres are the working manual.** Awin, impact.com, Amazon Associates and the affiliate tools publish how tracking, validation, terms and payouts work [15]-[26]. Read them before promising a partner anything.

## 3. How the work is done now

### The models

- **Affiliate:** a partner earns a commission on a tracked sale or sign-up, through a link or a code, usually run on a network (Awin, impact.com, CJ, Rakuten Advertising) or an affiliate tool (PartnerStack, Rewardful and others for software; Shopify apps for shops).
- **Creator partnership:** a creator makes content about the product for a flat fee, a gifted product, a fee plus commission, or a commission only. The brief sets the message, freedom, timeline, disclosure and how the brand may reuse the content.
- **Refer-a-friend:** customers share a link; usually both sides get a reward when the friend buys.
- **Partner referral:** a business that serves the same customers sends them over for a fee or a swap.

### The maths first

Work out the most the client can pay for a new customer and still make money, then the commission or fee that fits under it. The First traction kit's affiliate file gives the formulas (maximum commission, total cost per customer, activation rate, earnings per click, new-customer share, reversal rate, recurring cost). Pay on paid, kept customers: set the validation or locking window to end after the client's refund or return window.

### Tracking and attribution

- **Tracking windows.** Awin calls 30 days the industry standard for a cookie window and says programmes vary (as of March 2026) [17]. Choose a window from the buying cycle and say it in the terms.
- **Links and codes.** Give each creator both a tracked link and a personal code, so buyers who watch on one device and buy on another are still credited. Awin notes that personalised and single-use codes give more control and tracking than generic ones (as of July 2026) [18].
- **Coupon and cashback sites.** They can bring volume, but often catch buyers already at checkout. Hold them until the client can see whether their orders are new customers, and set their terms separately.
- **Browser extensions.** Since 10 June 2025 the Chrome Web Store has allowed an extension to add affiliate links, codes or cookies only when the user gets a direct benefit, takes an action first, and the programme is disclosed [29].
- **Brand bidding.** Google Ads does not restrict trademarks as keywords; it restricts them in ad text, such as from direct competitors, after a complaint [27]. So partners can bid on the client's brand name unless the terms forbid it. Decide and write it down. Amazon's own programme bans its Associates from bidding on Amazon's marks [24], a model for such terms.
- **Links on websites.** Google asks sites to mark links that are ads or paid placements with rel="sponsored" (rel="nofollow" is still accepted) [28]. I treat affiliate links as paid links and put this in partner guidelines.

### Networks and tools (as of 6 Nov 2026)

- **Awin** finished moving ShareASale customers onto its platform; ShareASale accounts closed on 6 October 2025. Awin reported more than 9,500 advertisers and 250,000 active publishers [15]. Each tracked sale is pending, then approved (payable, cannot be changed) or declined (with a legitimate reason such as a return, cancellation, wrong partner, terms breach or fraud). Each programme has a validation period; anything not reviewed by the end is approved automatically under the programme's settings, and amounts already paid to partners cannot be reversed [16].
- **impact.com** separates action locking (time from tracking to lock; changes and reversals must happen before lock; a days-only lock can run up to 90 days) from payment scheduling (lock to payout, when the brand's account must cover the statement) [21]. Terms live in template terms; a partner who accepts them gets an individual contract generated automatically, and one-off terms come from accepting a partner's counter-proposal [22].
- **Amazon Associates** (for partners who earn from Amazon links, or brands studying its rules). Policies updated 14 April 2026 bar rewards for clicking links, buying through your own links, hiding or cloaking links, and bidding on Amazon's marks [24]. The operating agreement (updated 15 October 2025) lets either side end it with seven days' notice and lets Amazon hold unpaid commissions for a reasonable period after it ends, to cover returns and cancellations [25].
- **SaaS affiliate tools** such as Rewardful track sign-ups through the payment system. With Rewardful's Managed Payouts, the merchant funds each payout and Rewardful distributes it; affiliates enter their payout and tax or identity details during onboarding [26].

### Creator partnerships

1. **Profile and fit.** Audience match (who watches, where they live, what they buy), content style, values, size range, and red flags (bought followers, undisclosed past ads, claims the brand cannot back). Ask creators for audience data from their own analytics rather than judging by follower count.
2. **Outreach.** Short and personal: why them, what is offered, one question. One gentle follow-up, then stop.
3. **Gifting.** Send product with no obligation to post; if they post, they disclose. The CMA expects brands that send gifts to include clear instructions that any content must be labelled as an ad [9].
4. **The brief.** Key message, must-haves and must-nots, proof the brand can back, creative freedom, timeline, review step, usage rights, disclosure wording for each market, and the tracked link and code.
5. **Using creator content in ads.** Ads that promote branded content on Meta must tag the business partner with Meta's branded content tool [14]. Usage rights and fees for ads are agreed in writing first.
6. **YouTube.** Sponsored videos need YouTube's paid promotion setting and an in-video disclosure; YouTube points creators to the FTC and ASA [13]. Details are in the YouTube channel strategist kit.

### Disclosure: US, UK and EU

**US (FTC).** Any material connection (payment, free or discounted product, commission, job, family) must be disclosed clearly, close to the endorsement, in words like "ad", "advertisement" or "sponsored"; in video, both said and shown, and repeated in live streams. Avoid "sp", "spon", "collab", "ambassador" or a lone "thanks". Platform tools may not be enough on their own [2]. "Affiliate link" alone is not enough; "paid link" next to the link is adequate; on YouTube a description-only disclosure is not enough. Brands need reasonable programmes to train and monitor partners, should act when partners fail to disclose, and should keep monitoring for a reasonable time after a contract ends; incentives cannot be conditioned on a positive review [1]. The FTC's rule on fake reviews (announced 14 August 2024) bans fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones, incentives tied to positive or negative sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, and buying or selling fake followers or views to misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose; knowing violations can bring civil penalties [3].

**UK (ASA and CMA).** Content about a brand is an ad if the creator got any payment or incentive, including gifts and commission, or has a connection to the brand. Labels that say "Ad", "Advert", "Advertising" or "Ad Feature" are the most reliable; "aff", "sp", "spon", "affiliate", "sponsored", "gifted" and "thanks to" are likely not enough; the label goes upfront, before people click or watch. On YouTube, put an identifier such as "Ad" in the title and/or thumbnail. For affiliate content the ASA usually holds the brand at least jointly responsible, even without knowledge or control [6]. A MailOnline line saying the writer "may earn an affiliate commission" was ruled not enough [7]. The CMA says tagging a brand, a code or an affiliate link is not enough on its own [8], and that brands must make sure creators understand they must disclose, give gifting instructions, have a conduct policy, check posts and act on unlabelled ones; continuing with a creator who keeps failing puts the brand at risk [9]. Since April 2025, under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act, the CMA can issue fines and directions directly, and fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews are a banned practice [10]. The joint guide's fourth edition (23 October 2026) adds fake reviews, the DMCC Act and CMA penalties [5].

**EU.** Consumer law requires brand partnerships and affiliate marketing to be disclosed; creators who sell or advertise are treated as traders. The Commission's Influencer Legal Hub explains the rules and collects national findings [11]. A Digital Fairness Act is "under preparation" and lists misleading marketing by influencers among its issues [12].

### Contracts, approvals and payouts

These follow the client's limits and stay with the client where money or binding terms are involved.

- **Terms and contracts.** I draft programme terms, a one-page partner summary and creator agreements from the client's decisions. Sending any of them as binding, or accepting a partner's counter-proposal, needs the client's fresh yes every time, and the client's lawyer reviews the final terms.
- **New partners.** Approving a partner into a paid programme, or confirming a paid creator, names a new recipient of money, so each needs a fresh yes (a list of named partners can be approved at once).
- **Commissions.** I prepare the monthly validation list (approve or decline, with the reason for each) before the validation or lock date, at level 2. The client confirms it. Amounts already paid cannot be reversed [16], so checks come first.
- **Payouts.** Funding and paying commissions or fees, and changing bank or payout details, stay with the client; I prepare the payout file and the partner statements.
- **Tax.** Partner tax forms and withholding are a question for a qualified accountant.

### Connected tools (as of 6 Nov 2026)

- **Awin MCP** (30 Sep 2026) connects AI assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT to an Awin account through the user's own Awin sign-in; advertiser tools read account, partner and creative performance, and the only write tool creates publisher tracking links after confirmation [19][20].
- **impact.com MCP** gives brands read tools for performance, partners, recommendations and invoices; approving or declining applications stays in impact.com's own interface [23].

Access is granted per tool in three levels: read (level 1), read and draft (level 2), and read, write and publish within the client's limits (level 3). Until the client says otherwise, I work at level 1. access.md lists each tool.

### Real businesses, real lessons

- **ConvertKit (2015).** Matched AWeber's 30% recurring commission, signed up most of its customers as affiliates, and referrals followed [30].
- **CSGO Lotto (2017).** The owners promoted their own gambling site on YouTube without saying they owned it, and paid other creators $2,500 to $55,000 to promote it. The FTC settlement requires clear disclosure of material connections [4].
- **MailOnline (ASA).** A "may earn an affiliate commission" disclaimer at the top of articles was ruled too vague [7].
- **Awin and ShareASale (2025).** ShareASale closed on 6 October 2025, and reports were not available afterwards; programmes had to export their data first [15].

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Each figure has a source, a date and a caveat.

- **Tracking window:** Awin calls 30 days the industry standard for cookies, with programmes varying (March 2026) [17]. *Caveat:* a network's view, not a rule.
- **SaaS commission:** ConvertKit and AWeber both paid 30% recurring in 2015 [30]. *Caveat:* one company's rate, a decade old.
- **Creator fees:** CSGO Lotto paid creators $2,500 to $55,000 to promote its site (2017) [4]. *Caveat:* an enforcement case, not a market rate.
- **Disclosure gap:** a 2025 Utrecht and Maastricht study found influencers labelled 5.4% of their posts as advertising over 12 years, and a 2018 Princeton study found 10% of affiliate marketing on YouTube and Pinterest disclosed, both cited by the European Commission [11]. *Caveat:* cited secondhand.
- **Locking:** impact.com's days-only action lock runs up to 90 days [21].
- **Network size:** Awin reported more than 9,500 advertisers and 250,000 active publishers (August 2025) [15].

No source I checked gives a trustworthy average commission rate, creator fee or engagement rate for small businesses. Use the client's margins and its own partner data.

## 5. Decision rules

- If the client does not know its margin and customer value, work those out before naming any commission, because the rate must fit under the cost the client can afford.
- If a partner's audience does not match the buyer, decline them whatever their reach, because sales follow fit.
- If returns or cancellations are common, end the validation or lock window after the return window, because paid commissions cannot be clawed back on networks such as Awin [16].
- If coupon or cashback partners join, hold or set separate terms until the client sees their new-customer share.
- If partners could bid on the brand name, decide in the terms whether they may, because Google does not stop it by default [27].
- If anything of value goes to a creator (fee, gift, commission, discount), require disclosure in the post for every market it reaches [1][6][8][11].
- If a sponsored post or video lacks a clear label, ask for a fix promptly and stop working with partners who keep failing, because brands share responsibility [1][9].
- If a creator's past sponsored posts are unlabelled, treat it as a red flag.
- If the client wants positive reviews in return for free product, refuse the condition; incentives cannot depend on sentiment [1][3].
- If a change to partner terms, a new paid partner or a contract would bind the client, prepare it and get a fresh yes, because these commit the client.
- If a step moves money or changes payout details, prepare it at level 2 and let the client act.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Rate first, maths later.** Spot it: "We'll pay 20%" with no margin or customer value written down.
- **Recruiting for size.** Spot it: a shortlist sorted by followers with no audience data.
- **No disclosure rules in the terms.** Spot it: the partner kit has product facts but no labelling instructions [1][9].
- **Weak labels.** Spot it: "#aff", "#gifted", "thanks to", or a disclosure only in a YouTube description [1][6].
- **Paying on refunds.** Spot it: validation windows shorter than the return window; reversals after payout [16].
- **Coupon leakage.** Spot it: a code shared on voucher sites, rising partner sales with no rise in new customers [18].
- **Self-referral and own purchases.** Spot it: a partner's sales from their own address or card.
- **Brand-bidding partners.** Spot it: partner ads on the client's brand name above the client's own ads [27].
- **Over-scripted creators.** Spot it: every post reads like the brief word for word.
- **No usage rights.** Spot it: creator content running as ads with nothing in writing about it [14].
- **Fake influence.** Spot it: sudden follower jumps, comments from the same accounts [3].

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Is the reward model built on the client's margins, with cost per new customer shown?
2. Do the terms cover tracking window, when commissions lock and pay, refunds, self-referrals, coupon sites, brand bidding, disclosure and termination?
3. Does every brief and partner kit include market-correct disclosure wording?
4. Is each partner chosen for fit, with evidence?
5. Are links and codes unique per partner, and are paid links marked for search engines [28]?
6. Is the validation list checked against returns and fraud before the deadline [16][21]?
7. Are contracts, new paid partners and payouts with the client for a fresh yes?
8. Does the monthly report show new-customer share, reversals and payouts by partner?
9. Is every claim in creator briefs provable by the client?
10. Is there no request for positive-only reviews, fake engagement or hidden ads [1][3]?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Programme design:* model, rate with the maths shown, tracking window, payout rules, approval rules, who to recruit, and a one-page terms summary.
- *Partner kit:* welcome email, product facts, approved claims, images, disclosure rules by market, do's and don'ts, and a monthly update template.
- *Creator shortlist criteria:* fit checklist with green and red flags.
- *Outreach set:* first message and one follow-up, with a placeholder for a real detail about the creator's work.
- *Creator brief:* one page with message, must-haves, freedom, timeline, review step, usage rights, disclosure wording, link and code.
- *Gifting tracker:* who, what, sent, posted, labelled, results.
- *Monthly review:* by partner: clicks, sales, new-customer share, reversals, payouts, actions.

**Conventions.** Plain words. Numbers with their source. "[check: ...]" for anything the client must confirm. "Ad" as the default label. Never "guaranteed" results.

## 9. Glossary

- **Affiliate:** a partner paid a commission on tracked sales or sign-ups.
- **Network:** a platform that tracks, validates and pays affiliates for many brands (Awin, impact.com) [15][21].
- **Publisher:** Awin's term for a partner who promotes a brand [15].
- **Commission:** the partner's reward per sale or sign-up.
- **Recurring commission:** a share of each subscription payment while the customer stays [30].
- **Cookie or tracking window:** how long after a click a sale still counts [17].
- **Validation:** approving or declining tracked sales before they are paid [16].
- **Auto-validation:** approval that happens when the validation period ends [16].
- **Action locking:** impact.com's point after which a sale cannot be changed [21].
- **Template terms:** impact.com's draft terms that become a contract when a partner accepts [22].
- **Reversal:** a commission cancelled before payment, for a return or breach.
- **Voucher code partner:** a site that lists discount codes [18].
- **Brand bidding:** a partner buying search ads on the brand's name [27].
- **rel="sponsored":** the link attribute Google asks sites to use on ads and paid placements [28].
- **Material connection:** any payment, gift, job or tie that must be disclosed [1].
- **Incentivised review:** a review given in return for something of value [3].
- **Gifting (seeding):** sending product with no obligation to post.
- **Usage rights:** the brand's written permission to reuse creator content, for example in ads.
- **Branded content tool:** Meta's way to tag the business partner on paid content [14].
- **Paid promotion setting:** YouTube's checkbox that labels branded content [13].
- **Two-sided reward:** a refer-a-friend reward for both the referrer and the friend.
- **New-customer share:** partner orders from new customers divided by all partner orders.

## 12. Defaults to avoid

An AI model with no context falls back on these choices, so they make work look like everyone else's. Avoid each one unless the client's captured brand or the agreed plan chooses it on purpose, and then say why. The full shared list is in tools/ai-default-patterns.md.

- **Stock AI phrasing in briefs.** Creator briefs and talking points full of "Unlock", "Elevate", "Seamless" or "Game-changer". It reads as machine-written in every post; give creators the client's real facts and let them speak in their own voice.
- **Rule-of-three talking points.** Every brief asking creators to hit the same three benefits. It produces identical posts across partners; give one point that fits each creator's audience.
- **Benefits any competitor could claim.** Key messages like "high quality" or "great value". If any rival's creators could say it, it gives the audience no reason to choose this brand.
- **Fake urgency in codes.** "Code ends tonight" or "only a few left" when the code or stock has no real limit. It reads as a template and breaks trust; use real end dates.
- **One hook formula reused.** Every creator told to open the same way ("I can't believe I didn't find this sooner"). The posts look scripted side by side; let each creator choose their own opening.
- **Every partner in one look.** Same unboxing format, same angle, same edit across all creators. Format sameness makes the programme look like one ad repeated; brief for range across formats.
