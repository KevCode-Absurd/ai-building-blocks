# Creator shortlist and outreach

Small brands win with creators whose audience is the buyer, not with the biggest account they can afford. This skill defines the right creator, gives the client a checklist to score profiles, and writes outreach that is short, specific and easy to answer. It cannot browse social profiles or see private audience data; the client or a creator-search tool supplies profiles, and creators supply their own audience figures.

## When to use

- A first creator or influencer programme.
- A product gifting round.
- Re-starting outreach after poor replies.
- A cross-promotion with a business that serves the same customers.

## What you need

- **Brand and product:** what it is, who buys it, price, what is visual or demonstrable.
- **Goal:** awareness, sales, content the brand can reuse, or reviews.
- **Budget:** gift value, fee budget, or commission offer (from the reward-maths skill if affiliate).
- **Voice:** two or three messages the client has sent that sound like them.
- **Profiles:** links or exports of candidate creators, or the client's own list of who their customers follow.
- **Markets:** where the brand sells, for disclosure rules.

**Access:** level 1 to read a creator tool or exports. Level 2 to save outreach as drafts in the client's mailbox or tool. Level 3 to send approved outreach to the approved list and log gifts within the budget. Confirming a paid creator needs the client's fresh yes each time.

## Steps

1. **Describe the right creator.** Audience (who, where, what they buy), content style (tutorials, reviews, day-in-the-life), values, size range, platforms, and whether they already make content in the category.
2. **Build the fit checklist.** Green flags: audience matches the buyer; comments show real questions and trust; they disclose past sponsored posts clearly; content quality the brand would reuse. Red flags: sudden follower jumps or comments from the same accounts (signs of bought influence, which the FTC's fake reviews rule targets); past paid posts with no label; claims the brand could never back; values that clash. Score each profile.
3. **Ask for real audience data.** Before any paid offer, ask the creator for screenshots from their own analytics: audience location, age range, views per post. Never judge by follower count alone.
4. **Write the outreach.** A few sentences that fit on a phone screen. Why them (one real detail about their work; leave a placeholder the client fills in), what is offered (gift, fee, commission, early access), one easy question. No pressure, no fake urgency.
5. **Plan one follow-up,** about a week later, then stop. Silence is an answer.
6. **Plan the gifting round.** Who gets what, the card in the box, and the tracking sheet (sent, received, posted, labelled, link or code used). The gift carries no obligation to post. The CMA expects brands that send gifts to say clearly that any resulting content must be labelled as an ad, and the FTC treats free product as a material connection. Put the disclosure line on the card.
7. **Pitch a partner business (cross-promotion).** One paragraph on the shared customer, what each side does (a joint offer, a shared post, a referral swap), how results are counted, and the end date. Say who owns sign-ups from any joint event.
8. **Hand off.** Creators who say yes get the brief (skill: creator-brief-and-disclosure). Paid creators and partners are approved by the client before any terms are sent.

## Checks

- The creator profile is about the buyer, not reach.
- The checklist includes disclosure history as a red flag.
- Each message has one real detail, one offer and one question.
- One follow-up only.
- Gifts carry no obligation, and the card says how to disclose if they post.
- Nothing is promised that the client has not approved: fees, rights, dates.

## Output format

1. **Right-creator profile.**
2. **Fit checklist** with scoring.
3. **Shortlist table** (if profiles supplied): name, platform, audience fit, style, disclosure history, score, next step.
4. **Outreach:** first message and follow-up, in the brand's voice.
5. **Gifting plan:** list, box card, tracking sheet.
6. **Partner pitch** (if asked).

## Example of good output (short)

*Ridgeback Climbing Gym is a made-up business used only to show the format: a bouldering gym in a mid-sized city. All details are invented.*

**Right creator:** local climbers and outdoor creators within an hour's drive whose followers are mostly nearby; beginner-friendly content; past gym or gear posts labelled clearly.

**Outreach (first message):**

"Hi [name], your video on [real detail: e.g. your first V4 after the shoulder injury] is the most honest climbing content I've seen from round here. We'd love to give you and a friend a free month at Ridgeback, no strings. If you do post about it, we'd just ask you to label it as an ad. Would you like a pass?"

**Follow-up (one week later):** "Just checking this reached you. The offer stands until the end of the month. No worries if it's not for you."

**Box card line:** "Enjoy this on us. No obligation to post, but if you do, please label it 'Ad' (or use the platform's paid partnership label), because we gave you this for free."

**Partner pitch to a nearby café:** "Our members come to you after a session; your customers ask us about lessons. Joint offer for March: show a Ridgeback day pass at the café for 10% off, show a café receipt at the gym for a free shoe hire. We count codes on both tills and share the numbers on 1 April."

*Why this works:* the message is short and specific, the offer is clear, disclosure is built in from the first contact, and the partner pitch says how results are counted.
