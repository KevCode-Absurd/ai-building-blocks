# Reward maths and programme terms

A programme pays for itself only if the reward fits under what a new customer is worth, and only if the rules stop paying for sales the business would have made anyway. This skill does the maths first, then writes the rules in words partners understand. It is not legal or tax advice: the client's lawyer finalises the terms, and a qualified accountant answers questions about tax forms and withholding on payouts.

## When to use

- Starting an affiliate programme on a network or affiliate tool.
- Starting a customer refer-a-friend scheme.
- An existing programme whose partner sales cost more than the client can afford.
- Before recruiting partners, so the offer is settled.

## What you need

- **Economics:** average order or contract value, gross margin (after shipping and payment fees for shops), repeat purchase rate or average months a subscriber stays, refund or return rate and window.
- **Target:** the most the client wants to pay for a new customer (target CPA), or enough data to set one.
- **Other costs per partner customer:** network or tool fees, samples, discounts given through codes, team time.
- **Platform:** which network or tool will run it, and its validation or locking rules.
- **Markets:** where customers and partners are.

**Access:** level 1 to read exports. Level 2 to draft terms and commission settings in the tool. Publishing terms, changing rates or approving partners needs the client's fresh yes even at level 3.

## Steps

1. **Work out what a new customer is worth.** For a one-off sale, the gross margin on the first order (after the code discount, if any). For repeat or subscription businesses, margin over a payback window the client chooses. Label every assumption.
2. **Set the ceiling.** Maximum reward per new customer = target CPA minus other costs per partner customer. If the ceiling is near zero, say the programme will not pay and suggest a partner-referral swap instead.
3. **Compare models,** each shown as cost per new customer:
   - Percentage of sale: simple; cost rises with basket size.
   - Flat fee per new customer: predictable; good when baskets vary.
   - Tiered: higher rate for partners who pass a volume; rewards the best partners.
   - Recurring (subscriptions): a share of each payment for a set number of months or for the customer's life; say which.
   - Two-sided (refer-a-friend): a reward for both the referrer and the friend; count both in the cost.
4. **Pick the tracking window** from the buying cycle. Awin calls 30 days the industry standard for cookies and says programmes vary (as of March 2026). State it in the terms.
5. **Set when commissions become payable.** End the validation or lock period after the return window, so refunds are declined before they are paid. On Awin, amounts already paid cannot be reversed; on impact.com, changes must happen before the action locks.
6. **Write the rules:**
   - Who can join and how partners are approved (each new paid partner approved by the client).
   - Paid on: paid, kept, new customers only; no self-referrals or own purchases.
   - Coupon and cashback sites: allowed, held, or on separate terms.
   - Brand bidding: whether partners may buy search ads on the brand name (Google does not restrict trademarks as keywords, so the terms must decide).
   - Disclosure: a clear label such as "Ad" upfront; in the US, "affiliate link" alone is not enough; in the UK, "aff", "sp" and "spon" are likely not enough; in the EU, affiliate marketing must be disclosed. Websites mark paid links with rel="sponsored".
   - Claims: only the approved product facts.
   - Payout timing and method, and the minimum payout if any.
   - Changes and ending: notice before rates change; what happens to pending commissions.
7. **For refer-a-friend,** pick the moments to ask (after a delivery, a good review, a renewal), the reward for each side, limits per customer, and fraud rules (same address or card, rapid sign-ups).
8. **Write the one-page summary** in plain words for partners, and a list of decisions for the client's lawyer.

## Checks

- Every number shows its source or is labelled as an assumption.
- The reward fits under the ceiling for each model shown.
- Commissions become payable only after the return window.
- Self-referrals, coupon sites and brand bidding are decided, not left open.
- Disclosure rules cover each market the client sells in.
- The summary says the terms need the client's lawyer and that tax questions go to a qualified accountant.

## Output format

1. **Economics table:** inputs, assumptions, value of a new customer, ceiling.
2. **Model comparison:** each model's cost per new customer at expected volumes.
3. **Recommendation** and why.
4. **Rules** in full.
5. **One-page partner terms summary.**
6. **Decisions for the lawyer** and **questions for the accountant.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: an online shop selling loose-leaf tea. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Average first order | $48 |
| Gross margin after shipping and fees | 55% (client figure) |
| Margin on first order | $26.40 |
| Customers buying again within 6 months | 40% (client figure); repeat margin assumed $20 |
| Value over 6 months | $26.40 + 0.4 x $20 = $34.40 |
| Target: pay at most half the 6-month value (client's choice) | $17.20 |
| Other costs per partner customer (tool, samples) | $4.00 (assumption) |
| **Ceiling per new customer** | **$13.20** |

| Model | Cost per new customer | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 20% of first order | $9.60 | Fits; rises with bigger baskets |
| $12 flat per new customer | $12.00 | Fits; predictable |
| 10% off code plus 15% commission on the discounted order | $4.80 + $6.48 = $11.28 | Fits, but the discount also goes to buyers who would have paid full price |

**Recommendation:** $12 flat per new customer, paid after the 30-day return window, tracking window 30 days, no coupon sites in the first three months, no bidding on "[Business]".

*Why this works:* the reward comes from the shop's own margin, every assumption is labelled, and the rules stop paying for refunds and existing customers.
