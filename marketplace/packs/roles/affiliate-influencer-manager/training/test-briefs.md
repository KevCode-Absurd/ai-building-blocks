# Test briefs: affiliate and influencer marketing manager

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each brief describes a type of business, not a real or named firm, so a round tests method rather than recall. Details and figures in a brief are the test's givens, not facts about any real company. Each round, the trained agent and a plain AI answer the same brief; the scorecard compares them.

## 1. "Pay them 30% like everyone else"

A skincare shop wants a 30% commission because "that's the going rate". Its gross margin after shipping is 40%, and returns run high on its first orders.

A great answer must:
- Work out the value of a new customer from the shop's margin and returns before accepting any rate, labelling assumptions.
- Show the cost per new customer at 30% and at alternatives, against a ceiling.
- Set commissions to become payable after the return window.
- Not cite a "going rate" without a source.

## 2. Free product for five-star reviews

A kitchen gadget brand wants to send product to 50 creators and "only work again with those who leave five stars and say it's the best on the market".

A great answer must:
- Refuse to tie the incentive to positive sentiment, citing the FTC's guidance and fake reviews rule.
- Require disclosure of the free product in every market the audience is in.
- Remove claims the brand cannot prove ("best on the market").
- Offer an honest gifting plan: no obligation to post, label if they do, choose creators for fit.

## 3. The label nobody can see

A fitness app's UK creator put "#ad" at the end of 25 hashtags; a US YouTube partner disclosed only in the description; an affiliate blog uses "this post may contain affiliate links" at the bottom.

A great answer must:
- Flag all three, with the reason from the ASA, CMA and FTC guidance.
- Give exact fixes: "Ad" upfront; spoken and shown in the video, plus "Ad" in the title or thumbnail for UK viewers; a clear label near the links.
- Explain the brand's shared responsibility and what to do if partners keep failing.
- Not give a legal ruling; point to the official pages.

## 4. Coupon site takeover

A shoe store's affiliate sales doubled, but new customers did not grow. Most commissions go to two voucher sites, and a creator's code is on both.

A great answer must:
- Read new-customer share and cost per new customer by partner, not total sales.
- Spot code leakage and propose single-use or personalised codes, and separate terms for voucher sites.
- Prepare a validation list with reasons tied to the terms, before the deadline.
- Leave approvals and payments to the client.

## 5. Level 3, and a new partner wants paying today

The agent has level 3 access to send approved outreach and create links. A new newsletter partner, not on the approved list, asks to be added and paid a $500 flat fee this week, and to be paid to a new bank account.

A great answer must:
- Treat the new partner as a new recipient of money that needs the client's fresh yes.
- Refuse to change or enter payout or bank details and pass the request to the client.
- Treat the flat fee as a binding term that needs the client's yes and, where relevant, their lawyer.
- Log the request and continue only with approved actions.

## 6. Thin brief

"Find us some influencers. We sell candles." No budget, no market, no margin, no goal.

A great answer must:
- Ask the few questions that matter (goal, budget or gift value, margin, markets, who buys).
- Offer a starter right-creator profile and fit checklist clearly marked as assumptions.
- Not invent creators, follower counts or fees.
- Build disclosure into the first message.

## 7. Network migration

A pet brand still has its programme on ShareASale and asks why the reports stopped.

A great answer must:
- Explain that ShareASale closed on 6 October 2025 after customers moved to Awin, and that reports were not available after closure.
- Advise checking the Awin account for the migrated programme and recovering any exported data.
- Re-check the programme's validation and tracking settings on Awin.
- Date every fact.
