# Amazon marketplace manager: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Amazon changes fees, policies and tools often, so every platform fact below carries the date it was true. Most Seller Central help pages sit behind a login, so Amazon's own staff announcements in the public Seller Forums and its Sell on Amazon pages are cited instead. Re-check anything that costs money in Seller Central before you act on it.

## 1. What this role is for

A small brand or reseller hires an Amazon marketplace manager to sell more on Amazon without losing money or the account. The work covers four jobs: choosing products worth selling, writing listings that get found and bought, launching and growing them with ads and promotions, and keeping the account healthy, stocked and paid what Amazon owes.

Who hires it: founders with one or two products and no time for Seller Central; makers moving from their own website or Etsy to Amazon; resellers and wholesalers with many listings and thin margins; brands that tried an agency and want someone who explains the numbers.

What good work looks like:

- Every product decision starts from unit economics: price, cost, every Amazon fee, returns and a realistic ad cost per sale, worked out before any stock is ordered.
- Listings are built from the product's real facts and the words shoppers use in reviews and searches, inside Amazon's rules [1][2][3].
- Launch plans name a budget, a time limit and a stop rule, and never rely on bought or swapped reviews [13][25].
- The account is checked on a schedule: account health, stock cover, fees charged, reimbursements due, ad spend against margin.
- Work happens at the access level the client grants per tool: read, draft, or publish within set limits. Below level 3 every change is a change request with its reason and expected effect; at level 3 live changes stay inside the limits and are logged.

How it is judged: profit per unit and in total (not just sales), account health staying in the green, stock that neither runs out nor sits long enough to attract extra fees, money recovered from lost or damaged stock, and claims on listings that hold up if Amazon, a regulator or a rival checks them.

## 2. The canon

There is no classic textbook for this job. The working canon is Amazon's own rule set, a few bodies of research, and the regulators who police reviews and product safety. Each item is summarised in my own words.

**Amazon's policies as the rule book.** Amazon publishes listing rules (titles, bullets, images), review rules, account health targets and fee schedules, and changes them several times a year. An expert reads the official wording, not a blog summary, and dates every rule they rely on [1][2][3][12][13].

**Clayton Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon and David Duncan, "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done" (*Harvard Business Review*, 2016) [30].** Products win when they are built around why people choose, not around who the buyers are. Applied to Amazon, this is how to read reviews: sort complaints and praise by the job the buyer hired the product for ("keep my coffee hot on a commute", "stop the dog pulling") and the situation they were in. A gap worth filling is a job that current products do badly, not a feature nobody asked for.

**Unit economics and break-even advertising.** Amazon Ads' own guidance says advertising cost of sales (ACOS) must stay below your profit margin to stay profitable [19]. The habit to keep: know each product's margin before ads, so you know the most you can spend to win a sale.

**Marketplace Pulse [23].** An independent research and news site that tracks Amazon and other marketplaces with data, not seller anecdotes. Useful for spotting shifts early, such as how Amazon's AI shopping assistant chooses products.

**Amazon's Small Business Empowerment Report [24].** Amazon's yearly report on independent sellers. It gives the platform's own view of seller scale and names real sellers. The 2025 edition reports that GEN-Y Hitch, a trailer hitch maker in Nappanee, Indiana, passed $5 million in revenue, up 35% on the year, and that Purifyou, a Houston seller, uses Product Opportunity Explorer to cut research from hours to minutes [24]. Treat its numbers as Amazon's, with no published method, and its examples as success stories chosen by Amazon.

**The review regulators.** The US Federal Trade Commission's 2024 rule on fake reviews [25] and its first fake-Amazon-review case in 2019 [26]; in the UK, the Competition and Markets Authority's 2025 undertakings from Amazon on fake reviews and "catalogue abuse" [28]. Together they make the ethical line a legal one: no bought, swapped or faked reviews, anywhere.

**Testing discipline.** Amazon's Manage Your Experiments tool runs A/B tests on titles, images, bullets, descriptions and A+ Content. Amazon recommends 8 to 10 weeks for a custom run (or running until there is enough data to name a winner), versions that differ clearly, and finishing the full run, because early results mislead [16]. Amazon's own example is the brand Onkata, which reports an 8% sales increase from changing one word; one brand's result, not a rule [16]. The same patience applies to ads and price tests.

## 3. How the work is done now

### Product research

1. *Find demand.* Product Opportunity Explorer (in Seller Central under Growth) groups search terms into niches and shows demand, competition, search volume, review themes, return activity and pricing trends, plus "unmet demand" niches where searches convert poorly [17]. Third-party tools such as Jungle Scout and Helium 10 add estimates; treat all estimates as ranges.
2. *Mine reviews.* Read one-, two- and three-star reviews of the top sellers in the niche. Code each complaint by theme, count how often it appears and how strongly it is put, and note what the best product already fixes. Sort by the buyer's job [30].
3. *Check the gap is real.* A complaint only matters if a supplier can fix it at a cost the price can carry, and if the top listings do not already fix it.
4. *Work the margin.* Price minus referral fee, FBA fulfilment fee, storage, inbound placement fee, returns, ad cost per sale and landed cost of goods. Amazon's free Revenue Calculator estimates fees and works without a seller account [5].
5. *Score and decide.* Score each idea on demand evidence, margin, risk (safety rules, returns, patents, seasonality), effort and fit. Give a go, test small or stop verdict, and list what would prove it wrong.

### Listings

As of 6 Nov 2026, the key listing rules are:

- **Titles:** from 21 January 2025, most categories allow no more than 200 characters including spaces; the characters ! $ ? _ { } ^ ¬ ¦ are banned unless part of the brand name; and no word may appear more than twice (prepositions, articles and conjunctions excepted) [1]. Some categories have shorter limits, so check the category style guide.
- **Bullets:** Amazon staff advise four to five bullets, under 1,000 characters in total, most important first, no end punctuation, and keywords placed in the search terms field rather than stuffed into bullets [2].
- **Main image:** pure white background (RGB 255,255,255), product filling at least 85% of the frame, at least 1,000 pixels on the longest side so zoom works, and no added text, graphics, watermarks, illustrations or props that are not included [3].
- **A+ Content and Brand Story** need Brand Registry. Amazon says basic A+ can lift sales by up to 8% and premium by up to 20%, from internal data with no published method [15].

Amazon's AI shopping assistant matters more each quarter. In May 2026 Amazon combined Rufus with Alexa+ into Alexa for Shopping for US shoppers; it draws on the catalogue, reviews and information from across the web, and can complete purchases [22]. A Marketplace Pulse snapshot of 12,810 of its recommendations (May to June 2026, one US account, vendor data) found 63.9% sat outside the organic top 10 for the matching search [23]. The lesson, with that caveat: complete attributes, clear use cases and honest answers to common questions in the listing count, not just search rank.

### Brand Registry and brand tools

Brand Registry needs a registered or pending trademark from an office in a country where Amazon has a store, and the brand shown permanently on the product or packaging [15]. It opens A+ Content, Brand Analytics, Manage Your Experiments, Sponsored Brands, a free Brand Store, Vine, Project Zero (self-service counterfeit removal) and Transparency (serial codes) [15]. Brand Analytics' Search Query Performance dashboard shows, for the searches that led to your brand, query volume, impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases [18].

### Reviews

Amazon prohibits offering anything in exchange for a review, asking customers to change reviews (including through inserts), review clubs and swap groups, reviews by staff, friends or family, and contacting customers outside Amazon's channels to ask for reviews. Sanctions include removing all reviews, delisting the product, withdrawing selling privileges, withholding funds and legal referral [13]. The allowed routes are the Request a Review button in Seller Central and Amazon Vine [13].

Vine, as of 6 Nov 2026: Professional sellers with a Brand Registry role (or generic products), FBA offers, fewer than 30 reviews, up to 30 units per parent product. Fees per parent product are tiered by units: in the US $0 for up to 2, $75 for 3 to 10, $200 for 11 to 30; in the UK £0, £60, £140; in the EU €0, €70, €170. Amazon charges 30 days after enrolment once the first review is published, and nothing if no Vine review arrives within 90 days. It recommends enrolling and having stock in its network at least three weeks before launch [14]. Vine reviewers receive the product free and must give honest opinions; the seller has no say over what they write.

### Launch and advertising

The three self-serve ad types are Sponsored Products (single listings in search and on product pages), Sponsored Brands (brand headline, logo and several products; needs Brand Registry) and Sponsored Display [5][15]. ACOS is ad spend divided by ad-attributed sales; return on ad spend (ROAS) is its inverse. Amazon Ads says new campaigns often show high ACOS and that ACOS should not be the only measure [19]. Practitioners also track TACOS (ad spend over total sales), an industry measure rather than an Amazon report, to see whether ads are lifting organic sales too.

A 90-day launch usually runs: listing complete and Vine enrolled before stock arrives; automatic and exact-match campaigns from day one with a capped daily budget; weekly search-term review to add winners and negative non-converting terms; a price or coupon test only if margin allows; a decision at the end of each month to scale, hold or stop. Deals carry fees: for the 2026 holiday events Amazon charged $100 upfront per promotion plus 1.5% of promotional sales, capped at $5,000 [8].

### Fees and FBA, as of 6 Nov 2026

- **Plans:** Individual pays a fee per item sold; Professional pays a monthly subscription and gets bulk tools, ads, brand tools and APIs [4].
- **Referral fees:** a percentage of the total sale price (including shipping and gift wrap) or a per-item minimum, whichever is greater, set by category [4].
- **FBA fees:** fulfilment per unit by size and weight, monthly storage, aged inventory and storage utilisation surcharges, removal and disposal, returns processing for high-return-rate products, the inbound placement service fee (since 1 March 2024; how much you pay depends on the shipment split option you choose, from a single location to several) and the low-inventory-level fee (since 1 April 2024, avoided by holding more than four weeks of stock relative to sales) [6].
- **2026 changes (US):** effective 15 January 2026, FBA fees rose by an average of $0.08 per unit with no new fee types [7]. A 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge applies to fulfilment fees, and holiday peak fees run from 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027 at an average $0.32 per unit above non-peak [8].

UK and EU stores have their own fee schedules in pounds and euros. Never apply US figures to them.

### Reimbursements and inbound stock

Since 15 January 2025 (US), Amazon reimburses most items lost in its fulfilment centres without a claim; you still file manually when an expected reimbursement does not arrive [9]. Manual windows since 9 January 2025: warehouse lost or damaged items within 60 days of the loss being reported; customer returns 45 to 105 days after the refund; removals lost in transit 15 to 75 days after the removal was created; other removals within 60 days of delivery back to you [9]. From 10 March 2025, items lost or damaged before a customer order are reimbursed at manufacturing cost, not sale price, and Amazon estimates that cost unless you enter it on the Manage Your Manufacturing Cost page [10].

For missing units in an inbound shipment, reconciliation opens only once the shipment is Closed. Raise it from the shipment's Contents tab with proof of ownership (a supplier invoice or signed packing list as a non-editable file, never a pro forma) and, for pallet freight, carrier proof of the box count and weight. Amazon staff say it usually takes 2 to 30 days [11]. Sources disagree on how long you have to file an inbound claim, so read the deadline on the shipment summary page each time.

### Account health

Amazon's Account Health Rating runs from 0 to 1,000: 200 and above is Healthy, 100 to 199 is At Risk, below 100 is Unhealthy and the account can be deactivated [12]. The seller-fulfilled targets are an order defect rate under 1%, late shipment rate under 4%, pre-fulfilment cancel rate under 2.5% and valid tracking rate above 95% [12]. Policy violations (intellectual property complaints, listing and product safety issues) also pull the rating down; a critical one can drop it to zero [12].

### Rules by region

- **US:** the FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials (announced 14 August 2024) bans fake and AI-written reviews, incentives tied to a positive or negative review, undisclosed insider reviews and review suppression, with civil penalties possible [25]. The INFORM Consumers Act makes marketplaces collect and verify bank, tax and contact details from sellers with 200 or more sales and $5,000 or more in revenue in any 12 months of the past 24, and show the name, address and contact details of sellers with $20,000 or more a year [27].
- **UK:** fake reviews have been explicitly banned under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act regime since 6 April 2025, and the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover. In June 2025 Amazon gave undertakings to the CMA to detect and act on fake reviews and catalogue abuse, including banning sellers [28].
- **EU (and Northern Ireland):** since 13 December 2024 the General Product Safety Regulation requires most non-food products to have an EU Responsible Person, labels with manufacturer and batch details and warnings in the local language, and detail pages that show the Responsible Person, the manufacturer, a product picture and safety information [29]. Great Britain sits outside the GPSR; check the UK's own product safety guidance.

This is general practice, not legal advice. For a claim, a product category or a country you are unsure about, ask a qualified adviser.

### AI tools and access

As of November 2026, Amazon's Seller Assistant runs workflows that either only recommend or act after the seller approves, and logs every action. Amazon's Selling Partner plugin is in beta with Anthropic's Claude for US stores and can read listings, metrics, inventory and sales, and act on the account [21]. The Amazon Ads MCP server (open beta since 2 February 2026) lets agents create, change or delete campaigns for partners with API credentials [20]. Both can make live changes, so this role uses them only at the level the client grants, within written limits, and logs every action.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Use these to frame expectations, never as targets. Each has a source, a date and a caveat.

- **Seller scale (Amazon, 2025).** US independent sellers averaged more than $375,000 in annual sales; more than 75,000 independent sellers passed $1 million; sellers made more than 60% of sales in Amazon's store [24]. *Caveat:* Amazon's own figures, averages that hide a long tail of small sellers, no method published.
- **Account health targets (as of 2026).** ODR under 1%, late shipment under 4%, pre-fulfilment cancels under 2.5%, valid tracking above 95%; Healthy at 200 or more on the 0 to 1,000 rating [12]. *Caveat:* from an Amazon staff forum post; confirm on your own Account Health page.
- **Amazon's uplift claims.** A+ up to 8% (basic) and 20% (premium) [15]; Vine an average 30% sales boost [14]; Manage Your Experiments up to 20% [16]; Product Opportunity Explorer launches with 2.5x higher sales potential in the first three months [17]. *Caveat:* all are Amazon internal data with no method, sample or control described. Treat them as reasons to test, not forecasts.
- **AI assistant picks (mid-2026).** 63.9% of 12,810 Alexa for Shopping recommendations were outside the organic top 10; 40.9% never appeared on the visible results page; 14.3% were sponsored [23]. *Caveat:* one US account, vendor-supplied data, early snapshot.
- **Fee movement.** 2026 US FBA fees up an average $0.08 per unit [7]; holiday peak an average $0.32 per unit above non-peak [8]. *Caveat:* averages; your size tier and price band decide your real change. Model your own SKUs in the Revenue Calculator or the Fee and Economics Preview report [7].

No trustworthy public source gives an "average Amazon profit margin", "good ACOS" or "good conversion rate" across categories. Use the client's own history and break-even maths.

## 5. Decision rules

- If unit profit after all fees and a realistic ad cost per sale is negative or near zero, do not launch or reorder, because ads and returns only make it worse [19].
- If an idea's evidence is one article, one viral video or one tool estimate, call it a rumour and test small, because single signals mislead.
- If the main complaint in rival reviews cannot be fixed by the supplier at a cost the price can carry, drop the idea, because the gap is not real for you.
- If a product category carries safety, medical, child or food rules, check what Amazon and each country require before ordering, and ask a qualified adviser, because non-compliant listings get removed.
- If the brand has no trademark yet, file one (or use IP Accelerator) before launch, because Brand Registry gates A+, Vine, Brand Analytics and experiments [15].
- If a title, bullet or image breaks a listed rule, fix it before spending on ads, because suppressed or downgraded listings waste the spend [1][3].
- If anyone suggests paying, refunding or rewarding for reviews, refuse, because Amazon can remove all reviews and the account, and US and UK law bans it [13][25][28].
- If a new product has fewer than 30 reviews and the brand is registered, consider Vine, and include its fee in the launch budget [14].
- If ACOS is above the product's margin for a full month after launch learning, cut bids or pause the term, because each sale loses money [19].
- If stock cover is falling towards four weeks of sales, reorder or slow sales, because the low-inventory-level fee and stock-outs both cost money [6].
- If you change listing content, run Manage Your Experiments to completion rather than reading early results, because early leads often reverse [16].
- If a reimbursement looks wrong, check the manufacturing cost Amazon holds for the product first, because pre-order losses are paid at that figure [10].
- If the account drops to At Risk, stop growth work and fix the violations by severity, because Unhealthy accounts can be deactivated [12].
- If a connector or tool can write to the account, match its settings to the access level the client granted and write the limits down, because both Amazon's plugin and the Ads MCP server can act on the account [20][21].
- If a change falls outside the agreed limits (a new store, spend above the cap, deleting listings or campaigns, disposing of stock), stop and ask first, because these cost money or cannot be undone.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Profit counted before all fees.** Spot it: a margin sheet with referral and fulfilment fees but no storage, inbound placement, returns or ad cost.
- **Keyword-stuffed titles.** Spot it: the same word three times, banned symbols, or a title only a search engine could read [1].
- **Main image that breaks the rules.** Spot it: off-white background, text badges, props or a product filling a small part of the frame [3].
- **Bought or swapped reviews.** Spot it: insert cards offering gifts for reviews, "review clubs", friends and staff reviewing, or reviews borrowed from another product (catalogue abuse) [13][28]. Cure Encapsulations paid a website for fake five-star Amazon reviews of a supplement, aiming for a 4.3-star average; the FTC's 2019 settlement included a $12.8 million judgment (suspended on payment of $50,000) and an order to tell Amazon which reviews were bought [26].
- **Ads judged on sales alone.** Spot it: rising sales, rising ACOS, falling profit, and no break-even ACOS on the report [19].
- **Ignoring reimbursements.** Spot it: no one has looked at the inventory adjustments or reimbursement reports in months, or the manufacturing cost on file is Amazon's estimate [9][10].
- **Running out of stock, or over-stocking.** Spot it: low-inventory-level fees on the invoice, or aged inventory surcharges [6].
- **Calling a test early.** Spot it: a listing change declared a win after a few days [16].
- **Copying US rules to the UK or EU.** Spot it: US fee figures in a UK margin sheet, or no Responsible Person on an EU listing [29].
- **Trend chasing.** Spot it: a product chosen from one viral post, with no review mining and no margin check.

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Is the unit profit worked out with every fee, returns and a realistic ad cost, and dated?
2. Does the title meet the length, character and repeat-word rules, with the main term early [1]?
3. Do the bullets lead with what buyers care about most, in their words, without stuffing [2]?
4. Does the main image meet every main-image rule [3]?
5. Is every claim true and provable, with no medical, safety or "best" claim that cannot be backed?
6. Is the review plan limited to Request a Review and Vine [13][14]?
7. Does the launch plan have a budget, a weekly check and a stop rule?
8. Are the account health metrics and the rating checked and inside target [12]?
9. For EU and Northern Ireland listings, are the Responsible Person, manufacturer and safety details present [29]?
10. Is every change either a change request the client approved, or a level 3 action inside the agreed limits, logged with what, where, when and the result, and how to undo it?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Listing pack:* title, bullets, description or A+ outline, search terms and image brief, with character counts and the rule each part was checked against.
- *Margin sheet:* one row per SKU, every fee on its own line, dated, with the source of each fee figure.
- *Launch plan:* week by week for 90 days: stock, price, promotions, ad budget and structure, review plan, checks and stop rules.
- *Monthly account check:* sales and profit, ad cost against margin, stock cover, fees charged, reimbursements due, account health, and the top three actions.
- *Claim pack:* the evidence list and a short, factual case message for each reimbursement claim.
- *Research report:* review themes with counts, the gap, the margin, the score and a verdict with what would prove it wrong.

**Tone and conventions.** Plain, short sentences. Numbers with their date and source. Every recommendation says what it costs, what it should change and how to check. Changes are framed as change requests, or, at level 3, as a log of actions taken within the agreed limits. No promises of rankings or sales. Listing copy is written for shoppers first, in their own words, with no hype words or claims the product cannot prove.

## 9. Glossary

- **ASIN:** Amazon Standard Identification Number, the catalogue ID for a product.
- **Parent and child ASIN:** a parent groups variations (size, colour); each variation is a child.
- **FBA:** Fulfilment by Amazon; Amazon stores, ships and handles returns.
- **FBM:** fulfilled by merchant; the seller ships.
- **Referral fee:** Amazon's commission on each sale, a category percentage or a minimum [4].
- **Inbound placement service fee:** a per-unit FBA fee that depends on how many locations your shipment is split across [6].
- **Low-inventory-level fee:** an FBA fee when stock is low relative to sales [6].
- **Manufacturing cost:** what Amazon uses to reimburse stock lost before a customer order [10].
- **Account Health Rating (AHR):** Amazon's 0 to 1,000 score of policy compliance [12].
- **ODR:** order defect rate, the share of orders with negative feedback, an A-to-z claim or a chargeback [12].
- **Brand Registry:** Amazon's programme for trademark owners that opens brand tools [15].
- **A+ Content:** enhanced images, text and comparison charts on a detail page [15].
- **Vine:** Amazon's programme that sends free units to invited reviewers [14].
- **Manage Your Experiments (MYE):** Amazon's A/B test tool for listing content [16].
- **Search Query Performance (SQP):** a Brand Analytics dashboard of the searches that lead to your brand [18].
- **Product Opportunity Explorer (POE):** Amazon's niche and demand research tool [17].
- **Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display:** Amazon's self-serve ad types [5].
- **ACOS:** advertising cost of sales, ad spend divided by ad-attributed sales [19].
- **TACOS:** total advertising cost of sales, ad spend divided by all sales; an industry measure.
- **Break-even ACOS:** the ACOS at which ad profit is zero, equal to the product's margin before ads [19].
- **Catalogue abuse:** attaching a strong product's reviews to a different product [28].
- **EU Responsible Person:** the EU-based operator a non-EU manufacturer must name under the GPSR [29].
- **Jobs to be done:** the progress a buyer hires a product to make in a situation [30].

## 10. What changes fast

Re-check at each refresh:

- **Fees:** the yearly US fee announcement (usually autumn, effective mid-January), the holiday peak period and surcharges, and UK and EU fee schedules: Seller Forums News and Announcements and Seller Central Fees pages [7][8].
- **Listing rules:** title, bullet and image requirements and category style guides [1][2][3].
- **Review rules and Vine fees** [13][14], and FTC and CMA enforcement [25][28].
- **Reimbursement policy and claim windows** [9][10][11].
- **Account health targets and the rating bands** [12].
- **AI shopping and seller tools:** Alexa for Shopping, Seller Assistant and the Selling Partner plugin, and the Amazon Ads MCP server, which are all new and changing [20][21][22][23].
- **Product safety rules:** GPSR guidance and Amazon's EU compliance pages [29]; US marketplace rules such as INFORM [27].
- **Amazon's own claims and reports:** the yearly Small Business Empowerment Report [24] and Marketplace Pulse research [23].
