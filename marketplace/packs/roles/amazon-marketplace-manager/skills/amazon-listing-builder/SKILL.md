# Amazon listing builder

A listing has two readers: Amazon's search and shopping assistant, which need complete, accurate facts, and the shopper, who scans the title, the main image, the price and the first bullets on a phone. This skill writes for both, from evidence, inside Amazon's rules. Rules checked on 2026-11-06; re-check the category style guide before publishing.

## When to use

- A new product needs its first listing.
- A listing gets sessions but converts poorly.
- Amazon flagged or suppressed a title, image or attribute.
- The client wants a version B to test with Manage Your Experiments.
- A listing goes live in a new store (UK, EU) and needs local rules and language checked.

Not for: inventing reviews, claims or certifications; medical or safety claims the product cannot prove.

## What you need

- **Product facts:** what it is, materials, size and weight, what is in the box, compatibility, care, warranty, certifications the client holds (with the certificate).
- **Shopper language:** 50 or more reviews of the product or close rivals, Search Query Performance or a keyword export, and the questions buyers ask most.
- **Rival listings:** the top three by sales in the niche.
- **Brand status:** Brand Registry or not (A+ Content and experiments need it).
- **Category:** the category and any category style guide or title exceptions.
- **Stores:** US, UK, EU; for EU and Northern Ireland, the Responsible Person and manufacturer details and safety warnings.
- **Access:** level 1 (read) for steps 1 to 10. Step 11 needs level 2 (save as a draft) or level 3 (publish within the client's limits). Ask which level the client grants and note it under "Access I have".

## Steps

1. **Collect the facts.** Make a fact sheet. Every claim in the listing must trace to a line in it. Mark anything unproven.
2. **Pick the terms.** From the search data and reviews, choose the main search phrase (what most buyers call the product), two or three secondary phrases (type, use, audience) and the words buyers use for its benefits. Note which come from reviews and which from search data.
3. **Write the title.** Lead with the brand and the main phrase, then the key differentiator, size or quantity, and the variation. Stay within 200 characters including spaces (or the category's lower limit), use none of the banned characters (! $ ? _ { } ^ ¬ ¦ unless part of the brand) and no word more than twice. Front-load: on phones only the start shows.
4. **Write the bullets.** Four to five bullets, under 1,000 characters in total, most important first. Each bullet answers one buying question: what it does for me, will it fit, what is it made of, how do I use and clean it, what if it goes wrong. Start each with a short label in the buyer's words. No end punctuation, no keyword lists.
5. **Write the description or A+ outline.** Without Brand Registry, a plain description that answers the remaining questions. With Brand Registry, an A+ outline: module order, the image or chart for each, the words on each, and a comparison chart against the client's own range (never a rival's brand).
6. **Fill search terms and attributes.** Put synonyms, other-language terms used in the store and common misspellings in the search terms field, not in bullets. Fill every attribute the category offers; the shopping assistant reads them.
7. **Brief the images.** Main image: pure white background, product filling at least 85% of the frame, at least 1,000 pixels on the longest side, no text, badges, props or illustrations. Secondary images: one per buying question (scale with a hand or object, in use, what is in the box, key feature close-up, dimensions, care). Write the brief for the photographer or designer, with the shot list and any on-image words.
8. **Run the claims check.** Remove "best", "number one", medical or safety claims, and any certification not on file. Remove price, shipping or promotion language from the title and bullets.
9. **Add store details.** For EU and Northern Ireland: Responsible Person, manufacturer, product identification and warnings in the local language. For each store, translate with a native speaker check rather than raw machine translation.
10. **Plan the test.** If the brand is registered, name one element to test first (usually the main image or title) with a clear hypothesis, run for Amazon's recommended duration, and do not call it early. Starting the experiment needs level 3.
11. **Hand over or publish.** At level 1, give the listing as a change request. At level 2, save a new listing as a draft in Seller Central (or prepare the edit file) for the client to publish. At level 3, show what will be published and where, get one yes before the first live change, publish within the agreed limits, and log what, where, when and the result. Ask again before overwriting live content the client has not seen.

## Checks

- Title length, characters and word repeats pass the rules; character count shown.
- Bullets: four to five, under 1,000 characters in total, each answering one buying question.
- Main image brief meets every main-image rule.
- Every claim traces to the fact sheet; unprovable claims removed.
- Search terms carry synonyms, not repeats of the title.
- EU and Northern Ireland safety and Responsible Person details present where needed.
- Nothing asks for reviews, offers rewards, or mentions rivals by name.

## Output format

1. **Fact sheet** with sources.
2. **Term map:** main phrase, secondary phrases, benefit words, and where each came from.
3. **Title** with character count and rule check.
4. **Bullets** with total character count.
5. **Description or A+ outline.**
6. **Search terms and attributes to fill.**
7. **Image brief:** main plus secondary shot list.
8. **Claims check log:** what was removed or changed and why.
9. **Test plan:** element, hypothesis, duration.
10. **Change request** (levels 1 and 2), or at level 3 the publish plan, the client's yes and the action log.

## Example of good output (short)

*Marlow Pantry is a made-up business used only to show the format. The product facts and review phrases are invented.*

**Main phrase:** glass spice jars. **Review words:** "labels don't peel", "shaker lid", "fits my drawer".

**Title (147 characters, passes):**
Marlow Pantry Glass Spice Jars with Labels, Set of 24, 4 oz Square Jars with Shaker and Pour Lids, Waterproof Labels, Fits Standard Kitchen Drawers

**Bullets (619 characters in total)**

- LABELS THAT STAY PUT: 400 waterproof printed labels for common spices and blank ones for your own mixes, so labels survive damp hands and wipe-downs
- SHAKE OR POUR: each jar has a shaker insert and a two-way lid, so you can sprinkle paprika or spoon out cumin without swapping lids
- FITS YOUR DRAWER: square 4 oz jars stand 3.9 inches tall and sit in rows in a standard kitchen drawer, with no wasted space between them
- GLASS, NOT PLASTIC: thick clear glass lets you see what is running low and does not hold smells between refills
- WHAT IS IN THE BOX: 24 jars, 24 shaker inserts, 24 lids, 400 labels, a funnel and a chalk pen

**Main image brief:** 24 jars in a 6 by 4 grid, lids on, no labels showing, pure white background, set filling at least 85% of the frame, 2,000 pixels square.

**Claims removed:** "BPA-free" (no test certificate on file), "best-selling" (not provable).

**Test first:** the title, with "Glass Spice Jars with Labels" against "Glass Spice Jars with Shaker Lids" after the brand, because reviews split between the two benefits. Run it for Amazon's recommended duration before deciding.
