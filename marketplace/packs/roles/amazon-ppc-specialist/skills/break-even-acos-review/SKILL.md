# Break-even ACOS review

## When to use

- At the start of any Amazon PPC job, before opening a single campaign.
- Monthly, as the first page of the account review.
- When the blended ACOS looks acceptable but the seller's profit is falling.
- Before raising budgets on a product, to check each extra ad sale makes money.

## Why it matters

Amazon Ads' own guidance is that ACOS must stay below your profit margin, or ad spend exceeds what the sale earns, and that there is no single "good" ACOS (see knowledge.md, section 2). One blended number for the account hides products that lose money on every ad sale. This review gives every product its own yardstick.

## What you need

- For each product (child ASIN or SKU): selling price, Amazon referral fee, fulfilment fee, any other per-unit fees (storage, inbound placement, returns allowance), and landed cost (product, freight, duty, prep).
- Advertised product report for the period (ad spend, ad sales, orders, clicks by ASIN).
- Business Report by child ASIN for the same dates (total sales and units), for TACOS.
- Which products are in launch, growth or defend phase, if the seller knows.

**Access:** level 1 is enough. This is analysis only. If the seller can't share costs, stop and ask; don't guess margins.

## Steps

### 1. Build the cost line for each product
Profit before ads = price − referral fee − fulfilment fee − other per-unit fees − landed cost. Use the seller's own fee report or the Revenue Calculator, and date the fee figures. If price varies (coupons, deals), use the average selling price for the period and say so.

### 2. Work out break-even ACOS
Break-even ACOS = profit before ads ÷ price. Show it as a percentage with one decimal place. A product with a negative profit before ads can't be advertised profitably at any ACOS; flag it for the seller's pricing or sourcing decision.

### 3. Pull actual ACOS per product
ACOS = ad spend ÷ ad-attributed sales, per ASIN, from the advertised product report. Note the attribution window shown in the report's column names. If a product is advertised in several campaigns, sum across them.

### 4. Work out TACOS per product
TACOS = ad spend ÷ total sales (ad and organic) for the same dates. Compare with the previous period if available. Rising total sales with flat or falling TACOS suggests ads help; rising TACOS with flat total sales suggests ads are replacing organic sales.

### 5. Label each product
- **Profitable:** actual ACOS clearly below break-even, with enough orders to trust (as a working rule, at least a handful of ad orders in the period).
- **Break-even:** actual ACOS close to break-even.
- **Losing money:** actual ACOS above break-even with enough orders to trust.
- **Not yet proven:** too few clicks or orders to judge, or a launch product inside its agreed launch window.

Keep "losing money" and "not yet proven" apart. They need different actions.

### 6. Give one action per product
- Profitable: test more reach (higher budget, broader match, more placements) inside the client's cap.
- Break-even: tighten (harvest to exact, add negatives, lower bids on weak targets).
- Losing money: cut bids, narrow match types, pause the worst targets; if the product's margin is the problem, say so plainly.
- Not yet proven: keep spend capped, set a review date and the result that would move it.

### 7. Rank the actions
By money at stake: monthly ad spend on losing products first, then profitable products held back by budget.

## Checks

- Every number traces to a named report and date range.
- Fee figures are dated and match the marketplace (never US fees on UK or EU products).
- Break-even uses price after any regular discount, not list price.
- No product is called good or bad without its own break-even ACOS.
- The account-level blended ACOS appears only after the per-product table, with a note on what it hides.
- Actions that change bids or budgets are marked as proposals (level 1 or 2) or as inside the limits (level 3).

## Output format

1. **Per-product table:** ASIN or name, price, profit before ads, break-even ACOS, actual ACOS, TACOS (and change), ad orders, status, action.
2. **What the blend hides:** two or three sentences.
3. **Ranked actions:** numbered, each with the money at stake and a review date.
4. **Missing data:** anything assumed or estimated, labelled.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business: Copperline Kitchen, 6 products on Amazon US. Figures invented for illustration.*

> | Product | Price | Profit before ads | Break-even ACOS | Actual ACOS | TACOS (vs last month) | Ad orders | Status | Action |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Garlic press | 18.99 | 5.89 | 31.0% | 44.2% | 19% (up from 15%) | 61 | Losing money | Cut broad bids 25%, pause 4 targets |
> | Bamboo board | 29.99 | 11.40 | 38.0% | 21.5% | 9% (flat) | 140 | Profitable | Raise budget within cap; out of budget by 2 pm daily |
> | Herb keeper | 24.99 | 7.10 | 28.4% | 35.0% | 22% | 6 | Not yet proven | Launch week 5 of 8; review 20 Nov |
>
> The account's blended ACOS of 24% looks healthy because the bamboo board carries it. The garlic press loses about 2.50 on every ad sale.
>
> 1. Garlic press: cut bids and pause four broad targets (412 of ad spend last 30 days).
> 2. Bamboo board: raise daily budget from 40 to 55, inside your 60 cap.
>
> Fees from your 1 to 31 Oct fee report.
