# Phase bid rules

## When to use

- Before launching a product on Amazon Ads.
- When bids and budgets change by mood or by whoever looked last.
- When the client grants level 3 access and you need written limits for each product.
- At each monthly review, to move products between phases.

## What you need

- Each product's price, profit before ads and break-even ACOS.
- The launch date and the amount the client is willing to spend to launch (a total, not a hope).
- Current performance by product: clicks, orders, ACOS, TACOS, organic sales trend.
- The client's daily and monthly spend caps and the biggest single bid or budget change they allow.
- Stock cover: weeks of stock on hand and on the way.

**Access:** level 1 to write the rules; level 2 to set up the campaigns and settings paused; level 3 to apply bid and budget moves that follow the rules, inside the caps. Moving a product into launch spend, or any change that would exceed the cap, needs a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Confirm the listing gate
Amazon advises advertising products that are in stock, can win the Featured Offer and have a strong detail page; its best-practice guide points to five or more reviews and 3.5 stars or higher. If a product fails the gate, the rule is "fix the listing first", not "bid more".

### 2. Write the launch phase
- **Goal:** visibility and first sales data, at an ACOS above break-even that the client accepts for a set time.
- **Limits:** a total launch budget, an end date, and the highest ACOS allowed. All three are the client's numbers.
- **Campaigns:** automatic (Amazon suggests running it about two weeks before building manual campaigns), exact and phrase on the core keywords, and product targeting on close rivals.
- **Bidding:** dynamic up and down on auto and core exact; consider a top-of-search placement adjustment only for the core exact campaign.
- **Exit to growth:** when the product has a steady flow of orders and its core terms have enough data to judge, or when the end date arrives.
- **Exit to stop:** launch budget spent with ACOS still far above break-even and no improvement trend; stop and review the listing, price or product.

### 3. Write the growth phase
- **Goal:** more profitable sales.
- **Limits:** ACOS at or below target, where target sits under break-even by a margin the client chooses for profit.
- **Moves:** harvest converting terms to exact, add negatives, raise budgets on campaigns that run out while under target ACOS, test new keywords and product targets in phrase or broad.
- **Bidding:** down only on mature exact campaigns; up and down where you still want reach.
- **Exit to defend:** when extra spend stops adding total sales (TACOS rising, total sales flat), or the product has a strong organic position worth protecting.

### 4. Write the defend phase
- **Goal:** keep the sales and organic position at the lowest ad cost.
- **Moves:** tighter ACOS target; brand terms in exact; product targeting on your own pages to block rivals; test lower bids on branded terms (JLab cut bids on its top branded keywords and held page traffic, in Amazon's case study).
- **Exit back to growth:** a new rival takes share, or a seasonal peak is coming.

### 5. Set step sizes and review rhythm
- The biggest bid change in one step (the client's number).
- How often bids are reviewed: twice a week in a launch's first week, as Amazon suggests, then weekly.
- Wait two weeks or more after adding negatives before changing strategy again.
- Low stock: if cover falls below what the client set, lower bids or pause, because ads on a product about to stock out waste money and the ads stop when it does.

### 6. Write it as a rule card per product
One short card per product: phase, limits, campaigns, bidding strategy, step size, review day, exit conditions. These become the written limits for level 3 access.

## Checks

- Every number on a card is the client's or comes from the product's own data.
- Launch has a budget, an end date and a stop condition.
- Growth target ACOS is below break-even ACOS.
- Exit conditions are written in terms you can check in a report.
- Step sizes and caps match what the client granted.
- Stock cover is part of every phase.

## Output format

1. **Phase table:** product, current phase, break-even ACOS, target ACOS, budget limits, bidding strategy, review day.
2. **Rule cards:** one per product, as above.
3. **Phase moves this month:** product, from, to, the evidence.
4. **Approval line:** numbered items for the client's yes, and which rules level 3 may apply on its own.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a kitchen tools seller on Amazon US. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Bamboo cutting board: launch rule card**
> - Price 29.99, profit before ads 11.40, break-even ACOS 38.0%.
> - Launch budget 1,200 total, 25 Nov to 5 Jan. Highest ACOS accepted during launch: 60% (your number).
> - Campaigns: BambooBoard_SP_Auto_Launch (up and down), BambooBoard_SP_KW_Exact_Launch (up and down, +30% top of search), BambooBoard_SP_PT_Rivals_Launch (down only).
> - Bid step: no more than 15% per change. Review Mon and Thu in week 1, then Mondays.
> - Exit to growth: 5 Jan, or earlier if core exact terms hold ACOS under 38% for two weeks.
> - Stop and review: launch budget spent with ACOS above 55% and no downward trend.
> - Stock: pause launch spend if cover drops under 4 weeks (your number).
>
> At level 3 I may change bids and budgets on these three campaigns within these limits. Anything else, I ask.
