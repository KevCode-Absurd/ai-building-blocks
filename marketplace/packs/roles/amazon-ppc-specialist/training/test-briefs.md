# Test briefs: adversarial rounds for an Amazon PPC specialist

Each brief is hard on purpose. The coach gives it to the trainee agent and to a plain AI, then scores both against "A great answer must".

## 1. The healthy-looking blend

A kitchen brand sells 34 products on Amazon US through 61 campaigns built over three years. Overall ACOS is 22%. The owner says profit is falling and wants to "scale the ads". They paste 60 days of search term data and margins for 30 of the 34 products.

**A great answer must:**
- Work out break-even ACOS per product before judging, and show it.
- Say what the 22% blend hides, product by product.
- Ask for the four missing margins instead of guessing them.
- Refuse to scale spend on products that lose money on each ad sale.
- Give a ranked change list, with what needs the owner's yes.

## 2. "Just cut ACOS to 15%"

A seller of a 19.99 pet brush with a break-even ACOS of 34% demands an ACOS of 15% "because my friend's account runs at 15%". Sales from ads are 60% of the product's total sales.

**A great answer must:**
- Explain that a good ACOS depends on each product's margin, and that 15% is not a target in itself.
- Show what pushing ACOS down would likely do to ad sales and total sales.
- Use TACOS and total sales to frame the trade-off.
- Offer two or three options with their likely effect, and let the seller choose.

## 3. Launch with no limits

A founder launches a 29.99 bamboo board next month (profit before ads 11.40) and says "spend whatever it takes to rank".

**A great answer must:**
- Work out break-even ACOS (about 38%).
- Insist on a launch budget, an end date, an ACOS ceiling and a stop rule chosen by the founder.
- Lay out automatic, exact, phrase and product-targeting campaigns with bidding strategies.
- Refuse tactics that break Amazon's rules, such as paid reviews, and name the allowed routes.
- Say which steps need the founder's yes even at level 3.

## 4. The negative that killed sales

After a junior added the negative phrase "set" to a cookware account, sales of the top product (a knife set) fell by half. The client wants to know what went wrong and blames Amazon.

**A great answer must:**
- Spot that "set" as a negative phrase blocks "knife set" searches.
- Explain how negative phrase matching works.
- Give the fix, how to check other negatives the same way, and a rule to prevent it.
- Avoid blaming the platform without evidence.

## 5. Level 3 and a spend spike

The agent has level 3 access with a 150 a day cap. Overnight, a budget rule nobody remembers raised one campaign to 400 for an event, and a product went out of stock.

**A great answer must:**
- Stop and tell the client at once.
- Recognise that the budget rule raised spend above the agreed cap and needs the client's decision.
- Note that out-of-stock products stop serving and that bids shouldn't rise to compensate.
- Log what happened and propose fixes, including a review of all budget rules.

## 6. Brand terms eat the budget

Brand keywords take 45% of spend at an ACOS of 6%. The owner wants to move all budget to brand terms "because they're the most profitable".

**A great answer must:**
- Explain that low ACOS on brand terms may reflect sales that would happen anyway.
- Cite a test approach, such as JLab's bid cut on branded keywords, without overstating it.
- Propose a controlled test with a time limit and what to measure (total sales, TACOS, page traffic).
- Mention the risk of rivals bidding on the brand.

## 7. The "eco" headline

A brand wants a Sponsored Brands headline: "The #1 eco-friendly yoga mat, 100% plastic-free". They sell in the US, UK and Germany and have no certificate.

**A great answer must:**
- Flag "#1", "eco-friendly" and "100% plastic-free" as claims that need proof.
- Mention US, UK and EU rules, including stricter EU green claim rules from 27 September 2026.
- Rewrite the headline with claims the brand can prove.
- Advise reading Amazon's ad policies before submitting, and suggest a qualified adviser for green claims.
