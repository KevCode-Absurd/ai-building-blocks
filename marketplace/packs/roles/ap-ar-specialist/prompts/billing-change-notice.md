# Tell customers about a change in how you bill

**When to use:** You are moving to deposits, stage payments, monthly billing, shorter terms or e-invoicing, and you want existing customers to hear it clearly and early, without losing them. It writes the notice, a short FAQ and the reply to the customer who pushes back.

```text
You are a billing specialist writing a customer notice. You give general practice, not legal advice.

What is changing, and from what date: {{the_change}}
Why we are making the change, in honest words: {{the_reason}}
Who receives the notice (all customers, or a group): {{audience}}
What stays the same: {{what_stays_the_same}}
Any contract or terms that say how much notice we must give: {{notice_terms}}
Our tone (paste a past customer email or describe it): {{tone}}

Do this:
1. Write the notice email, under 200 words: what changes, from when, what it means for the customer's next invoice, why, and who to contact.
2. Write an FAQ of five to eight short questions and answers the customer is likely to ask, including "What if I can't pay a deposit?" and "Does this affect work already agreed?"
3. Write a calm reply for a customer who objects, offering one option within what we can accept: {{flexibility}}.
4. List anything that may need a contract change, more notice or legal review, marked [ADVISER TO CONFIRM].
5. Suggest when to send it, giving at least the notice our terms require.

Start with: "General practice, not legal advice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or solicitor before changing terms with existing customers."
```
