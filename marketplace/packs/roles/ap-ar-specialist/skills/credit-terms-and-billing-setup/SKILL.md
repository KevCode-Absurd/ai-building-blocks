# Credit terms and billing set-up

## When to use

- A business starts selling on credit, or gives 30 days to anyone who asks.
- Invoices confuse customers or bounce back from their payables teams.
- Projects are billed in one lump at the end and cash arrives months late.
- After a bad debt, when the owner asks "how do we stop this happening again?"
- Before moving to e-invoicing, when invoice data must be complete.

Do not use it to write legal terms of sale, to set tax rates, or to decide whether a customer is creditworthy beyond the rules the client approves. The client's solicitor and accountant review the result: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or solicitor.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** current invoices, quotes, contracts, price list, terms, sales ledger and aged debt report.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** create the invoice template, reminder templates and credit application in the client's tools or documents, unpublished.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** switch the new template and numbering on in the accounting software, publish the credit application form, and send an approved billing-change note to customers on the list the client approved. New terms sent to customers as binding, and any account stop, need a fresh yes.

## What you need

1. Five recent invoices, a recent quote and any contract or terms of sale wording.
2. How the business charges: one-off, deposits, stages, hours, monthly, usage.
3. The aged debt report and the customers who paid late or not at all, with what happened.
4. Typical customer types, order sizes and how often they buy.
5. Countries sold to, and VAT, GST or sales tax registrations (for the adviser to confirm tax lines).
6. Whether any customers or countries require e-invoices or purchase order numbers.
7. Who can approve credit limits and exceptions.

## Steps

### 1. Design the invoice

Fields: unique sequential number, invoice date, supply date, seller's full legal name, address and contact, customer's name and address, customer reference or purchase order number, clear line items with quantity and price, tax lines (marked for the accountant to confirm), total, due date as a date (not only "30 days"), payment methods, and who to contact about a query. In the UK, check the GOV.UK list of required fields; VAT invoices need more. Where e-invoicing applies, map each field to the structured format the platform needs.

Numbering: one sequence, no gaps, no reuse. Credit notes have their own sequence and always reference the invoice they correct.

### 2. Build the billing schedule

Tie each bill to a trigger the customer can see: a signed contract (deposit), a delivered stage (milestone), a calendar date (monthly). For each stage, state what the customer gets and the amount. For recurring billing, write a rule and a worked example for each case: mid-month start, upgrade, downgrade, pause, failed payment, refund and cancel.

### 3. Write the credit application

Business name and legal form, registration number, trading address, years trading, contacts for orders and accounts, expected monthly spend, trade references, bank reference if the client wants one, and space for the declarations and signatures the client's adviser approves. Collect only what the decision needs, and say how the data will be used and kept.

### 4. Set limit rules

- A starting limit for new accounts set by the client.
- Rules to raise a limit (time trading with the client, payment record, order size) and to cut one (late payments, returned payments, a published poor payment record).
- Show the scoring so anyone can follow it. Do not invent thresholds: the client sets every number.
- For large UK customers, check the public payment practices record before agreeing terms.

### 5. Write the terms policy (one page)

Standard terms, when invoices go out, reminder steps by days overdue, what counts as late, who approves exceptions, when interest or compensation may be considered (for the adviser to word), and how disputes are raised and handled.

### 6. Write stop and reopen rules

The trigger for a stop, who decides, how the customer is told (before, not after, the order is refused), and what reopens the account: payment, a plan kept for a set period, or the client's decision.

### 7. Plan the change

If billing changes for existing customers, draft the customer note: what changes, from when, why, and an FAQ. Send it only with the client's yes.

## Checks

- Every invoice field present; numbering rules clear; credit notes linked.
- Every bill in the schedule has a visible trigger and amount.
- Every number in the limit rules came from the client.
- Legal and tax items marked [ADVISER TO CONFIRM], never written as settled.
- The credit application asks only for what the decision needs.
- The output carries the line: *General practice, not legal or tax advice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or solicitor before using these terms.*

## Output format

1. Invoice template with a filled-in example.
2. Billing schedule table: stage, trigger, amount, what the customer gets.
3. Recurring billing rules with a worked example for each case.
4. Credit application form.
5. Limit rules table: factor, how it is scored, effect on the limit.
6. One-page terms policy.
7. Stop and reopen rules.
8. Customer change note (draft) and adviser questions.

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real business: a three-person design agency that billed projects in one invoice at the end. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.

> **New schedule for brand projects (fee 12,000.00):**
>
> | Stage | Trigger | Amount | Customer gets |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Deposit | Signed proposal | 3,600.00 (30%) | Kick-off workshop booked |
> | Concepts | Three concepts presented | 4,200.00 (35%) | Concept deck |
> | Handover | Final files delivered | 4,200.00 (35%) | Logo files and guidelines |
>
> **Invoice due date wording:** "Due 4 December 2026 (14 days from invoice date)." Payment link and bank details on file; queries to accounts@ (named person).
>
> **Stop rule:** If a stage invoice is 21 days overdue, the next stage does not start. The client tells the customer by email 5 days before. Work restarts when the invoice is paid.
>
> [ADVISER TO CONFIRM] VAT lines and the late payment clause.
>
> *General practice, not legal or tax advice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or solicitor before using these terms.*
