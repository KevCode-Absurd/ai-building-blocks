# Chart of accounts set-up

## When to use

- A new business is opening its first set of books.
- An owner is moving from a spreadsheet or shoebox to QuickBooks, Xero, FreeAgent, Zoho Books, Wave or similar.
- The current chart has duplicates, dead accounts or a catch-all "expenses" account, and reports cannot answer simple questions.
- The owner wants profit by service, site, project or channel.

Do not use it to set tax codes, choose cash or accrual accounting, or decide business structure. Those need the accountant. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before the software goes live.

## What you need

1. What the business sells and how it gets paid (cash, card, invoices, shop, marketplace, subscriptions).
2. Its main costs: stock or materials, staff, contractors, premises, vehicles, software, marketing.
3. Country and region, and whether it is registered for VAT, GST or sales tax (yes, no or unsure).
4. Legal form (sole trader, partnership, company) as the owner understands it.
5. The software chosen, and its default chart if already created.
6. The old ledger or spreadsheet, if any, and the last bank statements.
7. What the accountant wants: any report layout, account names or tax groupings. If unknown, mark [ASK ACCOUNTANT].
8. The questions the owner wants the books to answer ("Which service makes money?", "What does the van cost us?").

**Access:** level 1 to read the current chart, ledger and bank rules; level 2 to set up the new accounts, tracking categories and bank rules switched off or set to suggest only; level 3 to make the agreed accounts and rules live and enter opening balances, within your limits. Archiving or merging accounts that hold history, rules that post on their own, and user roles always need a new yes.

## Steps

### 1. Start from the questions, not the software default

List the five to ten questions the owner wants answered each month. Each income line and each tracking category must serve one of them. If no question needs an account, leave it out.

### 2. Draft the five sections

- **Income:** one account per way of selling that the owner wants to compare (for example services, product sales, subscriptions). Keep refunds and discounts as separate accounts or as contra lines if the accountant prefers.
- **Direct costs (cost of sales):** costs that rise with each sale: materials, stock, delivery, payment fees, subcontractors on client work.
- **Overheads:** rent, utilities, software, insurance, marketing, professional fees, bank charges, travel, staff costs.
- **Assets:** bank accounts (one per real account), money owed by customers, stock, equipment, deposits paid, money in transit from payment platforms.
- **Liabilities and equity:** money owed to suppliers, card balances, loans, tax owed (names from the accountant), owner's capital and drawings or director's loan.

Keep it small. A small business rarely needs more than a few dozen accounts in daily use. Each account must have a distinct use.

### 3. Write the use notes

For every account: code, name, a one-sentence "use this for", a "do not use this for", and two real examples from the business. This is what makes two people code the same line the same way.

### 4. Choose tracking, not more accounts

To see profit by service, site, project or channel, use the software's tracking categories (classes, tags, locations or projects) instead of copying the income and cost accounts for each one. Write the naming rule (for example "Site: Leeds", "Site: York") and who assigns it.

### 5. Plan the opening balances

List what is needed for the start date: bank and card balances from statements, money owed by customers (unpaid invoices list), money owed to suppliers (unpaid bills list), loans (lender statement), stock count, equipment list with cost and date. Mark each as supplied or [NEEDED].

### 6. Draft the bank feed and payee rules

From the bank export, list regular payees and the account each goes to. Mark which rules should suggest only and which may auto-post once the owner has reviewed a month. At level 2, create them as suggest-only rules (level 2); switching a rule to auto-post always needs the owner's yes.

### 7. Plan user access

Who gets which role: owner (admin), bookkeeper (standard, no user management), accountant (accountant role), payroll provider (payroll only), and any AI connector (scopes that match the level granted, with write tools off at level 1). No shared logins. The owner sets the roles; I only propose them.

### 8. Write the routine

A weekly list (code new lines, attach receipts, send invoices, chase overdue) and a monthly list (reconcile each account, clear suspense, review profit and loss, send the questions list), with rough times the owner can adjust.

## Checks

- Every account has a code, a name, a use note and two examples.
- No two accounts share a use.
- Tracking categories answer the owner's questions without duplicating accounts.
- Tax codes, method and asset thresholds are marked [ASK ACCOUNTANT], not set by me.
- Opening balance sources are named and dated.
- Every document carries: *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before you file or claim anything.*

## Output format

1. The owner's questions the chart answers.
2. Chart of accounts table: code, section, name, use for, do not use for, examples.
3. Tracking categories and naming rules.
4. Opening balance checklist with status.
5. Payee rule table.
6. User access plan.
7. Weekly and monthly routine.
8. Questions for the accountant.

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real business: a dog-walking company with two vans and four walkers. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> | Code | Section | Name | Use for | Do not use for | Examples |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 4000 | Income | Walk packages | Prepaid weekly walk bundles | One-off grooming add-ons | "10-walk pack, Smith"; "Monthly plan, Patel" |
> | 4100 | Income | Add-on services | Grooming, pet sitting | Walk packages | "Bath and brush"; "Weekend sit" |
> | 5100 | Direct costs | Walker contractors | Freelance walkers paid per walk | Employed staff wages | "Invoice, walker #3"; "Holiday cover walker" |
> | 6200 | Overheads | Van running costs | Fuel, servicing, parking | Buying a van (asset) | "Fuel, van 1"; "Annual service" |
>
> **Tracking:** Van: Van 1 / Van 2. Answers "What does each van cost us?" without separate accounts.
>
> **Questions for the accountant:** Which tax codes apply to walk packages? Should prepaid packages be recorded as income when paid or when walked?
>
> *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before you file or claim anything.*
