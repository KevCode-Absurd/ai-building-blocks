# Training interview: Bookkeeper

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client names, account numbers, tax IDs or amounts that could identify a client; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of businesses do you keep books for, in which countries or states, and on which software?**
   Why it matters: business type, region and software decide which rules, reports and shortcuts apply, and set the limits of the agent's advice.

2. **Walk me through your month-end close for a typical client, from the first statement to the note you send.**
   Why it matters: shows the real order of work and where the expert saves time or catches errors.

3. **How do you find the true starting point when you take over messy books?**
   Why it matters: a wrong opening balance poisons every later month; the expert's method is the most reusable thing they know.

4. **When a reconciliation will not balance, what do you check first, second and third?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's search order for differences, which the agent can follow step by step.

5. **Tell me about the strangest difference you ever traced. What was it?**
   Why it matters: real stories teach the agent which odd causes to keep on its list.

6. **How do you design a chart of accounts, and what do you refuse to add?**
   Why it matters: records the expert's rules for a small, clear chart that people code consistently.

7. **How do you decide when to code a line and when to ask the owner?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's line between evidence and guessing in the expert's own words.

8. **How do you review what bank rules or AI matching did, and what have you caught them getting wrong?**
   Why it matters: AI now does the first pass; the expert's review habits are what keep the books right.

9. **How do you handle online sales: payouts, fees, refunds, chargebacks and reserves?**
   Why it matters: captures the gross-to-net method and the platform reports the expert trusts.

10. **What do you do when an owner mixes personal and business spending?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert codes it, talks to the owner about it and hands it to the accountant without blame.

11. **What is in your questions list for owners, and how do you get answers back quickly?**
    Why it matters: the questions list is the bookkeeper's main tool; its format and follow-up habits make it work.

12. **What does your accountant hand-off pack look like at year-end?**
    Why it matters: turns the records pack into the expert's real template and checklist.

13. **Which adjustments do accountants most often send back to you, and what did you change after?**
    Why it matters: reveals the quality bar from the accountant's side and the lessons the agent should apply up front.

14. **Where do you draw the line between bookkeeping and tax advice? Give an example of a question you passed on.**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's safety line and the wording the expert uses to pass a question to a qualified accountant.

15. **Which record rules and deadlines do you track for your clients' regions, and where do you check them?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent which official sources the expert trusts and how often they check.

16. **What controls do you suggest to small businesses to cut the risk of fraud or payment diversion?**
    Why it matters: captures cheap, practical controls the agent can recommend.

17. **How do you work when a client will not give you software access?**
    Why it matters: shows the expert's export-only method, which is how the agent works at level 1 or with no connection at all.

18. **How do you log your changes so someone else could follow them a year later?**
    Why it matters: records the change log format that makes the agent's work auditable.

19. **What does a client do that makes your job twice as hard, and how do you fix that habit?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent advice for owners that prevents the mess instead of cleaning it.

20. **Describe a catch-up job that went badly or ran over. What would you do differently?**
    Why it matters: a hard story shows the risks, the estimates the expert now uses and when to warn the client early.
