# Brand guidelines book

Most small businesses do not need a new identity. They need the one they have written down, so that the shop sign, the Instagram posts, the invoices and the agency's ads all look like the same business. This skill documents, it does not redesign: every value comes from the owner's choices, and every conflict goes on a list for them to decide.

## When to use

- The business has several versions of its logo and colours, and staff improvise.
- A new agency, printer or designer needs one document to follow.
- After a new identity is approved, to turn it into rules.

Not for: designing a new logo (use the logo-design-brief skill), trade mark advice, or making the template files themselves.

## What you need

- Every logo file the business has, with where each is used.
- Colours in use (values if known, or photos of printed items).
- Fonts in use and how they were licensed.
- Photos, illustrations and templates in use (Canva, Office, email).
- Any existing guide, even a one-pager.
- Who will use the book: staff, agencies, printers, developers.
- **Access I have.** Steps 1 to 8 need level 1. Step 9 (building the book or a template set as a draft in Canva, Figma or a brand portal) needs level 2. Publishing the book to a shared portal, replacing the live brand kit or updating shared templates needs level 3 within the client's limits.

## Steps

1. **Collect and map.** List every asset: file name, version, where it appears, and its values. Group duplicates and near-duplicates (three greens that are almost the same).
2. **List conflicts.** For each conflict, show the options side by side with where each is used, and ask the owner to choose. Do not choose for them. Until they choose, mark the item "[decision needed]".
3. **Set the source of truth.** One primary logo, approved variants, and one value per colour, all from the owner's choices. Note what is retired.
4. **Write logo rules.** Versions and when to use each (full colour, one colour, reversed, symbol only); clear space defined by part of the mark so it scales; minimum sizes for screen in px and print in mm; approved backgrounds; and a misuse page. Public guides show the level of detail that works: Spotify sets a clear zone of half its icon's height, minimum sizes for the full logo and icon, and a list of banned changes such as rotating, stretching and recolouring (checked 6 Nov 2026).
5. **Write colour rules.** Each colour's role, hex and RGB, CMYK and Pantone where supplied (marking any conversion "[check with your printer]"), roughly how much of a layout it covers, and the contrast pairing table: which text may go on which background, with ratios against WCAG 2.2 (4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text; logos exempt, other brand text not).
6. **Write type rules.** Heading and body fonts with weights and a size hierarchy for web, print and Office, with system fallbacks staff actually have. Record each font's licence and what it covers.
7. **Write image rules.** Photos: subjects, light, crop, colour treatment, and what to avoid, each as one short do and don't. Illustrations and icons: style in plain qualities, line and fill rules. Note that people in photos need consent and that stock needs a licence covering the use.
8. **Specify applications and templates.** Email signature, letterhead and business card layouts with sizes, type and spacing; a list of templates to make in Canva or Office, each with its master layout and what is locked or editable.
9. **Level 2: build the draft book.** With level 2 access, build the book or template set in the client's tool as a draft named "DRAFT", not shared. Log what you created. At level 3, after a confirmed action list, publish it to the place the client named and log it; ask again before replacing or deleting any live asset.

## Checks

- Every value matches an asset the owner approved; nothing is redesigned.
- Every conflict is either decided by the owner or marked "[decision needed]".
- Logo rules include clear space, minimum sizes, backgrounds and misuse.
- Every text and background pair in use has a ratio and a permitted use.
- Fonts have licences recorded and fallbacks for Office.
- Each rule is one short sentence with a reason where it helps.
- A new staff member could make an on-brand post from the book alone.

## Output format

1. **Contents** and a one-page "brand on a page" summary.
2. **Logo:** versions, clear space, minimum sizes, backgrounds, misuse.
3. **Colour:** palette table and pairing table.
4. **Type:** fonts, hierarchy, fallbacks, licences.
5. **Imagery:** photo and illustration rules.
6. **Applications:** signature, letterhead, cards, templates.
7. **Decision list** (conflicts and choices) and **change log.**
8. **Action log** for any level 2 or 3 work.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a joinery firm. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Decision list:** three greens in use. A #2F5D3A (van livery, website), B #2E6B3F (business cards), C #335C38 (Instagram templates). Owner to choose one; the others retire. "[decision needed]"

**Logo rules (extract):**
- Use the full-colour lockup on white or Pale Oak; use the white version on photos and on Forest Green.
- Keep clear space equal to the height of the pine-tree symbol on every side.
- Minimum width: 120 px on screen, 30 mm in print; below that, use the symbol alone.
- Do not stretch, rotate, outline, add shadows or change the colours.

**Type (extract):** Headings in the brand serif, bold; body in the brand sans, regular. In Word and PowerPoint, use Georgia and Arial, which staff already have.
