# Logo design brief

A logo brief does not describe a picture. It describes the problem the mark has to solve and the tests it has to pass. Designers do their best work against clear tests, and clients choose better when they judge drafts against the tests instead of their mood that day.

## When to use

- A new business is about to commission a logo.
- An existing brand wants a refresh without losing what customers recognise.
- Logo drafts have arrived and the client needs a fair way to judge them.

Not for: drawing the logo, legal trade mark clearance, or copying another brand's mark.

## What you need

- What the business sells, to whom, and how it differs, in the owner's words.
- Three to five feeling words.
- Every place the logo will appear, with the smallest real size (favicon, app icon, social avatar, embroidery, receipt, sign).
- For a refresh: the current logo and what customers say about it; what must stay.
- Competitors' names (for a first search and to avoid looking alike).
- Who will design it, the budget and the deadline.
- **Access I have.** Steps 1 to 8 need level 1. Step 9 (setting up a draft brief page or review board in the client's Figma or Canva) needs level 2. Uploading a final logo to a shared brand kit or library needs level 3 within the client's limits.

## Steps

1. **Write the idea in one line.** What the mark should make people feel or understand about the business, in plain words. Not a picture ("a leaf"), a meaning ("grown here, sold here").
2. **Turn feeling words into design choices.** For each word, one choice and one reason: shape (round or angular), weight (light or heavy), colour temperature, contrast, and whether a character or symbol fits.
3. **Choose the mark type with reasons.** Wordmark (the name is distinctive and short), lettermark (the name is long and the initials are known), symbol plus wordmark (the brand needs a square icon), or badge (heritage, craft or a club feel; hard to use small). Say what the business gives up with each.
4. **List must-haves and must-avoids.** Must keep (for a refresh: the colour, shape or character customers already know); must avoid (clichés of the trade, shapes close to competitors, anything that only works in colour).
5. **List every place and size.** A table of touchpoints with the smallest size for each. Include the hard cases: a website favicon must be square, at least 8 x 8 px, and Google recommends larger than 48 x 48 px; a Google Play icon is a 512 x 512 px full square to which Play adds 30% rounded corners and a shadow (both checked 6 Nov 2026). Note embroidery, single-colour print and dark mode where they apply.
6. **Specify versions and files.** Primary lockup, horizontal and stacked versions, symbol alone, one-colour black and white, and reversed. Files: SVG and PDF or EPS vector masters, PNG with transparent background, and a favicon set. The client receives editable source files.
7. **Write the draft checks.** At least these, each pass or fail: works in solid black and solid white; reads at the smallest size on the list; has a square version; works on light, dark and photo backgrounds; does not rely on fine detail or thin lines; matches the idea line; no obvious clash in a first search. Add rounds: concepts, refinement, final.
8. **Run a first trade mark search and log it.** Search the name, and the symbol if there is one, in the official tools: USPTO Trademark Search (design search codes for logos), the UK IPO search (keyword, phrase or image), the EUIPO's search on its own site, and WIPO's Global Brand Database (including image similarity). Log what you searched, where, when, and anything close in the same kind of goods or services. Mark the log "first look only, not clearance: ask a trade mark attorney before filing or launching".
9. **Level 2: set up the review.** With level 2 access, set up a draft page or board in the client's design tool with the brief and the checks, for the designer to place drafts against. Log it.

## Checks

- The idea line is a meaning, not a picture.
- Every design choice has a reason tied to the brief.
- The smallest real size is listed, and the hardest touchpoint is named.
- The draft checks are pass or fail, not matters of taste.
- Files include editable vector masters for the client.
- The search log is dated and labelled as not clearance.
- No other brand, artist or logo is named as a style to copy.

## Output format

1. **Summary:** business, buyers, idea line, feeling words with choices and reasons.
2. **Mark type** and why.
3. **Must-haves and must-avoids.**
4. **Touchpoint table:** place, smallest size, background, colour limits.
5. **Versions and files.**
6. **Draft checks and rounds.**
7. **First search log** (date, tool, terms, results, "not clearance").
8. **Open questions** with defaults, and an action log for any level 2 or 3 work.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a tea room cafe. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Idea line:** "A proper sit-down tea, made by people who know your name." **Feeling words:** warm, unhurried, local. Warm becomes a rounded serif and copper-brown; unhurried becomes generous spacing and no motion lines; local becomes the town's name in the lockup.

**Mark type:** symbol plus wordmark, because the cafe needs a square avatar and a sign. Badge rejected: it would fail at avatar size.

| Touchpoint | Smallest size | Background | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website favicon | Square; Google recommends larger than 48 x 48 px | Browser tab | One shape, no words |
| Instagram avatar | Circle crop | Photo grid | Symbol only |
| Paper cup | 30 mm wide | Kraft brown | One colour |
| Shop sign | 2 m wide | Painted wood | Gold leaf, one colour |

**Draft checks:** works in one colour; symbol reads at 48 px; no thin lines that blur on kraft paper; matches the idea line; first search shows nothing close in cafe or tea services.

| Date | Tool | Term | Goods or services | What the search showed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [date searched] | UK IPO search, keyword | [Business] | Cafe and tea room services | [record each close result: mark, owner, goods, live or dead] |
| [date searched] | UK IPO search, keyword | [Business] split into two words | Cafe and tea room services | [as above] |
