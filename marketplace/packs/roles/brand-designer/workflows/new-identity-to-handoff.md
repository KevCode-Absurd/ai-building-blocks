# New identity, from discovery to handoff

**Trigger:** a new business, or one ready for a refresh, needs a visual identity and will hire a designer or illustrator to draw it.

**Time:** usually three to eight weeks, most of it in drafting rounds, decisions and any trade mark attorney's review.

## Steps

1. **Intake and access.** Ask the access.md questions: what the business sells, to whom, feeling words, every touchpoint with its smallest size, what must stay, budget, deadline, who draws. Ask which access level the client grants in each tool (Figma, Canva, brand portal) and record it as "Access I have". Until they answer, work at level 1.
2. **Audit what exists (prompt: brand-asset-audit).** For a refresh, collect every version and list what customers recognise. For a new business, collect competitors' looks as qualities to avoid copying.
   **Approval point 1:** the client confirms the summary, feeling words, touchpoints and what must stay.
3. **First name and mark search (prompt: trademark-first-search-log).** Search the name in the USPTO, UK IPO, EUIPO and WIPO tools for the regions the client trades in. Log it as "first look only, not clearance". If anything looks close in the same trade, pause and recommend a trade mark attorney before more design spend.
4. **Logo brief (skill: logo-design-brief).** Idea line, mark type with reasons, must-haves, touchpoint table, versions and files, draft checks.
   **Approval point 2:** the client approves the logo brief and the draft checks. Ownership of the work is agreed in writing with the designer before drafting starts (the client's lawyer writes the terms).
5. **Drafts and review (prompt: logo-draft-review).** The designer delivers drafts; I review each against the checks, rank them and write specific feedback. Repeat for the agreed rounds.
   **Approval point 3:** the client chooses the final mark. If the mark changed materially since the first search, run the search again and send the result to the attorney.
6. **Palette and type (skill: palette-and-type-system).** Colour roles, values, contrast pairing table, fonts with licences and fallbacks, and a tokens file (prompt: design-tokens-from-brand).
7. **Imagery (skill: illustration-and-mascot-brief; prompt: icon-set-spec).** Photo rules, and briefs for any mascot, illustration set or icon set.
   **Approval point 4:** the client approves palette, type and imagery rules.
8. **Brand book (skill: brand-guidelines-book).** Logo rules, colour, type, imagery, applications and a template list.
9. **Build and roll out.**
   - Level 1: deliver the brief pack and book; the client's designer builds files and templates.
   - Level 2: build drafts in the client's tool (styles, variables, a draft brand kit or template set named "DRAFT"); log what was created.
   - Level 3: show the action list and limits (which libraries, kits and templates, and when) and get a yes; then publish the brand kit or library updates within those limits. Ask again before replacing or deleting any live asset. Log each action.
10. **Hand over.** Deliver final files (vector masters and PNGs), the book, tokens, briefs, the search log, the decision and change log, and the action log.

## Outputs

- Discovery summary and asset audit.
- Dated first search log, labelled not clearance.
- Approved logo brief with draft checks, and review notes per round.
- Palette, contrast pairing table, type system and tokens file.
- Imagery, mascot, illustration or icon briefs.
- Brand book and template list.
- Drafts in the client's tools (level 2) or published brand kit updates within limits (level 3).
- Action log of every change made in the client's tools.

## Needs the client's approval

- The access level for each tool, and the limits for level 3.
- Discovery summary and must-keeps (point 1).
- Logo brief and checks (point 2), and the written ownership terms with the designer.
- The final mark (point 3), and the trade mark attorney's review where anything looked close.
- Palette, type and imagery (point 4).
- Any font, illustration or stock licence bought, each time; payment stays with the client.
- Anything outside the level 3 limits, asked again each time.
