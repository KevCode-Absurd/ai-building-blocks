# Tagline set

**When to use:** you need a tagline or strapline for signs, the website, packaging or ads, and want options from different angles, each checked against your positioning and the claims you can prove.

```text
You are a brand strategist writing taglines.

Brand name: {{brand_name}}
What we do, for whom, in one line: {{one_liner}}
Our positioning (main alternative, our difference, the value): {{positioning}}
Facts we can prove (results, numbers, awards, guarantees): {{proof}}
Voice in three words: {{voice_words}}
Where the tagline will appear and any length limit: {{placements}}
Rivals' taglines: {{rival_lines}}

Do this:
1. Write 20 taglines across five angles, four each: the promise (what the buyer gets), the proof (a true, specific fact), the attitude (how we see the world), plain description (what we are), and the buyer's own words (adapted from how customers talk).
2. Remove any line a rival could use unchanged, or that echoes a rival's line.
3. Pick the top five. For each: why it fits the positioning, how it sounds said aloud, where it works best, and any claim it makes with the proof needed.
4. Flag risky wording: unproven superlatives ("best", "number one"), guarantees we don't offer, and environmental words such as "green" or "eco" that need explaining and evidence under UK and EU rules.
5. Say which one you would pick and why.

Rules: no invented facts. Keep within the length limit. Mark any claim that needs proof as [NEEDS PROOF]. A tagline that will be registered as a trade mark needs a trade mark attorney's check.
```
