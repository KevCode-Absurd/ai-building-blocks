# Voice and message check

A guide only works if new copy is checked against it. This skill reads drafts line by line, and reads the brand's touchpoints side by side, to find where the voice slips or the message drifts, then fixes only what needs fixing.

## When to use

- Before publishing a launch email, homepage, campaign or sales deck.
- When a new writer, agency or AI tool starts writing for the brand.
- For a regular review across the website, emails, social posts and sales material.
- When the team argues about whether something "sounds like us".

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read drafts and live copy in docs, the website, email platform and social accounts. Level 2 to leave suggested edits or comments in the client's docs, or draft revised copy as unpublished drafts. Level 3 to publish approved fixes on the pages and channels the client names, within their limits. A change to the brand name, tagline or a public claim needs a fresh yes every time.

- The voice guide and messaging hierarchy (or run the voice-guide and positioning-and-messaging skills first).
- The drafts to check, with channel and reader situation.
- For a touchpoint review: the homepage, key pages, three recent emails, ten recent social posts and the sales deck or proposal template.
- The fact sheet of allowed claims and their proof.

## Steps

1. **Set the frame.** For each draft, note the channel, the reader and their situation (buying, waiting, complaining). Tone depends on it.
2. **Read once as the reader.** Does it make sense at a glance? What is it asking them to do?
3. **Check line by line.** For each sentence, mark:
   - **Pass:** sounds like the brand, true, clear.
   - **Fix:** right idea, wrong voice (too formal, jargon, banned word, wrong tone for the moment). Give a rewrite.
   - **Fail:** off message, unproven claim or risky wording. Give a rewrite or cut it.
4. **Check claims.** Any objective claim needs proof the client already holds (FTC reasonable basis in the US; CAP Code rule 3.7 in the UK). Unqualified superlatives count as comparisons with every rival. Vague environmental words need explaining and evidence, and checking against current UK and EU rules. Flag anything regulated for a qualified adviser.
5. **Check the message.** Does the piece carry the core message or a supporting one from the hierarchy, in the agreed order? Note any new claim or promise that isn't in the hierarchy.
6. **For touchpoint reviews, compare side by side.** Make a table: touchpoint, how it describes the business, core message present or missing, voice score, drift notes. Look for different one-liners, different names for the same product, and tone that changes for no reason.
7. **Find patterns.** Group repeated slips (for example, "formal in emails", "jokes in service replies", "three names for one plan") and suggest one fix for each pattern, such as a guide update or a template.
8. **Write the fixes.** Rewrite only failing and fix-marked lines; leave passing lines alone so the writer's work stays theirs.
9. **Recheck after edits.** Up to three rounds; if the same slip keeps coming back, add it to the guide's examples.

## Checks

- Every fix or fail has a rewrite.
- Passing lines are not rewritten for taste.
- Claims are checked against proof and the relevant rules.
- Tone is judged against the reader's situation, not in the abstract.
- Patterns are reported, not just single lines.
- No change to the brand name, tagline or a public claim goes live without a fresh yes.

## Output format

1. Summary: overall verdict, the top three issues, and patterns.
2. Line table: line, mark (pass, fix, fail), reason (trait, word list, tone, claim, message), rewrite.
3. Claims list: claim, proof held, risk, action.
4. For touchpoint reviews: drift table and one-liner variants found.
5. Suggested guide updates.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a coffee roaster whose guide says "warm, plain, a little dry". Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

| Line | Mark | Reason | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| "We are thrilled to announce our most sustainable blend ever!" | Fail | "Sustainable" with no evidence or explanation; exclamation breaks "dry" | "Our new [Business] Blend uses beans from two farms we buy from directly. Here's what that means." |
| "Utilising our world-class roasting expertise, we deliver an unparalleled cup." | Fix | Jargon and an unprovable superlative | "We roast in small batches every Tuesday, so it's fresh when it reaches you." |
| "Order by Sunday and it ships Monday." | Pass | Plain and specific | — |

**Pattern:** big adjectives replace facts in launch emails. **Guide update:** add a "facts, not adjectives" example to the "plain" trait.

**Claim flagged:** "direct from two farms" needs the supplier records on file before publishing.
