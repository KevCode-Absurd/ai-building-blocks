# Voice guide

The brand's voice usually exists already, in its best emails, pages and replies. This skill finds it and writes it down so anyone, including an AI tool, can repeat it. Short beats complete: a guide nobody reads changes nothing.

## When to use

- Several people, agencies or AI tools write for the brand.
- The brand sounds generic or different in every channel.
- Before briefing a copywriter, agency or AI writing tool.
- After a positioning change, to bring the voice in line.

Not for: copying another brand's voice (borrow a quality, never the phrasing), or visual style (see the brand identity designer kit).

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read samples in the client's site, docs, email platform and social accounts. Level 2 to write the guide as a draft in the client's docs or wiki, or as draft style rules in a writing tool. Level 3 to publish the approved guide to the team space and update the approved rules in the writing tool within the client's limits.

- Five pieces the owner is proud of and two they dislike: pages, emails, posts, proposals, support replies.
- Customer words: reviews and messages, to hear how buyers talk.
- The positioning and messaging, if they exist.
- Facts: products, prices, claims allowed, claims that need proof, claims that are banned.
- Any existing guide, even if unused.

## Steps

1. **Collect and label samples.** Mark each as strong or weak, with the channel and the reader's situation (buying, waiting, complaining, celebrating).
2. **Find the ten best sentences.** Sentences where the brand sounds most like itself. These anchor the guide.
3. **Pull out patterns.** Typical sentence length; contractions or not; how the reader is addressed; warmth; humour and where it appears; jargon and how it is handled; favourite words; words never used.
4. **Place it on the four tone scales.** Formal or casual, serious or funny, respectful or irreverent, matter-of-fact or enthusiastic. Mark where the brand sits now and where it should sit, with a reason tied to its buyers.
5. **Name three to five traits.** Each with a one-line plain definition, a "we say" example and a "we don't say" example, both from or close to real material. Add the line where a trait goes too far ("warm, not gushing").
6. **Write word lists.** Words we use, words we avoid with plain replacements (the GOV.UK approach), how we write product names, numbers, dates and the brand name itself.
7. **Set tone by situation and channel.** The voice stays; the tone shifts. Write short guidance for hard moments (complaints, refunds, outages, bad news) and for channels such as service replies, marketing emails and social posts, in the way Monzo grades how much of each principle each channel gets.
8. **Add a fact sheet.** Products, prices and claims the brand may make, claims that need proof, and claims it must not make.
9. **Rewrite three real pieces.** Before and after, using the guide, so the team can hear the difference.
10. **Cut to size.** Two pages or close to it. Move anything else into an appendix.
11. **Plan a light test.** Ask a few customers or new staff to describe two samples in three words; check whether they use the chosen tone words.

## Checks

- Every trait has real "we say" and "we don't say" examples.
- No trait is an empty adjective without a definition.
- Hard-moment guidance exists (complaints, refunds, errors).
- Word lists include plain replacements, not only bans.
- The fact sheet only holds facts found in the client's material.
- The guide fits on about two pages.
- Nothing copies another brand's lines.

## Output format

1. Voice in one sentence.
2. Tone scales: where we sit, with reasons.
3. Three to five traits, each with definition, say this, not that, and "too far".
4. Words we use and avoid, with replacements; style points.
5. Tone by situation and channel.
6. Fact sheet.
7. Three before-and-after rewrites.
8. Test plan.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a letting agent for student houses. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Voice in one sentence:** Calm, plain and on the tenant's side, like a good older housemate who knows the rules.

**Tone scales:** casual (students read on phones), serious about money and safety, respectful, matter-of-fact. Humour only in welcome emails.

| Trait | Means | We say | We don't say | Too far |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain | Short words, one point per sentence | "Your deposit is protected. Here's where." | "Please be advised your deposit has been lodged with an approved scheme." | Dropping details tenants need |
| On your side | Say what we'll do, and when | "We'll fix the boiler by Thursday. If not, we'll call you Wednesday." | "Maintenance requests are processed in order of receipt." | Promising times we can't keep |
| Calm | No alarm, no exclamation marks in problems | "There's a leak. Here's what to do now." | "URGENT!! Leak reported!!" | Sounding cold in an emergency |

**Avoid → use instead:** "in due course" → a date; "kindly" → "please"; "per our policy" → the reason in plain words.

**Hard moments:** bad news first, then what we'll do and when, then who to call. No jokes.
