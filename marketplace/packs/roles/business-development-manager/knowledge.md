# Business development manager: what the role knows

Checked 6 November 2026. Facts that can change carry a date and a source number from `sources.json`. This is general business development practice, not legal advice. Tender terms, contracts, partnership agreements, competition law, insurance and anti-bribery questions go to the client's lawyer, insurer or accountant. I never say whether a contract, a partnership or a campaign is lawful.

Related kits cover the neighbouring work, so this one points to them instead of repeating them: `sdr` (cold email and calling rules in detail, lead scoring), `proposal-writer` (public tenders, portals and bid writing), `channel-sales-manager` (reseller and partner programmes, vendor partner incentives), `revops-specialist` (CRM stages and forecasting) and `sales-enablement-specialist` (negotiation and battlecards).

## 1. What this role is for

**Who hires a business development manager.** Firms that want new kinds of customers, not just more of the same. Two common cases. First, small service firms moving from homeowners or one-off jobs to commercial contracts: cleaning, security, grounds maintenance, trades, signage and IT support firms selling to facilities managers, property managers, schools, councils and housing providers. Second, business-to-business firms opening new accounts or partnerships: a referral partner, a firm to team with on a bigger contract, or a prime contractor to subcontract to. The agents built on this kit produce a one-page capability statement, a supplier pre-qualification checklist, briefs and plans for target accounts, intro emails, site walk question sheets, partnership proposals and deal reviews.

**What good work looks like.**
- A short list of target accounts chosen by rules, not by who the owner happens to know.
- A capability statement a buyer keeps: what you do, proof you have done it, and the facts they must check, on one page.
- Paperwork ready before the buyer asks: insurance, certificates, policies, registrations, each with an expiry date.
- First meetings and site visits prepared with questions, so the quote covers what the buyer expects.
- Each live deal reviewed against a qualification checklist, with unknowns named, not guessed.
- Partnerships written down: who does what, who owns the customer, how money flows, and how it ends.

**How the work is judged.**
- New accounts won and the contract value they bring, against the plan.
- Places on approved supplier lists and frameworks gained, and kept at renewal.
- Meetings and site visits held with the right buyer, and the share that become quotes.
- Partnerships that produce introductions or shared work within an agreed time.
- No claim, accreditation or reference later found untrue.

**What this role never does.** Claim a licence, insurance cover, accreditation or client it does not have; name a client without permission; share pricing or bid plans with a competitor; offer gifts or hospitality to win a decision; send a binding quote, contract or declaration without a fresh yes; or give a legal reading of tender or contract terms.

## 2. The canon

**MEDDIC, MEDDICC and MEDDPICC [1][2].** The qualification method most used in larger B2B sales. According to meddicc.com, MEDDIC was created at PTC in 1996, credited to Dick Dunkel, with Jack Napoli also named [1]. The letters stand for Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain and Champion. MEDDICC adds Competition; MEDDPICC adds Paper process (how a decision becomes a signature) [1]. Andy Whyte's book *MEDDICC* is the best-known guide [2]; meddicc.com is the site of his training business, so read its history as the business's own account. In this kit, MEDDICC is a set of questions about a deal, used to find what is unknown. It is not a form to fill with guesses.

**Gong's talk-to-listen analysis (vendor data) [26].** Across 326,000 sales calls of 10 minutes or more, sellers talked 60% of the time on average: 57% in won deals and 62% in lost deals [26]. One vendor's sample across call types. The lesson for first meetings and site walks: the buyer should do most of the talking.

**The buyer's rulebooks.** Public buyers publish how they buy (see the `proposal-writer` kit). Competition law limits what competitors may agree when they team up [10][11][12]. Anti-bribery law limits hospitality and gifts [21]. Read these before the first partner meeting or client dinner, not after.

**Books practitioners return to.** Credited as a reading list; this kit makes no specific claim from them.
- Neil Rackham, *Major Account Sales Strategy*. Read it for planning sales to large accounts over many meetings.
- Robert Miller and Stephen Heiman, *The New Strategic Selling*. Read it for mapping everyone who influences a buying decision.
- Mike Weinberg, *New Sales. Simplified.*. Read it for choosing target accounts and opening new business.
- Bob Moore, *Ecosystem-Led Growth*. Read it for finding customers through partners' relationships.
- Andy Whyte, *MEDDICC* [2]. Read it for qualification in depth.

## 3. How the work is done now

### Choosing target accounts

- **Write the rules first.** What makes a good account for this firm: size, number of sites, contract length, location within the service area, how they buy (direct award, quotes, tender, framework), who signs, and red flags (slow payers, work the firm cannot staff). Use the firm's best existing contracts as the model. If there are none, mark the rules as a first guess to test.
- **Research from public facts.** Each target's own website, published tenders and contract notices, annual reports, planning applications and news. For UK companies, Companies House shows company details, officers, filed documents and disqualified directors [22]. The `sdr` kit's account research brief covers more sources. Date every fact and keep guesses separate.
- **Tier the list.** A few top accounts get a full account plan and personal outreach; the rest get a brief and a lighter sequence.

### Account plans and qualification

- **The account plan.** For each top account: what they buy today and from whom, contract end dates if known, the people involved and what each cares about, the problems the firm could solve, the way in (a small job, a trial site, a framework, a partner), the next three actions with dates, and what is still unknown.
- **MEDDICC review for live deals.** For each letter, write what is known, the evidence, and the next question to ask [1]. Metrics: what the buyer would measure. Economic buyer: who signs and approves the money. Decision criteria and process: how and when they choose. Paper process: purchase orders, supplier set-up, insurance checks, contract signature. Pain: the problem in their words and its cost. Champion: who inside wants this and will act. Competition: the incumbent, other bidders, or doing nothing.
- **In HubSpot (as of November 2026).** Deal stages carry win probabilities, and pipelines can require properties before a deal enters a stage, for example "economic buyer named" before a proposal stage [3]. Association labels, on Professional or Enterprise, let a team describe how a contact relates to a record [4]; some teams create labels such as "Champion" or "Economic buyer" on deals. Quotes need Revenue Hub Professional or Enterprise, as HubSpot's October 2026 docs name it; quotes needing approval wait in Pending approval, e-signature is the default way to accept, and expiry dates can be set [5]. Advanced approvals (Enterprise) can route on discount, amount and term length [6].
- **In Salesforce.** Its pricing page lists a Free Suite and paid editions, with Agentforce for Sales as an add-on [8]. Salesforce Help pages, including Account Plans, did not load for automated checks on 6 November 2026, so this kit makes no claim about specific Salesforce features. Check Opportunities, Account Teams and Account Plans in Salesforce Help before relying on them.

### Capability statements

- **One page, five blocks.** What you do (one line). Core services (a short list). Why choose you, each point backed by a fact (years, sites served, response times you actually meet, retention of a named client who agreed). Past work (named clients only with permission; otherwise "a 12-site housing provider"). Company facts: legal name, registration number, insurance cover, accreditations with expiry dates, areas covered, contact.
- **For US federal buyers,** add the identifiers they check: the UEI from SAM registration (12 characters) and the NAICS codes that match your services; SBA size standards decide whether you count as small for each code [16]. SBA advises learning what agencies or prime contractors need and showing how you meet it; agency small business offices (OSDBU or OSBP) and Procurement Center Representatives help find opportunities [15].
- **For UK and EU public buyers,** match the wording to the buyer's own categories and keep registrations current; the `proposal-writer` kit covers Find a Tender, the central digital platform and the EU's ESPD.
- **No adjectives without proof.** "Industry-leading" and "one-stop shop" tell a facilities manager nothing; "we clean 38 sites a week" does, if it is true.

### Pre-qualification packs

Buyers often check a supplier before any quote: a questionnaire, a supplier portal or an approved list. The pack is a table of every document they ask for, who holds it, its expiry date and status, and the gaps to close, ordered by what would stop an application first.

- **Insurance (UK).** Employers must hold employers' liability cover of at least £5 million from an authorised insurer once they employ anyone (some exemptions, such as employing only a close family member). The fine is £2,500 for each day without proper cover and £1,000 for not displaying the certificate, which may be shown electronically [19]. Buyers often also ask for public liability and professional indemnity at levels they set; take the levels from the buyer's own document.
- **Cyber Essentials (UK).** Five technical controls; Cyber Essentials Plus adds independent testing; IASME runs certification [20]. Buyers often ask IT support firms for it, and some UK government contracts require it (see the `proposal-writer` kit).
- **Public buyers (UK).** Notices are on Find a Tender, where suppliers register before bidding. The government's commercial body is now the Government Commercial Agency, formerly Crown Commercial Service (GOV.UK updated 21 July 2026). Government aims to pay 90% of invoices within 5 days and all within 30 [18].
- **Public buyers (US).** SAM registration and a UEI come first [16]. See the `proposal-writer` kit for the bid steps.
- **Public buyers (EU).** Groups of firms may bid together, and the buyer cannot require a specific legal form just to submit a tender [17]. See the `proposal-writer` kit for thresholds and the ESPD.
- **Trade and safety schemes.** Many buyers, especially in construction and housing, ask for a health and safety accreditation from a scheme they name. Use the buyer's list; do not guess which schemes they accept.

### First contact

- **Rules differ by region.** US: CAN-SPAM has no business-to-business exception; messages need accurate headers, an honest subject line, a postal address and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days [23]. UK: marketing messages must identify the sender, say they are selling and offer an unsubscribe; check the Telephone Preference Service before telesales calls [24]; the `sdr` kit explains how UK rules treat companies differently from sole traders. EU: email marketing needs prior consent, with a soft opt-in for existing customers, and how business subscribers are protected is left to each country [25].
- **What works with facilities and property buyers.** Short, plain intro emails that lead with the buyer's problem and one proof point, then a small ask: a site walk, a trial area, a quote for one site. Follow-ups add something new each time.

### Site walks and first visits

- Bring a question sheet grouped by area (access and security, hours, surfaces or assets, waste, health and safety, reporting, who checks the work) so the quote covers what the buyer expects.
- Ask about the current supplier's gaps, the contract's end date, and what a good month looks like to the buyer.
- Let the buyer talk [26]. Write the notes the same day and confirm the scope back to the buyer in writing before quoting.

### Partnerships

- **Types.** Referral partners (they introduce, you deliver); teaming, where one firm leads and others subcontract, or firms form a joint venture; subcontracting to a larger prime; co-marketing. Reseller and vendor partner programmes are in the `channel-sales-manager` kit.
- **US federal teaming.** FAR Subpart 9.6 recognises team arrangements, either a partnership or joint venture acting as prime, or a prime with agreed subcontractors, when members and relationships are disclosed in the offer; it authorises nothing that breaks antitrust law (FAC 2026-01, effective 13 March 2026) [13]. Under SBA's Mentor-Protégé Program, a mentor and a small protégé may form a joint venture that bids as a small business; the protégé must do at least 40% of the joint venture's work, agreements run up to six years, and SBA says processing takes about 105 days [14]. SBA's SUBNet lists subcontracting opportunities from large primes [15].
- **UK supply chains.** Since 1 September 2019, bidders for central government contracts over £5 million a year must show they pay 95% of supply chain invoices within 60 days [18]. Useful when a small firm is deciding whether to subcontract to a large prime.
- **EU consortia.** Groups may bid without a specific legal form; a legal form can be required after award if needed to perform the contract [17].
- **Competition law.** Teaming with a firm that could compete with you for the same work needs care. In the US, price fixing, bid rigging and dividing customers or markets are treated as always illegal [10]. The UK CMA's horizontal agreements guidance (16 August 2023) applies to agreements between actual or potential competitors [11]; the EU's revised Horizontal Guidelines were published on 21 July 2023 [12]. Practical rule: do not discuss prices, bids or who bids where with a competitor-partner unless the client's lawyer has set the boundaries.
- **Bribery (UK).** A company can commit an offence if someone acting for it bribes to win business; adequate procedures are a defence. The guidance (updated 22 January 2025) sets six principles, including risk assessment and due diligence [21]. Hospitality should be modest and recorded; ask the client's policy before offering any.

### CRM and AI connectors (as of November 2026)

- HubSpot's remote MCP server reads and writes contacts, companies, deals, quotes and logged activity, with access set by a user-level app's scopes; an account admin connects first [7].
- Salesforce's local DX MCP server reached end of life on 2 November 2026; Salesforce says its tooling now comes as skills [9]. Check what your AI tool offers for Salesforce today.

### Access and what needs a fresh yes

Access is granted per tool at three levels (see `access.md`). Level 3 covers the work itself: updating CRM records, logging visits and calls, enrolling approved contacts in approved sequences and sending approved follow-ups within the client's limits. Some steps need a fresh yes every time, even at level 3, because they commit the client: any discount, any quote the client treats as binding, any contract, partnership agreement, NDA or teaming agreement, any declaration or form submitted to a supplier portal, and anything that names a new recipient for money. Seats tied to one person, such as a LinkedIn Sales Navigator seat or a named portal login, are used only by their holder.

## 5. Decision rules

- If the client cannot say what a good account looks like, write the rules from their best existing contracts first, because a long list built on hunches wastes visits.
- If a target account fails a red-flag rule (outside the service area, work the firm cannot staff), drop it, however famous the name.
- If a "why choose us" line has no fact behind it, cut it or replace it with one the client can prove.
- If a client is not named with permission, describe them instead ("a 12-site housing provider").
- If an accreditation is in progress, write "in progress" with the expected date, or leave it out, because buyers check.
- If the buyer's pre-qualification list asks for a document the client lacks, list it as a gap with the lead time to get it, ordered by what would stop the application first.
- If insurance levels are asked for, take them from the buyer's own document, never from a general guide.
- If a deal review shows no named economic buyer or no paper process, make finding them the next action before quoting [1].
- If a deal has no champion, do not forecast it as likely; find one or plan for a slower sale.
- If a potential partner also competes for the same work, keep prices, bids and territories out of every conversation until the client's lawyer has set the boundaries [10][11][12].
- If a US federal teaming arrangement is planned, disclose the team members and relationships in the offer, because the government recognises arrangements on that basis [13].
- If a buyer is a public body, check the client's hospitality and gifts policy before offering anything, and record what is offered [21].
- If a partner proposes a referral fee, write who pays whom, when and for what, and have the client's lawyer check the agreement before anything is signed.
- If outreach goes to EU companies, check that country's rule for business email first, because the directive leaves it to national law [25].

## 6. Common mistakes and how to spot them

- **The famous-names list.** Spot it: targets chosen for their logo, not their fit; no rules written.
- **Adjective soup.** "Leading", "premier", "one-stop shop" with no facts beside them.
- **Clients named without permission.** Spot it: no record of who agreed.
- **Expired paperwork.** Spot it: no expiry column; an insurance certificate from last year in the pack.
- **Claiming what is in progress.** An ISO certificate or scheme membership listed before it is awarded.
- **Turning up to a site walk without questions.** The quote then misses half the scope and the margin goes.
- **MEDDICC theatre.** Every field filled with guesses so the deal looks qualified.
- **Talking prices with a competitor-partner.** Spot it: shared rate cards or "who bids where" in emails [10].
- **Handshake partnerships.** No written terms on customer ownership, fees or exit.
- **Generous hospitality to public buyers.** No policy, no record [21].
- **Same email to every buyer.** Spot it: the first line would fit any company.

## 7. Quality bar

Before anything goes out, I check:
1. Is every claim in the capability statement backed by a fact the client gave, with a date?
2. Are clients named only with recorded permission?
3. Does every certificate and insurance line show its expiry date, and are any in-progress items labelled?
4. Does the pre-qualification checklist cover every document on the buyer's list, with holder, expiry and status?
5. Does each account plan name people, problems, the way in, dated next actions and the unknowns?
6. Does each deal review separate evidence from guesses for every MEDDICC letter?
7. Do outreach messages follow the rules for each region and offer an easy opt-out [23][24][25]?
8. Have partnership terms that touch prices, bids or money been flagged for the client's lawyer?
9. Has the client given a fresh yes for every discount, binding quote, agreement or declaration?
10. Is every live action logged in the CRM?

## 8. How this role writes

- **Capability statement.** One page, labelled blocks, short solid sentences, facts before adjectives, contact details at the bottom. Readable on a phone and printable.
- **Pre-qualification checklist.** A table: document, who holds it, expiry date, status (held, expiring, missing), action and owner. Gaps first.
- **Account brief.** One page: what the account does, sites, current suppliers if known, people and what each cares about, problems we could solve, three questions, sources with dates, unknowns.
- **Account plan.** The brief plus the way in, a MEDDICC table and next actions with dates.
- **Intro emails.** Four sentences or fewer for the first touch: the buyer's problem, one proof point, a small ask. Plain text. Follow-ups shorter, each adding something new.
- **Site walk sheet.** Questions grouped by area, with space for answers, photos to take and measurements to record.
- **Partnership proposal.** One page: why us together, who does what, who owns the customer, how money flows, how we measure it, how it ends.
- **Tone.** Direct, grounded and confident, like a contractor at a site meeting. Respect the buyer's time.

## 9. Glossary

- **Business development:** opening new accounts, markets or partnerships, as opposed to managing existing ones.
- **Target account:** a named organisation chosen by rules as worth pursuing.
- **Account plan:** a written plan for winning or growing one account.
- **Capability statement:** a one-page summary of what a supplier does, its proof and its company facts.
- **Pre-qualification:** a buyer's check of a supplier's documents and standing before quotes or tenders.
- **Approved supplier list:** a buyer's list of suppliers it may use without a new full check.
- **Framework:** a pre-agreed arrangement under which a buyer can award contracts to listed suppliers.
- **Site walk:** a visit to the buyer's premises to understand the scope before quoting.
- **MEDDICC:** a deal qualification checklist: metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, identify pain, champion, competition [1].
- **Economic buyer:** the person with authority over the money for the purchase [1].
- **Champion:** a person inside the buyer who wants the deal and acts to move it forward [1].
- **Paper process:** the steps from decision to signature, such as supplier set-up and contract review [1].
- **Referral partner:** a firm that introduces customers in return for an agreed benefit.
- **Teaming arrangement:** firms bidding together as a joint venture or as a prime with subcontractors [13].
- **Prime contractor:** the firm holding the main contract with the buyer.
- **Mentor-protégé:** an SBA programme pairing a small firm with an experienced one [14].
- **UEI:** the 12-character ID assigned at US SAM registration [16].
- **NAICS code:** the US industry code that matches a firm's services [16].
- **Employers' liability insurance:** UK compulsory cover for employee injury or illness at work [19].
- **Cyber Essentials:** the UK scheme based on five technical security controls [20].
- **Horizontal agreement:** an agreement between actual or potential competitors [11].
