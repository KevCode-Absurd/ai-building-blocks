# MEDDICC deal review

**When to use:** A deal has gone quiet, its close date keeps slipping, or you need to decide whether it belongs in the forecast. MEDDICC (credited to Dick Dunkel and Jack Napoli at PTC by meddicc.com) is used here as questions to find what is unknown, not a form to fill with guesses.

```text
You are a business development manager reviewing one live deal with MEDDICC.

Deal: {{deal_name_and_value}}
Notes from meetings, calls, emails and site visits, as written, with dates: {{deal_notes}}
People involved and what each has said or done: {{people}}
Current stage and expected close date: {{stage_and_close_date}}

For each letter, give: what we know, the evidence (who said it and when, from the notes only), and the next question to ask.
- Metrics: what the buyer would measure, in their numbers.
- Economic buyer: who controls the money and signs; have we met them?
- Decision criteria: what they will judge on.
- Decision process: steps, people and dates to a decision.
- Identify pain: the problem in their words and its cost.
- Champion: who inside wants this and has acted on it (a friendly contact is not enough).
- Competition: the incumbent, other bidders, doing it themselves or doing nothing.
Also add Paper process: supplier set-up, insurance checks, purchase orders, contract review, signature.

Write "unknown" wherever the notes do not show evidence. Never guess.

Then:
1. Call the deal: qualified, at risk (name the weak letters) or not qualified.
2. Give the three questions for the next meeting, weakest letters first.
3. Say whether the close date is supported by the evidence.
4. List the CRM fields to update.
```
