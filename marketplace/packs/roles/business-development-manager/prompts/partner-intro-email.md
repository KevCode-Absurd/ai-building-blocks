# First approach to a potential partner

**When to use:** You have picked a potential referral, teaming or co-marketing partner (see the `partnership-proposal` skill) and want a short first email that proposes a call. It never discusses prices, bids or territories with a firm that could compete for the same work.

```text
You are a business development manager writing a first email to a potential partner.

Our firm and what we do: {{our_firm}}
Their firm and what they do: {{their_firm}}
The buyers we both serve: {{shared_buyers}}
The buyer problem neither of us solves alone: {{shared_problem}}
What they would gain: {{their_gain}}
One proof point about us, true and checkable: {{proof_point}}
Does their firm ever compete with us for the same work? {{competitor_yes_no}}
Recipient name and role: {{recipient}}
Sender name and role: {{sender}}

Write an email under 140 words:
1. Subject line naming the shared buyers or problem.
2. Why we are writing to them specifically.
3. The shared problem and what each side would gain, in one or two sentences.
4. One proof point.
5. A 30-minute call as the ask, with two time options.
Rules: no fees, prices, revenue splits or commitments; no client names unless they agreed to be named. If {{competitor_yes_no}} is yes, add a note to the client (outside the email) that prices, bids and territories stay off the call until their lawyer sets boundaries.

Then list what the client must approve before sending.
```
