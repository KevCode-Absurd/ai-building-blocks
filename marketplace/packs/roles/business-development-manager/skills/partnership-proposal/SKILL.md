# Partnership proposal

Many partnerships fail quietly: a handshake, a logo swap, then nothing. This skill picks partners for a reason, writes down who does what and how money moves, and flags the legal points before anyone signs.

## When to use

- A firm wants introductions from businesses that already serve its buyers (a referral partner).
- Two or more firms want to bid together for work none could win alone (teaming or a joint venture).
- A small firm wants to subcontract to a larger prime on a bigger contract.
- Two firms want to market together to a shared audience.

For reseller programmes, distributor margins and vendor partner incentives, use the `channel-sales-manager` kit.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the client's CRM, past deals and notes on candidate partners. Level 2 to draft the proposal, the terms checklist and partner records in the CRM. Level 3 to create and update partner records, log meetings and send approved intro emails to the named partner contacts within the client's limits. Sending, signing or accepting any partnership, referral, teaming or NDA agreement, and naming any referral fee payee, needs a fresh yes every time.

- What the client wants from a partnership: introductions, capacity, a capability they lack, access to a framework or contract.
- What the client offers in return.
- A list of candidate partners, or the kinds of firms to look for.
- Any terms already discussed.
- Whether any candidate also competes for the same work.

## Steps

1. **Set the goal.** One sentence: what the partnership must produce, by when. "Three introductions a quarter to property managers we do not reach today."
2. **List and score candidates.** For each: do they serve the same buyers without competing for the same work? What do they gain? Can they deliver to the client's standard? Do they have the paperwork the buyer needs? Are they a potential competitor (if yes, flag it)?
3. **Choose the type.** Referral (they introduce, you deliver); teaming (one leads as prime with subcontractors, or a joint venture); subcontracting to a prime; co-marketing. Say why this type fits the goal.
4. **Check the rules that apply.**
   - **Competitors.** If the partner could compete for the same work, keep prices, bids, territories and customer allocation out of all talks until the client's lawyer sets boundaries. In the US, price fixing, bid rigging and dividing customers or markets are treated as always illegal; the UK and EU publish guidance on agreements between competitors.
   - **US federal bids.** Team arrangements are recognised when team members and their relationships are disclosed in the offer. Under SBA's Mentor-Protégé Program, a small protégé and a mentor may form a joint venture to bid as small, with the protégé doing at least 40% of the work. Point the client to SBA and their lawyer for eligibility.
   - **EU public bids.** Groups may bid without forming a company first; the buyer can require a legal form after award if needed.
   - **UK public supply chains.** Large central government primes must show they pay supply chain invoices promptly, which helps a subcontractor judge a prime.
   - **Bribery and hospitality.** Referral fees and hospitality must follow the client's policy and be recorded; in the UK a company can be liable for bribery by people acting for it.
5. **Write the one-page proposal.** Why us together (the buyer problem neither solves alone), who does what, who owns the customer relationship, how leads are shared and recorded, how money flows (referral fee, subcontract price, revenue split) as options for the client to set, how success is measured, review dates, and how either side can end it.
6. **Write the terms checklist.** Questions for the client's lawyer: customer ownership and non-solicitation, confidentiality, referral fee terms and who pays whom, liability and insurance, data sharing, use of each other's names and logos, competition-law boundaries, exit and notice. The agent lists the questions; the lawyer answers them.
7. **Draft the first approach.** A short email to the partner contact: the shared buyer problem, what each side gains, one proof point, and a 30-minute call. Use the `partner-intro-email` prompt.
8. **Set up tracking.** [L3] Create the partner record, the agreed measures and review dates in the CRM, and log every introduction both ways.
9. **Review.** At each review date, compare results with the goal and recommend continue, change or end.

## Checks

- The goal is specific and has a date.
- Each candidate's gain is as clear as the client's.
- Potential competitors are flagged, and no draft discusses prices, bids or territories with them.
- Money flows are options for the client to decide, not commitments.
- Every legal point is a question for the lawyer, not a ruling.
- Nothing is sent or signed without the client's fresh yes.

## Output format

1. Goal (one sentence).
2. Candidate table: partner, shared buyers, what they gain, fit, paperwork, competitor flag, score.
3. Recommended type and why.
4. Rules flags (as relevant).
5. One-page partnership proposal.
6. Terms checklist for the client's lawyer.
7. Draft first approach.
8. Measures and review dates.

## Example of good output

*Made-up business for illustration: Keystep Locksmiths, proposing a referral partnership with a made-up commercial cleaning firm, Glenmarsh Clean.*

**Goal.** Six introductions to facilities managers in the next two quarters.

**Why together.** Glenmarsh cleans 40 offices a week and hears about broken locks and lost keys first; Keystep fixes them same day. Neither competes for the other's work.

**Who does what.** Glenmarsh mentions Keystep when a site reports a lock or access problem and passes the contact with permission. Keystep quotes and delivers, and tells Glenmarsh the outcome.

**Customer ownership.** Each firm keeps its own customers; neither solicits the other's services.

**Money (options for the client).** A) A fixed thank-you fee per completed first job. B) No fee; reciprocal introductions. C) A co-branded leaflet for building managers, costs shared.

**Measures.** Introductions made, first jobs completed, repeat jobs. Review at 90 days.

**Ending it.** Either side ends with 30 days' written notice.

**For the lawyer:** referral fee terms and invoicing; non-solicitation wording; use of each other's names; data shared when passing contacts.
