# Site walk and first visit

Service contracts are often lost, or won at a loss, on the quote: a missed area, an unasked question, a buyer who expected more. This skill prepares the visit so the quote covers what the buyer expects, and confirms the scope in writing before any price goes out.

## When to use

- A first meeting or site visit is booked with a commercial buyer.
- A buyer has asked for a quote for a site the firm has not seen.
- A past quote was lost or turned out to be under-priced because the scope was wrong.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the CRM record, the buyer's emails and any specification. Level 2 to draft the question sheet, visit plan and scope confirmation, and CRM notes. Level 3 to book or confirm the visit, log it in the CRM and send the approved scope confirmation to the named buyer contact within the client's limits. Sending a quote the client treats as binding, or any discount, needs a fresh yes every time.

- What the buyer has said so far, in their words.
- The site address, size and type, and any floor plans or specification.
- The firm's services and what it cannot do.
- Who from the firm will attend, and who from the buyer.

## Steps

1. **Read what the buyer has said.** Note their stated problem, the current supplier if known, the contract end date if known, and anything they have asked for in writing.
2. **Build the question sheet by area.** Adapt to the trade; for a cleaning or grounds contract, typical areas are:
   - **Access and security:** opening hours, keys or fobs, sign-in, alarms, restricted areas, vetting the buyer requires.
   - **Hours and timing:** when work can happen, quiet periods, events, holidays.
   - **Assets and surfaces:** floor types, glass, plant rooms, grounds, equipment the firm must use or avoid.
   - **Waste and consumables:** who supplies, where waste goes, recycling rules.
   - **Health and safety:** site rules, hazards, lone working, risk assessments and method statements the buyer expects.
   - **Standards and reporting:** what a good job looks like to them, how they check it, how complaints are logged, how often they want reports.
   - **Commercial:** current contract end date, how they choose a supplier, who signs, purchase order and payment process, insurance levels required.
3. **Plan the visit.** Who attends, what to bring (question sheet, capability statement, insurance certificate copy), photos and measurements to take, and the order of the walk.
4. **Ask, then listen.** Open with the buyer's problem, then let them lead the walk. The buyer should do most of the talking. Write down their words, especially about what annoys them today.
5. **Record what you see.** Photos (with the buyer's permission), measurements, counts (rooms, windows, bins, square metres), and anything that changes effort: access delays, hazards, special surfaces.
6. **Close with next steps.** Confirm what happens next, when the scope confirmation arrives, and who will decide.
7. **Write the scope confirmation the same day.** What we understood: areas, tasks, frequencies, hours, standards, reporting, and anything excluded. Ask the buyer to correct it before a price is prepared. [L3] Send the approved confirmation to the named contact.
8. **Log the visit.** [L3] Notes, people met, their concerns in their words, photos and measurements filed, next step and date in the CRM. Update the account plan and any MEDDICC fields (see the `account-plan-and-meddicc` skill).
9. **Hand to pricing.** Pass the confirmed scope to whoever prices. [Fresh yes] Sending a binding quote or any discount.

## Checks

- Every area on the sheet has an answer or is marked "ask".
- The buyer's own words are recorded for their main problem.
- Exclusions are written down, not assumed.
- Photos were taken only with the buyer's permission, and no people's faces or personal information appear in them.
- The scope confirmation went to the buyer before any price.
- Nothing in the confirmation promises a standard the firm cannot staff.

## Output format

1. Visit plan: who, when, what to bring, walk order, photos and measurements to take.
2. Question sheet by area, with space for answers.
3. Scope confirmation email draft (areas, tasks, frequencies, hours, standards, reporting, exclusions).
4. CRM visit note.

## Example of good output

*Made-up business for illustration: Brindlecote Cleaning, visiting a made-up four-storey office managed by Quarrow Estates.*

**Question sheet (extract)**

| Area | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Who holds keys out of hours, and how do our staff sign in? | |
| Hours | Can cleaning start at 6 p.m., or must floors be empty first? | |
| Surfaces | Which floors are carpet, and which are vinyl or stone? | |
| Standards | What does a good week look like to you? How do you check? | |
| Reporting | How do tenants raise complaints today, and how fast do you need a fix? | |
| Commercial | When does the current contract end, and who signs the new one? | |

**Scope confirmation (draft):** "Thanks for the walk today. Here is what we understood: daily cleaning of floors 1 to 4 (offices, kitchens, toilets), weekly stairwell deep clean, monthly internal glass, 6 p.m. to 9 p.m. weekdays. Excluded: external windows, car park. You check weekly with a walkround; complaints go through the tenant portal and you want a same-day response. Please correct anything we have wrong, and we will prepare a price by Friday."
