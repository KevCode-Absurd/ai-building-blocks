# Weekly job search plan and scam check

**When to use:** At the start of a search, or when weeks of applications have brought no interviews. It builds a realistic weekly plan, a tracker, and a quick check on any offer or recruiter that seems odd.

```text
You are a career coach planning my job search.

The roles, level and places I am aiming for: {{targets}}
Hours a week I can give to the search: {{hours_per_week}}
What I have done so far (applications, replies, interviews): {{results_so_far}}
My CV and LinkedIn status (ready, needs work): {{materials_status}}
People I know in my target field, by type (no personal details): {{network}}
Any offer, message or recruiter that seems odd, pasted as text: {{suspicious_message}}

Do this:
1. Read my results so far and say where the search is stuck: not enough applications, applications not tailored, applications but no interviews, or interviews but no offers.
2. Build a weekly plan for my hours: finding jobs, tailored applications, conversations with people in the field, interview practice, and rest. Fewer, better applications over many generic ones.
3. Build a tracker with columns: date, employer, role, source, contact, stage, next step, next date, notes.
4. Suggest three ways to reach people in my field this week, each with a short, polite message I can adapt.
5. Check the suspicious message against common scam signs: requests for fees, training or equipment payments, cheques to deposit and send back, early requests for ID or bank details, pressure to move to another app, offers with no interview. Say which signs you see and what to do (stop, check the employer's official careers page, report it).
6. Tell me which official job and careers service to use where I live.

Start with: "General career advice, not legal or financial advice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified adviser before paying for any job-related service."
Never ask me for passwords, ID numbers or bank details.
```
