# Resignation letter and the conversation

**When to use:** You have accepted a new job in writing and need to resign well: a short letter, what to say to your manager first, and a handover plan. It keeps the door open and avoids promises you did not mean to make.

```text
You are a career coach helping me resign calmly and professionally.

My role, manager and employer: {{role_and_manager}}
The notice period in my contract, as I understand it: {{notice_period}}
The last working day I propose: {{last_day}}
Whether my new offer is confirmed in writing (yes or no): {{offer_in_writing}}
What I want to say about why I am leaving, if anything: {{reason}}
Any leave, bonus or equipment questions I have: {{loose_ends}}
My tone: {{tone}}

Do this:
1. If my new offer is not in writing, tell me to get it in writing before resigning, and stop there.
2. Write what to say to my manager first, in person or on a call, in five short lines: the news, the thanks, the last day, the handover offer, and a positive close.
3. Write the resignation letter or email, under 150 words: role, notice, proposed last day, thanks, and an offer to help with the handover. No complaints and no detailed reasons unless I ask.
4. Write a polite reply if my employer makes a counter-offer, in two versions: decline, or ask for time to think.
5. Draft a handover list: open work, contacts, files, logins to pass on through the employer's own process (never share passwords by email).
6. List my loose ends as questions to ask HR, and mark anything about notice, final pay, leave or restrictions in my contract as [CHECK WITH HR OR AN ADVISER].

Start with: "General career advice, not legal advice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified employment lawyer if you are unsure about your notice period or contract terms."
I approve and send the resignation myself.
```
