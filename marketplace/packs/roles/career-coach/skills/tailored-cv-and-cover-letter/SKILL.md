# Tailored CV and cover letter

## When to use

- The person has a job ad and wants their CV and letter aimed at it.
- The CV lists duties, not results, or runs to four pages.
- A career changer needs to show transferable evidence.
- Someone is returning after a break and does not know how to show it.
- An application form asks for statements against criteria with word limits.

Do not use it to invent or stretch experience, to write a CV for someone else to pass off as theirs, or to answer questions about visas or the right to work. This is general career advice: check this with local rules, and ask a qualified employment lawyer or a regulated immigration adviser where the law decides.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the CV, the job ad, past applications and notes in the person's documents.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** create the tailored CV, letter and answers as new documents, and save a covering email in drafts.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** update the master CV with facts the person confirmed, export final PDFs, and email the CV to a named recruiter the person approved. Submitting through job portals stays with the person.

## What you need

1. The current CV, or a list of jobs with dates, and any old versions.
2. The full job ad, and the employer's website or careers page if relevant.
3. The country and sector the person is applying in.
4. Results the person can stand behind: numbers, before and after, awards, feedback.
5. Any gap, career change or worry the person wants handled.
6. The employer's rules on AI use in applications, if published.

## Steps

### 1. Inventory the history

List every role, date, qualification, tool, skill and result the person gave. Do not add anything. Mark each result as "number given" or [add a number].

### 2. Read the ad for the top needs

Pick the five to eight things the employer cares about most: essential criteria, repeated words, the first lines of the duties. Write each as a need in plain words.

### 3. Match needs to evidence

Make a table: need, best evidence from the history, strength (strong, partial, none). Every "none" goes on the gap list for the person, not on the CV. A partial match is written honestly ("supported", "part of a team that").

### 4. Check the country norms

- UK: no age, date of birth, marital status or nationality; clear font, size 11 or larger.
- US: no photo and no personal details such as age.
- EU: norms vary; some countries expect a photo. Ask where they are applying.
- Public sector: use the employer's criteria and the form's word limits.

### 5. Write the summary

Three to four lines: who the person is professionally, their strongest proof, and the role they want. No buzzwords.

### 6. Rewrite each role

Two to five bullets per recent role, fewer for older ones. Each bullet: a verb, what the person did, how much, and what changed. Use the person's numbers; leave [add a number] where one is needed but missing. Put the bullets that match the top needs first.

### 7. Handle gaps and changes honestly

- A break: one line with dates and what the person did or learned, if they want to say.
- A career change: a short "relevant experience" section that leads with transferable evidence.
- No gap is hidden by changing dates or job titles.

### 8. Fit and format

Reverse date order. Two pages unless the norm differs (academic CVs, some public sector forms). A plain layout with standard headings, so it reads well in an applicant tracking system and on a phone.

### 9. Write the cover letter

Under 400 words. Open with the role and why this person. Give one or two results tied to the top needs. Show you know what the employer does. Close with a clear next step. Use the person's voice.

### 10. Answer criteria and questions

One answer per question, in order, inside the word limit, each using a STAR example: situation and task briefly, the person's own action, the result.

## Checks

- Every fact, title, date and number is the person's own.
- Keywords from the ad appear only where the history supports them.
- No gap hidden; gap list delivered.
- Country norms followed.
- The employer's AI rules followed.
- The person reads and approves the final version before it is used.
- The output carries the line: *General career advice, not legal or immigration advice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified adviser where the law decides.*

## Output format

1. Needs-to-evidence table with the gap list.
2. The tailored CV.
3. The cover letter.
4. Answers to application questions, with word counts.
5. A list of what changed from the old CV and why.
6. Questions for the person (missing numbers, unclear dates).

## Example of good output (short)

Made-up person for illustration: a garden centre assistant manager applying for a UK retail store manager job.

> **Needs and evidence**
>
> | Need in the ad | Evidence | Strength |
> |---|---|---|
> | Grow sales | Plant sales up 18% in spring 2026 after re-laying the entrance displays | Strong |
> | Lead a team of 20+ | Leads 12 staff on weekends; deputises for manager | Partial |
> | Stock control | [add a number: stock loss before and after?] | Partial |
> | P&L ownership | None yet | Gap |
>
> **Bullet before:** "Responsible for plant area displays."
> **Bullet after:** "Redesigned entrance plant displays and weekly promotions; plant sales rose 18% year on year in spring 2026."
>
> **Gap list:** Ask your manager to involve you in next quarter's budget review, so you can show P&L experience.
>
> *General career advice, not legal or immigration advice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified adviser where the law decides.*
