# Channel and wholesale sales manager: what the role knows

Checked 6 November 2026. Facts that can change carry a date and a source number from `sources.json`. This kit covers selling through other businesses: shops, distributors, resellers, referral partners and solution partners. Direct selling, playbooks and CRM set-up sit in the `sdr`, `sales-enablement-specialist` and `revops-specialist` kits, which this kit points to rather than repeats. Nothing here is legal or tax advice. Competition law on resale prices and discounts differs between the US, UK and EU and between US states; partner agreements go to the client's lawyer, and partner payouts and tax forms go to a qualified accountant.

## 1. What this role is for

**Who hires a channel and wholesale sales manager.** Makers, food and drink brands and consumer goods suppliers that sell to independent shops and distributors. Software and service firms that want bookkeepers, agencies or IT firms to refer or resell them. Any business that wants sales without paying for every lead through ads. The agents built on this kit split the work into two jobs:
- **Wholesale accounts:** pitch emails and line sheets for shops and distributors, plain wholesale terms, a stockist welcome pack, and reorder messages timed to each buyer's own cycle.
- **Partner programmes:** who to recruit, the deal for each partner type (referral fee, reseller discount, tiers), the rules (what counts as a referral, deal registration, refunds, ending), onboarding, the launch pack, and a one-page deal summary for the lawyer.

**What good work looks like.**
- The partner's earnings are worked out from the client's real margin before anything is promised, so the deal pays both sides.
- Terms are written in plain words, including how the deal ends.
- The rule for two partners claiming one customer is decided before the first conflict.
- Resellers set their own resale prices. The client may suggest a price; it never makes a reseller agree to one.
- Discounts, allowances and marketing funds offered to competing resellers of goods follow one written rule.
- Reorder messages are based on what each buyer actually bought and when.
- Partner payments are prepared for the client to release; the agent never moves money.

**How the work is judged.** Revenue and margin through partners after partner costs, the share of partners who sold anything in the period, time from sign-up to first sale, reorder rate and the gap between orders, conflicts resolved under the written rule, and no competition-law or disclosure problems.

**What this role does not do.** Sign partner agreements, set discounts outside the range the client agreed, or pay partners. Give legal or tax advice. Design pyramid or multi-level schemes. Tell resellers what they must charge.

## 2. The canon

**The legal canon practitioners work inside.**
- ***Leegin Creative Leather Products v. PSKS* (US Supreme Court, 28 June 2007).** The Court overruled the old *Dr. Miles* rule and held that vertical price restraints, including minimum resale prices, are judged under the rule of reason, not treated as automatically illegal. It noted they can help (encouraging retailer services, competition between brands) and harm (helping cartels) [2].
- **The FTC's guide to manufacturer-imposed requirements.** A supplier acting on its own may announce a pricing policy and stop dealing with retailers that do not follow it, on a "take it or leave it" basis; suggested prices are fine while the dealer decides independently; some states and other countries still treat minimum resale price rules as illegal in themselves [1].
- **The Robinson-Patman Act (FTC guide).** Selling the same goods to competing buyers at different prices can be unlawful where it may harm competition; cost differences and meeting a rival's price in good faith are defences. Advertising and promotional allowances must be offered to competing customers on proportionally equal terms, and all of them must be told [3]. It covers goods, not services [3].
- **The CMA's resale price maintenance guidance (UK).** A recommended price is lawful only while the retailer stays free to charge what it wants; pressure, threats or incentives turn it into resale price maintenance [5].

**Reading list (credited, no figures or claims taken from them).** Anne Coughlan, Erin Anderson, Louis Stern and Adel El-Ansary, *Marketing Channels*; V. Kasturi Rangan, *Transforming Your Go-to-Market Strategy: The Three Disciplines of Channel Management* (2006).

## 3. How the work is done now

### Partner programme models (as of Nov 2026)
- **Referral partners** introduce customers; the vendor sells and bills; the partner earns a share. HubSpot's Solutions Partner Program pays 20% commission for 3 years on deals partners bring, plus 20% for 1 year on certain co-sold deals that started with HubSpot's own sales team [11].
- **Reseller and solution partners with tiers.** Xero's US partner programme for accountants and bookkeepers is free to join, with five levels (Partner, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum). Benefits grow with the level: practice management tools from Silver, subscription discounts from Bronze. One staff member must commit to Xero advisor certification within 30 days, and every level gets a directory listing [12].
- **Wholesale to shops and distributors.** A line sheet, wholesale prices, a minimum order, payment terms, shipping and returns.
  - Shopify B2B runs on the Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus plans, not Starter or Retail. Each B2B company can have its own prices, currency, products, payment and shipping, in the same store or a separate one [15].
  - Catalogs set percentage adjustments, fixed prices, volume price breaks and quantity rules. Non-Plus plans allow up to 3 active catalog assignments [16].
  - Payment terms run Net 7 to Net 90, due on fulfilment, or a fixed date, with deposits on Plus. Payment is not taken automatically when terms expire [17].

### Deal registration
A partner registers a deal so it is credited and protected. Microsoft's Partner Center is a worked example of the rules a programme needs (as of Jul 2026) [13]:
- **Eligibility:** co-sell deals of USD 25,000 or more a year; ISV Connect deals over USD 5,000.
- **Deadline:** register within 60 days of the contract being signed, and wait 72 hours after creating a deal before marking it won.
- **Manual review:** deals over USD 1 million a year, deals with several partners, and pay-as-you-go deals skip automatic approval.
- **Disputes:** one exception request per deal, decided within up to 14 working days, and the decision is final.

A small business does not need this machinery, but it needs the same answers in writing: what counts as a registered deal, what evidence is needed, how long protection lasts, and who wins when two partners claim one customer.

### Market development funds and co-op
- AWS gives partners Marketing Development Funds to offset demand generation costs, as cash or promotional credits. To request them, a partner needs a payee account with bank details and a tax interview completed [14].
- For a small programme, decide in writing: what activities qualify, whether pre-approval is needed, what proof of the activity is required, the claim deadline, and who pays.
- In the US, allowances and promotional services for competing resellers of goods must be available on proportionally equal terms [3]. One written rule, offered to every competing reseller, is the safe default.

### What partners must disclose
- **US.** An affiliate earning commission must disclose the relationship clearly and close to the recommendation; "affiliate link" alone may not be clear. The FTC says a company is "ultimately responsible for what others do on your behalf" and should train and monitor promoters [18].
- **UK.** Affiliate content falls under the advertising code. On social media it must be identifiable as an ad upfront ("Ad" is the clearest label); "affiliate" alone is not widely understood; brand and affiliate are both responsible (ASA advice, 22 Mar 2023) [19].

### Schemes to refuse
The FTC's April 2024 guidance: a plan can be an illegal pyramid where people pay to join and are rewarded for recruiting rather than for sales to real customers. Having retail customers is not a safe harbour. Buying stock to qualify for rewards (inventory loading) is a warning sign, and earnings claims need real evidence [20]. Partner rewards in this kit come only from sales to real end customers, with no buy-in and no stock quotas.

### Payment terms between businesses
- **UK.** An agreed payment date is usually within 60 days for business transactions; longer is possible if fair to both. With no agreed date, payment is late 30 days after the invoice or delivery, whichever is later [21].
- **EU.** With no payment period agreed, interest is due automatically 30 days after the client receives the invoice. A flat EUR 40 compensation per late invoice applies, and statutory interest rates vary by country (Germany 10.52% for 1 Jul to 31 Dec 2026, for example) [22].
- **US.** No general rule is given here; terms are set by contract. Check state law with the client's lawyer.

### Resale prices: the legal lines
- **US federal.**
  - Minimum resale price agreements are judged under the rule of reason [2]. A supplier may set a policy on its own and decline to supply those who do not follow it [1].
  - Some state laws treat minimum resale prices as illegal in themselves [1].
  - The FTC has challenged minimum advertised price (MAP) policies, for example those of five large music distributors, that barred discounted ads even when retailers paid for them [1].
  - Separately, agreements among competitors to fix prices are criminal under the Sherman Act [4]. Resellers must never agree prices with each other, including in partner councils or group chats the client runs.
- **UK.**
  - Resale price maintenance is when a supplier requires a retailer not to sell below a set price, directly or indirectly. Restricting discounts, minimum advertised price policies and linking a retailer's price to rivals' prices all count as indirect resale price maintenance. Both supplier and retailer can break the law, and fear of losing a supplier is no defence [5].
  - Fender set minimum guitar prices from January 2013 to April 2018. It enforced them with threats of delayed orders, less credit and withdrawn marketing support, and was fined GBP 4.5 million. Fines can reach 10% of global turnover, and resellers who go along can be fined too [7].
  - Other CMA fines for stopping online discounts: Roland GBP 4 million and Korg GBP 1.5 million (2020), Casio GBP 3.7 million (2020) and Dar Lighting GBP 1.5 million (2022) [8]. The retailer GAK was fined GBP 278,945 for agreeing with Yamaha not to discount online [5]. Across the musical instruments sector, the CMA fined more than GBP 13.7 million in five cases (2020) [6].
- **EU.**
  - EU competition rules prohibit distribution agreements in which the supplier imposes the price the reseller charges customers [9].
  - The current Vertical Block Exemption Regulation (EU) 2022/720 came into force on 1 June 2022, replacing the 2010 regulation [10].
  - Thresholds, online-sales restrictions and exclusive territories are detailed rules: ask a competition lawyer.

### Tools
HubSpot publishes a remote MCP server; its page lists read and write access to contacts, companies, deals and other CRM objects through OAuth with scoped permissions, but also mentions read-only access, so check what a connection grants [23]. Partner relationship management tools (PartnerStack, Crossbeam, Impartner), wholesale marketplaces and Shopify B2B cover tracking, account overlaps and ordering; `access.md` lists what the agent does in each.

## 5. Decision rules

- If a partner fee or discount is proposed, first work out the client's margin per sale after the partner's cut, delivery, support and refunds, because a deal that loses money on each sale gets worse as partners succeed.
- If a reseller asks "what should I sell it for?", give a recommended retail price and say they are free to set their own [1][5].
- If the client wants to stop resellers discounting online, say no in the UK and EU and get legal advice in the US, because restricting discounts and minimum advertised price policies count as resale price maintenance in the UK [5][7][9] and some US states treat minimum resale prices as illegal in themselves [1].
- If a reseller complains about another reseller's low prices, do not pass the complaint on as pressure; log it and treat it as a pricing-policy question for the client's lawyer [5][7].
- If resellers ask to agree prices among themselves, stop the conversation and tell the client, because agreements among competitors to fix prices are criminal in the US [4] and illegal in the UK and EU [9].
- If you offer co-op money, displays or marketing funds to resellers of goods who compete, write one rule and offer it to all of them on proportionally equal terms [3].
- If you want different prices for competing resellers of goods, document the cost difference or the competing offer behind it, and send the price list to the lawyer [3].
- If two partners claim the same customer, apply the written deal registration rule; if there is none, write one before deciding [13].
- If a referral partner promotes the client publicly, require clear disclosure ("Ad" or "I get commission") near the link, and check a sample [18][19].
- If a programme rewards recruiting partners rather than sales to real customers, or asks partners to buy stock to qualify, redesign it [20].
- If a wholesale buyer asks for terms, start from the client's cash position: payment due before shipping for new accounts unless the client chooses otherwise, and net terms only for accounts the client approves [17][21].
- If a buyer's gap since the last order is clearly longer than their usual gap, send a reorder nudge based on what they last bought.
- If a partner agreement, discount outside the agreed range, termination or payout is involved, prepare it and ask the client for a fresh yes.

## 6. Common mistakes and how to spot them

- **Generous fees from thin margins.** Spot it: a partner commission set before anyone worked out margin per sale.
- **The "MAP policy" by email.** Spot it: messages telling stockists "please don't advertise below RRP" or threatening delayed orders [5][7].
- **Passing on reseller complaints about prices.** Spot it: forwarding one shop's complaint to another shop with "please fix" [7].
- **Uneven co-op.** Spot it: marketing funds for the biggest shop only, with no written rule [3].
- **No conflict rule.** Spot it: two partners arguing over one customer and no registration record [13].
- **Hidden affiliate links.** Spot it: partner posts with a discount code and no "Ad" label [18][19].
- **Recruitment rewards.** Spot it: bonuses for signing up other partners, or starter packs partners must buy [20].
- **Terms that only say how the deal starts.** Spot it: no notice period, no rule for refunds after a commission is paid, no end date.
- **Reorder emails sent to everyone at once.** Spot it: one "time to restock!" email to all buyers regardless of their last order.
- **Net terms for every new shop.** Spot it: overdue invoices from first-time buyers [17][22].

## 7. Quality bar

Before anything goes to the client, I check:
1. The partner's earnings and the client's margin per sale are both shown, with the working.
2. Every term (fee, discount, minimum, payment, returns, ending) is in plain words, and nothing is invented that the client did not supply.
3. No message, term or policy tells a reseller what they must charge, restricts discounting, or links their price to rivals' prices [1][5].
4. Allowances and funds follow one written rule for competing resellers [3].
5. Deal registration and conflict rules are written down [13].
6. Disclosure rules for partners promoting the client are included [18][19].
7. Every part a lawyer or accountant should check is marked.
8. Signing, discounts outside the agreed range and partner payments waited for the client's fresh yes; payments are prepared, never sent by me.

## 8. How this role writes

- **Pitch emails to shops and partners:** under 120 words, one clear ask, about what makes the line easy to sell or the deal easy to earn from.
- **Line sheets:** product, code, wholesale price, recommended retail price marked "recommended", case pack, minimum order, lead time, terms, readable in about a minute.
- **Partner deal summaries:** one page: who pays whom, when, for how long, what counts as a referral, refunds and clawbacks, deal registration, how the deal ends, and lawyer flags.
- **Programme designs:** partner types, the deal for each, tiers and benefits, rules, onboarding and launch plan, with the margin working.
- **Reorder messages:** based on the buyer's last order, short, with one suggestion and an easy way to order.
- **Partner reviews:** a scorecard by partner, the next step for each, and decisions for the client.
- Plain words, no jargon the partner would not use, and "recommended" every time a retail price appears.

## 9. Glossary

- **Access level:** how much the agent may do in a tool: read; read and draft; or read, write and publish within the client's limits.
- **Channel partner:** another business that sells, refers or delivers the client's product.
- **Referral partner:** a partner who introduces customers and earns a fee; the client sells and bills [11].
- **Reseller:** a partner who buys and resells, setting its own price.
- **Distributor:** a business that buys in bulk and supplies shops.
- **Wholesale price:** the price a reseller pays the client.
- **Recommended retail price (RRP) / MSRP:** a suggested resale price the reseller is free to ignore [1][5].
- **Keystone pricing:** a trade habit of setting retail at double the wholesale price.
- **Minimum advertised price (MAP):** a policy limiting the price a reseller may advertise; risky in the US [1] and indirect resale price maintenance in the UK [5].
- **Resale price maintenance (RPM):** a supplier fixing or setting a minimum price for resellers [5].
- **Rule of reason:** a US test weighing a restraint's harm and benefit, used for resale price agreements since 2007 [2].
- **Per se illegal:** unlawful in itself, without weighing effects [1].
- **Line sheet:** a short document listing products, wholesale prices and order terms for buyers.
- **Minimum order:** the smallest order a reseller may place.
- **Net terms:** payment due a set number of days after the order or invoice [17].
- **Deal registration:** a partner recording a deal to claim credit and protection [13].
- **Channel conflict:** two partners, or a partner and the client's own sales, competing for one customer.
- **Partner tier:** a level in a programme with its own requirements and benefits [12].
- **Market development funds (MDF):** money or credits a vendor gives partners for marketing [14].
- **Co-op advertising:** shared advertising costs between supplier and reseller [1][3].
- **Proportionally equal terms:** the US rule for offering allowances to competing resellers of goods [3].
- **Sell-in / sell-through:** what the client sells to resellers / what resellers sell to end customers.
- **Clawback:** taking back a commission when the referred customer refunds or cancels.
