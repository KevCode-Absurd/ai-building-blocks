# Partner onboarding and scorecard

## When to use

- Partners sign up but few ever bring a customer.
- The client cannot tell which partners are worth time and which have gone quiet.
- Two partners claim the same customer.
- Commission or rebate payments are due and need checking.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** partner list, referrals, deals with source, payouts and commission reports.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft onboarding emails and training, update partner records as pending, prepare the payout file and the conflict decision for review.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send approved onboarding emails, approve partners and deal registrations that meet the written rules, and update partner status. Ending a partner, exceptions to the rules and every payment need the client: a fresh yes for the first two, and payments stay with the client.

## What you need

1. The programme rules: deal, tiers, registration and conflict rules, funds, disclosure.
2. Partner list with join date, type, tier and contact owner.
3. Referrals and deals by partner, with dates and values.
4. Commissions owed and paid.
5. Product materials: demo, pitch deck, FAQ, pricing.

## Steps

### 1. Write the onboarding checklist

What a new partner gets in week one: welcome email, login to the partner tool, the deal summary in plain words, the disclosure rule for public promotion, a short product training, the pitch and FAQ, a demo account or samples, and one named contact.

### 2. Plan the first 30 days

- **Days 1 to 7:** training done, materials received, first call with the partner contact.
- **Days 8 to 21:** the partner names the first customers they could introduce; the client helps with the first conversation if asked.
- **Days 22 to 30:** first referral or deal registered, or a call to find what is in the way.

If the programme has a certification step, put it here (Xero, for example, asks partners to commit to certification within 30 days; see `knowledge.md`, section 3).

### 3. Build the scorecard

Per partner, per quarter:
- Deals registered and won, and revenue and margin after the partner's share.
- Active or not (any registered deal or sale in the period).
- Days from joining to first deal.
- Conflicts raised and how they were settled.
- Disclosure: a spot check of public posts with the client's links.
- Support load: tickets from the partner's customers, if the client tracks it.

### 4. Settle conflicts under the rule

When two partners claim one customer, or a partner and the client's own sales do, apply the written registration rule: who registered first, with what evidence, within the protection period. Write the decision and the reason for the client. If there is no rule, draft one for the client to approve before deciding.

### 5. Prepare payouts

From won deals and the programme terms: commission per deal, clawbacks for refunds or cancellations, funds claims with proof. Produce a payout file with the working. The client checks it and releases payment; I never send money or change payout details. Tax forms for partner payouts go to a qualified accountant.

### 6. Run the quarterly review

Group partners: growing, steady, new, quiet. For each group, one action: more support for growing partners, a check-in for quiet ones, a decision on partners who have never sold. Recommend tier changes against the written criteria.

### 7. Watch for warning signs

A partner pressing others on price, public posts without disclosure, a partner asking for exclusive territory not in the agreement, or rewards being paid for recruiting other partners. Report each to the client.

### 8. Research (both modes)

With web search connected, check that partners' public pages describe the client accurately and carry the disclosure label. Without it, ask partners to send links to their posts.

## Checks

- Every partner has a named contact and a 30-day plan.
- Scorecard numbers trace to the CRM or partner tool.
- Conflicts are settled under the written rule, with the reason recorded.
- The payout file shows the working, clawbacks and claims; payment is left to the client.
- No message to a partner tells them what to charge or asks them to report another partner's prices.
- Ending a partner waits for the client's fresh yes.

## Output format

1. Onboarding checklist and 30-day plan.
2. Partner scorecard table.
3. Conflict decisions with reasons.
4. Payout file with working, for the client to release.
5. Quarterly review: groups, actions, tier changes.
6. Warning signs and decisions for the client.
