# Wholesale terms and line sheet

## When to use

- A maker or brand is about to approach independent shops or distributors.
- Buyers keep asking about minimums, payment and returns.
- Wholesale terms were never written down, or differ from buyer to buyer.
- The business is opening a B2B store (for example Shopify B2B) and needs catalogs and terms to set up.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** price list, cost data, order history and current B2B settings.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** build catalogs and price lists not assigned to anyone, draft the line sheet, pitch and welcome pack.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish the agreed terms page, assign approved catalogs and payment terms to approved accounts, and send the approved pitch to the agreed list. Net terms for a new account, discounts outside the agreed range, and any new list need a fresh yes.

## What you need

1. Price list: retail price, wholesale price, unit cost, case pack.
2. Delivery cost per order and who pays it.
3. Current or wanted minimum order, payment terms and returns policy.
4. Best sellers and what makes the line easy to sell (margin, reorder rate, display, story).
5. Lead times and stock levels.
6. Countries the buyers are in.

## Steps

### 1. Check the margin per unit

For each product: wholesale price minus unit cost minus delivery and packing per unit at the minimum order. Flag any product that loses money at the minimum, or leaves nothing for promotions. Show the retailer's margin too, from the recommended retail price, so the pitch can say what a shop earns per unit.

### 2. Set the minimum order

Large enough that the order covers picking, packing and delivery with a profit, small enough for a first-time shop to try. Offer a lower reorder minimum if it helps reorders.

### 3. Set payment terms

Start from the client's cash: payment before shipping for new accounts is the cautious default. Net terms (for example Net 30) only for accounts the client approves. In Shopify B2B, terms range from Net 7 to Net 90, plus due on fulfilment or a fixed date, and payment is not collected automatically when terms expire (see `knowledge.md`, section 3). For UK and EU buyers, note the late payment rules in `knowledge.md`, section 3.

### 4. Write shipping, returns and lead times

Who pays shipping and from what order size, damaged-goods process with a photo deadline, whether unsold stock can be returned (usually not, unless the client offers it), and lead times by product.

### 5. Write about retail prices carefully

Show a recommended retail price, labelled "recommended". Never write "must sell at", "minimum price", "do not discount" or "do not advertise below". In the UK, restricting discounts or advertised prices counts as resale price maintenance; in the US, some states treat minimum resale prices as illegal in themselves (see `knowledge.md`, section 3). If the client insists on price control, stop and send them to a lawyer.

### 6. Write the line sheet

One page per collection: product photo placeholder, name, code, wholesale price, recommended retail price, case pack, minimum, lead time. Terms in a short box at the bottom. Readable in about a minute.

### 7. Write the pitch email

Under 120 words, to one named buyer type: what makes the line easy to sell, the retailer's margin per unit, the minimum, one proof (a stockist, a reorder rate, a press mention the client supplies), and one ask (request the line sheet, or a call). No invented stockists or numbers.

### 8. Write the welcome pack

How to order and reorder, who to contact, display tips, product facts for shop staff, and the first-order offer if the client has one.

### 9. Research (both modes)

With web search connected, check how similar brands present wholesale terms on their public trade pages and the current Shopify B2B or marketplace features, with dates. Without it, work from what the client supplies and mark comparisons as unchecked.

## Checks

- Every product makes money at the minimum order, or is flagged.
- Payment terms match the client's cash; net terms only for approved accounts.
- Every retail price is labelled "recommended"; no wording restricts discounting or advertised prices.
- No invented stockists, reviews or numbers.
- The pitch is under 120 words with one ask.
- Lawyer points marked: returns, liability for damaged goods, governing law for overseas buyers.

## Output format

1. Margin table by product (client and retailer margin per unit).
2. One-page terms summary.
3. Line sheet text.
4. Pitch email.
5. Welcome pack.
6. B2B store set-up notes (catalogs, terms, minimums), if the client sells online.
7. Facts to confirm and lawyer points.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a maker of hand-poured candles selling to independent gift shops. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Terms summary**
> - Minimum first order: USD 300. Reorders: USD 150.
> - Payment: card at order for first orders. Net 30 for repeat accounts we approve.
> - Shipping: free over USD 500; otherwise at cost.
> - Damage: send a photo within 7 days of delivery and we replace it.
> - Recommended retail prices are shown on the line sheet. You set your own prices.
>
> **Pitch (87 words)**
> Hi Sam, I make hand-poured soy candles in Asheville. Gift shops tell us they reorder because customers come back for the same scent. Our 8 oz jar has a recommended retail price of USD 28 and costs you USD 14, so you earn USD 14 a jar. First orders start at USD 300, about 21 jars across four scents, and we replace anything that arrives damaged. Could I send you our line sheet and a sample of our best seller, Cedar and Smoke? Thanks, Jo at [Business]
