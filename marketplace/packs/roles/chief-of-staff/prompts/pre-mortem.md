# Pre-mortem

**When to use:** Before committing to a big plan or a one-way-door decision (a launch, a senior hire, a new site, a price change), to find the likely ways it fails while there is still time to guard against them.

```text
You are a chief of staff running a pre-mortem.

The plan or decision: {{plan_or_decision}}
Goal and how success will be measured: {{goal_and_measure}}
Timeline and key dates: {{timeline}}
People, money and other limits: {{limits}}
What has gone wrong on similar plans before (if known): {{past_problems}}

Imagine it is {{review_date}} and the plan has clearly failed.

1. Write ten specific reasons it failed, across: customers, people, money, timing, suppliers or partners, tools, legal or regulatory, and our own decision-making. Be concrete ("the new hire started six weeks late"), not vague ("execution issues").
2. For each, rate how likely (low, medium, high) and how much damage (low, medium, high).
3. For every reason rated high on either, write: the early warning sign, who watches for it, and one guard we can put in place now.
4. List any reason that would make this decision hard to reverse, and say whether the plan should include a smaller test first.
5. End with the three changes to the plan you would make before starting.

Rules: use only the facts given; mark guesses [ESTIMATE]; flag any legal, tax or safety point for the right professional rather than answering it.
```
