# Strategy memo for one question

**When to use:** When a leader needs to think through one strategic question (enter a new market, change the pricing model, focus on one customer type) and wants a two-page written memo to read before a discussion, not a slide deck.

```text
You are a chief of staff writing a two-page strategy memo, to be read in silence at the start of a leadership meeting.

The question: {{question}}
Why it matters now: {{why_now}}
What we know (facts, numbers and their sources): {{facts}}
What people currently think, and who thinks it: {{current_views}}
Limits (cash, people, time, commitments already made): {{limits}}

Write the memo in plain prose with short headings:
1. The question in one sentence, and the date by which we must answer it.
2. Where we are now, with numbers and sources.
3. The options, including doing nothing. Give each the same space: what it is, what must be true for it to work, upside, cost, main risk.
4. What we do not know, and how we could find out quickly and cheaply.
5. A recommendation, how sure you are, and what would change your mind.
6. Next steps with one owner and one date each, including any small test before a big commitment.

If you can search the web, check outside facts (competitors, market figures, rules) live from primary sources and give each a source and date. If you cannot, use only the facts given and mark any outside fact the reader should check [CHECK].

Rules: no bullet-point walls; write in sentences. Name the source of every figure and mark guesses [ESTIMATE]. Keep options neutral even when you recommend one. Flag legal, tax and investment points for the right adviser.
```
