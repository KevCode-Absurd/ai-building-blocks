# Training interview: chief of staff

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share names, figures, board papers or confidential decisions from real employers or clients; anonymise every story.

1. **Which leaders have you worked for (company size, stage, sector), and what did they hire you to fix?**
   Why it matters: sets the kind of leader and business the agent is built for.

2. **In your first month with a new leader, what do you do, week by week?**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's start-up routine.

3. **How do you find out what the leader actually cares about, as opposed to what they say?**
   Why it matters: a chief of staff works from real priorities; the expert's questions reveal them.

4. **Walk me through how you cut a long priority list. What do you say to the person whose project gets parked?**
   Why it matters: cutting is the core skill; the wording keeps trust.

5. **Show me an OKR set you were proud of, anonymised. What made it good?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a model for objectives and key results.

6. **What makes a key result bad? Give me three you rewrote and how.**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's rewrite checklist.

7. **How do you run a weekly check-in so it takes little time and still catches problems early?**
   Why it matters: the check-in format is what keeps goals alive between quarters.

8. **How do you decide whether a decision needs a full brief or a quick call?**
   Why it matters: sorting decisions by how reversible they are saves the leader's time.

9. **How do you stay neutral when you have a strong view on the options?**
   Why it matters: neutrality is the role's currency; the agent needs the habits that protect it.

10. **Tell me about a decision that kept coming back. How did you finally close it?**
    Why it matters: teaches escalation and decision logging in practice.

11. **What goes in your board pack, in what order, and what do you cut?**
    Why it matters: board packs are a core deliverable; structure varies by board.

12. **When did you last have to put bad news in a board or investor update? How did you write it?**
    Why it matters: shows how to frame problems with cause and plan.

13. **Which numbers do you insist on in every monthly report, and how do you stop definitions drifting?**
    Why it matters: consistent definitions make reports trustworthy over time.

14. **How do you run the weekly leadership meeting? What did you change after it went badly?**
    Why it matters: the weekly meeting is the heart of the rhythm.

15. **Which meetings have you killed, and how did you get people to agree?**
    Why it matters: meeting cuts need tact as well as rules.

16. **What do you send in the leader's name, and where is your line? Has it ever gone wrong?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's limits for messages in the leader's name.

17. **How do you keep cross-team projects from colliding? What do you track each week?**
    Why it matters: cross-team visibility is a common reason this role is hired.

18. **Which tools do you use for goals, decisions and packs, and what do you change in them yourself?**
    Why it matters: turns the three access levels into real practice and limits.

19. **Where does your job end and the company secretary's, lawyer's, accountant's or HR adviser's begin?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's referral rules.

20. **Tell me about the hardest quarter you have supported a leader through. What did you do differently?**
    Why it matters: a full story shows judgement under pressure that short answers miss.
