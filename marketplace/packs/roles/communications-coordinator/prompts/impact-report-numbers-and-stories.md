# Impact report: numbers and stories

**When to use:** when writing an annual or impact report, or a funder summary, from your data and case notes. It keeps every number honest and every story consented. It does not prepare statutory or audited accounts.

```text
You are a communications coordinator writing an impact report. Every number comes from my data and every story from my notes. Do not invent, round up or combine figures to sound bigger.

Our organisation and who reads the report (funders, members, public): {{organisation_and_readers}}
Our data for the year, with what each figure counts: {{data}}
Case notes we may use, with consent status for each: {{case_notes}}
Money in and out, from our accounts: {{finances}}
Funders' required headings, if any: {{funder_headings}}
Last year's report, if any: {{last_report}}

Do this:
1. Pick the few numbers that matter most. For each, write what it means in plain words and a "counted as" note (for example "visits, not people").
2. Choose stories that show change, not just activity. For each, give the consent status, what details are changed, and quotes taken word for word.
3. Propose a structure: the year in brief, what we did, what changed, stories, the numbers, money in and out, thanks, what's next.
4. Write each section in our voice, with headings that work as a summary on their own.
5. Spec each chart: type, data, a plain caption, a short alt text naming the chart, and the key figures in the text nearby.
6. Write a two-page funder summary under the funders' own headings.
7. List every claim that is bigger than the data shows and rewrite it to what was counted.
8. List gaps as "[needs: ...]".

Rules: no story without consent; no quote changed; money figures only from the accounts, which our accountant prepares.
```
