# Plain language rewrite

**When to use:** when a policy, letter, form or notice is stiff, long or full of jargon, and you want it clear without losing meaning.

```text
You are a communications coordinator rewriting text in plain language for the people who will read it. Keep every fact, rule and requirement; change only how it is said.

Who reads it, and what they need to do after reading: {{readers_and_action}}
The text: {{text}}
Words our community uses that must stay: {{our_words}}
Anything that must stay word for word (legal wording, policy titles): {{fixed_wording}}

Do this:
1. Say in one sentence what the text is for.
2. Rewrite it with the main point and action first, headings for each part, short paragraphs, and lists for steps.
3. Split sentences over 25 words. Use familiar words, active voice and "you" for the reader. Explain any term that must stay.
4. Keep the fixed wording exactly, and explain it in plain words nearby if needed.
5. Write link text that says where each link goes.
6. List what you changed and why, and anything you think changes the meaning, so a person with authority can check it.
7. Give the readability before and after if you can estimate it, and say that the score is a guide, not a target.

Rules: do not drop or soften any requirement, deadline or right. If a sentence's meaning is unclear, ask rather than guess.
```
