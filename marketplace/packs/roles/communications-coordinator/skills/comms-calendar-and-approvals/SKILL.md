# Comms calendar and approvals

Most communication failures are planning failures: two big asks in one week, a closure notice nobody approved in time, a website that still shows last term's dates. This skill puts every message on one calendar and gives every message a clear path from draft to send.

## When to use

- A new term, season, year or campaign is starting.
- Messages clash, go out late, or say different things on different channels.
- Nobody is sure who approved a message, or approval takes too long.
- A new coordinator is taking over and needs the system written down.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the organisation's calendar, past messages and channel results. Level 2 to build the calendar in the client's shared sheet, planner or project tool, and to add draft items. Level 3 to update the live calendar and send approved reminders to approvers within the client's limits. The calendar itself publishes nothing.

- The fixed dates: term dates, services and festivals, closures, events, fee and form deadlines, report and funder dates.
- The channels in use and who runs each.
- The people who approve, and for which kinds of message.
- Any rules from leaders, governors, trustees or owners about what needs their sign-off.
- Past results, if any: which messages got replies, forms back or attendance.

## Steps

1. **List the channels and their jobs.** For example: newsletter (round-up), text or app message (urgent), website (what people look up later), notices read aloud (this week), social (life and invitations), press (news for outsiders), staff email or chat (internal). Note who runs each and how far ahead it needs content.
2. **Add fixed dates first.** Then regular slots (weekly notices, monthly newsletter), then one-offs. For each item: date, channel, audience, one-line message, owner, approver, status, link.
3. **Check the load.** Flag weeks with several big asks of the same people (forms, money, volunteering) and move what can move. Flag gaps where people will wonder what is happening.
4. **Match urgent items to more than one channel.** Closures, safety notices and deadlines go out by more than one channel with identical facts, and on the website.
5. **Write the sign-off chain.** A table of message types (routine notice, newsletter, website change, social post, press note, all-staff announcement, all-customer or all-parent message, formal statement, sensitive message) against who drafts, who checks facts, who approves, who sends, and who can stop it.
6. **Mark the fresh-yes messages.** Formal statements and anything to all staff or all customers, members or parents always need the decider's fresh yes, even if level 3 access covers routine sending. So does anything about complaints, incidents, safeguarding, disputes, money owed or someone's job. Note who stands in when the decider is away.
7. **Set approval times.** Agree with the client how long before send each approver needs a draft, and what happens if no answer comes (the message waits; it is never sent on silence).
8. **Record approvals.** For each message: the version, approver, date and channel. Keep the record next to the calendar.
9. **Hand off to other kits.** Press pitches and crisis statements go to the PR specialist kit; social growth plans to the social media manager kit; marketing email programmes to the email marketing kit.
10. **Review monthly.** What went out, what was late, what caused repeat questions, and what to change.

## Checks

- Every item has an owner, an approver and a status.
- Facts for the same event match across every channel.
- Urgent items use more than one channel.
- Fresh-yes messages are marked, with the decider and a stand-in named.
- No message is set to send on silence.
- Approval record exists for every sent message.

## Output format

1. Calendar table: date, channel, audience, message, owner, approver, status, link.
2. Channel guide: channel, job, who runs it, lead time.
3. Sign-off matrix: message type against draft, check, approve, send, stop, fresh yes.
4. Load and gap notes for the period.
5. Approval record template.

## Example of good output (short)

*St Brendan's Primary is a made-up school used only to show the format.*

**Calendar (extract)**

| Date | Channel | Audience | Message | Owner | Approver | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon 3 Mar | Newsletter | All parents | Book week, trip forms due Fri | Office | Head | Draft |
| Mon 3 Mar | Parent app | Year 4 parents | Trip form and payment by Fri 7 Mar | Office | Year 4 lead | Approved |
| Thu 6 Mar | Website | Public | Open morning page live | Office | Head | Scheduled |
| Fri 7 Mar | Staff email | All staff | Inspection prep timetable | Deputy | Head (fresh yes) | Waiting |

**Sign-off matrix (extract)**

| Message type | Drafts | Checks facts | Approves | Sends | Fresh yes every time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly newsletter | Coordinator | Office manager | Head | Coordinator (level 3) | No |
| All-parent message | Coordinator | Office manager | Head | Coordinator after yes | Yes |
| Statement to local press | Head | Chair of governors | Head and chair | Head | Yes |

**Load note:** week of 3 March asks Year 4 parents for a form, a payment and volunteers. Move the volunteer ask to the following week.
