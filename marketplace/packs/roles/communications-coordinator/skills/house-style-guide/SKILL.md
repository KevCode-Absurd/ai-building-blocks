# House style guide

When five people write for one organisation, readers get five voices, three ways of writing a date and two names for the same room. A house style guide fixes that in a few pages, built from how the organisation already sounds at its best.

## When to use

- Several people write messages for the same organisation.
- Messages sound different from each other, or contradict each other on small things.
- A new coordinator, volunteer or staff member is starting.
- The organisation's name, structure or services changed.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read past newsletters, notices, the website and any brand files. Level 2 to save the guide as a draft document and add comments or suggestions to drafts. Level 3 to update the shared guide and templates within the client's limits.

- A good spread of past messages, including some the client likes and some they do not.
- Any existing brand or style rules.
- The community's own words: services and festivals, year groups and classes, rooms and buildings, staff and leader titles.
- Words to avoid, from the client and from past complaints.

## Steps

1. **Read the past messages.** Mark lines that sound most like the organisation at its best, and lines that jar.
2. **Describe the voice.** Three or four traits with a "this, not that" for each (for example "warm, not gushing"). Voice stays the same; tone changes with the moment (a celebration, a reminder, bad news), as Mailchimp's public style guide explains. Give one example line per tone.
3. **List the community's words.** The exact terms for services, festivals, groups, rooms and roles, with spelling and capitals. In faith communities, use the tradition's own words and let leaders confirm them. In schools, the school's names for year groups and classes.
4. **List words to avoid,** with a better choice for each: jargon, in-group acronyms, pressure words ("mandatory", "you should attend"), labels for children ("naughty", "behind"), and anything that has caused complaints.
5. **Set the mechanics.** Choose one rule for each and write it down. GOV.UK's A to Z style guide is a good public model, for example:
   - dates: "Friday 14 March", no "14th", no comma before the year;
   - times: "3:15pm", "10am to 11am", "midday" not "12 noon";
   - numbers: numerals from 2, commas over 999, "%" for percentages;
   - "and" not "&", "select" not "click";
   - explain abbreviations on first use; sentence case; no block capitals;
   - link text that says where it goes.
6. **Names and titles.** How to write staff, leader and volunteer names and titles; whether to use first names; how to refer to children (placeholders in templates; first names only with consent in published pieces).
7. **Formats by message type.** For the newsletter, weekly notices, read-aloud notices, welcome messages, reminders, announcements and social posts: the order of parts, typical length the client prefers, sign-off, and where the call to action goes.
8. **Accessibility rules.** Headings, alt text, contrast, real text not images, link text (see the accessible-documents-and-emails skill).
9. **Keep it short.** A guide people use beats a long one they do not. Put the most-used rules on the first page.
10. **Check drafts against it.** Mark only the lines that break the guide, give the fix and the rule, and keep a list of repeat slips to discuss with the writers.
11. **Review it** when the organisation changes, or each year.

## Checks

- Every rule has an example.
- Community words are confirmed by the people who own them.
- No rule conflicts with another.
- The guide fits on a few pages, with a one-page summary.
- Draft checks change only what breaks a rule.

## Output format

1. One-page summary: voice, the most-used rules and the community words.
2. Full guide: voice and tone, our words, words to avoid, mechanics, names and titles, formats by message type, accessibility.
3. Draft check table: line, rule broken, fix.
4. Repeat slips list.

## Example of good output (short)

*Northfield Food Bank is a made-up charity used only to show the format.*

**Voice:** warm, not gushing; direct, not blunt; hopeful, not cheerful about hardship.

**Our words**

| Say | Not | Why |
|---|---|---|
| guests | clients, the needy | Our volunteers' word; respectful |
| food parcel | handout | Avoids charity-giving tone |
| Tuesday and Friday sessions | opening hours | What guests call them |

**Mechanics (extract):** "Tuesday 4 March, 10am to midday". "Northfield Food Bank" in full the first time, then "the food bank". No "&". Sentence case in headings.

**Draft check**

| Line | Rule | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "The needy can collect parcels Tues 10-12." | Our words; dates and times | "Guests can collect food parcels on Tuesday, 10am to midday." |
