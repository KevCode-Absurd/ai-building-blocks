# Newsletters and notices

People read these messages tired, on a phone, between other things. This skill writes them so the reader knows in one glance what is happening and what to do, in words that sound like the organisation and respect everyone it mentions.

## When to use

- The weekly notice sheet or monthly newsletter is due.
- A newcomer, new family or new member needs a welcome and next steps.
- A policy needs a reminder (pick-up, illness, fees, photos, parking).
- Parents need progress notes or daily updates written from staff notes.
- Staff need an announcement about a change.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read past messages, the calendar and policies. Level 2 to save the newsletter as an unsent campaign, the website news item as a draft, or the message in drafts. Level 3 to send or publish approved messages within the client's limits. Anything to all staff or all customers, members or parents, and any formal statement, needs the decider's fresh yes each time.

- The facts for each item: what, when, where, who for, cost, what to bring, how to reply.
- The house style guide, or three past messages the client likes.
- The community's own words (for services, year groups, rooms, roles).
- Staff notes for updates, with permission to use any story.
- The photo and name consent list for any images.

## Steps

1. **Sort the items.** Put them in order of what readers must act on, then what they should know, then what is nice to know. Anything urgent also goes out separately.
2. **Check the facts.** Dates, times, places, costs and names against the calendar and the organiser. List gaps as "[needs: ...]" and do not guess.
3. **Lead with the point.** Each item opens with what is happening and what to do: "Trip forms are due on Friday 7 March. Return them to the office or the app." Then the detail.
4. **Write plainly.** Short sentences (split anything over 25 words), familiar words, active voice, terms explained, no in-group acronyms. Use the community's words for itself. Warm, never pushy.
5. **Make it scannable.** Clear headings, short paragraphs, dates in bold, one link per action with link text that says where it goes.
6. **Write the read-aloud version** for notices read in a service, assembly or meeting: shorter, slower rhythm, no links or brackets, numbers said as spoken ("a quarter past three"). Read it aloud with a timer and cut until it fits the slot the client gives.
7. **Handle people with care.**
   - Children: placeholders for names, observations not labels, nothing that could embarrass them, and never a named child in a group message about behaviour or health.
   - Members and newcomers: one next step per message, no pressure.
   - Staff: say what changes, when, why, and who to ask; route anything about someone's job to the manager or HR first.
8. **Photos.** Check every photo against the consent list for this use (newsletter, website, social). No consent, no photo.
9. **Policy reminders.** Rule, one-line reason, what to do, and who to ask. Kind wording, never "you failed to".
10. **Parent updates.** Three parts from staff notes: what went well, what we are working on, something to try at home. No grade promises and no diagnosis; anything touching health, development or safeguarding goes to the professional who owns it.
11. **Accessibility pass.** Real text not images of text, alt text, headings, contrast, link text (use the accessible-documents-and-emails skill).
12. **Approval and send.** Send the draft to the approver named in the sign-off chain, with any gaps listed. Send at level 3 only after approval and within limits; log it.

## Checks

- First line of each item says the point and the action.
- Every fact matches the calendar and other channels.
- No sentence over 25 words; terms explained.
- Read-aloud version fits the slot when read aloud.
- No child named or described beyond what staff supplied; photos consented.
- Fresh yes on file for all-staff, all-customer and formal messages.

## Output format

1. Newsletter or notice text, in order of action.
2. Read-aloud version, with its timed length.
3. Short version for app, text or social, with a link.
4. Gaps and facts to confirm.
5. Approval request with the approver's name.

## Example of good output (short)

*Riverside Community Church is a made-up community used only to show the format.*

**This week**

**Harvest lunch: Sunday 12 October, after the 10:30am service.** Everyone is welcome, including first-time visitors. Bring a dish to share if you can, or just bring yourself. Lunch is in the hall, which has step-free access from the car park.

**Youth group starts again on Friday 17 October, 7pm to 8:30pm.** For ages 11 to 16. Parents, please fill in the new contact form before the first night: [Youth group form].

**Read-aloud version (about one minute)**

"Next Sunday, the twelfth, we have our harvest lunch straight after the half past ten service. Everyone is welcome. If you can bring a dish to share, wonderful; if not, just come. The hall has step-free access from the car park.

Youth group is back on Friday the seventeenth, from seven until half past eight, for ages eleven to sixteen. Parents, the new contact form is on the website and on the welcome desk."

**To confirm:** who is leading youth group this term [needs: name for the contact line].
