# Interview: Communications coordinator

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you give me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work.

## The organisations and readers

1. **Which kinds of organisations do you write for, and who reads their messages?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and the readers it writes for.
2. **What do readers most often get wrong or ask about after a message goes out? Give me a recent example.**
   Why it matters: repeat questions show where messages fail, and how the expert fixes them.
3. **How do you learn a community's own words before you write for it?**
   Why it matters: using the wrong word for a service, room or role loses trust fast.

## Planning and approvals

4. **Show me how you plan a term or season. What goes on the calendar first?**
   Why it matters: the expert's calendar method becomes the agent's.
5. **Who signs off what in the organisations you work with, and what happens when an approver is away?**
   Why it matters: sign-off chains keep messages safe without making them late.
6. **Which messages do you never send without the head, minister, manager or owner seeing the final version?**
   Why it matters: defines the fresh-yes list the agent must respect.
7. **Tell me about a message that went out wrong. What happened next, and what did you change?**
   Why it matters: real recovery steps and the safeguards that followed.

## Writing

8. **How do you decide what goes first in a newsletter?**
   Why it matters: order decides whether people act.
9. **Read me a notice you are proud of. Why does it work?**
   Why it matters: a real example teaches tone and structure.
10. **How do you write a reminder about a rule people keep breaking without sounding cross?**
    Why it matters: hard messages written kindly are a core skill.
11. **How do you write about children, members or staff so nobody is embarrassed?**
    Why it matters: dignity and privacy rules the agent must follow.
12. **What changes when you write something to be read aloud?**
    Why it matters: read-aloud notices are common in faith and school settings.

## Style, plain language and access

13. **What is in your house style guide, and which rules do people break most?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent real rules and the slips to watch for.
14. **How do you check that a message is plain enough? Do you use any tools or tests?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert measures clarity in practice.
15. **What do you check for accessibility before sending an email or publishing a document?**
    Why it matters: the expert's checklist becomes the agent's.
16. **How do you handle photos and names: consent, children, people who opt out?**
    Why it matters: consent mistakes are hard to undo.

## Reports and results

17. **When you write an annual or impact report, how do you keep numbers honest and stories consented?**
    Why it matters: protects the client's credibility with funders and members.
18. **How do you know whether your messages are working?**
    Why it matters: keeps the agent focused on action, not open rates.

## Clients and hard jobs

19. **What does a client do that makes your job harder, and how do you handle it?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's intake and boundaries.
20. **Tell me about the hardest message you have had to write. What did you do?**
    Why it matters: shows judgement under pressure.
