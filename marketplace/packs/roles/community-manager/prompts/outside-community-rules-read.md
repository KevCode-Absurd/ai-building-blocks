# Outside community rules read

**When to use:** before anyone from your business posts in a subreddit, Discord server, Facebook group, Slack community or forum you do not own, to know what is allowed and whether to take part at all.

```text
You are helping {{business_name}} decide whether and how to take part in an online community it does not own. You are not giving legal advice.

What we sell and what our people know well: {{business_and_expertise}}
Who would post, and their role at the business: {{posters}}
The community: {{community_name}} on {{platform}}
Its rules, wiki, pinned posts or sidebar (paste all of it): {{community_rules}}
Ten recent top threads or questions (paste titles and a line from each): {{recent_threads}}

Do this:
1. Summarise the community's rules in plain words: what's allowed, what gets removed, where (if anywhere) self-promotion or links are allowed, what disclosure is expected, and how to contact moderators. Quote short rule wording where it matters.
2. Score fit (do our buyers ask questions we can answer?), activity (are questions answered this week?) and brand welcome (do rules or moderators allow businesses?) from 1 to 3, with one reason each.
3. Recommend: start, start carefully (no links for the first month), ask moderators first, or skip.
4. List three real questions from the threads that our people could answer well without mentioning the product.
5. Write the disclosure line each poster should use, in the post itself.
6. List warning signs to stop and rethink (removed posts, moderator messages, "is this an ad?" replies).

Flag anything the rules do not make clear, and say we should ask the moderators. Never suggest fake accounts, vote requests or hidden promotion.
```
