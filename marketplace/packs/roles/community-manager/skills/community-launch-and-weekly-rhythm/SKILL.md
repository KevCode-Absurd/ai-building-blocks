# Community launch and weekly rhythm

Most communities do not die from a crisis. They fade: a burst of posts at launch, then the founder stops posting and nobody else starts. This skill builds the structure that keeps members talking to each other: a clear purpose, a welcome that gets a first post, a weekly pattern members can predict, and replies that pull other members in.

## When to use

- Launching a new community on Discord, Slack, Circle, Facebook Groups, Discourse or a forum.
- Relaunching a group that has gone quiet.
- The owner or manager writes most posts and members only react.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the community and its numbers. Level 2 to place drafts in a staff space. Level 3 to publish posts, run the welcome flow and reply, in the spaces and for the period the client sets.

- Who the members are and what they come to do.
- Platform, current spaces or channels, and member numbers by month (new, posting, returning).
- Ten recent posts or questions, and which got replies.
- Who can host events, and the hours they have each week.
- The client's house rules (see the house-rules-and-moderation-ladder skill if there are none).

## Steps

1. **Purpose line.** "A place for [who] to [do what] together." If the client gives two purposes, ask which wins.
2. **Prune the spaces.** Start with few spaces or channels: welcome and rules, introductions, the main discussion, and one for the most common need (help, show your work, events). Empty spaces make a community look dead. Add a space only when one topic crowds the main feed.
3. **Welcome flow.**
   - A welcome message that says what the community is for, the top three rules, and where to start.
   - An introduction prompt with an easy, specific question ("What are you working on this month?" beats "Tell us about yourself").
   - One small first task that gives a quick win: post a photo of your setup, ask one question, react to three introductions.
   - A reply to every introduction by name, with a link to one useful thread or member.
4. **Pick the weekly formats.** Three or four repeating formats on fixed days. Common ones: a weekly question, a share thread (wins, work in progress, questions), a member spotlight, and an event or office hour. Choose formats that ask little of members, since lowering the effort to contribute is how Nielsen suggests shaping the 90-9-1 pattern.
5. **Write eight weeks of posts.** For each week and format: the post text, the question it asks, and who hosts. Rotate topics across the purpose. Keep each post to one question.
6. **Reply habits.** Answer the question asked. Link to an earlier answer when there is one. Invite a member who knows ("Priya solved this last month; Priya, any tips?"). Thank people for answering each other. Move disputes and personal matters to private messages.
7. **Recognise contributors moderately.** Spotlight helpful members by name, rotate who is featured, and avoid rewards that hand the room to the loudest few.
8. **Flag for a person.** Safety, harassment, legal threats, refunds and anything involving money go to the owner, not into a public reply.
9. **Monthly health read.** From the client's counts: new members, members who posted for the first time, returning members, questions answered by another member, reports, removals and appeals. Compare with last month and with the same month's joiners last quarter. Pick one or two changes.
10. **Plan the relaunch, if needed.** Announce the change, archive empty spaces, run a two-week "reintroduce yourself" thread, and personally invite the twenty most helpful past members.

## Checks

- The purpose line is one sentence and every format serves it.
- Every new member gets a welcome, a first task and a reply.
- Each weekly post asks one easy question.
- The schedule fits the hours the client actually has.
- No fake members, seeded posts or bought activity.
- Health numbers come from the client's data, with the period stated.
- Level 3 posting stays in the spaces and dates the client set, and every post is logged.

## Output format

1. **Purpose line.**
2. **Spaces or channels:** name, what goes there, and what to archive.
3. **Welcome flow:** welcome message, introduction prompt, first task, reply template.
4. **Weekly rhythm:** a table of day, format, host.
5. **Eight weeks of posts:** text for each format and week.
6. **Reply habits:** five lines the team can follow.
7. **Monthly health read:** the counts to pull and the template.
8. **What needs the client's approval** before launch.

## Example of good output (short)

*Ledgerly is a made-up bookkeeping app with a Slack community of 640 customers, used only to show the format. Numbers are invented.*

**Purpose:** A place for small-business owners using Ledgerly to get their books done faster, together.

**Spaces:** #welcome-and-rules, #introductions, #ask-anything, #month-end (closing the month), #events. Archive #random and #feature-ideas-old (no posts in 90 days).

**Introduction prompt:** "Welcome! What kind of business do you run, and what's one bookkeeping task you'd happily never do again?"

**First task:** "Post one question in #ask-anything this week, even a small one. Someone here has probably solved it."

**Weekly rhythm**

| Day | Format | Host |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Month-end check-in thread | Agent at level 3 |
| Wednesday | "Ask an accountant" 30-minute office hour | Partner accountant |
| Friday | Small win of the week | Agent at level 3 |
| Last Thursday | Member spotlight | Community lead |

**Week 1, Monday post:** "Month-end check-in: what's left on your list before you can close October? Reply with one item, and if you can help someone else with theirs, jump in."

**Reply habit:** "Good question, Jo. Malik asked something similar in September (link). Malik, did the bank-rule fix work for you?"

**Health read (last month):** 52 new members, 19 posted for the first time (37%), 41% of questions answered by another member. Change: add the first-task line to the welcome message and track first posts again next month.

**Note:** this community is on Slack's free plan, so members only see 90 days of history. Ask the owner whether month-end answers should also live in a help doc.
