# House rules and moderation ladder

Moderation goes wrong in two ways: rules nobody understands, and actions nobody can explain afterwards. This skill fixes both. It produces short rules with reasons, a ladder that says exactly what happens for each problem, messages that tell members what happened and how to appeal, and a log. It also says, in writing, which actions an agent may take alone and which need a fresh yes.

## When to use

- A new community needs rules before launch.
- Members complain about unfair or silent removals.
- Moderators disagree about what to do with the same kind of post.
- After an incident: a raid, a pile-on, spam, a threat.
- Before the client grants level 3 moderation access, so the limits are written down.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the community and past moderation. Level 2 to post the draft rules in a staff space. Level 3 to publish the rules and act under them, within the limits the client sets.

- Platform (Discord, Slack, Circle, Facebook Groups, Discourse, other) and size.
- The one-line purpose. If there is none, write it first.
- Current rules, if any, and what goes wrong most often (paste ten real examples with names removed).
- Who moderates, when, and who the owner is for serious cases.
- What the platform lets moderators do. Check the platform's own pages on the day; for example, Circle separates deactivate (reversible) from delete and ban (permanent), and Slack deactivation keeps messages and can be reversed.

## Steps

1. **Write the purpose line.** "A place for [who] to [do what] together." Every rule must serve it.
2. **Draft five to eight rules.** Each has a short title in the second person, a one-line reason starting "because", and one example of what breaks it. Cover: be kind, stay on topic, no spam or self-promotion outside the set place, no personal data, follow the platform's own rules. Drop any rule you would not enforce.
3. **List problem types.** From the examples: spam, off-topic, self-promotion, heated argument, harassment, misinformation, personal data shared, illegal or dangerous content. Add any the community has.
4. **Build the ladder for each type.** Steps in order: reminder, warning, hide, remove, time-out, ban. Serious harm (threats, child safety, doxxing) skips straight to removal and the serious-harm route. Prefer the reversible step at each rung.
5. **Mark access and approval.** For each step, write who may take it and at which level. Default: at level 3 the agent may remind, warn, hide, remove and time out under a listed rule. Bans and deletions need a fresh yes unless the rule lists them for that case. Erasing a member's account or data always needs a fresh yes.
6. **Write the messages.** A reminder, a warning, a removal notice and an appeal reply. Each names the rule, says what happened, what happens next, and how to appeal. Friendly first, firm when needed, never sarcastic. Send privately where the platform allows.
7. **Set the appeal route.** Who reviews (someone not involved in the first decision), how fast, and how the member hears back. This mirrors the Santa Clara Principles and the EU DSA's statement of reasons and internal complaint route.
8. **Write the serious-harm route.** Act to stop the harm. Save only the evidence the rules allow (link, time, usernames, screenshot). Never download or forward suspected child sexual abuse material; report it to the platform and to NCMEC's CyberTipline (US) or the Internet Watch Foundation (UK). Tell the owner the same day; the owner decides on police or legal advice.
9. **Set up the log.** Columns: date and time, member (handle), content link, rule, action, who acted, level, message sent, appeal, outcome.
10. **Check fairness.** Read the rules as a newcomer and as a regular. Would the owner's friend get the same step? If not, fix the rule.

## Checks

- Every rule has a reason and an example.
- No rule treats people differently for who they are.
- Every ladder starts with the gentlest step that would work, except serious harm.
- Each step names who may act and at which access level.
- Bans and deletions not listed for a case are marked "fresh yes".
- Every member message names the rule and the appeal route.
- The serious-harm route uses only official reporting channels and names the owner.
- No legal rulings. Where a case may need legal advice, it goes to the owner.

## Output format

1. **Purpose line.**
2. **House rules:** numbered, each with title, "because" line and example.
3. **Moderation ladder:** a table with columns problem, step 1 to step 5, who may act, level, fresh yes needed.
4. **Messages:** reminder, warning, removal notice, appeal reply.
5. **Appeal route.**
6. **Serious-harm route.**
7. **Action log template.**
8. **Open questions** for the owner.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a pottery community on Circle with 1,200 members. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Purpose:** A place for home potters to share work, ask for help and learn from each other.

**House rules (3 of 6)**

1. **Be kind about work.** Because people share pieces they are proud of. Example: "That glaze is ugly" breaks it; "The glaze pooled at the base; try a thinner coat" does not.
2. **Sell in the Market space only.** Because the feed is for learning. Example: a shop link in a technique thread.
3. **No personal details.** Because members' safety comes first. Example: posting someone's address or phone number.

**Ladder: selling outside Market**

| Step | Action | Who may act | Level | Fresh yes? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Private reminder with link to Market | Agent or moderator | 3 | No |
| 2 | Hide post, send warning | Agent or moderator | 3 | No |
| 3 | Remove post, flag member so new posts go to review | Moderator | 3 | No (rule lists it) |
| 4 | Ban | Owner | 3 | Yes |

**Removal notice:** "Hi Sam, we removed your post in Techniques because of rule 2: selling happens in Market. You're welcome to post the same thing there. If you think we got this wrong, reply here and Dana, who wasn't involved, will review it within two days."

**Open question:** Do you want "obvious spam accounts" listed for ban on sight? If yes, I will add it to rule 2, so it no longer needs a fresh yes.
