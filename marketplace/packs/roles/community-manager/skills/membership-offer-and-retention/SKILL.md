# Membership offer and retention

A paid community sells two things: a result and a sense of belonging. People cancel when they stop getting either, usually early. This skill designs the offer around what members can actually get every week, gets each new member to a first win fast, and makes leaving honest and easy, which also makes coming back easier.

## When to use

- A creator, coach, club, studio or association wants to start charging.
- A free group is being turned into a paid one.
- Members cancel after the first month or two.
- The owner is burning out delivering benefits.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read member and cancellation data. Level 2 to draft pages, emails and messages. Level 3 to publish community posts and send member messages within the limits set. Prices, payments, refunds above the client's limit and payout details stay with the client; I prepare them at level 2.

- Who the members are and what they want to achieve.
- What the business offers now, the audience size, and the email list size.
- Monthly costs: platform, tools, people, and the owner's hours.
- If running: sign-ups, cancellations and active members by month, and any cancel reasons collected.
- Countries members live in, for cancellation and renewal rules.

## Steps

1. **Member promise.** One line: the result or belonging people pay for each month. "Finish one painting a month with feedback from working artists" beats "Access to our exclusive community."
2. **Benefits the team can deliver every week.** List each benefit, who delivers it, and the hours it takes. Cut anything that depends on the owner working weekends.
3. **Levels.** One level is often enough. Add a second or third only if a clear group wants more and will pay for it (live feedback, small groups). For each: who it is for, benefits, limits.
4. **Break-even check.** Members needed = monthly fixed costs ÷ (price per member − variable cost per member). Show the sum with the client's numbers. If break-even needs more members than the audience can plausibly supply, change the offer or the costs before launch.
5. **The member path.**
   - Joining: what they see right after paying.
   - Week one: a welcome, an introduction, and a first win they can reach within the first week (define it concretely).
   - Monthly rhythm: the repeating events and resources.
   - Renewal: what the member sees before a renewal, and what they have achieved.
6. **Quiet-member actions.** Define "quiet" from the platform's activity data (for example, no visit or post in a set period the client picks). For each quiet member: one useful, personal nudge (a thread they would like, an event invite). No guilt, no fake scarcity.
7. **Cancel flow.** Cancelling must be as easy as joining. Offer a pause and a lower level as options, ask one optional question about why, and confirm the cancellation clearly. Rules here are changing: check the FTC's negative option rulemaking (US) and the UK subscription contracts regime before building the flow, and ask a qualified adviser for anything binding.
8. **Launch, if new.** A waitlist, a founding-member offer with an honest deadline that is honoured, and a launch timeline. Never a countdown that resets.
9. **Read cancellations.** Group members by the month they joined. For each group, show how many are still active after one, two and three months. Look for where the drop happens and whether those members reached the first win.
10. **Pick what to test first and what to drop.** One change at a time, with what you will measure and when.

## Checks

- The promise is a result or belonging a member can feel within weeks.
- Every benefit has an owner and fits the hours available.
- The break-even sum uses the client's numbers and is shown.
- The first win is concrete and reachable in week one.
- Cancelling is as easy as joining; a pause is offered; nothing is hidden.
- Deadlines are real. No fake scarcity or resetting timers.
- Payments, refunds and price changes are prepared for the client, not carried out.

## Output format

1. **Member promise.**
2. **Levels table:** level, for whom, benefits, limits, delivery hours.
3. **Break-even working.**
4. **Member path:** joining, week one, monthly rhythm, renewal, with the message at each step.
5. **Quiet-member plan.**
6. **Cancel flow:** screens or messages, pause and lower-level options.
7. **Cancellation read** (if data exists): joiners by month, still active at months one to three.
8. **First test and what to drop.**
9. **Rules to check** for the client's countries, with official links.

## Example of good output (short)

*Northlight Painters is a made-up online club for amateur painters, used only to show the format. All numbers are invented.*

**Promise:** Finish one painting a month, with feedback from a working artist.

**Levels**

| Level | For whom | Benefits | Delivery hours a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | Most members | Monthly brief, live demo, feedback thread, member gallery | 10 |
| Studio Plus | People who want detailed critique | All of Studio, plus a 20-minute one-to-one critique each month | 1 per member |

**Break-even (Studio):** fixed costs 1,800 a month ÷ (price 25 − variable cost 3) = 81.8, so 82 members.

**First win:** by day 7, post a photo of a finished 15-minute warm-up sketch in the gallery and get one comment from an artist.

**Cancellation read:** of 120 members who joined in July to September, 74% were active after month one and 51% after month two. Of those who posted a warm-up sketch in week one, 72% were still active at month two; of those who didn't, 33%. The first win matters more than price.

**First test:** a personal message on day 3 to anyone who has not posted the warm-up sketch. Measure: share posting in week one, and active at month two.

**Cancel flow:** one "Cancel" button in account settings; options to pause for a month or switch to a lower level; one optional "Why are you leaving?" question; a clear confirmation screen and email.
