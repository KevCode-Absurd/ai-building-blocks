# Outside community participation

Communities can tell an advertiser from a member in one post. The business that shows up with real know-how, says who it is, and follows the room's rules earns trust and early customers. The one that drops links, hides its connection or fakes enthusiasm gets removed, banned and remembered. This skill plans the first kind.

## When to use

- A founder wants honest feedback or early users from Reddit, Discord or forums.
- A brand wants to answer questions where its buyers already talk.
- Planning an AMA or a launch post in a community the business does not own.
- Staff or ambassadors are already posting and the business wants it done properly.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the communities' rules and threads (paste them in if I have no web access). Level 2 to write the map, plan and drafts. Personal accounts are used only by their holders, so the people named in the plan post from their own accounts. A shared brand account can be used at level 3 only after we have read the platform's current rules together and the community's moderators allow brand posts.

- What the business sells, what its people know well, and who will post.
- The communities the client has in mind, plus any it should consider.
- Each community's rules, wiki or pinned posts, and ten recent top threads (pasted).
- Any past posts by the business in these places, and how they went.

## Steps

1. **List candidates.** Five to ten communities where the buyers already ask questions the business can answer.
2. **Summarise each one's rules in plain words.** What is allowed, what gets removed, where self-promotion is allowed if at all, what disclosure is expected, and who the moderators are. Quote the rule's own short wording where it matters. Read platform-wide rules on the day; Reddit's pages could not be checked when this kit was written.
3. **Score each community** on fit (do our buyers ask questions here?), activity (are questions answered this week?), and brand welcome (do the rules or moderators allow businesses?). Use words or a 1 to 3 scale with a reason for each score. Start with two or three.
4. **Plan to give before asking.** For 30 days: who posts where, how often, and the topics they lead on. Most posts answer real questions with no link at all. A link appears only when it truly answers the question and the rules allow it.
5. **Write the disclosure line.** Each person who works for, owns or is paid by the business says so in the post itself: "I work at [business]" or "Disclosure: I'm the founder." In the US, the FTC says employees and owners must disclose in each post, not only on a profile, and that "#ambassador" alone is not enough. For UK and EU rules, see the social media manager kit.
6. **Draft value-first replies.** Answer the question as if the product did not exist. Then, only if it helps, mention it with the disclosure. Each draft should be useful even with the product removed.
7. **Plan AMAs and launches with moderators.** Write a short request to the moderators: who is answering, why their members would care, proposed date and format, and the disclosure. Go ahead only with written approval.
8. **Set warning signs.** Removed posts, downvotes or negative replies, a moderator message, or "is this an ad?" comments. Any of these means stop, read the feedback and adjust before posting again.
9. **Brief ambassadors, if any.** What they may and may not claim, how to disclose, and that the business will check what they post. The FTC expects brands to train and monitor ambassador programmes.
10. **Review weekly.** Which answers helped, which drew questions, any removals, and what members asked that the business could answer better.

## Checks

- Every community has a rules summary with the self-promotion and disclosure points.
- Nothing in the plan breaks a community's stated rules.
- Every draft is useful without the product.
- Every post by someone connected to the business carries a disclosure in the post.
- No fake accounts, sock puppets, bought upvotes, vote requests or seeded posts.
- AMAs and launch posts have moderator approval in writing.
- People post from their own accounts; the agent does not post as a person.

## Output format

1. **Community map:** a table with community, rules summary, fit, activity, brand welcome, start or skip.
2. **30-day participation plan:** who, where, how often, topics, share of posts with a link.
3. **Drafts:** ten replies or posts, each with the thread it answers and its disclosure line.
4. **Moderator request** for any AMA or launch.
5. **Warning signs** and what to do for each.
6. **Ambassador brief**, if needed.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a hiking GPS app founded by Mei. The communities below are described generically, not real ones. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Map (2 of 6)**

| Community | Rules in plain words | Fit | Activity | Brand welcome | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A large hiking forum | No ads; links only if they answer the question; staff must say who they are | 3: route-planning questions weekly | 3 | 2: allowed with disclosure | Start |
| A gear-deals group | Deals posts only from approved sellers | 1 | 3 | 1: approved sellers only | Skip |

**Plan:** Mei answers three route-planning questions a week in the hiking forum for 30 days. Links to the app in at most one answer in five, and only where offline maps are the actual question.

**Draft reply:** "For a three-day loop with no signal, download the map tiles before you leave and carry a paper map as backup, because phone batteries die in the cold. Check water sources against the latest trip reports, as the dry-season ones go fast. Disclosure: I'm the founder of [Business], an offline map app. It does offline tiles, but any app that downloads tiles will do the job."

**Warning sign:** a moderator asks Mei to stop linking. Action: stop all links in that forum, reply to thank them, and keep answering without links.
