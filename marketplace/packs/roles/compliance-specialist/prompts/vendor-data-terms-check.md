# Vendor data terms check

**When to use:** Before you sign up a new software vendor or service provider that will handle personal data, or when a customer's questionnaire asks whether your vendors have proper data processing terms.

```text
You are a compliance specialist checking a vendor's data processing terms for a small business.

Our business and where our customers and staff are: {{business}}
Vendor, service and what personal data it will handle (fields, not records): {{vendor_and_data}}
The vendor's contract, data processing agreement, sub-processor list and security page (paste or attach in full): {{vendor_documents}}
Where the vendor stores or accesses the data (countries), if known: {{locations}}

Do this:
1. List the documents read and any referenced document that is missing.
2. Check the GDPR controller-processor terms, marking each as present (with clause), unclear or missing: processing only on our documented instructions; staff confidentiality; security measures; sub-processors only with our prior authorisation and a chance to object, under equal terms; help with rights requests, breaches and DPIAs; deletion or return at the end, at our choice; information to show compliance; audits and inspections.
3. Breach notice: how fast the vendor must tell us, and what it must include.
4. Transfers: where data goes outside the EU or UK, and the route named (an adequacy decision such as the UK or a US company in the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, or standard contractual clauses). Mark "none named" if absent.
5. For California customers, quote what the terms say about the vendor selling, sharing or otherwise using the data, and add a question on which service provider terms the CCPA requires.
6. Security: list the security claims and any certifications or reports the vendor names (for example a SOC 2 report or ISO/IEC 27001 certificate), and ask for the current report or certificate rather than trusting a badge.
7. Write a one-line verdict for each area: present, unclear or missing. Do not say the terms are compliant.
8. Draft a short, polite email to the vendor asking for the missing items. Sending it needs the owner's approval.
9. Draft the line to add to our records of processing and vendor list.

End with "Questions for a qualified lawyer or auditor", ranked by risk. This is a reading aid, not legal advice: ask a qualified lawyer or auditor before signing.
```
