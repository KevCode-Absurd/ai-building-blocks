# Privacy register builder

## When to use

- Nobody can say what personal data the business holds, where, or for how long.
- A new tool, vendor, AI feature or marketing idea will use customer or staff data.
- The first access, deletion or opt-out request arrives.
- A customer's security or privacy questionnaire asks for records of processing or a sub-processor list.
- Something went wrong (a misdirected email, a lost laptop) and the team needs a log.

## What you need

1. What the business does, where its customers and staff are (EU, UK, California, elsewhere) and its headcount.
2. A list of systems and vendors that touch personal data (from the tool list or the finance ledger).
3. Forms, sign-up pages and the current privacy notice.
4. Field descriptions for each system (for example "email, name, order history"), not real records.
5. Any request log, incident notes and vendor contracts.

**Access:** level 1 to read tool lists, notices and contracts; level 2 to draft the registers and notices in your documents or privacy tool; level 3 to keep the registers up to date and assign follow-up tasks to named owners, within your limits. Replies to rights requests, deletions, and any breach notification need a fresh yes every time, and the notification decision belongs to the business and its lawyer.

## Steps

### 1. Build the data map

One row per processing activity (a purpose, not a tool): "sell and deliver orders", "send marketing email", "run payroll", "screen job applicants".

| Activity | Data categories | Whose data | Source | Systems | Recipients and vendors | Transfers outside EU/UK | Retention | Security notes | Lawful basis (to confirm) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

This doubles as the GDPR record of processing: purpose, data categories, recipients, transfers, storage period and security. Organisations under 250 employees may be exempt from recording purely occasional processing, but most routine activities still belong in the record. Mark special category data (health, biometric, genetic and similar) clearly; it needs an exception to be used at all. Leave the lawful basis column as "to confirm" unless the client's lawyer has set it.

### 2. Check each vendor

For each processor, mark the contract terms GDPR requires as present (with clause), unclear or missing: instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processor authorisation, help with rights requests, breaches and DPIAs, deletion or return, information and audits. Note where data goes and the transfer route: an adequacy decision (for example the UK, or a US company in the EU-US Data Privacy Framework) or standard contractual clauses.

### 3. Screen new uses (DPIA screening)

For a new tool or use, answer:
- What is the purpose, and is each data item needed for it?
- Who will see the data, and does any of it leave the EU or UK?
- Could it affect people significantly (automated decisions, monitoring, special category data, children, large scale)?
- What would people reasonably expect?
- Which safeguards cut the risk (collect less, shorter retention, tighter access, clear notice, a way to delete)?

If the EDPB's high-risk criteria seem to apply (two or more usually means a DPIA), write: a DPIA is likely needed; ask a qualified lawyer or auditor to confirm. In California, note that risk assessment duties under the new CCPA regulations started on 1 January 2026, and ADMT duties start on 1 January 2027.

### 4. Set up the rights request log

| Received | Channel | Law (GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA) | Request type | Identity check | Due date | Systems searched | Reply sent (date) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Due dates: GDPR and UK GDPR, one month (extendable by two months for complex requests, telling the person within the first month); CCPA, 45 calendar days (extendable by 45), opt-outs within 15 business days, and the Global Privacy Control signal honoured as an opt-out. Draft the reply at level 2; sending it needs a fresh yes.

### 5. Set up the breach log

| Found (date, time) | What happened | Data and people affected | Contained? | Risk view (to confirm) | Who was told, when | Decision on notifying, by whom | Lessons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Under the GDPR, every breach is documented, reported or not, and the 72-hour clock to notify the authority runs from awareness. Start the log at once and alert the decision makers and their lawyer.

### 6. List actions and questions

A short action list with owners (fix a missing contract term, shorten a retention period, update the notice), and a list of questions for a lawyer.

## Checks

- No real personal records were requested or copied; fields only.
- Every activity has an owner and a retention period, or a [NEEDED] mark.
- Lawful basis and DPIA conclusions are marked "to confirm" unless a lawyer set them.
- UK documents reflect the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changes and the move of the Information Commission's functions to the Information Commission on 30 September 2026; check current guidance.
- Due dates in the request log are calculated per law.
- The document ends with "Questions for a qualified lawyer or auditor" and the line: this is a working document, not legal advice.

## Output format

1. Summary: activities found, special category data, vendors without full terms, top three actions.
2. Data map and records of processing table.
3. Vendor and processor check table.
4. DPIA screening notes for new uses.
5. Rights request log template (or current log with due dates).
6. Breach log template (or current log).
7. Actions with owners; questions for a qualified lawyer or auditor.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business: Orrin Physio, a 12-person clinic group in Leeds with an online booking tool and a newsletter.*

| Activity | Data categories | Systems | Vendors | Retention | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Book and run appointments | Name, contact, appointment history, health notes (special category) | Booking tool, clinic notes system | Booking tool provider | [NEEDED] | Practice manager |
| Send newsletter | Name, email, consent record | Email tool | Email provider (US) | Until unsubscribe | Marketing lead |

**Vendor check.** Email provider: sub-processor list present (cl. 9); deletion at end unclear; transfer route to the US not named.

**Questions for a qualified lawyer or auditor**
1. Which lawful basis and special category condition apply to health notes in the booking tool?
2. Is a DPIA needed before adding the booking tool's AI reminder feature?
