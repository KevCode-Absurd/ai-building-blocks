# Regulation to checklist

## When to use

- A new rule or a new version of a rule applies, or might apply, to the business (for example a privacy regulation, the EU AI Act, new CCPA regulations, a sector code).
- A customer, regulator or partner refers to a standard and the team needs to know what it asks.
- The team needs a calendar of filing, reporting and review dates from rules it already follows.
- Two versions of a rule need comparing.

Do not use it to decide whether the rule applies or whether the business complies. Those are questions to ask a qualified lawyer or auditor.

## What you need

1. The full official text (or the exact sections) and any official guidance, with the version and date. If only a news summary exists, ask for the official text first.
2. Country, state or region, business type, size and what the business does with the subject of the rule (data, AI, payments, products).
3. The decision the team needs to make.
4. For a version comparison: both versions in full.

**Access:** level 1 to read the rule and your policies and registers; level 2 to write the summary, checklist and calendar as drafts in your documents or compliance tool; level 3 to publish the approved checklist, add the dates to the shared calendar and assign tasks to the named owners, within your limits.

## Steps

### 1. Read the whole text first

Note the scope, the definitions, any exemptions or thresholds, the dates it applies from, and who enforces it. List any sections, annexes or linked acts you have not been given; the summary covers only what you have.

### 2. Summarise in the original's order

Section by section, in plain words, keeping the original numbering so a lawyer can check it side by side. One to three sentences per section. Cite the section for every point. Never add a duty the text does not state. If a word is defined in the text, use the definition, not the everyday meaning.

### 3. Mark what depends on the business's facts

Highlight thresholds, exemptions and definitions that decide whether a duty applies (size, revenue, role such as controller or processor, provider or deployer, type of data). Do not decide them. Write each as a question.

### 4. Turn duties into tasks

For each duty write one row:

| Task | Section | Applies to | Deadline or trigger | Proof to keep | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

"Proof to keep" is the evidence that would show the task was done (a register entry, a notice, a log, a signed document, a training record).

### 5. Build the deadline calendar

List every date the text sets: when it applies, phased dates, reporting and filing dates, review cycles. Lay them out by month with owners. Flag dates that a later amendment may have moved and say where to check.

### 6. Compare versions (if asked)

Clause by clause: old text, new text, what it means in practice, with section references. Show only what changed.

### 7. Write the questions for a lawyer

Turn every unclear point, every fact-dependent threshold and every penalty into a short, specific question, ranked by risk. Good questions name the section and the fact in doubt.

## Checks

- Every summary point and task has a section reference.
- No task exists without a duty in the text.
- The version and date of the text are stated at the top.
- Thresholds and exemptions are listed as questions, not decided.
- Dates were checked against the official page on the day of writing.
- No sentence says the business complies or is exempt.
- The document ends with "Questions for a qualified lawyer or auditor" and the line: this is a working document, not legal advice.

## Output format

1. Header: rule name, version and date, sections covered, sections missing.
2. One-paragraph summary for a director.
3. Section-by-section summary with references.
4. Duty checklist table.
5. Deadline calendar by month.
6. Version comparison table (if asked).
7. Questions for a qualified lawyer or auditor.
8. Access I have, actions taken and the action log.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business: Tallow Hiring Ltd, a 40-person UK recruitment firm using an AI tool to sort CVs for EU clients.*

**Rule:** EU AI Act, articles on high-risk systems and Article 4 (consolidated text as published on the Commission's AI Act Service Desk, read 6 Nov 2026). Annexes other than Annex III not provided.

| Task | Section | Applies to | Deadline | Proof to keep | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Take measures to support AI literacy of staff who use the CV tool | Art. 4 | Deployers | In force now | Training record and short guide | Ops lead | Open |
| List every AI tool used in hiring and what it decides | Annex III (employment uses) | To confirm | Before 2 Dec 2027 | AI use register | Ops lead | Open |

**Questions for a qualified lawyer or auditor**
1. Is Tallow a deployer of a high-risk system under Annex III (employment uses) when the tool ranks candidates for EU clients, given Tallow is based in the UK?
2. Which duties fall on Tallow and which on the tool's provider?

*This is a working document, not legal advice.*
