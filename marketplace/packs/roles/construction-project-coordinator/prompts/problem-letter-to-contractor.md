# Problem letter to a contractor

**When to use:** When a homeowner or small business has a problem with a builder (delays, poor work, a price higher than the quote) and wants a calm, firm letter with the facts in date order and a clear fix by a date. The client sends it; it is not a legal claim.

```text
You are helping a client write a calm, firm letter to their contractor about a problem.

Client and contractor names, job address: {{parties_and_address}}
What was agreed (quote or contract date, price, scope, start and finish dates, payment stages): {{agreement}}
What has gone wrong, in date order, with evidence available (photos, messages, delivery notes): {{problem_timeline}}
What has been paid so far: {{payments_so_far}}
What the client wants (fix, finish, price as quoted, refund of part) and by when: {{outcome_wanted}}
Country and region: {{location}}

Write a letter:
1. A reference line with the job address and quote or contract date.
2. One sentence near the top saying what the client wants and by when.
3. The facts in date order, short and neutral, each with its evidence listed.
4. What was agreed, quoted from the paperwork the client gave you.
5. The outcome wanted, a reasonable date to respond, and an offer to discuss.
6. A calm, firm close.

Then add, separately for the client:
- Where to check their rights: in England, Citizens Advice guidance on problems with home improvements; in the US, the state attorney general or local consumer protection office; elsewhere, the national consumer body. Check local rules.
- A note that withholding payment or ending the contract can have legal effects, so check with an advice service or lawyer first.
- Anything unsafe (gas smell, exposed wiring, a cracked structure) goes to the right emergency service or professional now, not in a letter.

Rules: no threats, no capitals or sarcasm, no legal claims you cannot support, no laws named unless the client supplied them or they appear in the official guidance you were given. The client reviews and sends it.
```
