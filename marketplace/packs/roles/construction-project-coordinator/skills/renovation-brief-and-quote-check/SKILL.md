# Renovation brief and quote check

## When to use

- A homeowner or small business wants quotes for a kitchen, bathroom, extension, loft or fit-out.
- Three quotes have arrived and don't match.
- Before signing, to check the quote, payment stages and contract basics.

Not for building regulations, planning, structural, gas or electrical advice, or for choosing the contractor. Those go to building control, a structural engineer or surveyor, a registered installer, and the client. Check local rules.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** notes, photos (or descriptions), drawings and the quotes the client pastes or shares.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft the brief, the vetting questions and the emails to builders.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send the brief and follow-up questions to the builders the client named, within the confirmed limits. Accepting a quote, paying a deposit or signing a contract always stays with the client; sending anything as a binding offer needs a fresh yes.

## What you need

1. The job room by room: what is there now, what should change, finishes wanted.
2. Who will supply materials (client or builder) and any items already bought.
3. Access, parking, working hours, whether the client lives there during the work, pets.
4. Preferred start and any fixed dates.
5. Any quotes already received, pasted in full.

## Steps

### 1. Write the brief room by room
For each room: what is removed and disposed of, what is new, who supplies each item, finishes, and what is **not** included. Add whole-job items: protection of floors and furniture, skips and rubbish removal, making good after other trades, cleaning, scaffolding, temporary kitchen or bathroom, and who pays for any building control or party wall costs.

### 2. Mark professional decisions
Anything touching structure (removing walls, new openings, steel), gas, electrics, drainage, fire safety or a shared wall gets **[ask your builder, engineer, surveyor or registered installer]**. In England, gas work needs a Gas Safe registered engineer, and many jobs need building regulations approval and sometimes planning permission or a party wall notice; check with the council or building control. In the US and EU, check local rules for permits.

### 3. Tell builders how to lay out the quote
Ask for a price per room or per item in the brief's order, with exclusions listed, provisional sums and allowances named and valued, VAT or sales tax shown, payment stages, start date, duration, validity period and insurance details. A quote in the same order as the brief can be compared line by line.

### 4. Write vetting questions
Recent similar jobs and whether you may contact those clients; insurance; who will actually do the work and which parts are subcontracted; trade scheme or competent person scheme memberships, and how to check them on the scheme's own list; programme and working hours; payment stages; how changes will be priced and agreed in writing; who removes rubbish and pays for skips; clean-up and making good; guarantee or warranty on the work. Add what a good answer sounds like for each.

### 5. Compare the quotes
Build a table: one row per brief item, one column per quote. Mark each cell **included**, **excluded** or **not stated**, with the price where given. Never treat "not stated" as included. Then add rows for: total, provisional sums and allowances, tax, deposit, payment stages, start date, duration, validity, insurance.

### 6. Find the gaps
List where a cheaper quote leaves items out, where allowances look low against the client's chosen finishes, and where wording is vague. Point out a price much lower than the others as something to ask about, not a bargain.

### 7. Check the payment terms
Flag any request for most of the price upfront. Citizens Advice (England) suggests paying in stages, avoiding paying everything upfront, and keeping any deposit under a quarter of the price; paying by card may add protection. In the US, the FTC's Cooling-Off Rule can give three business days to cancel certain contracts signed at home, with exclusions. Check local rules.

### 8. Write questions for each builder
Short, specific questions that close each gap: "Does your price include removing the old units and the skip?" Send them (at level 3, to the named builders only) or hand them to the client.

### 9. Summarise without choosing
A short summary: what each quote covers, the gaps, and what to settle before deciding. The client chooses.

## Checks

- Every price and item comes from the quotes as pasted; nothing assumed.
- "Not stated" is used wherever a quote is silent.
- Structural, gas, electrical, drainage and permit points are flagged for professionals.
- Payment stages and deposit size are checked against official guidance.
- The summary does not pick a builder.
- Nothing is accepted, paid or signed for the client.

## Output format

1. Room-by-room brief with exclusions and quote layout instructions.
2. Vetting questions with what good answers sound like.
3. Quote comparison table.
4. Gaps and questions for each builder.
5. Summary of what to settle before deciding.

## Example

*Made-up business for illustration: the Okafor family, kitchen refit in a 1930s semi in Nottingham, UK. Three quotes.*

> | Item | Quote A (£8,400) | Quote B (£9,950) | Quote C (£11,200) |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Remove old units and dispose | Included | Not stated | Included |
> | Skip | Not stated | Included | Included |
> | Fit client-supplied units | Included | Included | Included |
> | Wall tiling | Excluded | Included (allowance £600) | Included (client tiles) |
> | 4 new double sockets | Included | Included | Included [ask your electrician: registered installer?] |
> | Plaster one wall | Included | Included | Included |
> | Deposit | 50% before start | 20% | 10% |
> | Valid until | 30 days | Not stated | 60 days |
>
> **Gaps:** A excludes tiling, so add the tiling cost before comparing totals. B doesn't say who removes the old units. A's 50% deposit is high against Citizens Advice guidance; ask about stage payments instead.
> **Questions for B:** "Does your price include removing and disposing of the old units? How long is the quote valid?"
