# RFIs, submittals and changes

## When to use

- A drawing, specification or site condition raises a question the documents don't answer.
- Before work starts, to list every submittal the specification asks for.
- Whenever someone asks for, or causes, work that differs from the contract.
- Each payment period, to check a payment application against progress before the client or their certifier acts on it.

Not for deciding entitlement to extra time or money, valuing disputed changes, or certifying payment. Those belong to the contract administrator, quantity surveyor or lawyer under the contract in use. Check local rules.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** drawings list, specification, contract (or its key clauses), programme, RFI, submittal and change logs, payment applications.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft RFIs, submittal entries, change events, change orders and a payment application check.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** raise and distribute RFIs to the design team, chase ball-in-court items, update submittal dates and statuses, within the confirmed limits. Sending or signing a change order, variation or change directive, submitting or certifying a payment application, and any notice under the contract need a fresh yes every time. Payments stay with the client.

## What you need

1. The drawings list and specification, with revision letters.
2. The contract form in use and who signs changes and certifies payment.
3. The programme, for dates needed.
4. Supplier lead times for long-lead items.
5. For payment checks: the application, the schedule of values, the latest daily reports and photos.

## Steps

### RFIs

1. **Check the documents first.** Search the drawings, specification and earlier RFI answers. Many questions are already answered there.
2. **Write one question per RFI.** Give the location, the drawing and revision or specification clause, what is unclear or in conflict, photos or a marked-up sketch, and a suggested answer: "Drawing A-201 rev C shows the steel beam at 2.4 m; S-101 rev B shows 2.6 m. Suggest 2.6 m per the engineer's drawing. Confirm?"
3. **Set the date needed** from the programme (the task start minus time to act on the answer), and state the impact if late.
4. **Log it:** number, date raised, question, to whom, ball in court, date needed, status, answer, and whether the answer affects cost or time.
5. **Chase** before the date needed, not after. If the answer changes cost, time or scope, open a change log entry before the work proceeds.

### Submittals

1. **List every submittal** the specification asks for (shop drawings, product data, samples, test certificates, warranties), by specification section.
2. **Set three dates** for each: needed on site (from the programme), order date (needed date minus supplier lead time), and submit-by date (order date minus review time agreed with the designer).
3. **Track** who submits, who reviews, ball in court, status (not submitted, submitted, approved, approved as noted, revise and resubmit, rejected) and revision.
4. **Flag** any item whose submit-by date has passed, and its effect on the programme.

### Changes

1. **Catch every change:** a client request, a designer instruction, an RFI answer that adds work, or a site condition. Log it the day it appears.
2. **Describe it** in plain words with the drawing or clause, who asked, and when.
3. **Assess** cost (from the contractor's priced breakdown, never guessed) and time (which programme tasks move, and whether the critical path moves).
4. **Prepare the paperwork** in the form the contract uses (for example a change order or variation; under AIA, G701, or G714 where price or time is not yet agreed). Leave signatures blank.
5. **Get the client's fresh yes** before the change is instructed or signed. If the client wants work to go ahead before the price is agreed, record that decision in writing.
6. **Update** the change log, programme and budget once signed.

### Payment application check

1. Compare each line of the application with the schedule of values, the progress shown in daily reports and photos, approved changes and materials on site.
2. Flag lines where claimed progress is ahead of the record, changes claimed but not signed, and arithmetic errors.
3. Note retention and previous payments as stated in the contract; do not interpret disputed terms.
4. Give the client a short list of questions for the contractor. In the UK, payment notices and deadlines under the Construction Act may apply to commercial contracts (not to residential occupiers): check with a quantity surveyor or lawyer.
5. The client, or their certifier, decides and pays.

## Checks

- One question per RFI, with reference, suggested answer and date needed.
- Every submittal has needed-on-site, order and submit-by dates.
- No change is instructed before it is logged and the client has said yes.
- Cost figures come from priced breakdowns, not guesses.
- Nothing is sent, signed, certified or paid without the client's fresh yes; money stays with the client.
- Contract questions are flagged for the contract administrator, quantity surveyor or lawyer.

## Output format

1. RFI (ready to send) and RFI log.
2. Submittal register with dates and flags.
3. Change log, and change order or variation drafts with blank signature lines.
4. Payment application check with questions for the contractor.

## Example

*Made-up business for illustration: Larchfield Homes, a small developer building two houses in Leicestershire, UK.*

> **RFI-07 (raised 9 Apr; to: structural engineer; date needed: 15 Apr)**
> Plot 2 kitchen: drawing A-110 rev D shows a 3.6 m opening; S-201 rev B shows a 3.0 m beam. Which is correct? Suggest 3.6 m opening with a beam to the engineer's design. Impact if late: steel order (2-week lead) misses the 4 May install; roof follows.
>
> **Change log C-04:** client requests underfloor heating to plot 1 ground floor (email 10 Apr). Cost: awaiting the plumber's priced breakdown. Time: screed moves 3 days [ESTIMATE]; not on the critical path. Status: **awaiting client decision**; no work instructed.
>
> **Payment check, application 3:** line 6 "roof covering 100%" but daily reports show plot 2 roof at about half; change C-02 claimed but not yet signed. Two questions for the contractor attached.
