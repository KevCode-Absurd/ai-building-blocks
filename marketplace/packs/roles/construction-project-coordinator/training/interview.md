# Training interview: construction project coordinator

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client names, addresses, prices, contract terms or incident details from real jobs; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of jobs have you coordinated or priced (type, size, length, client), and in which countries or regions?**
   Why it matters: sets the jobs and the local rules the agent is built for.

2. **When a new job lands, what do you do before the first day on site, step by step?**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's job setup routine.

3. **How do you build a programme? Where do your durations come from, and how do you mark guesses?**
   Why it matters: honest durations are the base of every date the client hears.

4. **Which tasks most often end up on the critical path on your jobs, and why?**
   Why it matters: teaches the agent where to look first.

5. **How do you run a look-ahead with trades? What makes a task "ready" for you?**
   Why it matters: the readiness test is what keeps trades from turning up to blocked work.

6. **Show me a daily report you were happy with, anonymised. What headings do you never skip?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a model for site records.

7. **How do you record a delay on the day? Tell me about a time a good record mattered later.**
   Why it matters: shows what evidence holds up and how it is kept.

8. **What makes a good RFI, and what makes designers ignore one?**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's RFI checklist.

9. **How do you set submit-by dates, and which long-lead items catch people out?**
   Why it matters: late submittals are a common hidden cause of delay.

10. **Walk me through how a change goes from a client's "while you're at it" to a signed variation.**
    Why it matters: sets the change control steps and the points where the client must say yes.

11. **How do you check a payment application against progress? What red flags do you look for?**
    Why it matters: protects the client's money without the agent ever paying.

12. **Which contracts do you work under (AIA, JCT, NEC, the builder's own terms), and what do you check in each before acting?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent to follow the contract in use, not a generic rule.

13. **How do safety roles work on your jobs, and what does the coordinator track versus the principal contractor or safety adviser?**
    Why it matters: draws the line between recording and signing off.

14. **Which permits, inspections and notices do your jobs usually need, and who books them?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a local pre-start checklist to confirm.

15. **For homeowner jobs: what goes into a brief so three builders price the same job?**
    Why it matters: becomes the brief template.

16. **What do quotes most often leave out, in your experience?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's "not stated" checklist.

17. **Which questions sort good contractors from risky ones, and what do good answers sound like?**
    Why it matters: becomes the vetting questions with model answers.

18. **Which project tools do you use, and what would you let an assistant change in them without asking?**
    Why it matters: turns the three access levels into real limits.

19. **Where does your job end and the engineer's, surveyor's, building control's or lawyer's begin?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's referral rules.

20. **Tell me about the hardest job you have coordinated. What went wrong, and what would you do differently?**
    Why it matters: a full story shows judgement under pressure that short answers miss.
