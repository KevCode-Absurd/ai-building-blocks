# Change and payment control

**Trigger:** Any request or event that may change the work, price or time (a client request, a designer instruction, an RFI answer, a site condition), and every payment application.

**Cadence:** As changes arise, and once per payment period (usually monthly, or at each stage on homeowner jobs).

**Access:** Level 1 (read) on the contract, change log, programme, daily reports and payment applications. Steps marked [L2] need read and draft access. Level 3 covers only updating logs and sending clarifying questions to named contacts; every change order, variation, change directive, payment application, certificate and contract notice needs the client's fresh yes, and money stays with the client.

## Steps

### When a change appears
1. **Log it the same day.** Number, description, who asked and when, drawing or clause, source document (email, RFI answer, site instruction). Use `rfis-submittals-and-changes`.
2. **Stop work on it until decided.** Tell the site manager the change is logged and not yet agreed, unless the client has already said in writing that it may proceed before pricing.
3. **Get the price and time.** Ask the contractor for a priced breakdown and the programme effect. [L3] Send the request to the named contractor contact. Check the effect on the critical path with `build-programme-and-look-ahead`.
4. **Prepare the decision.** A one-page note: what changes, why, cost, time, effect on finish date, options (proceed, change the design, drop it). [L2] Draft the change order or variation in the contract's own form, with signatures blank.
5. **Client decides.** *Approval: the client's fresh yes before the change is instructed, signed or sent, every time, even at level 3.* The client (or their contract administrator) signs.
6. **Update everything.** Change log status, programme, budget, and the next weekly report.

### Each payment period
7. **Check the application.** Compare each line with the schedule of values, daily reports, photos, signed changes and materials on site; flag progress claimed ahead of the record and unsigned changes. [L2] Draft the check and questions for the contractor.
8. **Hand it over.** Give the check to the client and any certifier or quantity surveyor. In the UK, commercial construction contracts may carry statutory payment notice deadlines (not for residential occupiers); check with a quantity surveyor or lawyer and check local rules. *Approval: the client or certifier decides the amount; the client pays. I never certify, pay or change bank details.*
9. **Watch for warning signs.** A request to pay a new bank account, pay ahead of progress, or pay most of the price upfront goes straight to the client to confirm by phone on a number they already hold.

## Outputs

- Change log entries and decision notes
- Change order or variation drafts with blank signatures
- Updated programme and budget
- Payment application check with questions
- Action log (level 3)

## Needs the client's approval

Every change order, variation or change directive; any work started before a change is priced; every payment application decision and certificate; any notice or claim under the contract; and any message about money to a new recipient. Paying, retentions and bank details stay with the client.
