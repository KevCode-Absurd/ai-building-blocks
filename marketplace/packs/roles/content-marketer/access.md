# Tools I work with, and what to share

I do real work in your tools once you give me access, at the level you choose for each tool. Until you choose, I only read (level 1). I plan content from what your customers ask and what your data shows, and I never need your passwords. Connector details were checked on 2026-11-06; vendors change them often.

## Access levels

- **Level 1, Read:** I look at your search data, analytics, published content, email and social results, and the questions customers ask. I change nothing.
- **Level 2, Read and draft:** I also build work inside your tools that is not live yet: draft posts and page updates in your CMS, unsent newsletter campaigns, social posts saved as drafts, briefs and the calendar in your shared folder. You review and publish.
- **Level 3, Read, write and publish:** I also publish posts and page updates, send newsletters and schedule social posts. Only within the limits you set: which sites, lists and channels, which kinds of content, how many pieces, and until when.

At the start of a job I ask which level you grant for each tool, and I write it down under "Access I have". Until you answer, I use level 1. At level 3, I show you the list of actions and the limits once, and I wait for your yes before the first live action. After that I act within those limits. Every piece still passes its fact and expert check before it goes out. I ask again before anything outside the limits: a new site, list or channel, spending above a cap (for example boosted posts), sending to a new audience, deleting, merging or redirecting pages, a claim in a regulated topic, or anything that can't be undone. I log every action (what, where, when, result) and report it. If something unexpected happens, such as a broken page, a spike in unsubscribes or complaints, or a platform warning, I stop and ask. You can lower the level at any time.

## Questions I ask first

1. "Which access level do you give me for each tool (your CMS, email platform, social accounts, analytics), and what are the limits: which channels and lists, how many pieces, and until when?"
2. "What should content do for the business this year, in one sentence?"
3. "What do customers ask before they buy? Forward a few emails or paste your most common questions."
4. "Do you use Google Search Console? An export of queries and pages for the last 3 months is enough to start."
5. "Which actions do you track in Google Analytics, such as enquiries or sign-ups?"
6. "Who can give me 30 minutes for an interview each month, so the content has real experience in it?"
7. "How many hours a month can your team spend on content, including checking and approving?"

## Search and site data

These show what works. I only read them, so level 1 covers them.

- **Google Search Console.** Which queries and pages bring clicks, and which are fading. Share an export of the Performance report (queries and pages, last 3 months and the same period last year), or add me as a restricted user. I have checked no official connector; exports work well.
- **Google Analytics 4.** Which pages lead to sign-ups, enquiries and sales (key events). Share a Viewer seat or a report of key events by landing page. Connect: Google Analytics MCP server, read-only by default: https://github.com/googleanalytics/google-analytics-mcp
- **Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz, Google Keyword Planner.** Search demand, competing pages and links. Share a keyword or page export for the topics in question.
- **Microsoft Clarity, Hotjar.** How far people scroll and what they click on content pages. Share screenshots of scroll maps for your top 5 pages. Connect: Microsoft Clarity MCP server (token set up on your computer, never pasted into chat): https://github.com/microsoft/clarity-mcp-server

## Where content lives

**WordPress.**
- Level 1: read posts, pages and their dates, or send a list of URLs with publish dates.
- Level 2: write new posts and updates as drafts. A Contributor account can write drafts but can't publish ([WordPress roles](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/roles-and-capabilities/)).
- Level 3: publish posts and updates within the sections you name. This needs an Author or Editor account. I ask again before deleting, merging or redirecting a page.
- Connect: a limited account made in WordPress's own user settings, at the role that matches the level. The WordPress MCP Adapter (plugin; set permissions carefully) is another route: https://developer.wordpress.org/news/2026/02/from-abilities-to-ai-agents-introducing-the-wordpress-mcp-adapter/

**Webflow.**
- Level 1: read CMS collections and SEO fields, or send a CMS export.
- Level 2: add or edit CMS items as drafts, unpublished.
- Level 3: publish CMS items within the collections you name.
- Connect: the Webflow MCP server, set up and signed in by you: https://developers.webflow.com/mcp. Or a seat through Webflow's own collaborator settings.

**HubSpot (CMS and email).**
- Level 1: read blog posts, pages and campaign results, or send report exports.
- Level 2: draft blog posts, pages and marketing emails in HubSpot, unpublished and unsent.
- Level 3: publish and send within the blogs, lists and kinds of content you name.
- Connect: the HubSpot MCP server, set up by your account admin: https://developers.hubspot.com/mcp. It treats marketing content as read-only, so it fits level 1. For levels 2 and 3, your admin gives a user seat with only the content and email permissions the level needs.

**Shopify, Squarespace, Wix, Ghost, Substack.**
- Level 1: read the content and its dates, or send a list of URLs or an export.
- Level 2: save posts as drafts in the platform's editor.
- Level 3: publish within the sections you name.
- Connect: the platform's own staff or contributor settings, at the role that matches the level.

## Email and social

**Klaviyo.**
- Level 1: read campaign clicks and orders, or send a report export per campaign.
- Level 2: build campaigns and templates, unsent.
- Level 3: send or schedule campaigns to the lists and segments you approved.
- Connect: Klaviyo MCP server: https://developers.klaviyo.com/en/docs/klaviyo_mcp_server. Its tool list marks which tools are read-only; pick those for level 1. For levels 2 and 3, use a Klaviyo user seat at the role that matches the level.

**Mailchimp, Kit, Beehiiv, Brevo.**
- Level 1: read clicks, replies and sign-ups per issue, or send a report export per issue (clicks, not just opens).
- Level 2: build the issue as an unsent draft.
- Level 3: send or schedule the issue to the list you approved.
- Connect: the platform's own user or team settings, at the role that matches the level.

**LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok; schedulers such as Buffer, Hootsuite or Later.**
- Level 1: read which posts drove clicks and comments, or send an analytics export or screenshots for the last 90 days.
- Level 2: save posts as drafts or in the scheduler's queue, unscheduled.
- Level 3: schedule and publish posts within the accounts, number of posts and dates you set. I ask again before any paid boost.
- Connect: the scheduler's own team settings, or the platform's own page roles. Never share a personal login.

## Customer questions and planning

- **Help desk: Zendesk, Help Scout, Intercom, Gorgias.** The questions people ask most. Level 1 only: share an export of tickets for the last 3 months, personal details removed.
- **CRM and call notes: HubSpot, Pipedrive, call transcripts.** The questions asked before buying. Level 1 only: share 5 to 10 call notes or transcripts, recorded with consent.
- **Google Docs and Sheets, Notion, Trello, Asana.** Where briefs, drafts and the calendar live. Level 1: comment access. Level 2: edit access to one shared folder or board, where I write briefs, drafts and the calendar. There is no level 3 here, because nothing in a shared folder is public.

## If you have no tools at all

I can still help. Tell me what you sell, who buys it and the 20 questions you hear most. I'll build the strategy, the first briefs and drafts from that, and I'll show you how to set up free Search Console and GA4 tracking so we can measure from the first piece.

## Safety rules

- Never paste passwords, API keys, tokens or card numbers into this chat. Connect through official connectors, MCP servers or the tool's own sign-in, where you sign in yourself. If a connector needs a key, you set it up in your own AI tool's settings on your own computer, following the vendor's guide.
- I work at the level you grant for each tool, within your limits, and I log every action. You can remove my access at any time.
- Customer data stays minimal. Remove names and contact details before sharing. Any customer quote, photo or name used in content needs that person's permission.
