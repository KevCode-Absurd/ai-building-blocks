# Content brief

A good brief is the difference between a piece that only this business could publish and a piece that looks like every other search result. It is also the best defence against AI slop: the brief tells the writer, or the AI, exactly what original material to use.

## When to use

- Before drafting any article, guide, landing resource, video script or newsletter feature.
- When handing work to a freelance writer or an AI drafting step.
- When refreshing an old piece, to set what must change.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 for every step, because the brief is a document. Level 2 to save it in your shared folder or as a draft in your CMS.

- The topic or question, and which pillar it belongs to (from the content strategy).
- The reader: who they are and what situation they are in.
- Search data if the piece targets search: the main query, related queries, and what currently ranks.
- The business's own material: customer questions in their words, examples, prices, data, photos, case details, an expert who can be interviewed.
- The goal of the piece and the call to action.

## Steps

1. **State the reader and the question.** One line each, in the reader's words.
2. **Read the intent.** Search the main query. Note what kind of pages rank (guides, lists, product pages, videos), what they cover, and what they all miss. If the top results are product pages, a guide may be the wrong format.
3. **Find the angle.** What can this business say that others cannot? First-hand experience, its own numbers, a strong opinion based on work done, real examples, a tool or template. If there is no angle, say so and suggest a different topic or an interview to find one.
4. **List the must-includes.** The facts, examples, data and quotes the piece must contain, with where each comes from. Name the expert to interview, with three questions to ask them.
5. **Answer first.** Write the answer to the reader's question in two or three sentences. The draft must open with this, or with a version of it.
6. **Outline the structure.** Headings that make sense alone, in the order the reader needs them. Note where each must-include goes.
7. **Set the links.** Link up to the pillar page, across to one or two related cluster pieces, and out to primary sources for any facts.
8. **Set the call to action.** One action that fits the reader's stage: subscribe, download a template, read the next piece, or book a call.
9. **Set search details** if relevant: working title, search title (around 50 to 60 characters is a common guide to avoid cut-off, not a rule), and description.
10. **Plan distribution.** The email, social and sales uses of this piece, written into the brief so the writer can collect what those need (a quote, a chart, a short clip).
11. **Set the measure and the review date.** What success looks like after 30 and 90 days, and when to check.
12. **Set the rules for AI**, if AI drafts: use only the facts in the brief, flag anything it adds for checking, and leave placeholders for experience and examples rather than inventing them.

## Checks

- The angle is specific and only this business could deliver it.
- Every must-include has a source the writer can use.
- The answer-first summary actually answers the question.
- Format matches what searchers want.
- One call to action, matched to the reader's stage.
- Distribution and measurement are in the brief, not left for later.
- Nothing asks the writer to invent experience, quotes or statistics.

## Output format

A single page with these headings: Reader; Question; Intent and what ranks; Angle; Answer first; Must-includes (with sources); Expert questions; Structure; Links; Call to action; Search title and description; Distribution; Measure and review date; Rules for AI drafting; Word range and deadline.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a two-vet practice. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Reader:** a first-time dog owner whose puppy is 8 to 12 weeks old.
**Question:** "When should my puppy have its vaccinations, and what does it cost?"
**Intent and what ranks:** informational with a local edge. Results are national charity guides and clinic price pages; none show a real local schedule with prices together.
**Angle:** the clinic's actual schedule and price list, plus the three questions owners ask most at first visits (from reception notes).
**Answer first:** a short paragraph stating the clinic's schedule and total cost, then "here is what each visit involves."
**Must-includes:**
- The clinic's current vaccination schedule and prices (from the practice manager; check the date).
- What happens at each visit (Dr. Hale, 15-minute interview).
- The three most common first-visit questions (reception notes, last 3 months).
- When puppies can go out for walks, as the clinic advises it.
**Expert questions:** What do owners most often get wrong? What should they bring? What happens if a vaccination is missed?
**Structure:** Answer first / Schedule and prices (table) / What happens at each visit / Common questions / When to walk your puppy / Book the first visit.
**Links:** up to the pillar "New puppy guide"; across to "Puppy insurance: what to check"; out to official guidance on vaccinations where the clinic agrees with it.
**Call to action:** book the first vaccination visit (GA4 key event: booking form submitted).
**Search title:** Puppy vaccinations: schedule and costs at [Business]
**Distribution:** new-client welcome email; a reception handout version; one social post with the schedule table.
**Measure and review:** bookings from this page at 30 and 90 days; Search Console clicks for "puppy vaccinations [town]". Review prices every six months.
**Rules for AI drafting:** use only the schedule, prices and quotes supplied; leave [VET QUOTE] placeholders; flag any medical statement for Dr. Hale to approve.
**Word range and deadline:** 900 to 1,300 words; draft by the 14th.
