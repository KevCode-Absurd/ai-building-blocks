# Content performance review

Content is judged by what it earns for the business, not by how much was published. This skill turns exports the client already has into decisions: which pieces to grow, fix, combine, reuse or retire.

## When to use

- A monthly or quarterly content report.
- "Is our content working?" or "Why did our traffic drop?"
- Before planning the next quarter's calendar.
- After a Google core or spam update, to check what moved.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 for every step (Search Console, GA4 and email results, or exports). Level 2 or 3 only to carry out the decisions afterwards, such as a refresh or a new search title, at the level you grant. Merges, redirects and retired pages always need a new yes.

- **Search Console export** (Performance, Search results): pages and queries, last 3 months and the same period a year earlier if available. Clicks, impressions, CTR, average position.
- **GA4:** key events (sign-ups, enquiries, purchases) by landing page for the same period, and which events are marked as key events.
- **Email platform:** clicks and conversions per newsletter or campaign. Ignore opens; privacy features make them unreliable.
- **Self-reported attribution:** answers to "How did you hear about us?" if the forms ask it.
- **The content list:** URL, title, publish and update dates, pillar, author.
- **The goal and primary measure** from the content strategy.

If GA4 key events are not set up, the first recommendation is to set them up; until then, report search and email results only and say so.

## Steps

1. **Restate the goal and primary measure.** Every judgement below is against these.
2. **Join the data by page.** One row per piece: clicks, impressions, CTR, position, key events, key event rate, email clicks where it was featured, attribution mentions.
3. **Compare periods.** Change in clicks and impressions against the previous period and the same period last year. Seasonal businesses need the year-on-year view.
4. **Check the context.** Did a Google core or spam update run in the period (check Google's Search Status Dashboard)? Did tracking change? Did the site change? Note anything that explains a shift before blaming the content.
5. **Sort each piece** into one decision:
   - *Grow:* earns key events; add internal links to it, feature it in email, promote it.
   - *Refresh:* clicks or position falling, or facts out of date; update facts, depth and answer-first summary.
   - *Fix the click:* high impressions, low CTR; rewrite the search title and description to match intent.
   - *Fix the conversion:* good traffic, few key events; improve the call to action and the match to the reader's stage.
   - *Merge:* two pages competing for the same query; combine into the stronger URL and redirect the other.
   - *Repurpose:* strong piece not yet used in email, social or sales.
   - *Retire:* no traffic, no key events, no sales use, no link value; remove or redirect if it serves no reader.
   - *Leave:* doing its job.
6. **Rank the actions** by likely business value: pieces close to key events first, then high-impression pages, then the rest.
7. **Find the pattern.** Which pillars, formats and channels earn the most per hour spent? What should the next quarter do more of, and less of?
8. **Write the report** in plain language for the owner, with the decisions table attached.

## Checks

- Every decision ties back to the goal and primary measure.
- Context (updates, tracking changes, seasonality) checked before conclusions.
- Opens are not used as a success measure.
- Retire decisions consider sales and link use, not just traffic.
- Each action has an owner and a date.
- Numbers in the report match the exports; nothing estimated without saying so.

## Output format

1. **Headline:** what content earned this period against the goal, in one or two sentences.
2. **Three numbers:** primary measure, search clicks, email clicks; each with change and a one-line reason.
3. **What worked and what did not**, by pillar and format.
4. **Decisions table:** page, decision, reason, next action, owner, date.
5. **Next period's focus:** three priorities.
6. **Data notes:** gaps, tracking issues, assumptions.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a rota and shift app for independent cafes, with a blog and a monthly newsletter; the period is one quarter. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

**Headline:** content brought in 41 trial sign-ups this quarter (up from 29), almost all from three guides about staff scheduling rules and rota templates. Newer opinion posts drew visits but no sign-ups.

**Three numbers**

| Measure | This quarter | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trial sign-ups from content pages (key event) | 41 | +41% | Rota template guide added a download step |
| Search clicks | 6,240 | +12% | Two refreshed guides moved up |
| Newsletter clicks | 380 | -8% | Fewer issues sent (2, not 3) |

**Decisions (top five)**

| Page | Decision | Reason | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free cafe rota template | Grow | 19 sign-ups; top earner | Link from 6 related posts; feature in next email |
| How to plan cafe staff hours | Refresh | Clicks down 30% year on year; costs from 2024 | Update numbers, add worked example |
| Rota apps compared | Fix the click | 9,800 impressions, 0.9% CTR | New title naming the cafe use case |
| Cafe rota tips / Rota tips for small cafes | Merge | Both rank 15th to 25th for the same queries | Combine into one; redirect the weaker URL |
| Why we love Mondays (opinion) | Leave | No search role, but strong email clicks | Keep for newsletter use only |

**Next quarter's focus:** two new template-led guides; refresh the three oldest guides; send three newsletters again.

**Data notes:** "How did you hear about us?" was added to the sign-up form only in week 6; earlier sign-ups have no self-reported source.
