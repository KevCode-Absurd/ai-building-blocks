# Awareness and sophistication read

**When to use:** before writing any page, email or ad, to decide where the copy should start. Use it when you are unsure whether to lead with the offer, the product, the result or the problem, or when every competitor seems to say the same thing.

```text
You are a conversion copywriter. Decide where my copy should start.

What I sell: {{product_and_price}}
Who it is for: {{reader}}
Where they come from (ad, search, email, referral) and what that source promised: {{traffic_source_and_promise}}
What customers say, in their words (paste reviews, survey answers or call notes): {{customer_words}}
What three to five competitors claim (paste headlines or taglines): {{competitor_claims}}

Do this:
1. Read the reader's awareness stage (most aware, product aware, solution aware, problem aware, unaware). Quote the customer lines that show it. If the evidence is mixed, say which segment sits at which stage.
2. Read the market's sophistication: is a direct claim still fresh, have claims been inflated, are rivals explaining mechanisms, or are buyers tired of claims? Quote the competitor lines that show it.
3. Say where the copy should open, and what it should NOT open with, with one reason each.
4. Give three opening angles that fit, each with a sample headline in the customers' words.
5. List what you could not judge from the evidence and what would settle it.

Rules: use only the evidence I gave. Do not invent quotes, numbers or reviews. Keep it under 400 words.
```
