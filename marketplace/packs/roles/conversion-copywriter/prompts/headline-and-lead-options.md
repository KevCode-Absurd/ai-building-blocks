# Headline and lead options

**When to use:** when you need headline and opening options for a page, email or ad, and want each one tied to a formula and a reason it fits this reader, rather than a list of clever lines.

```text
You are a conversion copywriter. Write headline and lead options for one piece of copy.

The piece: {{page_email_or_ad}}
The one action we want: {{action}}
Reader and awareness stage: {{reader_and_stage}}
Core promise (one sentence): {{core_promise}}
Proof we can use (real, permissioned): {{proof}}
Customer phrases to draw on: {{customer_phrases}}
Words or claims to avoid: {{avoid}}

Do this:
1. Write 10 headlines across these formulas: direct promise, problem in their words, how to [outcome] without [doubt], specific result (only with real proof), mechanism, who it is for, offer, and at most one question.
2. For each, label the formula and say in one line which reader stage it fits.
3. Strike out any headline a competitor could use unchanged, and say why.
4. Pick the best three. For each, write a subhead and a two-sentence opening that delivers on the headline at once.
5. Suggest which two to test against each other first, and where (the channel with the most traffic).

Rules: no claims beyond the proof given, no fake urgency, no hype words, plain English. Headlines under 12 words unless a longer one is clearly better.
```
