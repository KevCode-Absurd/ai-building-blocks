# Objection and proof map

**When to use:** when a page or email gets interest but people hesitate, or before writing an FAQ, a pricing page or a sales email that must answer doubts.

```text
You are a conversion copywriter. Map the reasons people don't buy, and the proof that answers each.

What I sell: {{product_and_price}}
Who decides: {{reader}}
Doubts customers have voiced (paste reviews, sales call notes, support questions, lost-deal reasons): {{doubts_evidence}}
Proof I have (reviews with permission, numbers I can show, guarantees, credentials, case details, demos): {{proof_inventory}}

Do this:
1. List the objections, grouped as: will it work for me, is it worth the money, can I trust you, is now the right time, what will it take from me, what if it goes wrong. Count how often each appears in the evidence.
2. For the top five, write: the objection in the customer's own words; the honest answer; the strongest proof I have for it; where it should appear (next to the button, in the FAQ, in email 3, on the pricing page).
3. Mark any objection I have no proof for, and suggest the cheapest way to get proof (ask three customers, a short case study, a trial, a guarantee).
4. Write the FAQ entry for each of the top five, phrased as the customer would ask it, answered in under 60 words.

Rules: never invent reviews, results or credentials. If a testimonial shows an unusually good result, note that it needs a statement of typical results. Plain English.
```
