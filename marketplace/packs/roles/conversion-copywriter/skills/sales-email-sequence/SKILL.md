# Sales email sequence

A sales sequence is a short conversation with one person who has shown some interest. Each email does one job, asks for one thing, and earns the next open. This skill plans the sequence from research, then writes it.

## When to use

- New subscribers or lead-magnet sign-ups who never buy.
- Enquiries or quotes that go quiet.
- Free trials that end without upgrading.
- A launch, a reopening, or a seasonal offer with a real end date.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read past results. Level 2 to build the sequence in your email platform, switched off. Level 3 to switch it on for the audience you approved, within your limits. A new audience or list always needs a new yes.

- **Trigger and audience:** what put someone into the sequence, and what they already know.
- **The goal:** the one action (buy, book, upgrade, reply).
- **The offer:** price, terms, guarantee, any real deadline and why it exists.
- **Research:** a VoC bank or at least the top doubts and the words customers use. If you have none, ask for ten reviews and the five most common pre-sale questions.
- **Proof you can use:** permissioned reviews, case details, numbers you can show.
- **Platform and limits:** the email tool, whether it can stop the sequence when someone buys or replies, and sender name.
- **Past results**, if any: clicks and sales by email, not just opens.

## Steps

1. **Set the stop rules first.** The sequence ends when someone buys, replies, books, or unsubscribes. If the platform cannot stop on purchase, say so: buyers who get "last chance" emails after buying lose trust.
2. **Map the forces.** From research, write the main push (why now), pull (what they want), anxiety (what worries them) and habit (what they will do instead). Most sequences fail because they repeat the pull and never touch anxiety or habit.
3. **Plan the sequence.** Usually four to six emails over 10 to 14 days. A common shape:
   1. *Deliver and orient:* give what they signed up for, set expectations, one small next step.
   2. *Name the problem:* their words, the cost of leaving it, a useful tip they can use today.
   3. *Show the way:* how the product solves it, with one concrete proof.
   4. *Answer the biggest doubt:* the top anxiety, with proof and the guarantee.
   5. *Break the habit:* compare the cost of carrying on as now with the cost of acting.
   6. *Decide:* a plain summary of the offer, the real deadline if there is one, and an easy "not for me" option.
4. **Write each email** with: subject line, preview text, one idea, one link or button, and a plain sign-off from a real person. Short paragraphs. The call to action names the outcome.
5. **Subject lines:** specific and honest. Use the reader's words. No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:", no false urgency, no bait that the email does not pay off. Write one final subject line per email, within any character limit in the brief. If the brief asks for options, label them as options below the email, never inside the subject field, each within the same limit.
6. **Timing:** space the first three emails a day or two apart, then slow down. Put the decision email close to any real deadline, not before it.
7. **Compliance and deliverability:** a working unsubscribe link in every email. Gmail requires one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders (over 5,000 messages a day to Gmail) since February 2024; most email platforms handle it. Any deadline or limited quantity must be real. Testimonials must be real and permissioned.
8. **Measure:** judge by clicks, replies and sales per email. Open rates are unreliable because Apple Mail Privacy Protection loads images for many readers whether or not they open.

## Checks

- One job and one call to action per email.
- Stop rules are written and the platform can apply them.
- At least one email deals with the top anxiety and one with habit.
- Every claim and testimonial is real; any deadline is real and honoured.
- Subject lines are honest and match the email.
- Each email makes sense on its own, for someone who skipped the last one.
- A reader can say no easily (a "not for me" link or reply option).

## Output format

1. **Sequence plan:** table with email number, day, job, force addressed, subject line, call to action.
2. **Stop rules and segment rules.**
3. **Full copy** for each email: subject line, preview text, body, button text, sign-off.
4. **What to measure** and what would make you change the sequence.
5. **Assumptions:** at most three lines, only those that change what to do. Where the law needs a sign-off (such as permission to quote a customer), put one line next to the email it affects.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a small pottery studio that sells a £25 taster class and a six-week wheel course. Trigger: someone books the taster. Goal: book the six-week course. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Sequence plan**

| # | Day | Job | Force | Subject | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Prepare for the taster | Pull | "What to wear on Saturday (and what not to)" | Add to calendar |
| 2 | 1 after taster | Celebrate the first pot | Pull | "Your pot goes in the kiln on Tuesday" | See the six-week course |
| 3 | 3 | Answer "I'm not artistic" | Anxiety | "Most of our students said this first" | Read three students' first pots |
| 4 | 6 | Habit: "I'll do it next term" | Habit | "Next term starts in 9 days" | Book my place |
| 5 | 8 | Decide | Push | "Course starts Monday" | Book my place, or tell us not now |

**Stop rules:** stop on booking, reply, or unsubscribe. Anyone who replies "not now" moves to the monthly newsletter.

**Email 3 (body)**

Subject: Most of our students said this first
Preview: You do not need to be good at art. You need six Saturdays.

Hi Sam,

On Saturday a lot of people tell us the same thing: "I'm not artistic."

Here is what actually happens on the six-week course. Weeks one and two are just centring clay, the part everyone finds hard. By week four most people have a set of bowls they use at home. We keep groups to eight, so you are never waiting long for help.

[Three first pots from last term's beginners, shown with their permission]

If you finish the course and feel it was not for you, we refund the last two weeks.

**See the six-week course**

Maya
[Business]

*Why this works:* it answers the top doubt in the customer's own words, shows proof from people like the reader, states the guarantee plainly, and asks for one thing. Email 5 uses the real start date; a scarcity line such as "last 2 places" goes in only if it is true on the day it sends.
