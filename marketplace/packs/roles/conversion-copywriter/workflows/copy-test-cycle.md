# Copy test cycle

**Cadence:** one cycle per test, typically two to eight weeks. Review the test log monthly.

**Trigger:** a page, email or ad already has steady traffic and the client wants to improve it with evidence rather than opinion.

## Steps

1. **Check the numbers (level 1; tool: analytics export, such as GA4, or the email or ad platform's report).** Get visits, conversions and conversion rate for the last 4 to 8 weeks, by device and traffic source.
2. **Find the biggest problem (skill: copy-review-and-test-plan).** Score the current copy, rank the top three problems, and look at behaviour data if available (session recordings or heatmaps from Microsoft Clarity or Hotjar).
3. **Back the hypothesis with research (skill: voice-of-customer-mining).** Make sure the change comes from what customers said or did, not from taste. If research is thin, run a one-question poll on the page first.
4. **Write the variant (prompt: headline-and-lead-options, or skill: landing-page-copy for a section rewrite).** Test one big idea, not five small tweaks at once.
5. **Size the test (skill: copy-review-and-test-plan, step 6).** Calculate sample size and run time. If it would take more than about six to eight weeks, switch the test to a higher-volume channel (ad headlines, email subject lines) or ship as a judgement call and record it.
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves the variant, the metric, the guardrail, the run time and the decision rule before launch. At level 3 this is the one-time confirmation of the action list.
6. **Run the test (level 2 to set it up without starting it, level 3 to start it; tool: the client's testing feature in their page builder, email platform or ad platform).** Run whole weeks. Do not stop early because it looks good. Check once a week only for bugs and broken tracking.
7. **Read the result.** Compare against the decision rule set in advance. If the lift looks surprisingly large, check tracking and the split before believing it.
   **Approval point 2:** the client decides to ship, keep the control, or run a follow-up. At level 3, if the client agreed in point 1 that a clear win ships, I ship it and log it; any other result goes back to the client.
8. **Log and learn.** Add one line to the test log: date, hypothesis, variant, result, what we learned, next idea. Feed the lesson back into the message brief.

## Outputs

- Ranked problem list and hypothesis.
- Variant copy.
- Test plan with sample size working.
- Result summary and updated test log.

## Needs the client's approval

- The variant and test plan before launch (point 1).
- The ship or keep decision (point 2).
- Any change to a live page, email or ad outside the agreed test. At level 3 I make only the changes in the test plan; at level 1 or 2 the client or their team publishes.
