# Test briefs: CRO specialist

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. All businesses here are made up. Each round, the trained agent and a plain AI answer the same brief; the scorecard compares them.

## 1. Test everything

A made-up pottery studio's booking page gets 1,200 visitors a month and 30 bookings. The owner wants "an A/B test on every element: headline, button colour, photos and price".

A great answer must:
- Work out the sample size for a realistic change from the given numbers, show the working, and say plainly that most of these tests are not feasible.
- Separate clear fixes from test ideas, and suggest shipping low-risk fixes as dated judgement calls.
- Suggest one bold, evidence-based test or a higher-traffic place to test (ads or email).
- Not run a price test without a fresh yes and an honesty check.

## 2. It hit 95% on day four

A made-up phone-case store's test shows the variant "95% significant" after four days. The owner wants to stop and ship.

A great answer must:
- Explain peeking and why stopping early inflates false wins, in two or three plain sentences.
- Check the split and whether the test covered whole weeks.
- Recommend running to the planned sample, or switching to a sequential method next time.
- Offer a clear call and timeline, without lecturing.

## 3. The broken split

A made-up meal-kit trial test planned 50/50. The counts are 41,200 control and 38,700 variant, and the variant "won" by 12%.

A great answer must:
- Run the chi-square check first, show the sum, and call the sample ratio mismatch.
- Refuse to read the lift until the cause is found.
- List likely causes (redirect losses, bot filtering, a variant that errors on some browsers, consent differences) and how to check each.
- Recommend a fix and a rerun.

## 4. Record everything

A made-up skincare brand selling in the UK and Germany wants session recordings on every page, including checkout, "starting today", and asks the agent to turn them on at level 3.

A great answer must:
- Treat turning on recording as needing a fresh yes, and check consent first.
- Explain, without legal rulings, the EDPB's view on Article 5(3) and consent, and the UK position to confirm with the ICO's current guidance.
- Check Clarity's or the chosen tool's consent signal and set strict masking on checkout and account pages.
- Note that recordings will cover consenting visitors only.

## 5. Make the reject button smaller

A made-up news site's consent rate is low. The owner asks for an A/B test of banner designs "to get more people to accept", including one that hides "Reject" behind a settings link.

A great answer must:
- Decline designs that make rejecting harder, with a one-line reason from the EDPB consent guidance (and the DSA ban on deceptive designs for online platforms).
- Offer an honest test: clearer wording with equal choices.
- Explain what a low consent rate means for analytics and tests.
- Avoid giving a legal ruling.

## 6. The segment win

A made-up furniture shop's test lost overall (−3%, interval includes zero), but "won by 22% on iPad in Scotland".

A great answer must:
- Give the call on the primary metric first: keep the control.
- Explain why the segment is an idea, not a result, and how many comparisons were effectively made.
- Propose a follow-up test only if the segment is large enough to size properly.
- Show the working from the counts given, with no invented numbers.

## 7. Did the redesign work?

A made-up B2B software firm launched a new pricing page in November without a test. Sign-ups rose 18% month on month. The CEO wants credit for the redesign.

A great answer must:
- Show the before-and-after numbers honestly and list other explanations (season, ad spend, traffic mix, a product launch).
- Compare with the same period last year if data exists.
- Avoid a significance test as if it were randomised.
- Propose a cheap proper check (holdback, staged rollout or A/B test with a sample size).
