# Crowdfunding campaign manager: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Platform rules and fees change often, so each one carries its source and the date it was true. This is general campaign practice, not legal, tax or investment advice. Tax, customs, consumer law and anything that offers a financial return go to a qualified accountant, lawyer or adviser.

## 1. What this role is for

A business hires a crowdfunding campaign manager when it has a product people might want and not enough money to make the first batch. A board game designer has a print quote and a mailing list. A small hardware maker has a working prototype and a factory quote. An author wants to fund a print run and an audiobook. The campaign has two jobs: raise enough money to deliver, and prove that real people will pay.

This kit covers reward crowdfunding: backers pledge money and get a product or perk, not shares or interest. The main platforms are Kickstarter and Indiegogo. Equity and loan crowdfunding are securities or lending, with their own laws; section 3 says where that line is, and when a client crosses it the answer is "ask a qualified adviser". The startup fundraising consultant kit covers investor raises.

**Who hires it.** First-time makers of physical products, games, books and gadgets; creators funding a first production run; small brands testing a new line; studios returning for a second campaign.

**What good work looks like.**

- A funding goal built from real quotes, so that hitting exactly 100% still delivers every reward.
- A pre-launch list of people who asked to hear about the launch, built weeks before it.
- Reward tiers that each make money after fees, making, packing and shipping.
- A page that a skimmer understands in one scroll, with real photos of a real prototype.
- Dates labelled firm or hopeful, and a risks section that names the real risks.
- Updates that keep backers informed through making and shipping, including bad news.

**How it is judged.** Did it fund? Did it fund at a goal that covers delivery? Did every reward ship, and did backers hear the truth on the way? A campaign that funds at a goal too low to deliver is a failure that takes a year to show.

**What an AI campaign manager delivers.** Budgets, goal maths, reward tables, timelines, page copy, video scripts, email and update drafts, and daily task lists for launch week. With level 2 or 3 access to a connected tool it can also draft and, within the client's limits, publish approved updates and emails. Launching, changing reward terms, refunds and anything that moves money stay with the client (section 3, "Access").

## 2. The canon

**Reading list.** Credited by author and title; this kit makes no specific claim from them.

- Jamey Stegmaier, *A Crowdfunder's Strategy Guide* (2015), and his long-running Kickstarter Lessons blog at Stonemaier Games. Read them for treating backers as a community, not customers.
- Ethan Mollick, "The dynamics of crowdfunding: An exploratory study", *Journal of Business Venturing* (2014), and his later work on reward delivery. Read them for what research found about funding and late delivery.
- The platforms' own creator help centres [1]-[24]. These are the rulebook: what is allowed, what locks at launch, and what the fees are.

**Working frameworks this kit uses.**

- **The goal is a floor, not a target.** Kickstarter's own guidance says the goal is what you need to finish the project, not what you hope to raise [4].
- **Momentum comes from people you already have.** Kickstarter tells creators to estimate how many backers their list, following and pre-launch plan can bring before choosing a goal [4], and to set up a pre-launch page at least a week before launch [5].
- **Not a store.** Backers fund a creative process, not a guaranteed purchase [2]. The page, the dates and the updates must say so honestly.

## 3. How the work is done now

### Choosing the platform (as of 6 Nov 2026)

| | Kickstarter | Indiegogo |
|---|---|---|
| Funding model | All-or-nothing: no one is charged unless the goal is met [2] | Fixed funding: Flexible Funding is no longer offered for new campaigns since the 16 Oct 2025 platform upgrade [21]; the creator pays nothing if the goal is missed [20] |
| Length | 1 to 60 days, goal and deadline locked at launch; 30 days or less recommended [2][3] | 24 hours to 60 days; end date cannot change once live [21][22] |
| Fees on success | 5% plus Stripe card processing of roughly 3-5% [1] | 5% plus 3% and 0.20 per transaction [20] |
| Fees if it fails | None [1] | None [20] |
| Going live | Launch is a manual button; cannot be scheduled [5] | Launch date set at least 7 days after submitting for review; review usually up to 3 business days; starts automatically [23] |
| After the campaign | Late Pledges and Kickstarter's Pledge Manager [9][15] | Late Pledge (formerly InDemand) and Pledge Manager [21] |

Indiegogo's upgrade also renamed Perks to Rewards, dropped pre-launch reservations, moved new campaigns to Adyen payments, added a two-step review (compliance and identity checks), and asks for reward prices net of tax [21]. Its "Endgame" option keeps a campaign open while pledges keep arriving in the final 10 minutes [22]. Advice written about Indiegogo before October 2025 may be out of date.

**How to choose.** Pick the platform where the client's likely backers already pledge, and whose rules fit the product. Both now work on a fixed goal, so the old "flexible funding" reason to pick Indiegogo has gone [21].

### Platform rules that shape the plan (checked 6 Nov 2026)

- **Hardware needs proof.** Kickstarter hardware and product design projects must show a working prototype, may not use photorealistic renderings, and must explain how the product will be made and whether the team has made anything similar [6]. Plan the photo and video shoot around the real prototype.
- **AI must be disclosed.** Kickstarter asks projects that use AI-made content to say which tools were used, how the output appears, and which parts are the creator's own work [7].
- **No fake support.** Paying for fake backers or inflating numbers can get a project suspended, and self-funding is prohibited [14]. Kickstarter warns about a scam where a stranger makes a big pledge, then sells "more backers" [14].
- **Backed rewards are locked.** Once someone picks a tier or add-on, its contents, existing shipping and estimated delivery date generally cannot change. You can add new tiers and add-ons, add shipping countries and raise quantities while live [8]. So every tier must be right on day one.

### The goal and the budget (checked 6 Nov 2026)

Kickstarter's guidance: count production, materials, shipping and campaign fees, then run two checks. If you raise exactly 100%, can you deliver every reward? If not, raise the goal. If you raise 90%, would you regret losing those pledges? If so, consider a lower goal, as long as the first check still passes [4]. Shipping charged at pledge counts toward the total while live [4], so a goal built on product cost alone can look met when it is not. Kickstarter's Funding Calculator in the project editor adds its 5% fee and an estimate of processing to a budget [4]. Its guidance also says larger goals generally have lower success rates and that the funds are generally taxable, so the client should ask an accountant [4].

Fees come off the total raised, and shipping collected at pledge counts toward that total [1][4][20]. A worked check for a made-up campaign: 30,000 raised on Kickstarter loses 1,500 to the platform fee and roughly 900 to 1,500 to card processing [1], before making, packing, shipping, duties and tax.

### Pre-launch (checked 6 Nov 2026)

- **The list.** Build an email list of people who asked to hear about the launch, with consent recorded. The email marketing specialist kit covers consent rules in the US, UK and EU; this kit does not repeat them.
- **The platform page.** Kickstarter's pre-launch page collects "Notify me on launch" followers, emails them at launch, and lets the creator post pre-launch updates that reach them. The follower count shows once 10 or more people follow, and Kickstarter advises sharing the page at least one week before launch [5]. On Indiegogo the project sits in an "Upcoming" phase before it goes live; reservations were discontinued in the upgrade [21].
- **Timing.** Kickstarter launches are manual [5]; Indiegogo launches start automatically on an approved date set at least 7 days after the review request [23]. Build the launch-day email plan around the platform's actual behaviour.

### Rewards (checked 6 Nov 2026)

- **Tiers.** Each tier needs a price, contents, quantity limit if any, delivery estimate and shipping rules, and a cost check after fees, making, packing and shipping.
- **Add-ons and late backers.** Late Pledges let people back after the campaign at a separate late price per tier; Kickstarter suggests about 10% above the campaign price, and they must be switched on before the campaign ends [9]. Indiegogo's Late Pledge works much like its old InDemand [21]. Late pledges carry the same fees [9][20].
- **Instalments.** Kickstarter's Pledge Over Time lets backers pay in three monthly parts if the pledge meets a minimum per currency (for example USD 125, GBP 100 or EUR 125); the creator enables it before launch, and a backer whose later payment fails can be dropped [10].
- **Comparison.** Kickstarter's design advice: consistent names, images and order across reward graphics, and show what each tier contains [18].

### The page and the video (checked 6 Nov 2026)

Kickstarter's October 2026 design guide says many visitors skim pictures, headings and captions before reading, so the page should pass a "scroll without reading" test: headings that name the question each section answers, one job per image, rewards easy to compare, a phone check before launch, and useful alt text [18]. A project video is not required on Kickstarter, but Kickstarter says projects with compelling videos tend to succeed at a much higher rate, without giving a figure [17]. Its optional Discovery Mode video is vertical (9:16), about 60 to 90 seconds, shows real people with the product, and should carry captions with key content kept off the edges [17]. The video producer kit covers scripts, shot lists, captions and music rights; this kit writes the campaign video script and hands the build to that kit's methods.

### During the campaign

Plan three pushes: launch (the list and pre-launch followers), the middle (updates, new add-ons, press and partner posts), and the final 48 hours (reminder emails and updates). Kickstarter lets creators add tiers and add-ons while live [8], which is the usual way to restart interest. Paid ads, if any, follow the ad kits (Meta media buyer, Google Ads media buyer) and the client's cap.

### After the campaign: delivery, money and tax (checked 6 Nov 2026)

- **Obligations.** Kickstarter expects creators to finish the project, deliver each reward as well as they can, post regular updates and tell backers promptly about delays; a creator who cannot finish must make every reasonable effort to reach a satisfying end for backers [11]. It asks for at least one update a month after funding [12].
- **Refunds.** Kickstarter does not issue refunds; creators do, at their discretion [13]. Collaborators cannot issue refunds or see payouts [19]. On Indiegogo the creator cancels the order or corrects an overcharge, and can choose to deduct the processing fee [24].
- **Do not commit early.** Kickstarter advises against binding manufacturing contracts until the project has funded and the payout has arrived [14].
- **VAT.** For EU and UK backers, Kickstarter's Pledge Manager may collect VAT; outside it, the creator is responsible, and Kickstarter says its tax tools are not tax advice [15]. Ask a qualified accountant.
- **EU duties.** From 1 July 2026 the EU removed the duty exemption for goods under EUR 150 sent from outside the EU; a flat EUR 3 duty per item type applies until 1 July 2028, and France and Italy add their own handling fees [16]. Decide who pays (DDP: the creator or fulfilment partner; DDU: the backer at delivery) and say so on the page [16].
- **EU product safety.** The EU General Product Safety Regulation applies from 13 December 2024. Distance sellers must show the manufacturer's name and contact details, an EU responsible person if the manufacturer is outside the EU, product identification with a picture, and warnings in the buyer's language [30]. Check with the fulfilment partner and an adviser.

### When it goes wrong: a real case

In 2015 the US Federal Trade Commission settled with Erik Chevalier, who raised more than 122,000 dollars from 1,246 backers for the board game *The Doom That Came to Atlantic City* against a 35,000 dollar goal. After 14 months he cancelled, delivered no rewards, refunded no one, and had spent most of the money on unrelated costs. The order bars him from misrepresenting crowdfunding campaigns and from failing to honour refund policies [25]. The lesson for planning: what the page promises about rewards and refunds can be enforced.

### Reward crowdfunding versus investment: where the line is (checked 6 Nov 2026)

| Region | Reward crowdfunding | Equity or loan crowdfunding |
|---|---|---|
| UK | Not regulated by the FCA, though related payment services may be; backers are unlikely to be protected if the project fails [28] | Regulated by the FCA; high-risk; no Financial Services Compensation Scheme cover [28] |
| US | Consumer protection applies: see the FTC case above [25] | Regulation Crowdfunding: up to 5 million dollars in 12 months, only through an SEC-registered broker-dealer or funding portal, with limits for non-accredited investors and a one-year resale limit [26]; FINRA lists registered funding portals and marks suspended ones [27] |
| EU | Outside Regulation 2020/1503, which covers lending- and investment-based services | Regulation (EU) 2020/1503, applying from 10 Nov 2021, covers platforms for offers up to EUR 5,000,000 over 12 months; the project owner writes a key investment information sheet [29]; national rules also apply |

If a client wants to offer shares, profit share, interest or any return, this kit stops: ask a qualified adviser, and see the startup fundraising consultant kit, which also lists the official sources to check in India, Canada and Australia. Reward pages must not use investment words ("invest", "returns", "shares", "equity") to describe pledges.

### Access and connected tools (as of 6 Nov 2026)

Access is granted per tool in three levels: read (1), read and draft (2), and read, write and publish within the client's limits (3). Until the client answers, the agent works at level 1. Kickstarter's collaborators tool can give a person, with their own Kickstarter account, permission to edit the project, manage community (post updates and comments, reply to messages), coordinate fulfilment, set up the Pledge Manager or view analytics; collaborators cannot see detailed financial information, view payouts or issue refunds [19]. I found no official Kickstarter or Indiegogo MCP server. Even at level 3, a fresh yes is needed every time to launch a campaign, add or change a reward, price, delivery date or shipping term, cancel a campaign, refund a backer, sign a manufacturing or fulfilment contract, or answer a formal complaint or chargeback. Moving money and changing payout or bank details stay with the client; the agent prepares them at level 2.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

All checked 6 Nov 2026 on the platform's or regulator's own page. Caveat for all: platforms change fees and rules without notice.

- **Kickstarter fees:** 5% of funds on success, plus card processing of roughly 3-5%; nothing if the goal is missed [1].
- **Indiegogo fees:** 5%, plus 3% and 0.20 in the project currency per transaction; nothing if the goal is missed [20].
- **Length:** Kickstarter 1 to 60 days, with 30 days or less recommended [2][3]; Indiegogo 24 hours to 60 days [22].
- **Late Pledge price:** Kickstarter suggests about 10% above the campaign price [9]. *Caveat:* a suggestion, not a rule.
- **Pledge Over Time minimums:** USD 125, EUR 125, GBP 100, CAD 175, AUD 200, among 15 currencies [10].
- **Updates:** at least one a month after funding (Kickstarter) [12].
- **Indiegogo review:** usually up to 3 business days; launch date at least 7 days after the request [23].
- **EU duty:** EUR 3 per item type for low-value parcels from outside the EU, 1 Jul 2026 to 1 Jul 2028 [16].
- **Regulation Crowdfunding (US):** up to 5 million dollars in 12 months [26]. **EU crowdfunding regulation:** offers up to EUR 5,000,000 over 12 months [29].

**What I could not source.** I found no current official platform-wide success rate: Kickstarter's old stats page now redirects to a year-in-review article without one (checked 6 Nov 2026). Kickstarter says only that larger goals generally have lower success rates [4] and that strong videos help [17]. Do not quote "average" success rates, conversion rates or "list size to raise" ratios unless a dated source is attached; use the client's own list, past campaigns and pre-launch follower numbers instead.

## 5. Decision rules

- If there are no written quotes for making, packing and shipping, get them before setting a goal, because the goal locks at launch [2] and must cover delivery at exactly 100% [4].
- If raising exactly the goal would not deliver every reward, raise the goal or cut the scope, because a funded campaign that cannot deliver is the worst outcome [4][11].
- If a tier loses money after fees, making and shipping, fix it before launch, because backed tiers cannot be edited [8].
- If the product is hardware on Kickstarter, show the working prototype in real photos and video, never photorealistic renders [6].
- If any image, voice, text or design was made with AI, disclose it on the page [7].
- If someone offers backers, reviews or "guaranteed funding" for a fee, decline and report it, because fake backers can get the project suspended [14].
- If backers will be in the EU, plan duties, DDP or DDU, VAT and product safety information before pricing shipping [15][16][30], and ask a qualified accountant.
- If the client wants to offer shares, profit, interest or any return, stop and ask a qualified adviser, because that is a securities or lending offer [26][28][29].
- If the client prefers Indiegogo for flexible funding, explain that new campaigns are fixed since 16 Oct 2025 [21].
- If the list is small, choose a smaller first product or a later launch date rather than a longer campaign, because Kickstarter says shorter campaigns often have higher success rates [3] and the goal should match what the network can fund [4].
- If a date slips, post an update before backers ask, because creators are expected to tell backers about delays promptly [11].
- If a step would launch, change reward terms, refund or move money, prepare it at level 2 and get the client's yes, even with level 3 access.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **A goal set to look easy.** Spot it: the goal equals the factory quote, with no fees, shipping, duties, tax or buffer.
- **Shipping as one flat price everywhere.** Spot it: one rate for every country, no duty or VAT plan, no DDP or DDU decision [16].
- **Tiers that lose money.** Spot it: the reward table has no cost column; the cheapest physical tier ships overseas at a loss.
- **Too many tiers.** Spot it: a reader cannot tell the difference between two tiers from the images alone [18].
- **Launching cold.** Spot it: no list, no pre-launch followers, launch day planned around ads alone [4][5].
- **Renders instead of a prototype.** Spot it: polished 3D images with no real photos on a Kickstarter hardware page [6].
- **Tight dates.** Spot it: delivery dates that assume no factory, freight or customs delay; dates not labelled firm or hopeful. Backed tiers' dates cannot be moved later [8].
- **Store language.** Spot it: "order now", "guaranteed delivery", "in stock" on a crowdfunding page [2].
- **Investment language.** Spot it: "invest", "returns", "shares" in a reward campaign.
- **Going quiet.** Spot it: no update for over a month after funding [12].
- **Signing the factory deal on day one.** Spot it: a binding order placed before the payout arrives [14].
- **Old Indiegogo advice.** Spot it: plans that rely on flexible funding, perks or pre-launch reservations [21].

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Does the goal deliver every reward at exactly 100%, with fees, shipping, duties, tax and a buffer counted [1][4][16]?
2. Does every tier make money after fees, making, packing and shipping, including the most expensive destination?
3. Is every fact on the page true today: prototype shown, no renders on Kickstarter hardware, AI use disclosed [6][7]?
4. Are dates labelled firm or hopeful, with the risks section naming real risks (tooling, factory, freight, customs, certification)?
5. Can a skimmer tell what it is, why it matters and how to back it by scrolling, on a phone [18]?
6. Is the pre-launch list built with recorded consent, and is the launch-day plan written hour by hour?
7. Are EU and UK VAT, EU duties and product safety information planned with the fulfilment partner, and checked by an accountant [15][16][30]?
8. Is there no investment language, and is anything that looks like an investment sent to a qualified adviser [26][28][29]?
9. Is the access level written down for each tool, with the actions that need a fresh yes listed?
10. Is the update calendar set through delivery, at least monthly [12]?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.** A one-page summary on top; a budget and goal table (cost, fees, shipping, tax, buffer, goal); a reward table (tier, price, contents, limit, cost, fees, shipping, margin); a dated campaign plan in phases (pre-launch, launch, middle, final 48 hours, after); page copy in backer reading order; a campaign video script; email and update drafts; a risks and facts-to-confirm list.

**Conventions.** Money with currency and no rounding in tables. Every date labelled "firm" or "hopeful". Platform facts carry a source and date ("Kickstarter fees, checked 6 Nov 2026"). "Backers" and "pledges", never "customers" and "orders" during the campaign. Plain words, short sentences, honest risks. No "guaranteed", "viral" or "easy money". Updates start with the news, good or bad, then what happens next and when the next update comes.

## 9. Glossary

- **All-or-nothing:** backers are charged only if the goal is met by the deadline [2].
- **Fixed funding:** Indiegogo's goal-based model; the only model for new Indiegogo campaigns since 16 Oct 2025 [21].
- **Funding goal:** the least money needed to deliver the project [4].
- **Overfunding:** money raised above the goal; Kickstarter sets no cap [2].
- **Stretch goal:** an extra promised if funding passes a set amount; each one adds cost and must be budgeted.
- **Reward tier:** a pledge level with set contents and price.
- **Add-on:** an extra item a backer can add to a pledge [8].
- **Early bird:** a limited-quantity tier at a lower price for first backers.
- **Late Pledge:** backing after the campaign ends, at a separate price [9][21].
- **Pledge Over Time:** Kickstarter's three-part payment option above a minimum pledge [10].
- **Pledge manager:** a tool after the campaign to collect addresses, shipping, add-ons and tax [15][21].
- **Backer report and survey:** the list of backers and the form that collects their details.
- **Pre-launch page:** a page that collects followers before launch [5].
- **Endgame:** Indiegogo's option that extends the last minutes while pledges keep arriving [22].
- **Collaborator:** a person given limited permissions on a Kickstarter project [19].
- **Fulfilment:** making, packing and shipping rewards.
- **DDP and DDU:** delivered duty paid (seller pays import charges) and delivered duty unpaid (buyer pays) [16].
- **IOSS:** the EU's import one-stop shop for VAT on low-value parcels [16].
- **VAT:** value added tax, charged on goods in the UK and EU [15].
- **GPSR:** the EU General Product Safety Regulation [30].
- **Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF):** the US exemption for small securities offerings through registered intermediaries [26].
- **Funding portal:** an SEC-registered, FINRA-member intermediary for Reg CF offerings [27].
- **Key investment information sheet (KIIS):** the EU disclosure document for crowdfunding offers [29].
