# Backer updates and emails

Backers are partners in a project that has not shipped yet. Kickstarter expects creators to post regular updates and tell backers promptly about delays, and asks for at least one update a month once funding is finished (checked 6 Nov 2026). Silence is read as bad news. This skill writes every message in the order the campaign needs it and says which step needs which access level.

## When to use

- Building the campaign's message calendar, from about eight weeks before launch to delivery.
- Launch day, the middle of the campaign, and the final 48 hours.
- After funding: the monthly making and shipping update.
- A delay, a cost change, a quality problem or any bad news.

## What you need

- The campaign plan with dates, the reward table and the delivery dates labelled firm or hopeful.
- The client's email platform and list, with consent recorded (the email marketing specialist kit covers consent rules in the US, UK and EU).
- The platform: Kickstarter pre-launch updates reach pre-launch followers; Indiegogo projects sit in an "Upcoming" phase before going live.
- Real progress material: photos, factory messages, test results, shipping status.
- **Access I have.** Writing and planning is level 1. Saving updates and emails as unsent drafts is level 2. Publishing approved updates and sending approved emails to the client's own list, on the agreed dates, is level 3. Messages that change reward terms, offer refunds, answer a formal complaint or chargeback, or go to a new audience need the client's fresh yes even at level 3.

## Steps

1. **Map the calendar.** Pre-launch (weekly emails that show the making, ask for followers on the platform page, and give the launch date); launch day (email at the launch hour, a pre-launch follower update, social posts); the middle (an update every few days: progress, a new add-on, a behind-the-scenes look, answers to common questions); the final 48 hours (a reminder email and update); after funding (thanks, the pledge manager timeline, then monthly updates to delivery).
2. **Pre-launch emails.** Each one gives one reason to care and one action, usually "follow the pre-launch page so you get the launch email". Kickstarter's pre-launch page emails followers when the project goes live, and its launch is a manual button, so the launch email goes out after the client presses it. On Indiegogo, the launch time is set at review, so schedule to it.
3. **Launch day.** Short: it is live, what it is, the early bird and its limit, the link. Send only to the client's own consented list.
4. **Mid-campaign updates.** Each update has news, not just "please share". Good news: a milestone, a factory sample, a new add-on (a new tier needs the client's fresh yes before it goes live). Answer the top questions from comments.
5. **The final 48 hours.** One reminder email and one update: the deadline in the backer's time zone where possible, what is included, and what happens after funding. No false scarcity.
6. **After funding.** Thank backers, then give the next dates: survey or pledge manager, production steps, shipping window. Then at least one update a month: what was done, what is next, photos, and the delivery date restated as firm or hopeful.
7. **Bad news first.** A delay or problem update starts with the news in the first sentence, then why, then the new date labelled firm or hopeful, then what backers can do, then when the next update comes. On Kickstarter, also mark delayed rewards as "Delayed" in the backer report status. If the client offers refunds, say how; the client issues them, not the agent.
8. **Check tone and claims.** No "guaranteed", no blame on backers, no promises the factory has not confirmed in writing.
9. **Draft, approve, publish.** Level 2: save as drafts, unsent. Level 3: show the client the list of updates and emails with dates, get one yes, then publish and send within those limits. Log each post and send. Stop and ask on any spike in unsubscribes, cancelled pledges, angry comments or a platform warning.

## Checks

- Every email goes only to people who asked for it.
- Every update has real news and a next date.
- Delay updates lead with the news and give a new date labelled firm or hopeful.
- No message changes reward terms or offers refunds without the client's fresh yes.
- Monthly cadence after funding is on the calendar through delivery.
- Action log kept for every level 3 post or send.

## Output format

1. **Message calendar:** date, channel (email, platform update, social), audience, purpose, status (draft, approved, sent).
2. **Drafts:** subject or title, preview line, body, call to action.
3. **Approval list** for level 3 work, with limits.
4. **Action log** after sending.

## Example of good output (short)

*Kettle Hill Games is a made-up studio used only to show the format. Access I have: Kickstarter level 3 (Manage community and Coordinate fulfilment) for updates and reward statuses; email platform level 2.*

**Update 9: Our print date has moved to March**

The printer has moved our print slot from January to March, so games will reach you about eight weeks later than we planned.

Why: the printer found a fault in the box insert sample and needs a new cutting die. We chose to fix it rather than ship a box that rattles. Here is the photo of the faulty insert and the new one.

New dates: printing in March (firm, the printer confirmed in writing on 3 Feb). Delivery to US backers in May (hopeful, it depends on freight). We have marked all rewards as Delayed in the backer report and will change them when they ship.

What you can do: nothing is needed. If you have moved house, update your address in the pledge manager by 1 April.

Next update: 1 March, with photos from the first print run.

**Log:** posted Update 9 to the Kickstarter project at 10:00 on 5 Feb, within the agreed limit of one update per week; 0 errors.
