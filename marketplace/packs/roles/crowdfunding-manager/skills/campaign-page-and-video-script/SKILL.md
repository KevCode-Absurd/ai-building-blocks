# Campaign page and video script

A backer decides fast. Kickstarter's own design guide (19 Oct 2026) says many visitors skim pictures, headings and captions before reading a word. So the page is written for a scroll first and a read second, and every claim on it must be something the creator can show. This skill writes the words and the script; the video producer kit's methods cover shot lists, captions and music rights for the build.

## When to use

- The goal and reward tiers are set and the page needs writing.
- A draft page needs a pre-launch review.
- The campaign needs a main video script, or a short vertical version.

## What you need

- The product facts: what it is, who it is for, what problem it solves, and what is finished and unfinished.
- Real photos and video of the working prototype, testing and the team.
- The goal and reward tables, delivery dates labelled firm or hopeful, and the shipping, VAT and duty plan.
- Manufacturing plan: factory, stage reached, certifications needed, and the team's past experience making anything similar.
- Any AI tools used in images, video, voice or text.
- Permission for any quote, review, logo or press mention used.
- **Access I have.** Writing is level 1. Building the draft page in the platform before launch is level 2. Editing story text or images on a live page is level 3 within the client's limits, with approved copy. Launching the campaign needs the client's fresh yes.

## Steps

1. **Write the one-line promise.** What it is and who it is for, in plain words. This becomes the title and subtitle. No "revolutionary", no "guaranteed".
2. **Plan the scroll.** Draft headings that each name the question the section answers: What is it? Why we made it. Does it work? How it works. Rewards. Timeline. Who we are and how it gets made. Risks and challenges. Shipping, tax and duties. FAQ. Each image gets one job, with a caption that states the point.
3. **Proof before promises.** Lead with real prototype photos and video. On Kickstarter, hardware and product design projects must show a working prototype and may not use photorealistic renderings (checked 6 Nov 2026). Say what stage the prototype is at, and what still changes.
4. **How it gets made.** Name the stages (final design, tooling, samples, production, freight, fulfilment), who does each, and whether the team has made anything similar. Kickstarter asks hardware projects to explain this.
5. **Rewards.** One comparison graphic with the same order, names and images as the reward list. Say what each tier contains, its limit, and its delivery month labelled firm or hopeful. Stretch goals only if budgeted.
6. **Timeline.** A dated plan from campaign end to delivery, each date labelled firm or hopeful, with the factory's written lead time behind it.
7. **Risks and challenges.** Name the real risks (tooling delays, factory capacity, certification, freight, customs, a key supplier) and what the team will do about each, including how backers will hear about delays. Kickstarter expects creators to tell backers promptly about delays.
8. **Shipping, tax and duties.** Where you ship, how shipping is charged, who pays import charges (DDP or DDU), and how VAT is handled for UK and EU backers. Have the accountant confirm the wording.
9. **Disclosures.** If AI made or changed any image, voice or text, say which tool and how its output appears (Kickstarter rule, checked 6 Nov 2026). No investment words: "invest", "returns", "shares" or "equity" do not describe pledges.
10. **Video script.** Two columns, what we hear and what we see, timed to about two minutes: the maker and the problem (0:00 to 0:20), the product working on camera (to 0:50), how it works and who it is for (to 1:20), proof and how it gets made (to 1:40), rewards and the ask (to 2:00). Real people, real prototype, captions on. For the optional Kickstarter Discovery Mode video: vertical 9:16, about 60 to 90 seconds, people holding or talking about the product, captions, key content kept away from the edges.
11. **Scroll test.** Read only headings, images and captions on a phone. Can you tell what it is, why it matters and how to back it? Fix until yes.

## Checks

- Every claim traces to a photo, test, quote with permission, or document.
- No photorealistic renders on a Kickstarter hardware page; prototype stage stated.
- Dates labelled firm or hopeful and matching the reward table.
- Risks section names real risks and the plan for each.
- AI use disclosed; no investment language; no store language like "order now" or "in stock".
- The page passes the phone scroll test.
- Video script times add up; every line has a picture.

## Output format

1. **Title and subtitle** (two or three options each).
2. **Page copy** under the planned headings, with image notes in brackets.
3. **Risks and challenges** section.
4. **FAQ** (eight to twelve questions backers will ask).
5. **Video script** in a two-column table with timings, plus the vertical cut if wanted.
6. **Facts to confirm** and permissions needed.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real product: a folding solar camping lantern. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Title:** [Product]: the solar lantern that charges your phone at camp
**Subtitle:** A folding lantern with a full day of light from a morning in the sun. Third working prototype, tested on 14 trips.

**Risks and challenges**
The design is final and our third prototype works, but three things could slow us down. First, the steel mould for the case takes about six weeks to make and can need a second round. Second, the battery needs transport certification before it can ship by air; we have booked the test lab but have no result yet. Third, freight times change. Our May 2027 delivery date is hopeful, not firm. If any step slips, we will post an update that week saying what happened and the new date.
