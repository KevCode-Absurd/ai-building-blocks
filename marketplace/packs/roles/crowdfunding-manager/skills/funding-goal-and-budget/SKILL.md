# Funding goal and budget

The goal is the least money that delivers the project, not the number that looks easy to hit. On Kickstarter the goal and deadline lock at launch, and if a project funds it must deliver. This skill builds the goal from written quotes and shows the client the maths in one table. It gives no tax or accounting rulings: tax lines are set with a qualified accountant.

## When to use

- A creator has quotes and a launch window and asks what goal to set.
- A goal has already been chosen and needs a check before launch.
- The client wants to cut the goal to "make it look achievable".
- The factory quote changed, or the platform changed its fees.

## What you need

- Written factory quotes at two or three quantities, including any tooling, moulds, samples or certification costs.
- Packaging, freight to the fulfilment point, and pick-and-pack quotes.
- Shipping quotes for the top destinations, and whether shipping is charged at pledge or later in a pledge manager.
- Platform choice (Kickstarter or Indiegogo) and the reward price range.
- Email list size, pre-launch followers and any past campaign results.
- The client's own buffer figure, and their accountant's advice on tax set-asides. If the client has no buffer figure, ask for one; do not invent it.
- **Access I have.** This skill needs level 1 only (reading quotes, exports and dashboards). Entering the goal in a draft project is level 2. Launching, which locks the goal, needs the client's fresh yes even at level 3.

## Steps

1. **List every cost line.** Making (per unit times the minimum order quantity), tooling and one-off costs, packaging, freight, fulfilment, art or editing, certification, the cost of stretch goals already promised, and replacements for damaged or lost parcels at the client's chosen allowance. Mark each line with its source (quote, estimate, guess). Guesses go to the facts-to-confirm list.
2. **Decide how shipping is charged.** If shipping is charged at pledge, it counts toward the total while live but goes straight back out to carriers, and fees apply to it too (Kickstarter guidance, checked 6 Nov 2026). Show shipping as its own pass-through line so it does not hide a shortfall.
3. **Add fees on the gross total.** As of 6 Nov 2026: Kickstarter takes 5% of funds on success plus card processing of roughly 3-5%; Indiegogo takes 5% plus 3% and 0.20 per transaction. Neither charges if the goal is missed. Because fees come off the total raised, solve for the goal: goal = net need / (1 - fee rate). Use the top of Kickstarter's processing range for a cautious goal, and say so.
4. **Add tax and duty set-asides.** Crowdfunding income is generally taxable (Kickstarter's guidance says to ask an accountant). For EU and UK backers, VAT may be collected by Kickstarter's Pledge Manager in some cases and is otherwise the creator's job; EU import duty for low-value parcels from outside the EU is EUR 3 per item type from 1 July 2026 to 1 July 2028. Put the accountant's figures in, or leave a clearly marked blank and say the goal will rise. Ask a qualified accountant before launch.
5. **Add the buffer** at the client's chosen figure, on the cost lines, not on fees.
6. **Run the two checks** from Kickstarter's guidance. At exactly 100%, does the net cover every cost line? If not, raise the goal. At 90%, would the client regret losing those pledges? If so, look for a lower goal, but only if check one still passes.
7. **Translate the goal into backers.** Goal divided by the expected average pledge gives the backers needed. Compare it with the list and pre-launch followers. Do not quote a list-to-backer conversion rate unless the client has their own from a past launch; say what is unknown.
8. **If the goal is out of reach, change the plan, not the maths.** Options: a smaller first run (get a quote at a lower quantity), fewer variants, digital or local-pickup tiers, a later launch to grow the list, or a different first product. Never lower the goal below what check one allows.
9. **Write the summary.** One table, then three to five plain sentences: the goal, why, what it buys, the backers needed, the biggest risk, and the facts still to confirm.

## Checks

- Every cost line has a source, and every guess is on the confirm list.
- The goal passes the 100% check after fees, with shipping shown as pass-through.
- Tax and duty lines are filled from the accountant's advice or clearly marked blank.
- The buffer figure came from the client.
- Backers needed is compared with real list and follower numbers.
- No success-rate or conversion "averages" without a dated source.

## Output format

1. **Goal table:** cost line, amount, source, note.
2. **Fee and goal maths:** net need, fee rate used, goal, rounded goal.
3. **Checks:** 100% result, 90% result.
4. **Backers needed** versus list and followers.
5. **Plan changes** if the goal is out of reach.
6. **Facts to confirm** and who confirms each (factory, carrier, accountant).

## Example of good output (short)

*Kettle Hill Games is a made-up studio used only to show the format. Platform: Kickstarter. Access I have: Kickstarter level 1 (analytics), Google Sheets level 2.*

| Cost line | Amount (USD) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Print 2,000 copies | 28,000 | Factory quote, 2 Oct |
| Final art and rules edit | 2,500 | Freelancer quote |
| Freight to US warehouse | 3,200 | Freight quote |
| Pick and pack, 2,000 at 4.00 | 8,000 | Fulfilment quote |
| Buffer, client's 10% of the above | 4,170 | Client's figure |
| **Net need** | **45,870** | |
| Tax set-aside | blank | Accountant to confirm |

Fees: 5% platform plus up to 5% processing = 10%. Goal = 45,870 / 0.90 = 50,967, rounded to **51,000**. Shipping is charged per country at pledge and shown separately.

- **100% check:** 51,000 less 10% = 45,900, which covers 45,870. Pass, before tax.
- **90% check:** 45,900 raised nets 41,310, short by 4,560. Lowering the goal fails check one.
- **Backers needed:** at a 45 average pledge, about 1,134 backers. The list has 900 subscribers. That gap is the biggest risk.
- **Plan change to test:** ask the printer for a 1,000-copy quote; a smaller run may lower the goal enough to match the list.

**Confirm:** tax set-aside (accountant), 1,000-copy quote (printer), EU duty and VAT plan if shipping to the EU (fulfilment partner and accountant).
