# Reward tier pricing

A tier that loses money gets worse the more people pick it. On Kickstarter, once a backer picks a tier, its contents, existing shipping and delivery date generally cannot change (checked 6 Nov 2026). So every tier is costed twice before launch: once with fees, once with a broken parcel. This skill gives no tax or customs rulings; VAT and duties are confirmed with a qualified accountant and the fulfilment partner.

## When to use

- Planning the reward ladder for a new campaign.
- A client sends planned tiers and a quote and asks "does each tier make money?".
- Adding a tier or add-on mid-campaign.
- Setting Late Pledge prices or deciding on Pledge Over Time.

## What you need

- Landed cost per unit for each item (from the funding-goal-and-budget skill or the quotes).
- Packaging and pick-and-pack cost per parcel and per extra item.
- Shipping quotes per destination zone and parcel size, and who pays import charges (DDP or DDU).
- The client's damage and loss allowance (their figure; ask, do not invent).
- Planned retail price after the campaign, if any.
- Factory lead time, to set delivery dates.
- **Access I have.** Costing is level 1. Entering tiers in a draft project before launch is level 2. Adding or changing any tier, price, add-on, shipping rule or date on a live campaign needs the client's fresh yes even at level 3.

## Steps

1. **Draft the ladder.** A usual shape: a small entry tier (digital or thank-you, if it suits the product), a limited early bird, the core product, a bundle, a premium or collector tier, and a few add-ons. Five to eight tiers is plenty; each extra tier is one more thing to compare and fulfil.
2. **Name and describe each tier the same way.** Name, price, exact contents, quantity limit, estimated delivery month, and shipping rule. Use the same order and images across tier graphics, as Kickstarter's design guidance advises.
3. **Cost each tier.** Columns: pledge price; fees (as of 6 Nov 2026, Kickstarter 5% plus roughly 3-5% processing; Indiegogo 5% plus 3% and 0.20 per transaction); goods; packaging; pick and pack; damage allowance; total cost; margin; margin %. Use the top of any fee range.
4. **Cost shipping per destination.** If shipping is included in the price, cost the most expensive zone. If it is charged at pledge, remember fees apply to it too. Decide DDP or DDU, and for EU backers note the EUR 3 per item type duty in force from 1 July 2026 and the creator's VAT duty outside Kickstarter's Pledge Manager. Ask a qualified accountant.
5. **Run the broken-parcel pass.** Apply the client's damage allowance to goods and packing, and check margin again. Flag any tier where one replacement wipes out the margin.
6. **Set limits.** Early birds get a quantity limit the client can afford. Kickstarter lets creators raise quantities while live but not lower them, so start small.
7. **Set delivery dates.** Factory lead time, plus freight, plus fulfilment, plus the client's buffer. Label each "firm" or "hopeful". On Kickstarter a backed tier's date cannot be moved later in the editor, so pick a date the client can keep.
8. **Late Pledges.** If the client wants backers after the campaign, set a late price per tier. Kickstarter suggests about 10% above the campaign price, and Late Pledges must be switched on before the campaign ends.
9. **Pledge Over Time.** On Kickstarter, only pledges at or above the currency minimum qualify (USD 125, GBP 100, EUR 125 among others, checked 6 Nov 2026), and the creator turns it on before launch. Note which tiers qualify.
10. **Write the recommendation.** Fix or drop any tier with a loss in any zone; say why in one line each.

## Checks

- Every tier shows margin after fees in the most expensive zone.
- Damage allowance applied, with the client's figure.
- No tier contents or dates you cannot keep; dates labelled firm or hopeful.
- Early bird limits set; nothing relies on lowering a quantity later.
- Late Pledge and Pledge Over Time choices made before launch.
- Tax and duty lines marked for the accountant, not guessed.

## Output format

1. **Reward table:** tier, price, contents, limit, delivery, fees, goods, packing, pick, allowance, cost, margin, margin %.
2. **Shipping table:** zone, parcel, quote, charged or included, DDP or DDU.
3. **Flags:** tiers that lose money, and the fix.
4. **Late Pledge prices and Pledge Over Time eligibility.**
5. **Facts to confirm.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real product: a solar camping lantern. Costs in USD: lantern 17.50 landed, packaging 1.80, pick and pack 3.50 (0.50 per extra unit), client's damage allowance 3% of goods and packing. Fees at 10% (5% platform plus up to 5% processing, Kickstarter, checked 6 Nov 2026). Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

| Tier | Price | Limit | Fees | Goods, pack, pick | Allowance | Cost | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early bird, 1 lantern | 49 | 300 | 4.90 | 22.80 | 0.68 | 28.38 | 20.62 (42%) |
| Standard, 1 lantern | 59 | none | 5.90 | 22.80 | 0.68 | 29.38 | 29.62 (50%) |
| Two-pack | 109 | none | 10.90 | 40.80 | 1.22 | 52.92 | 56.08 (51%) |
| Add-on: carry case | 12 | none | 1.20 | 4.60 | 0.14 | 5.94 | 6.06 (51%) |

**Flag:** the plan says the early bird includes free worldwide shipping. One lantern costs 8.40 to ship in the US and 23.80 to Australia. With shipping included, the early bird makes 12.22 in the US and loses 3.18 in Australia. **Fix:** charge shipping by zone at pledge, or limit free shipping to the US.

**Late Pledge prices** (about 10% up, Kickstarter's suggestion): Standard 65, Two-pack 120. **Pledge Over Time:** no tier reaches the USD 125 minimum; a four-pack at 199 would.

**Delivery:** "Hopeful: May 2027" until the factory confirms the lead time in writing.
