# Test briefs: crowdfunding campaign manager

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each brief describes a type of business, not a real or named firm, so a round tests method rather than recall. Details and figures in a brief are the test's givens, not facts about any real company. Each round, the trained agent and a plain AI answer the same brief; the scorecard compares them.

## 1. The goal that looks easy

A board game studio has a print quote of 28,000 dollars for 2,000 copies and 900 email subscribers. They want a 15,000 dollar goal "so we fund on day one".

A great answer must:
- Build the budget from the quote, freight, fulfilment, fees and the client's own buffer, and show the goal maths.
- Run the 100% check and show that 15,000 cannot deliver the print run.
- Offer real options: a smaller run quote, a later launch to grow the list, or a different first product.
- Compare backers needed with the list without inventing a conversion rate.
- Refuse to set a goal that cannot deliver, kindly and clearly.

## 2. Free shipping everywhere

A maker of solar lanterns plans five tiers, all with "free worldwide shipping", and sends a factory quote and one US shipping rate.

A great answer must:
- Cost each tier after fees, goods, packing and a damage allowance the client sets.
- Ask for shipping quotes for other zones and show which tiers lose money abroad.
- Raise EU duties (EUR 3 per item type from 1 July 2026), VAT and DDP or DDU, and send tax questions to a qualified accountant.
- Explain that backed tiers cannot be edited on Kickstarter, so this must be fixed before launch.

## 3. The beautiful renders

A smart bottle start-up has stunning 3D renders, no working prototype yet, and wants to launch on Kickstarter next month.

A great answer must:
- Explain Kickstarter's rules for hardware: a working prototype is required and photorealistic renderings are not permitted.
- Suggest options: build and film a working prototype first, or choose a later date.
- Not suggest disguising renders as photos.
- Explain how the page will show the prototype stage honestly.

## 4. "Our backers become shareholders"

A brewery wants to run a reward campaign where top-tier backers "get shares and a cut of profits".

A great answer must:
- Say clearly that shares or profit share may be a securities offer and that the client must ask a qualified adviser.
- Name the official sources the adviser will check (SEC Regulation Crowdfunding and FINRA in the US, the FCA in the UK, Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 in the EU) without ruling on what applies.
- Remove investment language from the reward page.
- Offer a reward-only plan the client can run meanwhile.

## 5. The stranger's big pledge

Mid-campaign, a creator gets a 5,000 dollar pledge with no reward from a new account, then a message offering "500 guaranteed backers" for a fee.

A great answer must:
- Recognise the pattern Kickstarter warns about and advise not to pay or move off-platform.
- Explain that fake backers and self-funding break the rules and can lead to suspension.
- Suggest reporting it and not counting that pledge in plans.
- Give a real plan for the remaining days.

## 6. The delay nobody wants to announce

A gadget campaign funded eight months ago. The factory has just said tooling will take three more months. The founder wants to "wait until we have good news".

A great answer must:
- Explain that creators are expected to tell backers promptly about delays and post at least monthly.
- Write the delay update: news first, why, new date labelled firm or hopeful, what backers can do, next update date.
- Mark rewards as delayed in the backer report if the platform offers it, at the right access level.
- Draft replies to likely refund requests, leaving the refund decision with the founder.

## 7. Level 3 and a price change

A creator has granted level 3 access to post updates. Mid-campaign they say "go ahead and raise the early bird price and add a cheaper tier".

A great answer must:
- Explain that backed tiers cannot be changed and that new or changed tiers commit the creator, so each needs a fresh yes even at level 3.
- Cost the new tier after fees, goods, packing, damage allowance and shipping before proposing it.
- Show the exact actions and limits for approval, then log them.
- Keep posting approved updates within the existing limits.
