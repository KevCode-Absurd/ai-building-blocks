# Scope and sequence with alignment

## When to use

- A small school or department needs a year plan for a subject.
- A tutoring centre, language school or training provider maps a course of set weeks and hours.
- A homeschool co-op plans a shared year for a mixed age group.
- A school wants to check a current plan for gaps against its syllabus or standards.
- An association or professional body plans a year of member education (use the member calendar steps below).

Do not use it for accreditation decisions, inspection judgements or official CPD hours. For single ready-to-teach lessons, use `lesson-plan-and-class-materials`.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** syllabus or standards documents, term dates, existing schemes of work and resources lists.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** create the year plan, unit plans and alignment grid in the agreed folder; build hidden course sections in the LMS.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** make the agreed sections visible to the named course and share final plans with named staff. Replacing a live course structure that holds learner work needs a fresh yes.

## What you need

1. The syllabus, programme of study or standards, pasted with exact codes, plus the country or state and the adoption year.
2. Term dates, holidays, exam weeks, trips and events; hours per week.
3. The learners' starting point and anything they already covered.
4. Existing materials and resources, so units use what the client has.
5. Assessment rules: how often, what kinds, and any external exam dates.

## Steps

### 1. List every outcome

Copy each outcome with its code or wording exactly as given, one per row. If a document is missing, stop and ask; never fill codes from memory. Note the version: for England, plans that run past summer 2028 must flag that the revised national curriculum is due by spring 2027 for first teaching in September 2028; for US states, note the adoption year and any new standards scheduled to take effect.

### 2. Count real teaching weeks

Term by term: total weeks minus holidays, exam weeks, events and transition days. Show the count. Plan one buffer week per term if the calendar allows; if it does not, say so.

### 3. Group outcomes into units

Each unit gets one big idea in a sentence, 3 to 6 outcomes, and an estimated number of weeks. Combine outcomes that share content. Keep skills that need practice across the year spread over several units rather than one block.

### 4. Order the units

Earlier units build what later ones need. Check each unit: "What must learners already be able to do?" If the answer is in a later unit, reorder. Put review of earlier units into later ones.

### 5. Place units and assessment points

Lay units across the weeks. Mark assessment points: short checks inside units, an end-of-unit test, and any termly or external exams. Check the total weeks against the count from step 2. If outcomes do not fit, show the maths and offer what to combine, cut or move.

### 6. Write unit plans

For each unit: big idea, outcomes with codes, key knowledge, key skills, a lesson-by-lesson outline (one line per lesson), the assessment that checks each outcome, resources from the client's list, and common misconceptions.

### 7. Build the alignment grid

Rows are outcomes; columns are the unit that teaches it and the assessment that checks it. Mark every outcome that is "not taught" or "not checked". Fix gaps or list them for the client to decide.

### 8. Member education variant

For associations and networks: list member segments and their needs from the client's survey or brief; set four quarterly themes; place sessions month by month avoiding busy seasons and conference dates; for each session give goal, segment, format and a takeaway. Official CPD hours and accreditation rules come from the awarding body and are marked [confirm].

## Checks

- Every outcome appears once in the grid with its exact code or wording.
- Every outcome is taught at least once and checked at least once, or the gap is named.
- Weeks add up to the counted calendar.
- Units are ordered so no unit depends on a later one.
- Curriculum version and adoption year are stated; England plans past 2028 carry the change flag.
- Resources come from what the client has; new purchases are listed separately as suggestions.
- No learner names or records.

## Output format

1. **Teaching week count** per term.
2. **Year at a glance:** term, weeks, unit, big idea, assessment point.
3. **Unit plans**, one per unit, with headings.
4. **Alignment grid** with gaps marked "not taught" or "not checked".
5. **Decisions for you:** what to cut, combine or move if it does not fit, and [check] items.
