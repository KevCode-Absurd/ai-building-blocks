# Training interview: Curriculum developer

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out pupils' and members' names, school names and anything under a confidentiality agreement; describe classes and clients in general terms.

1. **Who do you plan for: which ages, subjects, settings and curricula or standards?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and which official documents it must work from.

2. **Walk me through how you plan one lesson, from the brief to the last check.**
   Why it matters: captures the real order of work, not the textbook version.

3. **How do you turn a vague aim like "understand fractions" into something you can check?**
   Why it matters: aim writing is the step most plans get wrong.

4. **What does your exit ticket usually look like, and what do you do with the results the next day?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert links assessment to the next lesson.

5. **How do you time a lesson so it survives a fire drill or a slow start?**
   Why it matters: records the practical habits behind timings that really work.

6. **How do you write support and stretch without making separate lessons for every learner?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's way of keeping one goal for a mixed class.

7. **Tell me about a worksheet you adapted for a wide reading range. What did you keep and what did you change?**
   Why it matters: teaches the line between scaffolding and giving answers away.

8. **How do you match work to curriculum or standards codes, and what do you do when you are not sure of a code?**
   Why it matters: prevents invented codes, the most damaging error in this role.

9. **How do you build a test blueprint, and who agrees it before you write items?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert keeps tests balanced and defensible.

10. **What makes a multiple-choice wrong option good? Give me one you were proud of.**
    Why it matters: distractor craft separates a fair test from a guessing game.

11. **How do you write rubric descriptors that two markers apply the same way? Tell me about a time they did not.**
    Why it matters: captures the moderation habits behind reliable marking.

12. **How do you count teaching weeks and decide what to cut when a year will not fit?**
    Why it matters: grounds year planning in real calendars and trade-offs.

13. **How are you handling the coming changes to your curriculum or standards, such as England's revised national curriculum or a state's new standards?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert labels drafts and avoids planning on rules not yet published.

14. **What do you check before a digital worksheet or quiz goes to learners with access needs?**
    Why it matters: records the accessibility checks the expert actually runs.

15. **What do you do if pasted student work or notes suggest a child may be at risk?**
    Why it matters: safeguarding steps must be exact and must point to the right person.

16. **What is your rule on pupils' names and data in AI tools, and on AI tools that pupils use directly?**
    Why it matters: aligns the agent with the expert's data and safety practice.

17. **Tell me about a hard brief: a client who wanted something you thought was wrong for learners. What happened?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent how to push back and still deliver.

18. **For member education or CPD, how do you decide themes, and how do you handle CPD hours you cannot confirm?**
    Why it matters: captures the planning and the limits around accreditation.

19. **Which resources and tools do you rely on, and which free ones do you trust?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's real toolkit and sources.

20. **What is your final check before you send anything to a teacher?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's quality bar.
