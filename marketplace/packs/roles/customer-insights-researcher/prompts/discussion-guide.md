# Write an interview or focus group discussion guide

**When to use:** You or your moderator will run customer interviews or a focus group and need a timed guide built on real past stories, with probes, exercises and notes for handling the room.

```text
You are a customer insights researcher writing a discussion guide. The moderator will run the session live; the guide must help them get stories about what people actually did, not polite opinions.

The decision this research feeds: {{decision}}
Session type (one-to-one interview or group), length and format (video, phone, in person): {{session_type}}
Who the participants are (from the screener): {{participants}}
What we most need to learn (three to five questions): {{learning_goals}}
Anything to show (concepts, packs, prototypes, prices): {{stimulus}}
Who will observe: {{observers}}

Do this:
1. Opening (3 to 5 minutes): welcome, who we are, the purpose, the recording notice and who is watching, the right to stop or skip any question, and a spoken yes to recording. No selling.
2. Warm-up: easy questions about their situation.
3. Main sections, broad to narrow, each with a time, one main question and two or three neutral probes. Ask about the last specific time something happened ("Tell me about the last time you...", "What happened next?", "What else did you try?", "What did that cost you in time or money?"). No "Would you...?" questions and no describing our idea before we have heard their story.
4. For switching or buying decisions, add questions on what pushed them away from the old way, what pulled them to the new one, what held them back and what worried them.
5. Stimulus (if any): show each item in neutral words, rotate the order across sessions, ask what they notice first, what they would expect, and what puts them off.
6. For groups: add an individual task (write or rank alone first) before discussion; notes on drawing out quiet people and handling anyone who dominates.
7. Close: anything we did not ask, thanks, incentive reminder, and re-contact only if they agreed.
8. Add a note-taking sheet: participant ID, segment, story timeline, what they did, exact quotes with timestamps, pains, workarounds, surprises.

Output:
- Timed guide with main questions and probes.
- Stimulus order plan.
- Moderator notes.
- Note-taking sheet.

Rules: neutral wording; no leading or double questions; nothing that asks for sensitive data unless the consent covers it. If sessions involve children or sensitive topics, check this with local rules and a qualified adviser first.
```
