# Interview and feedback synthesis

## When to use

- A round of 5 to 20 interviews, a set of focus groups or a diary study has finished and the transcripts or notes are in.
- A pile of open survey answers, support tickets or sales call notes needs to be turned into themes and actions.
- A team ran its own customer calls and wants to know what it really learned.

## What you need

1. The decision the research was for, and the questions it had to answer.
2. Transcripts or notes, one file per session, with participant IDs instead of names, and the screener answers (segment, tenure, region).
3. Which tool made each transcript (human, call tool, AI transcription) and whether audio is available for checking quotes.
4. What participants agreed to: whether quotes or clips can be shared and with whom.
5. Any earlier findings to compare against.

Access: level 1 (read) for transcripts, notes and exports. Level 2 to create tags, themes and highlight reels as drafts in the client's research repository (such as Dovetail or Notion). Level 3, within the client's limits, to publish the readout or insights to the named people or spaces. Sharing any clip, image or name needs the participant's consent and a fresh yes every time. Check this with local rules before recordings leave the research team. I log every action.

## Steps

### 1. Get to know the data

Read every transcript once without coding. Write a short summary per session: who (ID, segment), what happened in their story, anything surprising. Note quality problems: short sessions, a leading moderator, a participant who did not fit the screener.

### 2. Check the transcripts

If a transcript came from an AI tool, spot-check it against the audio and check every quote you plan to use word for word. Transcription tools can add whole phrases that were never said; one study found this more often for speakers who paused for longer.

### 3. Code

Go through each transcript and give each meaningful passage a short label: what the person did, felt, needed, worried about, or the words they used. Code everything relevant to the questions, not only what supports the client's hopes. Keep a code list with a one-line definition for each. Do a second pass after the first full round.

### 4. Build themes

- Group codes into candidate themes. A theme is a claim about shared meaning ("People cancel when the box stops fitting the week, not because of price"), not a topic ("Price").
- With a team, run an affinity session: everyone writes notes alone first for a short timed window, then cluster by content before naming, keep outliers and opposing views, and do not force notes into clusters.
- Review each theme against all the data: does it hold across sessions? Split, merge or drop.
- For each theme, count how many participants it covers, and in which segments.

### 5. Separate did from said

Mark which evidence is past behaviour ("I cancelled the week we went on holiday") and which is opinion or prediction ("I'd come back if it were cheaper"). Weight behaviour higher.

### 6. Map the decision

For switching or buying decisions, map the forces: what pushed them away from the old way, what pulled them to the new one, the habits holding them back and the worries about changing. For product work, place themes on an opportunity map under the outcome the client wants, with solution ideas and the assumption each would need tested.

### 7. Rank and label

- **Finding:** backed by enough participants across segments, with consistent stories.
- **Lead:** backed by a few, or by one segment only; say how to test it.
- Rank by how many people, how painful (cost, time, anger, cancelling) and how fixable.

### 8. Pull out customer words

Collect the exact phrases people used for the problem, the benefit, doubts and the moment they decided. Group them for copy. Mark each with the participant ID.

### 9. Write the readout

Answer first. Then theme cards, the decision map, customer words, the fix list, open questions and the method box.

## Checks

- Every session was read in full; none skipped because it did not fit.
- Every theme is a claim, has a count, at least two exact quotes and a counter-example if one exists.
- Every quote was checked against the transcript, and against audio if AI transcribed.
- Did and said are labelled; findings and leads are labelled.
- No participant can be identified from quotes, job titles or small-segment details unless they agreed.
- No composite or invented customer presented as real.
- AI use in transcription or coding disclosed in the method box, and AI output checked by a person.
- The readout answers the decision it was for.

## Output format

1. Answer first: the three things that matter most.
2. Theme cards: claim, count, segments, quotes, counter-example, finding or lead, what to do.
3. Decision map (forces) or opportunity map.
4. Customer words grouped by problem, benefit, doubt, trigger.
5. Fix list ranked by reach, pain and ease.
6. Open questions and the next study.
7. Method box: who, how many, how recruited, dates, incentive, recording, transcription and analysis tools (including AI), limits.

## Example of good output

Illustrative structure, not a real business: an online grocer that ran 12 interviews with cancelled subscribers in October 2026. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> **Answer first.** 1) Most people cancelled when a week went off routine (holiday, illness, guests) and skipping felt harder than stopping. 2) Price came up often but rarely as the trigger. 3) Two people left over substitutions; small numbers, but both were angry.
>
> **Theme card: "Skipping is harder than stopping."** 8 of 12 (both tenure groups, 3 outside London). Finding.
> - P04: "I went to pause it and just ended up cancelling because that was the button I found."
> - P09: "We were away for two weeks and I didn't want to pay for food rotting on the step."
> - Counter-example: P11 skipped twice without trouble ("it's in the app, under the box").
> - Do: put "Skip a week" next to "Cancel" and remind people before cut-off.
>
> **Lead: substitutions.** 2 of 12, both long-standing customers. Test: add a question to the cancel survey.
>
> **Method:** 12 video interviews, 7 to 21 October 2026, recruited from cancellations in the last 60 days, £30 gift card; transcribed by the call tool and every quote checked against audio.
