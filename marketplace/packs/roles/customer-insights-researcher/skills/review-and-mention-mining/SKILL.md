# Review and mention mining

## When to use

- A shop, app or service has hundreds of reviews and nobody has time to read them.
- Ratings dropped, returns rose or a complaint is spreading on social media.
- Copywriters want buyers' own words for a product page or ad.
- A product team wants ideas ranked from real complaints.
- A brand wants its mentions compared with rivals'.
- A client wants more honest reviews and asks how to request them.

## What you need

1. The decision: fix a product, write copy, find a gap, explain a drop, or plan review requests.
2. The export: reviews (with date, rating, product or version, and text), returns reasons, support tickets, or a listening tool export (with date, source, author type and text). Personal details removed where possible.
3. Context: product changes, price changes, delivery changes, campaigns and outages, with dates.
4. For rival comparisons: the rivals' public reviews or the listening export covering them.
5. For review requests: where reviews are collected (Google, Trustpilot, a marketplace, a shop app) and how customers are contacted now.

Access: level 1 (read) for exports, dashboards and review platforms. Level 2 to draft tags or saved searches in the client's listening or support tool, and draft review request emails and timing. Level 3, within the client's limits, to save queries, apply agreed tags, and send review requests to all eligible customers the same way. Replies to reviews and formal complaints are outside this skill and need a fresh yes. Collect data only through exports, official APIs and licensed tools; no scraping against site terms and no shared passwords. I log every action.

## Steps

### 1. Describe the data

Source, date range, number of items, rating spread, products or versions, languages. Note gaps (a missing month, one marketplace only).

### 2. Clean

Remove duplicates, spam, bot posts, and mentions that match a keyword by accident (a brand name that is also a common word). Count what was removed and why.

### 3. Build the label list

Read a sample of 50 to 100 items across ratings and dates. Draft labels for praise and complaints in plain words: fit and sizing, quality, taste, delivery speed, packaging, value, support, ease of use, missing feature. Add labels as new ones appear. Give each a one-line definition.

### 4. Code everything

Give each item one or more labels and a mood (positive, neutral, negative, mixed). Hand-check the listening tool's sentiment on a sample and report where it is wrong, especially sarcasm and mixed reviews.

### 5. Count and track

For each label: count, share of items, share of negative items, and change over time. Look at what changed when ratings moved. Split by product version, channel and region where the data allows.

### 6. Find causes

For the top complaint labels, read every item and map the wording to likely causes: "runs small" points to the size chart; "not as pictured" points to the listing photos; "arrived late" by region points to a carrier. Mark each cause as likely or confirmed (confirmed only with the client's own data, such as returns records).

### 7. Rank actions

Score each candidate fix on reach (how many raised it), pain (returns, refunds, one-star reviews, "never again") and ease. Order fixes by payoff: listing text and images first, then support, packaging, delivery and product.

### 8. Collect buyer words

Group exact phrases by benefit, doubt and use case, with counts. These feed product pages, ads and FAQs. Quote only what is public or what the client has rights to use; do not publish a reviewer's name without permission.

### 9. Plan honest review requests (if asked)

- Ask every eligible customer the same way, at the same point after delivery, whatever their experience.
- No rewards tied to leaving a review or to a positive rating; no "if you loved it, leave five stars".
- No routing happy customers to public reviews and unhappy ones to private email.
- No reviews from staff, family or shareholders without the platform's rules allowing it and clear disclosure.
- Follow the platform's own rules (Google and Trustpilot both ban selective requests and incentives). In the US the FTC rule bans fake reviews and rewards tied to sentiment; in the UK fake and hidden incentivised reviews are banned. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified adviser if unsure.

## Checks

- Removed items counted by reason.
- Every label defined; every item coded.
- Sentiment hand-checked on a sample.
- Counts and shares have their base; small groups called hints, not patterns.
- Causes marked likely or confirmed.
- Quotes exact; no names published without permission.
- Review request plan treats every customer the same and offers no rating-linked reward.
- Data collected only through exports, APIs or licensed tools.

## Output format

1. Answer first: the top three things buyers say and what to do.
2. Theme table: label, count, share, trend, mood, two quotes.
3. Rating or mood change explained, with dates.
4. Cause map for top complaints.
5. Ranked fix list (reach, pain, ease).
6. Buyer words for copy.
7. Rival comparison (if asked).
8. Review request plan (if asked).
9. Method box: sources, dates, counts, cleaning, tool sentiment accuracy, limits.

## Example of good output

Made-up business for illustration: Hollow Oak Candles, 1,140 reviews from its shop and one marketplace, January to October 2026.

> **Answer first.** 1) Scent strength is the top praise (31% of positive reviews). 2) "Tunnelling" (wax burning down the middle) is the top complaint and rose after the June wick change: 3% of reviews before (12 of 440), 16% after (109 of 700). 3) Gift buyers love the box but want a note card (42 mentions).
>
> | Label | Count | Share | Trend | Quote |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Tunnelling | 121 | 11% | Up since June | "Burned a hole straight down, half the wax wasted" |
> | Scent strength | 298 | 26% | Steady | "Fills the whole downstairs" |
>
> **Likely cause (tunnelling):** the thinner wick introduced in June; 109 of 121 mentions are after that date. Confirm with the burn tests on file.
>
> **Fix list:** 1) Return to the old wick or test a medium one (reach high, pain high). 2) Add first-burn instructions to the box and listing (ease high).
>
> **Removed:** 37 duplicates, 12 spam, 8 off-topic.
