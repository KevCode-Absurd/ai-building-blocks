# Training interview: Customer insights researcher

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client names, participants' names, quotes that could identify anyone, or confidential results; anonymise every story.

1. **What do clients most often ask you to find out, and in which sectors?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and which methods it reaches for first.

2. **How do you turn "we want to understand our customers" into a decision the research can feed?**
   Why it matters: framing decides what to ask and when to stop; the expert's questions become the agent's intake.

3. **How do you choose between interviews, a focus group, a diary study, a survey, review mining or a product trial?**
   Why it matters: method choice decides cost and truth; the expert's rules guide the agent's first recommendation.

4. **Walk me through how you recruit for a round of interviews, from screener to booked sessions.**
   Why it matters: captures the real recruiting method, channels and tricks that fill sessions with the right people.

5. **Who do you screen out, and how do you spot people who are just there for the incentive?**
   Why it matters: sample quality is the first thing that goes wrong; the expert's filters are reusable.

6. **How do you set incentives, and how have you handled no-shows and late payments?**
   Why it matters: fair pay keeps participants honest and protects the client's name.

7. **What goes into your consent and information sheet, and what do you say in the first minute of a session?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a tested consent routine that fits the codes.

8. **Tell me about a time privacy, recording or a child's involvement made you change a study.**
   Why it matters: shows where the rules bite in practice and when to bring in an adviser.

9. **What makes a discussion guide good? Show me a question you rewrote and why.**
   Why it matters: captures the wording habits that get stories instead of opinions.

10. **How do you handle a dominant voice in a focus group, or a quiet participant in an interview?**
    Why it matters: the agent writes moderator notes; the expert's moves make them real.

11. **How do you get from 15 transcripts to five themes? Walk me through your last synthesis.**
    Why it matters: synthesis is the core skill; the expert's steps become the agent's method.

12. **How do you decide whether something is a finding or just a lead?**
    Why it matters: protects clients from acting on one loud voice.

13. **How do you check AI-made transcripts and AI coding before you trust them?**
    Why it matters: transcription and coding tools make errors; the expert's checks become the agent's routine.

14. **How do you read reviews or social mentions differently from interviews?**
    Why it matters: unprompted data has its own biases; the expert's cautions shape the mining skill.

15. **What rules do you follow when a client wants more reviews?**
    Why it matters: review gating and incentives are common traps; the expert's practice keeps clients safe.

16. **How do you design a taste test or product trial so the result can be trusted?**
    Why it matters: serving order, coding and decision rules are easy to get wrong.

17. **What does your readout look like? Walk me through one a client acted on.**
    Why it matters: captures formats that lead to action, which the agent should copy.

18. **How do you turn findings into copy that customers recognise?**
    Why it matters: customer words are one of the most used outputs; the expert's method keeps meaning intact.

19. **What do you check in the last ten minutes before you send a readout?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's quality bar.

20. **Tell me about a hard job: a client who wanted research to confirm a decision already made. What happened?**
    Why it matters: shows how to hold the line on evidence while keeping the client.

21. **What mistakes do newer researchers make that you have stopped making?**
    Why it matters: turns experience into rules the agent can follow from day one.

22. **Which tools do you use for recruiting, scheduling, transcripts, analysis and listening, and what does each do well or badly?**
    Why it matters: fills in access.md with tested tools and the expert's cautions.
