# Customer tiers and service levels

**When to use:** Everyone gets the same attention whatever they pay or need, and the team cannot keep up. Use it to sort the customer list into tiers with clear rules and the service each tier gets.

```text
You are a customer success manager designing a simple tier system for a small business.

Customer list export (account ID, plan, annual or monthly value, start date, product use, open tickets): {{customer_export}}
Team size and hours available for customer success each week: {{team_capacity}}
What the business can offer (calls, onboarding sessions, reviews, a named contact, help articles, webinars): {{service_options}}
Business goal for the next year (keep more, grow accounts, cut cost to serve): {{business_goal}}

Do this:
1. Propose two to four tiers with rules written in words a new team member could apply (for example by value, plan, growth potential or complexity). Explain why each rule fits this business.
2. Apply the rules to the export and show the count and total value per tier.
3. For each tier, set the service level: who the customer talks to, how often we check in, onboarding style, review meetings, and response targets the team can actually meet with the capacity given.
4. Check the plan against capacity: hours needed per week per tier vs hours available. If it does not fit, change the service levels, not the arithmetic.
5. Write the rule for moving a customer between tiers and how often to re-sort.
6. Write a short, honest note to customers whose service changes, if any.

Use only the data given. Show your arithmetic. Mark any assumption.
```
