# Renewal and price change

## When to use

- Annual or multi-year contracts come up for renewal in the next few months.
- A price rise is planned and the owner fears a wave of cancellations.
- Customers complain or dispute charges right after renewal.
- Happy customers could use a higher plan but nobody has asked them.

## What you need

1. For each customer: plan, price, term, renewal date, notice period in the contract, and how they signed up (online, by contract, by phone).
2. Where the customers are (country and US state) and whether they are consumers or businesses.
3. Usage, tickets and any complaints over the term.
4. Results the customer got, with numbers you can show (from reports, usage or the customer's own words).
5. For a price change: old and new price, the date, the reason, and what customers can do (stay, change plan, cancel).
6. The current written renewal and cancellation terms, with their date.

## Steps

### 1. Build the renewal calendar

Sort customers by renewal date. For each, work back from the date to set the steps. A common starting cadence is a check-in about 90 days out, a value recap at 60, the renewal conversation or offer at 30 and a final reminder about a week before; change it to fit the contract's notice period and any legal notice window. For California consumers on terms of a year or more, the renewal notice must go 15 to 45 days before renewal, and an accepted fee increase needs notice 7 to 30 days before it starts (California AG alert, September 2025). In the UK a new subscription regime is expected from January 2027 with renewal reminders; check the final rules. Check this with local rules for every place your customers are.

### 2. Mark the risk

For each customer note: low or falling use, open issues, a new decision maker, a rival mentioned, late payments, a price rise landing at renewal. Use the health score if one exists. Risky renewals get a person's call before any email.

### 3. Write the value recap

Use only numbers and events the customer can check: reports run, orders processed, tickets solved, goals agreed at the start and where they stand. If a number is not in the data, leave it out. Name one thing that did not go well and what was done about it; a recap that admits a problem is believed.

### 4. Write the renewal notices

Facts first, in this order: what renews, the date, the amount and how often, any change from last term, and how to cancel or change plan (with a link or the exact steps). Then one line on what is coming next. Keep the notice a service message: no upgrade pitch inside it. Make the cancel route match how the customer signed up.

### 5. Write the price-change announcement

- Lead with the change: new price, which plan, from which date, for whom.
- Give the honest reason in one or two lines (costs, new features they actually use). Do not claim reasons that are not true.
- Say what they can do: keep the plan, move to another plan (with its price), or cancel before the date, and how.
- Send it early enough for the legal notice window and the customer's budget cycle.
- Check that the contract allows the change. In the UK and EU, one-sided price change terms are judged for fairness; ask a qualified adviser if the terms are unclear.
- Prepare a short FAQ and a reply macro for the questions that will come in.

### 6. Write the upgrade offer

Only for customers whose use already points to the higher plan (near a limit, asking for a feature it includes, adding users). Tie the offer to that fact, show the price difference plainly and make it easy to say no. Send it separately from renewal and price notices; in the US and UK, a promotional email follows marketing rules (opt-out, sender details).

### 7. List facts to confirm

Before anything is sent: dates, prices, notice periods, plan names and links.

### Access needed

- Level 1: read contracts, subscriptions, usage and tickets.
- Level 2: create renewal deals and tasks as drafts; save notices, the announcement and the FAQ as drafts.
- Level 3: send notices and the announcement to the agreed customers on the agreed dates, update renewal fields and apply plan changes customers ask for, within your limits. A price change itself always needs your explicit sign-off.

## Checks

- Every notice states what renews, the date, the amount and how to cancel, in plain words.
- Notice timing meets the contract and the legal window for each place, or the gap is flagged.
- The value recap contains only checkable facts.
- Renewal and price notices carry no promotion; upgrade offers are separate and follow marketing rules.
- The price-change reason is true and the options include cancelling.
- Risky renewals have a person's call planned before the email.
- The plan carries the line: *Check this with local rules before sending.*

## Output format

1. Renewal calendar (customer, renewal date, notice period, value, risk, steps with dates, owner)
2. Value recap per key customer
3. Renewal notice set (check-in, recap, renewal, final reminder)
4. Price-change announcement, FAQ and reply macro (if needed)
5. Upgrade offers for qualifying customers, with the usage fact behind each
6. Facts to confirm and rules to check by place

## Example of good output (short)

> **Illustrative structure, not a real business: a stock app for garden centres. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.**
>
> **Subject:** Your [Product] plan renews on 1 March
>
> "Hi Mo, your [Product] Team plan renews on 1 March 2027 for £1,080 for the year, charged to the card ending 4417. Nothing changes in your plan. If you'd like to switch plan or cancel, go to Settings > Billing before 1 March, or reply to this email and I'll do it for you. In the last year your two stores logged 9,400 stock counts in [Product], and the barcode import you asked for in June went live in September. Thanks for being with us. Alex"
