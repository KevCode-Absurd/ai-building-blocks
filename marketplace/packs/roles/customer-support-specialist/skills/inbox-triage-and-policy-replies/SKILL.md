# Inbox triage and policy replies

## When to use

- The daily run through a shared support inbox or help desk.
- A backlog after a sale, a launch, a shipping delay or time away.
- The owner wants every reply checked against the written policy before it goes out.
- A new team member needs a ranked queue with drafts to learn from.

Do not use it to give legal, medical or safety advice; those cases go to the owner. Refunds and order changes belong to the returns skill.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** read tickets, order facts and past replies.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** add internal notes, save reply drafts and set suggested tags, without sending.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send replies, apply macros, set tags and status, and close resolved tickets, for the ticket types in the confirmed limits. Safety, legal, data and payment-error tickets always go to the owner first. Log every send.

## What you need

1. The messages: pasted, exported from the help desk, or through the access level granted. Remove full card numbers; customers' emails and phones can be replaced with ticket IDs.
2. The written policies: returns, refunds, shipping, warranty, subscriptions and cancellations, with their dates.
3. Order facts for each ticket where relevant: order date, items, delivery status.
4. Five past replies the owner considers their best, to match the tone.
5. The owner's limits: what I may say yes to, and what always needs them (refunds over a set amount, exceptions, legal threats, press).

## Steps

### 1. Read the policy first

List the rules that matter (windows, conditions, who pays postage, refund timing) and any gaps or conflicts between them. Note anything the policy does not cover; those tickets will need the owner.

### 2. Sort every message

Three bands:
- **First:** safety or injury, legal threats or regulator contact, data requests (see and delete my data), account access problems, payment errors or double charges, chargeback threats.
- **Next:** anything with a time limit (a delivery date, an event, a return window closing, a subscription renewing).
- **Then:** general questions, product questions, feedback.

Within each band, oldest first. Flag customers who have written more than once about the same issue; they move up.

### 3. Pick a path

For each message: answer (the policy and facts give the answer), ask for details (one clear question, never "please send more information"), escalate (to the owner, with a two-line summary and the options), or decline kindly (the policy says no, and here is what we can do).

### 4. Draft the reply

- Greeting with the customer's name.
- The answer in the first line.
- The next step: what happens, who does it.
- When they will hear back, as a date or a time window.
- The business's sign-off.
Answer every question in the thread. Never ask for information that is already in it. Use the business's voice; adjust the tone for an upset customer (calmer, more direct, one apology, no jokes).

### 5. Check against the policy and facts

For each draft, check every promise, date, amount and condition against the policy and the order facts. Mark any line the policy does not support: [OWNER: policy does not cover a refund for opened items; approve?]. Never write that a refund, replacement or fix "has been" done unless the owner has confirmed it.

### 6. Write escalation notes

For each escalation: what the customer wants, the facts, what the policy says, the options with their cost, and a recommended reply once the owner decides. Data requests note the date received, because the reply clock (one month under GDPR) has started.

### 7. Hand back

A ranked table, drafts beside each ticket, escalations, and the short list of decisions only the owner can make. Add a note of any pattern seen (several tickets about the same product, page or delay). At level 3, send the replies that sit inside the confirmed limits after the step 5 check, and add the action log: ticket, what was sent, when, and the result.

## Checks before handing over

- Every message is in a band, with a path.
- Every draft answers the main question in the first line and every question in the thread.
- No promise, date or amount goes beyond the policy and facts; anything else is marked for the owner.
- No draft says a refund or fix has happened unless confirmed.
- Safety, legal, data and payment issues are flagged, not answered.
- Every reply sent stayed inside the access level and limits, and is in the action log.
- Tone matches the business's best replies.
- For consumer-law questions, the note says: check this with local rules.

## Output format

1. Flags (safety, legal, data, payment)
2. Ranked table (ticket ID, band, issue, path, what to check)
3. Draft replies, with owner questions in brackets
4. Escalation notes with options and costs
5. Decisions only you can make
6. Patterns seen this run
7. Action log (level 3 only)

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: an online candle shop in Portland, Maine, with a 30-day unopened returns policy. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> | Ticket | Band | Issue | Path | Check |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | #1182 | First | Candle jar cracked while lit; small burn on table | Escalate | Safety: owner only. Ask for photos. |
> | #1179 | Next | Gift order must arrive by Saturday | Answer | Tracking shows Friday delivery |
> | #1175 | Then | Can I return an opened candle? | Decline kindly | Policy: unopened only |
>
> **Draft, #1175**
> Hi Jordan, thanks for asking. We can only take back candles that haven't been opened, so we can't accept this one as a return. If the scent isn't right for you, we're happy to help you choose another for next time, and [OWNER: offer 10% off the next order?]. Best, the [Business] team
>
> **Escalation, #1182:** Customer reports a cracked jar while burning and a small burn mark on a table. Wants a refund and to know if it is safe. Policy does not cover this. Options: full refund ($34); refund plus ask for the jar back for checking with the supplier. Do not give safety advice in the reply; the owner decides wording.
