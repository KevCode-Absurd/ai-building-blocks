# Returns and refunds

## When to use

- A batch of return, exchange or refund requests has come in.
- Customers argue about the returns window, postage or refund timing.
- The returns policy is long, unclear or out of date.
- The owner wants to know why items come back and what returns cost.

Do not use it to waive the policy without the owner's approval, or to decide what consumer law requires in a given country.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** orders, return requests, the return log and refunds in the store and payment provider.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** create returns waiting for approval, save reply drafts, and draft policy text.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** for requests inside the policy and under the refund cap, approve the return, send the label, issue the refund when the policy says, and send the reply. Exceptions, refunds above the cap and goodwill payments need a fresh yes. Log every action with the order and amount.

## What you need

1. The written returns and refund policy, with its date, and where it is shown to customers.
2. The requests, with order number, order and delivery dates, item, reason given and any photos.
3. The return log for the period, for the report: order, item, reason, outcome, refund amount, return shipping cost, restock or write-off.
4. Where the customers are (countries, US states), so the right official rules can be named.
5. The owner's limits: what I may approve in the draft, what always needs them.

## Steps

### 1. Map the policy

List each rule: window, condition of item, exclusions (final sale, personalised, hygiene), who pays return postage, how refunds are paid and when, exchanges and store credit. Note gaps and conflicts, such as a 30-day window on the website and 14 days on the packing slip.

### 2. Split faulty from change of mind

Faulty, damaged, wrong item, or not as described is not a change-of-mind return. It follows the legal rules for the customer's country, which can be wider than the store's window. For example, UK buyers of faulty goods have rights to a refund, repair or replacement, with up to six years to claim, and EU buyers get at least a two-year legal guarantee. Change-of-mind returns follow the store policy, plus any cooling-off period for online sales (14 days in the UK and EU). Check this with local rules for the customer's location.

### 3. Check each request

For each: order date, delivery date, days since delivery, item state, reason, the rule that applies, and the decision the policy gives. Where the policy is silent or the case is borderline, mark it for the owner.

### 4. Draft the reply

- The decision in the first line.
- The reason in one line, quoting the policy.
- The exact next step: return label or address, what to include, by what date.
- When the refund will be issued after the item arrives, as the policy states.
Write that a refund has been processed only once the refund log shows it; until then, write what will happen.

At level 3, for requests inside the policy and under the cap, carry out the decision: approve the return, send the label, issue the refund at the point the policy sets, send the reply, and log each step.

### 5. Flag cases outside the policy

For each: what the customer asks, why it is outside the policy, and the options with their cost: full refund, partial refund, exchange, store credit, keep-it refund (no return), decline. Note the risk of a chargeback where the customer paid by card and is unhappy, and the time and fee a dispute costs.

### 6. Rewrite the policy (when asked)

Plain words, one page: what can come back and when, what cannot, how to start a return, who pays postage, how and when the refund is paid, what happens with faulty items (a separate section that does not limit legal rights). Add short versions for the product page, order confirmation and packing slip, all saying the same thing. Mark it "check this with local rules before publishing".

### 7. Build the monthly report

Group returns by reason and by product. Where costs are given, work out the cost per return and the total. Find the top three products by returns and the top three reasons. Suggest fixes: clearer size guides, better photos, packaging changes, listing corrections, a supplier conversation, or a policy edit. Rank them by expected effect, showing the numbers behind each.

## Checks before handing over

- Faulty-item cases are not refused under change-of-mind rules.
- Every decision quotes the policy line it relies on.
- Every next step has a date.
- No reply says a refund has happened unless the refund log shows it.
- Every return and refund carried out stayed inside the policy, the cap and the confirmed limits, and is logged.
- Cases outside the policy go to the owner with options and costs.
- The policy rewrite separates faulty goods from change-of-mind and does not limit legal rights.
- Numbers in the report come only from the log; no invented benchmarks.
- The output carries: *Check this with local rules before use.*

## Output format

1. Policy map with gaps and conflicts
2. Request table (order, days since delivery, reason, faulty or change of mind, rule, decision)
3. Draft replies
4. Owner decisions with options and costs
5. Plain policy and short versions (when asked)
6. Monthly report and three fixes (when asked)

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: an online clothing shop in Brighton, UK, selling to UK customers, with a 30-day change-of-mind policy. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Template: check this with local rules before use. Applies to: UK customers.**
>
> | Order | Days since delivery | Reason | Type | Decision |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 30441 | 12 | Too small | Change of mind | Accept: within 30 days, unworn |
> | 30417 | 41 | Seam split after two wears | Faulty | Owner: faulty-goods rules apply, not the 30-day window |
> | 30399 | 33 | Didn't like the colour | Change of mind | Decline kindly: outside 30 days |
>
> **Draft, 30441:** Hi Amira, no problem, we can take this back. Please send it unworn with tags on, using the free return label attached, by 20 November. We'll refund you within 5 working days of it arriving, to your original payment method.
>
> **Owner decision, 30417:** Customer reports a split seam after two wears, 41 days after delivery. This is a fault claim, so the 30-day window does not apply. Options: replacement (£4 postage plus stock), full refund (£38), or repair. Ask for a photo first.
