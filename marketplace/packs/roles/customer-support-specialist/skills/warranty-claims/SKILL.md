# Warranty claims

## When to use

- The business offers a warranty or guarantee and claims arrive by email or form.
- Warranty terms are vague, buried in small print or contradict each other.
- The same fault keeps coming back and the owner suspects a bad batch.
- The owner wants to recover costs from a supplier for faulty stock.

Do not use it to decide what consumer law requires, or for change-of-mind returns (use the returns skill).

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the claims log, claim emails, orders and serial or batch records.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save reply drafts and internal notes, draft the terms and the supplier pack.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** for claims clearly inside the written rules and under the cost cap, send the evidence request or approval, create the repair or replacement order, and update the claims log. Declines, claims outside the rules, anything that may be a legal-rights case and supplier claims need a fresh yes. Log every action.

## What you need

1. The warranty rules as written today, where they appear, and the products they cover.
2. The claims log: order or serial number, product, batch if known, purchase date, claim date, fault, outcome, cost.
3. Recent claim emails, with photos or videos the customers sent.
4. Sales numbers by product and batch, if the owner wants failure rates.
5. Supplier terms for faulty stock, if a recovery pack is needed.
6. Where customers are (countries, US states).

## Steps

### 1. Read the rules and find the gaps

List what is covered, for how long, what is excluded, what proof is needed and what remedies are offered. Note gaps and clashes. Mark anything that may clash with consumer law for a qualified adviser to check, for example:
- In the US, a written warranty on a consumer product over $10 must be labelled "full" or "limited", and over $15 must be in one clear document available before purchase; coverage generally cannot require a specific brand of parts or service; and giving a written warranty means implied warranties cannot be disclaimed.
- In the UK and EU, a warranty is extra to the customer's legal rights and cannot reduce them; the EU minimum legal guarantee is two years.
Check this with local rules before publishing anything.

### 2. Write plain terms

One page: what is covered, for how long from which date, what is not covered (with plain examples such as accidental damage or normal wear), how to claim, what proof to send, what happens next and how long it takes, and a line that the warranty does not affect the customer's legal rights.

### 3. Set evidence and triage rules

For each product type: proof of purchase, serial or batch number, photos or a short video showing the fault, and when to ask for the item back. A triage table: fault type, likely cause, first step (troubleshooting, evidence, repair, replace, refund) and who decides.

### 4. Decide each claim against the rules

For each claim: purchase date and age at claim, fault, the rule that applies, and the remedy the rules give (repair, replace, refund or decline). Where evidence is missing, the path is "ask for evidence" with one clear request. Where the rules do not cover the case, it goes to the owner.

### 5. Draft the reply

The decision, the reason in one line quoting the rule, and the exact next step with a date. For a decline, say what the customer can do instead, and do not tell them they have no rights; where the claim may fall under legal rights rather than the warranty, flag it for the owner instead of declining.

### 6. Flag claims outside the rules

For each: the facts, why the rules do not cover it, the options (repair, replace, refund, partial refund, goodwill gesture, decline) and the cost of each.

### 7. Build the claims report

Group claims by product, batch, fault type and age at claim (for example, under 3 months, 3 to 12 months, over 12 months). Where sales numbers are given, work out claims per 100 units sold for each product and batch. Highlight batches with a much higher rate than the product's average and the faults that cost the most.

### 8. Build the supplier recovery pack

For a bad batch: a fault summary, counts, dates, photos and test notes, the cost of each claim, the supplier's own terms on faulty stock, and a draft claim letter. Supplier claims go out only after the owner's yes, at any access level.

## Checks before handing over

- Every decision quotes the rule it relies on.
- Missing evidence leads to a clear request, not a decline.
- Possible legal-rights cases are flagged for the owner, not declined.
- Terms say the warranty does not affect legal rights, and are marked for an adviser.
- No reply says a repair, replacement or refund has happened unless the log shows it.
- Every live action stayed inside the rules, the cost cap and the confirmed limits, and is logged.
- Failure rates use only the sales and claims numbers given.
- The output carries: *Check this with local rules before use.*

## Output format

1. Rules map with gaps and possible clashes
2. Plain warranty terms
3. Evidence and triage table
4. Claim decisions table and draft replies
5. Owner decisions with options and costs
6. Claims report by product, batch, fault and age
7. Supplier recovery pack (when asked)

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: an e-bike brand in Denver, Colorado, with a two-year limited warranty on frames and motors. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Template: check this with local rules before use. Applies to: US customers.**
>
> | Claim | Product and batch | Age | Fault | Rule | Path |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | W-208 | Trail 500, batch 24C | 7 months | Motor cuts out on hills | Motor covered 2 years | Ask for a 20-second video and the error code |
> | W-211 | Trail 500, batch 24C | 5 months | Same | Same | Approve repair once video received |
> | W-215 | City 300, batch 25A | 14 months | Cracked basket | Accessories not covered | Owner: decline or offer basket at cost |
>
> **Report note:** batch 24C has 9 motor claims per 100 units sold, against 2 per 100 for other Trail 500 batches. Recommend pausing sales of remaining 24C stock and opening a supplier claim.
