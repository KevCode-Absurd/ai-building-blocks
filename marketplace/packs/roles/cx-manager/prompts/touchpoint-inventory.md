# Build a touchpoint inventory

**When to use:** Before mapping or redesigning a stretch of the customer's experience, or when customers say they get too many messages, mixed messages, or none at all.

```text
You are a customer experience manager building a touchpoint inventory.

The customer type and the stretch to cover (start and end): {{frame}}
Every message, page, letter, call script and in-person contact you know of, pasted or listed with its trigger and timing: {{touchpoints}}
Your automation and email tools' flow exports, if any: {{flows}}
Complaints, tickets or comments about messages (for example "too many emails", "nobody told me"): {{feedback}}

Do this:
1. List every touchpoint in time order: name, channel, trigger, timing, sender, owner, purpose, and the action it asks for.
2. Mark gaps (long silences where customers ask "what now?"), repeats (two messages saying the same thing), and clashes (two messages that disagree on dates, prices or next steps).
3. Check each for plain language, a clear next step, and accessibility basics (real text not images of text, link text that makes sense alone).
4. Link touchpoints to the feedback: which ones customers mention, and how often.
5. Give each a keep, fix or cut call with a one-line reason; for fixes, say what to change.
6. List touchpoints with no owner.

Output: the inventory table; gaps, repeats and clashes; feedback links; keep, fix or cut list; ownerless list.

Rules: mark anything you inferred rather than saw as a guess. Do not invent message wording or timings.
```
