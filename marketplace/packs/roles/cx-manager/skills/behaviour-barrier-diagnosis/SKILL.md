# Behaviour barrier diagnosis

## When to use

- People start something and do not finish it: sign-up forms, applications, checkouts, onboarding steps.
- People are slow to do something that helps them and the business: paying on time, booking a follow-up, turning on a feature.
- A team wants "nudges" and needs ideas that are honest and testable.

Every idea here is a hypothesis until tested. I never promise that a change will work.

## What you need

1. The one behaviour you want: who, doing what, by when ("new clients pay their first invoice within 14 days").
2. What happens now, with numbers: funnel steps, completion rates, time taken, reminders sent.
3. The actual wording, emails, forms and screens involved.
4. Customer comments, tickets or survey answers about the step.
5. What you are able to change (wording, defaults, timing, number of steps, channel) and what you cannot.

**Access:** level 1 to read analytics, messages and forms; level 2 to draft changed wording, a test plan, or a paused test in the client's email, form or testing tool; level 3 to start an approved test within the agreed audience and dates.

## Steps

### 1. Define one behaviour

Write it as who, does what, by when. If the client has several, pick the one that matters most and park the rest.

### 2. Map intent to action

List the steps from "decides to" to "has done it", including the ones outside your product (finding the invoice, asking a partner, getting a document). Mark where people stop, using the client's data. Where there is no data, say what to measure first.

### 3. Name the barrier at each stop

For each stop, the likely reason, with the evidence for it:
- **Effort:** too many steps, fields, documents or decisions.
- **Doubt:** unclear cost, terms, next step or what happens if it goes wrong.
- **Timing:** the ask arrives at the wrong moment, or too early to act on.
- **Forgetting:** nothing prompts action when it is due.
- **Cost:** money, time or loss of something they value.
- **Social cues:** nothing shows that people like them do this.

### 4. Match barriers to small changes (EAST)

- **Easy:** remove steps and fields, pre-fill what you know, set a helpful default that people can change, make the next action one clear button.
- **Attractive:** make the benefit concrete and personal; make the action stand out on the page.
- **Social:** show what most people like them do, only if it is true and you can show the data.
- **Timely:** ask when people can act; remind them just before the due date; plan for the moments habits change.

### 5. Run the ethics test on every idea

Would the customer be happy if they saw why we did it? Drop anything that hides costs, invents urgency or scarcity, pre-ticks consent, makes cancelling harder than joining, shames people, or uses a social claim you cannot prove. The ux-designer kit sets out the deceptive design rules in the US, UK and EU.

### 6. Write each idea as a test

For the top ideas: the change, who sees it, the comparison (before and after, or a split test), how long, the measure, and the result that would count as a win, a loss or no answer, written before the test starts. Keep one change per test where possible, so the result means something.

### 7. Rate the evidence

Say for each barrier whether the evidence is strong (data shows the stop and customers say why), weak (one source) or missing (a guess to test).

## Checks

- One behaviour, written as who, what and by when.
- Every barrier has evidence or is marked as a guess.
- Every idea passes the ethics test, with the reason.
- Every idea is written as a test with a measure and a pre-agreed decision rule.
- No claim that "research proves" a nudge works; only studies the client supplied are cited.
- No fake urgency, hidden costs or pre-ticked boxes.

## Output format

1. The behaviour, and what happens now.
2. Behaviour map from intent to action, with drop-off points.
3. Barrier table: step, barrier, evidence, strength.
4. Ideas table: barrier, change (EAST type), ethics check, measure.
5. Test plans for the top two or three ideas.
6. What to measure next where data is missing.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bookkeeping firm whose new clients pay their first invoice late. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Behaviour.** New clients pay their first invoice within 14 days. Now: 41% do (last 6 months, 87 new clients).
>
> | Step | Barrier | Evidence | Strength |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Invoice email arrives | Doubt: amount differs from the quote because of set-up fees | 12 emails asking "why is this higher?" | Strong |
> | Payment | Effort: bank transfer only, details in a PDF | 9 clients asked for a card link | Medium |
> | Due date | Forgetting: no reminder before the due date | Most late payments arrive after the first reminder at day 21 | Strong |
>
> | Idea | Type | Ethics check | Measure |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Show set-up fees on the quote and repeat them in the invoice's first line | Easy, Attractive | Passes: more transparent | Share paid within 14 days; "why higher" emails |
> | Add a card payment link next to bank details | Easy | Passes | Same |
> | Friendly reminder on day 10 | Timely | Passes: no threat, states the due date | Same |
