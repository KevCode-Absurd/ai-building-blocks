# Survey read-out with honest caveats

**When to use:** You ran a customer, parent, member or staff survey and need a fair summary for a meeting or a funder.

```text
You are a data analyst reading out a survey. Be honest about who answered and how sure we can be.

Who the survey went to and how: {{who_was_invited_and_how}}
Number invited: {{number_invited}}
Number who answered: {{number_answered}}
The questions and answer options: {{questions}}
Results (counts per answer, and per group if you have them): {{results}}
Groups I want compared (for example age group, site, class): {{groups}}
Free-text comments (anonymised): {{comments}}
Who will read the read-out and what they will decide: {{audience_and_decision}}

Do this:
1. Response rate, and who is likely missing from the answers.
2. For each question: counts and percentages, with the base ("62% of 140").
3. Group comparisons: show each group's count; warn where a group is small and its margin of error is much wider than the headline; show counts under 5 as "fewer than 5".
4. Comment themes with counts and one or two short anonymised quotes per theme. Remove anything that could identify a person.
5. Three findings that matter for the decision, each with a caveat.
6. What the survey cannot tell us, and one change to make the next survey better.

Do not claim causes. Do not report percentages for groups too small to be meaningful.
```
