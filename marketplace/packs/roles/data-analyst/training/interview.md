# Training interview: data analyst

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out client names and any personal data; describe datasets in general terms.

1. **What kinds of data and businesses do you know best: sales, bookings, members, surveys, finance?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and where its advice stops.

2. **Walk me through the last analysis you did, from the first message to the final answer.**
   Why it matters: shows the real order of work and where time actually goes.

3. **When a client says "just look at the data and tell me something interesting", what do you ask next?**
   Why it matters: captures how the expert turns a vague request into a decision.

4. **What do you check in the first ten minutes with a new file?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's profiling habits and red flags.

5. **Tell me about the messiest export you have cleaned. What was wrong, and how did you make the fix repeatable?**
   Why it matters: records real clean-up rules and the tools used to repeat them.

6. **How do you handle duplicates that are not exact, like the same customer spelled two ways?**
   Why it matters: pulls out matching rules and where a person must decide.

7. **When do you set rows aside, when do you drop them, and how do you report it?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's line between cleaning and changing the answer.

8. **Which tools do you reach for first, and when do you switch (Excel, Power Query, Sheets, SQL, Python)?**
   Why it matters: grounds tool choices in practice, not fashion.

9. **How do you decide whether a finding is real or noise?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's tests for size, steadiness and outliers.

10. **Tell me about a time the overall number said one thing and the groups said another.**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a real Simpson's-paradox style case to learn from.

11. **How do you build a forecast for a small business with only two or three years of history?**
    Why it matters: records method choice, holdout testing and how ranges are set.

12. **How do you explain uncertainty to an owner who wants one number?**
    Why it matters: captures plain-language ways to present ranges and caveats.

13. **How do you read a survey with a low response rate or tiny groups?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's caveats on samples, margins and small counts.

14. **What do you do when someone asks you to compare their numbers with an industry figure?**
    Why it matters: pulls out like-for-like checks and how weak comparisons are labelled.

15. **How do you handle personal data you did not ask for, like names or health notes in a register?**
    Why it matters: makes the agent's privacy habits concrete.

16. **Have you ever been pushed to make the numbers say something? What did you do?**
    Why it matters: records how the expert holds the line on honesty with clients.

17. **Do you use AI tools in your analysis? Where do you trust them and where do you not?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's rules for checking AI-made formulas and labels.

18. **What does your finished brief look like, and what do clients actually read?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's output format around what gets used.

19. **What is the most common mistake you see in other people's analyses or dashboards?**
    Why it matters: adds checks the agent should run on every piece of work.

20. **Tell me about an analysis that changed a decision. What made it land?**
    Why it matters: captures how findings are framed so people act on them.
