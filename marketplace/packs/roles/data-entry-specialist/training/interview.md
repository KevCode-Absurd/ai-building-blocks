# Training interview: data entry specialist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share real customer names, card or bank numbers, ID numbers or account logins; anonymise every example.

1. **What kinds of documents have you entered most (invoices, receipts, forms, cards, orders, medical or legal records), and into which systems?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's default inputs, target systems and field lists.

2. **Walk me through one batch from the moment documents arrive to the moment the data is in the system. Where does the time go?**
   Why it matters: the expert's real sequence becomes the agent's batch workflow.

3. **What do you do first, before you type a single value?**
   Why it matters: shows whether the expert maps fields and counts documents up front, the habit that prevents rework.

4. **Tell me about a value you could not read. What did you do, and what happened next?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's exception habit and how they phrase questions to the client.

5. **Have you ever been pushed to fill in missing values so an import would work? How did you handle it?**
   Why it matters: teaches the agent how to hold the "never guess" line politely.

6. **What spreadsheet surprises have bitten you: lost zeros, dates, long numbers, codes turned into dates, odd spaces?**
   Why it matters: real traps make the agent's pre-import checks specific.

7. **How do you check your own work? When do you use a read-back, double entry, control totals or a spot check?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's rules for matching the check to the risk.

8. **How big is your spot-check sample, how do you pick it, and what makes you recheck a whole batch?**
   Why it matters: turns an instinct into a stated acceptance rule.

9. **Which OCR or scanning tools have you used, and where do they go wrong?**
   Why it matters: tells the agent which OCR errors to look for first.

10. **How do you handle handwriting?**
    Why it matters: handwriting is the weakest input, and the expert's rule protects the client.

11. **Tell me about an import that failed or created a mess. What was the cause and how did you fix it?**
    Why it matters: a real failure becomes a decision rule and a test brief.

12. **How do you find duplicates before they get into the system?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's duplicate rules and what to check against.

13. **When a document's total does not match its lines, what do you do?**
    Why it matters: confirms the copy-as-written rule and who decides.

14. **What sensitive data have you come across (card numbers, bank details, ID numbers), and what did you do with it?**
    Why it matters: makes the agent's leave-it-out rule match real practice.

15. **Have you ever seen a document that looked like a fake invoice or a changed bank detail? What gave it away?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent which payment red flags to pass to the client.

16. **Show me a rules sheet or set of entry instructions you wrote, anonymised. What made people follow it?**
    Why it matters: a model of format and tone for the rules sheet skill.

17. **How do you set up a sheet so others cannot enter bad data?**
    Why it matters: the expert's validation habits become the agent's defaults.

18. **What do you say in a batch handover? What do clients actually read?**
    Why it matters: shapes the batch summary and exceptions table.

19. **How fast is fast enough, and when do you slow down?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent when to trade speed for extra checks.

20. **What did you learn the hard way about backups before imports or bulk changes?**
    Why it matters: grounds the backup and fresh-yes rule in experience.

21. **Which mistakes do new data entry staff make in their first month?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's common mistakes list and coaching notes.

22. **Tell me about the hardest job you ever did: the documents, the deadline, what went wrong and how you finished it.**
    Why it matters: a story that shows judgement under pressure, for the agent's specialty section.
