# Training interview: data scientist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out client names, vendor names under agreement and any personal data; describe datasets and models in general terms.

1. **What kinds of questions do clients bring you most: model claims, tests, "did it work?", analysis of a dataset?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and where its advice stops.

2. **Walk me through the last model or claim you reviewed, from the first message to your verdict.**
   Why it matters: shows the real order of work and where the time goes.

3. **When someone says "the vendor says it is 95% accurate", what are your first three questions?**
   Why it matters: captures how the expert pins base rate, threshold and test data.

4. **How do you get a client to put a cost on each kind of mistake when they say they do not know?**
   Why it matters: costs drive every verdict; the expert's method for ranges is worth keeping.

5. **How do you explain precision and recall to an owner in one minute?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's plain-words explanations.

6. **Tell me about a time a model looked great and turned out to be leaking information. How did you find it?**
   Why it matters: records the leakage signs the expert watches for.

7. **How do you check whether a score's numbers can be read as probabilities?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's calibration habits.

8. **When have you recommended a simple rule instead of a model, and how did you show it was good enough?**
   Why it matters: the simple alternative is often the most valuable advice.

9. **How do you size an A/B test, and what do you say when the client does not have enough traffic?**
   Why it matters: records the expert's sample size method and the alternatives they offer.

10. **What do you do when you find out a test was stopped early or changed mid-run?**
    Why it matters: captures how the expert rescues or discounts a compromised result.

11. **How do you check the traffic split, and what have you found when it was off?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent real causes of sample ratio mismatch.

12. **How do you report a result that is not significant without it being read as "no effect"?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's language for inconclusive results.

13. **How do you pick a test when the data is small, skewed or full of zeros?**
    Why it matters: records the expert's choices beyond the textbook defaults.

14. **When a client wants to claim a cause from a before-and-after comparison, what do you do?**
    Why it matters: captures how the expert handles causal claims tactfully.

15. **How do you check a model that scores people for fairness, and what do you do with small groups?**
    Why it matters: records the expert's fairness method and its limits.

16. **Which rules do you flag for clients using AI on people, and where do you stop and send them to a lawyer?**
    Why it matters: sets the line between technical review and legal advice.

17. **What goes in your model card or one-page summary of a model?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's documentation format.

18. **How often do you recheck a model in use, and what tells you it has drifted?**
    Why it matters: captures monitoring triggers from real practice.

19. **What tools do you use, and what do you check in each before trusting its verdict?**
    Why it matters: records tool traps, such as Bayesian and frequentist engines reading differently.

20. **Tell me about the hardest review you have done. What went wrong, and what would you do differently?**
    Why it matters: a real story shows judgment under pressure that briefs cannot.

21. **What do plain AI tools get wrong when they analyse tests or models?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the mistakes to beat in adversarial rounds.

22. **How do you write the first line of a report so a busy owner acts on it correctly?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's verdict-first voice.
