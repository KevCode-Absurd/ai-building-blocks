# Infographic and data deck layout

## When to use

- A charity, council or business wants its yearly results on one page people will share.
- A survey summary or report needs a social carousel, one fact per slide.
- A spreadsheet must become a board, investor or client update deck.
- A monthly results deck needs a repeatable structure.

Not for: the final artwork itself when the client has a designer (I write what they build), animated graphics, or live dashboards.

## What you need

1. The numbers, steps or comparisons, each with unit, date and source.
2. The reader, where it will be shared (print, web page, LinkedIn, Instagram, a meeting) and how long they have.
3. For a deck: the talk length, the one decision or point the audience should leave with, and any template.
4. Brand colours (hex), fonts and logo rules.
5. The platform's current size for each format. Platform sizes change, so I check the platform's own help page on the day, or use the size in the client's template.
6. Access: level 1 to read. Level 2 to build drafts in Canva, Figma or a copy of the deck. Level 3 to publish or replace live files, after a yes on the list.

## Steps

### 1. Find the one message

Read every number and write the main message as a plain sentence a reader could repeat: "Our volunteers delivered 18% more meals this year with the same budget." That sentence becomes the title or first slide. Numbers that do not support it go to an appendix or a "more facts" strip, or get cut.

### 2. List the facts with their proof

A table: fact, number, unit, date, source, and whether it supports the main message. Check that totals add up and percentages use the same base. Anything that cannot be traced is marked and left out until the client confirms it.

### 3. Order the story

For an infographic: headline fact, context (compared with what), breakdown, what it means, call to action. For a deck: the answer first, then the evidence, one message per slide, then the decision or ask. For a carousel: one fact per slide, a hook on the first, the source and call to action on the last.

### 4. Pick a visual per fact

- One number that matters: a big number with its comparison ("up from 41,200 last year").
- Several values to compare: sorted bars, labelled directly.
- Change over time: a line with the start and end labelled.
- A process: numbered steps.
- Part of a whole: a stacked bar; an icon array only when parts are simple fractions; a pie only with five or fewer parts, a meaningful total and one clear winner.
- No 3D, no icons scaled by area, no decorative charts that carry no numbers.

### 5. Lay out the page or slide

- **Infographic:** canvas size, a simple column grid, zones from top to bottom with the share of height each takes, a clear reading path (top to bottom, left to right). One highlight colour for the main point; everything else neutral.
- **Deck:** a title that states the finding on every slide (not "Q3 revenue"), one chart or one big number per slide, the takeaway in the title and the evidence in the chart, source in the footer.
- **Carousel:** the same layout on every slide so the eye knows where to look; large type; the fact readable without the caption.

### 6. Write every word and number

Exact headline, labels, numbers with units and dates, and short notes. No lorem ipsum. Keep sentences short and plain. For decks, add two-sentence speaker notes per slide: what the slide shows and why it matters for the decision.

### 7. Set colours and check contrast

Hex codes for each role (highlight, text, neutral, background). Text 4.5:1 against its background (3:1 if large), chart parts 3:1, and a second cue besides colour. List the ratios. Check the page in greyscale.

### 8. Add the text alternative and source

Alt text for the whole graphic giving the main message and key numbers; a text version (the facts table) to publish next to it on the web; a source line with the brief's sources and dates on the graphic itself.

### 9. Write build notes or build it

At level 1, a layout sheet a designer can follow in Canva, Figma, Illustrator or PowerPoint, with sizes, grid, styles and each chart spec. At level 2, build a draft design or a copy of the deck from the client's template. At level 3, after the client confirms the list, publish or replace the live file and log it. Posting to social accounts is a separate access grant.

## Checks

- One main message, stated in the title.
- Every number matches the brief, with unit, date and source; nothing estimated.
- Bars start at zero; no 3D; pies only with five or fewer parts.
- Each slide or section makes one point.
- Contrast ratios listed and passing; greyscale check done.
- Alt text and a text version provided; source line on the graphic.
- Nothing published beyond the access level and limits granted.

## Output format

1. Main message (one sentence).
2. Facts table with sources.
3. Layout: size, grid, sections or slides in order, each with exact words, numbers and visual type.
4. Chart specs for each chart.
5. Colours with hex codes and contrast ratios; fonts and sizes.
6. Reading path, alt text, text version, source line.
7. Build notes for the named tool, or the draft link at level 2.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: "Brookfield Food Bank", one-page annual summary.*

> **Main message:** "We gave out 18% more food parcels in 2026 on the same budget."
>
> | Zone | Height | Content | Visual |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 Headline | 20% | "18% more parcels, same budget" | Big number "18%" in #12436D, with "12,480 parcels in 2026, up from 10,576" |
> | 2 Context | 20% | Parcels per month, Jan to Dec 2026 | Line, start and end labelled |
> | 3 Breakdown | 30% | Parcels by referral source, sorted | Four bars, labels on bars, one colour |
> | 4 What it means | 15% | Two sentences on volunteer hours | Text with one icon |
> | 5 Ask | 15% | "Volunteer on Saturdays: brookfield.example/help" | Button-style box |
>
> **Source line:** "Brookfield Food Bank parcel log and accounts, Jan to Dec 2026."
> **Alt text:** "Infographic: Brookfield Food Bank gave out 12,480 parcels in 2026, 18% more than 2025, on the same budget. Full figures in the text below."
