# Training interview: data visualisation specialist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out client names, confidential numbers and anything under a confidentiality agreement; describe the work in general terms.

1. **What kinds of charts, reports and readers do you know best, and what work do you turn down?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and the limits of its advice.

2. **When a client sends a spreadsheet and says "make it look good", what do you ask first?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's intake questions for thin briefs.

3. **How do you decide on the message of a chart before you design it? Walk me through a recent one.**
   Why it matters: records the step that turns data into a point.

4. **How do you choose between a bar, a line, a dot plot, a table or one big number?**
   Why it matters: grounds the agent's chart choice in real judgement, not a lookup table.

5. **Tell me about a time a client wanted a chart that would have misled readers. What did you do?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert holds the line on honesty while keeping the client.

6. **What is your rule on axes that do not start at zero, and when have you broken it?**
   Why it matters: captures the nuance between bars, lines and dot plots.

7. **How do you build a palette from brand colours that fail contrast checks?**
   Why it matters: records the practical colour method the agent should follow.

8. **Which tools do you use to check contrast and colour blindness, and what do you look for?**
   Why it matters: names the checks and tools the agent should suggest.

9. **How do you write alt text and long descriptions for charts? Show me one you were proud of.**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's standard for text alternatives.

10. **Which libraries or tools do you build in (Vega-Lite, Chart.js, Datawrapper, Flourish, Excel, Canva, Figma), and what traps does each have?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent tool-specific know-how beyond the docs.

11. **How do you handle a chart with too many series or categories?**
    Why it matters: captures fixes such as small multiples, focus charts and grouping.

12. **How do you lay out an infographic so people read it in the right order?**
    Why it matters: records the expert's reading-path and grid habits.

13. **How do you turn a results spreadsheet into a deck for a board or client?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's slide structure and headline writing.

14. **What do you check before anything you made goes live?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's pre-publish checklist.

15. **How do you make a monthly report's charts consistent from one month to the next?**
    Why it matters: captures style guides, templates and tie-out habits.

16. **When numbers in a brief do not add up, what do you do?**
    Why it matters: makes sure the agent stops and asks rather than fixes data silently.

17. **What access do you usually get in a client's tools, and what do you never do without asking?**
    Why it matters: aligns the agent's habits with the three access levels and the client's limits.

18. **Tell me about the hardest chart job you have done. What went wrong and what did you learn?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a real story of judgement under pressure.

19. **Which chart mistakes do you see most often from people who are new to this?**
    Why it matters: tunes the agent's review to the problems clients actually have.

20. **How do you explain a chart problem to a client without sounding like a critic?**
    Why it matters: shapes the tone of the agent's feedback.
