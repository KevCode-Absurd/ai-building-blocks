# B2B demand generation manager: what the role knows

Checked 6 November 2026. Every platform fact carries a source number [n] from `sources.json` and the date it was true. Platforms and rules change often, so the facts in section 10 are re-checked every 30 days. This is marketing practice, not legal advice: consent rules are summarised from official guidance and flagged for the client to check, never ruled on.

Related kits cover some of this ground in more depth, and this file points to them rather than repeating them: the Email marketing specialist kit (sender rules and the full email consent table), the Marketing analyst kit (lift tests and which number to trust), the Growth marketer kit (unit economics and the most a customer may cost), the Google Ads media buyer kit, and the Traction channel files on speaking and webinars, offline events and trade shows.

## 1. What this role is for

**Who hires me.** B2B firms with a considered sale: software, professional services, manufacturers selling to other businesses, training and safety providers, wholesalers. Deals are large enough, and slow enough, that several people at the buyer must agree. The person asking is usually a founder, a head of marketing or a sales leader who says "we need more pipeline" and means "we need more of the right companies talking to sales".

**The four jobs.**
1. **Choose the accounts.** Agree one target account list with sales, sorted into tiers by fit and by the buying signals the client can actually see.
2. **Run plays for each tier.** Decide what each tier sees across LinkedIn, email, webinars, events, content and sales touches, in order, with owners and dates.
3. **Turn interest into conversations.** Score leads and accounts, agree when sales takes over, and follow up fast.
4. **Report pipeline honestly.** Show accounts engaged, meetings, pipeline and won revenue, with each number labelled as counted, credited by a model, or tested.

**What good work looks like.** A target list both teams signed. A play plan where every step has an owner and a week. Messages written for each role in the buying group, not one message for "the company". A scoring rule tested on last quarter's leads before it goes live. A written handoff agreement with a response time and a way back for leads sales rejects. A monthly report that sales leaders read and act on.

**How the work is judged.** Qualified pipeline and won revenue from target accounts, the share of handed-over leads that sales accepts, follow-up speed, and cost per opportunity. Not leads, clicks or form fills on their own.

**What I never do.** Buy, scrape or guess personal contact details. Email or message people the law in their country does not allow me to contact. Promise pipeline or a return. Use a seat that belongs to one person, such as a Sales Navigator licence; its holder uses it and shares exports with me. Act beyond the access level the client granted.

## 2. The canon

Each idea is summarised in my own words and credited. None of these authors has reviewed or endorsed AI Blocks.

**The 95-5 rule (LinkedIn B2B Institute with the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute) [1].** At any moment only a small share of possible business buyers is ready to buy; the B2B Institute puts the rest, about 95%, out of the market for now. Its examples: most companies replace computers about every four years and change banks about every five. The lesson for demand generation: lead capture only reaches the few who are buying now, so a programme also needs work that keeps the brand remembered by the many who will buy later. The page notes that 96% of B2B marketers surveyed expected the main effect of advertising within two weeks, which it calls a myth [1].

**The B2B Effectiveness Code (WARC, Lions and the LinkedIn B2B Institute) [2].** A study of B2B advertising from 2010 to 2021 that sorts effects into a ladder, from triggering a response and generating leads, through closing sales and making the brand famous, to building a long-term strategic asset. Useful as a vocabulary: be clear which rung a campaign is meant to reach, and measure it on that rung.

**Forrester, Predictions 2027 for business buying (28 October 2026) [3].** Forrester predicts AI agents will take part in 75% of B2B purchases by 2027, doing bounded work such as supplier discovery and RFP drafting, while buyers double their human contacts (experts, references, analysts) to check what the AI recommends. It cites its 2026 buyer survey: 82% of buyers said their purchase included some AI functionality. The blog also says internal buying teams grew from 8 to 13 people, without a sample or date. Treat these as one analyst firm's predictions and survey, not facts about your market.

**Reading list (credited, no figures quoted from them).** Les Binet and Peter Field, *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA, 2013), on balancing brand building and short-term activation. Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (2010), on reaching all buyers in a category. Brent Adamson, Matthew Dixon, Pat Spenner and Nick Toman, *The Challenger Customer* (2015), on buying groups and the people inside them who push a decision forward. Bev Burgess and Dave Munn, *A Practitioner's Guide to Account-Based Marketing* (2017), on one-to-one, one-to-few and one-to-many account programmes. The SiriusDecisions demand waterfall (now part of Forrester), the stage-by-stage view of how inquiries become opportunities.

## 3. How the work is done now

### 3.1 Target accounts and tiers

Start from the client's won and lost deals, not from a wish list. Find what the best customers share: industry, size, location, tools they use, and the event that set off the purchase (a new site, a regulation, a funding round, a failed supplier). That becomes the ideal customer profile.

Score every candidate account on **fit** (how closely it matches the profile) and on **signals** the client can actually see: website visits from the company, event attendance, hiring for a relevant role, a renewal date in the CRM, a partner referral. Intent data from a third-party tool counts only if the client has it and shares it.

Sort accounts into three tiers, a convention many practitioners use:
- **One-to-one (tier 1):** a short list where named people write custom content, plan events and coordinate sales touches account by account.
- **One-to-few (tier 2):** clusters of similar accounts (same industry or same problem) that share tailored content.
- **One-to-many (tier 3):** the rest of the profile, reached with targeted ads, webinars and newsletters.

Size tier 1 by the hours the team really has, not by ambition. Sales must agree the list before any play starts.

### 3.2 Buying groups

A B2B purchase is a group decision. Map, for each account type, who uses the product, who champions it, who owns the budget and who can block it (security, legal, procurement, IT). Write each role's pain, the proof that role trusts, and one ask.

LinkedIn now offers a **buyer groups** audience in Campaign Manager, built by LinkedIn's models from profile and relationship signals across 56 product categories (as of November 2026) [5]. A/B testing is not available for it, predictive audiences switch off when it is on, and saved buyer group audiences can be shared across ad accounts while company list segments cannot [5]. It is a targeting option, not a substitute for the client's own map of who decides.

### 3.3 LinkedIn programmes (as of November 2026)

- **Audiences.** An ad set needs at least 300 member accounts [4]. Advertisers can upload company or contact lists (Matched Audiences) and target by company size, industry, job function, seniority and more; in the EEA and Switzerland, reach for some options, including Matched Audiences, may be limited, and Member Groups are not available there [4].
- **Retargeting event audiences.** Matched Audiences can target people by how they engaged with a LinkedIn Event: registered and attended, registered but did not attend, or engaged without registering. Each audience still needs 300 matched members [6].
- **Lead Gen Forms.** The advertiser must give a privacy policy URL and describe how leads will be used; up to five disclosure checkboxes can collect consent for specific uses, and since 21 April 2020 no default consent box is shown to EU members [7]. LinkedIn keeps Sponsored and Event leads for 365 days and Page, product and landing page leads for 90 days, and does not allow sensitive data to be requested [8]. Download or sync leads well inside those windows.
- **Thought Leader Ads.** Promoting a post from an employee rather than the company page. Snowflake used them as the only paid tactic for its first Dev Day in 2024: an advocate's post reached a 21.2% click-through rate and drove 64 sign-ups from 40 target accounts, in LinkedIn's own case study [14].
- **Event Ads and LinkedIn Live.** 360Suite, a software firm of 50 to 100 people, streamed virtual events on LinkedIn Live, promoted them with Event Ads, retargeted engaged non-registrants and reported more than 2,000 registrations and 632 sales-qualified leads in six weeks, at $19 per registration (LinkedIn case study, undated) [15]. To stream on LinkedIn Live a member or Page needs more than 150 followers or connections, an account at least 30 days old, a record of good standing and a third-party streaming tool [13].
- **Seats tied to a person.** Sales Navigator is licensed per seat. Only the seat's holder uses it; the agent works from the saved lists and exports the holder shares.

Vendor case studies show what is possible, chosen by the vendor. They are not averages.

### 3.4 Webinars and events in a demand programme

A webinar earns its place when it teaches the buying group something they need to decide, then offers one clear next step. Planning order: the next step first, then the follow-up, then the session, then the invitations.

- **Registration timing.** In Livestorm's 2025 data, 49.7% of registrations came in the final seven days and 15.3% on the day itself [20]. Keep promoting until the start, and send reminders in the final week and on the day.
- **Show-up.** Livestorm's average show-up rate was 47.7%, with 175 registrants and 83 live attendees per webinar on average [20]. Plan follow-up for both groups.
- **Replays.** Public replays drew about 3.7 times the views of gated ones in the same data [20]. Gate only when the lead is worth more than the reach.
- **Follow-up.** Separate messages for attendees, no-shows and the most engaged; route engaged target-account attendees to sales by name.

Physical events, booths and run sheets belong to the Event manager kit; the Traction speaking and offline-events files cover cheap first tests.

### 3.5 Lead scoring and the handoff to sales

- **Scores.** HubSpot's scoring tool (page dated 2 September 2026) builds fit scores (properties such as job title or company size), engagement scores (actions such as visits and form fills) and combined scores, for contacts and companies; points can be subtracted; engagement points can decay every 1, 3, 6 or 12 months; it needs a Professional or Enterprise subscription, and AI-built scores need Marketing Hub Enterprise and at least 50 contacts, 25 converted and 25 not [16]. Other CRMs have similar tools.
- **Build from outcomes.** Compare won and lost deals: which fit traits and actions came before a real opportunity? Weight those. Test the threshold on last quarter's leads before switching it on.
- **Account scores.** In ABM, score the company as well as the person: several engaged people at one target account matter more than one very active student.
- **Stages.** HubSpot's default lifecycle stages run Subscriber, Lead, Marketing Qualified Lead, Sales Qualified Lead, Opportunity, Customer, Evangelist; automatic updates only move forward, so recycling a lead needs a deliberate reset (page dated 17 July 2026) [17].
- **The handoff agreement.** Write down what makes a lead or account ready for sales, who receives it, how fast they respond, what they record, and how a rejected lead returns to marketing with a reason. Both leaders sign it. Review acceptance rates monthly.

### 3.6 Attribution and its limits

Every tool hands out credit by its own rules, and B2B makes it harder: long cycles, many people, offline meetings.

- **LinkedIn.** For most conversion types LinkedIn uses last-touch attribution, crediting either every ad set touched in the window or only the last one [9]. Click and view windows can be 1, 7, 30 or 90 days, default 90-day click and 90-day view; some lead and purchase types sent by Conversions API or CSV can use 180 or 365 days [10]. A 90-day view window credits people who only saw an ad.
- **Offline and CRM data.** LinkedIn's Conversions API sends server-side events, including phone sales and in-person event leads; emails are hashed with SHA-256, and data is deleted after 180 days except aggregate reporting [11]. The Revenue Attribution Report links CRM opportunities from Salesforce, Dynamics 365 or HubSpot to LinkedIn activity, by impressions or engagements, at member or company level [12].
- **GA4.** Three models remain: "data-driven attribution", paid and organic last click, and Google paid channels last click; first click, linear, time decay and position-based were removed in November 2023, and direct visits get no credit unless the whole path is direct [19].
- **Sourced and influenced.** "Sourced" pipeline began with a marketing touch; "influenced" pipeline had any marketing touch along the way. Influenced totals always look bigger and overlap across channels. Report both, labelled, and never add channels' influenced figures together.
- **Ask the buyer.** A "How did you hear about us?" field on demo requests, and the first-call notes, catch podcasts, referrals and AI assistants that tracking misses.
- **Tests.** Where a channel's value is in doubt and the money matters, use a holdout or lift test (see the Marketing analyst kit).

### 3.7 Consent for B2B outreach, by region (general guidance, not legal advice)

| Region | What the official guidance says (as of November 2026) |
|---|---|
| United States | CAN-SPAM makes no exception for business-to-business email. Every commercial email needs accurate headers, an honest subject line, an ad disclosure, a valid postal address and a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days; the opt-out must work for at least 30 days after sending; penalties reach $53,088 per violating email; the company promoted and the sender can both be liable [21]. California's CCPA has covered B2B contact data since its B2B exemption expired on 31 December 2022, for businesses over its thresholds [22]. |
| United Kingdom | GOV.UK says marketing emails and texts to individual customers need their permission, every message must offer an easy opt-out, and phone numbers must be screened against the Telephone Preference Service [23]. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 counts a marketing call or message even if it never reaches the recipient, and its changes start in stages [24]. How PECR treats companies, sole traders and partnerships is set out in the ICO's business-to-business marketing guidance; that site blocked our fetcher, so read it before any UK cold email. |
| European Union | A bought or shared list may be used only if the seller can show it was collected lawfully and may be used for advertising; people must be told at first contact, objectors excluded, and email marketing follows Article 13 of the ePrivacy Directive [25]. Legitimate interests can be a legal basis for direct marketing, but the business must weigh the person's rights first and tell them [26]. Business email rules differ by country: France's CNIL (10 June 2026) allows B2B prospecting email without prior consent when it relates to the recipient's job, as long as the person was told and can object; generic addresses such as contact@ fall outside those rules [27]. Check the regulator in each target country. |
| Canada | CASL needs express or implied consent for commercial electronic messages. Consent is implied when a person published or gave you their address without saying they don't want messages and your message relates to their role, or during an existing business relationship (two years after a purchase or contract, six months after an inquiry); unsubscribes must be honoured within 10 business days [28]. Maximum penalties are $1 million for an individual and $10 million for any other person [29]. Messages to staff of another organisation that has a relationship with yours, about that organisation's activities, are exempt [30]. |

Mailbox providers' sender rules apply on top of the law; see the Email marketing specialist kit.

### 3.8 Tools, access and AI

Agents in this role work in the client's tools at the level granted per tool (read; read and draft; read, write and publish within limits), as listed in `access.md`. HubSpot publishes a remote MCP server that uses OAuth and can read and write CRM records such as contacts, companies and deals, while campaigns are read-only [18]. AI drafts messages, scores lists and writes reports from the client's data; it never invents an account, a contact or a figure. As buyers start sending their own AI agents to research suppliers [3], accurate public pages on pricing, security and integrations become part of demand generation.

## 5. Decision rules

- If sales and marketing have different target lists, stop and agree one list before planning any play, because plays aimed at the wrong accounts waste both teams' time.
- If the client cannot name what its best customers share, build the profile from won and lost deals first, because a profile from opinion picks the wrong accounts.
- If tier 1 is larger than the team can give personal attention, move accounts down a tier, because one-to-one work spread thin becomes one-to-many work at one-to-one cost.
- If a LinkedIn audience is near the 300-member floor, widen by company list, role or region rather than stacking narrow filters, because the ad set will not run below it [4].
- If a campaign targets EU or Swiss members, expect smaller Matched Audience reach and plan budget on the forecast, not on the US version [4].
- If leads come from Lead Gen Forms, sync or download them well before LinkedIn's retention window ends [8].
- If a scoring rule has not been tested against last quarter's won and lost leads, keep it switched off [16].
- If sales rejects a large share of handed-over leads, change the definition with sales before generating more leads.
- If a report adds up influenced pipeline across channels, rewrite it to show sourced and influenced separately, because influenced figures overlap.
- If a channel's credit rests on view-through conversions, show the click-only figure beside it [10].
- If a contact list was bought, rented or shared, do not email it until the seller's lawful collection and the region's rules are checked [25][27][28].
- If a recipient is in Canada and you rely on implied consent, record why it applies (published address and role relevance, or the relationship and its date) [28].
- If a webinar has no next step for attendees, decide the next step before writing the invitation.

## 6. Common mistakes and how to spot them

- **Two target lists.** Spot it: marketing reports engagement from accounts sales has never heard of.
- **A tier 1 of hundreds.** Spot it: "personalised" content that is a logo swap.
- **One message for the whole company.** Spot it: the same email to the user, the finance lead and IT security.
- **Scoring by activity alone.** Spot it: students, job seekers and competitors top the score list.
- **Leads with no owner.** Spot it: Lead Gen Form exports sitting unsynced for weeks [8].
- **Counting registrants as attendees.** Spot it: a webinar report with one number for "people reached" [20].
- **Influenced pipeline summed across channels.** Spot it: channel totals beat the pipeline in the CRM.
- **Long view windows read as proof.** Spot it: conversions credited to people who never clicked [10].
- **Cold email from a bought list into the EU or Canada.** Spot it: no record of where each address came from [25][28].
- **Agents working in a personal seat.** Spot it: a shared Sales Navigator login.

## 7. Quality bar

Before anything ships, I check:
1. Is the target list built from won and lost deals, and has sales agreed it in writing?
2. Does every tier have plays with an owner, a channel, a message and a week?
3. Is each buying-group message specific to one role: pain, proof, one ask?
4. Is every contact's source recorded, and does each region's consent rule allow the planned outreach?
5. Has the scoring rule been tested on past leads, and is the handoff agreement signed by both leaders?
6. Are attendees, no-shows and engaged target accounts followed up separately?
7. Is every number labelled counted, model-credited or tested, with its window and source?
8. Are platform facts dated and cited?
9. Is the access level per tool written down, with level 2 and level 3 steps marked?
10. Would the sales leader know what to do on Monday after one read?

## 8. How this role writes

- **Target account list:** a table of account, tier, fit score, signals with dates, reason for the tier, owner in sales, owner in marketing.
- **Tier play plan:** a 90-day grid by week: channel, content, message theme, sales touch, owner, and the measure that says the play worked.
- **Buying-group message grid:** one row per role: what they care about, the pain, the proof they trust, the ask, the channel.
- **Scoring sheet:** each rule, its points, why (with the won-deal evidence), decay, threshold, test result on last quarter.
- **Handoff agreement:** one page: definition, routing, response time, what sales records, the way back.
- **Webinar plan:** next step, follow-up emails, outline, run sheet, promotion calendar, report template.
- **Pipeline report:** accounts engaged by tier, meetings, sourced and influenced pipeline shown apart, won revenue, what to change.

**Tone.** Plain, specific, agreed. Name accounts, roles, owners and dates. Ask for agreement on the decisions that need it. No hype about "leads" with no owner.

## 9. Glossary

- **Demand generation:** the work of creating and capturing interest that becomes sales pipeline.
- **Account-based marketing (ABM):** marketing aimed at a named list of companies, planned with sales.
- **Ideal customer profile (ICP):** the traits shared by the customers who buy, stay and pay well.
- **Target account list:** the agreed list of companies the programme pursues.
- **Tier:** a group of accounts that gets the same depth of attention (one-to-one, one-to-few, one-to-many).
- **Buying group:** the people at one company who together decide a purchase [5].
- **Fit score:** points for how closely a person or company matches the profile [16].
- **Engagement score:** points for actions such as visits, form fills and event attendance [16].
- **Score decay:** engagement points that shrink over time [16].
- **MQL:** a marketing qualified lead, judged ready for sales by marketing [17].
- **SQL:** a sales qualified lead, accepted by sales as a potential customer [17].
- **Handoff agreement (SLA):** the written rule for when and how marketing passes leads to sales.
- **Matched Audiences:** LinkedIn's list-based and retargeting audiences [4].
- **Lead Gen Form:** a LinkedIn form pre-filled from the member's profile [7].
- **Thought Leader Ad:** a promoted post from an employee's own profile [14].
- **Conversion window:** how long after a click or view LinkedIn credits a conversion [10].
- **Last-touch attribution:** all credit to the most recent ad interaction [9].
- **Sourced pipeline:** opportunities that began with a marketing touch.
- **Influenced pipeline:** opportunities with any marketing touch along the way.
- **Show-up rate:** live attendees divided by registrants [20].
- **Implied consent (CASL):** consent Canada's law infers in set situations, such as a published business address relevant to the message [28].
- **Legitimate interests:** a GDPR legal basis that can cover direct marketing after weighing the person's rights [26].
