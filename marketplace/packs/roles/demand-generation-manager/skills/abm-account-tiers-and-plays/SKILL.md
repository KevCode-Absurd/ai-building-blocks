# ABM account tiers and plays

Account-based marketing works when both teams chase the same short list for the same reasons. This skill builds that list from evidence, then plans what each tier sees and who does each step.

## When to use

- A B2B firm sells large, slow deals and wants marketing and sales on one list.
- An existing target list was built from opinion, a bought database or "everyone in the industry".
- The quarter is starting and the team needs a plan with owners, not a strategy deck.
- Sales complains that marketing's leads are the wrong companies.

## What you need

1. **Won and lost deals** for at least the last year: company, industry, size, location, deal value, cycle length, who signed, the trigger that started the purchase, why won or lost.
2. **Candidate accounts**: the list sales wants, the list marketing has, or both.
3. **Signals the client can see**: website visits by company, event attendance, renewal dates, hiring for relevant roles, partner referrals, intent data the client already pays for. Nothing the client does not have.
4. **Team capacity**: who in marketing and sales can work on accounts, and roughly how many hours a week.
5. **Channels available**: LinkedIn, email, webinars, events, direct mail, partners, sales calls.

**Access:** level 1 to read the CRM, ad accounts and exports. Level 2 to save the tiers as draft CRM lists or properties and draft campaigns. Level 3 to write tier values to the agreed accounts, build approved audiences and launch approved campaigns within the cap. Importing a new contact list, creating a new ad audience or spending above the cap needs a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Draw the profile from wins
Compare won deals with lost and stalled ones. For each trait (industry, size band, location, tools used, trigger event, deal size), note how often it appears among wins versus losses. Keep the traits that separate them. Write the profile in one paragraph and a short table of must-have and nice-to-have traits. If there are too few deals to compare, say so and use the client's best customers by revenue and retention instead, labelled as a weaker basis.

### 2. Score fit
Give each candidate account a fit score from the must-have and nice-to-have traits. Keep the scale simple (for example, 0 to 3 per trait) and write the rule down so sales can check any score. Accounts that miss a must-have trait drop out, with the reason noted.

### 3. Score signals
Add a separate signal score from what the client can see, each signal dated. Old signals count for less. Never infer a signal you were not given.

### 4. Sort into tiers
- **Tier 1, one-to-one:** high fit and live signals, limited to what named people can give real attention each week.
- **Tier 2, one-to-few:** good fit, grouped into clusters that share a problem or industry, so one piece of content serves the cluster.
- **Tier 3, one-to-many:** fits the profile, reached with targeted ads, webinars and newsletters.
Write the reason for each tier 1 placement in one line. Size tier 1 from the capacity figures, not from ambition.

### 5. Agree with sales
Send the list with reasons to the sales lead. Mark disputed accounts and settle them in one meeting. Record the agreed list with the date and both names.

### 6. Plan plays per tier for 90 days
For each tier, a week-by-week grid:
- **Channel and content:** LinkedIn company-list campaigns, event audiences, Thought Leader Ads, webinars, email to opted-in contacts, direct mail, events, partner introductions.
- **Message theme:** linked to the buying-group messages (see the `buying-group-messages` skill).
- **Sales touch:** what sales does and when, after which signal.
- **Owner:** a named person for each step.
- **Rules check:** for each country, whether the planned email or message is allowed (see knowledge section 3.7). Contacts with no recorded source get no email.

LinkedIn ad sets need at least 300 matched members, so tier 1 accounts usually share a campaign with tier 2 or are reached through sales and content rather than their own ad set.

### 7. Set the measures
Per tier: accounts engaged (several people active, not one), meetings held, opportunities created, pipeline value, and won revenue at the end of the normal cycle. Add one early sign per tier that the play is landing. Set a monthly review date.

## Checks

- The profile is drawn from wins versus losses, with the evidence shown.
- Every score has a written rule; every signal has a date and a source.
- Tier 1 fits the team's real hours.
- Sales has agreed the list, with a date.
- Every play has an owner, a week and a measure.
- No step relies on bought, scraped or guessed contact details.
- Each country's outreach rule is noted against email and message steps.
- Level 2 and level 3 steps are marked.

## Output format

1. Profile: one paragraph and a trait table with win and loss evidence
2. Scored account list: account, fit, signals with dates, tier, reason, sales owner
3. Tier summary: counts, capacity check, disputed accounts and how they were settled
4. 90-day play grid per tier
5. Measures and review dates
6. Access needed per step and open questions

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business: Northgate Freight Systems, freight software for mid-size manufacturers. Figures are invented for illustration.*

> **Profile.** Our wins are manufacturers with 200 to 1,000 staff, shipping to three or more countries, running a mid-market ERP. The trigger in 9 of 14 wins was a new warehouse or a lost carrier contract. Losses were mostly firms under 100 staff or with in-house logistics IT.
>
> | Account | Fit (0-12) | Signals | Tier | Reason | Sales owner |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Halden Pumps | 11 | New warehouse announced 2 Oct; 4 visits to pricing page in Oct | 1 | Best fit plus live trigger | Priya |
> | Corran Plastics | 10 | Attended our September webinar (2 people) | 2 | Export-heavy plastics cluster | Tom |
> | Elstow Foods | 8 | None | 3 | Fits, no signal yet | Tom |
>
> **Tier 1, weeks 1 to 4:** Priya calls Halden's logistics director about the new site (week 1). Marketing sends a one-page cost model for a second warehouse to the finance lead, who opted in at our webinar (week 2). Halden's people see the "new site" case in the tier 1 and 2 LinkedIn campaign (weeks 1 to 8).
>
> **Measure:** three or more Halden people engaged by week 6, and a discovery meeting booked. Review on the first Monday of each month with Priya.
