# Buying-group messages

A large B2B deal is several people saying yes, and any one of them can say no. This skill gives each of them a reason to say yes that fits their job.

## When to use

- The same email or ad goes to the user, the budget owner and IT security.
- Deals stall late because a role nobody addressed (finance, procurement, legal, IT) raised objections.
- A new segment or account type is starting, and the team needs messages before campaigns.
- A webinar or event needs invitations aimed at more than one role.

## What you need

1. **The account type**: industry, size and the problem the product solves for them.
2. **Evidence of how they buy**: won and lost deal notes, sales call notes or recordings, win-loss interviews, RFP questions, objections logged in the CRM.
3. **Proof available**: case studies, figures the client can stand behind, security documents, references, certifications.
4. **The offer**: the next step each role can take (a demo, a cost model, a security pack, a pilot).

**Access:** level 1 to read call notes, deal records and existing content. Level 2 to save messages as draft emails, ads or CRM snippets. Level 3 to publish approved ads or send approved emails to opted-in contacts. Sending to a new list or segment needs a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. List the roles
For this account type, list who:
- **Uses** it day to day.
- **Champions** it inside the company and builds the case.
- **Owns the budget** and signs.
- **Can block** it: IT or security, legal, procurement, a works council, a clinical or safety lead.
- **Influences** it from outside: consultants, auditors, partners.
Use the client's deal evidence to name real job titles. If a role is guessed, mark it [TO CONFIRM].

### 2. Find each role's pain in their words
From call notes and interviews, pull the phrases each role uses about the problem. Never invent a quote. Where evidence is thin, write the pain as a question to test in the next sales calls.

### 3. Match proof to each role
Each role trusts different proof. Users trust demos and peers in the same job; budget owners trust cost and payback with stated assumptions; IT trusts documentation, standards and integration details; procurement trusts clear terms and references. Use only proof the client can show.

### 4. Write one ask per role
One step that suits the role's place in the decision: a short demo for users, a cost model for finance, a security pack and a call with an engineer for IT, a reference call for the champion.

### 5. Write the messages
For each role: a subject line or hook, three to five sentences (pain, what changes, proof, ask), and a one-line version for ads or LinkedIn. Keep claims checkable. No urgency the client cannot honour.

### 6. Sequence the group
Decide order and owner: usually the champion and users first, finance and IT once interest is real, procurement when a proposal is near. Note which messages marketing carries (ads, content, webinars) and which sales carries (calls, emails from the account owner).

### 7. Check the rules
For each country in scope, confirm the email and message steps are allowed for the contact's source (see knowledge section 3.7). Ads to a company list do not need the same consent as emails to a person, but the list must have been gathered lawfully.

## Checks

- Every role is tied to evidence or marked [TO CONFIRM].
- No invented quotes, figures or customer names.
- Each message has one pain, one proof, one ask.
- Proof matches what that role trusts.
- The sequence says who sends what and when.
- Outreach rules are noted per country.
- Level 2 and level 3 steps are marked.

## Output format

1. Buying-group chart: roles, job titles, who influences whom
2. Message grid: role, pain (with evidence source), proof, ask, channel, one-line version
3. Full message drafts per role
4. Sequence by week with owners
5. Evidence gaps to test in the next sales calls

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business: Brightward Safety, a safety training platform for hospitals. Figures are invented for illustration.*

> | Role | Pain (evidence) | Proof | Ask | Channel |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Head of nursing (champion) | "Mandatory training eats ward time" (4 of 6 discovery calls) | A ward manager at a similar trust on a 20-minute video call | Reference call | Account owner email |
> | HR lead (user and admin) | Chasing completion records before inspections (RFP question 12) | Two-minute demo of the completion report | Short demo | LinkedIn ad and webinar |
> | Finance director (budget) | Agency cover costs while staff train [TO CONFIRM] | Cost model with the trust's own staff numbers and stated assumptions | 30-minute cost review | Account owner email after the demo |
> | IT security (can block) | Single sign-on and data location questions (lost deal, March) | Security pack and data processing terms | Call with our engineer | Sent by sales when a proposal is requested |
>
> **Head of nursing, email:** "Most of your mandatory training happens on shift, and it shows on the rota. Wards using Brightward run the same modules in short blocks that fit between handovers. Sister Ellis at a 600-bed trust can tell you how her ward did it. Would a 20-minute call with her next week help?"
