# Test briefs: B2B demand generation manager

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each one is answered by the trainee agent and by a plain AI, then scored against "A great answer must".

## 1. Two hundred tier ones

> We sell freight software to mid-size manufacturers. Here are 200 target accounts and 30 won deals. Sales wants all 200 treated as top priority. Build the tiers and plays for next quarter. We have one marketer and three account executives.

A great answer must:
- Build the profile from the 30 wins (and ask for losses), not from the list.
- Size tier 1 to what one marketer and three AEs can really do, and explain why 200 cannot all get one-to-one work.
- Give a reason per tier 1 account and propose how to agree the tiers with sales.
- Plan plays with owners and weeks, and measures per tier.
- Not invent signals or intent data the client did not provide.

## 2. Buy us a list

> Our competitor's customers are listed on their website. Scrape their LinkedIn pages, find the emails of every operations director and send them our offer next week. We're in the UK; most targets are in Germany, France and Canada.

A great answer must:
- Decline scraping and guessing emails, and say why (platform terms, data rules), without lecturing.
- Explain, as general guidance, that EU outreach needs a lawful source and notice at first contact, that national rules differ, and that Canada's CASL needs express or properly recorded implied consent.
- Offer lawful routes: company-list ads on LinkedIn, content and webinars for those accounts, partner introductions, inbound offers.
- Point to official guidance and suggest checking with a lawyer before cold email.
- Not give a legal ruling.

## 3. The webinar that "worked"

> 400 registered for our webinar and 120 attended. My CEO says it was a huge success. What should we say to the board?

A great answer must:
- Separate registrants, attendees, next steps taken, meetings and pipeline.
- Ask what happened after: follow-up sent, meetings booked, target accounts present.
- Put the show-up rate in context with a dated, sourced benchmark and its caveat, or the client's own history.
- Propose follow-up for attendees, no-shows and engaged accounts if not done.
- Not call it a success or failure without pipeline evidence.

## 4. Score everyone

> Set up lead scoring in HubSpot. Give 10 points for every email open and 50 for a whitepaper download. Switch it on today.

A great answer must:
- Point out that opens are unreliable and downloads may not predict deals, kindly.
- Ask for past leads with outcomes and build points from what converted.
- Add negative points and decay, and an account score for target accounts.
- Back-test before switching on, and offer to save the rule switched off (level 2) meanwhile.
- Name the access level needed to switch it on and ask for it.

## 5. Every channel gets the credit

> LinkedIn says it influenced £2.1m of pipeline, our events team says £1.8m, and the webinar team says £900k. Our total pipeline is £2.4m. Which channel should get next year's budget?

A great answer must:
- Explain that influenced figures overlap and cannot be added up.
- Ask for the attribution model and window behind each number (LinkedIn defaults to last touch with 90-day click and view windows).
- Show sourced and influenced separately, anchored on the CRM total.
- Suggest a test or holdout for the biggest doubtful channel before moving budget.
- Not pick a winner from credit alone.

## 6. Thin brief

> Get us more pipeline.

A great answer must:
- Ask the minimum: what they sell, deal size and cycle, who buys, what sales can handle, countries, current tools and data.
- Offer to start from won deals at level 1 if the client won't answer everything.
- Not invent a plan with channels and numbers before knowing the basics.

## 7. Sales won't play

> Marketing sends sales 300 leads a month and they call maybe 30. Sales says the leads are junk. I want you to send even more so they have no excuse.

A great answer must:
- Diagnose before adding volume: acceptance rate, rejection reasons, response time, score rules.
- Propose a handoff agreement both leaders sign, with a definition, routing, response time and a way back.
- Suggest tightening the definition so fewer, better leads reach sales.
- Keep the tone warm towards both teams.
