# Interview: ecommerce analyst

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you show me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work.

## How you work

1. **Walk me through your Monday report: which exports you pull, in what order, and what you check before you look at any trend.**
   Why it matters: shows the real routine and where the expert puts the data check.
2. **How do you define conversion rate, average order value and a returning customer, and why those definitions?**
   Why it matters: definitions decide whether weeks can be compared.
3. **What gap between your store and analytics do you treat as normal, and what makes you stop and investigate?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's own tolerance and warning signs, which the kit leaves as words.
4. **Tell me about a "sales crash" that turned out to be a tracking problem. How did you find it?**
   Why it matters: real stories hold the order of checks that works.
5. **How do you split a revenue change into traffic, conversion and order value, and what do you do next for each?**
   Why it matters: the drill-down path is the core of the job.

## Customers

6. **How do you score customers on recency, frequency and spend? Fifths, thirds, fixed cut-offs?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert fits scoring to store size.
7. **What do you call your segments, and how do you explain them to an owner?**
   Why it matters: plain names decide whether the segments get used.
8. **How do you build a cohort table, and how do you stop young cohorts from looking bad?**
   Why it matters: tests their handling of incomplete windows.
9. **Which customers do you put on a win-back list, and how do you decide someone is slipping?**
   Why it matters: the rule behind the list is the reusable part.
10. **How do you handle consent and personal data in customer files?**
    Why it matters: checks the expert's privacy line before any list leaves the store.

## Margin, returns and attribution

11. **What goes into your margin per order, and which costs do clients usually forget?**
    Why it matters: contribution layers are where analysts add the most value.
12. **How do you measure return rates, and how do you deal with refunds that land weeks after the order?**
    Why it matters: tests whether returns are tied back to the orders that caused them.
13. **When the ad platforms, GA4 and the store all disagree, which number do you report and how do you explain it?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert handles attribution without overclaiming.
14. **How has consent (cookie banners, consent mode, browsers) changed what you can see, and how do you report around it?**
    Why it matters: consent gaps change every report in the EEA and UK.

## Tools, people and results

15. **Which tools do you work in every week, and what access do you need in each?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent what to ask for and at which level.
16. **What do you change in a client's tools yourself, and what do you always hand to someone else?**
    Why it matters: maps the expert's habits onto access levels 1 to 3.
17. **Show me a report that changed a decision. What was in it?**
    Why it matters: captures the format that gets acted on.
18. **What do owners most often ask you for that you advise against?**
    Why it matters: prepares the agent for common pushback.
19. **Tell me about an analysis you got wrong. What did you learn?**
    Why it matters: mistakes carry the lessons a checklist misses.
20. **What do you check before any number leaves your hands?**
    Why it matters: defines the expert's personal quality bar.
