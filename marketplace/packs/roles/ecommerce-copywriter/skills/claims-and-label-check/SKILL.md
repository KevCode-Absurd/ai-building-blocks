# Claims and label check

Most product copy trouble comes from a few words: "Made in USA", "eco-friendly", "nut-free", "was £40", "today only", "loved by thousands". This skill finds every such word, matches it to evidence, and fixes it before it ships. It flags; it never rules on the law. For anything in a regulated area, the client checks with a qualified adviser or the regulator's guidance.

## When to use

- Before new product or collection copy goes live.
- The client asks for an origin, green, health or free-from claim.
- Product pages show reviews, star counts, "was" prices, percentage discounts or deadlines.
- Copy is going to a new market (for example, a US shop starting to sell into the EU).

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 is enough for the whole check. At level 3, a changed claim still needs the client's fresh yes before it is published.

- The copy: titles, bullets, descriptions, badges, image text, banners and small print.
- The label or packaging for each product, and the evidence behind each claim (certificates, test reports, supplier declarations, parts list with countries).
- Where reviews come from, and whether any reviewer got a free product, a discount or is staff or family.
- For any reduction: the price history for the last 30 days at least. For any deadline: what happens to the price after it.
- The markets the copy will be shown in.

## Steps

1. **List every claim.** Go line by line, including image text and badges. A claim is anything a shopper could check: origin, material, performance, environment, health, safety, free-from, award, review count or rating, price, saving, deadline, stock level.
2. **Match each claim to evidence.** Mark: proven (evidence in hand), partly proven (say what's missing), or unproven. Label facts (ingredients, allergens, fibre content, origin on the label) must match the label word for word.
3. **Run the market checks.**
   - *Origin, US:* unqualified "Made in USA" needs all or virtually all US content, with final assembly and all significant processing in the US. Soft phrases ("crafted in America", flags, "American made") are origin claims too. Offer a qualified claim ("Made in USA of U.S. and imported parts") only if US content is significant, or "Assembled in USA" if substantial final assembly is in the US. For clothing, the online description must say made in USA, imported, or both, matching the label.
   - *Environmental, EU (from 27 September 2026):* flag every generic claim ("eco-friendly", "green", "natural" used as a green claim, "climate friendly", "biodegradable", "sustainable"). Keep it only if the product holds a qualifying ecolabel; otherwise replace it with the specific fact, stated clearly on the same page ("Box made from 100% recycled card"). Flag any sustainability badge that isn't from a certification scheme or public authority, and any "climate neutral" claim based on offsetting. Watch product names too.
   - *Environmental, UK and US:* test each claim against the CMA's six Green Claims Code principles and the FTC's Green Guides: specific, true, nothing important left out, life cycle considered, evidence held.
   - *Reviews, all markets:* reviews must be real; incentives can't depend on a positive review; free products, staff and family links must be disclosed; no hiding or delaying only negative reviews; counts and averages must be true ("4.6 from 212 reviews", not "loved by thousands"). Five-star-first default sorting can mislead.
   - *Price and urgency:* EU reductions are measured from the lowest price of at least the previous 30 days, including percentage badges and "great price" wording. UK: the first price shown includes mandatory delivery and fees; a deadline must be real, with the price going back up when it ends. US: price comparisons must be truthful; a "was" price should be a real former price.
   - *Food:* allergens declared and emphasised as on the label (14 in the UK and EU, 9 major allergens in the US). For UK distance selling, allergen information must be there before the purchase and at delivery. "Free from" needs strict controls; if the maker can't rule out cross-contact, don't claim it.
   - *Health and safety:* any health benefit, "non-toxic", "safe for babies" or medical wording needs strong evidence. Hold it and refer the client to a qualified adviser.
4. **Decide the status of each claim:** keep, narrow (rewrite to what the evidence shows), cut, or hold (keep off the page until evidence arrives).
5. **Write the fixed version** of each flagged line, as close to the client's intent as the evidence allows.
6. **Point to the guidance** for the markets in play, without ruling on the law: FTC Made in USA guidance, Endorsement Guides and reviews rule; the Commission's Q&A on Directive 2024/825; the CMA's Green Claims Code, fake reviews guidance and price transparency guidance; FSA, EU and FDA allergen guidance.
7. **Write the claims log** and hand it over with the copy.

## Checks

- Every claim found, including image text, badges and product names.
- Each status has a one-line reason tied to evidence.
- No new claim added in a rewrite.
- Label facts unchanged.
- The note says plainly that this is general guidance, and names who should check regulated claims.

## Output format

1. **Summary:** number of claims checked, kept, narrowed, cut, held.
2. **Claims table:** claim (quoted), where it appears, market, evidence, status, fixed version.
3. **Guidance to read** for each market.
4. **Questions for the client** (missing evidence).
5. **Claims log** (claim, evidence, approver, date) for the client's records.

## Example of good output (short)

*Pine Hollow Home is a made-up business used only to show the format. It sells cotton throws to the US and the EU.*

**Summary:** 6 claims checked: 2 kept, 2 narrowed, 1 cut, 1 held.

| Claim | Market | Evidence | Status | Fixed version |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Made in USA" | US | Woven in North Carolina; cotton yarn imported from India | Narrow | "Made in U.S.A. and Imported", the FTC textile guide's wording for US-made goods from imported materials, matching the sewn-in label (client to confirm with their adviser) |
| "Eco-friendly throw" | EU | No ecolabel | Cut | "Made from 100% organic cotton (certificate no. [confirm])" if the certificate is supplied |
| "GOTS certified" badge | EU, US | Certificate supplied, valid until 2027 | Keep | Keep, with the certificate number on file |
| "Loved by thousands" | All | 312 reviews, average 4.7 | Narrow | "Rated 4.7 from 312 reviews" |
| "Today only: 30% off" | EU | Price was €49 for 20 days, €39 for the 10 days before | Held | The 30-day low is €39; a cut to €34.30 is about 12% off that, not 30%. Hold until the client decides the offer |
| "Machine washable at 30°C" | All | Care label | Keep | Keep |

**Guidance to read:** FTC, Complying with the Made in USA Standard; FTC Threading Your Way (textile origin online); European Commission Q&A on Directive 2024/825. This is general guidance, not a legal ruling.
