# Collection page copy

A collection page is where shoppers who know roughly what they want decide which one to look at. Most shops leave it as a grid of products with a heading. This skill adds the few words that help people choose, without pushing the products below the fold.

## When to use

- "Our category pages have no text. Can you write some?"
- "We want the 'walking boots' page to rank."
- A new collection, a seasonal edit or a gift page.
- A buying guide that compares products within a range.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the collection, its products and search data. Level 2 to write into an unpublished collection description or a document. Level 3 to publish copy on the collections you name, within your limits.

- The collection's products, with the fact sheet fields that differ between them (size, material, use, price band).
- The store's filter names and values for this collection (from the ecommerce manager, or a screenshot).
- Search terms for the collection: a Search Console export, ads search terms, or site search terms.
- 10 or more pre-sale questions about products in this range.
- For a gift page: delivery cut-off dates by service, from the client.

## Steps

1. **Name the job of the page.** One search intent per collection ("waterproof walking boots for women", not "boots"). If two collections target the same intent, flag it for the ecommerce manager rather than writing two similar pages.
2. **Find the real differences.** From the fact sheets, list the two to four things that actually split the range (for example: waterproofing, ankle height, weight, width fittings). These become the buying guide.
3. **Match the filter words.** Use the store's filter names and values in the copy ("Wide fit", not "roomy"), so the text, the filters and the product titles agree.
4. **Write the intro.** Two to three sentences above the grid: what the range is, who it suits, and the one thing that makes this shop's range worth browsing. Keep it short so products stay in view on a phone.
5. **Write the buying guide.** Below the grid or in a collapsible block: one short section per difference, each saying who should choose which option and why, linking to the filter or sub-collection. Use facts, not adjectives.
6. **Write the FAQ.** Three to six real questions with short answers. Only answer from the fact sheets or the client's policies; mark anything else [confirm].
7. **Add internal links.** Link to sub-collections, related collections and two or three best sellers, using plain text links with descriptive words.
8. **Write the SEO title and meta description.** SEO title: the main search phrase first, about 60 characters, brand at the end. Meta description: about 160 characters, saying what the shopper will find and one reason to choose this shop. No keyword lists.
9. **For gift and seasonal pages,** add the delivery cut-off dates the client gave, by delivery service, and the date the page was last checked. If a deadline appears, it must be real. Don't add countdowns unless the client confirms what happens when they end.
10. **Check prices and offers on the page.** If the collection is a sale, reductions shown to EU shoppers are measured from the lowest price of the previous 30 days, and UK prices must include mandatory delivery. Send any price or deadline wording through the claims-and-label-check skill.

## Checks

- One clear search intent, not shared with another collection.
- The buying guide explains differences a shopper can act on, and each one links to a filter or sub-collection.
- The copy uses the filter names exactly.
- The intro is short enough that products show on the first phone screen.
- Every FAQ answer comes from the fact sheets or client policy.
- No invented superlatives ("the best boots in Britain").
- Delivery cut-offs are dated and came from the client.

## Output format

1. **Page job:** the search intent and the shopper it serves.
2. **Intro** (with word count).
3. **Buying guide** (sections with links).
4. **FAQ.**
5. **Internal links** list.
6. **SEO title and meta description** with character counts.
7. **Notes for the ecommerce manager** (filter gaps, overlapping collections) and **open questions**.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: an outdoor shop. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Page job:** shoppers searching for "women's waterproof walking boots" who haven't chosen a style.

**Intro (37 words):** Women's walking boots with a waterproof lining, from light day-hike boots to stiff-soled boots for rocky ground. Every pair here comes in standard and wide fit, and you can filter by ankle height, weight and sole stiffness.

**Buying guide (extract)**

- *Ankle height.* Mid boots (filter: Mid) suit paths and gentle hills. High boots (filter: High) give more support with a heavy pack or on loose ground.
- *Weight.* Under 500 g a boot (filter: Lightweight) feels closer to a trainer. Heavier boots usually have stiffer soles for rock.

**FAQ (extract)**

- *Are they waterproof or water-resistant?* Every boot in this collection has a waterproof membrane, listed on each product page.
- *Do you do half sizes?* [confirm]

**SEO title (35 characters plus the brand name):** Women's Waterproof Walking Boots | [Business]

**Notes for the ecommerce manager:** the "Wide fit" filter is missing from 6 of 24 products; two collections ("Hiking boots" and "Walking boots") target the same search.
