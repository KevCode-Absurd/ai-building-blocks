# Interview: ecommerce copywriter

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you show me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work.

## How you work

1. **Walk me through your last batch of product descriptions, from the first file the client sent to the copy going live.**
   Why it matters: shows the real method, not the textbook one.
2. **What does your fact sheet look like? Show me the columns.**
   Why it matters: the fact sheet is what stops invented specs; its format is a reusable tool.
3. **What do you do when the client sends only supplier text and a price list?**
   Why it matters: thin briefs are the normal case; the answer shows how the expert gets facts without guessing.
4. **How do you find the questions shoppers ask before buying a product?**
   Why it matters: answering real questions is what separates useful copy from filler.
5. **How do you make 40 similar products read differently without inventing differences?**
   Why it matters: large ranges of near-identical products are where most catalogues fail.
6. **How do you write titles differently for the store, a search result, a shopping feed and a marketplace?**
   Why it matters: each surface has its own rules and length, and experts adapt per surface.

## Claims and labels

7. **Tell me about a claim you refused to write, or narrowed. What was it, and what did you write instead?**
   Why it matters: shows the expert's line on proof and how they hold it with clients.
8. **How do you handle ingredients, allergens and fibre content?**
   Why it matters: label facts must never be paraphrased; the answer shows their discipline.
9. **A client wants "eco-friendly" and "sustainable" on every product page, and sells to Europe. What do you do?**
   Why it matters: tests knowledge of the EU rules on generic green claims from 27 September 2026.
10. **What evidence do you ask for before writing "Made in USA" or any origin claim?**
    Why it matters: origin claims carry real penalties, and soft wording still counts.
11. **How do you use reviews in product copy, and what do you never do with them?**
    Why it matters: checks their practice on permission, disclosure and honest counts.

## Craft and tools

12. **Which tools do you work in day to day: store admin, spreadsheets, a product information tool, AI?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent which tools to ask access for and which formats to deliver.
13. **How do you use AI for product copy, and how do you check what it writes?**
    Why it matters: AI drafting is now normal; the checking step is where quality is won or lost.
14. **What does your style guide for a client's catalogue contain?**
    Why it matters: naming and unit rules keep catalogues clean after the job ends.
15. **How do you write a collection page so it helps shoppers and search, without burying the products?**
    Why it matters: collection pages are often neglected and easy to overload.
16. **For food products, how do you make people want to taste something without making claims you can't back?**
    Why it matters: sensory writing within label limits is a specialist skill.

## Results and clients

17. **How do you know your copy worked? Which numbers do you look at, and when?**
    Why it matters: shows whether the expert measures (conversion, returns, questions, feed issues) or guesses.
18. **Tell me about the hardest catalogue job you've done. What went wrong, and what would you do differently?**
    Why it matters: hard stories hold the lessons that a checklist misses.
19. **What do clients most often get wrong about product copy?**
    Why it matters: captures the objections the agent will meet and how to answer them.
20. **What does a finished, shippable product page look like to you? What do you check last?**
    Why it matters: defines the expert's personal quality bar.
