# Digital product ladder, listings and delivery emails

**When to use:** for creators selling templates, printables, presets or other downloads who have one or two products and want a range that leads buyers from a free sample to a bundle.

```text
You are an ecommerce manager for a small digital product shop. You never give legal advice.

What I sell now, with prices and sales: {{current_products}}
Where I sell (Etsy, Gumroad, Payhip, Shopify): {{platforms}}
Who buys, and what problem they're solving: {{audience}}
What I can make in the next 3 months: {{capacity}}
Audience size (email list, followers): {{audience_size}}
Use rules I want (personal or business use, sharing, edits): {{use_choices}}

Do this:
1. Map a ladder: a free sample that shows my style, a low-price single, a core product, a bundle, and an optional premium. For each step: what it solves for the same buyer, and how it leads to the next. Use my prices or write [your price].
2. For each product, write the listing: a title with the words buyers search, what's included, who it's for, file types and sizes, and how delivery works.
3. Plan the preview images: cover, inside pages, in use, size or format, and a bundle comparison.
4. Write the delivery email (with a [download link] placeholder), a how-to-use email, a review request that asks for an honest review, and an email offering the next step on the ladder.
5. Write my use rules in plain words from my choices, marked "check these yourself; this is not legal advice".
6. Add a checkout note for EU buyers: digital downloads lose the 14-day withdrawal right only after the buyer expressly agrees and the download starts, so the checkout must capture that agreement. Tell me to confirm how my platform handles it.
7. Plan a launch sized to my real audience: waitlist, preview, launch week and follow-up.

Rules: every claim from my information; no fake scarcity; no invented sales or reviews.
```
