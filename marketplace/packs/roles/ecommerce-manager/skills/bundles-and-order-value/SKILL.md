# Bundles and order value

The easy way to raise average order value is a deeper discount, and it usually costs more margin than it brings. This skill looks for what customers already buy together, builds offers they'd thank you for, and checks the margin on every one.

## When to use

- "We want a higher average order value."
- "Should we do bundles?"
- "Where should we set free delivery?"
- Planning cart or post-purchase add-ons.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 for steps 1 to 8. Level 2 to set up bundles, thresholds or add-ons without publishing them. Level 3 to publish the approved offers at approved prices. New prices, new delivery charges and any change to how delivery is shown need a fresh yes.

- Order history for 6 to 12 months: order ID, date, products, quantities, order value, delivery charged.
- Product list with cost and price.
- Current delivery charges and costs, by service and zone.
- The bundles app or method available (on Shopify, a bundles app is needed).
- Markets: EU, UK, US.

## Steps

1. **Find what's bought together.** From the orders, count pairs of products in the same basket. For each pair: how often it appears, and its share of orders containing the first product. Ignore pairs with very few orders.
2. **Look at basket sizes.** List order values in bands and find where orders cluster. Note the share of orders just below each possible threshold.
3. **Work out margin.** For each pair and candidate bundle: combined price, combined cost, margin at full price, and margin at each discount being considered.
4. **Design offers with a clear gain for the buyer.** A set (everything needed to start), a refill pair, a starter kit, a gift set, a quantity break. Drop any bundle where the buyer gains little and the margin falls.
5. **Set the free delivery threshold.** Choose a level just above a common basket size that can be reached with one more relevant item; check that delivery cost at that level still leaves margin. Show the threshold in the cart with how much more is needed.
6. **Check delivery and price display.** In the UK, any mandatory delivery charge must be in the total price shown, at every stage. Free delivery above a threshold is fine; prices that hide mandatory delivery are not. Show the cheapest delivery option until the shopper chooses.
7. **Write the offer wording.** Bundle names, what's inside, the price, and the saving against the items' real current prices. No false "was" prices: for EU shoppers, a stated reduction uses the lowest price of the previous 30 days. No fake deadlines.
8. **Choose placements.** Product page (bundle or pair), cart (threshold progress, one add-on), after purchase (a relevant next item). Add-ons are opt-in; never pre-tick a paid extra.
9. **Set up for Google and Meta if needed.** Flag bundles with `is_bundle` in Google feeds and describe the whole bundle in the title. On Shopify, only fixed bundles carry to Google and YouTube.
10. **Plan the test.** One main number (gross margin per order, or average order value with a margin guardrail), a run length in whole weeks, and a stop rule. If traffic is too low for a clean test, say so and compare against the same weeks before, as a trend.
11. **Apply and log (level 2 or 3).** Publish approved offers; log each change; review weekly.

## Checks

- Every figure from the order history and cost data, with the method shown.
- Margin checked at the offer price for every bundle and threshold.
- Savings measured against genuine current prices; EU 30-day rule applied.
- UK prices include mandatory delivery; no pre-ticked add-ons.
- Would the customer thank you or feel tricked? If tricked, drop it.

## Output format

1. **Pairs table:** pair, orders together, share, combined margin.
2. **Basket bands** and the recommended threshold with the reasoning.
3. **Bundle sheet:** name, contents, price, saving, margin, placement, wording.
4. **Display checks** by market.
5. **Test plan:** main number, guardrail, run length, stop rule.

## Example of good output (short)

*Brewhouse Lane Coffee is a made-up roaster used only to show the format. Figures are invented for this example.*

**Pairs (extract):** beans + paper filters appear together in 22% of orders containing beans; beans + grinder in 3%.

**Baskets:** 41% of orders fall between £18 and £24. Delivery costs the shop £3.90 an order.

**Recommendation:** free delivery from £25, shown in the cart as "£4 to go for free delivery". A 250 g bag (£8.50) gets most £18 to £24 baskets over the line.

**Bundle:** "Pour-over starter set": dripper, 100 filters, 250 g beans. Items at current prices £32.50; set price £29 (saving £3.50). Items cost the shop £15.60 together. Margin at £29: 46%, against 52% for the items bought separately; worth testing only if it lifts sets sold, not just discounts existing buyers.

**Test:** 4 weeks; main number gross margin per order; guardrail conversion rate; stop if margin per order falls for two weeks running.
