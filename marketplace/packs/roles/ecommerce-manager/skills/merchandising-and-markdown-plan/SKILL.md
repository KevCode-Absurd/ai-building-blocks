# Merchandising and markdown plan

Merchandising is choosing what shoppers see first. Done from data, it puts stock that sells in front of people, moves stock that won't sell on its own while there's still time, and stops the home page from being decided by whoever argues loudest.

## When to use

- "What should we feature this week?"
- "We have too much of the autumn range and six weeks left."
- A range review: keep, cut or reorder.
- Before a sale, to plan steps and floor prices.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 for steps 1 to 7. Level 2 to prepare collection sort orders, scheduled price changes or draft emails. Level 3 to reorder collections and home page slots and to run markdown steps in the approved plan, never below floor prices. Any price outside the plan needs a fresh yes.

- Stock on hand by product or variant, sales by week for at least 8 weeks (ideally a year), cost price and current price. Shopify's inventory reports (sell-through, days of inventory remaining, ABC analysis) are a good start.
- Price history for at least the last 30 days for anything that might be reduced.
- Selling dates: season end, sale dates, stock arriving.
- Rules: brands that can't be discounted, minimum margin, products that must be featured.
- Markets: EU, UK, US.

## Steps

1. **Work out the numbers per product.** Show each formula once.
   - Sell-through = units sold ÷ (units sold + units in stock), over the period.
   - Weeks of cover = units in stock ÷ average weekly units sold (last 4 weeks).
   - Margin % = (price − cost) ÷ price.
   - Trend = last 4 weeks' sales against the 4 weeks before.
2. **Sort every product.**
   - *Hero:* sells well, enough stock. Feature it.
   - *Push:* good margin, stock to move, sells when seen. Give it slots.
   - *Clear:* weeks of cover run past the season's end. Plan markdowns.
   - *Cut:* slow and low margin. Don't reorder; clear or bundle.
   - *Reorder:* selling out before more stock arrives. Flag to the buyer.
3. **Check against the calendar.** For each clear product, compare weeks of cover with the weeks left to sell. The gap sets how much faster it must sell.
4. **Build the trading calendar.** Week by week: home page slots, the first rows of each key collection, and email features, with the product, the reason, and a stock check (don't feature what will sell out mid-week).
5. **Write collection sort rules** the team can repeat: in-stock heroes first, push products in set rows, new products in fixed slots, sold-out products out of the first rows.
6. **Plan markdowns.** For each clear product: steps with dates (for example, a first cut, then a deeper cut two weeks later if sell-through is behind), floor price (never below cost unless the client says so), and expected sell-through, marked as an estimate.
7. **Check the price rules for each market.**
   - *EU:* the "was" price and any percentage must use the lowest price charged in at least the previous 30 days. A second step measured from the first sale price must still be checked against that 30-day low; check the country's own rules for stepped reductions.
   - *UK:* deadlines must be real, with prices going back up when they end; the price shown includes mandatory delivery.
   - *US:* a "was" price should be a genuine former price; the FTC holds price comparisons to the same truthfulness standard as other claims.
   - *Everywhere:* never raise a price just before a sale.
8. **Write this week's three actions.**
9. **Apply (level 2 or 3).** Schedule price changes and sort orders; log each change. Review sell-through weekly and move to the next step only by the plan's rule.

## Checks

- Every figure comes from the exports, with the formula shown.
- Every price change is a proposal until the client approves the plan.
- Floor prices are at or above cost unless the client says otherwise.
- EU "was" prices checked against the 30-day low; UK deadlines real.
- Featured products have enough stock for the week.

## Output format

1. **Product table:** product, stock, weekly sales, weeks of cover, sell-through, margin, trend, group (hero, push, clear, cut, reorder).
2. **Trading calendar:** week, slot, product, reason, stock check.
3. **Collection sort rules.**
4. **Markdown plan:** product, current price, 30-day low, step dates and prices, floor, expected sell-through (estimate), price-rule check by market.
5. **This week's three actions.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Thistle & Thread is a made-up knitwear shop used only to show the format. Figures are invented for this example. Season ends in 6 weeks.*

| Product | Stock | Sales a week | Weeks of cover | Margin | Group |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lambswool crew, oatmeal | 40 | 12 | 3.3 | 58% | Hero |
| Cable cardigan, green | 150 | 10 | 15 | 61% | Clear |
| Fair Isle hat | 18 | 9 | 2 | 55% | Reorder |

Weeks of cover = stock ÷ average weekly sales, e.g. 150 ÷ 10 = 15.

**Markdown plan, cable cardigan (sold to UK and EU):** current price £95; lowest price in the last 30 days £95. Step 1, week 1: £79 ("was £95", about 17% off). Step 2, week 3, only if sell-through is under 40%: £69. For EU shoppers the 30-day low on that date is £79, so the reduction is shown from £79 (about 13% off), not from £95. For UK shoppers, show the saving against a genuine recent price, and ask the client before reusing £95. Floor £52 (cost £37). Expected: 60 to 70% sold by season end (estimate).

**This week:** feature the oatmeal crew on the home page; move the cardigan into row 1 of Knitwear; ask the buyer to reorder the Fair Isle hat.
