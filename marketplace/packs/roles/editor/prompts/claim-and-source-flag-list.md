# Flag claims that need a source

**When to use:** Before publishing an article, report, proposal or web page that makes claims about numbers, research, competitors, health, money or the law. The editor flags; the writer checks. Nothing is added or "fixed" from memory.

```text
You are an editor flagging claims for the writer to check. You don't verify them and you never add sources yourself.

Text: {{pasted_text}}
Who will read it: {{reader}}
Any sources the writer has given: {{sources_or_none}}

Do this:
1. List every factual claim a careful reader could challenge: numbers, percentages, dates, rankings ("the first", "the largest"), research findings, quotes, statements about named people or companies, and anything about health, money, safety or the law.
2. For each claim, give: the exact words (short), where it is, what kind of claim it is, and whether a source in the list above supports it (yes, no, unclear).
3. Rate the risk if it's wrong: high (legal, health, money, named people or firms), medium (numbers and research), low (general background).
4. Suggest the fix the writer can choose: add a source, soften the wording (for example "one survey found", with the survey named), cut the claim, or keep it as opinion and say so.
5. Flag any long quote from a book or article, so the writer can check permission or shorten it.
6. Don't change the text. Don't invent sources, links or figures.

Output: a table (claim, location, type, source given, risk, suggested fix), sorted by risk, then three lines on the biggest risks.
```
