# Copy edit and proofread

## When to use

- A draft is nearly ready and needs a careful second reader before it goes out.
- The client asks to "tighten it" or "fix the grammar" and the structure already works.
- A designed or laid-out file needs a final proofread before print or launch.
- Several people wrote parts of a document and it reads like several people.

Not for: drafts whose main point is missing or buried (use `structural-edit`), text that must be rewritten for a lower reading level (use `plain-language-rewrite`), or checking facts against live sources unless the client asks and gives access.

## What you need

1. The draft (a .docx, a Google Doc with comment access, or pasted text).
2. The reader and the goal: who reads it and what they should do or know after.
3. The level: proofread only, copy edit, or copy edit plus line edit.
4. Style: the house style guide or style sheet, or the client's choice of US or UK spelling and a general guide (AP, Chicago, GOV.UK, Microsoft or similar).
5. What must not change: legal wording, quotes, product names, approved claims.
6. Length limit and deadline.

**Access:** level 1 to read the draft and style guide. Level 2 to edit with Track Changes in Word or in Suggesting mode in Google Docs, so the writer accepts or rejects each change. Level 3, within the client's limits, to accept the agreed changes into the master copy or update the named page. Changing published legal, medical or financial wording always needs a new yes.

## Steps

### 1. Read it all first

Read the whole piece without editing. Write the main point in one sentence. If you can't, stop and say so: the draft needs a structural edit, not commas. Note the writer's voice: three phrases or habits that are theirs and should survive.

### 2. Start the style sheet

Before the first change, open a style sheet with these headings: spelling (US or UK, and word choices such as "email" or "e-mail"), capitals, numbers (when to use numerals, how to write money, percentages and dates), punctuation (serial comma or not, dash style), names and terms (exact spelling of people, products and places), and abbreviations. Add every decision as you make it. If the client has a guide, record only where you had to choose.

### 3. Line and copy edit, in this order

Work paragraph by paragraph:

1. **Paragraph:** does it do one job? Does its first sentence say what the paragraph is about? Move or merge only when the logic clearly needs it; otherwise query.
2. **Sentence:** split sentences over about 25 words unless the logic needs them long. Put the doer first (active voice). Turn hidden verbs back into verbs ("make a decision" becomes "decide"). Cut "there is" and "there are" openings where a verb can lead.
3. **Word:** prefer the short, common word ("buy" not "purchase", "use" not "utilise"). Cut padding ("in order to" becomes "to"; "at this point in time" becomes "now"). Remove repeated points. Keep the writer's own phrases unless they block the reader.
4. **Consistency:** apply the style sheet. Check names, numbers, dates, units, capitals and terms against their first use.
5. **Grammar and punctuation:** agreement, tense, pronouns with clear antecedents, punctuation that matches the chosen style.

### 4. Flag, don't invent

- A claim the reader would challenge, with no source: add "Query: source for this figure?"
- A number that doesn't add up, a date that conflicts, a name spelt two ways: query it, suggest the likely fix, don't silently choose.
- A change that would alter meaning: query instead of changing.

### 5. Proofread pass (separate, last)

On the final or laid-out version, read slowly for what earlier passes miss: doubled words, missing words, wrong homophones, numbering in lists and headings, cross-references ("see page 4"), captions, headers and footers, links, spacing, and widows and orphans in print layouts. Read headings and numbers on their own. If you can, read on a different screen or printout from the one you edited on.

### 6. Run a checker, then judge it

Run a consistency or grammar checker if the client allows cloud tools. Treat every suggestion as a question. Reject anything that changes meaning or voice.

### 7. Hand back

Return the files and notes in the output format below.

## Checks before handing over

- Every change is visible as a tracked change or suggestion.
- Every number, name, date and quote matches the original or is queried.
- The writer's three voice markers are still there.
- The style sheet covers every choice you made, and the text follows it from first page to last.
- No sentence over 25 words remains without a reason.
- No fact, source or claim was added that the writer didn't supply.
- Length is within the limit.
- The change summary is seven bullets or fewer and ranks the most important first.

## Output format

1. **Clean copy:** all edits accepted (for reading).
2. **Tracked copy:** every edit visible.
3. **Main changes:** three to seven bullets: what changed and why.
4. **Queries:** numbered, each with the location, the question and a suggested fix.
5. **Style sheet:** the decisions made, grouped by heading.
6. **Access log** (levels 2 and 3 only): what was changed, where, when and the result.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bike repair shop's newsletter intro. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Before:** In order to ensure that all of our valued customers are able to take advantage of the opportunity to get their bikes serviced before the spring rush, we are pleased to announce that we will be extending our opening hours on Saturdays, which will now be 8am-6pm, starting from the 1st March.
>
> **After:** Beat the spring rush. From 1 March, we're open longer on Saturdays: 8am to 6pm.
>
> **Main changes:**
> - Cut the opening from 52 words to 15 and led with the reader's benefit, because the reader needs the new hours, not the build-up.
> - Wrote the date as 1 March and the range with "to", to match the UK style sheet.
> - Kept "spring rush", the writer's own phrase from earlier issues.
>
> **Query 1 (para 1):** Do the longer hours apply every Saturday, or only until a set date? If there's an end date, suggest adding "until 31 May".
>
> **Style sheet:** UK spelling; dates as 1 March (no ordinal); times as 8am, ranges with "to"; serial comma: no.
