# Plain language rewrite

## When to use

- Customers or staff keep asking what a page, letter or form means.
- A terms of service intro, policy, notice or instruction must be readable by the general public.
- A report written for experts now has to go to a wider audience.
- A client wants a "grade 8" version of a text.

Not for: deciding what a legal, medical or financial text should say. This skill makes the existing meaning clearer and marks every point for the client's expert to confirm.

## What you need

1. The original text, complete.
2. The reader: who they are, what they already know, and what they need to do after reading.
3. The points that must survive: legal duties, deadlines, amounts, safety warnings, rights.
4. Any wording that can't change (defined legal terms, regulator-required phrases).
5. The target: a reading level, a length, or both.

**Access:** level 1 to read the text. Level 2 to write the rewrite as suggestions in Google Docs, tracked changes in Word or a draft page. Level 3, within the client's limits, to publish the approved version on the page named. Publishing changed legal, medical or financial wording always needs a new yes after the client's expert has signed it off.

## The standards behind this skill

- **GOV.UK (UK):** plain English is mandatory on GOV.UK. Split sentences over 25 words, keep paragraphs to five sentences or fewer, prefer short words, be wary of words ending in -ion and -ment, use "must" only for legal requirements, "need" for steps, "can" for options. Specialists prefer plain English too.
- **Digital.gov (US):** write for your audience; plain language is not dumbing down. Use active voice and present tense, and turn hidden verbs back into verbs. Federal agencies work under the Plain Writing Act of 2010.
- **Readability formulas:** Word's Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level counts sentence length and syllables. Microsoft suggests about grade 7 to 8 for most documents. A formula can't tell whether the text makes sense, so it is checked against a reader test.
- **WCAG 3.1.5 (Level AAA):** text above lower secondary level (7 to 9 years of school) should have a simpler version or supporting content.

## Steps

### 1. List the points before you write

Read the original and number every point it makes: each duty, right, deadline, amount, condition and warning. This list is the contract. Nothing on it may be lost, and nothing may be added.

### 2. Put the points in the reader's order

Ask: what does the reader need first? Usually: what this is, what they must do, by when, what happens if they don't, and where to get help. Put background and history last, or cut it if it serves no point on the list.

### 3. Rewrite

- **Talk to the reader.** Use "you" for the reader and "we" for the organisation.
- **One idea per sentence.** Split at "and", "which", "however" and semicolons. Keep most sentences short, with none over 25 words unless the logic needs it.
- **Active voice, present tense.** "We will send you a letter", not "A letter will be sent".
- **Verbs, not nouns.** "Decide", not "make a decision". "Apply", not "submit an application".
- **Common words.** "Buy" not "purchase"; "start" not "commence"; "help" not "facilitate"; "about" not "in relation to".
- **Must, need, can.** "Must" for legal duties only; "need" for steps; "can" for options.
- **Define what you can't replace.** If a legal or technical term must stay, explain it the first time: "a lien (a legal claim on your property)".
- **Use structure.** Headings that say what's under them, numbered steps for a process, bullet lists for options, tables for comparisons.

### 4. Check coverage

Fill the coverage table: each numbered point, where it appears in the rewrite, and whether the wording changed its strength (for example "must" stayed "must"). Mark every legal, safety or money point "For your adviser to confirm".

### 5. Check readability two ways

- **Formula:** run Flesch-Kincaid in Word or a similar tool. Note the before and after scores. Don't chase the number by chopping sentences into fragments.
- **Reader test:** if the client can, ask two or three people like the real reader to find the answer to two key questions ("When must you pay?" "What happens if you miss it?"). If they can't, fix the text, not the reader.

### 6. Hand back

Return the rewrite, the coverage table, the terms list, the before and after scores and the queries.

## Checks before handing over

- Every numbered point from the original appears in the rewrite, at the same strength.
- Nothing has been added that the original didn't say.
- Legal, safety and money points are marked for the client's adviser, with the line: *Check this with your legal adviser before publishing.*
- Sentences over 25 words are rare and justified.
- Defined terms that must stay are explained on first use.
- Headings and lists help someone who only scans.
- The formula score moved in the right direction and the reader test (if run) passed.

## Output format

1. **Rewrite**, ready to paste.
2. **Coverage table:** point number, original wording (short), where it is in the rewrite, strength kept (yes or no), adviser check needed (yes or no).
3. **Terms kept and explained.**
4. **Scores:** Flesch-Kincaid grade and word count, before and after.
5. **Queries** for the client.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a self-storage firm's late payment clause. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Original (61 words):** In the event that the Customer fails to remit payment of any sum due under this Agreement within fourteen (14) days of the due date, the Company reserves the right, without prejudice to any other rights or remedies, to deny access to the Unit and to levy a late payment charge as set out in the Schedule of Fees appended hereto.
>
> **Rewrite (39 words):** **If you pay late**
> You must pay within 14 days of the due date. If you don't:
> - we can stop you getting into your unit, and
> - we can charge a late fee (see the fee list at the end).
>
> **Coverage:** 1. Payment due within 14 days: kept, "must". 2. Access can be denied: kept, "can". 3. Late fee per schedule: kept, points to fee list. 4. "Without prejudice to other rights": removed from the summary line; **Query 1** for the adviser: keep it as a sentence ("We may also use our other rights under this agreement")?
>
> **Scores:** 61 words before, 39 after. One 61-word sentence became a heading, a 10-word sentence and a two-item list. Run Word's readability statistics on both versions and record the grades here.
>
> Check this with your legal adviser before publishing.
