# Training interview: Editor and proofreader

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert not to paste clients' confidential drafts; describe them, or use short anonymised extracts.

1. **What kinds of writing and clients do you edit most: business, marketing, academic, books, government, technical?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and which style guides and conventions it should know best.

2. **When a client says "just proofread it", how do you decide what level of edit the draft really needs?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's test for matching the edit to the draft, the most common source of disappointment.

3. **Walk me through your last edit from the moment the file arrived to the moment you sent it back.**
   Why it matters: shows the real sequence of passes, tools and checks the agent should copy.

4. **How do you keep a writer's voice while cutting a lot?**
   Why it matters: voice is what clients fear losing; the expert's habits here become the agent's rules.

5. **Show me a before-and-after of a paragraph you're proud of. What did you change and why?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a real model of good judgment, not just rules.

6. **Which style guides do you use, and where do you depart from them?**
   Why it matters: records the expert's defaults and exceptions for the style sheet.

7. **What goes on your style sheet, and how do you build it as you go?**
   Why it matters: the style sheet is the editor's memory; its format becomes a template.

8. **How do you write a query to an author so they act on it rather than feel criticised?**
   Why it matters: captures tone and structure for author queries.

9. **What do grammar and consistency checkers get wrong, and how do you use them anyway?**
   Why it matters: teaches the agent when to trust a tool and when to overrule it.

10. **How do you handle a claim you suspect is wrong but can't check?**
    Why it matters: sets the line between flagging and fact-checking, and how to phrase it.

11. **How do you approach plain language rewrites of legal or policy text without changing what it means?**
    Why it matters: captures the expert's safeguards for high-stakes wording.

12. **Do you use readability formulas? When do you ignore them?**
    Why it matters: records how the expert balances scores against real readers.

13. **What do you check in a final proofread that earlier passes miss?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a proofreading checklist from experience.

14. **For academic papers: how do you review an argument and its methods when you're not a specialist in the field?**
    Why it matters: sets the limits of an editor's review and how to say "unclear from the text" honestly.

15. **How do you help authors answer reviewer comments they disagree with?**
    Why it matters: captures the expert's approach to polite, evidence-based replies.

16. **What are your rules on AI tools with client text, and how do you explain them to clients?**
    Why it matters: confidentiality and journal AI policies shape what the agent may do with a draft.

17. **Tell me about an edit that went wrong. What did you change in how you work?**
    Why it matters: reveals the checks the expert now adds and the warning signs to watch for.

18. **How do you scope and price a job, and how do you handle a client who wants more than they paid for?**
    Why it matters: helps the agent agree scope early and avoid silent scope creep.

19. **What does a client have to send you for you to do your best work?**
    Why it matters: builds the agent's intake list.

20. **What would you never do as an editor, even if a client asked?**
    Why it matters: records the expert's ethical lines, such as ghost-writing assignments or inventing sources.
