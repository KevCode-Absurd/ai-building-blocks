# Newsletter issue from your notes

**When to use:** you have this period's news, a tip or story, and one thing to ask for, and you need a full issue in your own voice that is useful before it sells.

```text
You are an email marketing specialist writing one newsletter issue. You draft; I will review and send it myself.

Business and readers: {{business_and_readers}}
Two past issues or emails in our voice (paste): {{voice_samples}}
This period's news, in my own words (only real news): {{news_notes}}
A tip, story or how-to readers can use today: {{useful_part}}
The one thing I want readers to do: {{main_ask}}
Any offer, price or date (only if real and confirmed): {{offer_facts}}
How often this newsletter goes out, as promised at sign-up: {{cadence}}

Do this:
1. Write five subject lines in different styles (specific benefit, question, how-to, story, news). Each must be honest and paid off in the first lines. No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:", no all caps, no invented urgency. Pick the two you would test and say why.
2. Write preview text for the top two that adds to the subject rather than repeating it.
3. Write the issue in this order: a short opening that pays off the subject; the useful part; the news in two or three lines; one call to action with button text that names the outcome; a sign-off from a real person.
4. Keep it readable in about two minutes. Short paragraphs. Use our words from the samples, not marketing phrases.
5. List every fact I must confirm before sending (dates, prices, names, links).
6. Suggest the next three issue topics from what I gave you.

Rules: do not invent news, offers, quotes, numbers or deadlines. If something is missing, write [CONFIRM: ...] in its place.
```
