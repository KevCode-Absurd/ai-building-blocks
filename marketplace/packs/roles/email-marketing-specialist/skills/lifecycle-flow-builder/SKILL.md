# Lifecycle flow builder

A flow is a short series of emails that starts when someone does something: signs up, enquires, buys, goes quiet. It runs the same way for everyone, so it is worth building carefully once. This skill plans the logic, then writes every email.

## When to use

- **Welcome:** new subscribers or lead-magnet sign-ups.
- **Lead nurture:** enquiries, trade-show contacts, webinar sign-ups or free-trial users in a long sales cycle.
- **Post-purchase or post-booking:** help people use what they bought, ask for a review, and invite the next purchase.
- **Quote or enquiry follow-up:** quotes that went quiet.
- **Win-back:** customers who have not bought in longer than their usual gap.
- **Sunset:** contacts who have not clicked, bought or replied in a long time.

## What you need

- **Trigger and data:** the event that starts the flow and the fields available (sign-up source, product, booking date, lead type).
- **Consent basis** for the people who will enter it, and their regions.
- **The goal:** one action (first order, booking, reply, call, review, staying subscribed).
- **What the reader needs to know** to take that action: top questions, doubts and the words customers use. Ask for ten reviews or five common pre-sale questions if there is no research.
- **Offer facts:** prices, terms, guarantee, any real deadline. Only facts the client confirms.
- **Platform abilities:** can it exit on purchase, booking, reply or tag? Can it branch on clicks?
- **Voice samples** and the name of the real person who signs off.

**Access:** level 1 to read existing flows; level 2 to build the flow in your platform switched off, with tests to internal addresses; level 3 to switch it on after you confirm, within your limits.

## Steps

1. **Write the logic before the copy.**
   - *Trigger:* the exact event and any filter (for example, "joined via the newsletter form, not already a customer").
   - *Exit rules:* purchase, booking, reply, unsubscribe, or entering a more relevant flow. If the platform cannot exit on an event, say so and propose a workaround (a tag set by the store, or a filter on each email).
   - *Timing:* first email immediately (people expect it), then space the next ones a day or two apart, then slow down.
   - *Suppression:* people in another active flow, recent buyers of the same product, complainers.
2. **Map each email to one job.** Typical shapes:
   - *Welcome (4 to 5 emails over about 10 to 14 days):* deliver what was promised and say what to expect and how often; the most useful thing you know for a beginner; a story or proof from someone like them; answer the biggest doubt; one clear, soft offer.
   - *Lead nurture (5 to 6 emails over about 4 weeks):* remind them what they asked about; help with one practical tip; prove with a short case; answer a doubt; offer a next step (a call, a quote, a visit); close the loop politely.
   - *Post-purchase or post-booking (3 to 4 emails):* how to get the most from it; check-in and help; review request (ask everyone, not only people likely to be happy); a relevant next purchase at the right time.
   - *Quote follow-up (3 emails):* a short reminder with the quote attached or linked; a different angle (a question they might have, a small example); a clean close that makes "no" easy.
   - *Win-back (2 to 3 emails):* what is new or improved; a reason to come back; a simple question ("Still want to hear from us?").
   - *Sunset (2 to 3 emails):* ask whether they want to stay; offer a lower frequency or topic choice; confirm you will stop if they do nothing, then stop.
3. **Write each email.** Subject line that is specific and honest; preview text that adds to it; an opening line that pays off the subject; one idea; one link or button that names the outcome; a real person's sign-off. Keep nurture and quote emails under about 120 words; welcome and post-purchase emails can be longer if every line helps.
4. **Write two subject lines per email** for testing over time, in the reader's words. No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:", no invented urgency.
5. **Add compliance basics.** Visible unsubscribe link, sender identity and contact address, postal address for US mail, and one-click unsubscribe headers from the platform. Testimonials must be real and permissioned. Deadlines must be real.
6. **Set measures.** For each email: clicks, the goal action, unsubscribes and complaints. For the flow: share of entrants who reach the goal, and how long it takes. Do not judge flows on opens.
7. **List what the client must confirm** (offers, prices, guarantee wording, names, quotes, dates).

## Checks

- Trigger, filters, timing and exit rules are written down and possible in the platform.
- The first email arrives right away and keeps the sign-up promise.
- Each email does one job, with one main action.
- At least one email answers the biggest doubt, and at least one gives real help with no ask.
- Nothing is invented: offers, deadlines, reviews and numbers come from the client.
- The reader can say no easily; sunset and quote flows end cleanly.
- People who already bought or booked will not get "last chance" emails.
- Every email works on its own for someone who skipped the last one.

## Output format

1. **Flow map:** trigger, filters, exit rules, suppression, timing.
2. **Email table:** number, day, job, subject A and B, main action, goal metric.
3. **Full copy:** for each email, subject lines, preview text, body, button text, sign-off.
4. **Measures** and the date to review.
5. **Facts to confirm** before the flow is switched on (by the client, or by me at level 3).

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a small online tea shop. Flow: sunset for contacts with no click or order in 9 months. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Flow map:** Trigger: no click and no order in 9 months, still subscribed. Exit: clicks "Keep me on the list", orders, or unsubscribes. After email 3 with no action, the platform removes them from marketing sends (suppressed, not deleted, so they are never re-added by accident).

| # | Day | Job | Subject A / B | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Ask | "Still want our tea letters?" / "Should we keep writing to you?" | Keep me on the list |
| 2 | 5 | Offer a lighter option | "Fewer emails, same tea" / "Just the seasonal ones?" | Choose seasonal only |
| 3 | 12 | Close the loop | "This is our last email unless you say so" / "We'll stop here" | Keep me on the list |

**Email 3 (body)**

Subject: This is our last email unless you say so
Preview: No hard feelings. One click keeps you on the list.

Hi Priya,

We haven't heard from you in a while, so we'll stop sending our tea letters after today. Fewer emails to people who aren't reading them keeps our list healthy.

If you'd like to stay, one click does it.

**Keep me on the list**

If not, you don't need to do anything. Thanks for reading with us.

Ines, [Business]

*Why this works:* it is honest about what happens next, gives one clear action, and actually stops, which protects the client's complaint rate and sender reputation.
