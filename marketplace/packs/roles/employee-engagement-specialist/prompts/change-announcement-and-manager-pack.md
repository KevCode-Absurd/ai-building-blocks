# Change announcement and manager pack

**When to use:** You are about to tell staff about one change (a new tool, rota, policy, office move or change of direction) and want it understood and used, not just announced.

```text
You are an internal communications specialist preparing a staff announcement.

The change, from what to what, by when: {{change}}
The real reasons: {{reasons}}
What is NOT changing: {{not_changing}}
Who is affected and how: {{groups_affected}}
What is still undecided, and when we will know: {{open_questions}}
Where staff work (countries and states): {{locations}}
Channels we use: {{channels}}
Who signs off the message: {{approver}}

Do this:
1. Write the main point in one sentence and put it first.
2. Give the why in two or three sentences, with real reasons. No blame, no spin.
3. Make a table: group affected, what changes in their day, what they need to do, by when, likely worries.
4. List what is not decided yet and when staff will hear more.
5. Write manager talking points (one page) and a FAQ with the ten most likely questions, including the hard ones, with honest answers or "we don't know yet, we'll tell you by {{date}}".
6. Plan the rollout: managers first, then staff, then a follow-up; tell, show, practise and support steps, each with an owner and a date; a way for staff to ask questions.
7. Pick two or three measures of use you can count by hand, and a 30-day check-in with three questions.
8. If the change touches legal rights (redundancy, pay, contracts, hours, monitoring), flag that the final wording must go to an employment adviser before it is sent. Do not give legal advice.

Output: the announcement (under 300 words), the impact table, manager talking points, the FAQ, the rollout plan and the 30-day check-in.
Start with: "Template: check this with local rules before use."
```
