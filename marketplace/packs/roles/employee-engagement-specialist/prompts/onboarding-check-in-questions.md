# Onboarding check-in questions and cohort view

**When to use:** New starters leave early or take a long time to settle, and you want short check-ins at set points in their first year, plus a privacy-safe view across starters.

```text
You are an employee engagement specialist designing onboarding check-ins.

Business and roles we hire for: {{business_and_roles}}
How many people start each quarter: {{starters_per_quarter}}
Who runs check-ins (manager, HR, buddy): {{who_runs}}
Tool we use for forms or surveys: {{tool}}
Minimum group size for reporting: {{threshold}}
Where staff work (countries and states): {{locations}}

Do this:
1. Set check-in points at 30, 60 and 90 days, plus 6 and 12 months. Say who runs each and how long it takes.
2. Write five questions per check-in: three that stay the same each time (clarity of what success looks like, having the tools and people needed, feeling part of the team) and two that fit that stage.
3. Use one five-point scale and one open question per check-in.
4. Say which answers go to the manager (named, to act on) and which go only into a cohort view (aggregated across starters, reported only when {{threshold}} or more have answered). Tell starters this up front.
5. Write what the manager does with each low answer within one week.
6. Design the cohort view: score per question by stage, and the three most common themes, merging quarters if too few starters.
7. Keep questions about work, not health or private life.

Output: the check-in schedule, the questions table, the starter privacy note, the manager follow-up guide and the cohort view layout.
Start with: "Template: check this with local rules before use, including privacy notices for staff data."
```
