# Recognition programme

## When to use

- Staff surveys or exit interviews say people feel unnoticed.
- Awards always go to the same visible people or teams.
- The owner has a budget and no plan, or a plan and no budget.
- A milestone (a year of trading, a big contract, a hard winter) deserves marking.

Not for: pay rises, bonus plans or commission design. Those are pay decisions for the owner with an HR or finance adviser.

## What you need

1. Team size, sites, shift patterns, and how many work remotely or part-time.
2. The budget: a total or per person per year, and who signs it off.
3. What staff say they value, from survey comments or stay interviews. If there is nothing, ask three quick questions first: "When did you last feel properly thanked at work? What happened? What would you rather not get?"
4. What has been tried and what fell flat.
5. Busy periods to avoid, and the channels people really read (team chat, a noticeboard, a shift huddle).
6. Countries where staff work, for the tax check.

**Access:** level 1 to read survey themes, calendars and chat channels; level 2 to set up a private recognition channel, draft the guide and add tentative calendar holds; level 3 to publish the approved guide, open the channel to the agreed staff, post approved milestone messages and send event invites, within the budget and limits you set. Buying gifts or vouchers stays with you: I prepare the order list at level 2.

## Steps

### 1. Start from what people value

Read the survey themes or the three answers. Group them: being thanked by name for something specific, being trusted with more, time back, small treats, public moments, private notes. People differ; the programme needs more than one form of thanks.

Motivation research (self-determination theory) stresses autonomy, competence and relatedness. Thanks that names what the person did and why it mattered feeds competence and relatedness. Prizes that feel like control ("hit this target or else") can backfire. Keep the core of the programme about meaning, not prizes.

### 2. Design four layers

| Layer | What it is | Rule | Owner | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peer thanks | Anyone thanks anyone, in the channel or on a card | Name the act and the effect | Everyone; channel owner checks weekly | Near zero |
| Manager praise | Specific thanks in one-to-ones and team huddles | At least once a fortnight per person, never generic | Each manager | Near zero |
| Milestones | Work anniversaries, qualifications, big finishes | Same treatment for everyone at the same milestone | HR lead or owner | From the client's budget only |
| Awards | A few categories a year | Written criteria, nominations, a panel of three, no self-scoring by managers of nominees | Panel chair | From the client's budget only |

Use only costs the client gives. Anything else is marked [get a quote].

### 3. Write fair award criteria

- Three to five categories, each linked to a value or behaviour the business wants ("fixed a problem for a customer without being asked").
- Plain criteria with an example of what meets it.
- Anyone can nominate, with a short form: who, what they did, the effect.
- A panel of three that changes each round, scoring against the criteria alone. A manager doesn't score their own team's nominees.
- Publish the winners and the reason, so others see what counts.
- Check the spread at year end: by team, site, shift, gender and part-time status where numbers allow. If one group never wins, change the criteria or the nomination route.

### 4. Make it reach everyone

- Shift staff: thanks in the huddle and on the noticeboard as well as in chat.
- Remote staff: the same milestones, posted or delivered, and a video moment.
- Part-time staff: awards judged on contribution, not hours.
- Quiet people: a private option (a handwritten note, a message to their family if they agree).

### 5. Check the tax and rules

- **UK:** a gift is a tax-free trivial benefit only if it costs £50 or less, isn't cash or a cash voucher, isn't a reward for work and isn't in the contract. Directors of close companies are capped at £300 a tax year (HMRC, checked 6 November 2026).
- **US:** cash and gift cards are never excludable as small (de minimis) benefits. Length-of-service or safety awards must be tangible property, with limits of $400, or $1,600 under a qualified written plan (IRS Publication 15-B, 2026).
- **Elsewhere:** check the national tax authority.
- Mark every gift and award line "ask a qualified accountant before buying". Check this with local rules.

### 6. Build the calendar and the guide

- **12-month calendar:** one or two moments a month, avoiding the busiest weeks, each with cost, owner and a remote or shift option.
- **Manager guide:** when to thank (soon after, specific, in the way the person prefers), strong and weak examples, and 20 message starters.

### 7. Budget at three levels

Show what a lean, a standard and a richer version buy, using only the client's figures, and where small money goes far (handwritten notes, time off in lieu where policy allows, a team lunch on site).

## Checks

- Every layer has a rule, an owner and a cost from the brief or [get a quote].
- Award criteria are written before nominations open, with a rotating panel.
- Remote, part-time and shift staff have a route for each layer.
- Tax lines are marked for a qualified accountant, with the official source named.
- No pay or bonus design has crept in.
- The document starts with: *Template: check this with local rules before use, and ask a qualified accountant about gifts and awards.*

## Output format

1. Summary: what staff said they value, budget, the four layers.
2. Layer table (as above).
3. Award categories, criteria, nomination form and panel rules.
4. Reach check for remote, part-time and shift staff.
5. Tax notes with official sources.
6. 12-month calendar table.
7. Manager guide with 20 message starters.
8. Budget at three levels.
