# Wellbeing plan and signposting

## When to use

- Survey comments or absence point to stress, burnout or low morale.
- After a hard period: a restructure, a busy season, a loss in the team.
- The owner wants a wellbeing offer and has a small budget.
- Managers don't know what to say when someone is struggling.

Not for: counselling, crisis support, diagnosis, or decisions about a named person's health, absence or adjustments. Those go to the employer's support service, occupational health, a doctor, or an HR or legal adviser.

## What you need

1. Team size, sites, shifts and who is hardest to reach (night shift, drivers, remote staff).
2. The budget and any costs already known. I use only costs you give; anything else is [get a quote].
3. Current support: an employee assistance programme (EAP), occupational health, insurance helplines, mental health first aiders, flexible working.
4. Aggregated survey results or absence trends by team, at or above your minimum group size. No named health details.
5. Busy periods to avoid.
6. Countries where staff work, for the help lines.

**Access:** level 1 to read aggregated results, calendars and the handbook; level 2 to draft the calendar as tentative holds, the manager guide and the handbook section; level 3 to publish the approved guide and signposting sheet, send calendar invites and post approved reminders to the agreed channels. Health information about named staff is never part of this work at any level.

## Steps

### 1. Look at the work before the perks

Use HSE's six areas of work design that can lead to stress: demands, control, support, relationships, role and change. For each, write what the aggregated data shows and one practical fix.

| Area | Question to ask the data | Example fix |
|---|---|---|
| Demands | Where are hours, workload or pace comments clustered? | Re-plan the peak rota; stop one low-value task |
| Control | Do people have a say in how they work? | Teams set their own break pattern within cover rules |
| Support | Do people get help and information? | Weekly 10-minute huddle with a "stuck" slot |
| Relationships | Are there conflict or bullying themes? | Refresh the dignity at work policy and how to report |
| Role | Is it clear who does what? | One-page role cards for the busiest roles |
| Change | Are people told and consulted? | Managers-first briefings and a question box for each change |

WHO's 2022 guidelines put organisational interventions first, and a large UK study (Fleming, 2024) found participants in individual programmes such as resilience training, mindfulness and apps appeared no better off than non-participants. So at least half the plan's actions should change how work is organised.

### 2. Build the calendar

- One or two activities a month, timed to the business's seasons, avoiding the busiest weeks.
- Themes that suit this team, not a list of awareness days.
- Each line: activity, cost (from the brief or [get a quote]), owner, and how night, remote or field staff take part.
- Mix the work fixes from step 1 into the calendar, so the plan isn't only events.

### 3. Write the manager guide

- **Opening a check-in:** "How are you doing, really?" Ask twice. Choose a private, unhurried moment.
- **Listening:** let them talk, don't fix at once, reflect back what you heard.
- **Phrases to avoid:** "Everyone's stressed", "Have you tried yoga?", "You don't look ill".
- **What isn't the manager's job:** diagnosing, counselling, or promising confidentiality the manager can't keep when someone is at risk.
- **Agreeing changes:** ask what would help at work and agree small adjustments; in Great Britain, poor mental health can be a disability under the Equality Act 2010 if it has a substantial, long-term effect, and employers must then make reasonable adjustments (Acas, January 2025). Involve HR or an adviser for anything formal. Check this with local rules.
- **Recording:** write only what was agreed, not guesses about health.

### 4. Write the signposting sheet

List only real services the client provides or names, plus a doctor and urgent help:
- The employer's EAP, occupational health or insurance helpline, with how to reach them.
- "See your doctor (GP) for ongoing worries about your health."
- Urgent help: UK, 999 or A&E, NHS 111 (mental health option), Samaritans 116 123; US, 911, or call or text 988; Canada, call or text 9-8-8, or 9-1-1 if safety is at risk; Australia, 000 or Lifeline 13 11 14; India, Tele-MANAS 14416; elsewhere, the local emergency number.

With web search connected, I check each number on the official page before use and note the date. Without it, I mark them "check before use".

### 5. Add a short pulse (optional)

Eight to ten questions each quarter, using the survey skill's privacy rules. Ask about work conditions (workload, support, clarity), not symptoms or diagnoses.

### 6. Draft the handbook section

What support exists, how to ask for it, who sees what, and how adjustments are agreed. Mark it for an HR or legal adviser to check.

## Checks

- At least half the actions change how work is organised.
- Every cost is from the brief or marked [get a quote].
- No medical advice, no diagnosis words, no named person's health.
- Signposting lists only real services, a doctor, and urgent help lines for the client's countries.
- Hard-to-reach staff have a way to take part in each activity.
- The document starts with: *Template: check this with local rules before use. This is not medical advice; talk to a qualified professional about health.*

## Output format

1. Six-area table with findings and work fixes.
2. 12-month calendar: month, activity or fix, cost, owner, hard-to-reach option.
3. Manager guide (one to two pages).
4. Signposting sheet (one page, printable).
5. Optional pulse questions.
6. Draft handbook section, marked for adviser review.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a logistics firm with 120 staff, two depots and 40 drivers, England. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Template: check this with local rules before use. This is not medical advice; talk to a qualified professional about health.**
>
> | Area | What the data shows | Work fix | Owner | Date |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Demands | 31 comments on late route changes | Routes locked by 4pm the day before, except emergencies | Transport planner | 1 Mar |
> | Support | Night shift says "nobody to ask" | Named night lead with a phone number on the board | Depot manager | 15 Feb |
>
> **Signposting (print for every cab and break room):** Our EAP: [number from client]. Your GP for ongoing health worries. If someone's life is at risk: 999. Urgent mental health advice: NHS 111, mental health option. To talk any time: Samaritans 116 123.
