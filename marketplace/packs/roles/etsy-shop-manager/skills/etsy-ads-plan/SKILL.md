# Etsy Ads plan

Etsy Ads can bring a good listing in front of more buyers, but they cannot fix a weak one, and by default every listing is advertised. This skill decides which listings deserve ads, sets a budget the owner is comfortable with, and judges results on profit, not clicks.

Access levels for this skill: reading Etsy Ads stats and listing data is level 1. Writing the ads plan is level 2. Changing the campaign is level 3, only within the daily budget and listings the owner writes in "Access I have". As of November 2026, Etsy's Shared access does not reach Marketing and I found no official connector that covers Etsy Ads, so in practice the owner applies the plan in Shop Manager; the agent prepares exact steps.

## When to use

- Before switching Etsy Ads on.
- Ad spend is rising but orders are not.
- The owner asks whether ads are worth it.
- The shop is approaching the Offsite Ads threshold.

## What you need

- **Access I have:** level and limits, including the most the owner will spend per day.
- Screenshots of Etsy Ads stats for the last 30 to 90 days, overall and by listing: views, clicks, click rate, orders, revenue, spend, ROAS.
- Each listing's price, cost to make, packaging, shipping cost and Etsy fees, to work out margin per order. The owner supplies costs; Etsy's fee page gives the fee types.
- Stats for the same listings without ads: visits, conversion rate, favourites.
- Offsite Ads setting, and the shop's sales over the last 365 days.

## Steps

1. **Fix before you fund.** Ads compete on listing quality (likelihood of a click, history, titles and tags) as well as bid. Run the listing through the search visibility checks first: first photo, category, return policy, shipping. Do not advertise a listing that fails them.
2. **Work out margin per order** for each candidate listing: price minus making cost, packaging, shipping cost not charged to the buyer, and Etsy's fees. This is the most an order can earn before ads.
3. **Set the break-even ROAS** for each listing: price divided by margin per order. A listing with a price of 30 and a margin of 10 must return at least 3 in sales for every 1 spent on ads just to break even. Use the owner's own numbers.
4. **Pick the listings.** Prefer listings that already convert well without ads, have good margins and photos, and ship to the countries ads will reach (ads show only where the shop ships). Remove from the campaign listings with thin margins, made-to-order items with long processing times in peak season, and one-off items.
5. **Set the budget and strategy with the owner.** The owner chooses the daily budget; spend never exceeds it. Etsy sets a maximum daily budget that it reviews weekly. Pick one of Etsy's strategies (Greater visibility, Efficient spending or Lower click cost, where the budget allows them) and write down why.
6. **Write the steps.** Exact clicks in Shop Manager: Marketing, Etsy Ads, budget, strategy, Manage advertised listings. At level 3 with a route that reaches ads, make the change within the budget; otherwise the owner does it.
7. **Wait 30 days.** Etsy credits orders within 30 days of an ad click and suggests running an ad for 30 days before judging it. Do not change everything after a week.
8. **Review by listing.** For each listing: spend, orders, revenue, ROAS against its break-even ROAS. Keep listings above break-even; remove those clearly below it after 30 days; fix and retest listings with many clicks and no orders (a photo, price or description problem, not an ads problem).
9. **Check Offsite Ads.** Is the shop opted in or out? Offsite Ads charge a fee on attributed orders (15% for shops under $10,000 in any 365 days, 12% at or above, capped at $100 per order, as of November 2026), and become compulsory for the life of the shop once it reaches $10,000 in any 365 days. If the shop is close, warn the owner and check that prices still leave a margin after the fee.
10. **Report** spend, orders, revenue and ROAS by listing against break-even, the changes made, and the next review date.

## Checks

- No listing is advertised before it passes the search visibility checks.
- Margins come from the owner's costs, not estimates.
- The daily budget is the owner's number, written down.
- Judgements use at least 30 days of data.
- ROAS is compared with each listing's own break-even, not a general target.
- Offsite Ads status and threshold are checked.
- Every change is logged with date and reason.

## Output format

1. **Margin table:** listing, price, costs, fees, margin per order, break-even ROAS.
2. **Ads plan:** listings in and out with reasons, daily budget, strategy, review date, exact steps.
3. **30-day review table:** listing, spend, orders, revenue, ROAS, break-even, keep, fix or stop.
4. **Offsite Ads note:** status, sales in the last 365 days, fee effect on margin.
5. **Action log.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a linen textile shop. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

| Listing | Price | Margin per order | Break-even ROAS | 30-day ROAS | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linen tea towel set | 28 | 11 | 2.5 | 4.1 | Keep |
| Linen apron | 46 | 19 | 2.4 | 1.2 | Stop for now: 64 clicks, 1 order; check photos and sizing info |
| Single napkin | 7 | 2 | 3.5 | 2.0 | Stop: margin too thin for ads |

**Plan:** advertise the tea towel set and the table runner only; daily budget as you set it; strategy Efficient spending; review on 6 December.

**Offsite Ads:** opted in; sales over the last 365 days are under the $10,000 level, so you can still opt out. At a 15% fee, an Offsite Ads order of the single napkin would cost about 1.05 of its 2 margin.
