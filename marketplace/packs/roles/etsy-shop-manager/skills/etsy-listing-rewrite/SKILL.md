# Etsy listing rewrite

A good Etsy listing reads like a clear shop label, not a keyword pile. This skill rewrites titles, tags, attributes and descriptions from the shop's own search terms and the owner's facts, and catches rule problems before they cost a listing.

Access levels for this skill: reading the listings CSV, stats screenshots and listing pages is level 1. Writing the rewrite sheet, or saving new versions as drafts in the shop through Shared access, is level 2. Publishing the edits is level 3, only on the listings and fields written in "Access I have". Any new claim about materials, safety, origin or "handmade" needs the owner's written facts first.

## When to use

- A batch of up to 15 listings needs new titles, tags and descriptions.
- A new range is about to launch.
- Listings get views but few favourites or orders.
- Titles are long keyword lists, or tags are half empty.

## What you need

- **Access I have:** level and limits.
- The listings CSV (Settings, Options, Download Data) or the listing pages.
- Etsy Stats screenshots: search terms for the last 90 days, and listing views, favourites and orders.
- Marketplace Insights results for the main product types, if the owner has run them.
- The owner's facts for each item: what it is, size, materials, colours, how it is made and by whom, production partner if any, AI use if any, care, processing time, countries it ships to.
- The categories and attributes Etsy offers for each item's category (visible in the listing form).

## Steps

1. **Check the item can be listed.** Which Creativity Standards category is it: made, designed, handpicked or sourced by the seller? If a partner makes it, note the partner. If AI helped with the design or text, note it for disclosure. If it is bought in and unchanged and is not a supply, vintage or a gift basket the seller assembles, stop and tell the owner.
2. **Pick the buyer phrase.** From the shop's search terms and Marketplace Insights, choose the clearest phrase a buyer uses for this item (for example "speckled stoneware mug"), not the owner's internal name.
3. **Write the title.** Lead with the buyer phrase, then the one or two traits that matter most (material, size, colour, personalisation). Aim for fewer than 15 words. No repeated words, no subjective words ("beautiful", "unique"), no price or shipping, and a recipient or holiday only if it is essential. Read it aloud: it should sound like a label.
4. **Set the category and attributes.** The most specific category. Then every attribute that applies: colour, material, size, occasion, recipient and others the category offers. If the exact colour is missing, choose the closest and keep the exact shade for a tag.
5. **Write 13 tags.** Each up to 20 characters, all different, mostly two- or three-word phrases. Cover: the item in other words buyers use, style, use or room, recipient, occasion, the exact shade or material not covered by attributes, and a long-tail phrase from the search terms. Do not repeat a term already set as an attribute.
6. **Write the description.** First sentence: what the item is, in plain words. Then size, materials, colour variation, personalisation steps, care, processing and shipping, and what is not included. Add the production partner note and the AI use note where needed. No claims the owner has not supplied.
7. **Check the photos.** The first photo shows the single finished item, clear and well lit, at 2000 pixels or more, not a collage. At least one photo is unedited, of the real item on a real background. Suggest the order: hero, scale, detail, in use, variations. Add alt text for each photo. Send deeper photo work to the product photographer kit.
8. **Check safety details.** If the listing ships to the EU or Northern Ireland, are the manufacturer and product safety fields filled? For toys and children's items, flag CE marking, GB rules and US children's product certification for the owner to confirm with an adviser.
9. **Note what not to change.** A listing's web address is fixed at first publish, so a big change of product may need a new listing instead of a retitle. Do not change a best seller's title wholesale without the owner's yes; test on similar listings first.
10. **Deliver or publish.** At level 2, save drafts or deliver the sheet. At level 3, publish within limits, log each change, and check the search visibility page and Stats a week later.

## Checks

- Every item fits a Creativity Standards category; partners and AI are disclosed.
- Titles lead with what the item is, in fewer than 15 words, with no repetition.
- 13 different tags, each within 20 characters, none repeating an attribute.
- Every applicable attribute is set; category is specific.
- The description's first sentence says what the item is; every fact came from the owner.
- First photo passes Etsy's checks; an unedited real photo exists.
- Safety fields are set for EU and Northern Ireland listings, and children's items are flagged.
- Changes are logged, and best sellers were not rewritten without a yes.

## Output format

A table or sheet with one row per listing: listing ID, current title, new title, category, attributes to set, 13 tags, first two description lines, photo notes, disclosure notes (partner, AI), safety notes, owner facts still needed. Then a short summary of what changed and what to watch in Stats.

## Example of good output (short)

*Fernhill Clay is a made-up pottery shop, used only to show the format.*

**Current title:** "Mug Handmade Mug Pottery Mug Coffee Mug Gift for Her Unique Ceramic Mug Speckled Mug"

**New title:** "Speckled stoneware mug, handmade, 350 ml, oatmeal glaze"

**Category:** the most specific mug category the listing form offers.
**Attributes:** colour: beige; material: stoneware; capacity as offered; dishwasher safe: owner to confirm.

**Tags (13):** speckled mug, stoneware coffee mug, oatmeal glaze mug, pottery mug gift, ceramic tea cup, rustic kitchen mug, wheel thrown mug, gift for tea lover, minimalist mug, housewarming gift, potter made cup, farmhouse mug, mug for latte

**Description, first lines:** "A wheel-thrown stoneware mug in a speckled oatmeal glaze, holding about 350 ml. Each mug is thrown by hand in my studio, so the speckles and size vary a little."

**Photo notes:** move the single mug on the table to first place (now a collage of four); add a hand-holding shot for scale.

**Owner facts needed:** dishwasher and microwave safe? Any glaze test results for food contact? (Ask your adviser what EU and GB buyers need.)
