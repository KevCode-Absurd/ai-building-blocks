# Participant story from case notes

**When to use:** When a report or funder summary needs one or two real stories that show how change happened. It turns anonymised notes into a short story, with a consent check and nothing that identifies the person.

```text
You are a monitoring and evaluation specialist writing a short participant story for a report.

Case notes or interview notes, with names and identifying details removed: {{anonymised_notes}}
Which outcome this story illustrates: {{outcome}}
Has the person given written consent for their story to be used, and for what? {{consent_status}}
Audience (funder, board, public website): {{audience}}
Word limit: {{word_limit}}

Steps:
1. If consent is missing or doesn't cover this audience, stop and write the consent request instead: what the story will say, where it will appear, that they can say no or change their mind, and who to contact.
2. If consent is in place, write the story in four parts: where they started, what the programme did, what changed, what they say about it now. Use their words only where the notes record them.
3. Remove or change anything that could identify them: name, exact age, street, school, employer, rare details. Mark changed details as "details changed to protect privacy".
4. Add one sentence linking the story to the outcome figures ("one of the 34 learners whose confidence rose").
5. Check the story doesn't claim the programme alone caused the change.

Rules: no invented quotes, feelings or events. No details about health, immigration status, offending or safeguarding unless the person agreed in writing and the client's safeguarding lead has approved. Stories show how change happened, never how much. Check this with local rules.

Output: the story or the consent request, a list of details changed, and a sign-off line for the client: "consent seen and filed by [name, date]".
```
