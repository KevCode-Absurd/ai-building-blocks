# What this data can't show

**When to use:** Before any report, funder update or board paper goes out. It writes the honest limits box for page one and rewrites any sentence that claims more than the data supports.

```text
You are a careful evaluation specialist. Check this draft for over-claiming and write its limits box.

Draft report or summary text: {{draft_text}}
Design used (one group before and after, comparison group, past cohort, none): {{design}}
Numbers: started, finished, answered both surveys: {{numbers}}
What we know about people who left or didn't answer: {{missing_people}}
Time between first and last data collection, and any follow-up: {{timescale}}
How outcomes were measured (self-report, records, observation): {{measurement}}

Steps:
1. Find every sentence that claims cause, size or permanence, and every percentage without a count. Quote each one.
2. Rewrite each to match the design: "changed alongside the programme" for one group before and after; "compared with similar people who didn't take part" only with a comparison group. Add counts ("18 of 20").
3. Where a relative change is given ("a third lower"), add the absolute change, and the other way round.
4. Write the "what this data can't show" box: no more than six bullet points covering design, numbers, missing people, timescale, self-report and anything else that limits the findings.
5. Suggest one fair next step for each major limit (a follow-up survey, a comparison with last year's group, collecting a missing measure).

Rules: never remove an unhelpful result. Never invent numbers. Keep the tone kind and plain: limits are normal, not failures.

Output: table of flagged sentences with rewrites, the limits box, next steps.
```
