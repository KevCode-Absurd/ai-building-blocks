# Before-and-after survey

## When to use

- The measures plan says an outcome is measured by asking participants.
- The current form asks only whether people enjoyed the sessions.
- A funder wants evidence of change, not satisfaction.
- A course or programme has a clear start and end point.

Do not use it for clinical or health outcome measurement, for research that needs formal ethics approval, or to survey people without the client's approval.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** existing forms and past survey exports.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** build the survey, consent page, invitation and reminders in the survey tool, unpublished and unsent.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send the approved survey to the approved participants on the agreed dates, send the agreed reminders, and close it on the date, within the client's limits. A new group, changed wording, children or sensitive topics need a fresh yes. Log every send.

## What you need

1. The outcomes and measures this survey covers (from the `outcome-measures-and-data-plan` skill).
2. Who takes part: ages, languages, reading levels, access needs, how they will answer (paper, phone, link).
3. Who gives consent: the adult, a parent or carer, or a young person with a parent informed.
4. The client's privacy notice and who will see the answers.
5. Dates: first session, last session, any follow-up point.
6. Any established question sets the funder expects.

## Steps

### 1. One or two questions per outcome
Write questions only for the outcomes in the plan. Ask about something people can report: what they did, how confident they feel, how often something happened. Avoid asking people to judge the programme in the "before" survey.

### 2. Write plainly
Short sentences, everyday words, one idea per question. Split any question that asks two things. Avoid agree-or-disagree statements where you can; people tend to agree. Use a balanced scale with clear labels at each end. If you use an established question set, copy its exact wording and scale.

### 3. Keep the pair identical
Same questions, same order, same scale, same mode where possible at both points. Add any "how was the programme?" questions only at the end, after the matched block, so they cannot shape the outcome answers.

### 4. Add the participant code
Use a code the person can recreate or that staff assign (for example, programme initials plus a number). Names stay in the client's own register; the survey holds only the code.

### 5. Write the consent and privacy page
In plain words:
- what the survey is for and who runs it;
- that taking part is voluntary, and saying no changes nothing about the help they get;
- that they can stop at any time and skip any question;
- who will see answers, and that reports show groups, not individuals;
- how long answers are kept and how to ask for them to be removed;
- who to contact with questions.

Under the EU and UK GDPR, consent has to be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, and as easy to withdraw as to give. Consent to take part is always needed ethically; whether consent is also the legal basis for using the data is a separate question for the client's privacy lead. Check this with local rules.

### 6. Extra checks before sending
- **Children and young people:** confirm who gives consent and the school's or organisation's rules.
- **US schools:** surveys given to students in Department of Education-funded programmes that touch protected areas (such as mental health, illegal behaviour, religion or family income) fall under PPRA; ask the school or district first.
- **Sensitive topics** (health, ethnicity, religion, sexuality): include only if truly needed, and plan to report only in large groups.
- **Staff surveys:** employees generally cannot consent freely to their employer, so say clearly that taking part is voluntary and anonymous or confidential, and which.

### 7. Pretest
Ask a few people like the participants to answer aloud. Fix any question they read differently from what you meant. Time it.

### 8. Write the invitation and reminders
Invitation: why, how long it takes, the code, the consent page link. One or two reminders, spaced out. For paper or phone surveys, a short script for staff.

### 9. Send and run (level 3)
After the client approves the wording, list and dates, send to the approved participants, send the agreed reminders, and close on the date. Watch for odd patterns: many identical answers, replies from outside the list. Stop and ask if anything looks wrong.

## Checks

- Every question links to an outcome in the plan.
- No double-barrelled, leading or jargon questions.
- Before and after blocks are identical in wording, order and scale.
- Answers pair by code, not name.
- Consent page covers purpose, voluntary, withdraw, who sees, how long, contact.
- Children's, school, sensitive and staff cases have been checked with the right person.
- Pretest done and changes made.
- At level 3, only the approved list received it, and the send log is updated.

## Output format

1. Question map: outcome → question(s) → scale.
2. "Before" survey and "after" survey, side by side.
3. Consent and privacy page.
4. Invitation, reminders and a staff script for paper or phone.
5. Pretest notes and changes.
6. Send plan: list, dates, reminders, close date, owner.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real programme: an adult literacy course with a week 1 and week 12 survey. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Your code:** first two letters of your town + the day of the month you were born (e.g. LE14).
>
> | Q | Week 1 and week 12 (identical) | Scale |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1 | How confident do you feel reading a letter from a bank or council on your own? | 1 Not at all confident to 5 Very confident |
> | 2 | In the last week, did you read a message, label or letter on your own? | Yes / No / Not sure |
>
> *Week 12 only, after Q1 to Q2:* "What, if anything, has helped you most?"
>
> **Consent (extract):** "Taking part is your choice. Saying no won't change your place on the course. You can stop at any time or skip any question. Only the course coordinator sees your answers, and reports only show totals for the group."
