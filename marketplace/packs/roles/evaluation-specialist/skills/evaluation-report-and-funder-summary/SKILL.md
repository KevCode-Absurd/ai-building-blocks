# Evaluation report and funder summary

## When to use

- A grant report or end-of-year report is due.
- Matched surveys and attendance data are in, and the team needs to know what they show.
- A board or council is deciding whether to keep, change, scale or stop a programme.
- A funder has questioned past results and wants a clearer account.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** survey exports, attendance and outcome records, case notes with names removed, funder forms.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** analysis tabs in a copy of the sheet; the draft report and summary in the client's documents.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish the approved report to the folder or page the client chose, within their limits. Submitting anything to a funder, or sharing data that could identify a participant, needs a fresh yes every time. Log every action.

## What you need

1. The logic model, measures plan and evaluation questions.
2. The data: matched survey answers by code, attendance, outcome fields.
3. Starters, completers and leavers, with what is known about leavers.
4. Funders' outcome headings, word limits and deadlines.
5. One or two stories from case notes or interviews, with written consent to use them.
6. The minimum group size for reporting.

## Steps

### 1. Clean and count
Count starters, completers and people who answered both surveys. Pair answers by code. Drop nothing silently: list exclusions and why. Check for impossible values and duplicates.

### 2. Write "who is missing" first
Before any result, write who is not in the data: people who left early, didn't answer, couldn't read the survey, or joined late. Say whether they look different from those who stayed (age, attendance, starting scores) if you can tell.

### 3. Analyse outcome by outcome
For each outcome: the before and after figures for people who answered both, the change, and the number of people. For scales, give the share who moved up, stayed the same or moved down, as well as averages. Give absolute and relative differences together ("from 25% to 17%, 8 percentage points, a third lower").

### 4. Say how sure we can be
Match the claim to the design:
- one group, before and after: "changed alongside the programme";
- comparison group: "compared with similar people who didn't take part", plus how similar they were;
- theory-based: "the evidence supports a contribution", with the other explanations you checked.

With small numbers, say so in words ("based on 18 people, so treat this as a signal, not a firm result"). Present nothing more definitively than the evidence allows.

### 5. Process findings
How was it delivered? Attendance patterns, drop-out points, what staff and participants said helped or got in the way. These explain the outcome results and point to fixes.

### 6. The "what this data can't show" box
State plainly: no comparison group, small numbers, short timescale, self-reported answers, missing groups. This box goes on the first page of the report, not in an appendix.

### 7. Recommendations
Three kinds: improve the programme (from process findings), improve the data (what to collect differently), and what to tell funders. Each recommendation names an owner.

### 8. Funder summary
Two pages at most per funder. Use the funder's headings in their order. Under each: outputs, outcomes with numbers and people, one sentence on how sure we can be, and one consented story that shows how change happened. End with the limits and what you will do next. Prepare it; the client reviews and submits, or approves each submission at level 3.

## Checks

- Every figure comes from the client's data and shows the number of people.
- Starters, completers and missing groups are stated.
- No group below the minimum size is reported.
- Claims about cause match the design.
- Absolute and relative differences appear together.
- The "can't show" box is on page one.
- Stories have written consent and no identifying details.
- No unhelpful result was dropped.
- Funder headings and word limits are followed.
- Anything about data law or consent that is unclear is flagged for the client's privacy lead. Check this with local rules.

## Output format

1. Page one: summary, key results with numbers, "what this data can't show" box.
2. Who took part and who is missing.
3. Results by outcome: result line, "how sure" line, "what to improve" line.
4. Process findings.
5. Recommendations with owners.
6. Funder summary per funder.
7. Method note: design, data sources, exclusions.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up programme for illustration: Larkspur Adult Literacy, 2025 to 2026 year.*

> **Who took part:** 72 started; 58 finished; 51 answered both surveys. Of the 14 who left, 9 left in the first three weeks, mostly evening shift workers.
>
> **Outcome 1: confidence reading official letters.** 34 of 51 (67%) moved up at least one point; 15 stayed the same; 2 moved down. The average rose from 2.1 to 3.4 out of 5.
> *How sure:* this changed alongside the course for the 51 who answered both. We had no comparison group, so we can't say how much was due to the course.
> *What to improve:* early leavers were mostly shift workers; test a weekend group.
>
> **What this data can't show:** whether changes last after the course; results for the 21 who didn't answer both surveys; how learners would have done without the course.
