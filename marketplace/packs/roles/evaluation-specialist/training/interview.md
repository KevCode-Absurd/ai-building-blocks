# Training interview: Monitoring and evaluation specialist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share participants' names, survey answers, case notes or anything that could identify a person or a small organisation; anonymise every story.

1. **Which kinds of programmes, organisations and funders do you know best, and in which countries?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and which funder habits, data rules and evaluation guidance apply.

2. **Walk me through your last evaluation from the first conversation to the final report.**
   Why it matters: shows the expert's real sequence and where they spend their time.

3. **How do you decide how much evaluation a programme needs?**
   Why it matters: proportionality is the judgement that separates useful evaluation from wasted effort.

4. **Show me a logic model you were proud of. What did you leave out to keep it to one page?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's sense of what belongs in a model and what is noise.

5. **Tell me about an assumption in a theory of change that turned out to be wrong. How did you find out?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert tests links instead of just drawing them.

6. **How do you choose measures a busy team can really collect?**
   Why it matters: feasibility is where most data plans fail.

7. **Which established question sets do you use, and when do you write your own?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent tested instruments and the judgement for when they fit.

8. **Show me a survey question you rewrote after a pretest. What was wrong with it?**
   Why it matters: pulls out real before-and-after wording the agent can learn from.

9. **How do you handle consent for children, young people or people in vulnerable situations?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's safeguards and who they bring in.

10. **How do you match before and after answers without names?**
    Why it matters: a practical method the agent can reuse, and a common source of lost data.

11. **What do you do when half the participants don't answer the second survey?**
    Why it matters: tests how the expert handles attrition honestly.

12. **How do you explain to a funder that you can't prove the programme caused a result?**
    Why it matters: this is the hardest conversation in the role, and the wording matters.

13. **Tell me about a time a client asked you to drop or soften an unhelpful finding.**
    Why it matters: shows the expert's line on honesty and how they hold it kindly.

14. **When have you used a comparison group, and how did you make it fair?**
    Why it matters: captures when stronger designs are worth it for small organisations.

15. **How do you choose and use participant stories?**
    Why it matters: stories are powerful and easy to misuse; consent and balance rules matter.

16. **What do funders in your area most often ask for that the data can't support?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a list of over-asks and tested responses.

17. **Which tools do you use for surveys, data and reports, and what would you never do in them?**
    Why it matters: maps the expert's tools to the access levels and safety rules.

18. **What does your evaluation report look like on page one?**
    Why it matters: captures the expert's house format for results, uncertainty and limits.

19. **Tell me about an evaluation that changed how a programme was run.**
    Why it matters: shows what "useful" looks like in practice and how findings were turned into action.

20. **What are the most common mistakes you see in reports written by programme staff?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's checklist of things to catch.
