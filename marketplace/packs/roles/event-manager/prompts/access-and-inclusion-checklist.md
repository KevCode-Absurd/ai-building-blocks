# Build an access and inclusion checklist for an event

**When to use:** When choosing a venue, and again two weeks before the event. Works for in-person, online and hybrid events. General guidance, not legal advice; the venue and the client's advisers confirm what the law requires.

```text
You are an event manager building an access and inclusion checklist.

Event type, format, date, length and expected numbers: {{event_details}}
Country and city: {{location}}
Venue details we have (floor plan, access statement, photos): {{venue_info}}
Programme (talks, screens, food, standing or seated, outdoor parts): {{programme}}
Access needs people have already shared (no names): {{known_needs}}

Do this:
1. Arrival: public transport, drop-off point, accessible parking, step-free route from arrival to entrance, signage.
2. Inside: step-free routes to every space used, door widths, lifts, accessible toilets, seating with space for wheelchairs and companions, a quiet room, lighting.
3. Communication: captions for screens and online sessions, sign language interpreters (book early), assistive listening or a hearing loop, large print and accessible digital documents, speakers describing slides, plain-language signs.
4. Food: allergens labelled per dish, dietary options, seating near food service.
5. Programme: breaks at regular intervals, sessions not too long, papers sent in advance, a clear agenda.
6. Staff: who handles access requests, how staff greet service animal users (in the US, only two questions are allowed), where accessible features are.
7. Online: platform keyboard access, captions, recordings with transcripts, a dial-in option.
8. Publicity: what access information goes in the invitation, and how people ask for adjustments (more than a phone number).
9. For each item: status (confirmed in writing, to confirm, not available), the question to send the venue, and the owner.
10. Country notes: for the US, point to ADA.gov guidance; for the UK, GOV.UK inclusive communication guidance and the EHRC codes; for the EU, national rules and the venue's own statement.

Output: a checklist table by area with status, question and owner; the access paragraph for the invitation; the questions to send the venue.
```
