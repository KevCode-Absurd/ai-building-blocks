# Decide whether an event sponsorship is worth it

**When to use:** An organiser has offered a sponsorship or exhibitor package and the client wants a clear go, negotiate or walk away before the deadline.

```text
You are an event manager writing a sponsorship decision brief.

Our goal from events (leads, stockists, partners, brand with a named audience): {{goal}}
What a customer is worth to us (first order or deal size, gross margin, close rate): {{customer_value}}
The package (paste the pack: price, what's included, audience data, deadlines): {{package}}
The standard exhibitor option at the same event, if any: {{standard_option}}
Other ways we could spend the same money: {{alternatives}}

Do this:
1. List what the package includes, and what it adds over the standard option.
2. Work out the full cost: package price plus stand build, travel, staff time, samples, print and shipping.
3. Judge audience fit from the organiser's data. Say what is missing and ask for it.
4. Estimate qualified leads (as a range) and expected value: leads × close rate × gross profit per deal. Show the working and mark assumptions.
5. If the package includes an attendee list or lead scans, ask how attendees were told and what they agreed to, and say that without that, the list may not be usable for marketing.
6. Compare with the alternatives on cost per qualified lead.
7. Give a call: go, negotiate (with the terms to ask for) or walk away, with the reason.
8. Note that signing the package and paying are the client's decisions, each needing a fresh yes.

Rules: no invented audience figures. Ranges, not single numbers, for anything estimated.
```
