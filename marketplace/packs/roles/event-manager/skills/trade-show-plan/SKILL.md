# Trade show plan

Most trade show money is wasted after the show, when leads go cold. This skill plans the follow-up first, then a stand that feeds it.

## When to use

- Choosing which shows or sponsorship packages to buy this year.
- Preparing a stand and staff for a booked show.
- A first-time exhibitor wants to avoid the usual mistakes.
- Last year's leads were never followed up and the client wants to know why.

## What you need

1. **The goal**: leads, stockists, distributors, partners, press, or meetings with named accounts.
2. **What a customer is worth**: average first order or deal size, gross margin, usual close rate.
3. **Show and sponsorship packs**: prices, audience data, stand sizes, what's included, deadlines.
4. **Team**: who can staff the stand and who will follow up.
5. **Past show results**, if any.
6. **Target accounts** expected at the show, if the client runs an account programme.

**Access:** level 1 to read packs, past results and the CRM. Level 2 to draft the shortlist, booth kit, lead form and follow-up emails, and prepare exhibitor portal orders for the client to submit. Level 3 to publish the lead form, send approved pre-show meeting requests and post-show follow-ups to lawful lists, and log leads in the CRM. Booking a stand, signing a sponsorship, ordering paid services and paying anything need the client's fresh yes every time; payments stay with the client.

## Steps

### 1. Shortlist shows and packages
For each option: audience fit (share of visitors who match the buyer profile, from the organiser's data and from asking best customers which shows they attend), full cost (space, build or shell scheme, furniture, power, lead-retrieval app, travel, accommodation, staff time, samples, print, shipping), expected qualified conversations, cost per qualified lead, and the expected value (qualified leads × close rate × gross profit per deal). Compare with other ways to spend the same money. Call each go, maybe or skip, with the reason.

### 2. Check sponsorship packages
For each package, list what it adds over a standard stand: speaking slot, meetings, branding, attendee lists. For any list, ask how it was collected and what attendees agreed to, because it can only be used for marketing if collection and consent allow it. Price the difference against the extra qualified leads it could bring.

### 3. Write the follow-up first
Lead grades (hot: need, timing and budget now; warm: need but later; cold: information only), the email for each grade, who calls hot leads and by when (the time the team agrees), a 30-day follow-up plan, and the CRM fields each lead needs. Check each region's rules for post-show email.

### 4. Plan the booth
Booth goal (for example, a number of qualified conversations and booked meetings), layout notes (open front, demo point, a place to sit and talk), a 20-second opener, three or four qualifying questions, the demo or sample, and what to hand over. Book meetings with target accounts before the show.

### 5. Set lead capture
Fields: name, company, role, need, timing, next step, grade, consent choice for marketing, who follows up, notes. Use the organiser's app, a form on a tablet or the CRM's mobile app. Record the date and context of each card or scan.

### 6. Plan the staff
A rota with arrival times, breaks and handovers, roles (greeter, demo, qualifier), dress, and a one-page brief: goal, opener, questions, how to grade, what never to promise. Tired staff lose leads, so breaks go in the rota.

### 7. Run the results
At the end of the show: conversations, qualified leads by grade, meetings booked. At 30 days: meetings held, opportunities, orders. At the end of the normal sales cycle: revenue and show return against full cost. Feed this into next year's shortlist.

## Checks

- Full cost includes staff time, travel and shipping, not just the stand.
- Every option has a go, maybe or skip with a reason.
- Follow-up emails and owners exist before the stand design.
- Lead fields include grade, consent and owner.
- The rota has breaks.
- Results count qualified leads, meetings and sales, not scans.
- Booking, signing and paying are marked "fresh yes".

## Output format

1. Show and package comparison table with go, maybe or skip
2. Follow-up plan: grades, emails, owners, 30-day schedule
3. Booth kit: goal, layout notes, opener, questions, lead fields, rota, staff brief
4. Pre-show meeting plan for target accounts
5. Results template

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a sauce maker selling to delis and farm shops. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> | Show | Full cost | Buyer share (est.) | Qualified leads (est.) | Cost per qualified lead | Call |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Regional Fine Food Fair | $9,800 | High: mostly independent retailers | 40 | $245 | Go |
> | National Food Expo, shell stand | $21,500 | Mixed: many consumers | 35 | $614 | Skip |
> | Speciality Buyers Day, table | $3,200 | High, small | 15 | $213 | Go |
>
> **Opener:** "Do you stock any sauces made locally? Try this one: it's our best seller in farm shops."
>
> **Questions:** What do customers ask you for that you can't find? How do you usually order: direct or through a wholesaler? When do you plan your spring range?
>
> **Follow-up:** hot leads get a call from Mia on the Monday after; warm leads get the line sheet and sample offer by email on Tuesday; everyone gets a reorder reminder at day 30.
