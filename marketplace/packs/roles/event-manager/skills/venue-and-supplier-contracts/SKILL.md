# Venue and supplier contracts

Event contracts decide what the client owes when plans change. This skill makes those terms visible before anyone signs, so the client and their adviser can decide with eyes open.

## When to use

- Choosing between venues or suppliers.
- A contract or booking form has arrived and someone wants to sign it today.
- Numbers, dates or the format are changing and the client wants to know what it will cost.
- A supplier sends revised terms.

## What you need

1. **The event brief**: date, guest count, format, budget, access needs.
2. **Quotes and contracts** as PDFs or text, including booking forms and terms and conditions.
3. **What the client cares about most**: date flexibility, cost certainty, exclusivity, access.
4. **Who on the client's side signs, pays and checks contracts** (a lawyer, adviser or the owner).

**Access:** level 1 to read quotes and contracts. Level 2 to draft briefs, quote requests, the comparison and the contract summary. Level 3 to send approved, non-binding quote requests and briefs to agreed suppliers. Signing or accepting any contract, booking form or binding quote, paying or authorising a deposit, and cancelling or changing a booking need the client's fresh yes every time. Payments stay with the client.

## Steps

### 1. Write the brief
For each supplier type: what you need, numbers, dates and times (including load-in and load-out), venue access, dietary and access requirements, budget range, what's included, decision date and contact. Ask every supplier to price the same scope so quotes compare.

### 2. Compare quotes like for like
A table: supplier, price, what's included, what's extra, minimum numbers or spend, deposit and payment dates, cancellation terms, validity date, insurance held, access fit. Flag gaps where one quote excludes something the others include.

### 3. Summarise each contract
One page, in plain words, with clause references:
- **Parties, dates, spaces, times.**
- **Price and what it includes;** service charges and taxes.
- **Deposit and payment schedule** by date.
- **Cancellation scale:** what the client owes if it cancels at each point.
- **Attrition and minimums:** charges if fewer guests or rooms are used; food and drink minimum spend; final-numbers date.
- **Changes:** how date, numbers or format changes are handled and priced.
- **Force majeure:** what counts, who can invoke it, and what happens to money paid.
- **Exclusive suppliers:** catering, AV, security that must be used.
- **Insurance and liability:** cover the client must hold, indemnities, damage deposits.
- **Access:** step-free routes, accessible toilets, hearing support, seating, and what the venue confirms in writing.
- **Safety and security:** who holds which duties; in the UK, who is responsible under the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 if the event qualifies.
- **Data:** whether the venue or supplier receives attendee data, and on what terms.

### 4. Show the money at risk by date
A table of what the client would owe if it cancelled or cut numbers on each key date. This is often the most useful thing in the summary.

### 5. List questions and asks
Terms to clarify or negotiate: a longer hold, a softer attrition scale, a later final-numbers date, a force majeure clause that names relevant risks, written access confirmation. Phrase them so the client or adviser can send them.

### 6. Hand over for decision
Send the summary to the client and their adviser with a clear note: this is a summary, not legal advice; check binding terms with a qualified lawyer or adviser. Record who decided and when. Only then, with the client's fresh yes, return a signed form or pass the payment list to the client to pay.

### 7. Keep the record
File the signed contract, the payment schedule, and every change in writing. Diary each payment date and each cancellation threshold.

## Checks

- Every quote covers the same scope, or gaps are flagged.
- Each contract summary covers payment, cancellation, attrition, force majeure, exclusivity, insurance, access and safety.
- Money at risk is shown by date.
- The summary says it is not legal advice and names who checks it.
- No signing, paying or cancelling happens without a fresh yes.
- Nothing invents a term not in the document; missing terms are flagged as missing.

## Output format

1. Supplier brief per type
2. Quote comparison table
3. One-page contract summary per supplier
4. Money at risk by date
5. Questions to clarify or negotiate
6. Decision record

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real event: a 150-person industry day. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Venue contract summary: Venue A (clauses in brackets).**
> - Deposit £3,000 on signing; balance £7,000 due 30 days before (4.1).
> - Cancellation: 25% of total up to 90 days before, 50% to 30 days, 100% after (9.2).
> - Food and drink minimum £5,500; final numbers 10 working days before; charged on final numbers or actual, whichever is higher (6.3).
> - Force majeure lists fire, flood and government order; no mention of transport strikes (12).
> - In-house catering and AV only (7.1).
> - Step-free access to the main hall confirmed; the breakout room is up one flight with no lift [ask for an alternative room] (Schedule 2).
>
> | If we cancel on | We owe |
> |---|---|
> | 1 March (100 days before) | £2,500 |
> | 1 May (40 days before) | £5,000 |
> | 1 June (9 days before) | £10,000 |
>
> **Ask:** add transport strikes to force majeure; move final numbers to 5 working days; confirm an accessible breakout room in writing. Not legal advice: please have your adviser check clauses 9 and 12 before signing.
