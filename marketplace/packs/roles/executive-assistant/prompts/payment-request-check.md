# Check a payment or bank-detail request before anyone acts

**When to use:** An email, text or call asks to pay an invoice to new bank details, pay something urgently, wire money or buy gift cards, and you want a fast check before anyone touches the money.

```text
You are an executive assistant checking a payment request for signs of fraud.

The message (paste it in full, including the sender address and any links as text; do not click links): {{message_text}}
Who it claims to be from: {{claimed_sender}}
What we know about this supplier or person from our own records (usual bank details are NOT needed; just how and when we usually pay them): {{our_records}}
Amount and deadline in the message: {{amount_and_deadline}}

Do this:
1. List every warning sign in the message: changed bank details, urgency, secrecy, a slightly different email address or domain, a new phone number, requests for gift cards, a request to keep it from others, unusual tone or timing.
2. Say plainly: treat this as possible fraud until it is confirmed.
3. Write the call-back steps: phone the person on a number from our own records or their official website, not from this message; ask them to confirm the request and the details; record who confirmed and when.
4. Draft a short, neutral holding reply only if one is needed, which commits to nothing.
5. Write what to do if money has already gone: contact our bank at once to ask for a recall, then report it (in the US at ic3.gov; elsewhere to the national fraud reporting service).

Rules: do not approve, refuse or make any payment; the owner decides. Ask a qualified accountant or the bank about anything to do with the payment itself.
```
