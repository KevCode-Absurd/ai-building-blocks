# Admin system setup

## When to use

- Nobody can find the signed contract, the insurance certificate or last year's invoices.
- A renewal, licence, filing or review was missed, or nearly missed.
- A supplier, customer or contact list has duplicates, blanks and five spellings of the same company.
- Admin is moving from the owner to a new assistant or office manager.

Do not use it to file with government bodies, change account settings, or decide how long records must be kept. Filings and settings are the owner's actions, and retention is a question for a qualified accountant or adviser.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the folder tree, file names and the list to clean.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** create the new folders and templates in a working area, a cleaned copy of the list, and the admin calendar with reminders not yet switched on.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** move and rename files to the agreed map in batches, apply approved fixes to the live list, and switch on calendar reminders, logging every change. Deleting or overwriting anything needs a fresh yes each time.

## What you need

1. A screenshot or list of the current folder tree and twenty typical file names.
2. Who needs to find what, and how often (owner, staff, accountant).
3. The list or spreadsheet to clean, as an export, with personal data kept to the minimum.
4. Every recurring item the owner can think of: insurance, licences, leases, software subscriptions, domain names, tax and company filings, equipment servicing, staff reviews, contract end dates.
5. Where proof is kept today (inbox, drive, paper).

## Steps

### 1. Design the folder tree

No more than three levels. Top level by area of the business (for example Clients, Suppliers, Finance, Staff, Company, Marketing), second level by year or by name, third level only if it is needed. Write one line for each top-level folder: what goes in it and what does not. Keep staff and health records in a restricted folder.

### 2. Set the naming rule

One pattern for every file: date written year-month-day first, then a short description, then a version or status, for example `2026-11-06 [Client] quote v2`. Dates first means files sort in time order. No "final", "new" or "latest" in names; use v1, v2 and "signed".

### 3. Map the old to the new

A table of old locations to new folders, so nothing is lost. At level 3, move and rename the files to this map in small batches and log each move; otherwise the owner or their IT person moves them. Nothing is deleted without a fresh yes.

### 4. Clean the list

Work through the export and flag, do not delete:
- duplicates (same email, same phone, or same name and postcode),
- blanks in required fields,
- odd formats (phone numbers, dates, postcodes),
- inconsistent spellings of the same organisation,
- entries that look like test data.

For each fix, add a column saying which rule was used. Give the owner a cleaned copy and a list of the changes to approve. At level 3, apply the approved changes to the live list and keep the change log.

### 5. Build the admin calendar

For each recurring item: what it is, due date, how often, lead time (when to start), owner, where the proof is stored, cost if known, and what happens if it is missed. Sort by due date. Add a reminder at the lead time, not the due date. Mark tax, payroll and company filing items "confirm dates with your accountant".

### 6. Record retention

List the record types the business holds and mark each "keep period: confirm with your accountant". Give the official starting points: in the US the IRS publishes record-keeping periods, and UK companies can start with GOV.UK's company and accounting records page. Do not set deletion dates on your own.

### 7. Write the one-page guide

How the folders work, the naming rule with three examples, where the admin calendar lives, who owns it, and the weekly and monthly checks. Written so a new starter can follow it on day one.

## Checks before handing over

- The tree has no more than three levels, and every top-level folder has a one-line rule.
- The naming rule has examples and sorts correctly.
- No file was deleted without a fresh yes; every move and list fix is logged with its rule.
- Every calendar item has an owner, a lead time and a proof location.
- Tax, payroll, company filings and retention items say "confirm with your accountant" (ask a qualified accountant).
- Sensitive records sit in a restricted folder.

## Output format

1. Folder tree with one-line rules
2. Naming rule with examples
3. Old to new location map
4. Cleaned list and change log
5. Admin calendar (table)
6. Retention list to confirm with your accountant
7. One-page guide

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a six-person print shop in Toledo, Ohio. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Folder tree**
> - `Clients/2026/` client quotes, orders and proofs, one folder per client.
> - `Suppliers/` contracts, price lists and certificates, one folder per supplier.
> - `Finance/2026/` invoices, receipts, bank statements. Accountant has read access.
> - `Staff (restricted)/` contracts and reviews; owner and manager only.
>
> **Naming:** `2026-11-06 [Client] menu proof v2`, `2026-10-31 Paper supplier contract signed`
>
> | Item | Due | Lead time | Owner | Proof | Note |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Business insurance renewal | 1 Feb | 6 weeks | Dana | Company/Insurance | Get two quotes first |
> | Press service contract | 15 Mar | 4 weeks | Luis | Suppliers/[Supplier] | Check call-out response times |
> | Quarterly tax filings | Per accountant | 3 weeks | Dana | Finance/Tax | Confirm dates with your accountant |
