# Inbox triage and reply drafts

## When to use

- The daily or twice-daily inbox run for a busy owner.
- A backlog after a trip, illness or a busy launch.
- The owner wants replies that sound like them, ready to send with light edits.
- Before handing an inbox to a new assistant, to set the sorting rules.

Do not use it to pay or sign anything, or for legal letters, staff disciplinary matters or anything the owner has marked "always mine".

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** read and sort the inbox and calendar.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save each reply, decline and delegation note in the drafts folder, and add tentative calendar holds.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send replies, chasers and declines, and accept or move meetings, only for the contacts and kinds of email in the confirmed limits. Anything that commits the owner to a price, date or term, or touches money, legal or staff matters, still goes to the owner first. Log every send.

## What you need

1. The batch of emails: pasted text, an export, or delegated read access. Remove bank details and ID numbers before pasting.
2. Five to ten emails the owner wrote, to learn their voice: greeting, length, sign-off, how direct they are.
3. The owner's rules: who I may say yes to, what always needs them, what never gets a reply, who handles what (delegates).
4. This week's calendar or free times, if replies involve scheduling.
5. Any open decisions the owner has already made ("yes to the March conference, no to new suppliers this quarter").

## Steps

### 1. Learn the voice

From the owner's sent emails, note: usual greeting and sign-off, typical length, how they say no, how they ask for things, words they use often, words they never use. Write a five-line voice note and reuse it. Keep their voice; adjust tone to each reader's situation.

### 2. Sort every email

For each email, choose one call and give the reason in one line:
- **Reply:** the owner should answer, and I can draft it.
- **Delegate:** someone else on the team should handle it; draft a short handover.
- **Decline:** a polite no.
- **Wait:** nothing to do until a date or event; note when to look again.
- **File:** information only; say where it goes.

Rank within "Reply": money, safety and access first; then anything with a deadline in the next two working days; then the owner's key clients and team; then the rest.

### 3. Flag risks first

Put these at the very top, before any draft:
- Requests to change bank details, pay urgently, wire money or buy gift cards. Mark "possible fraud: confirm by phone on a known number, do not reply to this email".
- Legal letters, regulator contact, complaints threatening action, staff grievances: "owner only".
- Anything that looks like a login or password reset the owner did not ask for.

### 4. Draft the replies

For each "Reply":
- First line answers the main question.
- Answer every question in the email, in order. List them first so none is missed.
- Next step and when it will happen.
- Anything that commits the owner to a price, date, term or promise becomes a bracketed question at the top: [Owner: agree to 15 March delivery?]. Leave the line open in the draft.
- Use the owner's greeting, length and sign-off.

### 5. Draft declines and delegations

Declines: thank them, say no clearly in the first two lines, offer an alternative only if the owner has one. Delegations: a two-line note to the colleague with what is needed and by when, plus a one-line holding reply to the sender if the owner wants one.

### 6. Scheduling replies

Offer two or three times that sit next to existing meetings, not in the middle of focus blocks. Give times with the time zone. At level 3, accept or propose times for the kinds of meeting the owner has approved; otherwise draft the reply.

### 7. Hand back

Return the flagged list, then a ranked table, then drafts grouped by call, then a short list of decisions only the owner can make. At level 3, add the action log for this run: what was sent or moved, to whom, when, and the result.

## Checks before handing over

- Every email has one call and a reason.
- Every question in each email is answered in its draft, or is a bracketed owner question.
- No draft promises a price, date, term or action the owner has not approved.
- Every name, date, time zone, amount and reference matches the source.
- Payment, bank-detail and gift-card requests are flagged at the top.
- Nothing went out beyond the access level and the confirmed limits, and every live action is in the log.

## Output format

1. Flags (possible fraud, owner-only items)
2. Triage table (sender, subject, call, reason, deadline)
3. Draft replies, with bracketed owner questions at the top of each
4. Declines and delegation notes
5. Decisions only you can make (one line each)
6. Action log (level 3 only)
7. Voice note (first run only)

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Fernhill Landscapes, a garden design firm with eight staff in Guildford, UK; the owner is Priya.*

> **Flags**
> - **Possible fraud:** "Updated bank details for invoice 2231" from a supplier address with one letter changed. Do not reply. Confirm by phone on the number in your accounts system.
>
> | Sender | Subject | Call | Why |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Client, Oakfield House | Planting date? | Reply | Asks two questions; site visit Friday |
> | Trade show organiser | Exhibitor offer | Decline | You said no new shows this year |
> | Nursery supplier | Order 448 delay | Delegate to Tom | Tom runs orders |
> | Accountant | VAT return reminder | Wait to 1 Dec | Due 7 Dec; papers ready 1 Dec |
>
> **Draft to Oakfield House**
> [Priya: are you happy to commit to the week of 17 March for planting?]
> Hi Sam, thanks for checking. Yes, the hedging can go in before Easter. We are looking at the week of [17 March], and Tom will confirm the exact day by Friday. The trees need two people on site, so we will come in the van, not the car. Best, Priya
