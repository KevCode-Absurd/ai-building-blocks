# Compliance calendar

## When to use

- Nobody can show when the last fire drill, legionella check or gas service happened.
- Each contractor holds their own dates and nobody sees them together.
- An inspector or insurer asks for records and the team scrambles.
- A new site, new building or new manager takes over.
- The team suspects some checks are being done from habit, not from a risk assessment.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** risk assessments, written schemes, certificates, contractor reports, current calendar.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** build the calendar and PM schedules saved paused; draft reminder emails to competent persons and contractors.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** switch on the schedules, send approved reminders and book approved contractor visits within the cap, and file the evidence against each item.

## What you need

1. Each site's address, type and age (and whether it was built before 2000, for asbestos).
2. The risk assessments and schemes that exist: fire risk assessment, legionella risk assessment and written scheme, asbestos register and plan, lockout procedures.
3. Certificates and reports from contractors: gas, electrical, lifting, extinguishers, alarms, vehicles.
4. Who the competent person is for each risk, in-house or contractor.
5. Where the sites are (country, US state).

## Steps

### 1. List the risk areas per site

Water (legionella), fire, gas, electrical installation and portable equipment, lifting equipment, pressure systems, work equipment isolation, vehicles and forklifts, asbestos, plus anything the client's own risk assessment adds. Mark which apply.

### 2. Name the competent person and the source for each

For each item: who decides (responsible person, engineer, inspector), and where the frequency comes from (their written scheme, the maker, a regulation, the risk assessment). If there is no source, mark it [NEEDED: competent person to set] and do not fill a habit in.

### 3. Fill the dates from the sources

Reference points to check against the client's own documents:
- **Legionella (UK):** routine checks come from the site's written scheme. HSE's technical guidance sets targets such as hot sentinel outlets at least 50°C within one minute (55°C in healthcare), cold below 20°C within two minutes, monthly sentinel checks and weekly flushing of little-used outlets, as the risk assessment says. In the US, the CDC toolkit helps decide whether a building needs a water management program.
- **Fire (England and Wales):** the fire risk assessment is reviewed regularly and recorded; alarms and emergency lighting are checked regularly with faults recorded; at least one fire drill a year. In the US, extinguishers are visually checked monthly and maintained annually.
- **Gas (UK):** services by a Gas Safe registered engineer, as the maker's instructions require.
- **Portable electrical equipment:** frequency by risk, not automatically annual.
- **Lockout procedures (US):** each procedure inspected at least annually.
- **Vehicles:** UK safety inspections at the interval set for the fleet (typically 4 to 13 weeks); US commercial vehicles inspected at least annually and driver reports kept three months.
- **Asbestos (UK):** management plan reviewed at least every 12 months.
Check this with local rules for every site.

### 4. Build the calendar

A table and the same items in the CMMS: item, site, task, frequency, source, competent person, last done, next due, evidence required, status. Use fixed intervals so legal dates never drift.

### 5. Set the evidence rule

An item is done only when the evidence is filed: the temperature log, the engineer's report, the drill record. File the evidence against the item. Failures or actions in a report become work orders with an owner.

### 6. Remind and book

At level 3, send the approved reminder to the competent person or contractor ahead of each date and book approved visits within the cap. Anything above the cap, or a new contractor, needs a fresh yes.

### 7. Review monthly

Overdue items, items due in the next 60 days, open actions from reports, and any item still marked [NEEDED]. Report to the owner.

## Checks

- Every item names its competent person and source, or is marked [NEEDED].
- No frequency was filled in from habit.
- Legal dates use fixed intervals.
- Every completed item has evidence filed.
- Report actions became work orders with owners.
- Nothing says a system or site is safe or compliant; the calendar says what was done and by whom.
- The calendar carries the line: *Frequencies come from your competent persons and risk assessments; check this with local rules.*

## Output format

1. Risk areas per site
2. Calendar table (item, site, task, frequency, source, competent person, last, next, evidence, status)
3. Gaps marked [NEEDED]
4. Open actions from reports, as work orders
5. Monthly review template
