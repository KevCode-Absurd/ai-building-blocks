# Repair or replace review

## When to use

- One asset has had three or more repairs in a year.
- A large repair quote arrives for an old machine or vehicle.
- Downtime is hurting the business (a van off the road, a treadmill out of use).
- The owner wants to plan which assets to replace next year.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** work order history, invoices, fuel or energy costs, meter readings, quotes.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** write the review in the shared folder; draft a purchase request for the owner.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** file the approved review against the asset and update the register's planned replacement date. Buying stays with the owner.

## What you need

1. The asset's work order and invoice history (two or three years if possible).
2. Running costs: fuel or energy, consumables, contracts.
3. Downtime per repair, and what a day of downtime costs (hire cost, lost jobs, staff time), from the client.
4. Current meter reading and the maker's expected service life, if known.
5. The repair quote (if any), the replacement quote, and the resale or scrap value.
6. The period to compare (one, two or three years) and any discount rate the owner uses.

## Steps

### 1. Build the history

Table of repairs: date, fault, cost, downtime. Total by year. Note repeat faults and whether each was fixed or patched.

### 2. Price downtime

Downtime cost = downtime days × cost per day, using the client's cost per day. Show the sum. If the client has no figure, give a range they choose and mark it as theirs.

### 3. Project "keep"

For the comparison period: expected repairs (from the history, stated as an assumption), planned services, running costs, downtime cost, and the pending repair quote. Do not invent failure rates; if the history is thin, say so.

### 4. Project "replace"

Purchase or lease cost, minus resale of the old asset, plus planned services, running costs and expected downtime for the new one (warranty period noted), over the same period.

### 5. Compare

Side by side, year by year, with totals and the difference. If the owner uses a discount rate, show present values too. The library item "Fleet replacement cash comparer" can build the cash schedule for vehicles.

### 6. Name what else matters

Reliability for customers, safety concerns raised by the competent person, parts availability, warranty, staff time. List them; do not score them unless the owner gives weights.

### 7. Recommend and hand over

State which option costs less over the period and by how much, the assumptions that would change the answer, and what the owner decides. Buying, leasing and disposal decisions stay with the owner.

## Checks

- Every figure traces to an invoice, work order, quote or the client's own estimate.
- Downtime is costed, not ignored.
- Assumptions are listed and labelled.
- Both options cover the same period and the same costs.
- No safety verdict is given; safety concerns come from the competent person.
- The review carries the line: *This is a cost comparison, not financial advice; ask a qualified accountant about tax and finance.*

## Output format

1. Repair and downtime history table
2. Keep vs replace table by year, with totals
3. Assumptions and gaps
4. Other factors
5. Recommendation and the decisions for the owner

## Example of good output (short)

> **Illustrative structure, not a real business: a delivery firm; VAN-07 is a 2019 panel van with 168,000 miles. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.**
>
> **History (last 12 months):** four brake jobs, one clutch; repairs 2,140 pounds; downtime 9 days.
> **Downtime cost:** 9 days × 180 pounds hire = 1,620 pounds.
>
> | Two years | Keep VAN-07 | Replace |
> |---|---|---|
> | Repairs (assumed same as last year) | 4,280 | 0 (warranty) |
> | Services | 900 | 700 |
> | Downtime (assumed same) | 3,240 | 360 (2 days, assumed) |
> | Vehicle cost less resale | 0 | 24,500 − 4,000 = 20,500 |
> | **Total** | **8,420** | **21,560** |
>
> **Reading:** keeping costs 13,140 pounds less over two years on these assumptions. The answer changes if VAN-07 needs the gearbox the garage flagged (quote pending) or downtime doubles. Decision for the owner: approve the pending quote or plan replacement for the next financial year.
