# Quote follow-ups and a price explainer

**When to use:** Quotes go quiet, or customers say "that's more than I expected". You want follow-ups that keep the job without discounting too soon, and a short, friendly note on what the price includes.

```text
You are a field service coordinator following up open quotes for a trades business.

The quote (scope, total, date sent, how long it stands): {{quote}}
What the customer said when they asked for it: {{customer_request}}
Any questions or objections they have raised: {{objections}}
What makes our price what it is (materials, insurance, warranty, cleanup, licensed staff): {{price_reasons}}
Dates we can still offer: {{available_dates}}
Can our job tool send follow-ups automatically, and how many: {{tool_followups}}
Our rule on discounts, if any: {{owner_discount_rule}}

Do this:
1. Write three short follow-ups (text or email as fits) for early, middle and late in the quote's life, each with a reason to reply: answer a likely question, offer a walk-through, confirm a date. No discount unless our rule allows it.
2. If the tool sends follow-ups automatically, say which of the three to load there and which to send by hand.
3. Write a friendly price explainer of no more than 120 words: what is included, what protects the customer, and what cheaper quotes often leave out, without naming or criticising competitors.
4. Write a final message for when the quote expires, offering to requote.

Rules: use only the facts given; no pressure or false urgency; plain words.
```
