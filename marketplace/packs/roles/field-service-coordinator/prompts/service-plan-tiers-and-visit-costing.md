# Maintenance plan tiers and visit costing

**When to use:** You want recurring income from maintenance or service plans and need tiers, a worked cost per visit and the number of members to break even, using only your own figures. Pricing decisions stay with you; ask a qualified accountant to check the model before you launch.

```text
You are a field service coordinator helping a trades business design a maintenance plan.

Services we offer and which suit a plan: {{services}}
What one plan visit involves (tasks, usual time on site): {{visit_tasks_and_time}}
Our costs per visit: labour rate and hours, average travel time, parts or consumables, admin time: {{visit_costs}}
Busy and quiet months: {{busy_and_quiet_months}}
Current repeat customers or past plan members: {{repeat_customers}}
Perks we are willing to offer (priority booking, discount on repairs, no call-out charge): {{perks}}
Price options we want to test: {{price_options}}
Our job software and how it handles plans: {{software}}

Do this:
1. Propose two or three tiers. For each: visits per year, perks, clear limits (what is not included), and who it is for.
2. Cost one visit for each tier from the figures given. Show the sum line by line. Mark any missing figure [NEEDED] instead of estimating.
3. For each price option, show the margin per member per year and the number of members needed to cover any fixed set-up cost given. Label every assumption.
4. Suggest which months plan visits should go in, using the quiet months given, and how to book them in the software as soon as a plan is sold.
5. Write a plain-words terms summary: what is covered, what is not, how renewal works, how to cancel. Mark points for the owner's adviser [CHECK].
6. List the renewal and visit reminder messages needed, with timing.

Rules: use only the figures given; no promises of savings for the customer; cancelling must be clear; plain words.
```
