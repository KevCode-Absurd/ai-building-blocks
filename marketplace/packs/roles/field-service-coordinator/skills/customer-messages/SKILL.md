# Customer messages

## When to use

- Customers ring to ask "are you still coming?" or "what happens next?"
- No-shows and no-access visits are wasting technician time.
- The business has no review routine, or replies to reviews late or badly.
- Maintenance plan members are not being reminded about visits or renewals.
- A complaint needs a calm, specific reply.

Do not use it for marketing campaigns to people who have not agreed to them, or for legal letters.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** current templates and automation settings in the job tool, recent customer messages, reviews.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** templates saved but not switched on; draft replies to reviews and complaints.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** switch on approved templates and automations, and send booking, reminder, on my way, running-late, job-done, review-request, invoice-reminder and plan messages from approved templates, in the hours and to the customers the client set. Replies to formal complaints and to negative reviews or reviews naming staff need a fresh yes each time. Messaging a group the client has not approved before needs a fresh yes.

## What you need

1. The stages of the client's service, from enquiry to paid job and next service.
2. Their current messages, if any, and two or three of their own emails or texts so the new ones sound like them.
3. The job tool and what it can send automatically, by which channel, and its limits (Jobber texts, for example, are capped at 160 characters with no links or emoji, and text reminders only work in the US, UK and Canada).
4. Arrival windows, the cancellation and no-access policy, payment terms and the review link.
5. For the US: whether the texting number is registered with carriers and the website forms carry consent wording and opt-out instructions.

## Steps

### 1. Map the stages
Enquiry received, quote sent, quote accepted, booking confirmed, reminder, on my way, running late, no access, job done, invoice, payment received, review request, next service due, plan renewal. Mark which ones the tool sends automatically today.

### 2. Decide the channel and timing for each
Texts for time-sensitive messages (reminder, on my way, late), email for documents (quote, invoice, handover pack). Use the tool's own timing options: a reminder the day before at a set time, for example. Keep sending hours polite and within the client's rules.

### 3. Write each message to one pattern
Who it is from, what is happening, when, what the customer needs to do, and one way to reply. Short. Name the window, not a single time. Turn waits into known waits: give the reason for any change and the new time.

### 4. Fit the limits
Count characters for texts. Remove links where the tool does not allow them. Use the tool's variables (customer first name, window, technician name) and check each one fills in.

### 5. Write the review request the lawful way
Ask every customer the same way, after every completed job. No discount, prize or entry tied to a review, and nothing that asks for a good rating. Do not filter who gets the review link by their survey score: Google's policy bans selectively asking for positive reviews, and the US FTC says this kind of gating may break the FTC Act.

### 6. Write review replies
Positive: thank them, mention the specific job, name the team if the customer did. Negative: thank them, address the facts without arguing, say what was done or will be done, offer a direct contact, and keep personal details out. Replies are public and the reviewer is notified. Draft negative replies for the owner's yes.

### 7. Write complaint replies
Same day. Acknowledge, restate the problem in the customer's words, say what happens next and by when (a return visit, a call from the owner), and avoid admitting fault or promising refunds before the owner decides. Mark these for a fresh yes.

### 8. Set up and test
At level 3, switch on the templates in the tool, send a test to the client's own phone and email, check variables, and log the change. At level 2, hand over the message set with the settings to use.

## Checks

- Every message says what happens next and when.
- Texts fit the tool's limits; variables fill correctly in a test.
- Running-late messages go before the window ends.
- Review requests go to every customer, with no incentive and no gating.
- No message promises a refund, discount, warranty cover or arrival time the owner has not approved.
- US two-way texting is registered and opt-out wording is in place before texts are switched on.
- Complaint and negative review replies waited for the owner's yes.

## Output format

1. Message map: stage, trigger, channel, timing, automatic or manual.
2. Message set: each template with character count.
3. Review reply guide with three example replies.
4. Complaint reply template.
5. Setup log (level 3): what was switched on, when, test result.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a landscaping business with two crews. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Reminder (text, day before, 5 pm, 124 characters before the names fill in):** "Hi {first name}, [Business] here. Your hedge trim is tomorrow, 8-10 am. Please leave the side gate open. Reply C to confirm."
>
> **On my way (text):** "Hi {first name}, {technician} from [Business] is on the way and should arrive in the 8-10 am window."
>
> **Running late (text):** "Sorry {first name}, we're running about an hour behind after a longer job. New window 9-11 am. Reply 2 to move to Friday instead."
>
> **Review request (email, sent to every customer after each job):** "Thanks for having us. If you have a minute, a review on Google helps local people find us: [link]. If anything wasn't right, reply to this email and Dan will call you."
>
> **Negative review reply (draft, for owner's yes):** "Thank you for telling us about the clippings left on the drive, Ms Hart. That's not our standard. Dan has booked a return visit for Thursday morning to clear them, and you can reach him directly on the office number."
