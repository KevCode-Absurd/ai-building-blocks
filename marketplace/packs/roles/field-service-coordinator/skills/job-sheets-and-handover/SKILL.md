# Job sheets and handover

## When to use

- Jobs close with no photos, readings or signature.
- Customers ring after the job with the same questions ("is this noise normal?", "when is the next service?").
- Warranty or guarantee claims turn into arguments about what was promised.
- Cleaners or technicians do the same job differently.
- A call-back or re-clean needs a quick, recorded fix.

Do not use it to write safety rules, set maintenance intervals or decide what a warranty covers. Care steps come from the maker's instructions and the business's own written terms; safety points go to a qualified person.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** existing forms and checklists, past job notes and photos, call-back records, the business's warranty terms.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** checklist templates saved but not attached to jobs; handover pack drafts.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** switch on checklist templates for job types, attach them automatically to new jobs, and email completed checklists or handover packs to customers, within the client's rules. Any warranty decision on a claim needs a fresh yes.

## What you need

1. Job types and what a finished job looks like for each, from the owner or lead technician.
2. The checks and readings the business already records, and anything a maker or regulator requires (from the maker's manual or the owner, not invented).
3. The questions customers ask after jobs, from calls, emails and reviews.
4. The business's written warranty or guarantee terms, and the makers' warranties for installed products.
5. The job tool in use, and whether it can block finishing a job until required items are done.

## Steps

### 1. List what proof each job needs
For each job type: photos (before, during, after; serial plates; meter readings), measurements, test results, materials used, and the customer's sign-off. Ask "what would we need to show if this job were disputed in six months?"

### 2. Build the checklist in work order
Sections that follow the visit: arrive and check, protect the area, do the work, test, clean up, explain to the customer, sign-off. One action per line, starting with a verb. Mark required items. Use pass, flag or fail where a check can go wrong, and a photo field where proof matters.

### 3. Match the tool's behaviour
Check whether the tool enforces required items. In Housecall Pro, required items must be done before the job can be marked finished; in Jobber, a visit can still be completed with required questions blank and the gap is flagged. If the tool allows blanks, add a daily check of unfinished checklists to the office routine.

### 4. Write the handover pack
In this order: what we did (from the job record), how to use it, how to look after it (from the maker's instructions only), what is normal and what is not, what to do if something goes wrong, who to call and when, and the next service date. Readable in five minutes. Where safety matters, point to the maker's manual instead of writing new rules.

### 5. Summarise the warranty from the client's own terms
What is covered, for how long, what is not covered, what the customer must do (for example annual servicing), and how to claim. Quote the terms rather than reword promises. Never add cover the terms do not give. Mark points for the owner to check with their adviser; consumer law adds rights that a written warranty cannot remove (for example the EU's two-year legal guarantee on goods, or the UK requirement that services are done with reasonable care and skill).

### 6. Build the care and service schedule
By month or season, from the maker's recommended intervals and the business's plans. Each entry gets a short reminder message to send before it is due.

### 7. Write the call-back or re-clean process
Reply the same day, offer a return visit inside a set time the owner chooses, record the cause with photos, fix, sign-off, and a team note so it does not happen again. Track call-backs by job type and technician to find the checklist step that is missing.

### 8. Switch on or hand over
At level 3, attach the checklists to job types and set them to add automatically, then email completed checklists or packs to customers as the client's rules allow. At level 2, save drafts for the owner to review.

## Checks

- Every required item is something a technician can actually do and record on site.
- Care steps and intervals come from the maker's instructions or the owner, with the source noted.
- The warranty summary matches the written terms word for word on cover, length and exclusions.
- Safety points name the qualified person or the manual, not new advice.
- The handover pack can be read in five minutes by someone with no trade knowledge.
- Call-backs have an owner, a time to return and a cause recorded.

## Output format

1. Checklist per job type: sections, items, required marks, photo fields, sign-off.
2. Handover pack (one to two pages).
3. Warranty summary (one page), with [CHECK] marks for the owner.
4. Care schedule with reminder messages.
5. Call-back process and log columns.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Clearwater Window Cleaning, five cleaners.*

> **Checklist: residential window clean (outside and frames)**
> 1. Photo of the front of the house on arrival. *(required)*
> 2. Check for cracked or loose panes; photo any found and tell the customer before starting. *(pass / flag / fail)*
> 3. Clean glass, frames and sills, top floor first.
> 4. Wipe any drips on doors and conservatory roofs.
> 5. Photo of each elevation when done. *(required)*
> 6. Text the customer: "All done at [address]. Photos are in your account. Reply if anything is missed and we'll come back within [owner's chosen time]."
>
> **Warranty summary (from Clearwater's terms):** If you are not happy with any window, tell us within 48 hours and we will re-clean it free. This does not cover marks caused by rain after the clean, or windows we could not reach safely. **[CHECK]** Terms say "48 hours"; the website says "two days". Make them match.
