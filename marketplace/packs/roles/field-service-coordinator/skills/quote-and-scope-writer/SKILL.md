# Quote and scope writer

## When to use

- A new enquiry or site visit needs a written quote or estimate.
- Customers keep arguing about what was included.
- Work changes on site and needs a written change note before it starts.
- Open quotes are going quiet and need a follow-up.
- A repair estimate from a technician's notes needs to be put in plain words (fix now, fix soon, can wait).

Do not use it to set prices or margins, to decide whether work is safe or meets building codes, or to write contract terms. Those belong to the owner, a qualified person or a lawyer.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the price list, past quotes, the request and site notes in the job tool.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the quote as a draft in the job tool, unsent.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send follow-ups on quotes the client already approved, and turn an accepted quote into a job. Sending the quote itself needs a fresh yes every time, because once accepted it binds the business.

## What you need

1. The customer's request in their own words, and the address.
2. Site notes: measurements, rooms or areas, access, what is there now, photos described in text.
3. The client's price list or rate card: labour rates, material prices, call-out or minimum charges, markups if they use them.
4. The client's standard terms: deposit, stage payments, how long quotes stand, what happens if hidden problems are found.
5. Who supplies what (materials, skips, permits) and any parts the customer has bought.
6. For repairs: the technician's notes and their order of urgency.

If any of these are missing, ask. Do not fill gaps with guesses.

## Steps

### 1. List every task and material
Read the notes and list every task in the order the work will happen, using the customer's words for rooms and areas. Under each task, list materials and quantities from the notes. Mark anything you are unsure of [CHECK].

### 2. Ask about the usual gaps
Access and parking, working hours, waste removal, making good (patching, painting), permits or notifications, who moves furniture, water or power shut-off, pets, and what happens if something hidden is found. Each unanswered one becomes an exclusion or a question.

### 3. Price from the client's figures only
Build price lines: labour (hours times the client's rate), materials (from their list), extras, subtotal, tax if they charge it, total. Show the sum. If a figure is missing, leave the line blank and mark it [PRICE NEEDED]; never estimate a price.

### 4. Write exclusions and allowances
Exclusions: what the price does not cover ("making good of plaster after rewiring", "inside of oven", "parts not yet diagnosed"). Allowances: a set sum for items not yet chosen ("tiles at the stated allowance per square metre; you pay the difference"). Say how hidden problems are handled: stop, explain, written change note, then continue.

### 5. Add the terms the customer needs to see
Payment stages and deposit (from the client's terms), start date or lead time, how long the quote stands, what the customer must do (clear the area, provide access), and how to accept. If the sale is made in the customer's home, add the cancellation information the client's region needs: the US Cooling-Off Rule covers some home sales, the UK and EU give 14 days for many off-premises service contracts, and urgent repairs are treated differently. Mark this for the owner to confirm against local rules.

### 6. For repair estimates, sort by urgency
Group items using only the technician's words: "fix now" (safety or further damage), "fix soon", "can wait". For each: what it is, why it matters in one plain sentence, and the price. Say what happens if the customer declines an item. Mark every safety statement for the technician to confirm.

### 7. Write the follow-ups
Three short messages for an open quote, each with a reason to reply: answer a likely question, offer a site walk-through, confirm the start date is still available. No discount unless the owner sets one. If the job tool sends follow-ups automatically, match its settings (Jobber, for example, sends up to two, timed in days after sending).

### 8. Change notes on site
When work changes: what changed and why, the extra cost from the price list, the effect on time, and a line for the customer to approve before the work starts. Some places require written or documented approval before extra work, as California does for auto repair; follow the strictest rule that applies.

### 9. Hand over or send
At level 2, save the quote as a draft and list the open questions for the owner. With the owner's fresh yes for this quote, send it. At level 3, send the approved follow-ups and convert accepted quotes to jobs within the client's rules.

## Checks

- Every price, rate and quantity comes from the client's list or notes, or is marked [PRICE NEEDED].
- Scope is in the order the work happens, with no jargon the customer would not know.
- Exclusions, allowances and the hidden-problem process are written down.
- Payment stages, validity and how to accept are clear.
- Safety, permit and code points are marked for a qualified person.
- Cancellation wording is marked for the owner to check against local rules.
- No quote was sent without the owner's yes for that quote.

## Output format

1. Quote: header (business, customer, address, date, quote number), the job in one line, scope table (task, materials, labour, price), extras, subtotal, tax, total, exclusions, allowances, payment stages, validity, how to accept.
2. Open questions for the owner, each marked [CHECK] or [PRICE NEEDED].
3. Three follow-up messages with send timing.
4. Change-note template for this job.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a plumbing business with two plumbers. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Quote 1147: replace kitchen mixer tap and isolation valves**
> | Task | Materials | Labour | Price |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Turn off water, remove old tap | none | 0.5 h | [from rate card] |
> | Fit two new isolation valves | 2 valves (list price) | 0.5 h | [from rate card] |
> | Fit customer-supplied mixer tap, test for leaks | none | 1 h | [from rate card] |
>
> **Not included:** the tap itself (you are supplying it), repairs to the worktop or cupboard base if the old fittings are seized, disposal of the old sink.
> **If we find a problem:** we stop, show you, and send a written change note with the price before doing more.
> **Valid for:** 30 days (from the business's standard terms). **To accept:** reply "yes" or tap Approve in the quote email.
>
> **Follow-up, day 3:** "Hi Sam, did the quote for your kitchen tap make sense? Happy to explain any line. We still have Thursday morning free."
>
> **[CHECK]** Is the customer's tap the 3/8-inch type in the photo? Confirm before sending.
