# Training interview: field service coordinator

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share customer names, addresses, access codes, staff details or prices from real clients; anonymise every story.

1. **Which trades or services have you run the office for (plumbing, heating, cleaning, landscaping, auto repair), how many people were in the field, and what was your job title?**
   Why it matters: sets the kind of business the agent is built for and the scale it should expect.

2. **Walk me through what happens from the moment a new enquiry comes in to the moment the job is paid. Where does it usually get stuck?**
   Why it matters: the expert's own flow becomes the agent's playbook, and the sticking points become its first checks.

3. **How do you decide whether to quote from photos or book a site visit?**
   Why it matters: this judgement saves or wastes technician time and is rarely written down.

4. **Show me a quote you are proud of, anonymised. What makes it hard to argue with later?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a model of scope, exclusions and wording that holds up.

5. **Which exclusions or "hidden problem" clauses have saved you an argument? Tell me about one job.**
   Why it matters: real exclusions are the difference between a quote and a dispute.

6. **How do you handle extra work found on site? Who approves it, and how is that recorded?**
   Why it matters: change-order habits decide whether invoices get paid without a fight.

7. **What arrival windows do you promise, and why those? What happens when a technician is running late?**
   Why it matters: window size and late-running habits drive customer trust and the agent's message timing.

8. **How do you build tomorrow's schedule? What do you look at first: skills, area, parts, the customer's history?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's matching order, which tools only partly automate.

9. **Tell me about the worst day you had on the dispatch board. What did you move first, and who did you call?**
   Why it matters: a real bad day shows the expert's priorities under pressure.

10. **What must a technician record on site before a job counts as finished? How do you make sure they do it?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's checklist standard and how to enforce it in the client's tool.

11. **What do customers ring about after a job, and what have you written to stop those calls?**
    Why it matters: call-back reasons become handover pack sections.

12. **Which customer messages do you send at each stage, and which one made the biggest difference when you added it?**
    Why it matters: shows which messages earn their place and how the expert words them.

13. **How do you ask for reviews, and how do you reply to a bad one? Show me a reply you would send again.**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a voice for reviews that stays within review rules.

14. **Have you sold or run maintenance plans? What made members renew, and what made them leave?**
    Why it matters: plan retention depends on habits that rarely appear in software guides.

15. **How do you make sure plan visits are actually booked and done?**
    Why it matters: the commonest failure of plans is visits nobody schedules.

16. **Which job management software have you used, what did you rely on most, and what did you work around?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent which tool features to trust and where to add manual checks.

17. **Which messages or actions would you happily let an assistant send on its own, and which must always come to you first?**
    Why it matters: sets realistic level 3 limits and the fresh-yes list for this expert's clients.

18. **When have you had to say no to a customer or a technician? How did you word it?**
    Why it matters: firm, polite refusals are a core office skill the agent needs.

19. **What consumer rules have caught out businesses you worked with: cancellations, warranties, reviews, estimates? Where do you send people to check?**
    Why it matters: shows where the expert points to official sources instead of guessing.

20. **What would you check on a new coordinator's first week of quotes and schedules before trusting them?**
    Why it matters: the expert's review list becomes the agent's quality bar.
