# Driver-based model

## When to use

- A business needs its first real 12-month budget.
- The year has changed and the budget needs a reforecast.
- A lender, investor or board asks for a three-year plan (the owner sends it).
- The owner wants to test a decision: a hire, a price change, a new site, a big contract.

It does not value the business, advise on raising money or set tax rates. Tax lines use the rate the accountant gives. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before the model goes to a lender or investor.

## What you need

1. What the model is for and who will read it.
2. Actuals: profit and loss by month for the last 12 to 24 months, and the latest balance sheet. For a new business, the plan and price list instead.
3. How the business makes money: price, units, customers, orders, hours, sites, contracts.
4. Costs: direct costs per unit or percent of sales, people (roles and pay bands, not names), rent, tools, marketing, loans.
5. Payment terms: how fast customers pay and suppliers are paid, stock held.
6. Today's cash and debt from the statements.
7. Tax rate or method from the accountant, if a tax line is needed.
8. Goals and limits: targets, a cash floor, loan covenants if any.

**Access:** level 1 to read actuals and any existing model; level 2 to build in a copy of the workbook or a draft version in your planning tool; level 3 to load the approved version into your planning or accounting software, within your limits. Overwriting an approved budget always needs a new yes.

## Steps

### 1. Lay out the workbook

Sheets: Cover (purpose, version, date, owner), Inputs, Actuals, Calculations, Outputs (profit and loss, cash, balance sheet if needed), Scenarios, Checks. Logic flows one way: inputs to calculations to outputs. Input cells have a fill colour and a label. No numbers typed inside formulas.

### 2. Study the actuals

Monthly trends, seasonality, one-offs (strip them out and list them), costs that grew faster than sales. Tie the totals to the accounting software.

### 3. Choose the drivers

For each revenue line, the fewest drivers that explain it: price times units, customers times spend, hours times rate times utilisation, sites times sales per site. For costs: per unit, percent of sales, per head, or fixed. One driver per row, with a unit.

### 4. Build revenue first

Month by month for the budget year (and the first 18 months of a multi-year model, then quarterly). Apply seasonality from the actuals. New products or sites start in the month they open, with a ramp the owner agrees.

### 5. Build costs and people

Direct costs from their drivers. A hiring plan by role and start month, with pay band and on-costs as the accountant or payroll provider gives them. Overheads by line, each with an owner. One line for the unexpected, sized by the owner.

### 6. Roll up profit, then cash

Profit and loss by month. Then cash: profit adjusted for when customers pay, when suppliers are paid, stock, loan repayments, equipment, tax payments and owner drawings. Show closing cash every month and the low point. Runway, if relevant: months until cash reaches the floor at the current plan.

### 7. Build the cases

Plan, cautious and stretch. Change only the few drivers that matter (price, volume, a hire date, payment days) and name each change. One table shows revenue, profit, cash low point and runway side by side.

### 8. Test sensitivity

Move each main input up and down by an amount the owner agrees is plausible, one at a time. Rank the inputs by their effect on the cash low point. List the top three: those are what the owner should watch and find out more about.

### 9. Write the assumptions page

Every input: value, unit, source (actuals, quote, owner's estimate, guess), date and confidence (known, estimated, guessed). Guessed inputs in the top three get a note on how to firm them up.

### 10. Build the checks

Balance sheet balances (if built), cash in the model ties to the cash flow, every row sums across, no negative cash without a flag, scenario switch works. A master check shows on every sheet. Only once every check passes and the owner approves the version do I load it into the live tool (level 3), and I log what I loaded and where.

## Checks

- Actuals tie to the books; opening cash ties to the bank.
- Inputs, calculations and outputs separate; no hardcoded numbers in formulas.
- The master check passes.
- Cases differ only by named drivers.
- The top three sensitivities are named.
- Tax lines use the accountant's rate, marked as such.
- The cover says: *A plan, not a promise. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before sharing with lenders or investors.*

## Output format

1. Cover: purpose, version, date, owner, how to use.
2. Inputs sheet.
3. Monthly profit and loss and cash (and balance sheet if needed).
4. Scenario table: revenue, profit, cash low point, runway for each case.
5. Sensitivity table, top three inputs.
6. Assumptions page with sources and confidence.
7. Check sheet.
8. A one-page summary for the owner: what the plan says, what could break it and what to watch monthly.
