# Training interview: FP&A analyst

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client names, figures that could identify a client, or personal pay data; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of businesses and sizes do you plan for, in which countries, and what decisions do your numbers usually feed?**
   Why it matters: business type, region and the decisions at stake set the agent's defaults and the limits of its advice.

2. **Walk me through the last budget you built, from the first conversation to sign-off.**
   Why it matters: shows the real order of work, who was involved and where the time went.

3. **How do you choose the drivers for a model, and how many is too many?**
   Why it matters: driver choice is the core skill; the expert's rules keep the agent's models small and useful.

4. **How do you lay out a spreadsheet model so someone else can update it next month?**
   Why it matters: captures layout, colour, checks and naming habits the agent can copy.

5. **Tell me about a model error that reached a client or a board. How was it caught, and what check did you add?**
   Why it matters: real failures teach the agent which checks matter most.

6. **How do you build a 13-week cash forecast, and how do you decide when a customer will really pay?**
   Why it matters: payment timing is where cash forecasts go wrong; the expert's method is reusable.

7. **How do you backtest your forecasts, and what did a backtest teach you?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert measures and improves forecast quality instead of claiming accuracy.

8. **How do you explain a variance when the data does not prove the cause?**
   Why it matters: records the expert's line between evidence and story, which the agent must keep.

9. **Show me how you do a price, volume and mix bridge, and when it misleads.**
   Why it matters: captures the formulas and the traps, such as mixing units that are not comparable.

10. **How do you keep the budget, the target and the forecast apart when the owner wants one number?**
    Why it matters: separating them is the main lesson from budgeting research; the expert's script makes it work in practice.

11. **How do you build scenarios that are useful, not just plus and minus ten percent?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent to change named drivers with a story, not every number at once.

12. **Which SaaS or subscription metrics do you trust, and how do you define them?**
    Why it matters: definitions differ by tool; the expert's choices become the agent's defaults.

13. **When do you use a published benchmark, and when do you refuse to?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's rules for comparing like with like and naming the source and date.

14. **How do accounting changes, such as lease or revenue rules, show up in your forecasts and covenants?**
    Why it matters: captures how the expert works with the accountant and restates comparisons.

15. **Where do you draw the line between planning and investment, valuation or tax advice? Give an example of a question you passed on.**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's safety line in the expert's own words.

16. **What do you put on the first page of a report for an owner, and what do you leave out?**
    Why it matters: records the expert's writing style: lead with the answer, then the why, then the decision.

17. **How do you handle an owner who wants a forecast to show a number for a lender or investor?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert stays honest under pressure and documents assumptions.

18. **Which tools do you use, and what would you tell a small business to start with?**
    Why it matters: feeds access.md with the expert's real tool advice.

19. **How do you use AI tools in your models today, and how do you check what they changed?**
    Why it matters: AI can now edit workbooks; the expert's review habits keep models safe.

20. **Describe a time the numbers changed a decision. What did you show, and what happened?**
    Why it matters: a real story shows how the expert turns analysis into action, which the agent should copy.
