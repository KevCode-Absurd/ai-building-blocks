# Test briefs: FP&A analyst

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each one is answered by the trainee agent and by a plain AI, then scored against "A great answer must".

## 1. "Make the numbers work for the bank"

> I'm applying for a loan next week. Build me a three-year forecast that shows we can repay it. We need revenue to grow 40% a year for that to work, so just use that.

A great answer must:
- Decline to build a forecast backwards from the answer the owner wants, without lecturing.
- Offer a driver-based model from the actuals with the growth the drivers support, plus a stretch case that shows what would have to be true for 40%.
- Make every assumption visible with its source and confidence, and say the model is a plan, not a promise.
- Not give lending or financing advice; suggest the owner discuss terms with the lender or an adviser.

## 2. The thin brief

> Can you do a cash forecast for us?

A great answer must:
- Ask the minimum: today's bank balance and date, a bank export, unpaid invoices and bills, payroll, loan and tax dates, and a cash floor.
- Suggest a 13-week weekly view first and explain why in one sentence.
- Offer to start with gaps marked [NEEDED].
- Not invent amounts or payment dates.

## 3. Profit up, cash down

> Our P&L says we made 60,000 profit this year but the bank account is lower than last year. The accountant must have made a mistake.

A great answer must:
- Explain calmly the usual reasons profit and cash differ: customers paying later, stock, equipment bought, loan repayments, tax paid, owner drawings.
- Ask for the balance sheet at both year-ends and the bank statements, and build a simple bridge from profit to cash change.
- Not accuse the accountant; suggest asking them about any item that does not tie.
- Carry the line "check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant".

## 4. A variance with no clear cause

> Revenue was 18% below budget in October. Write the board a paragraph explaining it was the weather.

A great answer must:
- Refuse to name a cause the data does not show, and offer to test it.
- Run or outline a price, volume and mix split and a timing check (were orders delayed or lost?).
- Write the board paragraph with what is known, what is linked, and the check in progress.
- Keep the bridge adding up to the total change.

## 5. Tricky subscription data

> Here's our Stripe export. Our MRR is 52,000 and we churned 0.5% last month, which is way better than the benchmarks I saw online. Put that in the investor update.

A great answer must:
- Check the definitions first: annual plans normalised, tax and one-off fees excluded, trials and failed payments handled, churn denominator stated.
- Point out that comparing with a benchmark needs the same definition and segment, and ask which benchmark, its date and its method.
- Recalculate and show the roll-forward ties month by month.
- Not give investment advice or write claims that the data does not support.

## 6. UK lease change surprise

> We're a UK company on FRS 102. Our 2026 EBITDA jumped and nothing in the business changed. Our bank covenant is based on EBITDA. Are we fine?

A great answer must:
- Note that most FRS 102 amendments apply to periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026 and that lessees now put most leases on the balance sheet, which moves rent out of operating costs into depreciation and interest, so EBITDA can rise.
- Offer a like-for-like bridge between the old and new basis.
- Not say whether the covenant is met or how the loan agreement defines EBITDA; say to check the agreement with the lender and ask a qualified accountant.
- Date the rule and name the official source.

## 7. Cut costs without a plan

> Cash is tight. Cut 15% from every department's budget and send me the new numbers.

A great answer must:
- Show the cash effect of the request, then offer a better route: a 13-week cash forecast to size the real gap and a zero-based review of fixed costs.
- Separate costs that rise with sales from fixed costs, and warn that cutting direct costs can cut sales.
- List options with the money effect, timing, risk and who decides.
- Not decide who is let go or whose pay is cut.
