# Annual budget cycle

**Trigger:** Two to three months before the financial year starts, or when the owner asks for a new plan after a big change (a new site, a lost customer, new funding). Repeat each year.

**Access:** Set at the start, per tool (see `access.md`). Steps marked level 2 or level 3 need that level in the named tool; at a lower level I hand you the files to load yourself. Until you choose, I work at level 1.

## Steps

1. **Agree the purpose and calendar (week 1).** Who the budget is for, what decisions it must support, the deadline, and who owns each area of spending. Agree that the budget (the plan), the target (the stretch goal) and the forecast (the expected outcome) are kept as separate numbers. *Approval: the owner signs off the calendar and owners.*
2. **Collect the actuals (week 1 to 2, level 1).** Monthly profit and loss for the last 12 to 24 months and the latest balance sheet, tied to the books. Ask the bookkeeper to close the latest month first.
3. **Review fixed costs from zero (week 2).** Run the `zero-based-cost-review` prompt on overheads and subscriptions. *Approval: the owner decides every keep, reduce or stop; I contact no supplier.*
4. **Build the driver model (week 2 to 4, level 2 in a copy or a draft version).** Run the `driver-based-model` skill or the `annual-budget-from-actuals` prompt: drivers, revenue, costs, hiring plan by role and start month, profit, cash and checks.
5. **Collect owners' inputs (week 3 to 4).** Each spending owner reviews their lines and assumptions and proposes changes with reasons. Keep a log of who changed what.
6. **Build cases and test sensitivity (week 4).** Plan, cautious and stretch, changing only named drivers; the top three inputs by effect on the cash low point. At level 2, save each case as a draft scenario in the planning tool.
7. **Check cash all year (week 4).** Turn the monthly plan into a cash view. If cash falls below the floor in any month or case, list options (timing of hires, payment terms, facilities). *Approval: financing decisions are the owner's, with their adviser.*
8. **Check region items (week 4).** Name the accounting framework and any changes taking effect in the year (for example UK FRS 102 for periods from 1 January 2026, IFRS 18 from 1 January 2027 where it applies). Ask the accountant how they affect reported lines and covenants, and restate the comparison year if needed. *Approval: accounting treatment is the accountant's call.*
9. **Present and agree (week 5).** A one-page summary, the scenario table, the top three risks and what to watch monthly. *Approval: the owner approves the final budget; a lender or board version always needs a new yes before it is sent.*
10. **Set up the year (level 3).** At level 3, load the approved budget into the planning or accounting software and set it as the fixed reference for the `monthly-performance-review` workflow; set the first reforecast date. At a lower level, I hand over the import file and the owner or their team loads it. *Approval: loading the approved budget is on the one-time action list; overwriting an earlier approved budget needs a new yes.*

## Outputs

- Budget calendar and line owners
- Zero-based cost review decisions
- Driver-based budget with assumptions page and checks
- Plan, cautious and stretch cases and sensitivity table
- Monthly cash view for the year
- One-page summary for the owner (and board or lender version if asked)
- Action log of everything done in the planning and accounting tools (what, where, when, result)

## Needs the client's approval

**Runs at level 3 within your limits, after one confirmation.** Before the first live action, I show the list (save draft versions and scenarios, load the approved budget, set the reforecast date) with its limits: which tools and versions, which kinds of change, and until when. After your yes, I do these and log each one.

**Always needs a new yes.** The calendar and owners, every cost decision, every assumption change, the final budget, any version sent outside the business, overwriting or deleting an approved version, and anything that moves money. A budget is a plan, not a promise; check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant about tax and accounting treatment.
