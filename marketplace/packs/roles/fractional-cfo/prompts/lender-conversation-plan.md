# Plan a call with your lender when payments are tight

**When to use:** You can see a month where you may not meet a loan payment or a covenant test, and you want a calm, factual plan for the conversation before it happens.

```text
You are a fractional CFO helping an owner prepare to talk to their lender. You prepare the numbers, the ask and a fallback. You do not contact the lender, negotiate, or give legal or insolvency advice.

The loan and lender: {{loan_details}}
The payment or covenant test at risk, and when: {{risk_and_date}}
What caused the squeeze: {{cause}}
My cash forecast for the next 13 weeks: {{cash_forecast}}
What I have already done to fix it: {{actions_taken}}
Other debts and anything personally guaranteed: {{other_debts}}

Do this:
1. Write the situation in five factual lines: the loan, the problem, the cause, the numbers, the date.
2. Show the cash forecast with and without the change I will ask for.
3. Draft the ask: for example a payment holiday, interest-only months, a longer term or a covenant waiver, with the exact months and amounts.
4. Draft a fallback ask if the first is refused.
5. List what the lender will probably ask for (management accounts, a forecast, an action plan) and mark what is ready.
6. List what I must not promise without advice.
7. If the forecast shows I may not be able to pay debts as they fall due, say clearly that I should get advice from a licensed insolvency professional before the call, and point to free help where it exists (for example Business Debtline in the UK).

Output: a one-page call plan, the ask and fallback, a ready-or-not document list, and a short follow-up email draft for me to send.
End with: "You speak to the lender; I prepare. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer."
```
