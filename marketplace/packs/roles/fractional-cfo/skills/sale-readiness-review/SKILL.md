# Sale readiness review

## When to use

- An owner wants to sell in one to three years and asks "how ready are we?"
- A broker has said buyers will ask for many documents.
- An owner has had an approach and wants to know what a buyer will find.
- Yearly, to track progress against the preparation plan.

I prepare the business and the evidence. The broker or a qualified valuer values it; the lawyer and accountant advise on structure and tax. In the US, the SBA's guide and the IRS asset sale form are useful starting points; in the UK, ask about Business Asset Disposal Relief. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer before acting on any of it.

## What you need

1. Three years of accounts and the current year's monthly figures.
2. Customer list with sales by year (names can be coded), and contracts for the largest customers.
3. Supplier list with spend, and key supplier contracts.
4. Staff list by role, with length of service and who holds which key skills or relationships (no names needed).
5. Leases, licences, permits, insurance, IP registrations, and any loans or guarantees.
6. A description of the owner's week: what only they do.
7. The owner's timing, goals after the sale, and who is advising them.

**Access:** level 1 to read files and folders; level 2 to build the document index and folder structure in a private area; level 3 to file and rename documents in the folders you name. Giving a broker or buyer access to the folders needs your fresh yes each time.

## Steps

### 1. Write down the goal

Timing, what the owner wants after the sale (stay on, leave at once, hand to family or staff), and the advisers in place. These shape which fixes matter.

### 2. Score the seven areas

Score each from 1 (a buyer would walk away or cut the price hard) to 5 (a buyer would find nothing to question), with the evidence:
- **Finances:** accounts tie to the bank; monthly figures are on time; profit trend; adjusted profit has proof (run the `quality-of-earnings-review` skill for the add-backs).
- **Customers:** concentration, contracts, renewal dates, change-of-ownership clauses, churn.
- **Staff:** key people, contracts, how long they've stayed, who would leave with the owner.
- **The owner's role:** sales, skills, supplier and banking relationships only the owner holds.
- **Contracts:** leases (length left, transfer terms), supplier agreements, licences.
- **Systems:** written processes, software the team can run without the owner.
- **Records:** corporate records, share register, filings up to date, disputes, compliance.

### 3. Build the document index

Group by area. For each document a buyer usually asks for: present, missing or out of date; where it is; who owns getting it. Start this first, because missing records take longest to rebuild.

### 4. Map owner dependence

List each thing only the owner does, why it matters to a buyer, who could take it over, and how long the handover takes. Write each as the question a buyer will ask: "Who holds your top three clients?"

### 5. Rank the fixes

By how much each would worry a buyer and how long it takes. Quick wins (filing gaps, missing contracts) go first; slow fixes (moving client relationships, reducing concentration) start now because they need a track record.

### 6. Write the 12-month plan

Month by month: action, owner, cost, and the evidence it will produce for a buyer. Mark where an accountant, lawyer or broker must be involved.

### 7. Draft the one-page profile (if asked)

What the business does, customers by type, team, results and strengths, with no confidential names, for the broker to use.

## Checks

- Every score has evidence, not opinion.
- The index marks missing items with an owner and a date.
- Owner dependence items are written as buyer questions.
- The plan has dates, owners and costs, and builds a track record for slow fixes.
- No valuation, no price talk, no tax or legal advice.
- The output says: *I prepare; I don't value. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.*

## Output format

1. Readiness scorecard: area, score, evidence, top gap.
2. Document index: area, document, status, location, owner, date.
3. Owner dependence map.
4. Ranked fixes.
5. 12-month plan by month.
6. Questions for the broker, accountant and lawyer.

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real business: an IT services firm with 15 people, whose owner plans to sell in two years. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> | Area | Score | Evidence | Top gap |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Finances | 4 | Accounts tie; monthly close by day 10 | Add-backs undocumented |
> | Customers | 2 | Two clients are 48% of sales; both month-to-month | No contracts |
> | Staff | 3 | Two engineers over 8 years' service | No written roles |
> | Owner's role | 1 | Owner leads every account review and holds the supplier relationships | Everything |
> | Contracts | 3 | Office lease ends in 14 months | Renewal terms unknown |
> | Systems | 3 | Ticketing system used by all | Processes not written |
> | Records | 4 | Filings up to date | Share register not checked |
>
> **Buyer question 1:** "Who holds your two biggest clients?" Today: you. **Plan:** the service manager leads both account reviews from March, with you attending until September; signed 12-month contracts by June.
>
