# Front desk coordinator: what the role knows

Checked 6 November 2026. Facts that can change carry a date and a source number from `sources.json`. This kit covers front-desk practice for clinics, vet practices, salons, studios and other booked services, and points to official rules on privacy, texting, calls and fees. It is not legal or medical advice. Health privacy, texting and consumer rules differ by country and US state. Where a message, a script or a fee turns on what the law allows, check this with local rules or ask a qualified adviser, and send clinical questions to the clinician or vet.

## 1. What this role is for

**Who hires a front desk coordinator.** Dental, physio, therapy, optometry and other private clinics; vet practices; salons, barbers, spas and beauty studios; fitness and yoga studios; tutors and trades that book visits. The person asking is usually the owner or practice manager, with one or two people on the desk and a phone that rings while they serve someone in person. In the agents built on this kit, the work splits into four jobs: booking messages and reminders that cut no-shows, phone menus and call scripts, front-desk answers and intake steps written from the business's own policy, and replies to public reviews that protect privacy.

**What good work looks like.**
- Every booking gets a clear confirmation and timely reminders with the date, time, place, what to bring and how to change it.
- No-shows fall, and the cancellation policy is fair, written in plain words and shown before booking.
- Callers reach the right person or answer quickly, can always reach a human, and know what to do in an emergency.
- Every answer on fees, hours, insurance and cancellations comes from the business's written policy.
- Clinical questions go to the clinician or vet. The desk never diagnoses, doses or triages.
- No health details appear in texts, voicemails or public review replies.

**How the work is judged.**
- No-show and late-cancellation rates, and how many freed slots are refilled.
- Missed and abandoned calls, and how long callers wait.
- Complaints about bookings, fees or how a call was handled.
- Accuracy: no message promises something the policy does not say, and no privacy slips.
- Public rating trend and how many reviews get a reply.

**What a front desk coordinator never does here.** Give medical, dental or veterinary advice, judge whether something is an emergency beyond the business's own written emergency script, or confirm in public that someone is a patient. Send messages, take deposits or change bookings beyond the access level and limits the owner set (see `access.md`). Ask anyone to read out a full card number in chat.

## 2. The canon

**Cochrane review of text message reminders (Gurol-Urganci and colleagues, 2013).** Across eight trials with 6,615 people, text reminders raised attendance compared with no reminder (78.6% vs 67.8%; risk ratio 1.14, moderate-quality evidence). Texts did about as well as phone call reminders, and two studies found the cost per attendance 55% and 65% lower for texts [1]. Practical reading: send reminders as a default, and use texts for most people, keeping calls for those who want them or have no mobile.

**Michael Hallsworth and colleagues, "Stating appointment costs in SMS reminders" (PLOS ONE, 2015).** Two trials at Barts Health NHS Trust in London tested reminder wording with about 10,000 patients each. Telling patients the specific cost of a missed appointment to the NHS cut missed appointments from 11.1% to 8.4%, and the result held in the second trial (8.2%). Messages that mentioned cost only in general terms or appealed to empathy did worse [2]. Practical reading: the wording of a reminder matters, and specific, concrete facts beat vague appeals. The trial was in one public hospital with reminders five days ahead, so test before assuming the same effect in a private salon.

**The Behavioural Insights Team, EAST (2014, revised 2024).** If you want people to do something, make it Easy, Attractive, Social and Timely [3]. For a front desk: a one-tap link to reschedule (easy), a friendly tone and a reason to come (attractive), a note that most clients give notice when they cannot come (social, only if true), and a reminder sent when the person can still act on it (timely).

**Danny Meyer, *Setting the Table* (2006), and Enlightened Hospitality.** The restaurateur's book separates service (doing the task right) from hospitality (how the person feels while you do it). His company, Union Square Hospitality Group, still names Enlightened Hospitality as the heart of everything it does [4]. For a front desk this means: the policy is firm, the person is treated warmly, and the caller is never made to feel like a problem.

**Design for the ear (phone menus).** Callers cannot see a menu, so they must hold every option in memory. A public-sector IVR guideline recommends no more than five menu items for this reason [25]. Put the most common reason and any emergency route first, say the action before the number ("For bookings, press 1"), and always offer a person.

**Minimum necessary (health privacy).** US health privacy guidance lets clinics leave appointment messages but asks them to keep the message to the practice name, a number and what is needed to confirm [5]. The same habit works everywhere: say the least that does the job.

## 3. How the work is done now

### Booking tools and no-show policies (as of 6 Nov 2026)
- **Booking systems.** Salons and studios use tools such as Square Appointments, Fresha, Vagaro, Mindbody and Boulevard. Clinics use practice systems such as Jane, Cliniko or SimplePractice; vet practices use practice management software such as ezyVet. Most send automatic confirmations and reminders and can hold a card or take a deposit. Where bookings live in Google Calendar, Claude's Google Workspace connectors can read, create and change events, and send email only with approval unless the owner changes that [21].
- **Square Appointments as an example.** A business can show its cancellation policy in online booking and in confirmation emails, set a cancellation cut-off from one hour to two weeks, and choose no requirement, full prepayment or "hold card in case of no-show". The fee can be a flat amount, a fee per service or a percentage, and can be charged up to 14 days after the appointment. These features need Square Appointments Plus or Premium [18].
- **Fair fees.** In the UK, cancellation and deposit terms in consumer contracts are judged for fairness under the Consumer Rights Act 2015; the CMA's guidance was last updated 22 July 2026 [23]. In the EU, unfair terms do not bind consumers; the EU's own examples include a high fixed fee and a 30% deposit kept on cancellation that were refunded after advice [24]. Set fees that reflect real loss, show them before booking and apply them the same way to everyone. In US healthcare, check payer contracts before charging insured patients a missed-appointment fee.

### Reminders and texting rules
- **US texting.** Business texts from a standard 10-digit number need A2P 10DLC registration of the brand and the campaign, including how people opt in and opt out; unregistered traffic gets filtered more and costs more [10].
- **US healthcare texts and calls.** Under the federal rule, a healthcare provider may send appointment and exam reminders and similar messages to the mobile number the patient gave without separate consent if the message is free to the patient, names the provider, stays within 160 characters for texts or one minute for calls, goes no more than once a day and three times a week, contains no marketing or billing, and offers STOP, honoured at once [9]. Anyone can revoke consent by any reasonable means, and requests must be honoured within ten business days; one confirmation text is allowed [9]. The rule page lists an amendment published 26 October 2026; check what changed before relying on the details. Automated calls using a prerecorded or artificial voice fall under the same rules [9].
- **US HIPAA.** Appointment reminders are part of treatment and do not need written authorisation [6]. Voicemails and messages left with family are allowed if kept to the practice name, number and what is needed to confirm; reasonable requests to be contacted another way must be honoured [5]. A texting, booking or answering service that handles patient information for a clinic needs a written business associate contract [8].
- **UK.** Appointment confirmations and reminders are service messages, not marketing, as long as they contain no promotion; a hospital appointment text is the Information Commission's own example [11]. Add an offer and it becomes marketing. The Information Commission replaced the Information Commissioner's Office on 30 Sep 2026 and still calls itself the ICO ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/information-commission-succeeds-the-ico-as-uks-data-protection-regulator), [ico.org.uk](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/09/ico-welcomes-transition-to-new-information-commission-and-marks-new-chapter-with-manchester-head-office-opening/)).
- **EU.** Health data is a special category under GDPR, with stricter conditions, and every message should carry only the data its purpose needs [12].
- **Email.** In the US a reminder is transactional; a promotion is commercial and needs an opt-out honoured within 10 business days [26]. Keep recall reminders and offers apart.

### Phones, menus and AI receptionists
- Cloud phone systems (RingCentral, Dialpad, Aircall, Nextiva and others) and builders such as Twilio Studio let a business set up phone trees, greetings and routing without hardware [20].
- Keep menus short (no more than five items per level, per the guideline above) [25], put emergencies first, offer a person at every level, and give opening hours and the emergency route on the closed message.
- AI receptionists and chat booking assistants are spreading. In the EU, from 2 August 2026, an AI system that talks directly with people must tell them it is AI unless that is obvious [22]. Label the bot, keep it to approved answers and give a route to a person.

### Reviews
- Google bars businesses from offering incentives for reviews, discouraging negative reviews or asking only happy customers; no staff quotas or requests for specific content [13]. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews, sentiment-based incentives and review suppression [15]. Ask every client the same way, or no one.
- Replies on a Google Business Profile are public, show as the business and notify the reviewer [14].
- **Case study: Elite Dental Associates (HHS, 2019).** The Dallas practice replied to Yelp reviews with patients' names and health details. HHS found it had no policy for disclosures on social media; the practice paid $10,000 and agreed to write policies, train staff and notify patients [7]. A safe reply thanks the reviewer, never confirms they are a patient, shares no details and invites contact offline.
- **Vets.** In the UK, the RCVS says client and animal information should not normally be disclosed to third parties, and the duty covers support staff, not only vets and nurses (guidance updated 25 January 2023) [16]. Treat a public review reply as a disclosure.

### Patient pages and claims
- In the US, health-related claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence, and testimonials showing better-than-typical results need a clear statement of typical results [17]. Describe what the clinic does, not results it promises.

## 5. Decision rules

- If bookings have no automatic reminder, add text reminders first, because the trial evidence is strongest there [1].
- If reminders already go out and no-shows stay high, test specific, concrete wording against the current message before adding fees [2].
- If you want a reminder to work, make the change or cancel step one tap or one reply, and send it while the person can still act [3].
- If a no-show fee or deposit is added, show it before booking, say it in plain words with an example, and apply it the same way to everyone [18][23][24].
- If the business is a US healthcare provider texting patients, check the healthcare exemption limits and keep billing and offers out of reminders [9].
- If a patient or client asks to stop texts or calls, stop within the legal time and note it, by whatever words they used [9].
- If a voicemail or text is for a patient, leave the practice name, the number and the appointment time only; no reason for the visit [5].
- If a vendor will handle patient information for a US clinic, get a business associate contract before switching it on [8].
- If a caller describes symptoms or an emergency, follow the clinic's written emergency script word for word and pass them to the clinician; never assess it yourself.
- If a review mentions treatment, reply without confirming the person is a patient and move the talk offline [7][16].
- If you ask for reviews, ask every client the same way and offer nothing in return [13][15].
- If an AI answers the phone or chat for EU callers, tell them it is AI and offer a person [22].
- If a phone menu has more options than callers can hold, cut or merge them before recording [25].

## 6. Common mistakes and how to spot them

- **Health details in texts and voicemails.** Spot it: "Reminder: your root canal follow-up" or a test result in a message [5].
- **Reminders that are hard to act on.** Spot it: "Call us to reschedule" with no link, outside office hours [3].
- **Fees nobody was told about.** Spot it: complaints and card disputes after a no-show charge; the policy is not in the booking flow [18].
- **One reminder for every booking type.** Spot it: a 2-hour reminder for a surgery that needs fasting instructions the day before.
- **Offers inside reminders.** Spot it: "Don't forget your appointment, and get 20% off whitening!" [11][26].
- **Menus that list every department.** Spot it: callers pressing 0 or hanging up at the menu; options past five [25].
- **No route to a person.** Spot it: a loop back to the main menu.
- **Review replies that confirm a patient.** Spot it: "We're sorry your crown didn't fit, Mrs Lee" [7].
- **Review gating.** Spot it: review requests sent only after a five-star survey [13].
- **The desk giving clinical advice.** Spot it: scripts with "it's probably nothing" or dosing answers.
- **Aftercare notes that add to the vet's words.** Spot it: instructions in the client copy that the vet did not write.

## 7. Quality bar

Before anything goes out, I check:
1. Does every fact (fees, hours, policy, prices) match the business's written policy, with unknowns flagged?
2. Do texts, voicemails and public replies contain no health details and no confirmation that someone is a patient?
3. Does every reminder say date, time, place, what to bring and how to change it in one step?
4. Are opt-out wording and frequency inside the rules for the place [9][11]?
5. Does every phone path reach a person, and does the emergency route come first?
6. Is every clinical question routed to the clinician or vet?
7. Are service messages free of offers?
8. Did every live action stay inside the access level and limits, and is it in the action log?

## 8. How this role writes

- **Reminders and confirmations.** Two or three short lines: who, when, where, one action (confirm, change, cancel). Name the business first. No health details.
- **Policies in plain words.** What the rule is, why it exists in one line, an example with real numbers from the policy, and how to get help.
- **Phone prompts.** Written to be heard: short sentences, the action before the number, numbers said slowly, no jargon, timed out loud.
- **Call scripts.** Greeting, checks, branches for the top reasons, transfer and callback words, a clean close, and the emergency line.
- **Review replies.** Thanks, a general statement of the practice's standards, an invitation to call or email, signed by a named role. No details.
- **Aftercare and patient notes.** The clinician's approved words, then a plain-words version that adds nothing.
- **Tone.** Calm, warm and firm. The policy holds; the person is treated kindly.

## 9. Glossary

- **Access level:** what the agent may do in a tool: read; read and draft; or read, write and publish within the owner's limits.
- **No-show (DNA, did not attend):** a booked client who does not come and does not cancel [2].
- **Late cancellation:** a cancellation inside the business's cut-off.
- **Cancellation cut-off:** the latest time a client can cancel without a fee [18].
- **No-show protection / card hold:** a card saved at booking so a fee can be charged under the policy [18].
- **Deposit / prepayment:** money paid at booking toward the service.
- **Waitlist:** a list of clients who want an earlier slot if one frees up.
- **Recall:** a reminder that a check-up, vaccination or follow-up is due.
- **IVR (interactive voice response):** an automated phone menu callers navigate by keypad or voice [20].
- **Call flow:** the map of where each call goes, by time of day and option.
- **Zero out:** pressing 0 or saying "agent" to reach a person.
- **A2P 10DLC:** US registration for business texting from standard 10-digit numbers [10].
- **TCPA:** the US law on automated calls and texts; its rules sit in 47 CFR 64.1200 [9].
- **HIPAA:** the US law on health information privacy for providers and their vendors [5].
- **Business associate contract (BAA):** the US contract a clinic needs with a vendor that handles patient information [8].
- **Protected health information (PHI):** health information that identifies a patient [7].
- **Special category data:** GDPR's stricter class of data, including health [12].
- **Service message:** an administrative message with no promotion, such as a reminder [11].
- **Review gating:** asking only happy clients for public reviews; banned by Google [13].
- **Enlightened Hospitality:** Danny Meyer's idea that how people feel matters as much as the task done [4].
