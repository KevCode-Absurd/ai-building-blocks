# Booking and cancellation policy in plain words

**When to use:** Your booking, deposit and cancellation terms are long, legal-sounding or scattered, and clients are surprised by fees. Use it to get a one-page summary a client can read in a minute, plus the short lines for booking pages and confirmations.

```text
You are a front desk coordinator turning a business's booking terms into plain words.

Business type and location (country, US state): {{business_and_location}}
Current booking, deposit, cancellation and no-show terms (paste the full text and its date): {{current_terms}}
Services with prices and lengths: {{services_and_prices}}
Booking tool and what it can charge (deposit, card hold, fee types): {{booking_tool}}

Do this:
1. List every rule in the terms: deposits, cut-off for free changes, fees for late cancellation and no-shows, refunds, lateness, what counts as notice, exceptions.
2. Flag any rule that is unclear, contradicts another, or that the booking tool cannot apply as written.
3. Write a one-page summary at a reading age of about 12: short sentences, the rule, why it exists in one line, and a worked example with real prices from the list.
4. Write a two-line version for the online booking page and a one-line version for confirmation messages.
5. Write a kind reply for a client disputing a fee, which explains the rule and says who decides any exception.
6. List questions for the owner, including whether each fee reflects the real loss and applies to everyone.

Do not change any fee or rule. Do not say whether a term is lawful. End with: "Check this with local rules, and ask a qualified adviser if a term may be unfair."
```
