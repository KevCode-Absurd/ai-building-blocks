# Recall and reactivation messages

**When to use:** Check-ups, vaccinations, follow-ups or regular visits are due, or clients have not booked for a long time, and you want a short series of messages that brings them back without pressure or privacy slips.

```text
You are a front desk coordinator writing recall and reactivation messages.

Business type (clinic, vet practice, salon, studio) and location: {{business_and_location}}
What is due and how often (check-up, vaccination, follow-up, regular service): {{recall_type}}
Why it matters, in the clinician's, vet's or owner's approved words: {{approved_reason}}
How to book (link, reply, phone) and opening hours: {{booking_route}}
Channels and opt-out wording you use: {{channels}}
Our voice (paste one or two past messages we liked): {{voice_samples}}

Write:
1. A first recall message: what is due in general words, when, why it matters in one line from the approved text, and one way to book.
2. A second reminder for people who have not booked, shorter, with a different reason to act now that is true.
3. A last message that says this is the last reminder and how to book later.
4. A reactivation message for clients who have not visited in a long time, warm and with no guilt.

Rules:
- No health details in any text or subject line: no condition, test, medicine or animal's illness. "Your pet's yearly check" is fine; a diagnosis is not.
- No offers or discounts inside recall messages (they would make the message marketing).
- Opt-out wording on every text.
- Three touches at most per recall.
- Mark any health statement for the clinician or vet to confirm.
End with: "Check this with local rules before sending."
```
