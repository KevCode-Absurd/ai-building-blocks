# Booking reminders and no-show plan

## When to use

- No-shows or late cancellations are eating into the diary.
- Reminders do not exist, go at the wrong time, or say "call us" when the office is closed.
- The business wants to add a deposit, card hold or no-show fee and fears upsetting clients.
- Recalls (check-ups, vaccinations, follow-ups) are missed because nobody sends them.

## What you need

1. Services with their length, price, lead time (how far ahead people book) and any preparation (fasting, forms, bring the pet's records).
2. A booking export for the last few months: booked, attended, no-show, late cancel, by service, day and lead time.
3. The current cancellation policy, deposits or card holds, and how fees are charged today.
4. The booking tool and what it can send (text, email, timing options, links to reschedule).
5. Where the business and clients are (country, US state) and whether it is a healthcare provider.
6. The business's voice: two or three messages it liked.

## Steps

### 1. Read the pattern

From the export, find where no-shows cluster: by service, day, time, lead time, new vs returning clients. Show the arithmetic. If there is no export, say so and plan to collect it.

### 2. List the booking moments

Confirm, remind, prepare (if the service needs it), reschedule, cancel, no-show follow-up, thank-you, recall. Not every business needs all of them.

### 3. Set reminder timing

Base timing on lead time and preparation, not habit. A common pattern is a confirmation at booking, a reminder a day or two before (so the person can still rebook the slot), and a short same-day reminder for services with high no-shows. Add a preparation message where the service needs it. Text reminders have the strongest trial evidence, with phone calls doing about as well at higher cost; keep calls for people who ask for them or have no mobile.

### 4. Write each message

- Two or three short lines: the business name, the service type in general words, date, time, place, one action.
- Make the action one tap or one reply: "Reply C to confirm, R to rebook" or a link.
- Use specific, concrete facts rather than vague appeals. In one NHS trial, stating the specific cost of a missed appointment cut missed appointments more than general or empathy wording; test any such line in your own setting.
- Never put health details in a text, email subject or voicemail: no condition, procedure, test or medicine. "Your appointment at [Business]" is enough.
- Add opt-out wording where the channel needs it (for example "Reply STOP to stop texts").
- Keep reminders free of offers. A promotion turns a service message into marketing in the UK and a commercial email in the US.

### 5. Check texting rules

- US: business texting from a standard number needs A2P 10DLC registration. Healthcare providers may send reminders without separate consent only within the federal limits (free to the patient, provider named, short, at most one a day and three a week, STOP honoured at once). Opt-outs in any reasonable words must be honoured within ten business days.
- US clinics: the texting or booking vendor needs a business associate contract.
- UK: reminders with no promotion are service messages.
- EU: health data is special category data; send only what the reminder needs.
- Check this with local rules for every place your clients are.

### 6. Write the policy in plain words

- The rule, with the cut-off and fee in plain numbers and an example: "If you cancel less than 24 hours before, we charge £15 of your £45 booking."
- Why it exists, in one line (the slot could have gone to someone else).
- How to cancel or change (link, reply, phone) and what counts as notice.
- What happens in genuine emergencies, if the owner allows discretion.
- Shown before booking and in the confirmation. Fees that reflect real loss and apply to everyone are easier to defend; in the UK and EU, unfair terms in consumer contracts may not bind the client.

### 7. Fill the gaps

Write a waitlist message that offers a freed slot to the next person, first come first served, with a time limit to reply. Write a no-show follow-up that is kind, states what happens under the policy and offers a new booking.

### 8. Write recalls

For check-ups, vaccinations or follow-ups: why it matters in one line (from the clinician's or vet's approved text), when it is due, how to book. Stop after a few reminders.

### Access needed

- Level 1: read the booking export and current templates.
- Level 2: save the new templates in the booking tool, switched off; save the policy text as a draft.
- Level 3: switch templates on, send waitlist offers and reschedule on request, within the agreed services, locations and quiet hours. Charging a fee needs the owner's yes per client.

## Checks

- Every message has date, time, place and one easy action.
- No health details in any text, subject line or voicemail.
- Opt-out wording and frequency fit the rules for the place.
- The policy has a worked example and matches what the booking tool will actually charge.
- Reminders carry no offers.
- The plan carries the line: *Check this with local rules before switching on.*

## Output format

1. No-show pattern (table with arithmetic, or the data needed)
2. Message set (moment, timing, channel, text)
3. Cancellation and no-show policy in plain words, with an example
4. Waitlist and no-show follow-up messages
5. Recall messages (if needed)
6. Rules checked by place, and facts to confirm

## Example of good output (short)

> **Illustrative structure, not a real business: a two-chair hair salon. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.**
>
> **48 hours before:** "[Business]: see you Thu 14 Nov at 10:30 with Kat. Reply C to confirm or R to rebook. Can't make it? Tell us by 10:30 Wed and there's no charge. Reply STOP to stop texts."
>
> **Waitlist:** "[Business]: a 3pm slot opened today with Kat. Reply YES in the next hour to take it. First reply gets it."
>
> **Policy line:** "Cancel or move up to 24 hours before for free. Later than that, or if you don't come, we charge 30% of the booked price, because we kept the chair for you. Example: £15 on a £50 cut."
