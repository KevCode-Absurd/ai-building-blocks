# Front-desk FAQ and intake

## When to use

- Desk staff give different answers to the same question.
- New or temporary staff start on the desk.
- Clients arrive without what they need, or fill in the same details twice.
- Intake forms use jargon or ask for more than the business needs.

## What you need

1. Written policies: hours, fees and price list, payment and insurance, deposits and cancellations, privacy notice, emergencies and out-of-hours.
2. The services, with who can have them and any preparation.
3. The current intake steps and forms, and the booking or practice system used.
4. The questions clients ask most (from calls, emails, chat or a staff tally).
5. Phrases the clinician, vet or owner must approve, and what the desk must never say.

## Steps

### 1. Collect the real questions

List the questions clients actually ask, in their words, with rough counts. Group them: booking and changes, fees and payment, insurance, what to bring and how to prepare, where and when, privacy and records, clinical.

### 2. Answer only from policy

For each question, write the answer from the written policy, with the policy line it relies on. If the policy does not answer it, do not invent a rule: list it under **Policy gaps** for the owner. Fees and hours are copied exactly.

### 3. Mark clinical questions

Questions about symptoms, treatment choices, medicines, doses, test results or an animal's health get one answer: the route to the clinician or vet (book a call, a nurse line, or the emergency route). The desk does not judge urgency beyond the business's written emergency script.

### 4. Write answers for each channel

- **Phone:** spoken version, short sentences.
- **Email or chat:** two or three lines and a link.
- **Website FAQ:** plain question, plain answer. In the US, health claims on pages need solid evidence and testimonials must not suggest unusual results are typical; describe what the business does, not results it promises.

### 5. Map the intake flow

From first contact to first visit: each step, who does it (client, desk, clinician), when, in which tool, and what "done" looks like. Ask once for each item and only for what the business needs. Health information goes only into the practice system or a secure form, never into email, text or chat. In the EU, health data is special category data; in the US, intake vendors that handle patient information need a business associate contract.

### 6. Write the form wording

Plain questions, one idea each, with why it is asked where that helps ("We ask for your GP's details so we can send them a summary, if you agree"). Optional questions marked optional. Consent and privacy wording comes from the business's privacy lead, not from the desk.

### 7. Write the desk card

A one-page card for the desk: greeting, the five most common answers, the emergency script, how to pass a clinical question, how to take a message, and the words never to use.

### Access needed

- Level 1: read policies, forms, booking settings and message history.
- Level 2: draft the FAQ page, form wording and desk card in the website or practice system, unpublished.
- Level 3: publish the approved FAQ and form wording and update booking-page text, within the limits you set. Clinical, privacy and consent wording always needs approval by the clinician, vet or privacy lead.

## Checks

- Every answer cites the policy line it comes from; gaps are listed, not filled.
- Clinical questions all route to the clinician or vet.
- Fees, hours and policies match the written versions exactly.
- The intake asks for each item once, and health data stays in secure systems.
- No promises of results on pages or in answers.
- The pack carries the line: *Check this with local rules and your privacy lead.*

## Output format

1. Question list with counts and groups
2. Answer book (question, answer by channel, policy line)
3. Clinical questions and their route
4. Policy gaps for the owner
5. Intake flow (step, who, when, tool, done when)
6. Form wording
7. Desk card

## Example of good output (short)

> **Illustrative structure, not a real business: a dental practice. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.**
>
> | Question | Phone answer | Policy line |
> |---|---|---|
> | "Do I need to pay a deposit?" | "For new patients, yes: £20 when you book, taken off your bill on the day." | Fees policy, section 2 |
> | "Is this tooth pain an emergency?" | "I can't judge that, but our dentist can. If you have swelling or bleeding that won't stop, [read the emergency script]. Otherwise I can book you the first emergency slot tomorrow." | Emergency script v3 |
> | "Can I pay in instalments?" | Not in policy | **Policy gap** for the owner |
