# Phone menu and call scripts

## When to use

- Callers press 0, hang up at the menu or reach the wrong person.
- The menu lists every department instead of what callers need.
- New or temporary staff answer the phone without a script.
- After-hours callers do not know what to do in an emergency.
- The greeting is out of date, too long or recorded badly.

## What you need

1. The top reasons people call, with rough counts (from call logs, staff tallies or a week of notes).
2. Opening hours, holiday closures and who answers which kind of call.
3. The business's written emergency script (clinics and vets) and out-of-hours arrangement.
4. Current menu, greetings and scripts, if any.
5. The phone system and what it can do (menus, voicemail to email, callback, queue messages, time-of-day routing).
6. Words the business must use or avoid, and how to say its name and any hard words.

## Steps

### 1. Rank call reasons

List reasons by volume. Merge ones that go to the same person. Note any that can be answered by a recorded message (hours, address, parking) so callers do not need a person for them.

### 2. Design the menu

- Emergencies first, if the business has an emergency route. For clinics and vets, the prompt follows the business's written emergency script exactly; the menu never judges what is an emergency.
- Then the most common reason. Order the rest by how often people need them.
- Keep each level short: a public-sector guideline recommends no more than five options, because callers must hold the list in memory. Two levels at most.
- Every level offers a person ("To speak to the front desk, press 0") and repeats the options if the caller does nothing.
- Map where each option routes by time of day, and what happens when nobody answers.

### 3. Write the prompts to be heard

- The action before the number: "For bookings, press 1", not "Press 1 for bookings".
- Short sentences, plain words, no jargon or internal team names.
- Say numbers and web addresses slowly, grouped as people say them.
- Time each prompt out loud and cut anything that does not help the caller act. The main greeting should be over before callers lose patience; aim for the shortest version that still does the job.

### 4. Write the greetings

- **Open:** name, a short welcome, the menu.
- **Closed:** name, opening hours, the emergency route, how to book online, voicemail.
- **Holiday:** the dates closed, the date you reopen, the emergency route.
- **Busy / queue:** an honest wait message and a callback option if the system has one.
- **Voicemail:** what to leave (name, number, reason in general words) and when you will call back.

### 5. Write hold messages

Six to eight short messages that answer common questions (booking online, what to bring, parking, the cancellation policy). Order them so the same one does not repeat too soon. No sales pressure.

### 6. Write the desk's call script

- Greeting with the business name and the person's first name.
- Checks: who is calling, which client or pet, what they need. Do not ask callers to read out full card numbers unless the payment system has a secure phone payment process; never note them on paper or in chat.
- Branches for the top reasons: book, change or cancel (with the policy in plain words), questions on fees and hours (from the written policy), anything clinical (pass to the clinician or vet, or book a call back), a complaint (listen, note, pass to the manager with a time for the call back).
- Transfer and callback wording: tell the caller who they are going to and why, and what happens if that person is busy.
- A clean close: repeat what will happen next and when.
- Messages left for patients follow privacy rules: practice name, number and appointment time only.
- Callers using relay services or with a speech or hearing difference get the same time and care; never hang up on a slow or relayed call.

### 7. Write the recording brief

Voice, tone and pace; how to say the name, street names and numbers; file names in the order the system needs; pauses between options. If an AI voice or AI receptionist is used, say so to callers in the EU (required from 2 August 2026) and give a route to a person.

### 8. Note what to check

Call recording notices (rules differ by state and country), opening hours, the emergency script with the clinician or vet, and the system's limits.

### Access needed

- Level 1: read call logs, missed calls and the current call flow.
- Level 2: build the new flow and greetings in the phone system, switched off, or as a written plan.
- Level 3: publish the approved flow and greetings, update holiday messages and hours, within the limits you set. Changes to the emergency message always need your yes.

## Checks

- Emergency route first, word for word from the business's script.
- No level has more options than callers can hold; every level reaches a person.
- Every prompt is timed out loud and has the action before the number.
- Closed and holiday messages give hours, reopening date and the emergency route.
- The call script answers only from written policy and passes clinical questions on.
- The plan carries the line: *Check this with local rules before going live.*

## Output format

1. Call reasons ranked, with counts
2. Menu map (level, option, prompt, routes to, by time of day, no-input action)
3. Greetings: open, closed, holiday, busy, voicemail (with timings)
4. Hold messages in order
5. Desk call script with branches
6. Recording brief and file list
7. Things to check

## Example of good output (short)

> **Illustrative structure, not a real business: a two-vet practice. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.**
>
> **Main menu (about 25 seconds):** "Thanks for calling [Business]. If your pet needs urgent help, press 1 now to reach the vet on call. To book or change an appointment, press 2. For repeat medicines, press 3. For anything else, or to speak to the team, press 0."
>
> **Closed:** "[Business] is closed. We're open Monday to Friday, 8 till 6, and Saturday, 9 till 12. If your pet needs urgent help, press 1 to reach the vet on call. To book, visit [domain], or leave your name and number after the tone and we'll call you back from 8 tomorrow."
