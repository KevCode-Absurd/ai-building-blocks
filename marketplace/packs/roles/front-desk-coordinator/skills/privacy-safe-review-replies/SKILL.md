# Privacy-safe review replies

## When to use

- A batch of new reviews needs replies.
- A harsh or detailed review mentions treatment, an animal's care, a fee or a staff member.
- The desk wants reply templates it can use safely.
- The owner wants more reviews and is thinking about how to ask.

## What you need

1. The reviews (text, star rating, date, platform).
2. The business type and where it is (country, US state).
3. Who may approve replies, and which ones the desk may publish alone.
4. The business's complaint process: who calls back, by when, and the contact route to give.
5. The business's voice: two or three replies it liked.

## Why it matters

Replies are public, show as the business and notify the reviewer. In the US, a Dallas dental practice, Elite Dental Associates, paid $10,000 in a 2019 settlement with HHS after replying to Yelp reviews with patients' names and health details. In the UK, vet confidentiality guidance covers support staff, not only vets. The safest rule for any health or care business: a reply is a disclosure, so it discloses nothing.

## Steps

### 1. Sort the reviews

| Type | Action |
|---|---|
| Praise with no care details | Reply from template, may be published at level 3 |
| Praise that mentions treatment or a pet's condition | Reply without repeating any detail |
| Mild complaint (wait, parking, booking) | Reply, own it in general terms, invite contact |
| Complaint about care, treatment, an animal's health, a fee dispute, a named staff member | Hold for the owner; draft only |
| Legal threat, regulator mention, safety issue, abuse | Hold for the owner; suggest advice before replying |
| Fake or wrong business | Hold; suggest the platform's reporting route |

### 2. Write the reply

- Thank the reviewer.
- Do not confirm or deny that they are a patient or client. Write about the practice's standards in general: "We aim for every visit to start on time."
- Repeat no detail from the review: no procedure, condition, animal's illness, date of visit, price paid or staff action.
- For complaints: say you take it seriously, give a direct route offline (phone or email to a named role), and say when someone will respond. Do not argue facts in public.
- Keep it short and human, signed by a role ("Practice manager").
- No offers in exchange for changing or removing a review.

### 3. Hold and escalate

For held reviews, write a short note for the owner: what the review says, the risk (privacy, clinical, legal, reputational), the suggested reply and whether advice is needed first. Clinical complaints go to the clinician or vet, not the desk.

### 4. Ask for reviews fairly

- Ask every client the same way, at the same point (for example in the thank-you message), or ask no one.
- No incentives, discounts or prize draws in return for reviews; no asking only happy clients; no staff quotas.
- In a clinic, make sure the review request itself carries no health details.

### 5. Build templates

Write a small set of reply templates by type, each with: when to use, when not to use, the reply, and words never to add. Templates are a start; each reply is checked against the review before publishing.

### Access needed

- Level 1: read reviews and ratings.
- Level 2: draft replies in the platform or in a document for approval.
- Level 3: publish replies to the review types the owner approved (usually praise and mild complaints with no care details); everything else is drafted and held.

## Checks

- No reply confirms that the reviewer is a patient or client.
- No reply repeats or adds any care, health, animal, date or price detail.
- Complaints get a named offline route and a response time.
- Held reviews have a risk note for the owner.
- Review requests go to everyone in the same way, with no incentive.
- The plan carries the line: *Check this with local rules and your privacy lead.*

## Output format

1. Sorted review list (review, type, action, reply or hold)
2. Replies ready to publish
3. Held reviews with risk notes and draft replies
4. Reply templates by type
5. A fair review request message

## Example of good output (short)

> **Made-up example: Brightside Physio (not a real business).**
>
> **Review (2 stars):** "Waited 40 minutes and my knee still hurts after three sessions with Tom."
>
> **Reply (held for the owner because it mentions care and a staff member; draft):** "Thank you for taking the time to write this. We aim for every appointment to start on time and for everyone to feel listened to, and we're sorry this falls short of what you expected. Please call our practice manager, Rachel, on 01632 960 000 or email hello@brightside.example, and she will get back to you within one working day. Practice manager, Brightside Physio"
>
> **Not this:** "Sorry your knee is still sore, Tom's notes say you missed your exercises." (Confirms a patient, discloses care details, argues in public.)
