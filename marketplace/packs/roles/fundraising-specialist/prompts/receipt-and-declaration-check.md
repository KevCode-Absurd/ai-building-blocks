# Receipt and declaration check

**When to use:** before a gala, auction or year-end mailing, to check your receipts, acknowledgments and Gift Aid wording against the official guidance, and list what to ask your accountant.

```text
Check the wording below against the official guidance for each country listed. You are not giving tax or legal advice: for anything uncertain, say "check this with local rules, and ask a qualified accountant".

Countries and our registration in each: {{countries_and_registration}}
Our receipt, acknowledgment or declaration wording (paste each): {{current_wording}}
The gifts or payments it covers (for example $300 gift; $150 gala ticket including dinner; £15 raffle ticket; £20 online gift): {{payment_types}}
Value of anything donors receive in return, and how we estimated it: {{benefits_and_values}}

Use these rules as of November 2026, and tell me to re-check the official pages before relying on them:
- US (IRS): a written acknowledgment for a single gift of $250 or more, with the charity's name, the cash amount, a description but not a value of non-cash gifts, and a statement that nothing was given in return or a description and good-faith estimate of what was. For a payment over $75 that is partly for goods or services, a statement that only the amount above the value received is deductible, with a good-faith estimate of that value.
- UK (HMRC Gift Aid): the declaration says the donor agrees to Gift Aid being claimed and gives a description of the gift, the charity's name, and the donor's full name and home address with postcode. No Gift Aid on company gifts, Payroll Giving, payments for goods or services, gifts with benefits over the limit, shares or charity vouchers; special rules for events, auctions, membership and sponsorship.
- Canada: follow the CRA's issuing receipts guidance.

Return a table: payment type, rule that applies, what our wording has, what is missing or wrong, suggested wording, question for the accountant.
```
