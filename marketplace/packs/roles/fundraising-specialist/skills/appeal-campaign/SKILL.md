# Appeal campaign

An appeal works when one person's true story meets one clear ask that a donor can picture. The craft is choosing the story, sizing the ask from real costs, saying exactly what the money is for, and thanking people before asking again. This skill turns a thin brief into a ready campaign.

## When to use

- A charity, school, club or faith group wants a seasonal or year-end appeal.
- A project needs a named appeal (a roof, a minibus, a new service).
- An emergency appeal must go out quickly but still truthfully.
- An event needs the ask written: invitation, the ask on the night, thank-yous.

Not for: inventing need, stories or numbers; guilt or false urgency; legal or tax advice on receipts (see the `gift-thanks-and-acknowledgement` skill and check this with local rules).

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read past appeals, results and donor counts. Level 2 to draft emails in the email tool and a draft campaign page. Level 3 to send the approved sequence to approved lists within the limits; sending to any list not in the limits needs a fresh yes.

- **The purpose:** general funds or a named project; the target and end date; what happens to any surplus.
- **One story,** with the person's written consent for this use (name, photo, details), or an anonymised version they approved.
- **Real cost figures:** what £25, £50, £100 (or $) actually pay for, from the charity's budget.
- **Who it goes to:** donor groups and counts (current, lapsed, never given), and the consent basis for each email or text list.
- **Registration facts:** charity name and number, countries where donors live, and whether paid fundraisers or a business partner are involved.
- **Past results,** if any: response, average gift, what worked.

## Steps

1. **Fix the purpose and the surplus line.** Write one sentence: what the money is for. If it is a named project, add a secondary purpose ("Any money raised beyond what the roof needs will go to our general work"), because funds raised for a stated purpose are restricted. Check this with local rules for the countries you fundraise in.
2. **Choose the story.** One person, one moment, one change. Check the consent record before writing a word. Remove anything the person did not agree to. Do not add detail to make it sadder.
3. **Find the one number.** The single figure that shows the need, from the charity's own records or a named public source ("We had 212 families on our list in October, up from 140 last year").
4. **Size the ask.** Turn real costs into two or three gift amounts the donor can picture. For lapsed or new donors, lead with a smaller amount; for current donors, suggest a step up from their last gift if the database shows it. Never round a cost to make it sound better.
5. **Add a match if one is real.** If a named funder will match gifts, say so plainly with the cap and the deadline. A 1:1 match is enough; a bigger ratio is not needed.
6. **Write the thank-you first.** It says what the gift will do, by when, and asks for nothing.
7. **Write the appeal.** Open with the person, then the need in one number, then the ask with amounts and what each does, then how to give (one link, one QR code, one phone number, one return envelope). One ask only. Add a P.S. that repeats the ask. Write to one reader, in plain words.
8. **Add the required lines.** UK charities with income over £10,000 last year: "Registered charity number ..." on every piece. Paid staff, professional fundraisers or business partners: the solicitation statement. Any costs taken from money raised: say so. US: check state registration where donors live.
9. **Build the sequence.** Lead-in (a short story or update, no ask), launch, mid-point update with progress, last call with a real deadline, thank-you to everyone who gave and a progress note to everyone who didn't. Each message has one job.
10. **Set up the tracker.** Gifts, new donors, returning donors, average gift, source (post, email, social, event) and thank-you sent date.
11. **Consent and list check.** Each email or text list has opt-in consent or a valid charity soft opt-in with a way to refuse in every message; post goes to people who have not objected.
12. **Prepare the go-live list.** At level 3, list each send (list, date, message) and get one yes before the first send.

## Checks

- Story consented, unchanged in substance, no extra identifying details.
- One ask per message; amounts tied to real costs.
- Purpose and surplus line present for a named project.
- Charity number, solicitation statement and expense note present where required.
- No guilt or pressure words: "desperate", "before it's too late", "only you can".
- Deadline is real and stated once, not repeated as pressure.
- Thank-you written and scheduled; tracker set up.
- Every list has a consent basis; every message has a way to refuse.

## Output format

1. **Campaign summary:** purpose, target, dates, surplus line, story used (with consent noted), the ask amounts and what they pay for.
2. **Messages:** each with channel, audience, send date, subject line or opening (two options for the launch), body, and call to action.
3. **Thank-you set:** for this appeal, by donor group.
4. **Compliance lines:** the exact wording added and why.
5. **Tracker:** columns and the weekly numbers to report.
6. **Go-live list and open questions** for the client.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real organisation: a community pantry charity. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures and story only show the shape.*

**Purpose:** Winter food parcels, December to February. Target £18,000. Any money raised beyond the parcels will fund the pantry's general work. Registered charity number [NEED: number].

**Story (consent on file, first name only, no photo):** "Marta started coming in October, after her hours were cut. Her first words were an apology. Now she volunteers on Thursdays."

**One number:** "We packed 212 parcels last week. Last winter it was 140."

**Ask:** £25 fills a parcel for a family of four (our cost per parcel, November budget). £50 fills two. £100 covers a family for a month.

**Launch subject lines:** "212 parcels last week" / "Marta's Thursday"

**Thank-you (sent within two days):** "Thank you. Your £50 will fill two parcels this week. In February we'll write and tell you how the winter went. We won't ask you for anything in that letter."

**Open question:** confirm the parcel cost includes the new fridge's running cost, or remove "our cost per parcel" until finance confirms.
