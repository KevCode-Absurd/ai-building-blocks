# Funder fit and grant writing

The best bid is often the one you don't write. This skill first cuts a long funder list to the few that fit, then writes those applications so each answer matches what the funder asked, in its order and words, using only the group's facts. For public portals, registrations (SAM.gov, Grants.gov, Find a grant, the EU portal) and criteria maps for government calls, use the proposal writer kit's `grant-application` skill alongside this one.

## When to use

- A group has a list of possible funders and limited evenings to write bids.
- A specific funder's form needs answering.
- A budget needs a narrative that matches the plan.
- A funder report is due.

Not for: promising a result, inventing statistics, partners, letters of support or past outcomes, or legal and tax questions about charity status (ask a qualified adviser, and check this with local rules).

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read guidelines, past bids and reports. Level 2 to draft in a shared document or fill in a funder's portal form if the account holder adds the agent as a user. Level 3 to file final versions and keep the funding calendar current. Submitting any application or report needs a fresh yes every time, and the account holder presses submit.

- **The group's facts:** what it does, where, for whom, yearly income, legal form and registration, latest accounts, governing document.
- **The project:** need and evidence, activities, who benefits, timeline, partners who have agreed, how success will be measured.
- **The budget sheet** and any match funding.
- **Funder material:** guidelines, eligibility rules, exclusions, priorities, scoring, word limits, deadlines, past award lists.

**Two research modes.** With web search connected, check each funder's current guidelines, deadlines and recent awards on its own website (and GrantNav for UK funders or Form 990-PF filings for US foundations), and record the date checked. Without web search, work only from what the client pastes, and mark anything unconfirmed as [check with funder].

## Steps

1. **Read the exclusions first,** then eligibility. For each funder, check every rule as met, not met or unclear, quoting the rule. One "not met" on a must-have rule moves the funder to "skip", with the reason. Unclear rules become questions for the funder's staff.
2. **Score the rest** on fit with the funder's stated priorities, grant size against need, odds (past awards to groups like this one, if known) and effort to apply. Show the weights so the client can change them.
3. **Give a verdict:** apply now, prepare first (and what to fix), or skip.
4. **Build the calendar.** For each shortlisted funder: prep start date, internal sign-off date, deadline, expected decision date and reporting dates. Put the prep start in, not just the deadline.
5. **Write approach notes.** The two or three things each funder's guidelines stress, quoted, and the questions to ask its staff before applying.
6. **Copy each question** from the form into the draft as a heading, with its word or character limit.
7. **Build a short logic model:** need, activities, outputs, outcomes, and how the group will know. Outcomes must be within the group's control to deliver and measure.
8. **Write the need** from the group's data and named public sources. Mark each fact with its source.
9. **Answer each question** in the funder's order, leading with the point, mirroring the funder's words where it helps the assessor. Stay inside limits.
10. **Make the budget add up.** Each line explained in one sentence that matches the budget sheet and an activity. Flag any cost the guidelines may not allow, for the funder's contact or the group's accountant.
11. **End with a still-missing list:** facts, figures, signatures, attachments (accounts, policies, governing document), and who provides each.
12. **For reports:** what was done, what changed, what was learned, how the money was spent against the budget, in the funder's template, from records only.
13. **Before submission:** final check against every limit and attachment; then ask for a fresh yes on this exact version.

## Checks

- Every eligibility rule is quoted and marked; no funder is scored that fails a must-have rule.
- Every rule, amount and date comes from the funder's guidelines (pasted or checked live, with the date).
- Every answer responds to its question, in order, within the limit.
- Every statistic has a named source; no invented partners, beneficiaries or outcomes.
- The budget table adds up and every line maps to an activity.
- The still-missing list is complete.
- The client has given a fresh yes for this version, and the account holder will submit.

## Output format

1. **Ranked table:** funder, verdict, fit score, grant range, deadline, reason.
2. **Eligibility table** per funder: rule (quoted), met / not met / unclear, fix.
3. **12-month calendar.**
4. **Approach notes and questions** per shortlisted funder.
5. **Draft application:** question headings, answers, logic model, budget table and narrative.
6. **Still-missing list** and submission plan.

## Example of good output (short)

*Hillside Sensory Garden Group is a made-up school parent group, used only to show the format. Funders and rules here are invented.*

| Funder | Verdict | Fit | Grant range | Deadline | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fund A (community trust) | Apply now | 8/10 | £1,000 to £10,000 | 31 Mar | Funds outdoor projects in your county; you meet all six rules |
| Fund B (company foundation) | Prepare first | 6/10 | Up to £5,000 | 15 May | Needs a bank account in the group's name; yours is the school's |
| Fund C (regional foundation) | Skip | n/a | n/a | n/a | Rule 4: three years of accounts; you have two. Move to next year |

**Question 3 (150 words): "How will you know the project has worked?"**
"We will count weekly garden sessions and the number of pupils using them each term, from the class timetable. Teachers will record, at the start and end of the year, how many pupils with sensory needs take part in outdoor learning without leaving early. [NEED: confirm the special needs coordinator will collect this.]"

**Still missing:** the group's constitution; two quotes for the raised beds; the head teacher's signature on the landowner consent.
