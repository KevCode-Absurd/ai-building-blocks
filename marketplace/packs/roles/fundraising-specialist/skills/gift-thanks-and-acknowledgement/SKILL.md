# Gift thanks and acknowledgement

A thank-you keeps a donor; an acknowledgment lets them (or the charity) claim tax relief. Both can sit in one message, but the wording for each must be right. This skill writes both and plans what happens between gifts.

## When to use

- A charity has no thank-you messages, or one generic message for everyone.
- Receipts, acknowledgments or Gift Aid declarations need writing or checking.
- An event, auction or dinner sold tickets that were partly gifts.
- First-time donors are not giving again and the charity wants a care plan.
- A year-end giving summary is due.

Not for: deciding whether a gift is deductible, whether Gift Aid applies to an unusual payment, or how to value a non-cash gift. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read gift records and current templates. Level 2 to set up draft thank-you emails, letters and automations in the email tool or donor database. Level 3 to send approved thank-yous and log thank-you dates within the limits. A Gift Aid claim to HMRC is made by the charity's authorised person; the agent can prepare the claim schedule at level 2.

- Countries where donors live and where the charity is registered.
- Gift types: one-off, monthly, major, in memory, event tickets, auctions, goods.
- The charity's legal name and registration details.
- Current thank-you, receipt and declaration wording.
- What typical gifts pay for, from the charity's own figures.
- Who signs thank-yous (a named person, ideally).

## Steps

1. **Map the gift types to the rules.** For each gift type, note which rule applies.
   - *US:* a single gift of $250 or more needs a written acknowledgment: the charity's name, the cash amount, a description (not a value) of any non-cash gift, and a statement that nothing was given in return, or a description and good-faith estimate of what was given. The donor must receive it by the earlier of the date they file or the return's due date (including extensions); many charities send by 31 January. An annual summary or email is fine; no Social Security number is needed. Separate gifts under $250 are not added together.
   - *US quid pro quo:* a payment over $75 that buys something (a dinner, a ticket) needs a statement that only the amount above the value received is deductible, with a good-faith estimate of that value. Token items and some membership benefits are exceptions; the low-cost limits change yearly, so check IRS.gov.
   - *UK Gift Aid:* the charity can claim 25p per £1 from a UK taxpayer's gift with a declaration giving the donor's agreement, a description of the gift, the charity's name, and the donor's full name and home address with postcode. Gift Aid cannot be claimed on company gifts, Payroll Giving, payments for goods or services, gifts with benefits above the limit, shares or charity vouchers. Events, auctions, membership fees and sponsorship have special rules. Keep declarations for six years after the most recent donation claimed.
   - *Canada:* official donation receipts follow the CRA's issuing receipts guidance, including split receipting for events.
   - *Elsewhere:* check this with local rules.
2. **Write the thank-you set by group.** First gift, monthly, major, in memory, lapsed and returning, event guest, corporate. Each says thank you in the first line, names the gift, says what it will do (from real costs) and by when, and asks for nothing. Sign with a real name.
3. **Add the acknowledgment wording** to the messages that need it, or prepare a separate receipt or year-end summary.
4. **Set the timing.** Thank promptly (the client sets the target, often within two working days). Automate the first email if possible; add a personal note for major and first-time gifts where the team has time. Note that a large trial found scripted thank-you calls to new donors did not change retention, so choose effort the charity can sustain and measure it.
5. **Plan the next 90 days for first-time donors.** A welcome, one impact update with a real result, an invitation (visit, event, volunteering) or a short survey, then the next ask. No ask in the thank-you.
6. **Check the data fields.** The donor database needs: thank-you sent date, acknowledgment sent date, Gift Aid declaration status and date, consent status per channel.
7. **Prepare the go-live list.** At level 3, list the automations and sends and get one yes before switching them on.

## Checks

- Every gift type has a rule mapped, or a note to ask a qualified accountant.
- US: $250+ gifts have acknowledgment wording; quid pro quo payments over $75 have the deductible amount and good-faith value.
- UK: declarations have every required item; ineligible payments are excluded from Gift Aid.
- Thank-yous name the gift and what it does, contain no ask, and come from a real person.
- First-time donors have a 90-day plan with one impact update before the next ask.
- Database fields exist to prove what was sent and when.

## Output format

1. **Rules table:** gift type, country, rule, wording needed, who checks it.
2. **Thank-you set:** one message per donor group, with acknowledgment wording where needed.
3. **Declaration or receipt text** (UK Gift Aid declaration, US year-end summary, or a note to use the CRA template).
4. **Donor care calendar:** first 90 days for new donors; yearly touchpoints for regulars and major donors.
5. **Data fields and go-live list.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Riverside Youth Orchestra is a made-up charity, used only to show the format. Names and figures are invented.*

| Gift type | Country | Rule | Wording needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| $300 one-off gift | US | Written acknowledgment ($250+) | "No goods or services were provided in exchange for this contribution." |
| $150 gala ticket, dinner valued at $60 | US | Quid pro quo (over $75) | "Your deductible contribution is limited to $90, the amount above our good-faith estimate of $60 for the dinner." |
| £20 online gift from a UK taxpayer | UK | Gift Aid declaration | Declaration with full name, home address and postcode, and agreement to claim |
| £15 raffle ticket | UK | Not a gift for Gift Aid | No Gift Aid; thank-you only |

**First-gift thank-you (email, sent within two days):** "Dear Sam, thank you for your $300. It pays for a term of cello lessons for one student. In March you'll hear the spring concert they've been working towards. No goods or services were provided in exchange for this contribution. Riverside Youth Orchestra. With thanks, Priya Shah, Director."

**Note for the client:** the $60 dinner value is our estimate from the caterer's per-head price; ask your accountant to confirm before the gala receipts go out.
