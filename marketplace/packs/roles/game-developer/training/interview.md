# Training interview: game developer

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out client names, unreleased game details, private code, keys and anything under a confidentiality agreement; describe projects in general terms.

1. **Which engines, versions and genres do you work in most, and what work do you turn down?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and the limits of its advice.

2. **When a client describes a feel ("snappy", "floaty"), how do you turn it into numbers?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's method for the hardest part of a mechanic brief.

3. **What do you write down before you open the code editor?**
   Why it matters: records the expert's planning format.

4. **How do you split a mechanic into components, and what makes you stop splitting?**
   Why it matters: shapes the agent's script size and structure habits.

5. **Where do tuning numbers live in your projects, and how do designers change them?**
   Why it matters: grounds the agent's ScriptableObject and Inspector choices.

6. **Tell me about a bug that only happened at some frame rates. How did you find it?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a real timing diagnosis to learn from.

7. **How do you test a mechanic across frame rates and hardware?**
   Why it matters: turns "plays right everywhere" into a method.

8. **Which edge cases do you always check (pause, respawn, reload), and which one bit you last?**
   Why it matters: records the expert's edge case list.

9. **What do you put in Edit Mode tests and Play Mode tests, and what do you skip on a small budget?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's testing floor for small studios.

10. **How do you design a save system that survives updates? Tell me about a save bug you fixed.**
    Why it matters: captures versioning and recovery habits.

11. **Walk me through your last Unity version upgrade. What broke, and in what order did you fix it?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a realistic upgrade sequence.

12. **How do you handle scene and prefab merges in a team?**
    Why it matters: records version control habits that documentation underplays.

13. **What do you check before a Steam release, and what has caused a review problem for you?**
    Why it matters: records real release lessons.

14. **How do you fill in rating questionnaires and the Steam Content Survey, and who signs them off?**
    Why it matters: captures the expert's declaration habit and approval line.

15. **Have you worked on paid random items or premium currency? What did you check?**
    Why it matters: grounds the agent's loot box checks in practice.

16. **How do you review purchase buttons and confirmations so players can't buy by accident?**
    Why it matters: captures a money-safety habit for game code.

17. **How do you work with AI coding helpers in Unity, and where have they misled you?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent which API mistakes to watch for.

18. **What access do you ask for at the start of a job, and what do you never touch?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's access habits and limits.

19. **How do you explain a technical trade-off (for example timestep versus CPU cost) to a designer or producer?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's plain-words explanations.

20. **Tell me the story of your hardest game programming job, start to finish.**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a worked example of judgement under pressure.
