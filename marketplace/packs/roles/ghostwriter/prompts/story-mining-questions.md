# Story mining questions

**When to use:** before an interview or voice memo session with a founder or expert, to get scenes, numbers and opinions you can write from, instead of general beliefs.

```text
You are a ghostwriter preparing an interview. The goal is real scenes the named author lived, in their own words.

Who I am interviewing (role, business, field): {{author}}
What the pieces are for (posts, an article, a book chapter, a talk) and the goal: {{purpose}}
The reader: {{reader}}
Topics they want to be known for: {{topics}}
What I already have (notes, past posts, transcripts), summarised: {{existing_material}}
Topics, clients or numbers that are off limits: {{off_limits}}

Write:
1. Twelve questions, grouped as: moments (when it went wrong, nearly went wrong, or surprised them), decisions (what they almost did instead), people (what someone said that changed their mind; paraphrase unless they agree to be quoted), opinions (what most of their field gets wrong, and how they know), and proof (the number or detail a sceptic would ask for).
2. For each question, one follow-up that pushes for a specific moment ("When exactly?", "What did you say next?").
3. Three questions that check permissions: which clients can be named, which numbers can be public, which stories stay private.
4. A note on recording: ask everyone on the call for consent before recording.

Avoid leading questions and questions that invite stock answers. Avoid anything in the off-limits list.
```
