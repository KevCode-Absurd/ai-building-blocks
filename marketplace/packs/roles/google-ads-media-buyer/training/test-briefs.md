# Test briefs: adversarial rounds for a Google Ads media buyer

Each brief is hard on purpose. The coach gives it to the trainee agent and to a plain AI, then scores both against "A great answer must".

## 1. The ROAS that hides a loss

An online furniture shop reports a blended Google Ads ROAS of 5.0 on 20,000 USD a month and wants to double spend. Break-even ROAS is 2.5. Brand Search and a Performance Max campaign with no brand exclusions take most of the credit.

**A great answer must:**
- Split brand and non-brand (including brand searches inside Performance Max) before judging.
- Explain why brand traffic is likely to convert anyway, citing the eBay experiment idea without overstating it.
- Propose brand exclusions or a clean reporting split, and a test before doubling.
- Give a staged, approvable plan.

## 2. Page views as conversions

A plumber's account shows 900 conversions a month at 3 USD each. The owner gets about 60 calls. Primary actions include "page_view" and "click to call button".

**A great answer must:**
- Identify the wrong primary actions and explain how they mislead Smart Bidding.
- Propose real outcomes as primary (qualified calls of a set length, form leads), the rest secondary.
- Warn that the change triggers a learning period of up to seven days and that reported conversions will fall.
- Set new expectations and targets from real leads.

## 3. The August surprise

A Target CPA campaign at 80 USD was achieving 45 USD and was limited by budget. Since mid-August its cost per conversion has climbed to about 75 USD with the same budget. The client asks what broke.

**A great answer must:**
- Recognise Google's 17 August 2026 change to budget-limited target campaigns.
- Explain it in plain words (the bidder now aims at the stated target).
- Offer the options: lower the target toward recent performance, raise the budget with care, or switch to Maximize conversions, with trade-offs.
- Recommend one and set a review after one to two conversion cycles.

## 4. Junk leads from broad match

A B2B software firm turned on AI Max and broad match. Leads doubled, demos did not. Search terms include students, "free alternative" and job seekers.

**A great answer must:**
- Diagnose that bidding optimises for form fills, not demos.
- Propose enhanced conversions for leads with demo and opportunity stages sent back, set as primary.
- Build negatives at the right level and match type, checked against converting terms.
- Consider an experiment comparing AI Max on and off rather than switching it off blindly.

## 5. The feed nobody owns

A clothing shop's Performance Max spends 70% on two products. Merchant Center shows 340 disapproved items (missing GTINs, price mismatch), and titles are just brand names.

**A great answer must:**
- Put feed fixes ahead of bid or budget changes.
- Explain how titles and GTINs affect which searches products can show for.
- Prioritise fixes by sales potential.
- Avoid inventing product data or prices.

## 6. The client who wants manual control

A dentist wants every keyword on manual CPC with exact match only, "like it was in 2018", on 1,500 USD a month.

**A great answer must:**
- Respect the request and lay out the trade-offs honestly.
- Explain what Smart Bidding and broader matching do now, without pressure.
- Offer a fair test (custom experiment) so the client can see results.
- Keep tracking clean either way.

## 7. Consent banner change

After a new cookie banner went live for UK visitors, recorded conversions fell 40% while CRM leads held steady.

**A great answer must:**
- Suspect consent signals and tag behaviour before blaming campaigns.
- Check whether consent mode signals (ad_storage, analytics_storage, ad_user_data, ad_personalization) reach the tags, and basic versus advanced mode.
- Advise against cutting budgets on the basis of the drop.
- Say that the banner's legal compliance is for the client's legal adviser.
