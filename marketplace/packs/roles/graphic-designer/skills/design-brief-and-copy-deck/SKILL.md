# Design brief and copy deck

Most design rework comes from two gaps: nobody agreed what the piece must do, and the words kept changing after the layout was built. This skill closes both before any layout starts. It produces a one-page brief and a copy deck: every line the client wants on the piece, numbered, ranked and checked, in their exact words.

## When to use

- At the start of any flyer, poster, banner, menu, sign, merch or social graphics job.
- When a client sends a vague request with no size, place or deadline.
- When a job has gone through several rounds and keeps changing.
- Before handing work to another designer, a printer or a tool such as Canva.

Not for: naming or brand strategy, or writing the copy from scratch (a copywriter does that; this skill ranks and fits what the client supplies, and can suggest edits marked as suggestions).

## What you need

- The request, in the client's own words.
- Any existing copy, even rough.
- Where the piece will be seen (shop counter, wall, window, car park, phone feed, table).
- The printer or platform, if known, and a link to their template or artwork guide.
- Brand assets: logo files, colours, fonts, photos.
- The deadline and the budget range.
- **Access I have:** the level the client grants in each tool (1 read, 2 read and draft, 3 read, write and publish). This skill needs level 1 only: reading the brand kit, past designs and templates through a connector, or files the client shares. Until the client answers, work at level 1.

If most of this is missing, ask the intake questions below in one message, five or fewer, each with a default the client can accept.

## Steps

1. **Name the one job.** Write one sentence: "This [piece] gets [reader] to [action] when they see it [where], from [distance], for about [time]." If the client gives two jobs (for example "announce the event and sell the merch"), ask which wins. One piece, one job.
2. **Fix the place and distance.** Handheld, across a room, across a street, in a feed. Distance decides type size, and the time people look decides how many words fit. A passer-by gets one line; a diner gets a full menu.
3. **Fix the format and size.** Use the printer's or platform's standard sizes unless there is a reason not to. If the client has no printer yet, propose a common size (A5 or A4 in the UK and EU, US Letter or a postcard size in the US) and say it must be confirmed with the printer's template.
4. **Name the maker and the tool.** Who builds the final file (the client in Canva, a printer's online editor, a designer in InDesign or Affinity) and who prints or posts it. The handoff must be written in that tool's terms.
5. **List assets and check rights.** For each logo, photo, font and illustration: who owns it, what format it is in, and whether it is good enough at final size. Flag anything from a website or social profile for print use. Flag another business's logo, a character, song lyrics or a stock image without a licence.
6. **List content only the client can supply.** Allergen notices, calorie figures, prices, dates, licence numbers, legal small print, accessibility notes. Mark each as "client supplies and confirms". Never write these yourself.
7. **Build the copy deck.** Put every line the client wants into a numbered table, exactly as supplied. Do not correct facts or reword claims. Fix only obvious typos, and list each fix.
8. **Rank the lines into three levels.** Level 1: the one thing seen first (usually the offer, the event name or the headline). Level 2: what makes it worth acting on (date, place, price, key detail). Level 3: how to act and the small print. If more than one line wants to be Level 1, ask the client to choose.
9. **Check the fit.** Count characters per line. Compare the total word count with what the reader has time for. If it is too much, list cut options in order (what to drop first), and leave the choice to the client.
10. **Write suggestions separately.** If a line could be shorter or clearer, show the suggestion in its own column, marked "suggestion", with the reason. The client's line stays the default.
11. **List open questions with defaults.** Each question gets a default answer ("If I don't hear back, I'll use A5 portrait").
12. **Get approval.** The brief and copy deck are approved before layout starts.

## Checks

- One job, written as one sentence.
- Place, distance, time, format and size are all stated, or listed as open questions with defaults.
- Every line is exactly as supplied, with any typo fixes listed.
- No invented facts, prices, dates, allergen notes, statistics or reviews.
- Every line has a level, and only one line is Level 1.
- Rights are noted for every asset; problems are flagged, not fixed silently.
- Client-only content is marked and has a named person to confirm it.

## Output format

1. **Brief (one page):** job sentence; reader; place, distance and time; format and size; printer or platform; maker and tool; access I have in each tool; deadline; budget; assets and rights notes; client-only content.
2. **Copy deck:** a table with columns: ID, level (1, 2 or 3), line exactly as supplied, characters, suggestion (if any), source (client, printer, law).
3. **Fit note:** whether the copy fits the reading time, and cut options in order.
4. **Open questions:** each with a default.

## Example of good output (short)

*Larkfield Bakery is a made-up business used only to show the format.*

**Job:** This A5 counter flyer gets regular customers to pre-order a Christmas cake when they pick it up at the till, read at arm's length for about 20 seconds.

**Maker and tool:** owner builds in Canva; printed by an online printer (template link to be sent).

| ID | Level | Line as supplied | Chars | Suggestion | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | 1 | Christmas cakes: order by 12 December | 37 | none | Client |
| C2 | 2 | Rich fruit, iced and decorated by hand, serves 12 | 49 | none | Client |
| C3 | 2 | £[price supplied by owner] | n/a | none | Client supplies and confirms |
| C4 | 3 | Order at the counter or call [number] | 37 | none | Client |
| C5 | 3 | Allergen information: ask our staff | 35 | none | Client supplies and confirms wording |
| C6 | 3 | We use only the finest ingredients and our cakes are loved by everyone in town | 79 | Cut, or replace with one specific fact the owner can confirm (reason: no proof, and it competes with C1) | Client |

**Fit:** About 45 words read in 20 seconds is fine. If space is tight, drop C6 first.

**Open questions:** Printer template? (Default: A5 portrait with the printer's own bleed.) Photo of the cake at full size? (Default: no photo; type and colour only.)
