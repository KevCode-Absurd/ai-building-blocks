# Print layout spec

A print layout spec lets someone build the piece exactly, first time, in their own tool, and get it through the printer's checks. It is written so that a person who has never met me can follow it. With level 2 access in a connected tool such as Canva, I can also build the draft myself; with level 3, I can export and send the final file within the client's limits.

## When to use

- Flyers, posters, banners and roll-up stands, postcards and direct mail, business cards, menus and menu boards, event and shop signs.
- A printer has rejected a file (missing bleed, low resolution, wrong colour mode, text too close to the edge).
- The client builds in Canva or a printer's online editor and needs exact steps.

Not for: packaging dielines, fire and safety signs, or permanent building signs that need sign-off from a qualified professional.

## What you need

- The approved brief and copy deck (see the design-brief-and-copy-deck skill).
- The printer's template or artwork guide: trim size, bleed, safe area, resolution, colour mode, PDF settings.
- The reading distance and the time people look.
- Logo (vector if possible), brand colours as hex plus CMYK or Pantone references, fonts and their licences, photos at full size.
- The tool that will build the final file.
- **Access I have** in that tool. Steps 1 to 10 need level 1 only. Step 11 (building the draft inside the tool) needs level 2. Step 12 (exporting final files or placing the print order) needs level 3, within the limits the client set.

## Steps

1. **Set the page.** Trim size, orientation, bleed and safe area from the printer's template. If there is no template yet, state a common size and write "confirm with the printer's template before building". Note fold lines for folded pieces and keep text away from them.
2. **Set the grid.** Margins equal to or wider than the printer's safe area, a column count that suits the copy (one for posters, two or three for menus and leaflets), and a baseline for text. Every element sits on the grid.
3. **Draw the zone map.** Split the page into named zones (for example, top band, hero, details block, action strip, footer). Give each zone's share of the page height and what it holds. Put the Level 1 line where the eye lands first and give it space.
4. **Place the copy.** Use the copy deck IDs. Every line goes in a zone, in reading order. Do not reword anything; suggestions stay in the copy deck.
5. **Set type for the distance.** Choose at most three sizes. For handheld pieces, set body text in the 10 to 12 point range with line spacing of about 120 to 145% (Butterick). For pieces read from further away, set the Level 1 size from the reading distance and plan a full-size test print of one line. For signs, use simple letterforms: no script or decorative faces for anything that must be read quickly.
6. **Set colour.** For each colour: its job, its hex value, its CMYK or spot reference, and the printer's colour mode. Check every text and background pair with a contrast checker and record the ratio; aim for at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (WCAG 2.2 AA, used here as a print guide). Make sure no information depends on colour alone.
7. **Place images and logos.** Size each at final dimensions, check its resolution against the printer's minimum, give the crop, and set clear space around the logo. Images that touch the edge extend into the bleed.
8. **Mark client-only spaces.** Allergen notices, calorie figures, legal small print, licence numbers: draw a labelled box with "client supplies and confirms". For a menu in the UK, include space for the notice that tells customers how to get allergen information; for US chains with 20 or more locations, space for calories and the required statements.
9. **Add an action and a way to measure.** QR code or short web address in the action strip, with clear space around it. Suggest a unique code or URL for this piece so the client can count results. Test-scan any QR code at real size and distance before print.
10. **Write the build notes** for the named tool: page set-up, guides, adding each element by zone, styles, export settings. Use the tool's own menu names where you know them; where you don't, describe the outcome ("export as a print PDF with bleed").
11. **Level 2: build the draft.** With level 2 access in a connected tool, create the design in a new file named "DRAFT", following the spec. Do not share, print or post it. Log what you created.
12. **Level 3: finish the job.** With level 3 access, show the action list and limits (which file, which printer, quantity, spending cap) and get a yes; then export the print file and send or order within those limits. Log each action. Ask again before any order above the cap or any change to quantity.
13. **Run the printer checklist and the proof checklist** (below).

## Checks

**Printer checklist:** trim size matches the template; bleed on every edge-touching element; nothing important outside the safe area; images at or above the printer's minimum resolution at final size; colour mode as the printer asks; fonts embedded or outlined; the printer's PDF preset; crop marks only if requested.

**Proof checklist:** every copy deck line placed, exactly as supplied; prices, dates and phone numbers checked against the source; client-only content supplied and confirmed; contrast ratios recorded; colour is not the only cue; QR code scans; a full-size print read from the real distance.

## Output format

1. **Page set-up:** size, orientation, bleed, safe area, folds, grid.
2. **Zone map:** a simple text diagram, plus a table of zones with height share and contents.
3. **Placement table:** zone, copy ID, text style, size, alignment, colour.
4. **Type and colour tables,** with the contrast ratio of each pair.
5. **Images and logo:** file, size, crop, resolution check, clear space.
6. **Client-only spaces.**
7. **Build notes** for the named tool.
8. **Access and log:** the level used, and every action taken.
9. **Printer and proof checklists,** ticked or marked open.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a fish and chip shop's A3 portrait window poster for a weekday lunch offer, read from the pavement about 3 m away. Access I have: Canva level 1 (view link only). Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Page:** A3 portrait, 297 x 420 mm trim; bleed and safe area from the printer's template (not yet received: confirm before building). Grid: one column, margins at the safe area.

**Zones:** top band 15% (logo, small); hero 40% (C1); details 25% (C2, C3); action strip 15% (C4, QR); footer 5% (C5).

| Zone | Copy ID | Style | Size | Align | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | C1 "Lunch box, weekdays 12 to 2" | Bold sans | Largest size; test-print one line at full size and read from 3 m | Left | Off-white on deep navy |
| Details | C2 "Fish, chips and a drink" | Regular sans | Middle size | Left | Off-white on navy |
| Action | C4 "Order ahead: scan to skip the queue" | Bold sans | Middle size | Left | Navy on yellow panel |
| Footer | C5 "Allergen information: ask our staff" | Regular | Smallest size | Left | Off-white on navy |

**Colour pairs:** record the ratio from the contrast checker for each pair before handoff; replace any pair under 4.5:1 for the footer text.

**Client-only:** C3 price and C5 allergen wording supplied and confirmed by the owner.
