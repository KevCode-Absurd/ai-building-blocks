# Social graphics spec

A social graphic is read on a phone, for a second or two, while the reader's thumb is moving. It has to say one thing, in big enough type, in the part of the frame the app does not cover. This skill turns the client's content into slide-by-slide specs that someone can build in Canva or a similar tool, or that I can build myself with level 2 access.

## When to use

- A carousel from a blog post, a tip list or a product launch.
- Single posts, quote graphics, Story covers and Pinterest Pins.
- A template kit so the client's team can make on-brand posts without a designer.

Not for: paid ad testing and media buying (a media buyer handles that), or video editing.

## What you need

- The content: the post, notes or product facts, in the client's words.
- The platforms and the formats wanted on each.
- Brand colours (hex), fonts and logo; any existing templates.
- The one thing each post should get people to do.
- The build tool (Canva, Adobe Express or other).
- **Access I have** in each tool. Steps 1 to 8 need level 1. Step 9 (building drafts in the tool or uploading drafts to the scheduler) needs level 2. Step 10 (scheduling or posting) needs level 3, within the accounts, dates and post types the client set.

## Steps

1. **Check current sizes.** Open each platform's own help page before starting, because sizes change. As checked on 6 Nov 2026: Instagram feed widths 320 to 1080 px, ratios from 1.91:1 to 4:5, with 1080 x 1350 for portrait; Meta's Stories ad spec is 9:16 at 1440 x 2560 with about 14% at the top, 35% at the bottom and 6% at each side kept free of text and logos; Pinterest standard Pins are 2:3 at 1000 x 1500 and carousels hold 2 to 5 images. Write down the size and the date checked in the spec.
2. **Pick one job per post.** Save, share, tap the link, book, visit. Write it at the top of the spec.
3. **Plan the carousel.** Slide 1 is the hook: the promise of the carousel in a few words, with a visible cue that there is more. Middle slides carry one point each, in order. The last slide asks for the one action. If a point needs two slides, it is two points.
4. **Write the slide text.** Use the client's words. Keep each slide short enough to read in a glance; record the character count. Mark any shortened line with "shortened from source" and keep the full line in the caption. Do not add statistics, claims or before-and-after results the client has not supplied and can prove.
5. **Lay out each slide.** For each slide: the zone map (top, middle, bottom), position of the headline and any body line, the image or icon, and the brand mark. Keep all words and the logo inside the safe zones. Use one grid across every slide so the set looks like one piece.
6. **Set type and colour.** At most three type sizes across the set. Large, bold headlines; body text big enough to read on a phone at arm's length (preview it on a real phone). Check every text and background pair with a contrast checker and record the ratio; aim for 4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text (WCAG 2.2 AA). For text over photos, add a solid panel or a dark overlay. Do not rely on colour alone to carry meaning.
7. **Write alt text and the caption.** Alt text says what the image is there to tell, in context; a graphic of words repeats the words; a chart gets the key numbers in text (W3C images tutorial). The caption carries the full message for people who do not swipe, and ends with the one action.
8. **For a template kit:** list each template, what it is for, what is fixed (logo, colours, grid) and what is editable (headline, photo, body), with rules for photos (crop, subject, no stock clichés) and a "do not" list.
9. **Level 2: build drafts.** With level 2 access, build the slides in a new design named "DRAFT" in the client's tool, or upload them to the client's scheduler as drafts with caption and alt text. Do not publish. Log what you created.
10. **Level 3: schedule or post.** With level 3 access, show the action list and limits (accounts, dates, number of posts) and get a yes; then schedule or post within those limits. Log each post. Ask again before posting to a new account, changing a date outside the plan or deleting a post.

## Checks

- Size and ratio match each platform's current help page, with the date checked.
- All words and the logo sit inside the safe zones.
- Slide 1 says what the carousel gives; the last slide asks for one action.
- Every line traces to the client's content; no new claims, numbers or reviews.
- Contrast ratios recorded; colour is not the only cue.
- Alt text and caption written for every post.
- Previewed on a phone before handoff.

## Output format

1. **Header:** platforms, sizes, date checked, job of the post, access I have.
2. **Slide table:** slide number, zone map, exact text, character count, type style and size, colours (hex), image or icon, notes.
3. **Contrast table:** each text and background pair with its ratio.
4. **Caption and alt text** for each post.
5. **Build notes** for the named tool.
6. **Action log** for any level 2 or level 3 work.
