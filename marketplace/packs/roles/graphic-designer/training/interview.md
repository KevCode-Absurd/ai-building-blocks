# Interview: graphic designer

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you show me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the designer's work (for example, merch questions for a menu specialist).

## How you approach a job

1. **Walk me through your last job from the first message to the file going to print or going live.**
   Why it matters: shows the real method, not the textbook one.
2. **What do you insist on knowing before you start a layout, and what do you do when the client cannot tell you?**
   Why it matters: the brief is where most rework starts, and how an expert fills gaps is a reusable skill.
3. **How do you decide what the reader sees first? Show me a piece where you changed the order and why.**
   Why it matters: captures their hierarchy judgement, the core of the craft.
4. **How do you choose type sizes for something read from across a room or a street? Do you test it, and how?**
   Why it matters: distance testing separates real print experience from screen-only design.
5. **How do you build a grid for a menu, a poster and a carousel? What is different about each?**
   Why it matters: shows how their layout method changes by format.

## Colour, contrast and accessibility

6. **How do you pick colours that work on screen and in print? What has gone wrong for you between the two?**
   Why it matters: real colour failures teach the agent what to check before print.
7. **How do you check contrast, and what do you do when the brand colour fails?**
   Why it matters: reveals how they hold the accessibility line with clients.
8. **Have you designed for people with low vision or colour blindness? What did you change?**
   Why it matters: practical accessibility habits beat rules recited from memory.

## Files, printers and platforms

9. **Which printers, print-on-demand services and sign makers do you use, and what do they reject most often?**
   Why it matters: fills access.md and the preflight checklist with what really happens.
10. **What is your checklist before a file goes to print? Give me the actual list.**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's preflight.
11. **How do you keep up with social media sizes and safe zones, and how do you check a post before it goes out?**
    Why it matters: platform specs change fast; their habit becomes the agent's.
12. **For merch: how do you decide between screen print, DTG and embroidery, and what design changes does each force?**
    Why it matters: method limits are where merch designs fail.

## Working with clients and tools

13. **When you hand a design to someone else to build, what do you give them so they get it right?**
    Why it matters: defines the handoff pack, the agent's main deliverable when it cannot make final artwork.
14. **Which changes would you make in a client's Canva, Figma or printer account on your own, and which do you always check first? What limits do you agree before you post or order?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's level 2 and level 3 habits and the limits it asks for.
15. **What client feedback do you push back on, and what do you always accept?**
    Why it matters: shapes how the agent handles disagreement.
16. **A client wants another brand's logo, a cartoon character or song lyrics on their merch. What do you say?**
    Why it matters: tests their rights line and how they hold it kindly.
17. **How do you handle menu allergens, prices or legal small print that the client has not confirmed?**
    Why it matters: these are the errors that cost money or harm people.

## Mistakes and specialty

18. **What are the three mistakes you see most in designs made by non-designers, or by AI tools?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's "spot it" list.
19. **Tell me about a reprint or a post you had to pull. What caused it and what do you check now?**
    Why it matters: a real failure gives the agent a check it would not think of.
20. **Which kinds of pieces or businesses do you know best, and what is different about designing for them?**
    Why it matters: feeds "My specialty".
21. **What kind of job do you turn down, and why?**
    Why it matters: defines what the agent is not for.

## A hard job

22. **Tell me about the hardest design job you have done: the brief, what went wrong, and how you got it over the line.**
    Why it matters: hard stories show judgement under pressure better than any checklist.
23. **If you had to train a junior designer in one week, what would you teach first?**
    Why it matters: reveals what the expert thinks matters most.
