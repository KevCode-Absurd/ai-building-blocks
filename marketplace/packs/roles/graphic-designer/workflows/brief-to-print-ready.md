# Brief to print-ready file

**Trigger:** a client needs a printed piece (flyer, poster, banner, postcard, menu, signs or merch) and has a deadline.

**Time:** usually two to seven working days, most of it waiting for copy, assets, approvals and a proof.

## Steps

1. **Intake and access (access.md questions).** Confirm the one job, where the piece is seen and from how far, the printer, the tool that builds the final file and the deadline. Ask which access level the client grants in each tool (design tool, printer account) and record it as "Access I have". Until they answer, work at level 1.
2. **Brief and copy deck (skill: design-brief-and-copy-deck).** Rank the client's exact words into three levels; list client-only content (allergens, calories, legal lines, prices) and rights questions. Level 1.
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves the brief and the copy deck. Layout does not start until the copy is final or the client accepts a draft layout.
3. **Get the printer's rules.** Download the template or artwork guide (size, bleed, safe area, resolution, colour mode, PDF preset). With level 1 access to the printer account, read them there. For merch, use the merch-print-spec prompt; for signs, the sign-schedule prompt; for menus, the menu-layout-plan prompt.
4. **Layout spec (skill: print-layout-spec).** Page set-up, grid, zone map, placement table, type and colour tables, images, client-only spaces and build notes. Level 1.
5. **Contrast and legibility (skill: contrast-and-legibility-check).** Measure every text and background pair and fix failures before the client sees the layout. Level 1.
   **Approval point 2:** the client approves the layout spec, colours and type.
6. **Build.**
   - Level 1 (no connector or read only): write the build steps (prompt: handoff-build-notes); the client or their designer builds the file.
   - Level 2: build the design myself in the client's tool as a new file named "DRAFT"; do not share, print or order; log what I created.
7. **Preflight (prompt: printer-file-preflight).** Check the built file against the printer's rules and the approved copy. At level 2, fix problems in the draft.
8. **Proof.** A full-size print read from the real distance, a phone preview, or a merch sample before the full run.
   **Approval point 3:** the client approves the final proof, quantity and spend.
9. **Send to print.**
   - Level 1 or 2: the client exports and orders.
   - Level 3: show the action list and limits (file, printer, product, quantity, spending cap, delivery address the client gave) and get a yes; then export and place the order within those limits. Ask again before anything outside them, such as a higher quantity or a rush fee. Log each action and its result.
10. **Hand over.** Deliver the brief, copy deck, layout spec, contrast table, final file (or its location), the action log, and a note on how to measure results (a unique code or link on the piece).

## Outputs

- Approved brief and copy deck.
- Layout spec with zone map, placement, type and colour tables.
- Contrast table with every ratio recorded.
- Build notes, or a draft design in the client's tool (level 2).
- Preflight report and proof checklist.
- Final print file, or a placed order within the client's limits (level 3).
- Action log of every change made in the client's tools.

## Needs the client's approval

- The access level for each tool, and the limits for level 3 (accounts, actions, spending cap, time).
- The brief and copy deck (point 1).
- The layout, colours and type (point 2).
- The final proof, quantity and spend (point 3).
- Any client-only content: allergens, calories, prices, legal lines, confirmed by the owner.
- Permission for any logo, image, character or phrase the client does not own.
- Anything outside the level 3 limits, asked again each time.
