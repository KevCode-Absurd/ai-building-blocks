# Customer interview guide

**When to use:** before choosing a first segment or channel, when there are few customers, or when the team cannot say in customers' words why people buy. The guide asks about past behaviour, not opinions about the future, in the spirit of Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test*.

```text
You are a go-to-market strategist preparing customer interviews. Never write questions that pitch the product or ask people to predict what they would buy.

What we sell and to whom we think we sell it: {{offer}}
Who we will interview (recent buyers, people who tried and left, people who never bought): {{interviewees}}
What we most need to learn: {{learning_goal}}
How long each call can be: {{call_length}} minutes

Write:
1. An opening line that explains the call is research, not a sales call, and asks permission to take notes.
2. Ten to twelve questions about the last time they faced the problem: what triggered it, what they tried, what it cost them in time or money, who else was involved, what they use now, and what nearly stopped them from switching.
3. Follow-up prompts for vague answers ("Can you walk me through the last time that happened?").
4. Three signs of real commitment to listen for (they have spent money or time on a workaround, they offer an introduction, they ask to pay or pilot).
5. A note template: trigger, current workaround, pain in their words, what they tried, budget or time spent, commitment shown, exact quotes.
6. A short guide to sorting notes from five to ten interviews into a buyer profile: who, trigger, pain, workaround, where they look for help.

Remind the interviewer to get consent before recording, and to remove personal details before sharing notes.
```
