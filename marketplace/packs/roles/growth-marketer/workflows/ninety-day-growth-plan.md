# Ninety-day growth plan

**Trigger:** a new client, a new quarter, or a plan that has grown too big to follow. Growth leads with the Traction pack run the Bullseye inside step 6 (`orchestrator.md`, sections 2 to 5).

**Cadence:** once per quarter, with the weekly growth review running inside it. About two weeks from intake to an approved plan.

**Access:** steps are marked L1, L2 or L3 as set out in access.md. Until more is granted, everything runs at L1.

## Steps

1. **Intake, L1.** What the business sells, to whom, at what margin; how the last 10 to 20 customers found it; hours and cash for growth; current tools. Ask which access level the client grants for each tool and the limits, and write it down as "Access I have".
2. **Goal and model (prompt: goal-to-growth-model), L1.** One goal with a date; the funnel table; the constraint.
3. **Funnel and retention check (skill: funnel-and-retention-diagnosis), L1.** Cohorts, activation moment, the order of fixes. If retention does not level off, the plan leads with product and customer research, and says so.
4. **Unit economics (skill: unit-economics-and-cac-limit), L1.** Allowable CAC chosen by the owner; pass and stop marks.
5. **Customer words (prompt: customer-interview-guide), L1.** If there is little customer evidence to work from (reviews, interviews, survey answers), run a round of interviews with recent buyers before choosing channels.
6. **Channel choice, L1.** Score channels on reach, likely cost per customer against the allowable CAC, speed to a readable result, and fit with the team's skills and the price point (Balfour's channel-model fit). Growth leads run the full 19-channel Bullseye from the Traction pack. Pick one main channel and one backup; park the rest with a one-line reason.
7. **Measurement (skill: measurement-and-attribution-plan), L1 then L2.** Event map, UTM rules, self-reported source question, consent checks for the regions served. Draft tracking changes in an unpublished workspace at L2.
8. **Write the plan, L1.** One page: goal, constraint, model, unit economics, chosen channels and parked ones, the first three tests (cards with sizing), 13 weeks of weekly actions with owners, budget by month, the three-number scoreboard and the review dates.
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves the plan, the budget, the test cards and the access levels.
9. **Build, L2.** Draft the first tests in the client's tools without starting them.
10. **Launch, L3 or the client.** At L3, confirm the action list and limits once, then launch within them and log every action. Otherwise the client launches.
11. **Run the weeks (workflow: weekly-growth-review).** Weeks 1 to 4: first tests and tracking fixes. Weeks 5 to 8: read round one, rerun unclear tests with one change, start the backup channel if the main one fails its stop mark. Weeks 9 to 13: focus effort on what passed, write the next quarter's question.
    **Approval point 2:** at week 6 and week 13, the client approves the keep, change or stop decisions and any budget shift.

## Outputs

- "Access I have" record.
- One-page 90-day growth plan.
- Unit economics sheet and funnel diagnosis.
- Measurement plan and tracking change list.
- First three test cards.
- Weekly memos, the learning log and the action log.
- A quarter-end review: what passed, what failed, what we learned, the next goal.

## Needs the client's approval

- The goal, the plan, the budget and the access level per tool (point 1).
- The allowable CAC level, chosen by the owner.
- Every test card before launch, and every draft before it goes live (published by the client at L2; one yes to the action list at L3).
- Anything outside the level 3 limits, and any change to prices, offers or claims customers will see.
- The mid-quarter and quarter-end decisions (point 2).
