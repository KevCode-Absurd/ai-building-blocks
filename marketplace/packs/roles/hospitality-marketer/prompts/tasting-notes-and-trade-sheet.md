# Tasting notes and trade sheet

**When to use:** for a beer, wine, cider or spirits release, to turn the maker's notes into tasting notes, a short story and a one-page sheet for bars, restaurants and shops.

```text
Write tasting notes and a trade sheet for {{producer_name}}. Use only the maker's facts below. Read like someone leaning on the bar telling a friend what's in the glass: plain, sensory, no hype.

Drinks (name, style, ABV, ingredients, process, maker's own tasting words): {{drinks}}
Awards we can prove (with the year and the body): {{awards}}
Pack sizes, prices for trade, minimum order and how to order: {{trade_terms}}
Where the copy will run (website, social, menus, trade sheet) and which countries or US states: {{where_and_markets}}
Rules our adviser has given us, if any: {{our_rules}}

For each drink write:
1. A tasting note of 40 to 60 words (nose, palate, finish) in the maker's terms.
2. A two-sentence story (the orchard, the batch, the idea), only from facts given.
3. Serving notes (temperature, glass) and one or two food pairings.

Then a trade sheet: a small table per drink (style, ABV, pack sizes, trade price, how to order) and three lines on why it sells, from facts given.

Check every line: no health, mood or performance claims; no emphasis on strength; nothing that appeals to under-18s; in UK material, anyone pictured drinking must be and look 25 or over. For US markets, list the mandatory ad information we need (responsible advertiser name and contact, class or type, alcohol content for spirits) and tell us to check state rules. List anything you removed and why.
```
