# Guest review replies

Guests read reviews and the owner's replies before they book, and for independents the rating moves revenue. A good reply is short, specific and human; a bad one argues in public. This skill replies at volume, holds the hard ones for the owner, and keeps the asking honest. For the full review request system, see the local marketing specialist kit's `review-request-and-reply-system` skill; for privacy-sensitive settings, the front desk coordinator kit's `privacy-safe-review-replies` skill.

## When to use

- A weekly batch of new reviews needs replies.
- A bad night produced several angry reviews.
- A review mentions illness, an allergy, injury, staff conduct, a refund or legal action.
- The owner wants more reviews and needs an honest way to ask.

Not for: fake reviews, replies posing as guests, rewards for reviews, asking only happy guests, or deleting genuine negative reviews.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read reviews and booking records. Level 2 to draft replies in a document or the platform's draft field. Level 3 to post replies within the limits the owner set, usually positive reviews only. Every reply to a negative review, and any reply touching illness, allergy, injury, safety, staff conduct, refunds or legal action, needs the owner's fresh yes every time.

- The reviews (platform, star rating, name as shown, date, text).
- What the venue knows about each visit, if anything (booking found or not, what happened).
- Fixes made since, if any.
- A named contact and channel for complaints.
- The owner's tone, sign-off name, and anything never to be said in public.

## Steps

1. **Sort the batch:** positive (reply within limits), mixed, negative, and risky (illness, allergy, injury, safety, staff conduct, discrimination, refunds, legal threats, personal data). Risky and negative go to the owner's queue.
2. **Reply to positives** in 25 to 60 words: thank them, name the specific thing they mentioned, and sign with the owner's name. No offers, no promotion, no copy-paste lines.
3. **Draft replies to mixed and negative reviews:** thank them, acknowledge the specific issue without arguing, apologise where the venue got it wrong, say what has changed if anything has, and invite them to contact a named person by phone or email. Don't reveal booking details, health information or staff names, and don't accept blame for things outside the venue's control.
4. **Risky reviews:** a short holding draft, plus a note to the owner on what to check first (incident log, allergen record, CCTV, the staff member's account) and who should reply. Allergy and illness reports go to the person responsible for food safety before any reply; legal threats go to the owner's adviser.
5. **Flag reviews that may break policy** (fake, from a competitor, conflict of interest, off-topic, offensive) with the reason, for the owner to report. Never flag a review just for being negative.
6. **Spot themes.** Count repeated praise and complaints across the batch (slow service on Saturdays, cold food on delivery, noise in room 4) and pass them to the owner as fixes, not as reply lines.
7. **Present the owner's queue** with drafts, notes and a recommended order. Post only after a fresh yes for each.
8. **Keep asking honestly.** One line staff say at the end of a good visit or in the post-stay email to every guest who agreed to hear from the venue, with a link or QR code. No rewards, no quotas, no filtering, no pressure at the table. On Booking.com, reviews come only from guests who stayed.
9. **Log** each reply (platform, review date, reply date, who approved).

## Checks

- Every reply speaks to that review, with no promotion and no private details.
- No reply argues, threatens or names staff.
- Every negative or risky reply has the owner's fresh yes before posting.
- Policy flags have a reason; negative reviews are not flagged for being negative.
- Themes passed to the owner as fixes.
- The asking routine has no rewards, filters, quotas or pressure.

## Output format

1. **Posted or ready replies** (positives, within limits).
2. **Owner's queue:** each negative or risky review with the draft reply, notes and what to check.
3. **Policy flags** with reasons.
4. **Themes table:** theme, count, example, suggested fix.
5. **Reply log.**

## Example of good output (short)

*The Fox and Barrel is a made-up pub, used only to show the format. Reviews are invented.*

**Positive (posted within limits):** "Thanks, Jen. Glad the Sunday lamb and the fire by the window did their job on a wet afternoon. Hope to see you again soon. Tom, landlord."

**Owner's queue, 2 stars, Saturday 8 Feb:** "Waited 50 minutes for mains, and nobody told us why." Draft: "Thank you for telling us, Chris, and I'm sorry. Saturday was too slow, and we should have told you what was happening. We've added a second chef on Saturday evenings from this week. If you'd like to talk, please email me at tom@[NEED: address]. Tom, landlord." Check first: the Saturday rota and the table's ticket times.

**Risky, held:** a review says a child reacted to a dessert. Not replied. Note: speak to the head chef and check the allergen record for that dessert first; the owner decides who replies and when.

**Theme:** slow mains on Saturdays (4 of 11 reviews). Suggested fix for the owner, not for the replies.
