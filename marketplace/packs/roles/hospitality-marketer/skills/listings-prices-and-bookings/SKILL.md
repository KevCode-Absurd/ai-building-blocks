# Listings, prices and bookings

Guests decide from listings before they ever see the website. When the menu on Google is last season's, the hours are wrong on a booking site, or a fee appears only at checkout, they leave, complain or stop trusting the place. This skill audits every listing, sets one source of truth, and makes the price honest from the first look.

## When to use

- A new venue, or a new owner taking over listings.
- A menu, price, fee or opening-hours change.
- Bookings fell, or guests complain the information was wrong.
- A hotel's Google booking links were switched off, or a price accuracy warning arrived.
- Before a busy season.

Not for: legal rulings on pricing, fees or contracts (point to the regulator's guidance and the owner's adviser), or changing payout and bank details.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read each listing, booking report and price setting (or screenshots). Level 2 to prepare the fix list and draft text. Level 3 to update menus, hours, photos and descriptions within the limits. A new fee, price or service charge that guests will see needs a fresh yes unless it is in the limits; payouts and bank details stay with the owner.

- Every place the venue is listed: Google, Apple Maps, booking sites (Booking.com, Expedia, Airbnb), reservation tools (OpenTable, Resy, SevenRooms), delivery apps, the website.
- The current menu, prices, fees and service charges exactly as charged, and whether each fee is optional or mandatory.
- Opening hours, holiday hours and private-hire closures.
- For hotels: the booking system or channel manager, Hotel Center status, any booking-site contract terms on rates.

## Steps

1. **List every listing** in a table: platform, who controls it, current hours, menu or room information, prices, photos, booking link, last updated.
2. **Choose the source of truth** for the menu and hours (usually the website menu or the till) and the order in which listings are updated.
3. **Google for restaurants.** Choose the menu editor or a menu link as the main menu, keep it current, and flag outdated customer menu photos. Check whether Google has copied menu items from the website and correct or opt out. Set the preferred order and booking providers; Reserve with Google providers appear within about a week and may charge; waitlists need a Reserve with Google provider. Menu changes can take 24 to 48 hours to show.
4. **Google for hotels.** Prices and booking links come from Hotel Ads and free booking links, fed by the booking system. Check the price accuracy score in Hotel Center: the total on the booking page must match Google's total for the same dates, room and guests, including resort and other mandatory fees, with optional extras not ticked by default. Fix the feed with the booking system provider before running ads. Amenities are edited in the dashboard; the hotel class is set by Google.
5. **Booking sites.** Check descriptions, photos, policies and review replies. Booking.com ranks by bookings, reviews, price, policies, photo quality, commission and programme status, and only guests who stayed can review. Read each site's partner terms before promoting a direct-booking perk.
6. **Delivery apps.** Menu text and allergen notes match the kitchen's records; information is given before purchase and at delivery in the UK.
7. **Check every price for mandatory charges.**
   - *US hotels and other short-term lodging:* the total price, including mandatory fees such as resort fees, comes first and is the most prominent price; only government charges and optional add-ons may be left out; any ad with a price shows the total (FTC rule, from 12 May 2025).
   - *US restaurants:* no federal rule found in the FTC's guidance; in California, a mandatory fee must be clearly shown wherever prices appear. Check other states.
   - *UK:* the price in any ad, post, email or web page includes mandatory charges, including a mandatory cover or service charge, cleaning fees, booking fees, resort fees and local taxes payable at a hotel; optional charges are disclosed but can be separate; amounts paid on arrival shown separately.
   - *EU:* total price with all taxes and charges; no pre-ticked extras.
8. **Write the fix list** in priority order: wrong hours and closed-door risks first, then price and fee issues, then menus, then photos and descriptions.
9. **Apply fixes** at the granted level, logging each change, and re-check each listing after it updates.
10. **Set a change routine:** every menu, price or hours change updates every listing the same day, in the agreed order.

## Checks

- Hours, holiday hours, menu and prices agree on every listing.
- One menu source on Google; stale menu photos flagged.
- Hotel totals match Google's price and include all mandatory fees.
- Mandatory fees and service charges are in the first price shown where the rules require.
- Booking-site perks checked against the venue's contract.
- Allergen notes on delivery apps match the kitchen's records.
- Every change logged; fresh-yes items marked.

## Output format

1. **Listing table** with current state and issues.
2. **Price and fee check** by country or state.
3. **Prioritised fix list** with the access level each fix needs.
4. **Change log** after fixes.
5. **Change routine** for future updates.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a 12-room inn with a restaurant. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

| Listing | Issue | Fix | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google (restaurant) | Menu photo from last winter shows old prices; menu link is current | Flag the old photo; set the menu link as preferred | L3 within limits |
| Google (hotel) | Price accuracy "At Risk": booking page adds a £15 nightly cleaning fee not in Google's price | Booking system provider to add the fee to the rate sent to Google | L2 note to provider; owner approves |
| Website rooms page | "From £95" excludes the mandatory cleaning fee | Change to "From £110 per night, including the £15 cleaning fee" | Fresh yes (new price shown) |
| Restaurant menu | "12.5% service charge" at the foot of the menu; owner says it is optional | Keep it separate, and label it "optional service charge" | L2 draft; owner approves |

**Change routine:** menu changes go to the till first, then the website, Google, booking sites and delivery apps the same day.
