# Menu and drinks copy

A menu line has one job: help a guest choose, quickly, and get what they expected. Tasting notes do the same for a drink. This skill writes both in the place's own voice, with allergen, calorie and alcohol rules handled from the records, not from guesswork.

## When to use

- A new or seasonal menu needs descriptions and a header line.
- A brewery, winery or distillery has a release and needs tasting notes, a short story, food pairings and serving notes.
- A sales team needs a one-page trade sheet for bars, restaurants and shops.
- A menu is being updated on Google, booking sites and delivery apps, and the words need to match.

Not for: allergen or health claims the kitchen or maker cannot verify, label and packaging copy (a separate legal job), or legal sign-off on alcohol advertising. Food-law and alcohol-rule questions go to the owner's adviser or local authority.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the menu, recipes and past copy. Level 2 to put drafts in a shared document or as unpublished items in the menu or till system. Level 3 to update the Google menu editor, website menu or delivery-app menus within the limits; a new price guests will see needs a fresh yes unless it is in the limits.

- The menu with ingredients and cooking methods, or the maker's notes (style, strength, ingredients, process).
- The kitchen's allergen records (matrix or recipe cards) and, where required, calorie figures.
- Prices exactly as charged, and any service or cover charge.
- Five examples of the owner's voice (past posts, a menu they like).
- Proof of any award or claim (certificate, link) the owner wants mentioned.
- Where the copy will run: printed menu, board, Google, delivery apps, website, social, trade sheet; and which countries or states.

## Steps

1. **Pull the facts first.** For each dish or drink, list: main ingredient, method, two or three taste or texture facts from the chef or maker, price, allergens from the records, and any provable claim. Mark gaps as [NEED].
2. **Pick the voice** with the owner: plain, warm, playful or upscale. Use their words where you find them.
3. **Write each menu line** in 20 words or fewer: main ingredient, method, one taste or texture word. Short enough for a board and a phone. No "best", "world-class", "mouth-watering" or "to die for".
4. **Write the header line:** one sentence on what the place is and why people come.
5. **Write tasting notes** in 40 to 60 words: what is on the nose, in the mouth and on the finish, in the maker's terms, plus style, strength as a plain fact, serving temperature or glass, and one or two pairings. Read them back to the maker and cut what they would not say.
6. **Add allergen information from the records only.**
   - *UK:* information for each of the 14 allergens an item contains, in writing (best practice, backed by a conversation) or with a clearly visible notice telling guests how to ask. Online orders: information before purchase and at delivery.
   - *US:* use the kitchen's records for the nine major allergens and follow the local food code.
   - *EU:* allergen information is mandatory for non-prepacked food; check the national rules on format.
   - Where no record exists: "Please ask our team about allergens before you order." No "free-from" claim unless the kitchen can rule out cross-contamination.
7. **Add calories where required:** US chains of 20 or more locations; businesses covered by England's out-of-home calorie rules (check the guidance). From the kitchen's figures only.
8. **Check alcohol copy** for where it will run.
   - *UK:* no appeal to under-18s; people shown drinking must be and look 25 or over; no health, mood or performance claims (only "low-alcohol", "reduced alcohol", "reduced energy"); no emphasis on strength; Portman Group code for naming, packaging and point of sale.
   - *US producers:* responsible advertiser name and contact, class or type, and alcohol content for spirits on ads, including menus, social posts and influencer posts; no health claims such as "clean" with health language; check state rules.
   - *EU:* check the member state's rules.
9. **Check prices and charges.** Prices match the till. A mandatory service or cover charge appears in the price guests first see in the UK, and is clearly shown wherever prices appear in California.
10. **Build the trade sheet** (drinks makers): name, style, strength, pack sizes, tasting note, why it sells, how to order, in a small table.

## Checks

- Every line uses only supplied facts; gaps are marked.
- Every allergen and calorie line comes from the kitchen's records.
- No health, award or "best" claims without proof.
- Alcohol copy meets the rules for each country or state where it runs.
- Prices and charges match what guests pay.
- The same words go to every listing: board, website, Google, booking sites, delivery apps.

## Output format

1. **Menu table:** dish, line, price, allergens (from records), calories if required, facts used.
2. **Header line,** two options.
3. **Tasting notes and trade sheet** (drinks).
4. **Rule notes:** allergen notice wording, alcohol checks done, any claim removed and why.
5. **Gaps** for the owner or kitchen.

## Example of good output (short)

*The Copper Kettle Café and Stonegate Brewing are made up, used only to show the format. Dishes, allergens and drinks are invented.*

| Dish | Menu line | Price | Allergens (kitchen matrix, 2 Nov) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smoked haddock rarebit | Smoked haddock under bubbling cheddar and mustard, on toasted sourdough | £9.50 | Fish, milk, mustard, cereals containing gluten |
| Beetroot and lentil salad | Roast beetroot, green lentils, orange and toasted seeds | £8.00 | [NEED: kitchen to confirm seeds; sesame?] |

**Notice (UK, on every menu page):** "Please ask our team about allergens before you order. We can show you the full list for every dish."

**Tasting note, Stonegate Hazy Pale, 5.2% ABV:** "Soft and cloudy. Mango and orange peel on the nose, a little bread and oats in the mouth, and a dry, gently bitter finish. Pour cold into a tulip glass. Good with fish tacos or a sharp cheddar."

**Removed:** "smooth and healthy" (health claim; not allowed in UK alcohol ads).
