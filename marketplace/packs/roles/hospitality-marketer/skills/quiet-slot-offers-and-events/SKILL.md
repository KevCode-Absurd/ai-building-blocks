# Quiet slot offers and events

A full room on a quiet night only helps if the night makes money and the offer doesn't empty a busy one. This skill finds the slots that need help, builds offers and events with the cost checked first, and writes the plan to fill them.

## When to use

- A restaurant, bar, café or taproom has the same dead nights every week.
- A hotel or venue has a slow season or empty midweek dates.
- A new menu, drink release or event series needs a launch plan.
- A private hire, tasting or supper club needs selling.

Not for: offers below cost without a reason the owner chooses, deals that encourage heavy drinking, or rewards for reviews.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read sales or bookings by day and hour, costs and past results. Level 2 to draft posts, emails, event pages and booking-system experiences. Level 3 to publish approved posts and pages and send approved campaigns within the limits; a new offer guests will see needs a fresh yes unless it is in the limits.

- Covers, bookings or sales by day and hour for recent months, and the same period last year if available.
- Food or drink cost per dish or drink (or recipe weights and supplier prices), and average spend per head.
- Capacity: seats, rooms, staff on shift.
- What the place is known for and what it never does.
- The event or holiday calendar, local events, and partners (a nearby theatre, a sports club, a supplier).
- For alcohol: who holds the licence, and the countries or states where promotions run.

## Steps

1. **Find the slots.** Rank days and hours by covers or bookings against capacity. Pick the one or two quietest slots that staff can still serve well.
2. **Find the reason to come.** For each slot, list who could come then (office workers, parents after drop-off, pre-theatre, locals on a school night) and what would bring them: a set menu, a quiz, a tasting, a release night, a local partner night, a class.
3. **Build three offers per slot,** each with: what the guest gets, price, food or drink cost, margin per cover, and the break-even number of extra covers. Prefer adding value (a paired glass, a dessert, a tasting flight) to cutting price. Drop any offer that loses money unless the owner chooses it as an investment for a stated reason.
4. **Protect busy nights.** No offer on nights already full; no deal that teaches regulars to wait for discounts. State the day, time, what is included and the end date exactly.
5. **Check drinks promotions.** UK licensed premises: no irresponsible promotions, free drinking water available, smaller measures available, an age verification policy, nothing below duty plus VAT; check with the licence holder. UK ads: no appeal to under-18s, people drinking must be and look 25 or over, no encouragement to drink more. US: check state rules on drink specials.
6. **Plan the event backward from the date:** announce (with booking link), remind, last call, day-of post, thank-you with photos and the next date. Add partner messages, a poster and window sign, and the day-of schedule.
7. **Write the pieces:** the post (one idea, one ask, a phone-only photo prompt), the email or text to regulars who agreed to hear from the venue, the event or booking page, the poster text.
8. **Set one measure per offer:** covers that night against the same night last month, redemptions, bookings from the link, spend per head.
9. **Prepare the go-live list.** At level 3, list each post, page and send with dates and get one yes before the first.
10. **Review after four weeks:** keep, change or drop each offer by its measure.

## Checks

- Every offer has a cost check and stays above the owner's margin floor, or the owner chose otherwise in writing.
- No offer on already-full nights.
- Deal, day, time, inclusions and end date stated exactly.
- Drinks promotions checked against licensing and ad rules.
- Prices shown include any mandatory charge where required.
- Messages go only to guests who agreed to hear from the venue.
- One measure per offer, with a review date.

## Output format

1. **Slot table:** day and hour, covers against capacity, who could come, reason to come.
2. **Offer table:** offer, price, cost, margin per cover, break-even extra covers, verdict.
3. **Event timeline** with every piece and its date.
4. **Copy:** posts, email or text, page, poster.
5. **Measure and review date.**
6. **Rule notes** (licensing, alcohol ads, pricing) and questions for the owner.

## Example of good output (short)

*Lucca's Trattoria is a made-up restaurant, used only to show the format. Figures are invented.*

| Offer (Tuesdays, 5:30 to 8pm) | Price per cover | Food and drink cost per cover | Margin per cover | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-course pasta night with a glass of house red | £22.00 | £7.40 | £14.60 | Run it: adds value without cutting the main's price |
| Two-for-one mains (£18 average main) | £9.00 | £5.40 | £3.60 | Drop: thin margin, and Friday regulars would ask for it too |
| Kids eat free with an adult main | £9.00 | £4.05 | £4.95 | Test in school holidays only |

**Post:** "Tuesday pasta night. Two courses and a glass of house red, £22, 5:30 to 8pm, until 31 March. Book at the link or just walk in." Photo prompt: the pasta on the pass, steam rising, shot from above on a phone.

**Measure:** Tuesday covers against the four Tuesdays before launch. Review on 2 April.
