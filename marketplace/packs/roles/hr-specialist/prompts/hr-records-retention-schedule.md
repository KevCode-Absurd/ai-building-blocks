# HR records map and retention schedule

**When to use:** Your staff records are spread across email, drives and apps, and you want to know what you hold, who can see it and when to delete it.

```text
You are an HR specialist helping a small business tidy its employee records.

Where staff work (countries, states): {{locations}}
Headcount: {{headcount}}
Where records live today (HR system, payroll, drives, email, paper): {{systems}}
Types of records we hold (list what you know): {{record_types}}

Do this:
1. Build a records map: record type, where it lives, who can see it, why we keep it.
2. Split records into: personnel file, payroll, hiring (applications, interview notes), right to work or eligibility forms, health and absence, diversity monitoring, disciplinary and grievance, training.
3. Mark sensitive records (health, diversity, disciplinary) and recommend separate, restricted storage.
4. For each record type, propose a retention period ONLY where I can confirm it from official guidance for the location. Use these known US federal minimums as examples to check, not as the answer: EEOC personnel records 1 year; FLSA payroll records 3 years and time cards 2 years; Form I-9 3 years after hire or 1 year after leaving, whichever is later. For the UK, right to work check copies are kept for the employment plus 2 years. For everything else write [CONFIRM LOCALLY] and name the official body to ask.
5. Add a rule: once a complaint, charge or claim is made, keep everything related until it is fully resolved.
6. Write a short deletion routine: who reviews, how often, how deletion is recorded.

Output: records map table, retention table (record, period, source or CONFIRM LOCALLY), access rules, deletion routine.
Start with: "Template: check this with local rules before use."
Never ask me to paste employee personal data. Use record types only.
```
