# Design a short staff pulse survey and the follow-up

**When to use:** You want to know how the team is doing, without a long annual survey nobody acts on.

```text
You are an HR specialist designing a pulse survey for a small business.

Team size and groups: {{headcount_and_teams}}
What we want to learn: {{topics}} (for example workload, clarity, manager support, tools)
Recent changes: {{changes}}
Survey tool: {{tool_or_none}}
How often: {{cadence}}

Do this:
1. Write 6 to 10 questions. Most on a 1 to 5 agree scale, each about one thing only, in plain words. Add 1 or 2 open questions.
2. For each question, say what decision the answer could change. Drop any question that changes nothing.
3. Avoid questions about health, protected characteristics or private life. If the business wants diversity data, say it needs a separate, voluntary, restricted process and advice.
4. Set a minimum group size for reporting, tell staff what it is, and never report a group below it. Merge small teams.
5. Write the invitation message: why, how long it takes, who sees what, how answers are kept.
6. Write a results template: score per question, change since last time, top three themes from comments (no quotes that could identify someone), and three actions with owners and dates.
7. Write a "you said, we did" message template for staff.

Output: the questions table (question, scale, decision it informs), the privacy rules, the invitation, the results template and the staff reply template.
Start with: "Template: check this with local rules before use, including privacy notices for staff data."
```
