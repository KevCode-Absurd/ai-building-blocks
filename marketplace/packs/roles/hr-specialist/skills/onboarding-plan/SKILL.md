# Onboarding plan

## When to use

- An offer has been accepted and the start date is set.
- New people take months to become useful, or leave in the first year.
- Day one is forms, a laptop and "shadow someone".
- A manager is onboarding their first ever hire.

## What you need

1. Role, manager, team and start date.
2. The six-month outcomes from the hiring kit (or ask the manager for three).
3. Location and working pattern (on site, hybrid, remote; country and state). This sets the paperwork.
4. Systems and tools the person needs on day one.
5. Two or three people who could act as a buddy.
6. Any known constraints: shift patterns, travel, training that must happen before certain work.

**Access:** level 1 to read the role, team and start date in your HR system; level 2 to set up the onboarding tasks and the welcome note without assigning or sending them; level 3 to assign the tasks, send the welcome note and book the check-ins, within your limits. At a lower level I hand you the plan to enter yourself.

## The model

Plan all four building blocks (Talya Bauer, SHRM Foundation, 2010):

| Block | Question it answers | Typical items |
|---|---|---|
| Compliance | What must be done by law or policy? | Contract or written statement, right to work check, payroll and tax forms, policies read, safety briefing |
| Clarification | What is my job and how will I be judged? | Outcomes, first real task, who decides what, how work is reviewed |
| Culture | How do things really work here? | How decisions get made, how people ask for help, unwritten rules made written |
| Connection | Who do I need to know? | Buddy, meetings with key people, team rituals |

Most small firms cover compliance and stop. The aim is all four, on purpose.

## Steps

### 1. Before day one (from offer accepted)

- Send a welcome note from the manager with the start time, place or link, who will meet them and what to bring (level 3 sends the approved note; otherwise the manager sends it).
- Start the paperwork early. In Great Britain the written statement of particulars is due on or before day one. In the US, Form I-9 has its own timing. Do the right to work or eligibility step exactly as the official guidance says; never improvise it and never give immigration advice.
- Order equipment and create accounts so they work on day one.
- Pick and brief the buddy: 20 minutes a day in week one, then weekly.
- Book the 30, 60 and 90 day check-ins in both calendars now (level 3 in the calendar; otherwise list them for the manager to book).

### 2. Day one

- Morning: welcome by the manager, tour (or video walk-through), equipment and accounts checked.
- One hour on the job itself: the outcomes, how this role helps the business, what the first week holds.
- Lunch or a call with the buddy and team.
- Afternoon: one small, real, finishable task. People remember whether they did something useful on day one.
- End of day: five-minute check-in with the manager. "What was confusing?"

### 3. Week one

- Day-by-day list of who they meet and why (15 to 30 minutes each).
- Read the policies that matter for the job, not the whole handbook at once.
- One real task per day, growing in size.
- End-of-week conversation: what is clear, what is not, what is in the way.

### 4. 30, 60 and 90 day goals

Write two or three goals per stage that step up toward the six-month outcomes:

- **30 days, learning:** knows the tools, has met key people, has finished small tasks with help.
- **60 days, contributing:** does core tasks alone, owns one piece of work.
- **90 days, owning:** delivers against one outcome measure, suggests one improvement.

Each goal is observable. Avoid "understands the culture"; prefer "runs the Monday stand-up alone".

### 5. Check-ins

Use the same five questions each time, and write down the answers:
1. What is going well?
2. What is harder than you expected?
3. What do you need from me?
4. Who have you not met that you should?
5. On a 1 to 5 scale, how clear is what success looks like for you?

Where there is a probation period, the 30, 60 and 90 day check-ins double as probation reviews. In the UK, from 1 January 2027 the qualifying period for ordinary unfair dismissal is six months, so honest, written feedback in these months matters more. Write what was observed, what was agreed and the next date. If concerns are serious, follow the probation policy and get advice before any decision.

### 6. Ninety-day look back

The new hire and manager each answer: what would have made the first 90 days better? Feed the answers into the template for the next hire.

## Checks before handing over

- All four blocks have at least two items.
- There is a real task on day one and on every day of week one.
- Check-ins are booked, with the five questions attached.
- Goals are observable and step toward the six-month outcomes.
- Paperwork steps name the official source for the location and do not give legal or immigration advice.
- The plan carries: *Check this with local rules before use.*

## Output format

One markdown document:

1. Summary (person, role, manager, buddy, start date, location)
2. Before day one checklist (owner, due date)
3. Day one schedule (time, activity, who)
4. Week one plan (day, meetings, task)
5. 30, 60, 90 day goals table
6. Check-in dates and questions
7. Paperwork list with the official source for each item
8. Look back questions

## Example of good output (short)

> **Template: check this with local rules before use. Applies to: Austin, Texas, US, hybrid.**
>
> | Day | Meet (why) | Real task |
> |---|---|---|
> | Mon | Buddy Ana (how we work), Ops lead Raj (order flow) | Fix the five oldest open tickets with Ana |
> | Tue | Finance, Dee (refund rules) | Process today's refund requests, Dee checks |
> | Wed | Two customers on calls (listen only) | Write a one-page summary of what they asked |
>
> **60-day goal:** Handles the refund queue alone, with fewer than 2 in 50 needing rework.
