# Policy template

## When to use

- A business needs a new policy (remote work, leave, expenses, equipment, conduct, social media, use of AI tools at work, data and privacy).
- An existing handbook is long, out of date or contradicts itself.
- Staff keep asking the same question, which means a rule is missing or unclear.

Do not use it to decide whether a rule is lawful. Policies on pay, hours, leave entitlements, monitoring, health data, discipline and dismissal touch the law in every country. Draft the template, list the legal questions and send them to an employment lawyer or official helpline.

## What you need

1. The topic and the problem the policy solves ("people expense things differently and finance cannot close the month").
2. Location of staff (countries, states). One template may need local versions.
3. Headcount and whether any staff are covered by a union or works council agreement.
4. What happens today, in practice.
5. The owner's decisions, or their leanings, on the key choices (see step 2).
6. Related documents: contracts, written statements, other policies, the handbook index.

**Access:** level 1 to read the handbook and policies where they live; level 2 to write the policy in a draft copy or as suggested edits; level 3 to publish the approved version and send the read-and-confirm request to the agreed staff, within your limits. Publishing always waits for the owner's approval of the text and the open legal questions.

## Steps

### 1. Find the real rule

Ask what decision staff need to make, and what the business needs to control. A policy that answers no real question is noise. Write one sentence: "This policy tells [who] how to [do what], so that [business reason]."

### 2. List the decisions before drafting

Every policy hides choices only the owner can make. List them as questions with options, for example for a remote work policy:
- Who is eligible? (all roles, some roles, case by case)
- Which days or hours must people be reachable?
- Who pays for equipment and internet?
- Can people work from another country? (flag: tax, payroll and immigration issues; needs specialist advice)
- How are requests decided and appealed?

Get the answers, or mark the template with [DECISION NEEDED] at each point.

### 3. Draft with the fixed skeleton

Use the same headings for every policy so the handbook reads as one document:

1. **Purpose** (one or two sentences)
2. **Who this covers** (employees, workers, contractors; locations)
3. **The rule** (numbered, one rule per line)
4. **How to ask, request or report** (who, how, by when)
5. **How decisions are made** (who decides, what they consider, how long it takes, how to appeal)
6. **What happens if the policy is not followed** (link to the disciplinary procedure; do not invent penalties)
7. **Related policies and documents**
8. **Data and privacy** (what personal data the policy creates, who sees it, how long it is kept)
9. **Owner, version, date, next review date**

### 4. Write it plainly

- Grade 8 to 9 reading level. Short sentences. "You" for the reader, "we" for the business.
- One rule per line. Put numbers where people need them (amounts, days, deadlines).
- Use "must" for rules, "should" for good practice, "can" for permissions. Never mix them.
- Inclusive, neutral wording: no gendered pronouns for roles, no assumptions about family set-up, identity-first or person-first language as people prefer.
- No legal Latin, no "notwithstanding", no 40-word sentences.

### 5. Add the staff summary

Five bullet points at the top: what changed, what you must do, who to ask. Most people will read only this.

### 6. Mark the legal questions

End with "Open questions for a lawyer or adviser". Typical ones:
- Does this rule change a contract term, and does it need consent or consultation?
- Does it meet the minimum legal entitlement here (leave, pay, rest breaks)?
- Does monitoring or data collection need a privacy notice or an impact assessment?
- Could any part affect one protected group more than others?

### 7. Plan the roll-out

- Who must approve it, and who must be consulted (staff representatives, union) where that applies.
- How staff will be told and asked to confirm they have read it (level 3 sends the approved request).
- Where it will live (one place, one current version). At level 3, publish the approved version there and mark the old one as replaced, never deleted.
- When it will be reviewed (at least yearly, and when the law changes).

## Checks before handing over

- Every [DECISION NEEDED] is either answered or listed for the owner.
- The template box sits at the top: *Template: check this with local rules before use. Applies to: [locations].*
- No sentence states that the policy is legal, compliant or "meets all requirements".
- The rule section uses must, should and can consistently.
- Personal data created by the policy is named, with who sees it and how long it is kept.
- Owner, version, date and review date are filled in.
- Reading level checked: no sentence over 25 words.

## Output format

1. Template box (applies to, version, check-with-local-rules line)
2. Staff summary (five bullets)
3. The policy, using the fixed skeleton
4. Decisions for the owner (table: question, options, recommendation, status)
5. Open questions for a lawyer or adviser
6. Roll-out checklist

## Example of good output (short)

> **Template: check this with local rules before use. Applies to: staff in England and Ireland. Version 0.1, 6 Nov 2026. Owner: Operations lead.**
>
> **Staff summary**
> - You can claim back reasonable costs of travel for work that is not your normal commute.
> - Submit claims in the expenses app within 30 days, with a photo of each receipt.
> - Your manager approves claims within 5 working days.
>
> **The rule**
> 1. You must get your manager's approval before booking travel over €300.
> 2. You should book the cheapest reasonable option.
> 3. You can claim a meal allowance of [DECISION NEEDED: amount] on overnight trips.
>
> **Open questions for a lawyer or adviser**
> - Are the mileage and meal rates tax-free at these amounts in each country?
