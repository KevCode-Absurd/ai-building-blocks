# Email or chat micro-course

**When to use:** You want to teach new customers, staff or followers one practical skill through short lessons by email, text, WhatsApp or Slack. Use it to get a course map, a welcome, every lesson with a task and a check, and a wrap-up.

```text
You are a learning content writer. Write a micro-course that teaches one thing at a time and gets people to act.

What readers can do after the last lesson: {{end_goal}}
Who they are and why they signed up: {{audience_and_signup}}
Source material (guide, manual, blog posts, notes): {{source_material}}
Channel (email, text, WhatsApp, Slack) and how often lessons go out: {{channel_and_frequency}}
Number of lessons: {{lesson_count}}
Anything you want to invite them to at the end (optional): {{final_invite}}

Steps:
1. Split the goal into small skills, one per lesson, each building on the last. If there are more skills than lessons, say which to drop.
2. Draft the course map: lesson, skill, send day.
3. Write the welcome: what they will learn, how often lessons come, and how to reply or stop.
4. Write each lesson: a subject line under 60 characters, preview text, a lesson under 350 words (or one message for chat), one task they can do in about ten minutes, and one quick check question.
5. Write the wrap-up: what they learned, one next step, and at most one honest invite.

Rules: every tip and fact comes from the source material; mark gaps [confirm with your team]. One idea per lesson. No fake urgency, no income or health promises. Send only to people who signed up. For email in the US, include the sender's postal address and a working unsubscribe, and keep subject lines honest; in the UK and EU, follow the national regulator's consent rules. Plain text that reads well on a phone.

Output: course map table, welcome, each lesson in order, wrap-up, and a list of sending settings for the client to check before switching it on.
```
