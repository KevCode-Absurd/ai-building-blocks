# Training interview: instructional designer

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client confidential material, learner names or real customer details; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of learning do you design (staff e-learning, paid courses, customer academies, email courses, video), and for which sectors?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's range and the formats it should be strongest in.

2. **When a client hands you a 60-page policy and asks for a 10-minute module, what do you say?**
   Why it matters: captures how the expert cuts scope and keeps reference material out of screens.

3. **How do you turn a subject expert's notes into goals? Show me a weak goal you rewrote.**
   Why it matters: goal-writing drives everything else; the agent needs the expert's habits.

4. **Do you write the checks before the content? Walk me through how.**
   Why it matters: backward design in practice, not in theory.

5. **Show me a storyboard you are proud of. What makes it easy for a developer to build?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a real model of format and build notes.

6. **How often do you put practice in a module, and what kinds of practice work best online?**
   Why it matters: practice density separates real e-learning from page-turners.

7. **How do you write distractors that are tempting but fair?**
   Why it matters: question quality is where most courses are weakest.

8. **Walk me through a branching scenario you designed. How did you keep the number of paths manageable?**
   Why it matters: scenario mapping is a craft skill the agent must copy.

9. **What do you do when the client's "right answer" in a scenario looks wrong or legally risky?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's line between design and legal or HR judgement.

10. **How do you script a how-to video so it stays short and still works for someone who cannot see the screen?**
    Why it matters: captures timing and integrated description habits.

11. **How do you handle captions and transcripts, and which tools do you trust for them?**
    Why it matters: caption quality is visible and often rushed.

12. **Which WCAG checks do e-learning tools fail most often in your experience, and how do you test them?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's real accessibility test routine.

13. **How do you decide between SCORM 1.2, SCORM 2004, xAPI and cmi5? Tell me about a package that broke in an LMS.**
    Why it matters: packaging decisions and failures are learned the hard way.

14. **Which authoring tools do you use, and what do you never do in them?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's tool advice and build notes.

15. **How do you write an email or chat course that people actually finish?**
    Why it matters: micro-courses need different pacing from modules.

16. **How do you link a customer academy to support tickets, and how do you report on it honestly?**
    Why it matters: customer education is judged on tickets; the agent must not overclaim.

17. **What do you look for in a pilot, and what was the most surprising thing a pilot showed you?**
    Why it matters: piloting habits catch problems the designer cannot see.

18. **How do you write sales copy for a course without over-promising?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's honesty line for course marketing.

19. **Tell me about your hardest design job. What went wrong, and what would you do now?**
    Why it matters: a real story gives the agent judgement that rules cannot.

20. **What do you check before you send a storyboard or script to a client?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's personal quality bar.

21. **Which design myths do clients bring you (learning styles, the learning pyramid, attention spans), and how do you answer them?**
    Why it matters: helps the agent push back politely with evidence.
