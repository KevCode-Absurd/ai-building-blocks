# Lighting and colour plan

Light and colour change how big, warm and clean a room feels for less money than new furniture. They also decide whether people can read a menu, see a step, find the door or lip-read at a counter. This skill plans both together, because a colour only exists in the light that falls on it.

## When to use

- The room feels dark, flat or cold, or customers say they can't read the menu or see the products.
- A salon needs true colour at the mirror, a studio needs even light for work, a shop needs light on the stock.
- A holiday let or cafe is being refreshed or photographed.
- Doors, steps, counters or toilets are hard to pick out.

Not for: new circuits, moved switches or sockets, fittings in bathrooms or near water, or emergency lighting design. Those need a registered electrician and, where required, building regulations approval.

## What you need

- **Room measurements, ceiling height, window sizes and which way they face.**
- **Photos** in daylight and after dark, with the current lights on.
- **Current fittings:** where they are, what type, whether dimmable, and where the switches are.
- **Brand colours** (codes if known) and finishes the client wants to keep.
- **What happens in each zone:** reading, cutting hair, eating, browsing, working at a screen.
- **Budget and whether the client rents** (paint and fixings may need landlord consent).
- **Access I have:** level 1 to read photos and plans; level 2 to draft the plan, a mood board and a shopping list; level 3 to update the shared plan or board the client named.

## Steps

1. **Read the daylight.** Where it comes from, when, and where glare or deep shadow falls. Plan seats and tasks to use daylight without glare on screens or mirrors.
2. **Set light levels by task.** Use the HSE's minimums as a floor, not a design target: about 20 lux average for corridors and circulation, 100 lux for work with limited detail such as kitchens, 200 lux for work needing detail such as offices, 500 lux for fine detail (HSG38, 1997). Tasks like colouring hair or reading small print need more light at the task, not more light everywhere. Avoid big jumps in brightness between neighbouring areas.
3. **Plan three layers.**
   - **General (ambient):** even, soft light so people can move and see faces.
   - **Task:** light where work happens: the mirror, the counter, the till, the prep area, a desk.
   - **Accent:** light on products, art, a feature wall or a window display, to draw the eye.
   For each fitting: type, position, warm or cool white, dimmable or not, and the zone it serves.
4. **Choose warm or cool.** Warmer white for relaxing (cafes, lounges, bedrooms), neutral for working and selling, and the same colour temperature within one zone so surfaces don't clash. Where colour judgement matters (salons, product photos, food), ask for lamps with a high colour rendering index.
5. **Check glare and reflections.** No bare lamp in the line of sight from seats or the counter. Avoid glossy surfaces and large repeating patterns where people read, lip-read or sign, such as a reception desk (Approved Document M Volume 2, 4.32).
6. **Plan colour.** Main (most of the walls), secondary (one wall, joinery or furniture) and accent (small items, a door, signage). For each: name or code, finish, where it goes and why. Test samples on the real wall in daylight and after dark.
7. **Use contrast to help people find things.** Doors against walls, floors against walls, step edges, counters and toilet fittings should stand out. In England, Approved Document M Volume 2 treats a light reflectance value (LRV) difference of more than 30 points as a visual contrast (20 points where light levels are over 200 lux). Ask the paint maker for LRVs.
8. **Choose finishes.** Matt hides wall flaws but marks easily; eggshell or satin wipes clean in busy areas; gloss for trim only where shine won't glare. Ventilate while painting and follow the label.
9. **Mark the electrician points.** New fittings on new wiring, moved switches or sockets, anything in a bathroom or near a basin, and any change to emergency lighting: "registered electrician; may need building regulations approval". Plug-in lamps and like-for-like bulb swaps don't.
10. **Draft it.** Level 1: the plan as tables. Level 2: a mood board and shopping list in the client's tool. Level 3: update the shared board or plan named; log it.

## Checks

- Every zone has general light, and every task has task light.
- Light levels meet at least the HSE minimum for the activity, with the source named.
- One colour temperature per zone; high colour rendering where colour matters.
- No glare from bare lamps at seats, mirrors or the counter.
- Doors, floors and step edges contrast with their surroundings, with LRVs where known.
- Every wiring change is marked for a registered electrician.
- Samples are tested in place before buying paint in bulk.

## Output format

1. **Daylight notes.**
2. **Lighting table:** zone, layer, fitting, position, warm or cool, dimmable, electrician needed (yes or no).
3. **Colour table:** role (main, secondary, accent, contrast), colour and code, finish, LRV if known, where, why.
4. **Contrast checks:** pair, LRV difference, pass or change.
5. **Professional points.**
6. **Shopping list lines** for fittings, bulbs and paint, with budget.
7. **Access and log.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Velvet Lane Salon is a made-up salon, 6 x 4 m with one front window, used only to show the format. Goal: brighter, truer colour at the mirrors. Access I have: none (photos only).*

| Zone | Layer | Fitting | Position | White | Dimmable | Electrician |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Styling (3 chairs) | Task | Vertical light either side of each mirror | Mirror wall, at face height | Neutral, high colour rendering | No | Yes: new wiring |
| Whole room | General | Replace two spotlights with an even ceiling panel | Centre of the ceiling | Neutral | Yes | Yes: new fitting |
| Retail shelf | Accent | Plug-in shelf strip | Under each shelf | Neutral | No | No: plug-in |

| Role | Colour | Finish | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main | Warm white, LRV from the paint maker | Matt | Three walls and ceiling | Bounces light, hides patched walls |
| Secondary | Sage green | Eggshell | Mirror wall | Calm backdrop, wipes clean |
| Contrast | Dark grey | Satin | Door frame to the back room and the step edge | Makes the step and door easy to see |

**Professional points:** mirror lights and the ceiling panel need a registered electrician. Test paint samples on the mirror wall morning and evening before buying.
