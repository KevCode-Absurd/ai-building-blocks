# Refresh budget and order of work

A good plan fails if it costs more than the budget or closes the business for a fortnight. This skill turns the design into a list the owner can buy from, a set of briefs for the trades, and a timetable that keeps trading going wherever it can.

## When to use

- After the space plan, lighting and colour plan or display plan is approved.
- When a client asks "what will it cost and how long will we be closed?"
- Before a holiday let is styled and photographed.

Not for: quotes for building work (ask contractors), tax or grant questions (ask a qualified accountant), or approving work under building regulations (building control).

## What you need

- **The approved plans:** placement table, lighting and colour tables, display plan.
- **Budget:** total, any split the client wants, and a contingency they accept.
- **What the client owns** and is willing to move, repaint or reupholster.
- **Opening hours and quiet days**, bookings for holiday lets, and any date the work must finish by.
- **Who does what:** the owner, staff, a decorator, an electrician, a joiner.
- **Country and region**, for the safety checks and the trades that need registration.
- **Access I have:** level 1 to read; level 2 to build the shopping list, saved baskets and briefs; level 3 to keep the shared list current and send approved briefs to the trades the client named for quotes. Placing orders, accepting quotes and paying need the client's fresh yes each time, and paying stays with the client.

## Steps

1. **List every item in the plans:** owned (move, repaint, repair) or new. Owned items first.
2. **Price new items** from suppliers the client uses or "any" with a typical retail price range the client can confirm. Never quote a price you haven't seen; label estimates.
3. **Check safety for each new or second-hand item.**
   - UK: upholstered furniture and furnishings should carry the fire safety label under the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988; check each model on the GOV.UK product safety alerts, reports and recalls database.
   - US: dressers and chests at least 27 in tall made after 1 September 2023 should come with an anti-tip device and pass CPSC stability tests; check the CPSC recall list.
   - EU: check Safety Gate.
   - Holiday lets in England: no candles, tea lights or ethanol burners on the list (Home Office small paying guest guide).
   - Paints: low-odour where people work nearby; ventilate and follow the label.
4. **Mark trades and sign-offs.** Electrics (registered electrician; some work needs building regulations approval), plumbing and gas (registered trades), walls, doors, windows or exits (building control), anything in an older building that disturbs walls, ceilings, floors or old paint (UK: ask for the asbestos records; US: lead-safe certified firms for pre-1978 homes and child care buildings). Fire risk assessment review by the responsible person when layouts or covers change.
5. **Total the budget.** By room or zone, with a contingency line the client accepts. If over budget, cut in this order: decorative extras, then items guests don't touch, then size or quantity; protect safety items, seating comfort and lighting.
6. **Order of work.** Sequence: clear and protect, trades first (electrics, joinery), then paint (dry time before furniture), then flooring if any, then furniture, lighting, soft furnishings and styling, then clean and photograph. Group noisy or messy work into closed days or out-of-hours; do quiet work zone by zone while trading. Note lead times so items arrive before they're needed.
7. **Briefs for trades.** One page each: what, where, measurements, finish, date window, access, what to protect. At level 3, send to the trades the client named for quotes; accepting a quote needs the client's yes.
8. **Prepare orders.** Level 2: saved baskets or draft orders by supplier. Show the total, delivery dates and returns terms. The client places the order and pays, or gives a fresh yes for each order.
9. **Track and log.** Keep the shared list current (ordered, delivered, fitted) and log every action.

## Checks

- Owned items are reused before buying new.
- Every new item has a price source or is labelled an estimate.
- Every upholstered item, dresser and child-related item has its safety check recorded.
- Trades and sign-offs are named with who to ask, and nothing needing a registered trade is planned as DIY.
- The total is within budget including contingency, or the cuts are listed.
- Closure days are minimised and named.
- No order placed, quote accepted or payment made without the client's yes.

## Output format

1. **Budget summary:** total, by zone, contingency, against the budget.
2. **Shopping list:** item, owned or new, quantity, size, supplier or "any", price or estimate, safety check, status.
3. **Trades and sign-offs:** what, who, why, when.
4. **Order of work:** day by day, what happens, who, open or closed, notes.
5. **Trade briefs** (one page each).
6. **Draft orders** (level 2) and approvals needed.
7. **Access and log.**

**Trades:** a second bedroom ceiling light needs a registered electrician (new wiring). Ask the owner, as responsible person, to update the recorded fire risk assessment after the change.

**Approvals needed:** each basket order, the upholstery quote and the electrician's quote.
