# Space plan and clearances

A space plan decides where everything goes and how people move between it. Most small-space problems are clearance problems: chairs that block the route to the toilet, a counter nobody in a wheelchair can use, a display that narrows the exit. This skill places furniture by measurement and checks every route before counting seats.

## When to use

- A cafe wants more covers, a salon wants another station, a shop wants a better route round the floor.
- A small office or workroom needs desks, storage and walkways.
- A holiday let room needs a layout that photographs well and is safe for guests.

Not for: moving walls, doors, windows, stairs or exits; changing electrics, plumbing, gas or ventilation; deciding whether work needs building regulations approval or meets fire safety law. Those go to building control, the right trade, or a fire risk assessor.

## What you need

- **Measurements:** wall lengths, ceiling height, every door (clear width and swing direction), windows (width and sill height), sockets and switches, radiators, columns, steps or level changes, and fixed items such as counters, basins and the toilet.
- **Photos** of each wall and a rough sketch, with the entrance and exits marked.
- **The goal** in one line, such as "seat 20 with room for a pram" or "three styling stations and a retail shelf".
- **What stays:** furniture and fittings the client keeps.
- **Rules:** lease or landlord limits, and the country and region.
- **Who uses the space:** customers, staff, wheelchair users, prams, children, guests with luggage.
- **Access I have:** level 1 to read plans and photos; level 2 to draw a draft plan in the client's tool or a copy of their model; level 3 to update the shared plan they named.

## Steps

1. **Check the measurements.** Add the wall lengths; opposite walls should roughly agree. List anything missing and say exactly what to measure (for example, "door to the toilet: clear opening width with the door open at 90 degrees").
2. **Write the goal and the limits.** One line for the goal; a short list of what can't move.
3. **Mark the fixed routes first.** Draw the route from the entrance to each exit, the toilet, the counter and the main zones. These routes are kept clear before any furniture goes in.
   - US: at least 36 in (915 mm) clear on the accessible route, with a 60 in (1525 mm) turning circle or T-shape where people need to turn (ADA 2010 Standards).
   - England: corridors and passageways at least 1200 mm unobstructed, with 1800 mm passing places where narrower than 1800 mm (Approved Document M Volume 2).
   - Workplaces in the US: exit access at least 28 in (71.1 cm) wide and kept free of furniture and stock (OSHA).
   - Elsewhere: use the national building guidance, and say "check this with local rules".
4. **Set the zones.** Entrance and waiting, service or counter, seating or stations, display, staff and storage. Give each zone its size.
5. **Place furniture with sizes.** For each item: size, position measured from two walls, clearance kept on each side used, and why it goes there. Leave room behind chairs for people to sit and stand, and for staff to pass with trays or trolleys.
6. **Check counters and tables.**
   - US: an accessible part of a sales or service counter at most 36 in (915 mm) high (check-out counters 38 in, 965 mm); dining and work surfaces 28 to 34 in (710 to 865 mm) high for at least 5% of seats, with knee clearance.
   - England: a reception or sales counter section at least 1500 mm wide and no higher than 760 mm with a knee recess; part of a bar or serving counter no higher than 850 mm.
7. **Check doors and reach.** Door clear widths against the guide (US 32 in, 815 mm; England Table 2 values). Anything customers must reach or use within 48 in (1220 mm) high and 15 in (380 mm) low (US reach ranges). Wall-mounted items on walkways between 27 and 80 in high stick out no more than 4 in (100 mm).
8. **Check exits and fire points.** No furniture, stock or display in an exit route or in front of a fire door, extinguisher or exit sign. If seating numbers rise or routes change, tell the client to have the fire risk assessment reviewed by the responsible person.
9. **Mark professional points.** Anything that touches walls, doors, electrics, plumbing, ventilation or exits: "ask building control" or the trade named.
10. **Draw it.**
    - Level 1: a text zone map and placement table.
    - Level 2: draw the plan in the client's tool, or in a copy of their model named "DRAFT".
    - Level 3: update the shared plan they named; log the change.
11. **Count the result** against the goal, and say what was given up to reach it.

## Checks

- Every item has a size and a measured position; nothing overlaps.
- Routes to the entrance, exits, toilet and counter keep the widths named, with the source.
- At least one counter section and the required share of tables are usable seated.
- No furniture blocks a door swing, exit, fire door or exit sign.
- Professional points are marked with who to ask.
- The plan says "check this with local rules" where the region's rules aren't covered.

## Output format

1. **Goal and limits.**
2. **Missing measurements** (if any).
3. **Zone map:** a text diagram, top-down, with the door and windows marked.
4. **Placement table:** item, size, position, clearance kept, reason.
5. **Route and counter checks:** each check, the value used, the source, pass or adjust.
6. **Professional points:** what, why, who to ask.
7. **Result against the goal.**
8. **Access and log.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Birch and Bean is a made-up corner cafe in England, used only to show the format. Room 8.0 m wide x 5.0 m deep; counter 5.5 m long and 0.7 m deep along the back wall from the left corner; toilet door in the back wall at the right; entrance in the centre of the front wall. Goal: seat 20, up from 14, with room for a pram and a wheelchair. Access I have: none (photos and sketch only).*

**Zone map (front wall at the bottom; depth 5.0 m = counter 0.7 + back route 1.2 + seating 3.1):**

```
back wall:  [ counter 5.5 m x 0.7 m ............ ]      [WC door]
            back route: 1.2 m clear along the counter to the WC
left 3.4 m: [2-top] [2-top]  | centre | right 3.4 m: [low 4-top] + 1.5 m turning space
            (0.8 m gap)      | route  |              (gap)
            [bench 3.2 m,    | 1.2 m  |              [2-top] [2-top]
             4 tables]       |        |
front wall: ------------------[ entrance ]---------------------------
```

| Item | Size | Position | Clearance kept | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wall bench + 4 tables, 4 chairs | 3.2 m bench; tables 0.6 x 0.6 m | Front wall, from the left corner | 0.8 m between chair backs and the next row; centre route untouched | 8 seats in the window |
| Two 2-tops | 0.6 x 0.6 m each, chairs at the sides | Left block, against the back route edge | 1.2 m back route kept | 4 seats |
| Low 4-top, open knee space | 1.2 x 0.8 m, top at 750 mm | Right block, beside the back route near the WC | 1.5 m turning circle beside it | 4 seats; wheelchair or pram space at one end |
| Two 2-tops | 0.6 x 0.6 m each | Right block, front window | Centre route untouched | 4 seats |

**Checks:** back and centre routes 1200 mm, using Approved Document M Vol 2 (3.14) as the guide; low table 750 mm with knee space. Counter: part of it should be no higher than 850 mm (4.16); the current height is not in the brief, so measure it. Seats: 8 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 20.

**Professional points:** lowering part of the counter needs a joiner, and any change to its plumbing or power needs the trade. Covers rise from 14 to 20, so ask the responsible person to review the fire risk assessment.
