# Window and display plan

A window has a few seconds to make someone slow down. A display inside has a few more to make them pick something up. This skill turns a brief into a plan someone can build in an afternoon: one idea, a clear focal point, every product and prop placed, lit and safe.

## When to use

- A monthly or seasonal window change, or a launch.
- A table, shelf or wall display for a new range or an offer.
- A market stall or pop-up table display (pair with the `retail-manager` kit's pop-up-and-market-plan skill for stock, float and staffing).
- A year of window themes (see the seasonal-window-calendar prompt).

Not for: electrical work in the window, structural fixings, large suspended loads, or planograms for chains with many branches.

## What you need

- **Space measurements:** width, depth and height of the window or display area, any step or plinth, back wall or open back, where the door and exits are, power points.
- **Fixed items:** shelving, plinths, existing lights.
- **Products to show:** list with sizes, quantities and prices, and which ones the shop most wants to sell.
- **Goal and dates:** what the display should do (draw people in, launch, clear stock, show a gift range) and the run dates.
- **Budget for props** and what the shop already owns.
- **Rules:** landlord or shopping centre rules on windows, signs and fixings.
- **Access I have:** level 1 to read photos; level 2 to draft the plan, a mood board and a props basket; level 3 to update the shared plan named. Buying props needs a fresh yes each time, and paying stays with the client.

## Steps

1. **Read the space.** List measurements and anything missing ("measure the window floor depth from the glass to the back panel"). Note where people walk past, from which side, and at what distance they first see the window.
2. **Write the idea in one line**, tied to the goal and season: "Autumn reading nook: settle in with a book this October."
3. **Set the focal point.** One hero item or group, placed where the eye lands first for people walking past. Set its height from the eye level of passers-by in this street; measure on site rather than using a fixed figure. Light it.
4. **Build the composition.** A triangle or step layout so heights lead the eye from low at the sides to the focal point. Group products in odd numbers, vary heights with risers, crates or books, and leave space around the hero. Repeat a shape or colour to make the idea read from a distance.
5. **Place every item.** A placement table: item, position (left, centre, right; front, middle, back), height, facing, and why. A top-down zone map in text.
6. **Make it reachable and safe inside the shop.** Anything customers must pick up, test or read goes within the reach ranges for seated people (US ADA: 15 to 48 in, 380 to 1220 mm). Shelves and props on walkways must not stick out more than 4 in (100 mm) between 27 and 80 in high. Stacks stable, heavy items low, no trip hazards, nothing in a doorway, exit route or in front of a fire door, extinguisher or exit sign.
7. **Plan the light.** What to light (the hero first), warm or cool white to suit the products, and where the fitting goes. Check heat on products and props (chocolate, candles, fabrics, paper) and keep cables away from walkways. Plug-in fittings only; anything wired goes to an electrician.
8. **Sign spots.** Where the price or offer sign sits, its size class (readable from the pavement or from arm's length), and the copy from the shop's promotion plan. Layout and print files follow the `graphic-designer` kit.
9. **Props and budget.** Reuse what the shop owns first. List new props with quantity, size, budget line and a fire safety check for fabrics and anything near lights. Total under budget.
10. **Build and take-down steps** with time estimates: clear and clean, set risers, place the hero, place groups back to front, light, sign, check from the pavement and from inside, photograph. Take-down: store props labelled for reuse.
11. **Final check from outside:** can someone walking past tell what the shop sells and what the idea is in a few seconds?

## Checks

- One idea, one focal point, readable from the pavement.
- Every product and prop comes from the brief or the shopping list.
- Placement table matches the measurements; nothing overhangs the window floor or a walkway.
- No exit, door, fire door, extinguisher or exit sign blocked or hidden.
- Reach and protrusion limits met for items customers use.
- Lights checked for heat; no wiring work without an electrician.
- Props total within budget; buying waits for the client's yes.

## Output format

1. **The idea** in one line.
2. **Missing measurements** (if any).
3. **Zone map** (text, top-down) and **front view** (text, left to right with heights).
4. **Placement table:** item, position, height, facing, reason.
5. **Lighting notes** with the heat check.
6. **Sign spots** and copy source.
7. **Props list:** owned or new, quantity, budget line, total.
8. **Build and take-down steps** with times.
9. **Safety checks** ticked.
10. **Access and log.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bookshop. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Window 2.4 m wide, 0.9 m deep, 2.1 m high, one step up from the shop floor; October; 12 featured books; props budget £150; owns two wooden crates and a set of plug-in warm white string lights. Access I have: none (photos only).*

**Idea:** "Autumn reading nook: settle in with a book this October."

**Front view (left to right):** low stack of 3 books on a crate | armchair-height crate with 3 books and a blanket | **hero: reading lamp beside an open book on a stand, highest point, centre-right** | 3 books on low risers | small price sign at the front right corner.

| Item | Position | Height | Facing | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero book on stand + lamp | Centre-right, middle depth | Highest point | Cover to the street | Focal point; lamp light draws the eye at dusk |
| Crate 1 + 3 books | Left, front | Low | Covers out, fanned | Starts the triangle |
| Crate 2 + blanket + 3 books | Centre-left, back | Medium | Spines and one cover | Builds height to the hero |
| 3 books on risers | Right, front | Low | Covers out | Closes the triangle; odd group |

**Light:** the string lights stay low and loose around the crates; the lamp uses a cool-running LED bulb; check the blanket isn't touching any bulb.

**Props:** crates and string lights owned (£0); reading lamp £35; blanket £20; three acrylic book stands £24; autumn leaves (fabric, fire-retardant label checked) £15. Total £94 of £150, waiting for your yes to order.

**Safety:** the window step and door stay clear; nothing stands on the shop floor side of the step.
