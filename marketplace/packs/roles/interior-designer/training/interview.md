# Interview: interior designer

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you show me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the designer's work (for example, holiday let questions for a window specialist).

## How you approach a job

1. **Walk me through your last refresh from the first message to the reopening.**
   Why it matters: shows the real method and its order, not the textbook one.
2. **What do you measure on a first visit, and what have you forgotten to measure before that cost you later?**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's measuring checklist.
3. **How do you turn a vague goal like "make it feel bigger" into something you can check?**
   Why it matters: a sharp goal is what every later choice ties back to.
4. **How do you decide where the walkways go before the furniture? What widths do you work to, and where do they come from?**
   Why it matters: captures clearance habits and the rules they rely on in their region.
5. **How do you fit more seats or stations without the space feeling cramped? Show me one you got right and one you got wrong.**
   Why it matters: the core trade-off for cafes and salons.

## Light, colour and materials

6. **How do you plan lighting for a small business on a budget? What do you change first?**
   Why it matters: becomes the lighting skill's priorities.
7. **How do you choose colours, and how do you test them before the client buys paint?**
   Why it matters: real testing habits prevent expensive repaints.
8. **How do you make doors, steps and counters easy to see for people with low vision, without the space looking clinical?**
   Why it matters: contrast is both an access need and a design skill.
9. **Which finishes and fabrics do you trust for busy cafes, salons and holiday lets, and which have let you down?**
   Why it matters: durability knowledge comes only from experience.

## Windows and displays

10. **How do you plan a window that makes people stop? Talk me through one that worked and how you knew.**
    Why it matters: becomes the display plan's method and its measure.
11. **How do you set heights for products in a window and on shelves?**
    Why it matters: replaces rules of thumb with the expert's tested practice.
12. **What safety checks do you do on a display before you walk away?**
    Why it matters: stability, heat and exits are where displays hurt people.

## Rules, professionals and access

13. **Which changes do you always send to building control, an electrician or a fire risk assessor, and how do you explain that to a client who wants to "just do it"?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's professional-check habits and how it holds the line kindly.
14. **What do you check on furniture before you put it on a shopping list, especially for holiday lets?**
    Why it matters: fire labels, recalls and anti-tip fixings protect guests.
15. **Which changes would you make in a client's plan files, shared lists or booking listing on your own, and which do you always check first? What limits do you agree?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's level 2 and level 3 habits.
16. **How do you handle orders, quotes and deposits? What has gone wrong with suppliers or deliveries?**
    Why it matters: orders and payments need a fresh yes, and real failures shape the timetable.

## Budget and order of work

17. **How do you keep a refresh inside a small budget? What do you cut first, and what do you never cut?**
    Why it matters: becomes the budget skill's cut order.
18. **How do you plan the work so a business stays open as much as possible?**
    Why it matters: closure days are the client's biggest hidden cost.

## Mistakes and specialty

19. **What are the three mistakes you see most in DIY refreshes, or in AI-generated room ideas?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's "spot it" list.
20. **Tell me about a job that went wrong. What happened, and what do you check now?**
    Why it matters: a real failure gives the agent a check it would not think of.
21. **Which kinds of spaces do you know best, and what is different about designing for them?**
    Why it matters: feeds "My specialty".
22. **What work do you turn down, and why?**
    Why it matters: defines what the agent is not for.
