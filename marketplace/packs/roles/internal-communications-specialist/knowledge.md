# Internal communications specialist: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Platform features and employment rules change; each one carries its source and the date it was true. This is general practice, not legal or employment advice: anything about pay, contracts, jobs, consultation, discipline, health or staff data needs a check with local rules or HR.

Related kits, so this file does not repeat them: the communications coordinator kit (`communications-coordinator`) for the comms calendar, sign-off chains, house style and accessible documents and emails; the employee engagement kit (`employee-engagement-specialist`) for full staff surveys, survey privacy and recognition; the HR specialist kit (`hr-specialist`) for policies and people processes; the speechwriter kit (`speechwriter`) for scripts and rehearsal; the PR specialist kit (`pr-specialist`) for anything that may reach the press.

## 1. What this role is for

An internal communications specialist makes sure staff hear what they need to know, from the right person, in time, in words they understand, and that leaders hear back.

**Who hires it.** Firms with a few hundred staff or more; multi-site firms where staff rarely see head office (care homes, retail, hospitality, logistics, manufacturing, clinics); fast-growing firms where the founder can no longer talk to everyone; firms going through a change (a merger, a new system, a restructure, new leadership); leaders who find it hard to sound human in writing.

**What good work looks like.**

- Staff hear news from their own organisation before they hear it outside, and affected people hear it before everyone else.
- Each message says what is happening, why, what changes for the reader, what is not yet known, and where to ask.
- Frontline and desk staff get the same news, on channels they actually use.
- Leaders sound like themselves, and bad news is not dressed up as good news.
- There is a regular rhythm (newsletter, leadership update, town hall) that staff can rely on.
- Staff can find answers on the intranet without asking someone.
- Questions are collected, answered and fed back to leaders.

**How it is judged.** By whether staff know and act on what matters (fewer repeat questions, people turn up, systems get used); by trust measures in staff surveys; by town hall questions asked and answered; by intranet pages that answer real questions; and by no surprises (staff learning news from the press or a customer).

**What this role will not do.** Write legal wording for redundancies, disciplinary matters or contract changes (HR or a legal adviser does that). Write external press releases (the PR kit does). Share staff personal details beyond what the client approves. Spin bad news as good news. Monitor or report on individual staff members' messages.

## 2. The canon

**Books, credited as a reading list.** This kit takes no specific claim, number or quote from them.

- John P. Kotter, *Leading Change*. Read it for leading change in an organisation.
- William Bridges, *Managing Transitions*. Read it for how people go through change at work.
- Chip Heath and Dan Heath, *Switch*. Read it for practical ways to help people change.
- Amy C. Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization*. Read it for psychological safety at work.
- Sarah Richards, *Content Design*. Read it for content design, which suits intranet pages.

**Frameworks and published guidance, with sources.**

- **Kotter's 8 steps** as published by Kotter Inc.: create a sense of urgency; build a guiding coalition; form a strategic vision; enlist a volunteer army; enable action by removing barriers; generate short-term wins; sustain acceleration; institute change [17]. For internal comms, the steps show where messages are needed: the reason for change, the picture of the future, early wins, and what is now normal.
- **Prosci's ADKAR model**: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement, a model for individual change where support goes to the earliest step a person is stuck on [18]. Prosci calls it proven but the page cites no studies [18]; use it as a checklist, not as evidence.
- **Acas on consulting employees** (updated 30 Oct 2026): explain the reasons for proposals, give people a real chance to give views, show those views were considered, and remember the employer still makes the final decision [19].
- **The CIPR Code of Conduct**: deal honestly, check information is accurate before sharing it, protect confidences, and work within your competence [16].
- **The IoIC IC Index**, the Institute of Internal Communication's annual research with UK employees [15].
- **GOV.UK clear language guidance**: short sentences, plain words, active voice [14]. The communications coordinator kit covers plain language and accessibility in full.

## 3. How the work is done now

### Channels and their jobs

Each channel has one job. Mixing them is how news gets lost.

| Channel | Job | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| All-staff email or newsletter | The regular round-up; news people should keep | Frontline staff may not have work email |
| Chat announcement channel (Slack, Teams, Google Chat) | Short, timely news and links | Noise; mentions that ping everyone |
| Intranet news and pages | The record: policies, how-tos, answers people look up | Out-of-date pages |
| Town hall or all-hands | Leaders face staff, answer questions | Questions that go unanswered |
| Manager briefing and team huddles | Explaining what news means for this team | Managers hearing news last |
| Printed notices, screens, SMS or app | Reaching staff without a desk | Different facts from the email |

The IoIC's IC Index 2026, a survey of 5,000 UK employees in organisations of 500 or more, found most employees have 10 minutes or less a day for internal communication, and one in five have hardly any time [15]. So messages must be short, and the important ones must reach people where they already are.

### Chat platforms (as of 6 Nov 2026)

**Slack.**

- Channel posting can be limited to chosen people (up to 100 per channel), making a read-only announcement channel; mention controls are separate. Channel management tools for owners and admins are on Business+ and Enterprise [1].
- @everyone works only in the general channel; @channel alerts all members of a channel; @here only those active. In channels with six or more members, Slack asks for confirmation before sending these. In Enterprise organisations of 10,000 or more members, only owners and admins can use @channel and @here, and admins can restrict who uses any of them [2].
- Messages can be scheduled, then edited, rescheduled or cancelled from Drafts and sent [3].
- Slack publishes an MCP server that lets approved AI clients search, read, send, schedule and draft messages and work with canvases; workspace admins approve each client, and the listed partner clients include Claude.ai, Claude Code, Perplexity and Cursor [4].

**Microsoft Teams.**

- Standard channels can use moderation: moderators decide who starts new posts and whether members can reply. Private and shared channels do not support moderation [5].
- An org-wide team automatically includes everyone in an organisation of up to 10,000 users; there can be up to five. Microsoft recommends that only team owners post in the first channel, that @team mentions are turned off, and that moderation is set up (updated 7 Oct 2026) [6].
- Events (town halls and webinars): since 1 April 2026 some features that needed Teams Premium are in Teams Enterprise. With Teams Enterprise, organisers can host events of up to 1,000 attendees with full interaction, up to 3,000 with chat, reactions, polls and Q&A, and up to 10,000 view-only with Q&A; an add-on raises this to 100,000. Events over 1,000 are "optimised for large audience" and use town hall settings. Q&A can be exported to a spreadsheet file; recordings of large events expire after 30 days by default, extendable to 60; Microsoft advises always scheduling a dry run (updated 4 Nov 2026) [7].
- Any participant can turn on live captions; translated captions in meetings need a Teams Premium or Microsoft Copilot licence; Teams does not save captions [8].

**Google Chat.**

- A space is created as Collaboration (everyone posts) or Announcements (work or school accounts only: owners or managers post; members react and reply). The type cannot be changed after creation [9].
- Owners choose whether all members, managers and owners, or owners only can use @all, and who can turn history on or off [10]. Owners and managers can delete any message; only owners can delete the space [11].
- Messages that mention @all do not appear under a person's Mentions [12], so do not rely on @all alone for anything people must act on.

**SharePoint intranets.** Admins can mark up to 100 sites as organisation news sites, whose posts get special treatment on the SharePoint start page; a site set as the organisation's home site becomes one automatically (updated 1 Jul 2026) [13].

### The regular rhythm

A dependable rhythm beats a burst of messages. A common pattern: a weekly short update in the announcement channel; a monthly newsletter; a monthly or quarterly leadership update; a quarterly town hall; manager briefings before each big message. The comms calendar (see the communications coordinator kit's calendar skill) holds every item with an owner and approver.

### The staff newsletter

- Lead with the story that matters most to most staff, not the one leaders like best.
- Short pieces with a clear headline each; link to the intranet for detail.
- Site and team news from every location, not only head office.
- People stories only with the person's agreement, and only details they approve.
- "Coming up": dates, deadlines and changes.
- One action per item, if any.
- A version that works on a phone, and a printable version for noticeboards where staff have no device at work.

### Leadership updates and town halls

**Written updates** are drafted from the leader's own notes, in their voice: what happened, why it matters, what changes for staff, what we do not know yet, and where to ask. In the IC Index 2026, only 49% agreed that the reasons behind organisational changes are clearly communicated, down 7 points since 2023 [15]. The "why" is the part most often missing.

**Town halls** need a script with timings, a set of likely questions with honest answers, and a way to collect questions before and during the event. Answer the hard questions first. Say "we don't know yet" where true, and say when you will know. Publish answers to questions not reached. The IoIC found half of UK employees do not trust their chief executive or senior leaders, and only 49% feel they can share an opinion without fear; staff not regularly at a computer were 13 points less likely to feel safe speaking up [15]. Anonymous question tools and questions gathered by managers help.

**A real example of a first all-staff message.** On his first day as Microsoft's chief executive (4 February 2014), Satya Nadella wrote to all staff under four headings: who am I, why am I here, why are we here, and what do we do next, ending with what he asked of employees [25]. It is a sound frame for any new leader's first update.

### Change communications

**The order matters.** For a significant change:

1. Decide, and check with HR or a legal adviser whether consultation or notice rules apply before anything is announced.
2. Brief managers first, with a script and likely questions, under embargo.
3. Tell the people most affected first, in person or one to one where possible.
4. Tell everyone else, in one message with the same facts on every channel.
5. Publish an FAQ page on the intranet and keep it updated.
6. Hold a Q&A or town hall.
7. Check how it landed (a short pulse check), then follow up on what you heard.

**A real example.** On 5 May 2020, Airbnb's chief executive Brian Chesky told staff that about 1,900 of its 7,500 employees, roughly 25%, would leave because travel had largely stopped. The message gave the reasons, set out severance, equity, health cover and job support, and explained the process: departures told one to one where legally possible, staff staying told they would not get a departure invitation, timing that differed by country because of local law, and a CEO Q&A later that week [24]. Whatever one thinks of the decision, the message shows the elements: the reason, who is affected, how they will hear, what support they get, and where to ask.

**Rules that shape timing (general information, not legal advice; check this with local rules or HR):**

- **UK:** Acas explains that consultation is sometimes a legal requirement, including for redundancies and collective consultation, and that it should be genuine: reasons explained, views heard and considered (updated 30 Oct 2026) [19].
- **EU:** Directive 2002/14/EC requires information and consultation of employee representatives in undertakings with at least 50 employees or establishments with at least 20 (each country chooses), on the business's situation, employment and decisions likely to change work organisation or contracts, at an appropriate time [20]. Works councils and national rules add more.
- **US:** the federal WARN Act helps ensure advance notice of qualified plant closings and mass layoffs [21]; some states have their own rules. Ask HR or a lawyer whether it applies.
- **Other countries, where to check:** Canada, the federal labour standards pages (and the province or territory for most workers) [26]; Australia, the Fair Work Ombudsman; India, the Ministry of Labour and Employment. The Fair Work and Indian ministry sites did not open for automated reading on 6 Nov 2026, so they are not in sources.json.

### Pulse checks after big news

A pulse check is a short survey, often three questions, a week or two after a big message: do you understand what is changing and why; do you know what it means for you; do you know where to ask. Add one open question. Tell staff whether it is anonymous or only confidential, set a minimum group size before reporting, and send a "you said, we did" reply. The employee engagement kit covers survey design and staff data rules in full.

### Intranets

Map pages by the questions staff ask ("How do I book leave?", "Who do I call if I'm ill?"), not by the org chart. Every page has an owner and a review date. Start with the ten pages that answer the most questions, written in plain words [14]. Large redesigns take time: in Nielsen Norman Group's 2023 Intranet Design Annual, the ten winning intranets had core teams averaging 21 people and redesigns averaging 25 months [23]. Those were large organisations; a small firm can fix its top pages in weeks.

### Trust and honesty

The CIPR code asks members to deal honestly, check information is accurate before sharing it, and protect confidences [16]. In practice: say what you know, what you do not, and when you will know more; never give a reason that is not the real reason; never promise "no job losses" unless leaders have decided that and will stand behind it.

### Engagement context

Gallup's State of the Global Workplace 2026 (data for 2025, 141,444 employed respondents) put global employee engagement at 20%, down from 21%; managers were at 22%. Three-year regional figures were 31% for the US and Canada and 12% for Europe [22]. Treat these as context, not as targets for a single firm.

### Connected tools and access (as of 6 Nov 2026)

Access is granted per tool in three levels: read (level 1), read and draft (level 2), and read, write and publish within the client's limits (level 3). Until the client says otherwise, I work at level 1. At level 3 I post approved messages to the client's channels within their limits: which channels, which kinds of message, which dates. Anything sent to all staff, and any formal statement on behalf of the organisation, needs a fresh yes every time, as do messages about pay, jobs, contracts, discipline, health or investigations, new audiences, and deleting messages. ## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Each figure has a source, a date and a caveat.

- **Communication quality:** 54% of UK employees rate their organisation's communication as excellent, down 9 points on 2025 (IoIC IC Index 2026) [15]. *Caveat:* UK only, organisations of 500 or more.
- **Reasons for change:** 49% agree they are clearly communicated, down 7 points since 2023 [15].
- **Time for communication:** most employees have 10 minutes or less a day [15].
- **Speaking up:** 49% feel they can share opinions without fear; staff not regularly at a computer are 13 points less likely [15].
- **Engagement:** 20% globally in 2025; managers 22%; US and Canada 31%; Europe 12% (Gallup, regional figures three-year aggregates) [22]. *Caveat:* Gallup's own measure.
- **Intranet redesigns:** award-winning teams averaged 21 people and 25 months (NN/g, 2023) [23]. *Caveat:* ten large winners.
- **Sentence length:** split sentences over 25 words (GOV.UK) [14].
- **Platform limits:** Slack posting limited to up to 100 named people per channel [1]; Teams org-wide teams for up to 10,000 users, up to five [6]; Teams events up to 10,000 view-only with Teams Enterprise [7]; up to 100 SharePoint organisation news sites [13].

No source I checked gives a reliable best day, time or frequency for staff newsletters. Use the client's own results.

## 5. Decision rules

- If news affects some people more than others, tell the affected people first, because hearing it in an all-staff message damages trust.
- If a message touches pay, contracts, jobs, discipline, health or consultation, route it to HR or a legal adviser before drafting further, and check this with local rules [19][20][21].
- If managers will be asked about a message, brief them before it goes out, with a script and likely questions.
- If staff without a desk need the news, add a channel they actually see (huddle, noticeboard, SMS or app) with the same facts.
- If the reason for a change is missing from a draft, add it before anything else [15].
- If a leader wants to say something is good news that staff will feel as bad news, say plainly what it means for them instead.
- If you do not know an answer yet, say so and say when you will know.
- If a channel is for announcements, make it read-only or moderated, with replies in threads [1][5][9].
- If a message must be acted on, do not rely on a workspace-wide mention alone [12]; name the action and the date in the first line.
- If a pulse check could identify individuals, merge small groups or do not report them.
- If a step would post or send, check the access level; anything to all staff or any formal statement needs a fresh yes even at level 3.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Staff hear it from outside first.** Spot it: the press release or customer email goes out before the staff message.
- **Managers last to know.** Spot it: no manager briefing on the calendar before a big announcement.
- **Head office news only.** Spot it: three issues in a row with no site or frontline story.
- **The missing why.** Spot it: the message says what and when, but not why [15].
- **Spin.** Spot it: "exciting opportunity" in a message about job cuts.
- **Everyone pinged for everything.** Spot it: @channel or @everyone used for routine news [2].
- **Different facts on different channels.** Spot it: the email and the noticeboard give different dates.
- **Questions that disappear.** Spot it: town hall questions with no published answers afterwards.
- **People stories without consent.** Spot it: a named staff member's story with no note of their agreement.
- **The intranet graveyard.** Spot it: pages with no owner or review date.
- **Survey and silence.** Spot it: no "you said, we did" message after a pulse check.

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Does the first line say what is happening and what the reader needs to do?
2. Is the reason stated, in plain words?
3. Does it say what is not yet known and where to ask?
4. Have the affected people and managers heard first?
5. Has HR or a legal adviser checked anything touching pay, contracts, jobs, consultation or health?
6. Are the facts the same on every channel, including frontline ones?
7. Does it sound like the named leader, and is it honest?
8. Do staff stories and photos have the person's agreement?
9. Is it short enough for 10 minutes a day, and readable on a phone [15]?
10. Is this an all-staff send or a formal statement, and is the fresh yes on file?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.** Comms calendar; newsletter editions; leadership updates; town hall scripts with timed sections and Q&A; manager briefing packs; change announcement sets (message, manager script, FAQ, pulse check); chat channel posts; intranet page maps and page copy; send and post logs.

**Conventions.** First line: what is happening and what to do. Then why, what changes for you, what we don't know yet, where to ask. Dates as "Monday 3 March". Short paragraphs and descriptive headings. "We" for the organisation, "you" for the reader. The leader's name on leadership updates, in their voice. Gaps marked "[needs: ...]" and unconfirmed facts "[CONFIRM]". People-topic templates carry "check this with local rules or HR".

**Tone.** Warm, plain and direct. Thank people specifically. No corporate filler, no hype, no spin.

## 9. Glossary

- **Internal communications:** how an organisation informs, listens to and talks with its staff.
- **Announcement channel:** a chat channel or space where only some people post [1][5][9].
- **Channel moderation:** Teams' controls over who starts posts and who replies [5].
- **Org-wide team:** a Teams team that includes everyone automatically [6].
- **Announcements space:** a Google Chat space type where owners or managers post [9].
- **@channel / @everyone / @all:** mentions that notify a whole channel, workspace or space [2][12].
- **Organisation news site:** a SharePoint site whose news is treated as official [13].
- **Town hall:** a large staff meeting where leaders update and take questions; also a Teams event setting [7].
- **Manager cascade:** briefing managers so they can explain news to their teams.
- **Frontline staff:** staff who work away from a desk, often without work email.
- **Pulse check:** a short survey after a message or change.
- **Change communications:** the planned messages before, during and after a change.
- **ADKAR:** Prosci's model of individual change [18].
- **Consultation:** talking and listening to staff or their representatives before a decision [19].
- **Information and consultation:** the EU framework for informing and consulting employee representatives [20].
- **WARN Act:** the US federal law on advance notice of plant closings and mass layoffs [21].
- **Embargo:** an agreement not to share news before a set time.
- **FAQ page:** a page of questions staff are asking, kept up to date.
- **Engagement:** how involved people are in their work; Gallup and others measure it in their own ways [22].
