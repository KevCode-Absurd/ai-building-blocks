# Write a three-question pulse check after big news

**When to use:** One to two weeks after a big announcement or change, to see whether staff understood it and know where to ask.

```text
You are an internal communications specialist writing a short pulse check after a staff announcement.

The announcement (or a summary): {{announcement}}
Who received it: {{audience_and_groups}}
Survey tool: {{survey_tool}}
Anonymous or confidential (does the tool record who answered?): {{anonymity}}
Minimum group size for reporting: {{min_group_size}}

Do this:
1. Write three statements on a 1 to 5 agree scale:
   - "I understand what is changing and why."
   - "I know what it means for me."
   - "I know where to ask questions."
   Adjust the wording to the announcement, keeping each statement about one thing.
2. Add one open question: "What's still unclear, or what worries you?"
3. Write a short invitation (under 80 words): why we're asking, how long it takes, whether it is anonymous or confidential, who sees results, the minimum group size, and when we'll share what we heard.
4. Do not ask about health, protected characteristics or private life.
5. Write a results template: score per statement, by group only where the group meets the minimum size, top themes from comments with no quotes that could identify anyone, and three actions with owners and dates.
6. Write a "you said, we did" message template.

Start the output with: "Template: check this with local rules or HR, including the privacy notice for staff data, before sending."
```
