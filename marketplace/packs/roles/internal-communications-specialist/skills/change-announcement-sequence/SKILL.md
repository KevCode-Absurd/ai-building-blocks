# Change announcement sequence

How people hear news shapes how they take it. The same message lands very differently when managers were briefed, affected people heard first and the reason is plain, than when staff learn it from a customer or a group email. This skill plans the order, writes every piece and checks how it landed.

Template: check this with local rules or HR. Changes to jobs, pay, contracts, hours or locations can trigger consultation or notice duties; the agent flags them and never gives a legal ruling.

## When to use

- A restructure, redundancies, a merger or acquisition, new leadership.
- A site opening, move or closure.
- A new system, rota, policy or way of working.
- Any announcement where some staff are affected more than others.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the decision, the plan and past messages. Level 2 to draft every message, the FAQ page, the event and the pulse survey in the client's tools. Level 3 to send and post approved pieces to the people, channels and dates set. Every all-staff message and any formal statement needs a fresh yes, every time, even at level 3. So do messages to affected individuals about their jobs, pay or contracts.

- The decision, its reasons, and what is and isn't decided.
- Who is affected, how, and when.
- What HR or the legal adviser says about consultation or notice, and the agreed timeline.
- Support on offer (for example training, redeployment, an employee assistance programme).
- The leader who will front the news, and the managers who will explain it.
- Channels that reach every group, including staff without a desk.

## Steps

1. **Check before announcing.** Confirm with HR or a legal adviser whether consultation, representatives or notice rules apply, and what can be said at this stage. In the UK, Acas explains when consultation is a legal requirement and what genuine consultation looks like; in the EU, employee representatives must be informed and consulted in many firms; in the US, the WARN Act may require advance notice of closings and mass layoffs. Check this with local rules or HR.
2. **Write the core message.** One page, in the leader's voice: what is happening; why; who is affected and how; what is not yet decided; what support there is; what happens next and when; where to ask. No spin. This page is the source for every other piece.
3. **Plan the order.** A dated timeline:
   - managers briefed (under embargo), with a script and likely questions;
   - affected people told first, one to one or in small groups where possible;
   - everyone else told, in one message with the same facts on every channel;
   - FAQ page live on the intranet at the same moment;
   - Q&A session or town hall within days;
   - pulse check one to two weeks later;
   - follow-up message on what was heard and what happens next.
   For multi-country firms, timing may differ by country because of local law; plan each country with HR.
4. **Write the manager pack.** The core message, a short script for team conversations, the top ten questions with answers, what managers must not promise, where to send questions they cannot answer, and the date and time they can start talking.
5. **Write the message to affected people.** Personal, specific, and with the next step and the support. HR approves wording about jobs, pay or contracts.
6. **Write the all-staff message and channel versions.** Email, chat announcement (one or two lines and a link), intranet news, noticeboard or huddle sheet, all with identical facts. Lead with what is happening and the reason.
7. **Write the FAQ page.** Grouped by the questions people will ask: "Does this affect me?", "What happens to my pay?", "Who do I ask?". Date it and update it as answers come.
8. **Plan the Q&A.** Use the leadership update and town hall skill. Answer the hardest question first.
9. **Write the pulse check.** Three questions on a 1 to 5 agree scale: "I understand what is changing and why"; "I know what it means for me"; "I know where to ask questions". One open question. Say whether it is anonymous or confidential, and the minimum group size for reporting. See the employee engagement kit for full survey rules.
10. **Follow up.** Share what you heard, what is changing because of it, and what is not, with dates.

## Checks

- HR or a legal adviser has checked the plan and wording; check this with local rules or HR.
- Managers briefed before staff; affected people before everyone else.
- One core message; identical facts on every channel.
- The reason is stated plainly; nothing is spun.
- What is not yet decided is said, with when it will be.
- Support is named.
- Staff without a desk are reached on the same day.
- Fresh yes on file for every all-staff send.
- Pulse check small-group rules set before launch.

## Output format

1. Timeline: date, time, audience, channel, piece, owner, approver.
2. Core message.
3. Manager pack.
4. Message to affected people (for HR approval).
5. All-staff message and channel versions.
6. FAQ page.
7. Pulse check and follow-up template.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a printing firm with 140 staff on two sites. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Timeline (extract):**

| When | Who | Channel | Piece | Approver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon 9am | 8 team leaders | Meeting | Briefing, script, Q&A | MD and HR |
| Mon 11am | 22 night-shift staff | Small groups with MD and HR | Night shift ends 30 June; options | HR (fresh yes) |
| Mon 2pm | All staff | Email, Slack announcement, canteen screen | Core message | MD (fresh yes) |
| Mon 2pm | All staff | Intranet | FAQ page | HR |
| Thu 12pm | All staff | Canteen and Teams | Q&A with MD | MD |

**Core message (opening):**

> From 30 June, we will stop running a night shift at the Harbour Road site. Orders for overnight printing have fallen by more than half in two years, and the shift now runs at a loss. Nobody's job is ending today. We are starting a consultation with the 22 people on nights about day roles at both sites. Here's what we know, what we don't, and where to ask.
