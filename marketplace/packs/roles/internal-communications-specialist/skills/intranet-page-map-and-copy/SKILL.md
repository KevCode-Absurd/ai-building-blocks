# Intranet page map and copy

Staff open the intranet to do something: book leave, find a form, check a policy, call the right person. An intranet built around the org chart makes them guess which department owns the answer. This skill rebuilds it around their questions, starting with the pages that answer the most.

Template: check this with local rules or HR before publishing any page about pay, leave, contracts, conduct, health and safety or staff data; policy wording belongs to its owner.

## When to use

- An intranet with many pages, little use and lots of repeat questions.
- A new intranet on SharePoint, Google Sites, Notion, Confluence or a staff app.
- A merger where two intranets become one.
- A help desk or HR team swamped with the same questions.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the current pages, page analytics and search terms. Level 2 to create draft pages and a draft site structure. Level 3 to publish approved pages and move or retire pages on the sites set. Changes to the home page or organisation-wide news need a fresh yes; deleting pages needs a fresh yes every time.

- The list of current pages, with views and last-updated dates if the tool shows them.
- Search terms staff use on the intranet, if available.
- The questions staff ask HR, IT, facilities, payroll and managers (a month of tickets or emails is ideal).
- Who owns each topic.
- Who has a desk and who reads on a phone or a shared screen.

## Steps

1. **Collect the questions.** Pull the most common questions from help desk tickets, HR emails, manager feedback, town hall questions and intranet searches. Write each as staff say it: "How do I swap a shift?"
2. **Audit the pages.** For each page: what it answers, its owner, last update, views. Mark it keep, merge, rewrite or retire. Pages with no owner are the first problem to fix.
3. **Group by task.** Build a page map around what people need to do: "Pay and leave", "Your shifts", "IT help", "Health and safety", "Who's who", "News". Use the words staff use, not department names.
4. **Pick the top pages.** The ten or so pages that answer the most questions. Write those first.
5. **Write each page.** Title as the task ("Book annual leave"). First line: the answer or the first step. Then numbered steps, who to contact, and links to forms. Plain words and short sentences. Date at the bottom with the owner's name and the next review date.
6. **Check policy wording.** Policy pages summarise in plain words and link to the official policy. The policy owner approves the summary. Check this with local rules or HR.
7. **Set up news.** Decide where official news lives. In SharePoint, an admin can mark sites as organisation news sites so their posts are treated as official on the start page, and a home site becomes one automatically. Keep news separate from reference pages.
8. **Make it findable.** Clear titles that match staff words, key pages linked from the home page, and the top pages pinned in the chat announcement channel.
9. **Plan reviews.** Every page has an owner and a review date. Retire or update anything past its date.
10. **Launch and listen.** Announce the new pages, then track the repeat questions to HR and the help desk. Fix pages where questions keep coming.

## Checks

- Every page answers a real staff question, in staff words.
- Every page has an owner and a review date.
- First line gives the answer or the first step.
- Policy summaries approved by their owner; check this with local rules or HR.
- Readable on a phone; real text, not images of text; descriptive links.
- No two pages give different answers to the same question.
- Retired pages redirect or point to the new page.
- Home page and organisation news changes have a fresh yes.

## Output format

1. Question list with counts and sources.
2. Page audit: page, owner, last update, views, keep, merge, rewrite or retire.
3. Page map, grouped by task.
4. Copy for the top pages.
5. Owner and review schedule.
6. Launch message and a measure to watch (repeat questions).

## Example of good output (short)

*Larkfield Clinics is a made-up group of eight clinics with 350 staff, used only to show the format.*

**Top questions (one month of HR and IT emails):** swap a shift (61), book leave (48), reset my password (44), sick day process (37), where's my payslip (29).

**Page map (extract):**

- Your shifts: Swap a shift; See your rota; Overtime
- Pay and leave: Book annual leave; Find your payslip; Pay dates
- When you're ill: Call in sick; Return to work
- IT help: Reset your password; Get a new phone

**Page: Call in sick**

> Call your clinic manager before your shift starts, at least by the start time. If you can't reach them, call the duty line on [number].
>
> 1. Say you're ill and when you think you'll be back.
> 2. Tell them if you have any shifts this week you can't do.
> 3. On your first day back, fill in the return to work form with your manager.
>
> Questions: your clinic manager, or HR on [email].
> Owner: HR team. Last checked: 3 March. Next review: 3 September. [Process approved by HR; check this with local rules or HR]
