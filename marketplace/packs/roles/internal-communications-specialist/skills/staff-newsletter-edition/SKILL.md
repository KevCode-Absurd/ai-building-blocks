# Staff newsletter edition

A staff newsletter earns its place by telling people something they need or want to know, from across the whole organisation, in less time than they have. Most staff have only a few minutes a day for internal communication, so the edition is short, scannable and honest.

Template: check this with local rules or HR before any item about pay, contracts, jobs, policies, health or staff data goes out.

## When to use

- The regular monthly or weekly edition.
- A firm starting a staff newsletter for the first time.
- An edition that keeps getting ignored and needs a new shape.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the news, notes and past editions. Level 2 to build the edition as a draft in the email tool, intranet news or staff app. Level 3 to send or publish to the lists and sites the client set. An edition to all staff needs the approver's fresh yes every time, even at level 3.

- This month's news from leaders, teams and sites.
- People stories, with a note that each person agreed to be featured and which details they approved.
- Key dates, deadlines and changes coming up.
- Who reads what: desk staff, frontline staff, managers; which have work email, which read on phones, which see noticeboards.
- The house style, if any, and two past editions.
- Who approves the edition.

## Steps

1. **Sort the material.** List every item with its source and whether it needs an action. Mark any fact you cannot confirm [CONFIRM].
2. **Flag people topics.** Anything about pay, contracts, jobs, restructuring, policies, health or individuals goes to HR or the right owner before it is written up. Check this with local rules or HR.
3. **Choose the lead.** The item that matters most to most staff, not the one leaders like best. A pay date change beats a new office plant.
4. **Spread the news.** At least one item from sites or teams outside head office. If a site has no news this month, ask its manager for one small story.
5. **Write short items.** Headline that says the news. Two to four short sentences. One action, if any, with a date. A link to the intranet page for detail. Plain words, sentences under 25 words.
6. **Write people stories with care.** Use only details the person approved. No health, family or private details unless the person offered them and approved the final wording. Thank people specifically, not with general praise.
7. **Add "Coming up".** Dates, deadlines and changes in date order.
8. **Make three versions.** A phone-friendly email or app version; a one-page printable version for noticeboards and break rooms; a short chat post (one or two lines and a link) for the announcement channel.
9. **Check access.** Real text, not images of text; descriptive link text; alt text on photos; readable on a phone. The communications coordinator kit has the full accessibility checklist.
10. **Approval and send.** The approver signs off the exact version. Send or publish at the level granted, log it, and note any replies or questions that come in.
11. **Read the response.** Clicks, replies, questions to managers and the help desk. Treat open rates with caution; they overstate reading. Feed what you learn into next month.

## Checks

- The lead story matters to most staff.
- At least one item from outside head office.
- Every fact confirmed or marked [CONFIRM].
- Every people story has a recorded agreement and approved details.
- People topics checked with HR; check this with local rules or HR.
- One action per item, with a date.
- Three versions with identical facts.
- Accessible: real text, alt text, clear links.
- Approved version on file; fresh yes for an all-staff send.

## Output format

1. Edition plan: items, source, action, owner, [CONFIRM] flags.
2. The edition (phone-friendly version).
3. Printable noticeboard version.
4. Chat announcement post.
5. Approval and send log.

## Example of good output (short)

*Brightwater Care is a made-up care provider with 600 staff across 14 homes, used only to show the format.*

**Lead: Pay day moves to the 25th from April**

From April, you'll be paid on the 25th of each month instead of the last working day. This lines us up with our new payroll provider. If the 25th is a weekend, you'll be paid on the Friday before. Questions? Ask your home manager or see the pay page. [Checked with HR, 3 Mar]

**From the homes: Elm House garden reopens**

Residents at Elm House planted 40 sweet peas with the activities team last week. Thanks to Marta, who rebuilt the raised beds on her days off. [Marta agreed to be named, 1 Mar]

**Coming up**

- Friday 14 March: last day to book Easter leave.
- Monday 17 March: new rota app training starts (details from your manager).

**Chat post:** "March staff news is out: pay day moves to the 25th from April, Elm House garden reopens, and Easter leave closes Friday. Read it here: [link]"
