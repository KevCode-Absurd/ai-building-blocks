# Interview: Internal communications specialist

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you give me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work.

## The organisations and staff

1. **What kinds of organisations do you work with, and how many staff, sites and shifts do they have?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and the staff it writes for.
2. **How do you reach staff who don't have a desk or a work email? Give me a recent example.**
   Why it matters: frontline reach is where most internal comms fails.
3. **How do you find out what staff actually want to know, rather than what leaders want to say?**
   Why it matters: shows the expert's listening methods.

## Channels and rhythm

4. **Show me the channel plan for a client you work with. What does each channel do, and what doesn't go there?**
   Why it matters: the expert's channel rules become the agent's.
5. **How do you set up announcement channels in Slack, Teams or Google Chat? Who can post, and who can mention everyone?**
   Why it matters: real platform settings prevent noise and mistakes.
6. **What's your regular rhythm (newsletter, leadership update, town hall), and how did you decide it?**
   Why it matters: a dependable rhythm is a core deliverable.

## Writing for leaders

7. **How do you write in a leader's voice? What do you ask them for?**
   Why it matters: voice capture makes leadership updates believable.
8. **Tell me about a time a leader wanted to spin bad news. What did you do?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert holds the line on honesty.
9. **How do you handle "we don't know yet" in a message or at a town hall?**
   Why it matters: uncertainty handling builds or breaks trust.

## Change and big news

10. **Walk me through the order you use for a big announcement. Who hears first, and why?**
    Why it matters: sequencing is the most important change comms decision.
11. **What goes in your manager briefing pack?**
    Why it matters: managers carry the message to most staff.
12. **When do you bring in HR or a legal adviser, and what do you hand them?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's escalation points on people topics.
13. **Tell me about an announcement that went wrong: a leak, a wrong fact, the wrong audience. What happened next?**
    Why it matters: real recovery steps and the safeguards that followed.

## Listening and measuring

14. **How do you run a pulse check after big news? How do you protect anonymity?**
    Why it matters: listening done badly harms trust more than not asking.
15. **How do you know internal comms is working? Which numbers do you trust, and which don't you?**
    Why it matters: defines the agent's measures.
16. **How do you run town hall questions so the hard ones get asked?**
    Why it matters: question handling is a repeatable method.

## Intranet and stories

17. **How do you decide what goes on the intranet and who owns each page?**
    Why it matters: ownership is what keeps an intranet current.
18. **How do you get consent for staff stories and photos, and what do you leave out?**
    Why it matters: protects staff privacy and trust.

## Limits and judgement

19. **What will you not write or send, even if a leader insists?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's boundaries.
20. **Which messages do you never send without the leader's fresh yes?**
    Why it matters: defines the fresh-yes list the agent must respect.
21. **Tell me about the hardest internal comms job you've done. What did you learn?**
    Why it matters: real stories teach judgement under pressure.
