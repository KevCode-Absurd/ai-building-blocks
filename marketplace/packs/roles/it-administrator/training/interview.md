# Training interview: IT administrator

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out client names, passwords, tenant names and anything under a confidentiality agreement; describe set-ups in general terms.

1. **What size and kind of offices do you look after, and on which platforms?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and the limits of its advice.

2. **Walk me through your first week with a new client whose IT "just grew".**
   Why it matters: reveals the real order of work and what the expert checks first.

3. **How do you find out who has admin rights, and how do you get a reluctant owner to give some up?**
   Why it matters: captures the people side of least privilege, not just the settings.

4. **Tell me about an MFA rollout that went badly or nearly did. What did you change next time?**
   Why it matters: surfaces the communication and lock-out lessons behind a safe rollout.

5. **What do you do about scanners, old apps or shared mailboxes that break when old sign-in methods are blocked?**
   Why it matters: records the practical fixes that make security changes stick.

6. **How do you hear about leavers, and what is your target time to remove access?**
   Why it matters: grounds the leaver process in how small offices really work.

7. **Tell me about a leaver whose data you needed after the account was gone, or nearly gone.**
   Why it matters: teaches the "block, hand over, then delete" habit through a real case.

8. **How do you decide what to back up, and what targets do you agree with owners?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert turns vague worries into backup and restore targets.

9. **When did a restore last surprise you, and what did you learn?**
   Why it matters: captures the restore-test lessons that "job succeeded" messages hide.

10. **How do you keep devices patched in an office with no device management?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent workable steps for the most common small-office case.

11. **What is in every script you write before it touches real data?**
    Why it matters: records the expert's safety habits for scripts: dry runs, logs, exit codes.

12. **Tell me about a script or bulk change that went wrong. What would have caught it?**
    Why it matters: turns a real failure into a check the agent always runs.

13. **How do you handle a request to reset a password or give someone access when you can't see who is asking?**
    Why it matters: captures identity checks that stop social engineering at the help desk.

14. **Which how-tos do staff actually use, and how do you write them so they work?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's staff-facing writing.

15. **What does your change list or change log look like, and who signs it off?**
    Why it matters: grounds the agent's approval and logging format in real practice.

16. **Walk me through the first hour of the last security incident you handled.**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a calm, ordered first response from experience.

17. **How do you explain Cyber Essentials, or a client's security questionnaire, to an owner who just wants to tick the box?**
    Why it matters: captures how the expert links standards to real risk.

18. **What admin access do you ask for from a new client, and what do you refuse to hold?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's default access requests and boundaries.

19. **Which settings do you always check in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace that people forget?**
    Why it matters: collects the expert's personal checklist beyond the published baselines.

20. **When do you tell a client "this is beyond me, call a specialist"?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's escalation line for forensics, legal and network work.

21. **What do you do when an owner asks you to do something you think is unsafe, such as turning MFA off for the boss?**
    Why it matters: records how the expert pushes back and what compromise they offer.
