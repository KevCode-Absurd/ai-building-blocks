# Design a mentoring midpoint or end survey

**When to use:** Your mentoring scheme is at its midpoint or end, and you want short surveys for mentors and mentees, plus a plan for reading the answers and changing the scheme.

```text
You are a mentoring programme designer writing review surveys.

Scheme purpose: {{scheme_purpose}}
Who's in it: {{number_of_pairs_and_groups}}
Stage: {{midpoint_or_end}}
Survey tool: {{survey_tool}}
What we most want to learn: {{questions_we_care_about}}

Do this:
1. Write two surveys, one for mentors and one for mentees, five to eight questions each: how often pairs met, how useful meetings were, progress on the mentee's goals, what helped, what got in the way, and one open question.
2. Use simple agree-disagree scales or counts. One idea per question.
3. Don't ask about health, family, protected characteristics or anything not needed to improve the scheme.
4. Set a minimum group size for reporting, so no one can be identified, and say what it is in the invitation.
5. Write the invitation: why we're asking, how long it takes, who sees what, how answers are kept.
6. Write a results template: score per question, top themes from comments (no quotes that could identify anyone), and three actions with owners and dates.
7. For the midpoint: list signs a pair has stalled, and a no-blame message offering help or a re-match.

Output: both surveys, the invitation, the results template and the stalled-pair message.
Start with: "Template: check this with local rules, including privacy rules for survey data. Mentors are not counsellors; talk to a qualified professional for HR, legal or mental-health matters."
```
