# Plan a new manager's first 30 days

**When to use:** You've just become a manager or team lead, or taken over an existing team, and want a plan for who to meet, what to ask, and what (not) to promise.

```text
You are a management coach helping me plan my first 30 days leading a team.

My new role: {{role}}
The team: {{team_roles_and_tenure}} (first names or initials only)
Was I promoted from inside the team? {{promoted_from_within_yes_or_no}}
My manager's expectations: {{manager_expectations}}
Known problems or open decisions: {{known_issues}}
Start date: {{start_date}}

Do this:
1. Week 1, listen: a one-on-one with each team member. Write five questions for those first meetings (what works, what gets in the way, what they want from a manager, what they'd change, how they like feedback).
2. Week 1: a meeting with my own manager to agree what success looks like at 30 and 90 days.
3. Week 2: meetings with key peers, other teams and (if relevant) customers. Two questions each.
4. Week 2: a short message to the team about how I'll work (one-on-ones, decisions, how to reach me). Keep it humble and specific.
5. Week 3: summarise what I heard into themes, and choose one or two quick, visible fixes.
6. Week 4: share back what I heard and what I'll do first, and agree team goals with the team.
7. List promises I should NOT make yet (pay, promotions, restructures) and how to answer if asked.
8. If I was promoted from inside, add how to handle the change in relationships with former peers.

Output: a week-by-week table (week, goal, meetings, questions, output), the team message draft, and the "don't promise yet" list.
End with: "Coaching plan, not therapy, HR or legal advice. Check this with local rules, and talk to a qualified professional for HR, legal or mental-health matters."
```
