# Coaching programme plan

This is coaching, not therapy, counselling or legal advice. Check this with local rules, and talk to a qualified professional for HR, legal or mental-health matters.

## When to use

- A coach wants a clear programme for a new offer (for example six sessions for first-time managers).
- An employer wants coaching for a group of leaders with agreed goals.
- A one-off session plan is needed (see the `coaching-session-plan` prompt for a single session).
- A loose programme needs structure: what happens in each session and between them.

## What you need

1. Who the clients are and what they want to change, in their words if possible.
2. Number of sessions, length, gap between them, and format (video, in person, one to one or group).
3. The coach's model, if any (GROW, strengths, values or another). Keep its language.
4. Who pays: the client or a sponsor (employer). If a sponsor, what they expect to hear.
5. Any limits: topics out of scope, budget, accessibility needs.

**Access:** level 1 to read the brief and past materials. Level 2 to write the plan, worksheets and messages as drafts in the coach's documents and email. Level 3, within the coach's limits, to book the sessions in the named calendar and schedule the agreed messages between sessions. Sending a coaching agreement as binding always needs a new yes.

## Steps

### 1. Write the goal and "done"

Write the programme goal in the client's words, then what "done" looks like at the last session: what the client, and people around them, would see them doing. Avoid goals that depend on others' choices (a promotion, a pay rise); turn them into what the client controls.

### 2. Set up the contract

Draft a one-page agreement covering: goals, number and length of sessions, how to reschedule, what is confidential, what the sponsor will hear (for example goals and attendance, not what was said), what the coach must pass on (serious risk of harm, or anything the law requires), how notes are kept and for how long, how either side can end the coaching, and the line "Coaching is not therapy". Mark it: *Template: check this with local rules before use, including privacy rules for client notes.*

### 3. Map the arc

- **First session:** trust, goals, ground rules, how the client likes to work, and a first small action.
- **Middle sessions:** explore, try, review. Each starts by reviewing the last task.
- **Last session:** review progress against "done", name what helped, plan the next three months without the coach.
- For six or more sessions, add a midpoint review with the client (and the sponsor, if agreed).

### 4. Plan each session

For each session write:
- **Purpose:** one line.
- **Opening question:** for example "What's changed since we last met?"
- **Main tool or exercise:** one only (a GROW conversation, a values sort, a stakeholder map, a feedback rehearsal, a week audit).
- **Three to five open questions:** short, one idea each, starting with what, how or who. Avoid "why" when it sounds like blame.
- **Agreeing actions:** the client chooses the action; the coach checks it's small, specific and has a day.
- **Closing question:** for example "What will you take from today?"
- **Timings:** that add up to the session length, with a little slack.

### 5. Write the worksheets

One per session, one page where possible: a title, two to five short prompts, and space to write. Use the client's language and the coach's model.

### 6. Write the tasks between sessions

Small, doable in a week, linked to that session: "Ask two people for one thing to keep and one thing to change", not "become a better listener". Add a two- or three-sentence message the coach can send midway between sessions.

### 7. Add the referral note

Write a short "What coaching is not, and when I'll suggest other help" note for the client:
- Coaching doesn't diagnose or treat mental health problems.
- If low mood, anxiety, grief, drinking, sleep problems or past trauma are getting in the way, the coach will suggest talking to a doctor, counsellor or the employer's support service.
- If anyone is at risk of harm, get urgent help: 999 or NHS 111 (mental health option) in the UK; 911, or call or text 988, in the US; 9-8-8 in Canada; 000 or Lifeline 13 11 14 in Australia; Tele-MANAS 14416 in India; or the local emergency number.
- Legal, HR and money questions go to a qualified professional.

### 8. Check and hand over

Run the checks below and deliver in the output format.

## Checks before handing over

- The goal is in the client's words and "done" is observable.
- Every session has one purpose, one tool, open questions, a client-chosen action and timings that add up.
- Each task between sessions is small and linked to its session.
- The contract covers confidentiality and what the sponsor hears.
- The referral note is there, with help lines for the client's country.
- Every client fact comes from the brief; nothing is invented.
- No promise of results, promotions or income.
- The coach's model language is kept if they named one.
- Every template carries: check this with local rules; talk to a qualified professional for HR, legal or mental-health matters.

## Output format

1. Programme summary: clients, goal, "done", format, number and length of sessions.
2. Coaching agreement (one page, template).
3. Arc table: session, purpose, main tool, task between sessions.
4. Session plans: one timed table per session.
5. Worksheets.
6. Messages between sessions.
7. "What coaching is not" note with local help lines.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: six 60-minute video sessions for Priya, a newly promoted team lead at a logistics firm. The coach uses GROW. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Goal (Priya's words):** "Stop doing my team's work for them and get my evenings back."
> **Done:** by session 6, Priya delegates the weekly route plan and two client calls, and logs off by 6pm on four days out of five.
>
> **Session 2, Delegation (60 min)**
> | Time | What | Detail |
> |---|---|---|
> | 0 to 10 | Review | "What happened with the week audit?" |
> | 10 to 40 | GROW on one task | Goal: hand over the route plan. Reality: "What do you do that only you can do?" Options: "Who could do 80% of it now?" Will: "What will you hand over, to whom, by when?" |
> | 40 to 50 | Rehearse | Priya practises the handover conversation |
> | 50 to 60 | Actions and close | "What will you take from today?" |
>
> **Task:** Hand over the route plan to one person by Thursday and write down what you were tempted to fix.
