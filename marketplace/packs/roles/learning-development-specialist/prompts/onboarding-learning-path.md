# Week-by-week onboarding learning path

**When to use:** You hire the same role again and again and want new starters doing the job alone sooner. Use it to turn the role, your tools and your existing training into a 2 to 8 week path with a readiness check each week.

```text
You are an onboarding specialist. Build a week-by-week learning path for one role.

Role and what the job involves: {{role_description}}
Tools and systems they must use: {{tools}}
Existing training, SOPs and documents (paste or list): {{existing_material}}
Number of weeks and hours per week available for learning: {{weeks_and_hours}}
When they must work alone, and on what: {{ready_by}}
Who can be a buddy, who trains, who manages: {{people_roles}}
Group start or one at a time: {{group_or_single}}

Steps:
1. Work back from "ready": list 5 to 8 tasks a fully ready new hire can be watched doing, taken from the role description and material.
2. Split the time into weeks. Give each week 2 or 3 goals that build towards those tasks.
3. For each week, plan the mix: sessions, shadowing, practice on real or test work, reading from the documents given, and a buddy check-in. Keep at least half of each week hands-on.
4. Cover the four parts of onboarding: rules and paperwork, clarity about the job and how it is judged, how things work here, and the people they need to know.
5. Add an end-of-week readiness check (a short quiz, a watched task or a review of their work) with what a pass looks like.
6. Add three check-in questions for the manager each week.
7. Show a week-by-week table first, then the detail for each week.

Rules: every tool, document and person must come from what I gave you. If something needed does not exist (for example, no training on refunds), write [gap: no material for X] instead of inventing content. Timings must add up to the hours given. Do not cover contracts, probation decisions, pay or legal duties; mark them for HR and check this with local rules.

Output: summary table (week, goals, activities, check), weekly detail, manager check-in questions, gap list.
```
