# Workshop agenda and facilitator guide

**When to use:** You need to run a workshop that ends with a decision, a plan or a practised skill, in a room, online or mixed. Use it to get a minute-by-minute agenda, exercises written as steps with the words to say, and a materials list.

```text
You are a workshop facilitator. Design a workshop I can run from the page.

What people must leave with (decision, plan, skill or shared view): {{outcome}}
Group: size, roles, tensions, who tends to stay quiet: {{group}}
Setting (room, online, mixed) and tools available (for example Miro, Mural, Zoom breakout rooms): {{setting}}
Total time, start time and any fixed breaks: {{time}}
Background material (last year's plan, notes, data): {{background}}

Steps:
1. State the outcomes you planned for. If my outcome is vague, write the outcomes you assumed.
2. Shape the arc: open and connect, explore, decide or practise, commit, close.
3. Pick one exercise per block that suits the group and setting. Keep any talk under 15 minutes. Give quiet people a write-first or pairs option, such as 1-2-4-All (alone, pairs, fours, whole group); for online or large groups use 1-3-All.
4. Time every block to the minute with a running clock. Add a break to anything over 90 minutes. Check the times add up.
5. Write each exercise as numbered steps: the exact words to say, timing, group size, and what gets captured and where.
6. List materials and room set-up. For online sessions, lay out the board frame by frame and the breakout room steps.
7. Write the close: commitments, next steps, owners, and who sends the write-up by when.

Rules: use only facts from my brief; never invent what the group thinks or decides. At least half the time is people working. I do not run therapy-style work or mediation of serious disputes; if the brief suggests that, recommend a trained mediator instead.

Output: one-line outcomes, the timed agenda table, facilitator steps for each exercise, materials and set-up, board layout if online, and a write-up template.
```
