# Training needs analysis and annual plan

## When to use

- The business has goals for next year and a pile of training requests, and needs one ranked plan.
- A board or owner asks what the training budget should buy, or what last year's bought.
- Survey, review or error data shows problems and someone has to say which ones training can fix.
- Several provider quotes need comparing on the same terms.

Do not use it to choose courses for named individuals, to rate named people's performance, or to advise on funding, levy or tax relief. Point those to the client's HR lead, the official pages and an adviser.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** survey exports, review themes, headcount by team and level, error, incident or ticket exports, last year's plan and spend, provider quotes. I change nothing.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the analysis, plan and calendar as draft documents or sheets; set up draft course entries or sessions in the learning system without enrolling anyone.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish the approved calendar, create the agreed sessions and enrol the agreed groups, within the limits the client set (which teams, which programmes, which dates). Booking a provider or signing a contract always needs a new yes, and payment stays with the client. Log every action.

## What you need

1. Next year's business goals, in the client's words, with any targets.
2. Evidence of gaps: survey export (team-level), review themes with names removed, manager requests, error or incident logs, complaint or ticket topics, audit findings.
3. Headcount by team, role and level, and who joins or moves next year.
4. Busy seasons, blackout dates and how many hours people can spend learning.
5. Costs: internal trainer time, provider quotes, platform fees, travel. If none are given, the budget section lists what is needed instead of guessing.
6. Last year's plan and what is known about its results.

## Steps

### 1. Write the goals as outcomes

List each business goal and the measure the client already tracks for it (for example "first-contact resolution" or "late deliveries"). A programme that serves no goal does not go in the plan.

### 2. Count the gaps

Read every source and log each gap once: what people cannot do, which team and level, how many people, and the source with its count ("12 of 31 team leaders rated delegation 1 or 2 out of 4; survey Q14"). Never quote an individual's review or rating.

### 3. Ask "is training the fix?"

For each gap, ask:
- Could they do it if their life depended on it? If yes, it is not a skill gap.
- Do they have the time, tools, information and clear rules?
- Is the right behaviour rewarded, or punished?
- Is it a hiring or role design problem?

Mark each gap **training**, **non-training** (with the likely fix and owner) or **both**. Report non-training gaps plainly; they often matter more than the courses.

### 4. Rank

Rank training gaps by business impact (which goal, how much), people affected and urgency (safety, legal deadlines, go-live dates). Show the reasoning in one line each. Required training (safety, AI literacy where the EU AI Act applies, sector rules the client names) goes first, marked "check this with local rules".

### 5. Design programmes

For each top gap: the behaviour that should change, who attends, format (on-the-job practice, workshop, e-learning, coaching, job aid, or a mix), length, timing, who delivers and the manager's role before and after. Prefer the smallest format that changes the behaviour.

### 6. Lay out the calendar

Month by month, avoiding busy seasons and stacking no more than the hours the client allowed per person. Mark dependencies, such as a new system's go-live date.

### 7. Build the budget

Only from costs the client gave. Each line: programme, cost type, amount, source ("quote from Provider B, 3 Oct"). Total against the budget. Where costs are missing, list them as **[cost needed]**. To compare providers, use the same criteria for each: fit to the gap, practice time, evaluation offered, delivery dates, total cost including staff time, references.

### 8. Add an evaluation hook to each programme

Name the Level 4 measure and its baseline, the Level 3 behaviour and how it will be seen, and when each will be checked. The full plan comes from the `training-evaluation-plan` skill.

## Checks

- Every gap shows its source and count; no individual can be identified.
- Every programme serves a named goal.
- Non-training gaps are listed with a suggested owner, not dropped.
- Calendar fits busy seasons and the hours allowed.
- Every cost comes from the client or is marked [cost needed].
- Funding, levy, pay and legal questions are flagged for an adviser, not answered.
- At level 3, only the agreed groups were enrolled, and the action log is updated.

## Output format

1. Summary (five lines): top three gaps, top three programmes, total cost, biggest non-training issue.
2. Gap table: gap, team and level, count, source, training / non-training / both.
3. Non-training issues with suggested owner.
4. Ranked programme list with one-line reasons.
5. Programme cards: behaviour, audience, format, length, timing, delivery, manager role, evaluation hook.
6. Month-by-month calendar.
7. Budget table and provider comparison.
8. Open questions for the client.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Harrow & Finch Logistics, 410 staff, goal "cut late deliveries".*

> **Gap 2:** Shift supervisors do not re-plan routes when a driver calls in sick. Count: 9 of 14 supervisors (incident log, Jan to Sep, 37 late-delivery incidents tagged "no re-plan"). Training? **Both.** The routing tool has the feature, but only two supervisors were shown it; the sick-call process also has no deadline (non-training, owner: operations manager).
>
> **Programme:** "Re-plan in 15 minutes", 90-minute hands-on session in the routing tool's test account, three real incident cases, one-page job aid at each desk. Delivered by the transport planner in February, before the spring peak. Managers run a 10-minute check-in two weeks later using the job aid.
>
> **Evaluation hook:** Level 4, late deliveries tagged "no re-plan" per month (baseline: about 4). Level 3, supervisor re-plans logged in the tool within the first hour of a sick call, checked in weeks 2 and 6.
>
> **Cost:** trainer time [cost needed: planner day rate]; no provider fees.
