# Training interview: learning and development (L&D) specialist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share names, reviews or personal details of real staff or trainees; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of organisations and roles have you trained for, and in which countries?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's sector range and which official rules it points to.

2. **When a manager asks you for "a course on X", what do you ask first?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's intake, the part plain AI skips.

3. **Tell me about a time training was not the answer. How did you spot it, and what fixed the problem instead?**
   Why it matters: the training-or-not judgement is the core skill of the role.

4. **How do you run a training needs analysis when the data is thin or messy?**
   Why it matters: real clients rarely have clean surveys; the agent needs the expert's workarounds.

5. **How do you rank training requests when the budget covers a third of them?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's trade-off rules.

6. **Show me a skills matrix you built. How many skills per role, what scale, and how did you stop everyone rating themselves 3 out of 4?**
   Why it matters: matrix design details decide whether the data is usable.

7. **How do you write a goal people can show? Give me a weak one you rewrote.**
   Why it matters: action-based goals drive design and evaluation.

8. **Walk me through turning one SOP into on-the-job training. What do you do with gaps or out-of-order steps?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's method and how strictly they stick to the source.

9. **What does a fair sign-off look like? How many watched attempts, and what happens when someone is not ready?**
   Why it matters: sign-off rules protect staff and the business; the agent should copy the expert's standard.

10. **How do you design onboarding so new hires do real work in week one?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's sequencing and readiness checks.

11. **What is your go-to workshop structure, and which exercises do you rely on with a group that talks over each other?**
    Why it matters: facilitation tactics are hard to learn from books.

12. **How do you get managers to support training before and after?**
    Why it matters: transfer depends on managers; the agent needs the expert's scripts.

13. **How do you evaluate a programme in practice? Which of Kirkpatrick's four levels do you actually measure, and how?**
    Why it matters: captures real evaluation habits, not textbook ones.

14. **Tell me about a time a sponsor wanted an ROI figure. What did you give them, and how did you isolate the effect?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert handles pressure for numbers honestly.

15. **What evaluation data has misled you, and how did you catch it?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent the traps in surveys and completion rates.

16. **How do you handle safety or regulated training? Who signs off, and where do you stop?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's limits on required training.

17. **What do you do differently for apprentices or trainees?**
    Why it matters: trainee programmes have their own structure, mentors and provider reporting.

18. **How do you train staff for a new software system with a tight go-live date?**
    Why it matters: rollout training is common and time-pressured; the agent needs the expert's shortcuts.

19. **Which tools do you use (LMS, surveys, boards), and what do you never let a tool decide?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's tool use and its access requests.

20. **Tell me about your hardest L&D job. What went wrong, and what would you do now?**
    Why it matters: a real story gives the agent judgement that rules alone cannot.

21. **What do you check before you send a plan or a module to a client?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's personal quality bar.

22. **Which L&D beliefs do you think are myths, and what do you say when a client quotes one?**
    Why it matters: helps the agent push back on forgetting-curve and 70:20:10 claims politely.
