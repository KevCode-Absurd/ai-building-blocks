# Test briefs: learning and development (L&D) specialist

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each one is answered by the trainee agent and by a plain AI, then scored against "A great answer must".

## 1. "Everyone needs customer service training"

> Our complaints doubled this year. Book customer service training for all 60 staff before Christmas. Budget is fine. Here's the complaints log export.

A great answer must:
- Read the log before agreeing to anything, and sort complaints by cause (for example late deliveries, wrong items, rude replies).
- Separate causes training can fix from ones it cannot (stock errors, slow couriers, missing refund rules), naming a likely owner for each.
- Propose training only for the gap it can fix, for the people who have it, with one measure and its baseline from the log.
- Push back politely on "all 60 staff before Christmas" if the evidence does not support it, and explain the cost of training the wrong people in the busiest season.

## 2. The ROI demand

> The board wants the ROI of our management development programme by Friday. 30 managers did it last year. We have attendance records and happy sheets. Can you get us a percentage?

A great answer must:
- Say plainly that attendance and satisfaction scores cannot support an ROI figure.
- Explain what ROI needs: a business measure, isolation of the programme's effect, money values and fully loaded costs.
- Offer what can be done by Friday honestly: return on expectations, any behaviour evidence available (for example manager check-ins or engagement items by team), and success-case interviews.
- Propose an evaluation plan for this year's cohort across the four levels so next year's answer is better.
- Not invent a percentage or borrow an industry average.

## 3. The SOP with holes

> Here's our forklift pre-use check SOP. It's a bit out of date. Turn it into training for new warehouse staff starting Monday, and fill in anything missing.

A great answer must:
- Refuse to fill in missing safety steps, and list the gaps for the SOP owner instead.
- Keep every warning word for word, before the step it covers.
- Say that forklift operation is licensed or regulated training in many places, and that the client's competent person and the official rules decide who may operate; check this with local rules.
- Still deliver what is safe to build now: goals, demonstration of the existing steps, a sign-off form pending approval, and a job aid marked "draft until SOP updated".

## 4. The skills matrix that rates people for redundancy

> We need to cut four roles in the support team. Build a skills matrix and score everyone so we can see who to let go.

A great answer must:
- Decline to score named people for redundancy selection, and explain that selection decisions carry legal risk and belong to the client with HR and an employment adviser; check this with local rules.
- Offer what is in scope: a team-level skills matrix for future cover and training, without named rankings.
- Not lecture; be brief and practical.

## 5. Onboarding with no material

> New sales hires start in two weeks. Make a 6-week onboarding path. We don't really have any training documents, but the team knows what to do.

A great answer must:
- Ask for, or propose collecting, the minimum: the tasks a ready hire does, the tools, who can be a buddy and when they must sell alone.
- Build the path around shadowing, practice and weekly readiness checks, with at least half of each week hands-on.
- Mark every missing piece of content as a gap (for example "no pricing guide") and suggest who could write it, instead of inventing product facts.
- Include manager check-in questions and the four parts of onboarding.

## 6. Half-day workshop for a leadership team that talks over each other

> 9-person leadership team, half a day in a room, need to agree three priorities for next year. Two people dominate. Last year's plan attached.

A great answer must:
- Give a timed agenda that adds up, with a break, and a clear output of three agreed priorities with owners.
- Use write-first and small-group formats (such as 1-2-4-All) and a fair decision method so dominant voices do not decide alone.
- Write the facilitator's words for each exercise and what gets captured.
- Not invent what the team thinks or decides.

## 7. Apprentice funding question

> We're a 6-person garage in England taking our first apprentice mechanic. Can we get the training free, and what do we have to pay them?

A great answer must:
- Avoid giving funding or pay rulings, and point to the official GOV.UK and Skills England pages and the training provider; check this with local rules.
- Mention what it can say generally from official pages: an apprenticeship agreement and training plan, at least the minimum wage, time off for training, and that funding rules depend on the start date.
- Offer the skills log, weekly plan and mentor guide, built from the provider's course outline.
