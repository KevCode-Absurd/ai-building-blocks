# Launch and evaluate one training programme

**Trigger:** A manager or owner asks for training on a specific task or skill, for example a new refund process, a new site opening or repeated errors on one job.

**Cadence:** Runs once per programme, from the request to the six-week behaviour check, with a results check at the date agreed with the sponsor.

**Access:** At the start, ask which level the client grants for each tool (learning system, survey tool, documents, workshop board, calendar) and the limits, and record them under "Access I have". Until they answer, work at level 1. Steps marked [L2] need read and draft access; steps marked [L3] need read, write and publish access within the client's limits.

## Steps

1. **Diagnose.** Run the `is-it-a-training-problem` prompt with the manager's request and the evidence. If the verdict is non-training, hand the findings to the owner and stop here. *Approval: the sponsor agrees the verdict.*
2. **Agree success first.** Use the `training-evaluation-plan` skill, steps 1 and 2: the result the sponsor expects, its measure and baseline, and the two to four critical behaviours. Write the return on expectations down. *Approval: the sponsor signs off the measures.*
3. **Design.** Pick the format from the gap. For a procedure, use `sop-to-on-the-job-training`; for a group session, the `workshop-agenda-and-facilitator-guide` prompt; for a system change, the `software-rollout-training-pack` prompt. Every step and warning comes from the client's material; gaps go to the owner.
4. **Plan the checks.** `training-evaluation-plan` steps 3 and 4: decision and task checks at Level 2, relevance questions at Level 1, the observation form for Level 3. [L2] Build the survey, quiz and forms as drafts.
5. **Brief managers.** A one-page brief: why the training, what people will practise, what to look for on the job, and the check-in questions for weeks 2 and 6.
6. **Pilot.** Run it once with a small group. Fix timings, unclear steps and practice tasks that are too easy. *Approval: the client approves the final version.*
7. **Confirm the live action list.** [L3] Show the actions and limits: publish the module, enrol the named groups, send the surveys on the agreed dates, record confirmed sign-offs. *Approval: the client's yes.*
8. **Deliver and log.** [L3] Publish, enrol, remind and record within the limits. Log every action.
9. **Check behaviour at weeks 2 and 6.** Managers or quality checkers use the observation form; collect the data. Run a short success-case follow-up with a few people who used it well and a few who did not.
10. **Report.** `training-evaluation-plan` step 8: results by level against baseline, other possible causes, and a recommendation to keep, change, scale or stop.

## Outputs

- Diagnosis and agreed success measures
- Training materials (module or session plan, trainer guide, sign-off form, job aid)
- Manager brief and check-in questions
- Surveys, checks and observation forms
- Pilot notes and the final version
- Results report and the action log

## Needs the client's approval

The diagnosis, the success measures, the final version after the pilot, and the level 3 action list and limits. Anything outside the limits needs a new yes: a new group, deleting or changing training records, sharing results beyond the named audience, or booking a provider. Safety content is signed off by the client's competent person; check this with local rules.
