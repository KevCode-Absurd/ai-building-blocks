# Subscription offer and cancel path

A subscription discount is paid every month, not once. A hidden cancel button wins a month and loses the customer, the reviews and sometimes a regulator's attention. This skill builds both halves: an offer that pays, and a way out that is easy and honest.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Subscription and auto-renewal rules differ by country and US state; the client checks them with a qualified adviser before launch.

## When to use

- Launching "subscribe and save" or a subscription box.
- Changing price, discount, perks or delivery frequency for existing subscribers.
- A churn read (see `retention-cohort-read`) shows a drop at one box or month.
- The client wants a "save flow" on the cancel page.

## What you need

- **Product and costs:** price, cost of goods, packaging, shipping per order, payment fees, and margin after all of them.
- **Data, if the subscription exists:** subscriber export with start, cancel and pause dates, cancel reasons, failed payments, and orders per subscriber.
- **The subscription app** and what it can do: pause, skip, swap, change frequency, cancel reasons, save offers, reminder emails.
- **Where subscribers live:** countries and US states.
- **Cash limits:** how many months the business can wait to earn back the first-order loss, if there is one.

**Access:** level 1 to read the app's settings and exports; level 2 to draft plans, discounts and screen copy; level 3 to publish approved changes within limits. Price or renewal changes for existing subscribers always go back to the client, because they need notice. Refunds and payment changes stay with the client.

## Steps

1. **Do the monthly maths.** For each plan: price after the subscription discount, minus cost of goods, packaging, shipping and payment fees, equals contribution per order. Multiply by the expected number of orders kept (use the client's own drop-out table; for a new subscription, show three cases: short, middle and long). If there is a first-order offer, subtract it once. Show the months needed to earn back any first-order loss. If contribution per order is thin, change the offer before launch, not after.
2. **Shape the offer.** Prefer perks that cost little and matter to the customer (choice of flavour, early access, free shipping already built into the price, a gift on a later box) over a deep, permanent discount. Keep one simple plan first; add a prepaid or longer plan only if the maths works.
3. **Plan the first boxes.** People decide in the first few deliveries. Write the first-box message (what to expect and when), a how-to-use note, and a check-in before the second charge. If the drop-out table shows a weak box, plan what changes in that box.
4. **Write the sign-up terms in plain words.** Next to the subscribe button: what renews, how often, the amount, when the next charge happens, and how to cancel. Get express agreement to the renewal terms; no pre-ticked subscription boxes.
5. **Write reminders and notices.** A reminder before each charge if the app supports it (Shopify Subscriptions sends one 3 days before each recurring charge). Price-change notices with the new amount, the date and how to cancel, sent early. For California consumers, the auto-renewal law sets notice windows for accepted price changes and for trials longer than 31 days; check current rules for every place the client sells.
6. **Build the cancel path.**
   - The cancel option is easy to find in the customer account and takes no more steps than signing up did.
   - Ask for a reason (one click, optional).
   - Show at most one save offer that fits the reason: too much coffee becomes "deliver every 6 weeks"; going on holiday becomes "pause for a month"; does not like the flavour becomes "swap"; too expensive becomes a smaller size or a skip. Keep a clear "cancel anyway" button on the same screen.
   - On a no, cancel at once and confirm in writing, with the date of the last charge.
   - No forced calls, chats or repeated offers.
7. **Handle failed payments separately.** Involuntary churn is a billing problem: clear payment-update emails, retries set in the billing tool, and no marketing in those messages.
8. **Write the post-cancel message.** Confirm the cancel, thank them, and leave the door open (how to restart). A win-back offer can follow later, timed to the product's usual gap, and only with marketing consent.
9. **Set the measures.** Subscribers kept past the weak box, save rate by reason, cancellations within a week of a save (a save that just delays a cancel is not a save), complaints about cancelling, and contribution per subscriber.

## Checks

- Contribution per order is positive after discount, shipping and fees, and the cash wait is within the client's limit.
- The sign-up shows renewal terms, amount, frequency and how to cancel next to the button.
- Cancelling takes no more steps than joining; "cancel anyway" is always visible; one save offer at most.
- Reminders and price-change notices exist, and the rules for each region have been flagged for the client to check.
- Failed-payment messages carry no marketing.
- The client has been told this is general guidance and to check the law with a qualified adviser.

## Output format

1. **Offer maths table** per plan, with the three cases and months to earn back.
2. **Offer and perks**, with reasons.
3. **First-box plan** and messages.
4. **Sign-up terms, reminders and notices**, ready to paste.
5. **Cancel path:** screen by screen, with copy and the save offer per reason.
6. **Failed-payment and post-cancel messages.**
7. **Measures, read date, region rules to check, and the access each step needs.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Hollowbrook Tea Club is a made-up business used only to show the format: a monthly tea box sold to UK and US customers. All figures are invented.*

**Offer maths (monthly box):** price £18 at 10% subscriber discount = £16.20; goods £5.40, packaging £0.90, shipping £3.20, fees £0.55; contribution £6.15 per box. Kept for 3, 5 or 8 boxes: £18.45, £30.75 or £49.20 per subscriber. First box at half price costs £8.10 once, earned back in the second box.

**Drop-out:** 31% of subscribers leave after box 3. Cancel reasons at box 3: "too much tea" 44%, "too expensive" 21%.

**This month's fix:** add a "every 2 months" option, offered first to "too much tea" cancellers. Keep "cancel anyway" on the same screen.

**Cancel screen copy:** "Before you go: would every 2 months suit you better? [Switch to every 2 months] [Cancel anyway]". On "Cancel anyway": "Done. Your subscription is cancelled and you won't be charged again. Your last box ships on 4 March."

**Check with an adviser:** UK subscription contract rules (regulations not yet in force when checked) and the US auto-renewal rules for the states customers live in.
