# SMS channel launch

**Trigger:** a shop decides to start texting customers, moves to a new SMS platform, or restarts texting after problems with opt-outs, complaints or carrier filtering. General guidance, not legal advice; the client checks the rules for their regions with a qualified adviser.

## Steps

1. **Access check.** Confirm the level for the SMS platform, the store and any sign-up form tool, and record it under "Access I have". Until the client answers, level 1.
2. **Regions and number type (level 1).** List where text subscribers will be (countries, US states) and pick the sending route: US 10-digit long code, toll-free or short code; UK or EU sender ID or number. For US long codes, the client's platform registers the brand and campaign; I draft the campaign description, sample texts and opt-in description for it.
3. **Consent set-up (skill: sms-consent-and-send-plan, steps 1 to 3).** Rewrite every opt-in point with a separate, unticked SMS box and clear wording. Decide on double opt-in (required by US carriers for cart texts; preferred in some EU countries). Define what consent record each number gets.
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves the opt-in wording, the forms and the frequency promised to subscribers.
4. **Replies and settings (skill steps 4 and 5).** First text, help and stop replies, local-language keywords, opt-out handling for replies and requests by email or phone, quiet hours by recipient location and the frequency cap.
5. **First flows (skill step 6).** Usually the welcome text, then cart (US: double opt-in only, one text within 48 hours), then reorder for consumables. Write each text with the prompt `text-message-pack-check` as the last check.
   **Approval point 2:** the client approves the texts, flows, timing and any offer.
6. **Build.**
   - Level 1: deliver the settings, copy and a build checklist.
   - Level 2: build forms unpublished, flows switched off and texts as drafts; test on internal numbers in each region (opt-in, first text, STOP, HELP, a free-text "please stop"). Log each test.
7. **Go live.**
   - Level 1 or 2: the client publishes the forms and switches on the flows.
   - Level 3: show the action list (forms, flows, caps, hours, which numbers can receive texts) and the limits, get a yes, then go live within them. Existing phone numbers without a consent record are not texted; an opt-in invitation goes by email instead.
8. **First two weeks.** Watch opt-out rate per text, complaints, failed and filtered texts, and replies that read as opt-outs. Stop and tell the client if any of them jumps.
9. **Report.** Subscribers gained by source, texts sent, orders from text flows against a holdout where the list allows, opt-outs and complaints, the action log, and next steps.

## Outputs

- Region and sending-route note, with registration drafts for US long codes.
- Opt-in wording for every form, and the consent record spec.
- Reply set-up, quiet hours and frequency cap settings.
- Welcome, cart and reorder texts with character counts.
- Test log, action log and a two-week report.

## Needs the client's approval

- The access level per tool and, for level 3, the limits.
- Opt-in wording, forms and the promised frequency (point 1).
- Texts, flows, timing and offers (point 2).
- Texting any number group not covered by the approved list, any import of numbers, and any change to the caps, asked again each time.
