# Link prospect scoring

## When to use

- A campaign needs a target list and you have a pile of possible sites.
- A competitor backlink gap export needs sorting into real opportunities and noise.
- An agency or vendor has offered a list of "high authority" sites and someone needs to check it.
- The client wants an approved list before any outreach at level 3.

Reading exports and visiting the sites needs level 1. Building the scored list in a sheet or outreach tool needs level 2. Nothing is sent from this skill; the approved list is the gate for outreach at level 3.

## What you need

1. The campaign: the asset or story, the target page, the audience and the region.
2. The prospect list: URLs pasted in, a backlink tool export (competitor backlinks, link gap), or search results the client collected.
3. Any sites the client already has relationships with, and any it must avoid (competitors, sensitive topics).
4. The client's contact sources: media database, own contacts, or editors' published contact pages.

## Steps

### 1. Set the rules before scoring

Agree three tests, in words the client understands:
- **Topic fit:** the site regularly covers the client's subject or audience.
- **Real readers:** signs people read it: recent dated articles, comments or shares, a newsletter, coverage elsewhere, search visibility in a tool if available.
- **Editorial standards:** named authors, an about page, corrections, a clear line between ads and editorial.

And one disqualifier: **link-selling signs**. A "write for us" page listing prices, "guest post" or "sponsored post" packages with followed links, articles on every unrelated topic, outbound links to casinos, loans or pills in body text, or many thin posts each with one commercial link. These match practices Google's spam policies describe as link spam.

### 2. Clean the list

Remove duplicates by domain, the client's own sites, competitors, social profiles that don't link, and dead pages. Group by site type: news, trade press, blogs, associations, local organisations, resource pages, directories.

### 3. Check each site

Visit the site (level 1, reading only). For each test record a short note and a pass, partial or fail. With web search or browsing connected, I check each site live: recent articles, author pages, "write for us" pages. Without it, I work from the export and anything you paste (screenshots, recent article links), and mark each test "not checked live" so you can spot-check before approving. Third-party scores (Domain Rating and similar) can help sort a long list, but they are the vendor's own numbers, not Google's, and they can be inflated by spam. Never let a score override a failed topic or editorial test.

### 4. Watch for site reputation abuse

Be wary of sections on strong sites that host third-party content unrelated to the main site, such as coupon, casino or "best of" pages on a news or school domain. Google treats content placed mainly to borrow a host's ranking signals as site reputation abuse. A link there is risky and often short-lived.

### 5. Score

- **Pitch:** passes all three tests; there is a clear reason this outlet's readers would care about the story.
- **Maybe:** fits but has a weakness (low activity, loose topic fit); pitch only if the list is short.
- **Skip:** fails a test or shows link-selling signs.

Write one line on why for each, and the best angle for each pitch.

### 6. Note the contact source

For pitch and maybe sites, note where the right contact should come from: the client's media database, the client's own contacts, or the author or contact page on the site. Don't guess personal email addresses or scrape against a site's terms. Leave blank where no proper source exists.

### 7. Hand over for approval

Present the pitch list, the maybe list and the skip list with reasons. The client approves the final list for this campaign; at level 3 outreach goes only to that list. Anyone added later needs a new yes.

### 8. Review vendor lists separately

When the list came from a vendor selling placements, add a summary: how many sites showed link-selling signs, whether links would be followed, whether the content would be labelled as an ad. Recommend against any offer of followed links for payment, and explain the alternative: earned coverage, or a labelled ad with `rel="sponsored"` if the client wants paid exposure.

## Checks before handing over

- The rules are written in plain words and applied the same way to every site.
- Every score has a one-line reason.
- No site with link-selling signs is in the pitch list.
- Third-party scores never override a failed test.
- Contact sources are proper ones; no guessed or scraped personal emails.
- The list is marked "for approval"; nothing has been sent.

## Output format

1. The rules (three tests and the disqualifier).
2. Summary: how many sites in, pitch, maybe, skip.
3. Table: site, page, type, topic fit, readers, editorial, seller signs, score, reason, angle, contact source.
4. Vendor list notes, if any.
5. Questions for the client.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: "Brightwater Tiles", a UK online tile shop with a new guide to waterproofing small bathrooms.*

> **Summary:** 40 sites in; 9 pitch, 7 maybe, 24 skip (14 for seller signs, 6 off-topic, 4 inactive).
>
> | Site | Type | Fit | Readers | Editorial | Seller signs | Score | Reason and angle |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | A home renovation magazine site | Consumer press | Pass | Pass: dated articles this week, newsletter | Pass: named editors | None | Pitch | Runs small-bathroom features; angle: the five mistakes that cause leaks, with photos |
> | A plumbers' trade association | Association | Pass | Partial: member news only | Pass | None | Maybe | Has a homeowner resources page; angle: offer the guide as a free resource |
> | "Best Home Tips Hub" | Blog | Partial | Fail: no comments, thin posts | Fail: no authors | Prices for "do-follow guest posts" | Skip | Sells followed links |
>
> **For approval:** the 9 pitch sites. Contacts: 5 from your media database, 4 from the sites' editor pages.
