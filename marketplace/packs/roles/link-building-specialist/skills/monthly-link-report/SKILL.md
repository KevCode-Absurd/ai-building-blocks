# Monthly link report

## When to use

- The month has ended and the client or manager wants to know what was earned.
- A campaign has finished and its results need writing up.
- Links seem to have been lost after a site change.
- The backlink profile shows something odd and someone needs a calm read.

Reading exports, Search Console and the tracking sheet needs level 1. Saving the report and updating the tracking sheet needs level 2. Sending recovery emails for lost links needs level 3 within the approved list. Uploading a disavow file or sending a reconsideration request needs the client's fresh yes every time, even at level 3.

## What you need

1. A backlink tool export for the month: new and lost links, with the linking page, target page, anchor and link type. Ahrefs, Semrush or Majestic all work.
2. Search Console's Links report export and a look at the Manual Actions report.
3. The outreach tracking sheet and the campaign list.
4. Site changes this month (redesigns, moved pages, deleted pages).
5. Referral traffic from analytics for linked pages, if available.

## Steps

### 1. Clean the data

Remove duplicates, internal links and links from the client's own other sites. Group links by referring domain; ten links from one site are one referring domain. Note that the Search Console Links report is a sample and doesn't show whether links are nofollow, so link type comes from the backlink tool or a manual check.

### 2. Match links to campaigns

Use the tracking sheet to match each new link to a campaign or mark it organic (not from outreach). Unlinked mentions from Google Alerts go in their own list.

### 3. Sort by value

For each new referring domain: topic relevance (high, medium, low), real readers (yes, unsure, no), link type (followed, nofollow, sponsored, ugc), target page, and referral visits if known. Put relevant links from real sites to important pages first. Third-party scores can be shown in a column, labelled as the vendor's own score, never as the headline.

### 4. Check lost links

For each lost link: the reason if visible (page removed, link edited out, the client's page now returns an error or redirects badly). Where the client's own change broke it, fix it with a redirect (level 3, within the site sections named) or ask the site to update the URL. Where the site removed it on purpose, let it go.

### 5. Read the risk signs

Look for spikes of links from unrelated or foreign-language sites, exact-match anchors in bulk, links from pages that sell links, or footer and widget links across many sites. Check the Manual Actions report. Then decide calmly:
- **No manual action and nothing that looks like the client's own past schemes:** do nothing. Google says most sites will not need the disavow tool, and its link spam systems aim to ignore bad links.
- **Links the client or an old vendor paid for:** ask those sites to remove or qualify them, and record the attempts.
- **A manual action for unnatural links, or one is likely:** prepare a removal log, a disavow file for what couldn't be removed, and a reconsideration request, at level 2, for the client's yes. A new upload replaces the old disavow list.

### 6. Write what worked

Which angle, outlet type or asset earned links, which pitches got replies, and which failed and the best guess why. Keep a short list of failed subject lines with reasons; it improves next month's pitches.

### 7. Three next steps

Concrete and small: "Pitch the regional cut to 12 local news sites", "Fix the redirect from the old guide URL", "Answer journalist requests on pet costs twice a week".

### 8. Keep it honest

No inflated totals, no counting nofollow or unlinked mentions as followed links, no claims that links caused a ranking change unless the timing and other changes support it. If a Google core or spam update ran during the month, say so before reading any ranking change. With web search connected, I check Google's Search Status Dashboard live for the dates. Without it, I ask you or note "update dates not checked".

## Checks before handing over

- Link totals count referring domains, not raw links, and say so.
- Link types are shown separately.
- Every new link is matched to a campaign or marked organic.
- Lost links have a reason and an action or "let go".
- Any disavow or reconsideration step is prepared for approval, never done silently.
- Updates from Google during the month are noted.
- The report fits on one page plus an appendix table.

## Output format

1. Headline: the month in two sentences.
2. Table of new referring domains: site, linking page, target page, campaign, relevance, link type, referral visits.
3. Unlinked mentions and the action for each.
4. Lost links worth chasing.
5. Risk check and decision.
6. What worked and what didn't.
7. Three next steps.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: "Brightwater Tiles", a UK online tile shop, October report.*

> **Headline:** 11 new referring domains in October, 7 from the waterproofing guide campaign. Two lost links came from our own URL change and are fixed.
>
> | Site | Target page | Campaign | Relevance | Type | Referral visits |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | A home renovation magazine | Waterproofing guide | Guide outreach | High | Followed | 140 |
> | A plumbers' trade association | Waterproofing guide | Guide outreach | High | Followed | 35 |
> | A regional newspaper | Home page | Local story | Medium | Nofollow | 60 |
>
> **Unlinked mentions:** 3; one replied and added a link.
>
> **Risk check:** 20 new links from unrelated foreign-language directories. No manual action. No disavow needed; we watch it next month.
>
> **Next steps:** pitch the regional cut to 10 local news sites; refresh the guide with winter tips; answer two journalist requests a week on home repairs.
