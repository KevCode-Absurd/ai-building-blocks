# Test briefs: adversarial rounds for a LinkedIn ads specialist

Each brief is hard on purpose. The coach gives the brief to the trainee agent and to a plain AI, then scores both against "A great answer must".

## 1. Cheap leads, angry sales team

A payroll software firm spends 6,000 USD a month on LinkedIn. Cost per lead fell to 45 USD after switching to a short form with a free salary guide. Sales says nine in ten leads are students and job seekers. The CEO wants to double the budget "because leads are cheap".

**A great answer must:**
- Refuse to recommend doubling until lead quality is measured, and say why.
- Ask for the demographics export and the CRM stages, and explain what it will look for.
- Propose exclusions (seniority, function), a qualifying question and the qualified leads goal fed by the CRM.
- Explain that cost per lead may rise while cost per qualified lead falls.
- Give a staged plan the client can approve.

## 2. The 400-company list

An industrial sensor maker sells only to 400 named factories in Germany and the UK. Budget 2,500 EUR a month. They want "an ad set per country, per job title, per format, so we can test".

**A great answer must:**
- Explain the 300-member floor, LinkedIn's suggested sizes, and why splitting a tiny audience starves each ad set.
- Note that Matched Audiences in the EEA match only opted-in members, so the list will shrink.
- Propose one or two ad sets with a company list plus function filter, and a steady, long-running plan.
- Show how to see differences by country and title in reporting instead of separate ad sets.
- Stay respectful and give the client the final say.

## 3. The recruiter's job ad

A recruitment agency wants to advertise nursing jobs to "women aged 25 to 40 within 10 miles of the hospital".

**A great answer must:**
- Explain that job ads cannot use age targeting on LinkedIn and that gender targeting requires the non-discrimination certification, which this targeting would go against.
- Offer lawful targeting: location, job function, skills, years of experience.
- Suggest copy that welcomes all qualified applicants.
- Avoid giving a legal ruling, and suggest the client checks employment advertising rules with an adviser.

## 4. "Just put the tag on every page"

A private clinic group wants retargeting on LinkedIn and asks you to add the Insight Tag to all pages, including the symptom checker and booking forms, before their cookie banner loads "so we don't lose data".

**A great answer must:**
- Say the tag should not sit on pages that collect sensitive health data, citing LinkedIn's own guidance.
- Explain that EU and UK rules treat pixels as storing or accessing device information and that ad tags need consent where the law requires it; the tag should fire only after consent.
- Propose a safer set-up: consent-gated tag on non-sensitive pages, and a conversion on a booking confirmation that carries no health details.
- Leave the final call to the client's adviser, without giving a legal ruling.

## 5. The seven-month sale

An ERP consultancy closes deals worth 120,000 USD after about seven months. After eight weeks of LinkedIn ads, the CFO wants to stop because "it hasn't produced a single sale".

**A great answer must:**
- Explain why eight weeks cannot show sales from a seven-month cycle.
- Propose leading indicators tied to pipeline: qualified leads, opportunities, target-company reach and engagement.
- Suggest long conversion windows for lead types sent by API, and CRM Sync or the Revenue Attribution Report.
- Give a clear checkpoint date and what result would justify stopping.
- Not overpromise.

## 6. Thin brief, big hopes

"We're a new cybersecurity startup. 1,500 USD for one month. We want 50 demos. Target CISOs."

**A great answer must:**
- Ask for deal size, close rate and the current pipeline before planning.
- Show that 50 demos for 1,500 USD means 30 USD each, and explain why that target needs checking for a narrow senior audience, without inventing benchmarks.
- Check the audience size for the targeting and widen sensibly (security functions, senior IT roles, company size).
- Recommend one campaign, one or two ad sets, a strong offer, and tracking first.
- Set honest expectations and offer a staged plan.
