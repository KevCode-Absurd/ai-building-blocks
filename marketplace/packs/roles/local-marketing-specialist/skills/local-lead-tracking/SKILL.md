# Local lead tracking

Most local customers call, message or walk in, so analytics alone misses them. This skill makes the front desk, the phone and the profile into a tracking system the owner trusts, and ends in one number per channel: cost per booked job.

Access levels for this skill: designing the tracker and reading reports is level 1. Building the sheet or booking-system field, tagged links and call-tracking set-up as drafts for approval is level 2. Changing the live profile link, phone numbers, Google Ads call conversions or Local Services Ads settings is level 3, within the agreed limits; a change to the phone number on the profile always needs a fresh yes and never happens while verification is pending.

## When to use

- "I don't know which marketing works."
- Before any paid local test (Local Services Ads, search ads, postcards, local radio).
- Before or just after switching on Local Services Ads.
- When the profile shows many call-button clicks but the diary is not filling.

## What you need

- **Access I have:** levels and limits per tool. Until the owner answers, level 1.
- How enquiries arrive today (phone, walk-in, booking app, website form, text or WhatsApp, Local Services Ads) and roughly how many a week.
- Who answers the phone, and the missed-call count if the phone system shows it.
- Average job value and gross profit per job.
- Monthly spend and staff time on each channel.
- The booking system or diary, and whether it has a "source" field.
- Business Profile performance downloads for the last few months.

## Steps

1. **Build the lead source tracker.** One row per new enquiry: date, contact method, "How did you hear about us?" (fixed list plus "other"), service, booked yes or no, job value, notes. The list must match the channels being tested (Google search or Maps, Local Services Ads, a friend, postcard code, Facebook, local paper, walked past). Put it in the booking system if it has a source field; otherwise a shared sheet.
2. **Train the question.** Staff ask every new caller and walk-in, and write the answer at the time. Owners skip this when busy, so check it weekly.
3. **Tag the profile link.** Point the website link at the most useful page and add UTM tags in lowercase (for example `utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`), so profile visits show separately in GA4. Tag every other link you control the same way (posts, emails, QR codes on print).
4. **Download profile data monthly.** Calls (call-button clicks), direction requests, website clicks, bookings and messages. Remember views count each person once a day, and search terms update monthly.
5. **Decide on call tracking.**
   - Website calls from ads: Google forwarding numbers on the site, where the country is supported, with a sensible minimum call length.
   - Other channels: a separate tracked number per print piece or campaign from a call-tracking tool (paid; CallRail is one example).
   - The profile itself: Google's guidelines ask for a local number the business controls and that does not redirect people elsewhere, and they do not mention tracking numbers. If the owner wants one there, keep it local and owned by the business, keep the main number as an additional number, and record it as the owner's decision.
6. **Match Local Services Ads leads.** From the LSA Leads page, list each lead with its status (charged, not charged, in review, credited) and match it to the diary: booked or not. Note missed calls; they cost leads and lower LSA ranking. In Europe, lead credits are not available, so budget for some poor leads.
7. **Count staff time.** Time spent handing out flyers, running an open day or answering LSA messages counts as cost.
8. **Build the weekly sheet.** Per channel: leads, booked jobs, booking rate, spend plus staff time, cost per booked job, against the owner's allowable cost per booked job.
9. **Check the gaps monthly.** Compare tracker totals with diary totals. A big "don't know" or "Google" bucket means the question is being skipped or the list is too coarse.

## Checks

- Every new enquiry has a source, or the gap is visible.
- Tracker channel names match the channels being tested.
- Links are tagged consistently, in lowercase.
- Phone number changes on the profile were approved and not made during verification.
- LSA leads are matched to bookings, not just counted.
- Cost per booked job includes staff time.
- No personal data beyond what the business needs; the sheet sits in the owner's own account.

## Output format

1. **Lead map:** how enquiries arrive and how each is tracked.
2. **Tracker template:** columns and the "how did you hear" list.
3. **Link and number plan:** tagged links, tracked numbers, who owns each, access level.
4. **Weekly sheet:** channel, leads, booked, rate, cost, cost per booked job, against the allowable figure.
5. **Gaps and next fixes.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Oakfield Electrical is a made-up two-electrician firm, used only to show the format. Numbers are invented.*

**Lead map:** phone (most enquiries), website form, Local Services Ads (calls and messages), walk-ins: none. Tracking: tracker question for phone and form; LSA Leads page; tagged profile link; a separate tracked number on the October postcard drop.

**Week of 13 October**

| Channel | Leads | Booked | Rate | Spend + staff time | Cost per booked job |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Maps or search (profile) | 14 | 8 | 57% | staff time 1 hr (60) | 7.50 |
| Local Services Ads | 9 | 4 | 44% | 210 + 1 hr (60) | 67.50 |
| Postcard (tracked number) | 3 | 1 | 33% | 95 (one week's share) | 95.00 |
| Friend or neighbour | 5 | 4 | 80% | none | none |
| Don't know | 6 | 3 | 50% | | |

**Allowable cost per booked job (set by the owner):** 80. LSA is inside it; the postcard is above it after one week of three, so read it again at the end of the drop.

**Gaps:** six "don't know" answers this week, all taken by the apprentice; a two-minute refresher on Monday. Two LSA calls were missed on Saturday; set message leads on so weekend contacts can reach the firm.
