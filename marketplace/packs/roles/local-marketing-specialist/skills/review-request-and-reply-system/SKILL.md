# Review request and reply system

Reviews decide many local choices, and the honest route is also the only safe one: Google bans rewards, selective asking and staff quotas, and US, UK and EU law covers fake and suppressed reviews. This skill builds a habit staff can keep: ask everyone, the same way, and reply to every review as if the next customer is reading.

Access levels for this skill: auditing reviews and writing the kit needs level 1. Drafting replies for the owner to post is level 2 (replies are written in a shared document, because Business Profile has no unposted draft). Posting replies as a manager, within agreed limits such as "reply to four- and five-star reviews; send me anything three stars or below", is level 3. Sending review requests by text or email from the client's system is level 3, only to customers the client confirms it may message.

## When to use

- Fewer reviews than nearby rivals, or none in the last few months.
- Reviews sit unanswered.
- "How do we get more five-star reviews?"
- A bad review has landed and the owner is upset.

## What you need

- **Access I have:** levels and limits per tool. Until the owner answers, level 1.
- The profile's review count, rating, and the last 30 reviews with replies (screenshots or export).
- When a job or visit naturally ends, and who is with the customer at that moment.
- How the business contacts customers after a visit (text, email, booking system) and whether it has their consent to message them.
- The Google review link or QR code (from the profile's "ask for reviews" option).
- Any existing review cards, scripts or tools, so you can check them for gating or rewards.

## Steps

1. **Audit what exists.** Count reviews per month for six months, average rating, share replied to, how specific the replies are. Check any current scripts, cards, emails or review tools for:
   - rewards of any kind for a review (banned on Google);
   - "happy? review us; unhappy? tell us privately" flows (review gating, banned);
   - staff targets for review numbers or wording (banned);
   - Yelp review requests (Yelp asks businesses not to ask).
   Anything found goes to the top of the report with the fix.
2. **Pick the moment.** The natural end: job finished and checked, class over, pet collected, treatment done, order picked up.
3. **Write the ask.** One line staff can say in their own words, the same for every customer: what you did, that reviews help other locals choose, and where to find the link. No reward, no "only if you were happy".
4. **Write the follow-up.** One text or email, sent the same day or the next, to every customer the business may contact: thanks, the link, nothing else. Check consent rules for the region with the client (marketing consent rules differ in the US, UK and EU) and never message people the client cannot contact.
5. **Make the card.** Counter or van card with the QR code and one plain sentence. No rewards.
6. **Write reply templates by star level** as starting points, never pasted unchanged:
   - *5 and 4 stars:* thank them by name if shown, mention the specific job, one human detail, no sales pitch.
   - *3 stars:* thank them, acknowledge the specific issue, say what changes, offer a private contact.
   - *1 and 2 stars:* calm, short, no argument, protect privacy (no job details or health information), apologise where right, give a named contact and a way to put it right.
   - *Suspected fake (no record of the customer):* polite, factual ("We can't find a booking under this name; please contact us at ..."), then report it.
7. **Set the reporting rule.** Report only reviews that break policy (spam, conflicts of interest, off-topic, offensive), using the profile or Google's Reviews Management Tool, with one appeal if needed. Do not report reviews for being negative.
8. **Set the rhythm.** Replies within an agreed time; monthly count of new reviews, rating, share replied to, and themes customers mention.
9. **Write it up** as a one-page kit for staff and an owner's note.

## Checks

- No reward, discount, draw or gift is tied to a review anywhere in the kit.
- Every customer is asked; nobody is filtered by happiness.
- No staff targets for review counts.
- No request for Yelp reviews.
- Replies are specific, not promotional, and reveal nothing private.
- Messages go only to customers the client may contact, under the region's rules.
- Level 3 replies stay within the agreed star levels; anything else goes to the owner.

## Output format

1. **Audit:** counts, rating, reply rate, and any banned practices found with the fix.
2. **The ask:** the spoken line and when to say it.
3. **Follow-up message:** text and email versions.
4. **Card wording.**
5. **Reply templates** by star level, with one worked example each.
6. **Reporting rule** and steps.
7. **Monthly tracker** columns.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a dog grooming salon. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Audit:** 23 reviews, 4.8 average, 3 new in the last six months, 4 replied to. The counter card says "Leave us 5 stars and get £5 off your next groom." That is a reward for a review, which Google bans: replace the card this week.

**The ask (at pick-up, every owner):** "Bella was great today. If you have a minute, a Google review really helps other local dog owners find us; the link's on this card and in your text."

**Follow-up text (same evening, to clients who agreed to texts):** "Thanks for bringing Bella in today. If you'd like to leave a review, here's our Google link: [link]. [Business]."

**Reply, 5 stars:** "Thank you, Priya. Bella was a star for her first full groom, and the bandana suits her. See you both in six weeks."

**Reply, 2 stars ("Waited 40 minutes at pick-up"):** "Sorry for the wait at pick-up, Tom. We ran late after a dog needed extra care, and we should have texted you. We now text when we're running behind. Please call Sam on [number] so we can put it right."

**Monthly tracker:** month, new reviews, average of new reviews, replied within two days, themes (praise and complaints), action taken.
