# Seasonal offer calendar

Local demand rises and falls with the weather, school terms, holidays and harvests. Owners tend to market only when the diary is empty, which is too late. This skill plans the year ahead from the business's own numbers, so each season has a job and every offer protects the margin.

Access levels for this skill: planning needs level 1 (booking history, sales by month). Drafting posts, emails, texts and flyers for approval is level 2 (draft posts in a shared document, unsent email campaigns in the client's email tool). Publishing profile posts or sending campaigns on the agreed dates is level 3, within the limits set; any new offer, price or discount customers will see always needs the owner's yes first.

## When to use

- At the start of a year, or the start of work with a new client.
- Before a known peak (heating season, January, summer holidays, Christmas).
- "Can you give me an offer to fill next month?"
- After a quiet season that caught the owner out.

## What you need

- **Access I have:** levels and limits per tool. Until the owner answers, level 1.
- Bookings or sales by month and by service for the last one to two years (or the owner's best memory, labelled as such).
- Capacity: how many more jobs or visits a week the team could take without new staff.
- Gross profit per job or visit for each main service.
- Past offers and how they went.
- Which past customers the business may contact, and by what channel.
- Local dates that matter: school holidays, events, weather patterns, harvests.

## Steps

1. **Map demand.** For each main service, mark each month busy, normal or quiet from the booking history. Add capacity: in busy months, the limit is staff, not demand.
2. **Give each season a job.** For example:
   - *Before the peak:* sell maintenance, check-ups, early bookings and memberships.
   - *Peak:* protect capacity, answer fast, ask every customer for a review.
   - *After the peak:* rebook, follow up quotes, ask for referrals.
   - *Quiet:* fill gaps with offers that keep margin, run community events, do the profile and website work.
3. **Design offers with a margin check.** For each offer: what is included, the price, the cost to the business, gross profit after the offer, a minimum job size if needed, an end date that is real, and who it is for (new or past customers). Prefer added value (a free add-on, a priority slot, a loyalty step) to deep discounts. Drop any offer that loses money on the job unless the owner chooses it knowingly as a way to win repeat customers.
4. **Choose the channels per month.** Business Profile posts (offers carry dates and terms; no phone numbers in the post text), emails or texts to past customers who may be contacted, a print piece with a tracked code for one area, a community event, website page updates. Keep each month to what the owner can actually do.
5. **Plan the review pushes** for the busiest weeks, using the review-request-and-reply-system skill.
6. **Plan the content.** Before-and-after photos, seasonal tips, team or animal features, with a photo consent note where people or customers' pets appear.
7. **Add tracking.** Each offer gets its own code, tagged link or tracked number, and a line in the lead source tracker.
8. **Write the calendar** and the first month's drafts.
9. **Review monthly:** what booked, cost per booked job, margin after the offer, and whether next month's plan still fits.

## Checks

- Every offer has a price, inclusions, an end date, a margin check and, where needed, a minimum job size.
- Deadlines are real and honoured; no fake countdowns or "last chance" that comes back.
- No licence, warranty, health or "best in town" claims the owner has not supplied.
- Messages go only to people the business may contact.
- Peak months protect capacity rather than adding demand the team cannot serve.
- Each offer has its own tracking.

## Output format

1. **Demand map:** service by month, busy, normal or quiet, with capacity notes.
2. **Season jobs:** one line each.
3. **Offer table:** offer, who it is for, price, cost, gross profit after offer, minimum, end date, tracking code.
4. **Month-by-month calendar:** channels, posts, messages, print, events, review pushes.
5. **First month's drafts.**
6. **Monthly review questions.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Northgate Heating is a made-up heating engineer firm, used only to show the format. Numbers are invented.*

**Demand map:** boiler repairs busy November to February; servicing quiet April to July; installations steady, with a dip in December.

**Season jobs:** August to September: sell boiler services before the cold. November to February: protect capacity for breakdowns, ask every customer for a review. March: follow up installation quotes. April to July: fill servicing gaps.

| Offer | For | Price | Cost | Gross profit after | Minimum | Ends | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spring service, free carbon monoxide alarm fitted | Past customers | 95 | 58 incl. 18 alarm | 37 (normally 55) | One boiler | 30 June | SPRING25 |
| Book your autumn service in August, priority slot | Everyone | 95 | 40 | 55 | None | 31 August | AUTUMN |

**August plan:** two profile posts (the autumn slot offer with dates and terms; a photo of a serviced boiler with a short safety tip), one email and one text to past service customers who agreed to messages, 500 postcards to one postcode with code AUTUMN. Review push: none, it is quiet; the profile description is updated instead.

**Monthly review questions:** how many bookings used the code; gross profit after the offer; did the August bookings ease November's queue?
