# International shipping and customs prep

## When to use

- A supplier or overseas buyer asks which Incoterms rule to use.
- The client imports stock and wants to know the true landed cost.
- The client starts selling parcels to customers in another country.
- A shipment is held at customs for missing or wrong paperwork.
- Low-value parcel rules have changed and checkout prices need rechecking.

Do not use it to classify goods with authority, calculate a binding duty amount, make a customs declaration in the client's name, or advise on sanctions or export controls. Those belong to the importer, a licensed customs broker, the customs authority or a trade lawyer.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** supplier invoices and contracts, product specs, past customs entries and broker invoices, the shipping platform's international shipments.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft commercial invoice data, product fact sheets for the broker, a landed-cost sheet, draft customs data in the shipping platform without buying the label.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** buy international labels within the client's cap and services once the customs data is confirmed. **Commodity codes, values and declarations need the client's or broker's fresh yes for each shipment.** Paying duty and broker bills stays with the client.

## What you need

1. What the goods are: materials, how they are made, what they do, brand and model, photos or spec sheets.
2. Country of origin (where made, not where shipped from), and supplier details.
3. Value and currency, the sale or purchase contract, and the Incoterms rule if one is already agreed.
4. Destination countries and who the importer of record is.
5. The client's customs broker or agent, if any, and any past rulings or entries for the same goods.
6. For parcels to consumers: typical order values and the platform used.

## Steps

### 1. Agree the Incoterms rule
Use ICC Incoterms 2020 and name the rule and the place, for example "FCA [seller's warehouse address], Incoterms 2020". Seven rules work for any mode (EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP); four are for sea and inland waterway only (FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF). Explain in plain words who books and pays freight, who insures, who clears export and import customs and where risk passes. Remind the client that Incoterms do not set price, payment terms or when ownership passes, and that DDP makes the seller responsible for import duty and clearance in the buyer's country. The contract wording is the client's to approve.

### 2. Gather the product facts for classification
Write a one-page fact sheet per product: what it is, materials and their shares if known, function, how it is used, and photos. This is what a broker or customs authority needs. Note any code the supplier gave and where it came from.

### 3. Point to the official tools, not a ruling
- **US:** the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, past CBP rulings in CROSS, and a binding ruling request under 19 CFR Part 177 for repeated or unclear goods.
- **UK:** the online Trade Tariff and HMRC's commodity code guidance; Advance Tariff Rulings for Great Britain (HMRC replies in 30 to 120 days).
- **EU:** TARIC for duties and measures, and the EBTI database of binding tariff information decisions.
Suggest candidate headings to discuss with the broker, clearly marked "not confirmed". Only the first 6 digits are shared internationally, so a supplier's code must be checked for the importing country.

### 4. Check the current low-value rules (as of 6 Nov 2026)
- **US:** CBP says de minimis duty-free treatment is suspended for goods of 800 dollars or less by any mode, including mail; these need formal or informal entry, and all duties apply. Mail entry dates have moved; check CBP's e-commerce page before each season.
- **UK:** for consignments of 135 pounds or less from outside the UK, UK VAT is charged at the point of sale; the limit is per consignment.
- **EU:** the 150 euro duty exemption ended on 1 July 2026; a temporary 3 euro duty per item applies until 1 July 2028, and a 2 euro Union handling fee on small parcels applies from 1 November 2026.
Re-check these at every refresh; they are changing.

### 5. Build the landed-cost sheet
Product cost, freight, insurance, duty (rate from the broker or official tariff, marked "estimate until confirmed"), import VAT or sales tax treatment, handling and broker fees, and any per-item or per-parcel charges. Show cost per unit and the effect on margin. The client's accountant checks tax treatment.

### 6. Prepare the commercial invoice data
Seller and buyer, importer of record, description in plain words (not "gift" or "samples" unless true), quantity, unit value, currency, country of origin, Incoterms rule and place, commodity code once confirmed, weights and package count. Check that the description matches the fact sheet.

### 7. Hand to the broker or confirm
Send the fact sheet and invoice data to the client's broker for confirmation, or to the client. Record who confirmed each code and value, and when.

### 8. Ship
With confirmed data and the client's yes for the shipment, at level 3, create the international label in the shipping platform within the cap. Log it.

## Checks

- The Incoterms rule names the place and the 2020 edition, and fits the transport mode.
- Every commodity code is marked "confirmed by [broker or client] on [date]" or "not confirmed".
- No duty figure is presented as final before the broker or authority confirms it.
- Country of origin is where the goods were made.
- Low-value rules were checked against the official pages this cycle.
- No label bought or declaration sent without confirmed data and the client's yes.

## Output format

1. Incoterms note (half a page): the rule, the place, who does and pays what, where risk passes.
2. Product fact sheet per product.
3. Landed-cost sheet with every estimate labelled.
4. Commercial invoice data table.
5. Questions for the broker.
6. Current low-value rules for the destinations, with the date checked.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a UK seller importing wool rugs from India and selling to EU customers. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Incoterms (imports):** "FCA Jaipur, supplier's warehouse, Incoterms 2020". The supplier loads and clears export; the seller's forwarder carries, insures and clears UK import. Risk passes when the goods are handed to the seller's carrier in Jaipur. Payment terms stay in the purchase contract.
> **Fact sheet, rug R-12:** hand-knotted, 100% wool pile, cotton backing, 160 x 230 cm, floor covering. Code on the supplier's invoice: copied from the Indian export paperwork, **not confirmed for UK use**; broker to confirm the UK code.
> **EU parcels (from 1 Jul 2026):** rugs under 150 euros no longer enter duty-free. Until 1 Jul 2028 a temporary 3 euro customs duty per item applies to these low-value consignments, and from 1 Nov 2026 the 2 euro Union handling fee on small parcels. Rugs over 150 euros pay normal duty as before. **Questions for the broker:** does the handling fee apply per parcel or per item on our route, and who collects it?
