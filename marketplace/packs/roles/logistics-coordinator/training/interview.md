# Training interview: logistics and fulfilment coordinator

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share customer names or addresses, carrier account numbers, negotiated rates, staff details or confidential figures from real employers or clients; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of businesses have you run shipping or fulfilment for (online shop, wholesale, local delivery, importer), at what volumes, and what was your role?**
   Why it matters: sets the scale and type of operation the agent is built for.

2. **When you look at a new client's shipping for the first time, what do you ask for first, and what do you look at in the first hour?**
   Why it matters: the expert's first-look routine becomes the agent's intake and first checks.

3. **Tell me about a carrier bill that hid a problem. How did you find it?**
   Why it matters: real invoice detective work teaches the agent which lines to distrust.

4. **How do you choose box sizes? Tell me about a packaging change that saved money or cut breakage.**
   Why it matters: box choice drives billed weight and damage, and is learned by doing.

5. **How do you compare carriers or services without breaking the delivery promise?**
   Why it matters: captures the trade-off between price and promise that a cost table alone misses.

6. **How do you compare 3PL quotes? Which fees catch people out?**
   Why it matters: hidden fees are where 3PL decisions go wrong.

7. **Walk me through your pick, pack and dispatch steps. Where do mistakes usually happen, and what check catches them?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a proven SOP shape and the checks that actually work.

8. **How do you decide which orders to hold, and what makes an order "at risk" in your daily watch?**
   Why it matters: exception rules are judgement calls the agent needs as defaults.

9. **Tell me about a lost or damaged parcel claim you won, and one you lost. What made the difference?**
   Why it matters: evidence habits and deadline discipline decide claim outcomes.

10. **How do you find out a carrier's real claim rules and deadlines, and how do you keep them current?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent where to check instead of guessing.

11. **What do you tell a customer whose parcel is lost, and when? Show me a message you would send again.**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a calm, honest voice for delivery problems.

12. **If you have planned delivery routes, how do you group drops and decide the order? What do drivers complain about?**
    Why it matters: route planning lives in local knowledge that tools do not hold.

13. **How do drivers' hours and breaks shape your routes? Tell me about a time a plan broke the rules or nearly did.**
    Why it matters: legal limits are a hard constraint the agent must respect.

14. **How do you plan for peak season? When do you start, and what do you get wrong most years?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a realistic peak timeline and common failure points.

15. **If you have shipped abroad, how do you agree the Incoterms rule with a supplier or buyer, and what has gone wrong?**
    Why it matters: real Incoterms mistakes teach the agent what to check in contracts.

16. **How do you work with customs brokers? What information do they always need, and where do you stop and let them decide?**
    Why it matters: sets the line between gathering facts and making customs decisions.

17. **How do you run returns: inspection, grading, restocking, refunds? What fraud have you seen, and how did you handle it without accusing anyone?**
    Why it matters: returns rules protect stock and margin without hurting honest customers.

18. **Which actions would you let an assistant take on its own (labels, holds, messages), and which must always come to you first?**
    Why it matters: sets sensible level 3 limits and the fresh-yes list.

19. **Which numbers do you report every week or month, and which do you ignore?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent its core measures and the vanity numbers to skip.

20. **What would you check before trusting a new coordinator's cost review or route plan?**
    Why it matters: the expert's review list becomes the agent's quality bar.
