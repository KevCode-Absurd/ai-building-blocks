# Compare freight against chronological stock risk

Use when choosing inbound transport or estimating contribution at risk before a shipment arrives. Read the actual stock, reservations, open POs, demand basis, goods-ready date, transit and customs assumptions first.

1. Reconcile opening available units to on-hand less existing commitments. Keep the SKU and unit consistent. Identify each separate PO once. State whether unavailable demand is lost, delayed or backordered; these require different models.
2. Calculate elapsed warehouse arrival for each mode from the same starting instant. Include production only if the goods are not already ready. When readiness is unclear, retain both cases with separate recommendations. Never combine a ready-now benefit with a production-delayed timeline.
3. For lost sales, run `tools/commerce-math.mjs` operation `stockoutRisk` for each arrival horizon. Supply `sku`, `availableUnits`, `dailyDemand`, `horizonDays`, `stockoutPolicy: "lost_sales"`, `constantDemand: true`, `contributionPerUnit`, `currency`, and `receipts`. Each receipt has a unique `receiptId`, matching `sku`, elapsed `day`, integer `units` and `status` of `confirmed`, `assumed` or `excluded`. Mark uncertain POs assumed in the conditional case and exclude them in a downside case; do not silently certify expected delivery.
4. The interval is `[0, horizonDays)`: a receipt at elapsed day 2 can serve demand from day 2 onward. A receipt exactly at the horizon cannot recover earlier demand. The compared shipment arrives at that horizon; do not also count it as an earlier separate PO. Publish the convention with the timeline. If demand varies by day or backlog is retained, use a separately reviewed chronological adapter and disclose that this constant-demand lost-sales operation does not model it.
5. Check conservation independently: opening available plus included receipts equals served units plus ending stock; demand equals served plus lost units. Cumulative lost units never decline. A surplus receipt after a stock-out can leave both lost sales and unused stock. Subtracting every eventual receipt from cumulative demand loses this timing information.
6. Compare contribution preserved by the faster mode with its incremental all-in cost, using the same SKU contribution basis and currency. Show the break-even contribution per preserved unit, or state that no positive preserved units exist. Include all quoted charges, identify quote exclusions and treat incomplete contribution as a proxy. Stock-out losses, customer delay costs and working capital are different quantities.
7. Save the source adapter, inputs, results and period-by-period timeline with the readable comparison. Every readiness and PO scenario in the recommendation must appear in those files. Recompute at least one interval by hand and ensure the first recommendation agrees with the scenario table. These are conditional forecasts, not observed lost revenue or permission to book freight.

Counterexample: with zero opening stock, demand of five units per day, and 100 units arriving at elapsed day 2, a three-day lost-sales horizon has ten lost units and 95 ending units. The later receipt cannot undo the first two days' losses. A backlog policy would produce different results and must be explicitly modelled.
