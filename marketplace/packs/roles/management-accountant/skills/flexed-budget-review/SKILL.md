# Flexed budget review

## When to use

- Monthly or quarterly budget against actuals in the management pack.
- A budget holder is told they are "over budget" in a month when activity was far above plan, or "under budget" because activity collapsed.
- A maker, kitchen or workshop has standard costs (a recipe, a bill of materials, standard hours) and wants to know where cost leaked.
- A charity reports programme spending against each funder's budget.

Do not use it to set targets for pay or bonuses, or to decide how a variance is treated in the statutory accounts. This is general management accounting practice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before variances change stock values, grant claims or anything filed.

## What you need

1. The budget by month and by line, with the activity it assumed (units made, orders, billable hours, meals, participants).
2. Actuals for the same lines from the profit and loss or general ledger, by cost centre, class, tracking category, job or programme.
3. The actual activity for the period from operations (production records, orders, timesheets, attendance).
4. Which budget lines are variable with activity, fixed, or stepped, and the cost driver for each variable line. If unknown, I propose a split for the owner to confirm.
5. For standard-cost businesses: the standard quantity and price per unit for materials, standard hours and rate for labour.
6. Who holds each budget, and the variance threshold that matters to the owner (an amount, a percentage or both).

Access: level 1 (read) is enough. Exports from the accounting software and one operations report are a good start.

## Steps

### 1. Map and tie

Map budget lines to ledger accounts and cost centres. Tie total actuals to the profit and loss for the period. Any difference is listed before analysis starts.

### 2. Flex the budget

Flexed budget for each variable line = budgeted cost per unit of activity x actual activity. Fixed lines stay at the original budget. Stepped lines move only if the step was crossed.

### 3. Split the total gap in two

- **Activity variance** = flexed budget - original budget. This is the effect of doing more or less than planned. It belongs to whoever owns sales or volume, not to the cost budget holder.
- **Spending variance** = actual - flexed budget. This is what the budget holder controls.

Label each F (favourable) or U (unfavourable). For cost lines, actual below the budget it is compared with is F.

### 4. Break down the spending variance

- Materials: price variance = (actual price - standard price) x actual quantity bought or used; usage variance = (actual quantity - standard quantity for actual output) x standard price.
- Labour: rate variance = (actual rate - standard rate) x actual hours; efficiency variance = (actual hours - standard hours for actual output) x standard rate.
- Variable overhead: spending against the flexed amount, and efficiency if it is driven by hours.
- Fixed overhead: spending variance = actual - budget.
The parts must add to the spending variance. If they do not, the inputs are wrong.

### 5. Filter and assign

Keep the lines over the owner's threshold. Assign each to its budget holder, job or programme. Note timing (an invoice in the wrong month, a cost the accountant will accrue) separately from real over- or underspend.

### 6. Find causes, carefully

For each kept line, look in the transactions and operations data: a supplier price rise, a substitute material, overtime, a new hire before the work arrived, waste, a one-off repair. If the data does not show the cause, write the question to the budget holder. Never invent one.

### 7. Charities and funded programmes

Keep restricted and unrestricted budgets apart. Show spend to date against each grant's budget and allowed use, and flag lines spent on costs the grant does not allow. Allocate shared costs with the same documented rule every month.

### 8. Actions and roll-forward

Three to five actions, each with an owner and the money it should move. If the gap is permanent (a price rise that will stay), say so and pass it to the forecast owner. At level 2, I can draft budget revisions or comments in the client's planning sheet or tool for approval; I never overwrite the approved budget. Any level 3 change, such as loading a revised budget, needs the client's yes on the action list first and is logged.

## Checks

- Actuals tie to the profit and loss; mapping shown.
- Every variable line has a driver and a cost per unit; fixed and stepped lines labelled.
- Activity and spending variances add to the total gap for every line.
- Price and usage, or rate and efficiency, add to the spending variance.
- Signs and F/U labels are used the same way on every line.
- Timing items are shown apart from real variances.
- Causes are proven from data or written as questions.
- The report carries the line: *General management accounting practice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before variances change stock values, grant claims or anything filed.*

## Output format

1. Summary: total gap, how much is activity and how much is spending, and the three spending lines that matter.
2. Table per cost centre: original budget, flexed budget, actual, activity variance, spending variance, F/U, owner, cause or question.
3. Standard cost breakdown (materials price and usage, labour rate and efficiency) where standards exist.
4. Programme or grant table for charities.
5. Actions with owners and money effect.
6. Questions for budget holders and for the accountant.

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real business: a wholesale bakery. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> **October, production cost centre.** Budget assumed 40,000 loaves; actual 46,000.
>
> | Line | Budget | Flexed | Actual | Activity var. | Spending var. |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Flour | 12,000 | 13,800 | 14,840 | 1,800 U | 1,040 U |
> | Bakers' wages | 16,000 | 18,400 | 19,200 | 2,400 U | 800 U |
> | Rent | 4,500 | 4,500 | 4,500 | 0 | 0 |
>
> The headline "flour 2,840 over budget" is mostly volume: 1,800 of it comes from baking 6,000 more loaves. Of the 1,040 spending gap: price 840 U (new contract from 1 October, +0.03 per kg on 28,000 kg) and usage 200 U (28,000 kg used against a standard of 0.60 kg x 46,000 = 27,600 kg; 400 kg over at the 0.50 standard price). Question for the production lead: was the extra flour waste from the new mixer?
>
