# Management accounts pack

## When to use

- Every month, once bank, card and payout reconciliations are done.
- A lender, investor or partner asks for monthly or quarterly management accounts.
- The owner gets a profit and loss from the software but cannot tell why profit moved.
- A board or trustees need a short finance report before each meeting.

Do not use it to produce statutory, filed or audited accounts, to certify figures, or to decide accounting treatment. This is general management accounting practice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before the pack goes to a lender or funder, or before any figure is used in your accounts.

## What you need

1. Trial balance at month end and at last month end, from the accounting software.
2. Profit and loss by month for the year to date, by class, tracking category or cost centre if used.
3. Balance sheet at month end; aged receivables and payables.
4. The reconciliation status of every bank, card, loan and payout account (from the bookkeeper or the software).
5. Budget for the year, and last year's actuals for the same month.
6. Payroll summary, stock count or stock report if stock is held, and any job or WIP schedule.
7. Who reads the pack (owner, board, trustees, lender), what they decide with it, and the variance threshold that matters.
8. Any covenant or reporting terms in a loan agreement, if the pack goes to a lender.

Access: level 1 (read) covers everything here. Through the official Xero or QuickBooks Online MCP servers, use read-only report scopes or switch write tools off.

## Steps

### 1. Close checklist

Confirm each item is done or open: bank, card and loan reconciliations; payment platform payouts; sales invoices out; supplier bills in; payroll posted; stock count or adjustment; depreciation; accruals and prepayments. Open items go on page one as limits on the pack.

### 2. Trial balance checks

Debits equal credits. Opening balances equal last month's closing balances. Suspense and uncategorised accounts are empty or listed. Balance sheet control accounts (receivables, payables, payroll liabilities, sales tax) agree with their sub-ledgers or reports.

### 3. Map accounts to report lines

One mapping table: account code, account name, report line, segment. Keep it fixed month to month and show changes. New accounts are mapped before the pack is built.

### 4. Accruals and prepayments to check

List costs that belong to the month but have no bill yet (utilities, subcontractors, a known invoice), and annual costs paid upfront (insurance, software, licences) that should be spread. For each: amount, basis, and the document that would prove it. At level 2 I can prepare these as draft journals for review; posting them needs level 3 within the limits the client set, or the owner or accountant posts them. Each action is logged.

### 5. Build the statements

- Profit and loss: month, year to date, budget, last year, by segment where tracked. Gross profit and contribution clearly labelled.
- Balance sheet summary: cash, receivables, stock, payables, loans, equity.
- Cash movement: opening cash, operating cash, capital spend, loans and owner movements, closing cash. Closing cash ties to the reconciled bank balances.

### 6. Explain the movements

For each line over the threshold against budget or last year, give the likely cause from transactions. Flex the budget for volume first (see the `flexed-budget-review` skill). If the cause is not in the data, ask; never fill it in.

### 7. KPIs and ratios

Choose those that suit the reader: gross margin, contribution margin, overhead as a share of sales, debtor days, creditor days, stock days, current ratio, cash runway in months of fixed costs. For a professional services firm, utilisation and effective rate; for a builder, WIP over and under billing; for a charity, reserves in months of spending and restricted cash. Write each definition once in the pack and use it every month. If the business reports under UK FRS 102 and leases moved onto the balance sheet, show EBITDA and gearing on the old and new basis.

### 8. Lender or board additions

For a lender: covenant calculations as defined in the loan agreement (quote the definition from the agreement and ask the accountant to confirm the reading), a short cash outlook, and answers to likely questions. For a board or trustees: decisions needed, risks and the questions they should ask.

### 9. Controls spot check

Each month, pick one: new payees added, bank detail changes, payments just under an approval limit, or journals posted by the person who also approves them. Report what was checked and what was found, as patterns to look at, never as accusations.

### 10. Write page one and close the loop

Profit, cash, the three things that moved and why, two risks, three actions for next month. Update the close checklist with what slowed the month down.

## Checks

- Trial balance balances; opening equals last close; suspense empty or listed.
- Pack profit ties to the ledger; closing cash ties to reconciled bank balances.
- Mapping table attached; changes since last month shown.
- Every line over threshold has a cause or a question.
- Every KPI has a written definition, used the same way as last month.
- Open close items and estimates are listed on page one.
- Nothing is labelled audited, certified or final accounts.
- The pack carries the line: *Management accounts, not filed or audited accounts. General management accounting practice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before this pack goes to a lender or funder.*

## Output format

1. Page one: headline numbers, three movements, two risks, three actions, open items.
2. Profit and loss: month, year to date, budget, last year, variances.
3. Balance sheet summary and cash movement.
4. KPI and ratio sheet with definitions.
5. Notes on movements over threshold.
6. Accruals and prepayments to check.
7. Controls spot check result.
8. Questions for the accountant; what I could not check.

## Example of good output (short)

Made-up business for illustration: Larkspur Studio, a 14-person design agency.

> **September in one page.** Profit 18,400 against a budget of 22,000 and 15,100 last September. Cash 96,300, down 11,800 because two large clients paid late (debtor days 52, up from 38).
>
> **What moved.** (1) Freelancer costs 9,200 over budget: the Harbourside rebrand ran 140 hours over its fixed fee. (2) Revenue 6,000 above budget from the Q4 retainer starting early. (3) Software 1,900 over: annual design-licence renewal posted in one month, should be spread (see prepayments).
>
> **Risks.** Late payers make up 41% of receivables. Fixed-fee overruns two months running.
>
> **Actions.** Chase the two late clients this week. Add a change-order step to fixed-fee projects. Spread the licence over 12 months (draft journal ready for your accountant).
>
> **Open items.** Card statement for September not yet reconciled.
>
> *Management accounts, not filed or audited accounts. General management accounting practice: check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant before this pack goes to a lender or funder.*
