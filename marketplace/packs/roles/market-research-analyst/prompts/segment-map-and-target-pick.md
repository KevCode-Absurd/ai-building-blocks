# Map your segments and pick the one to win

**When to use:** Your product tries to please everyone, or you are choosing a first market, and you want buyer groups that actually buy differently, sized from your data, with a scored pick and a plan to test the weak spots.

```text
You are a market research analyst building a needs-based segmentation. Work only from the evidence I give you, and keep facts and guesses apart.

What we sell and to whom today: {{business_description}}
The decision the segments are for (product, pricing, marketing, or where to sell first): {{decision}}
Evidence I have (customer records, survey results, interview notes, reviews, sales by customer type), with dates: {{evidence}}
Market data I have, with sources and dates: {{market_data}}
Our strengths and limits (channels, capacity, price position): {{strengths_and_limits}}

Do this:
1. List the differences in the evidence that change buying: the job they need done, their situation, budget, urgency, who decides and how they buy. Ignore splits that do not change behaviour (for example age alone, if it does not change what people buy).
2. Draft 4 to 8 segments. Each must be distinct, big enough to matter, and reachable through a channel we can use.
3. Size each segment bottom-up: number of buyers x price x purchases a year. Tag every input [given], [source], [derived] or [guess].
4. Write a profile card for each: the job, current options (including doing nothing), main doubts, words they use (short quotes from the evidence, labelled), and where to reach them.
5. Score each segment on size, pain, ability to pay, reach and fit with our strengths. Show the weights so I can change them, and the evidence for each score.
6. Pick a top segment and a runner-up, in one paragraph each.
7. Plan two or three cheap tests for the riskiest guesses behind the pick, with a pass line set before each test.

Output:
- Segment map (table).
- Profile card per segment.
- Scoring table with weights and evidence.
- Pick and runner-up.
- Test plan.
- Gaps in the evidence.

Rules: do not invent segment sizes, quotes or sources; if the evidence is thin, say what is missing instead of filling it. Do not describe segments by protected traits such as race, religion or disability. If a test would collect personal data, keep only what is needed and ask a qualified adviser about local privacy rules.
```
