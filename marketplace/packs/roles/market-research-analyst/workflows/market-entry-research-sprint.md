# Market entry research sprint

**Trigger:** A decision with money behind it: a new product, a new segment, a new country, a new site, or a price change. Typical length is two to four weeks, set by how fast interviews and a test can run. The sprint ends with a go, change or stop recommendation and the next test.

**Access:** Ask at the start which level the client grants for each tool (survey tool, website or landing page builder, ad accounts, CRM or email list, shared drive) and write it down as "Access I have". Until they answer, work at level 1 (read). Log every action: what, where, when, result.

## Steps

1. **Frame the decision (day 1).** One sentence: the decision, buyer, region, price, time frame, and what result would make the client say go, change or stop. *Approval: the client confirms the decision sentence and the budget for testing.*
2. **Ethics and privacy check (day 1).** Run the `research-ethics-check` prompt on the plan before anyone is contacted. *Approval: the client approves consent wording and the data kept.*
3. **Desk sizing (days 1 to 4).** Run the `market-sizing` skill with official data and the client's own numbers; tag every input; name the three assumptions that move the answer. *Level 1.*
4. **Competitor field (days 2 to 5).** Run the `competitor-research` skill on 3 to 6 rivals, including substitutes and doing nothing. *Level 1; public material only.*
5. **Interviews (days 3 to 10).** Use the `customer-interview-guide` prompt; recruit people with recent experience of the problem; stop when new themes stop. *Level 2: draft recruiting emails in the client's email or CRM tool for review. Level 3 within agreed limits (which list, how many, which dates): send the invitations after the client confirms the action list. Approval: the client approves every message before the first send.*
6. **Segments (days 8 to 11).** Use the `segment-map-and-target-pick` prompt to turn interview and desk evidence into segments and a pick. *Level 1.*
7. **Survey, if numbers are needed (days 10 to 18).** Run the `survey-design-and-analysis` skill: design, pretest, field, clean, analyse. *Level 2: build the survey as an unpublished draft. Level 3 within agreed limits: publish and send it. Approval: the client approves the questionnaire and the send list.*
8. **Validation test (days 12 to 25).** Run the `idea-validation-test` skill on the riskiest assumption left: landing page, pre-order, manual version or price test, with the pass line written first. *Level 2: build the page, form or ads as drafts. Level 3 within a budget cap and dates: publish and run them; stop and ask if spend or results look wrong. Approval: the client approves the offer wording, price, any money taken, refund terms and the budget cap.*
9. **Recommendation (final days).** Answer first: go, change or stop; the size range; the segment to start with; the position against rivals; the main risk; and the next test with its cost. *Approval: the client decides.*
10. **Hand-off.** Sources list with dates, workings, raw data stored as agreed, personal data deleted or anonymised on the agreed date, and the action log.

## Outputs

- Decision sentence and ethics check
- Market size (TAM, SAM, SOM) with tagged inputs and sensitivity
- Competitor grid, review themes and gaps
- Interview notes summary and themes
- Segment map, profiles and pick
- Survey findings with method box (if run)
- Validation test plan and result against the pass line
- Recommendation with the next test
- Action log of everything done in the client's tools

## Needs the client's approval

The decision sentence and test budget; consent and privacy wording; every recruiting message, survey and send list; any page, ad or offer before it goes live; any money taken and its refund terms; and anything outside the agreed level 3 limits. Legal questions on privacy, pre-orders, advertising claims or exports go to a qualified adviser.
