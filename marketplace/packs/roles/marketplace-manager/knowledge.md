# Online marketplace manager: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. This kit covers selling on marketplaces other than Amazon: eBay, Walmart Marketplace, Etsy, European marketplaces such as Zalando and bol, and food delivery apps. For Amazon, see the Amazon marketplace manager kit. For feeds, catalogue structure and EU price-reduction rules, see the ecommerce manager kit. For listing copy, see the ecommerce copywriter kit. For shipping and customs, see the logistics coordinator kit. Rules on tax, product safety and seller identity are summarised as general information, not legal or tax advice.

## 1. What this role is for

A brand hires a marketplace manager when its own store isn't enough: it wants eBay or Walmart buyers, a new country, a European fashion or general marketplace, or more delivery-app orders. The work falls into four parts:

- **Listings and product data.** Map the brand's product data to each marketplace's fields, fill bulk upload sheets, write titles and attributes to each marketplace's rules, and fix listings that are missing details.
- **Buyers, claims and account health.** Message templates by issue, claim and case responses with evidence, feedback replies within each marketplace's rules, and a watch on the account health numbers that keep the account open.
- **Prices and profit per channel.** What the seller keeps on each item after each marketplace's fees, with prices that still compete.
- **New marketplaces and countries.** Which marketplace or market to test first, what it costs to land and sell a unit there, which rules to check, and a 90-day test plan.

Who hires it: brands adding eBay or Walmart to a Shopify store; resellers and refurbishers; European brands selling on bol or invited to Zalando; restaurants on Uber Eats, DoorDash and Deliveroo; sellers on several marketplaces with rising defect rates.

What good work looks like: every required field filled from real data, with gaps listed rather than guessed; titles and attributes that fit each marketplace's rules; a "what you keep" sheet per channel; buyer replies within each marketplace's deadlines; claims answered with evidence; account health metrics inside the targets; identity, product safety and tax reporting steps ready before they block the account.

How it is judged: listings live and searchable, sales and margin by marketplace, account health metrics against each marketplace's standards, claims won or resolved, time to answer buyers, and no surprises from fees, suspensions or rules.

## 2. The canon

These are the ideas practitioners rely on, summarised in my own words.

**Marketplaces are two-sided platforms.** Reading list: Jean-Charles Rochet and Jean Tirole on platform competition in two-sided markets (2003), and Geoffrey Parker, Marshall Van Alstyne and Sangeet Paul Choudary, *Platform Revolution* (2016). The practical lesson: a marketplace serves buyers first, sets the fees and rules, and can change them. Sellers rent the shelf.

**The marketplace's rules are the spec.** Each marketplace publishes what it measures: eBay's seller performance standards [2], Walmart's Seller Performance Standards and Listing Quality score [8][9], bol's service norms [16]. Work starts from those pages, not from habits carried over from another marketplace.

**Product identity.** Marketplaces match offers to products by identifiers. Walmart asks for GTINs or UPCs from GS1 company prefixes [7]. Never invent or reuse an identifier; the ecommerce manager kit covers GTIN rules in feeds.

**What you keep.** For each item and channel: price received − marketplace fees (on the base the marketplace uses) − product cost − fulfilment − returns allowance − ads. Fees are often charged on totals that include shipping or tax [1][6][13], so a margin worked on item price alone is too high.

**Trust rules.** Governments now make marketplaces know their sellers: the US INFORM Consumers Act [18][19] and the EU Digital Services Act [20]. Tax authorities receive seller income reports [22][23][24]. A seller who isn't ready gets stopped at the marketplace's door.

## 3. How the work is done now

### 3.1 Where to sell (as of Nov 2026)

- **eBay:** new, used and for-parts items across many categories, including collectibles such as coins and trading cards, in fixed-price and auction listings [1][3][5]. US fees in 3.2.
- **Walmart Marketplace (US):** asks for a business tax ID (a Social Security Number is not accepted), documents for business name and address, a selling track record, GS1-prefix GTINs, compliance with its Prohibited Products Policy, fulfilment through Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS) or a US warehouse that can handle returns, and a compatible payment processor [7].
- **Etsy:** Etsy's help and policy pages blocked automated checks at this refresh, so this kit gives no Etsy fees or selling rules. With web search connected, check Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy live; without it, ask the client for a recent Etsy payment account statement and work from that.
- **Zalando:** wholesale (Zalando buys stock), Partner Program (the brand sets its own prices and keeps control of assortment and logistics) and Connected Retail (stores ship online orders; Zalando deducts commission). Zalando invites brands and does not take direct applications; it lists 25 markets [17].
- **bol (Netherlands and Belgium):** free to list; commission on each sale [13].
- **Other EU marketplaces** (Allegro, Cdiscount, OTTO, Kaufland and others): check each one's seller pages live, or work from the contract the client has.
- **Food delivery apps:** Uber Eats, DoorDash and Deliveroo; see 3.9.
- **Amazon:** see the Amazon marketplace manager kit.

### 3.2 Fees (as of Nov 2026)

| Marketplace | How it charges | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| eBay US [1] | First 250 listings a month free to list, then $0.35 each; final value fee for most categories 13.6% up to $7,500 plus 2.35% above, plus $0.30 per order of $10 or less or $0.40 above | Charged on the total including shipping and sales tax; many category exceptions; Below Standard adds 6% to final value fees (7% after four months); $20 per dispute found against the seller; 1.65% international fee on some sales |
| Walmart Marketplace [6] | Referral fee by category, some tiered by price (for example Everything Else 15%, Consumer Electronics 8%, Apparel 5%, 10% or 15% by price) | No setup or monthly fees; charged on the total sales price including shipping and handling |
| bol [13] | Fixed amount per item plus a percentage by item type (kitchen items: 5% to 12.5% plus €0.05 to €0.95) | Percentage charged on the price including VAT; listing is free; temporary lower commission when the price is at or below a set price point |
| Zalando Connected Retail [17] | Commission deducted from payouts | Rate not published on the partner page; get it from the contract |
| Uber Eats US [28] | Lite 20%, Plus 25%, Premium 30% marketplace fee | Pickup fee 7%, or 10% without validated in-store pricing; self-delivery 15% |

eBay's fee page shows no effective date [1]; take a dated screenshot or invoice for each fee review.

### 3.3 Listings and product data

- **Map before writing.** A field map per marketplace: the client's data column, the marketplace's field, required or optional, filled or missing. Missing required fields hide listings or block uploads.
- **eBay:** up to 24 pictures at no cost in almost any category; no HTML or JavaScript in titles [5]. Item specifics (brand, model, size, colour and category-specific details) drive search filters; fill every one the data supports. eBay's title length limit shows in the listing form; I couldn't confirm it from a page I could open at this refresh, so check the counter there.
- **Walmart:** the Listing Quality dashboard scores content, discoverability, offer, ratings and reviews, plus post-purchase quality. Walmart's own list of improvements: the correct category and attributes, a clear title with relevant keywords, detailed descriptions, high-resolution photos, faster shipping, competitive prices and good reviews [8].
- **EU product safety (GPSR).** Under the EU's General Product Safety Regulation, marketplaces now ask for manufacturer and EU responsible person details on listings. bol, for example, has blocked new listings without the manufacturer's name, postal address and electronic address since 5 March 2026, has required an EU responsible person since 1 April 2026, and is phasing out existing listings without them [14]. Sellers record their responsible economic operators and link them to products by EAN, in bulk or through bol's Retailer API [15]. Other marketplaces have their own dates; for Amazon's, see the Amazon kit.
- **Condition.** New, used and refurbished are different conditions with different rules; listing refurbished goods as new risks the account.

### 3.4 Prices across channels

- **Walmart compares your price with the web.** Its price competitiveness score compares the offer, without Walmart-funded incentives, with comparable prices on other sites including shipping; an offer is competitive at the same or a lower price. "Price not competitive" makes an offer ineligible for the Buy Box, and unpublished items show reasons in the pricing tools [10]. Walmart's free Repricer works within minimum and maximum prices the seller sets per item [11].
- **bol** gives a temporary lower commission when the price is at or below a set price point [13].
- **UK totals.** The CMA's open letter of 2 November 2026 says the total price including mandatory delivery must be shown at every stage, and that this applies to marketplace operators; where traders on a marketplace charge their own delivery, the delivery price should be included in each product's total price [27].
- **EU "was" prices** follow the 30-day prior price rule; see the ecommerce manager kit.
- **Price changes across the catalogue** are the riskiest routine action: one wrong rule in a repricer or bulk sheet can price hundreds of items below cost. Every bulk change has floors, a preview and a log.

### 3.5 Buyers, claims and feedback

- **eBay Money Back Guarantee (US, as of Nov 2026):** buyers can report an item not received, or request a return for an item not as described, up to 30 days after the (estimated) delivery date; sellers must respond within 3 business days and refund within 2 business days of receiving a return; eBay may step in from 3 business days after a report; signature confirmation is needed as delivery proof for orders of $750 or more; decisions can be appealed within 30 days [3].
- **eBay feedback:** fake, misleading or manipulated feedback is banned; feedback must reflect the buyer's real experience; removal requests go in within 90 days of the transaction; negative or neutral feedback doesn't affect seller performance [4].
- **US reviews rule:** in effect since 21 October 2024, the FTC rule bans fake reviews, incentives that require a particular sentiment, and suppressing reviews by threats or intimidation; asking all buyers for a review is allowed [26]. Never offer a refund in exchange for changing feedback.
- **Walmart:** answer customers within 48 hours on at least 95% of inquiries [9].
- **EU buyers** have 14 days to cancel online purchases from a trader; purchases from private individuals on marketplaces are not covered [29].

### 3.6 Account health and appeals

- **eBay (US):** evaluated on the 20th of each month; Below Standard if the transaction defect rate exceeds 2% (with defects from more than 4 buyers) or cases closed without seller resolution exceed 0.3% or 2; Top Rated needs a defect rate of 0.5% or less, few late shipments and tracking on at least 95% of transactions, among other conditions. Below Standard brings lower search placement, possible selling limits and fund holds, and higher fees from the next month. From 15 October 2026, eBay Live transactions are excluded from these calculations. Case decisions can be appealed within 30 days [2].
- **Walmart:** measured over 30 or 60 days; targets include cancellation 2% or below, on-time delivery 90% or above, valid tracking 99% or above, seller response 95% or above, negative feedback 2% or below, return rate 6% or below (9% for resold goods), item not received 2% or below and late shipment 5% or below. Failing to act can lead to suppression, suspension or termination [9].
- **bol:** nine service norms (on-time items, cancellations, track and trace, returns, customer questions, phone availability, rating, response time, NPS after contact) affect sales and the buy box; thresholds sit in the seller account [16].
- **Appeals and suspension replies** commit the client: they state facts about the business and promise changes. I draft them with evidence and a root-cause plan; the client says yes before anything is sent, every time.

### 3.7 Seller identity, product safety and tax reporting (general information)

- **US INFORM Consumers Act (FTC, in effect 27 June 2023):** a high-volume third-party seller has 200 or more sales of new or unused consumer products and $5,000 or more in gross revenue on a marketplace in any 12 months of the past 24. Marketplaces must collect a bank account, tax ID and contact details, verify them within 10 days, and ask sellers to certify them at least once a year. Sellers with $20,000 or more a year must have their name, address and contact details disclosed to buyers; home-based sellers may show only country and state with a contact method. If a seller doesn't respond within 10 days of notice, the marketplace must suspend future sales [18][19]. The FTC can fine marketplaces $53,088 per violation (January 2025 figure) [18].
- **EU Digital Services Act:** in force for all regulated services since 17 February 2024 [21]. Marketplaces must have sellers give verified identity information before they sell, so buyers can see who is responsible; when an illegal product has been sold, they must notify buyers, identify the seller and offer redress [20]. Each marketplace's onboarding lists what it asks for.
- **Tax reporting by platforms.** EU: DAC7 (in force 1 January 2023) makes platforms report seller identity and the money paid to each seller, including for sales of goods [22]. UK: platforms report sellers to HMRC, except sellers of goods who receive €2,000 (about £1,700) or less for fewer than 30 sales a year [23]. US: payment apps and marketplaces file Form 1099-K when payments total over $20,000 in more than 200 transactions (IRS page reviewed 28 October 2026) [24].
- **EU VAT on marketplace sales.** Since 1 July 2021 the One Stop Shop handles cross-border EU sales; a EUR 10,000 threshold applies to intra-EU distance sales by a supplier based in one Member State; marketplaces are treated as the supplier for imports in consignments up to EUR 150, and for sales of goods inside the EU by sellers based outside it [25]. VAT set-up is for the client's accountant.

### 3.8 Selling across borders

- **Landed cost first:** product cost, freight, duties and import taxes (to verify with a customs broker), marketplace fees in that market, local delivery, returns and currency conversion.
- **Rules to verify locally:** product safety and labelling, the EU responsible person, language requirements, VAT registration, and the marketplace's own category approvals.
- **Customs thresholds for low-value parcels:** I couldn't confirm the current US or EU figures from an official page at this refresh, so check live with web search or the client's customs broker before costing.
- **Test one market** with a small range and a stop rule before adding more.

### 3.9 Delivery apps

- **Uber Eats (US, as of Nov 2026):** Lite, Plus and Premium plans at 20%, 25% and 30% marketplace fees; pickup orders 7%, or 10% without validated in-store pricing; self-delivery 15% [28]. Plans differ in visibility, delivery fee to customers and Uber One participation.
- **DoorDash and Deliveroo:** their merchant pricing pages blocked automated checks at this refresh. Use the client's contract or partner portal rates.
- **The menu is the listing:** categories in ordering order, best sellers first, names that say what the dish is, modifiers for sizes and add-ons, and a price sheet showing what the restaurant keeps per item after commission and packaging. Allergen and diet claims come only from the restaurant.

### 3.10 Tools, access, AI and research

Agents in this role work in the client's tools at the level granted per tool (read; read and draft; read, write and publish within limits), as listed in access.md. Listings, prices and buyer replies are routine level 3 work within the client's limits. A few actions always need a fresh yes: price changes across the catalogue beyond the client's limit, replies to account suspensions or appeals, claim and dispute evidence, refunds above the client's limit, opening a new marketplace or country, and deleting listings or data. Bank and payout details, and identity documents for INFORM or DSA checks, stay with the client.

**Two research modes.** With web search connected, I check each marketplace's live fee and policy pages before quoting a fee or deadline, and note the date. Without web search, I work from this kit's dated facts and what the client supplies (fee invoices, performance pages, policy emails), and flag anything older than the kit's review cycle.

## 5. Decision rules

- If a required field has no value in the client's data, mark it missing and ask; never invent an identifier, size, material or safety detail.
- If a fee is charged on a total that includes shipping or tax, work margin on that same base [1][6][13].
- If an EU listing lacks manufacturer or EU responsible person details, fix that before any other listing work, because marketplaces now block such listings [14].
- If a price change would touch more items or go further than the client's limit, prepare it at level 2 and get a fresh yes, because a bulk pricing error spreads instantly.
- If Walmart flags "Price not competitive", check the external price including shipping before cutting, and keep the item's floor [10].
- If an account metric nears its standard, fix the cause (carrier, handling time, stock accuracy) before writing any appeal [2][9].
- If a suspension, appeal or formal complaint reply is needed, draft it with evidence and a root-cause plan, and send it only after the client's yes.
- If a buyer offers to change feedback for a refund, decline and solve the problem on its merits [4][26].
- If the seller is near the INFORM high-volume threshold, prepare the bank, tax ID and contact details before the marketplace asks, so the 10-day clock doesn't stop sales [19].
- If a new country is planned, cost one unit landed, with returns, before listing anything there.
- If a fee or rule can't be confirmed from the marketplace's own page or the client's documents, say so and don't quote it.
- If no access level has been agreed for a tool, work at level 1.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Copying one listing everywhere.** Spot it: the same title and attributes on eBay, Walmart and bol, with required fields empty on two of them.
- **Guessed specifics.** Spot it: sizes or materials not in the supplier data.
- **Margin on item price only.** Spot it: fees worked on price without shipping or VAT [1][6][13].
- **Repricer without floors.** Spot it: items selling below cost after a competitor's error [11].
- **Wrong condition.** Spot it: refurbished stock listed as new.
- **Late claim responses.** Spot it: eBay cases where eBay stepped in after 3 business days with no seller reply [3].
- **Feedback for refunds.** Spot it: templates that mention changing a review [4][26].
- **Appeals that argue instead of fix.** Spot it: no root cause, no evidence, no prevention plan.
- **Missing GPSR details.** Spot it: EU listings without manufacturer or responsible person details [14].
- **Identity checks ignored.** Spot it: INFORM or DSA verification emails unanswered [19][20].
- **Delivery missing from UK totals.** Spot it: marketplace listings showing a price without mandatory delivery [27].

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Every required field filled from the client's data; gaps listed by product.
2. Titles and attributes fit each marketplace's rules, checked in its own form or template.
3. Margin per item per channel on the marketplace's own fee base, with returns.
4. Prices inside the client's floors and limits; bulk changes previewed and logged.
5. Buyer replies inside each marketplace's deadlines and rules, with no review-for-reward language.
6. Claims answered with evidence; appeals and suspension replies approved by the client before sending.
7. Account health metrics checked against the published standards, with causes named.
8. EU listings carry GPSR details; identity and tax reporting steps ready.
9. Every live change logged, with a way to undo it.

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Field map:* client column, marketplace field, required, allowed values, filled or missing.
- *Bulk sheet:* the marketplace's own template, with a checks tab (lengths, allowed values, duplicates, blanks).
- *Listing health report:* listing, issues, fix, priority.
- *What you keep sheet:* item, channel, price, fee base, fees, product cost, fulfilment, returns allowance, keep, keep %.
- *Buyer templates:* by marketplace and issue, with order facts as fill-ins and one next step.
- *Claim draft:* deadline at the top, facts in order, evidence list, refund-or-defend note.
- *Account health report:* metric, standard, current, trend, cause, fix.
- *Market entry plan:* comparison table, landed cost per market, rules to verify, 90-day test.

**Tone.** Friendly, firm and exact. Tables over prose. Character counts next to titles. "To verify" next to anything not confirmed. Deadlines at the top.

## 9. Glossary

- **Item specifics:** eBay's structured product details that feed search filters.
- **Final value fee:** eBay's fee on a sale, charged on the total including shipping [1].
- **Referral fee:** Walmart's commission on each sale, by category [6].
- **Listing Quality score:** Walmart's score for content, offer and reviews [8].
- **Buy Box:** the main offer shown when several sellers sell one product [10].
- **Price competitiveness:** Walmart's comparison of an offer with prices elsewhere [10].
- **Repricer:** a tool that changes prices automatically within set limits [11].
- **WFS:** Walmart Fulfillment Services [7].
- **Transaction defect rate:** eBay's share of transactions with defects [2].
- **INR and SNAD:** item not received; significantly not as described.
- **Money Back Guarantee:** eBay's buyer protection for non-receipt and items not as described [3].
- **Account health:** the set of performance metrics a marketplace uses to judge a seller.
- **High-volume third-party seller:** a seller covered by the US INFORM Consumers Act [18].
- **Trader traceability:** the EU duty on marketplaces to verify who their sellers are [20].
- **GPSR:** the EU General Product Safety Regulation [14].
- **EU responsible person:** the economic operator in the EU responsible for a product's compliance [14][15].
- **DAC7:** the EU rule making platforms report seller income [22].
- **OSS and deemed supplier:** the EU VAT One Stop Shop, and the rule that treats a marketplace as the seller for VAT in some cases [25].
- **Landed cost:** the full cost to get one unit to a buyer in a new market.
- **What you keep:** price minus fees and variable costs, per item and channel.
- **Modifier:** a delivery-app menu option such as size or add-on.
