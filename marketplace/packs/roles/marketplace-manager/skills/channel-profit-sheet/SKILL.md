# Channel profit sheet

The same product can make good money on one marketplace and lose money on another, because each one charges different fees on a different base. This sheet shows, per item and channel, what the seller keeps, and turns that into prices the client approves.

## When to use

- "Is it worth selling on Walmart or bol?"
- "Our eBay margin looks fine on paper, but payouts are thin."
- Walmart shows "Price not competitive" on key items.
- A restaurant wants app prices that protect margin after commission.
- Before any price change across the catalogue.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 for steps 1 to 6. Level 2 to save a price sheet or repricer strategy as a draft. Level 3 to apply approved prices within the client's floors and limits. Any change beyond the client's limit (more items or a bigger move than agreed), or below a floor, needs a fresh yes every time.

- Products with cost price, weight and size, and current price on each channel.
- The latest fee invoice or payout report from each marketplace or app, and the client's plan or category on each. With web search connected, also check the marketplace's live fee page and note its date; without it, use the invoice.
- Fulfilment cost per order (pick, pack, packaging, postage) by channel; shipping charged to buyers.
- Return rate by product and channel, and what a return costs.
- Ad spend per channel, if any.
- The client's floor margin and price limits.

## Steps

1. **Set the fee base per channel.** eBay's final value fee is charged on the total including shipping and sales tax, plus a per-order fee. Walmart's referral fee is on the total sales price including shipping. bol's percentage is on the price including VAT, plus a fixed amount per item. Uber Eats charges a marketplace fee by plan (Lite 20%, Plus 25%, Premium 30% in the US as of Nov 2026) and a different fee on pickup orders. Write the base next to each fee.
2. **Work out what you keep per item and channel:**
   - Price received (excluding sales tax or VAT where the seller passes it on).
   - − marketplace fees on the correct base
   - − product cost
   - − fulfilment and packaging, + shipping charged
   - − returns allowance (return rate × cost of a return)
   - − ads per unit (spend ÷ units), if any.
   Show the formula once and the result per item.
3. **Flag losers:** items below the floor margin on any channel. Note whether price, fees, fulfilment or returns cause it.
4. **Check competitiveness** where the marketplace measures it. On Walmart, compare with the comparable external price including shipping; an offer is competitive at the same or a lower price.
5. **Suggest prices:** the lowest price that keeps the floor margin, and a target price. For delivery apps, show in-store price, app price, commission, packaging and what you keep for each item.
6. **Plan the change.** Group price moves; mark which are inside the client's limits and which need a fresh yes; set floors and maximums for any repricer; check UK listings show totals including mandatory delivery and EU reductions follow the 30-day prior price rule (see the ecommerce manager kit).
7. **Apply (level 2 or 3).** Save as a draft sheet or repricer strategy; at level 3, apply only the approved moves within limits, preview first, and log each change with the old and new price. Check the next day for errors and sales.

## Checks

- Each fee uses its marketplace's own base and the client's real plan or category.
- Fees come from the invoice or a dated fee page, never from memory.
- No suggested price below the floor or below cost unless the client chose it.
- Bulk changes previewed, inside limits, logged and reversible.
- Estimates (returns allowance, ad cost per unit) labelled.

## Output format

1. **Assumptions box:** fee sources and dates, plans, floors, limits.
2. **What-you-keep table:** item, channel, price, fee base, fees, cost, fulfilment, returns, ads, keep, keep %.
3. **Losers list** with the cause.
4. **Price proposal:** item, channel, current, floor, suggested, inside limit (yes/no).
5. **Change log** (level 3).

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a tools seller. Fees follow the US rules above as of Nov 2026. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

**Item: 20-piece screwdriver set. Cost $11.00, fulfilment $6.50, buyer pays $5.99 shipping on eBay, free shipping on Walmart.**

| Channel | Price | Fee base | Fees | Keep | Keep % of buyer's payment (excl. tax) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eBay | $29.99 + $5.99 ship + $2.25 tax | $38.23 | $5.20 + $0.40 = $5.60 | $29.99 + $5.99 − $5.60 − $11.00 − $6.50 = $12.88 | 36% |
| Walmart (a 15% category) | $34.99, free ship | $34.99 | $5.25 | $34.99 − $5.25 − $11.00 − $6.50 = $12.24 | 35% |

eBay fee: 13.6% × $38.23 = $5.20, plus $0.40 per order. Walmart: 15% × $34.99 = $5.25. Returns allowance not included (return data not yet supplied).

**Proposal:** keep both prices; set the Walmart repricer floor at $29.20, which keeps the client's 25% floor margin ($29.20 − $4.38 fee − $17.50 costs = $7.32), and the maximum at $36.99. Inside the client's limit.
