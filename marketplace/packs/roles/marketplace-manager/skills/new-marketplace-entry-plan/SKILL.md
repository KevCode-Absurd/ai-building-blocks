# New marketplace entry plan

Most marketplace launches that fail do so on cost or paperwork, not demand. This plan puts landed cost and the rules on one page before anything is listed, then tests one market small.

## When to use

- "Should we add Walmart, bol or eBay?"
- "We want to sell in Germany. Where do we start?"
- A brand invited to a marketplace (such as Zalando's Partner Program) wants to know if it pays.
- A restaurant choosing a delivery app plan.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 for every planning step. Level 2 to prepare listings and an application checklist. Opening a seller account in a new marketplace or country is the client's decision every time; the client enters identity, tax and bank details in the marketplace's own sign-in. Level 3 to publish the test range after approval.

- Products with costs, weights, sizes, current prices and identifiers.
- Sales history by product and country, if any.
- The markets and marketplaces under consideration, and why.
- Shipping quotes (or the 3PL's rates) and current return rates.
- Who the manufacturer and EU responsible person are, for EU sales.
- With web search connected: I check each marketplace's live seller pages, fees and requirements, and note the dates. Without it: I work from this kit's dated facts and the documents and quotes the client supplies, and mark every fee and rule "to verify".

## Steps

1. **List what's known and what must be verified.** Two columns, kept to the end.
2. **Check eligibility.** For example, Walmart Marketplace asks for a business tax ID (not a Social Security Number), documents for business name and address, a selling track record, GS1-prefix GTINs, US fulfilment that handles returns and a compatible payment processor. Zalando invites brands rather than taking applications. bol lists for free and charges commission per sale.
3. **Compare marketplaces or markets** on: fit with the range, fee level and base, fulfilment options, return rules and habits, competition seen on the marketplace, language and support needs.
4. **Work out landed cost per unit** for each option: product cost + freight + duties and import taxes (to verify with a customs broker) + marketplace fees on their own base + local delivery + returns allowance + currency conversion. Then margin at the local price.
5. **List the rules to verify,** with who checks each:
   - *US marketplaces:* INFORM Consumers Act seller details once the seller passes 200 sales and $5,000 in any 12 months of the past 24 on that marketplace; disclosure to buyers at $20,000 or more a year; Form 1099-K reporting above $20,000 and 200 transactions.
   - *EU marketplaces:* verified trader details under the Digital Services Act; GPSR manufacturer and EU responsible person on listings; DAC7 reporting of seller income; VAT through the One Stop Shop where it applies (accountant).
   - *UK marketplaces:* HMRC platform reporting of sellers; prices that show totals with mandatory delivery.
   - *Everywhere:* product safety, labelling and language rules; category approvals; restricted products. These go to a local adviser; I don't rule on them.
6. **Adapt the listings** for the market: language, units, sizes, claims that differ by region, local search terms, and the marketplace's own fields (use the marketplace-listing-mapper skill).
7. **Set a 90-day test:** one market or marketplace, a small range of proven products, a stock limit, the numbers to watch (sales, keep per order, return rate, account health), and a stop rule agreed with the client.
8. **Prepare (level 2) and launch (level 3 after the client's yes).** Application checklist, listings as drafts, then publish the test range; log everything.

## Checks

- Every fee and rule is dated and sourced, or marked "to verify".
- Landed cost includes returns and duties; duties are never assumed to be zero.
- No customs, tax or legal ruling; those go to a local adviser or accountant.
- One test market recommended, with a stop rule.
- No identity, tax or bank details handled in chat.

## Output format

1. **Known and to-verify list.**
2. **Comparison table** of marketplaces or markets.
3. **Landed cost and margin table** per option.
4. **Rules checklist,** with an owner for each item.
5. **Listing changes** for the chosen market.
6. **90-day test plan** with the stop rule.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a UK skincare brand. Fees and duties are marked to verify. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

| Option | Price excl. VAT | Landed cost | Fees | Returns | Keep per unit | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bol (NL) | €20.00 | €9.80 | to verify on bol's tariff for the category (charged on the price including VAT) | €0.70 | to verify | Test first |
| Germany via own site | €20.00 | €10.40 | payment fees €0.80 | €1.10 | €7.70 | Later |

**To verify:** bol's commission for skincare (download bol's tariff table); duties and import VAT from the UK (customs broker); EU responsible person (the brand has none yet, so no EU listings until one is named); cosmetics rules in the EU (local adviser).

**Test plan:** bol, 8 best sellers, 200 units each, 90 days. Stop if keep per unit falls below €6.00 after returns, or return rate passes the brand's UK rate.
