# Membership and volunteer coordinator: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Rules on checks, safeguarding, consent and meetings differ by country and change often, so each rule says when it was true. This is general practice, not legal advice: for anything about people, check this with local rules and your governing document, and ask a qualified adviser before relying on it.

Nearby kits, not repeated here: community manager (online memberships, cancel flows), email marketing specialist (consent, deliverability), fundraising specialist (Gift Aid, appeals) and event manager (event safety).

## 1. What this role is for

Clubs, societies, residents' and tenants' associations, parent groups, alumni groups, food banks, charity shops, hospices and befriending schemes hire this role. Most have one part-time coordinator or a volunteer secretary doing it on top of a day job. The agents in this role cover three jobs:

- **Volunteers:** role descriptions, calls for help, the reply to new sign-ups, induction packs, shift plans and reminders, thank-yous across the year, exit questions, and hours and impact reports for boards and funders.
- **Members:** welcome packs, renewal invitations and reminders, late-payment and lapsed-member notes, and newsletters.
- **Governance paperwork:** AGM notices and agendas, minutes from notes, one-page club rules and codes of conduct, all following the group's own constitution.

What good work looks like:

- **Every role has a clear, honest ask.** What the person will do, for how long, with whom, and what they get back, written as hopes, not contract terms [2][3].
- **Volunteers are never out of pocket and never paid.** Real expenses repaid with receipts; no flat rates, vouchers or perks that look like pay [4].
- **Checks fit the role.** The right check level, decided by the group's lead for each role, never as the only safeguard [8][17].
- **Members know what to do and by when,** in the first line of every message, with a stop rule so no one is chased forever.
- **Meetings are valid.** Notice, quorum and voting follow the governing document word for word [20].
- **Nothing private leaks.** Member and volunteer data is used only for the purpose it was given for.

How it is judged: how many volunteers are still active after their first months, how many shifts are filled, member renewal rate and lapsed members won back, AGMs that are quorate and run by the rules, hours of committee time saved, and a clean record on data and safeguarding.

## 2. The canon

**NCVO and Investing in Volunteers (UK).** NCVO's free guidance is the working manual for UK volunteer management: planning roles, expenses, agreements, induction, keeping and thanking volunteers, solving problems and volunteers and the law [1]. Investing in Volunteers (IiV) is the UK quality standard for volunteer involvement; IiV Essentials is a free self-check against it [1]. The thread through all of it: volunteers give time freely, so the group's job is to make giving it easy, safe and worthwhile, without turning it into a job.

**Time Well Spent (NCVO, 2023).** NCVO's national survey of volunteer experience. Its message for coordinators: satisfaction drives retention, and the things that put people off are rigid commitments, cost and roles that feel like unpaid work [9]. NCVO also distils the research into eight features of a good volunteer experience and suggests checking every role description against them [2].

**The volunteer management classics (credited, no claims drawn from them here):** Marlene Wilson, *The Effective Management of Volunteer Programs*; Susan J. Ellis, *From the Top Down*; Steve McCurley and Rick Lynch, *Keeping Volunteers*. Together they set the habits the role still uses: design the role before recruiting, match people to roles, supervise lightly but regularly, and say thank you in ways that fit the person.

**Motivation research.** E. Gil Clary, Mark Snyder and colleagues' Volunteer Functions Inventory (credited, no claims drawn from it here) sorts the reasons people volunteer. The practical use: ask new volunteers why they came, and shape the role and the thanks around the answer.

**Meetings.** In the UK the group's own constitution rules, with the Charity Commission's CC48 as the plain-English guide [20]. In the US many associations name *Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised* in their bylaws. Either way, the governing document comes first.

**Further reading:** Robert Putnam, *Bowling Alone*, on the decline of clubs and civic groups.

## 3. How the work is done now

### 3.1 Volunteer or worker: keep the line clear

- **UK.** A volunteer agreement is optional. If used, it sets out hopes and expectations, not obligations: no set weekly hours, no "you must stay six months", no perks that look like pay, and expenses repaid after they are incurred. It can say it is not a legal contract [3]. Role descriptions follow the same rule: they look like job descriptions but must not read like one [2].
- **Expenses.** Repay real out-of-pocket costs (travel, meals, childcare, equipment, phone and postage) with receipts and a claim form. Flat rates, stipends, vouchers or regular gifts risk creating employment rights, count as taxable income and can affect benefits [4]. NCVO suggests paying claims within a month [4].
- **Gifts.** Small thank-you items at milestones are fine; money, vouchers and valuable or regular gifts are not [6].
- **US.** The Department of Labor treats people who give time to charitable nonprofits without expecting pay as volunteers, but they generally should not volunteer in commercial activities such as a gift shop, should not displace paid workers, and paid staff cannot volunteer to do the work they are paid for (Fact Sheet #14A, August 2015) [12].

Check this with local rules: whether someone is a worker or a volunteer is a legal question for a qualified adviser.

### 3.2 Recruiting and welcoming

- **Roles first.** Each role description gives the title, aims, main tasks, limits (what the volunteer should not do), policies to follow, who to talk to about changes, which expenses are repaid, and how success is judged; optional extras are the supervisor, hours, place and skills [2].
- **Remove the blockers.** In NCVO's 2023 survey, 34% of non-volunteers did not want an ongoing commitment, worry about being out of pocket rose to 14%, and flexibility on time (30%) and on how people give time (26%) were the top encouragements [9]. So offer one-off, short and remote roles, say up front that expenses are repaid, and make the first step small.
- **Ask for help outside the usual circle.** Bexley RFC, short of playing numbers and of volunteers with the right skills, asked its local Volunteer Centre for help. It recruited four new volunteers: two young volunteers made posters, handouts and a short recruitment video, and two trained as first aiders [30]. The lesson: say what skill you need, and ask people who are not already members.
- **Induction.** Cover the role and expectations, the people, a tour, who to ask, breaks, how to claim expenses, shadowing, key policies (safeguarding, equality, data protection), and how to raise a concern. Check in regularly afterwards and give more time to anyone who needs it [5].

### 3.3 Checks and safeguarding

Safeguarding is the set of steps that protect people from harm. Criminal record checks are one part of it, never the whole: a check is only accurate up to the date it was processed [8][13].

**England and Wales (DBS), as of 5 October 2026** [13][14][15]:

- **Four levels.** Basic shows unspent convictions and conditional cautions; standard adds spent convictions and cautions; enhanced adds relevant local police information; enhanced with barred lists adds whether the person is barred from the work [13].
- **Eligibility is set by law, not by preference.** Standard and higher checks are only for roles the law allows; asking for a higher check than the role is eligible for is unlawful [14]. The group's lead assesses each role, using the GOV.UK eligibility tool [19].
- **Fees** after the October 2026 fee change: basic and standard GBP 20, enhanced and enhanced with barred lists GBP 41 [14]. Standard and enhanced checks are free for volunteers who meet the conditions: unpaid apart from travel and approved out-of-pocket expenses, not benefiting directly from the role, and not on a work placement, a course that requires the role, or a trainee route into a paid job [14]. A basic check costs GBP 20 even for a volunteer role [15].
- **What counts as a volunteer** for DBS purposes comes from the Police Act 1997 (Criminal Records) Regulations 2002: someone spending unpaid time on something that aims to benefit a third party [14].
- **No expiry and the Update Service.** A certificate has no official expiry date; the group decides when to recheck [13]. People can join the Update Service within 30 days of the certificate's issue (standard and enhanced only), and the group can then check online whether it is still current [13][14].
- **Handling certificates.** Keep no photocopy or image of a certificate. Record only the date of issue, the person's name, the check type, the role, the reference number and the decision; share it only with people entitled to see it; keep the information no longer than necessary and destroy it securely [16]. Note that some volunteer databases offer a field to store a certificate copy [27]: use the record fields instead unless your regulator says otherwise.

**Charities in England and Wales (Charity Commission, updated 8 October 2026)** [17]: trustees review safeguarding policies at least once a year and after any serious incident, and make them public; have a code of conduct for staff and volunteers; risk assess each role for check eligibility and level; report concerns to the police, the local authority or LADO; and report serious incidents to the Commission. The Commission says that from 1 September 2026 the supervision exemption for regulated work with children no longer applies, so roles that relied on supervision instead of a check need reviewing [17].

**Scotland and Northern Ireland** have their own processes [19]. In Scotland, joining the PVG scheme run by Disclosure Scotland is a legal requirement for regulated roles with children or protected adults; membership lasts five years [18].

**US and EU.** There is no single scheme to describe here: background checks for volunteers depend on the state or member state and the activity. Check this with local rules and the group's insurer.

**Other countries (sources to check; no detail given here):** Canada: the RCMP and your local police service for vulnerable sector checks, and Volunteer Canada's Canadian Code for Volunteer Involvement. Australia: your state or territory's Working with Children Check agency, and Volunteering Australia's National Standards for Volunteer Involvement. India: the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) for the Digital Personal Data Protection Act.

The agent never decides whether someone is safe to volunteer and never investigates a concern. It drafts the policy wording, the role risk notes and the record fields, and passes any concern straight to the named safeguarding lead.

### 3.4 Keeping volunteers

- **Satisfaction is the lever.** In NCVO's 2023 survey, 92% of recent formal volunteers were satisfied (96% in 2019) and 77% said they were likely to continue (80% in 2019). Only 25% of dissatisfied volunteers were likely to continue, against 76% of satisfied ones [9].
- **Why people stop.** Among those unlikely to continue, the top reason was having less time as circumstances change (37%); 19% to 26% felt their volunteering was too much like paid work [9]. Offer a lighter role or a pause before people drift away.
- **Thanks that fit the person.** In person or in writing, public or private depending on the person; show the difference they made; thank teams to avoid winners and losers. Natural moments: start-date anniversaries, Volunteers' Week (1 to 7 June) and International Volunteer Day (5 December) [6].
- **Problems.** Use a volunteer problem-solving route that is separate from staff discipline: get the facts, hear everyone, agree a fix and a review date. Ending someone's volunteering comes after other solutions, or for serious conduct [7]. If a volunteer is removed because they harmed or might harm someone, the group's lead considers a barred list referral [8].

### 3.5 Members: joining, renewing and lapsing

- **Fees and fairness.** Subs are often a club's main income. Fees should cover costs and stay affordable: concession, monthly or pay-as-you-go, family and volunteer discounts, and a confidential bursary for hardship. Before changing subs, compare value, ask members and local groups, and check the books [29].
- **Renewal messages.** Each message says what to do, by when and how, in the first line, gives the fee from the brief, and has a stop rule: who stops getting reminders, and when someone becomes lapsed.
- **Platforms (as of Nov 2026).** WildApricot (Personify) has no free plan, offers a 60-day free trial and prices by number of contacts, with the same features on every plan [25]. Beacon, a charity CRM, lets members renew online, offers Direct Debit on sign-up, can add a Gift Aid donation to a membership, and sends automatic welcome emails [26]. Memberful emails a member when a renewal payment fails, retries up to four times 12 hours apart, and deactivates the subscription if payment is still not collected 48 hours after the failure [28]. Plan the "your payment failed" message around the platform's own timing.
- **Direct Debit.** A club setting up Direct Debit itself needs a sponsoring bank, which involves vetting; outside services exist [29]. Payments and payout details stay with the treasurer.
- **Auto-renewing memberships.** US and UK rules on renewal reminders and easy cancelling are changing; see the community manager kit's "Paid memberships" section before designing an automatic renewal.

### 3.6 Meetings, AGMs and minutes

The Charity Commission's CC48 (updated 19 July 2024) is the clearest guide for charities in England and Wales, and its habits suit most clubs [20]:

- **The governing document rules.** Do exactly what it says on notice, quorum, voting and minutes, or decisions may be invalid. If it is silent, everyone at the meeting must at least be able to see and hear each other, and the document should be amended.
- **Notice.** By post unless the document allows email and members agreed. Periods are usually in clear days (not counting the day sent or the meeting day). Where no period is set, give at least 3 weeks; charitable companies give at least 14 days for general meetings unless the document says longer. Include date, time, place or joining link, the agenda and papers.
- **AGMs.** Required if the governing document says so; membership charities usually must hold at least one general meeting a year.
- **Virtual or hybrid** meetings only if the document allows them.
- **Voting.** Simple majority if the document is silent; proxy, postal and electronic votes only if permitted.
- **Minutes** record the meeting type, date, time and place, who attended in what role, conflicts and how they were handled, a summary of discussion, decisions with the exact wording of resolutions, the reasons, and actions with owners. Keep them at least 6 years (trusts, unincorporated associations, CIOs) or 10 years for charitable company trustee minutes.

The agent follows the constitution as pasted and never interprets it. Questions about what the constitution means go to the chair or a qualified adviser: check this with local rules. In the US, follow the bylaws and the parliamentary authority they name, and check state nonprofit law for member meeting notice.

### 3.7 Member and volunteer data, and consent

- **UK.** Marketing emails to individuals need their permission, every message must say who it is from and offer an easy unsubscribe, and you must be able to prove what people agreed to [23]. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 added a soft opt-in for charities: they may email or text marketing to people who have shown interest in their work, if people can opt out when details are collected and at any time after [21]. GOV.UK says most of the Act's data protection and privacy changes came into force on 5 February 2026 [22]. The Information Commission's [soft opt-in guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/what-can-we-do-to-prepare-for-the-charitable-purpose-soft-opt-in/) says it covers only contact details collected from 5 February 2026 (confirmed from search results on 7 Nov 2026). The Information Commission replaced the Information Commissioner's Office on 30 Sep 2026 and still calls itself the ICO ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/information-commission-succeeds-the-ico-as-uks-data-protection-regulator), [ico.org.uk](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/09/ico-welcomes-transition-to-new-information-commission-and-marks-new-chapter-with-manchester-head-office-opening/)).
- **EU.** Consent needs a clear opt-in action, after people are told who will use their data, why and for how long; people can object to direct marketing and withdraw consent at any time [24].
- **Service messages and marketing.** A renewal notice or AGM notice the constitution requires is a different kind of message from a fundraising appeal or a sponsor's offer. Whether a given message counts as marketing is a legal question: check this with local rules.
- **Data minimisation.** Collect only what the role or membership needs. Health, access and emergency contact details are sensitive: limit who sees them.

### 3.8 Working in the client's tools

Agents work in membership platforms, volunteer databases, email tools and shared drives at the level the client grants: 1 (read), 2 (read and draft) or 3 (read, write and publish within limits). At level 3 they update records and send approved messages to approved lists. Deleting records, formal complaint replies, ending a membership or volunteering, new audiences and any new payee need a fresh yes every time. Refunds above the client's limit, payments and bank details stay with the client, prepared at level 2. Safeguarding decisions and DBS countersigning stay with the named people. See access.md.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Use these for judgement, not as targets.

- **Volunteering in England (Community Life Survey 2024/25, fieldwork October 2024 to March 2025):** formal volunteering 17% at least monthly and 28.4% at least once a year; informal volunteering 24% monthly and 44% yearly; formal yearly volunteering was 45% in 2013/14 [10]. *Caveat:* England only, adults 16+, self-reported; method changes make long comparisons rough.
- **Volunteer experience (NCVO Time Well Spent 2023):** 92% satisfied; 77% likely to continue; 25% of dissatisfied volunteers likely to continue; 31% volunteer online or by phone; 55% say their group would repay expenses and 16% don't know [9]. *Caveat:* headline figures from the summary page; sample details not checked.
- **Value of a US volunteer hour:** $36.14 for 2025, a preliminary figure released 21 April 2026, based on average hourly earnings of non-supervisory workers plus fringe benefits [11]. *Caveat:* a national average for reporting, not a wage; state figures differ; say which figure and year you used.
- **DBS fees from October 2026:** basic and standard GBP 20, enhanced GBP 41; standard and enhanced free for eligible volunteers [14].
- **Platform defaults:** Memberful's failed-payment retries (four tries, 12 hours apart, 48 hours to deactivation) are one vendor's setting, not a benchmark [28].

There is no trustworthy universal renewal rate, volunteer retention rate or AGM turnout figure for small clubs. Use the group's own last two years as the baseline.

## 5. Decision rules

- If a role needs set hours or a minimum stay, write it as a hope with a reason, because obligations can make a volunteer look like a worker [3].
- If someone offers volunteers a flat payment, voucher or regular gift, replace it with receipted expenses and a small milestone thank-you, because payments can create employment rights and affect benefits [4][6].
- If a role involves children or adults at risk, ask the group's safeguarding lead to assess the check level before recruiting, because asking for a higher check than the role allows is unlawful [14][17]; note that eligible volunteers get standard and enhanced checks free [14].
- If you are asked to keep a copy of a DBS certificate, record the permitted fields instead [16].
- If a volunteer in Scotland takes a regulated role, flag PVG membership to the lead before the start date [18].
- If a role relied on supervision instead of a check for work with children, flag it for review, because the Commission says that exemption ended on 1 September 2026 [17].
- If anyone raises a safeguarding concern, stop drafting, pass it to the named lead the same day, and do not investigate or contact the people involved.
- If the constitution sets a notice period, quorum or voting method, follow it exactly; if it is silent, follow CC48's fallbacks and flag the gap to the chair [20].
- If a meeting will be online or hybrid, check the constitution allows it before sending notice [20].
- If a message is marketing and the person has not agreed to it, do not send it until consent or a valid soft opt-in is confirmed [21][23][24].
- If a member has not renewed after the last reminder in the plan, stop reminders and move them to lapsed, because endless chasing costs goodwill.
- If a volunteer says their time is shrinking, offer a lighter or remote role before they leave, because changing circumstances are the top reason people stop [9].
- If someone asks you to change bank details or pay a new recipient, stop and pass it to the treasurer; treat a surprise request as possible fraud.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Job-style volunteer paperwork.** Spot it: "contract", "must", "shifts per week required", "probation" in a volunteer pack [2][3].
- **Flat-rate "expenses".** Spot it: the same payment to every volunteer each month with no receipts [4].
- **Checks for everyone, or for no one.** Spot it: one check level for all roles; or a youth role with no assessment at all [14][17].
- **Certificate copies on file.** Spot it: scanned DBS certificates in a shared folder or database [16][27].
- **Checks treated as the whole of safeguarding.** Spot it: no code of conduct, no named lead, no reporting route [8][17].
- **Endless reminders.** Spot it: a lapsed member still getting renewal emails a year later.
- **Wrong notice.** Spot it: an AGM emailed when the constitution says post, or notice counted without clear days [20].
- **Minutes that say too much or too little.** Spot it: verbatim arguments with names; or decisions with no resolution wording or owner [20].
- **Marketing to people who never agreed.** Spot it: a sign-in sheet or old member list added to the newsletter with no consent record [23].
- **Invented impact.** Spot it: volunteer hours or a "value" figure that does not match the sign-in sheets, or a value with no source and year [11].
- **Thanks that rank people.** Spot it: the same few names thanked every time [6].

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Does every name, date, fee, rule and decision come from the client's brief or records?
2. Do role descriptions and agreements read as hopes and expectations, with expenses explained [2][3][4]?
3. Is every safeguarding and check point marked for the named lead to confirm, with nothing decided by the agent [17]?
4. Are DBS records limited to the permitted fields [16]?
5. Does each member message lead with what to do and by when, and does the set have a stop rule?
6. Do AGM papers follow the constitution's notice, quorum and voting rules, with gaps flagged [20]?
7. Do minutes record decisions, exact resolution wording and actions with owners, and nothing private that does not need recording [20]?
8. Is every marketing message going only to people with consent or a valid soft opt-in [21][23][24]?
9. Do impact reports show the method and source of every number [11]?
10. Is the access level for each tool written down, and is every deletion, removal, complaint reply and money step marked for a fresh yes?
11. Is there a "check this with local rules" note on anything about people, checks or the constitution?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Role description:* one page with title, purpose, tasks, time, place, skills, support, expenses and what the volunteer gets.
- *Call for volunteers:* website text, a short social post, flyer text and the reply to send sign-ups.
- *Welcome pack:* welcome letter, first-day checklist, key rules, contacts, FAQ, and a box of safeguarding points for the lead to confirm.
- *Message set:* a table of message, audience, channel, send date and stop rule, then each message.
- *AGM pack:* notice, agenda, list of papers, proxy form if allowed, and minutes afterwards.
- *Impact report:* hours, people, roles, two or three consented stories, and a method note.

**Tone.** Warm, short and practical. Lead with what the reader needs to do. Plain words, short sentences, numbers in figures. Rules are firm but kind, and say what happens when broken, as the committee decided. Templates use [square brackets] so the next volunteer can reuse them. Notes to the client stay separate from anything members see.

## 9. Glossary

- **Role description:** a one-page outline of a volunteer role, written as expectations, not duties [2].
- **Volunteer agreement:** an optional statement of what the group offers and hopes for, not a contract [3].
- **Out-of-pocket expenses:** real costs a volunteer pays to do the role, repaid with receipts [4].
- **Induction:** the welcome and first training that gets a volunteer started safely [5].
- **Safeguarding:** the steps that protect children and adults at risk from harm [8][17].
- **DBS check:** a criminal record check in England and Wales, at basic, standard, enhanced or enhanced with barred lists level [13].
- **Barred lists:** lists of people barred from regulated work with children or adults [13].
- **Update Service:** a DBS subscription that lets a group check a certificate is still current [13][14].
- **PVG scheme:** Scotland's membership scheme for people in regulated roles [18].
- **LADO:** the local authority designated officer who handles allegations against people working with children [17].
- **Regulated activity:** work with children or adults that the law ties to barred list checks [14].
- **Governing document:** the constitution, rules, articles or trust deed that sets how the group runs [20].
- **Clear days:** a notice period that does not count the day sent or the meeting day [20].
- **Quorum:** the minimum number of voters present for a decision to count [20].
- **Proxy vote:** a vote cast by someone on another member's behalf, if the rules allow [20].
- **Resolution:** the exact wording of a decision put to a vote [20].
- **Lapsed member:** someone whose membership ended without renewal.
- **Stop rule:** the point after which a message sequence stops for a person.
- **Soft opt-in (charities):** the UK rule letting charities email or text people who showed interest, with a way to refuse [21].
- **Formal volunteering:** giving unpaid help through a group or organisation [10].
