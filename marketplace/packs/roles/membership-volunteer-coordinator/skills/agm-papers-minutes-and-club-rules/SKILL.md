# AGM papers, minutes and club rules

An AGM that breaks its own constitution can make its decisions invalid. This skill builds the pack from the constitution's own words, writes minutes that record what was decided and who will act, and puts the club's rules on one page people will read.

## When to use

- An AGM, special general meeting or committee meeting is coming up and notice must go out.
- The secretary has rough notes or a recording transcript and needs minutes.
- Elections for officers (chair, treasurer, secretary) are due.
- The club's rules or code of conduct are long, scattered or out of date.

Not for: deciding what the constitution means, ruling on disputes, changing the constitution's legal effect, or advising on charity or company law. Check this with local rules, and pass those questions to the chair or a qualified adviser.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the constitution, last year's papers and minutes, and the member list. Level 2 to draft the notice, agenda, forms and minutes in the group's shared drive or email tool. Level 3 to send the approved notice to the members entitled to it and file final papers, within the client's limits. Sending notice to anyone outside the member list, and any formal reply to a complaint or appeal, need a fresh yes.

- The full governing document (constitution, rules, articles or bylaws), pasted or shared, plus any standing orders.
- Meeting date, time, place or joining link, and who is entitled to notice and to vote.
- Business for the meeting: reports, accounts, elections, motions, rule changes.
- Nominations received, and the officers standing down.
- For minutes: the notes or transcript, the attendance list and any votes counted.
- For rules: the committee's decisions, including what happens when a rule is broken.

## Steps

1. **Pull the rules out of the constitution** into a short table, quoting clause numbers: who gets notice, how (post, email), how long and whether in clear days, quorum, who chairs, how votes are taken, whether proxies, postal or electronic votes are allowed, whether online or hybrid meetings are allowed, how motions and nominations are submitted and by when, and how rule changes pass. Where the document is silent, note CC48's fallback (for example, at least 3 weeks' notice where no period is set; simple majority where voting is not specified) and flag the gap for the chair. Never fill a gap with your own reading.
2. **Count the dates.** Work back from the meeting date to the last day to send notice, counting clear days if the constitution uses them (not the day of sending or the meeting day). Add posting time if notice goes by post.
3. **Write the notice:** meeting type, date, time, place or link with joining details, the agenda, the papers and where to find them, how to nominate or submit a motion, and proxy or postal voting instructions only if allowed.
4. **Write the agenda** in the constitution's order. A common pattern: welcome and apologies, quorum check, minutes of the last AGM, matters arising, reports (chair, treasurer with accounts, secretary), elections, motions and rule changes, any other business if the constitution allows it, date of next meeting.
5. **Prepare forms:** nomination form, proxy form if permitted, and a ballot paper if a vote is contested.
6. **Write minutes from the notes,** recording only what the notes show: group name, meeting type, date, time and place; who attended and in what role, and apologies; whether quorum was met; conflicts of interest and how they were handled; a short summary of each item; each decision with the exact wording of the resolution and the vote; actions with owner and date; date of the next meeting. Mark anything unclear as [check with secretary]. Leave out personal details that don't need recording.
7. **Draft one-page rules:** plain sections (behaviour, bookings, kit or facilities, safety, children and young members, data, how to raise a concern), and what happens when a rule is broken, exactly as the committee decided. Point to the safeguarding policy rather than restating it.
8. **Prepare the send list.** At level 3, confirm the member list and channel, show the send plan, get one yes, then send and log it.

## Checks

- Every notice rule, quorum and voting method traces to a clause or a flagged CC48 fallback.
- The notice deadline counts clear days correctly.
- Online or hybrid joining is offered only if the constitution allows it.
- Proxy and postal voting appear only if permitted.
- Minutes contain decisions, resolution wording, votes and actions with owners, and nothing invented.
- Rules say what happens when broken, as decided by the committee, not by the agent.
- Retention: minutes are filed where the group keeps records (CC48 suggests at least 6 years for many charities and 10 years for charitable company trustee minutes).
- Every paper carries "Template: check this with local rules and your constitution before use."

## Output format

1. **Rules table** from the constitution, with clause numbers and flagged gaps.
2. **Timeline:** last day for notice, nominations and motions, papers, meeting.
3. **Notice and agenda.**
4. **Forms:** nomination, proxy (if allowed), ballot.
5. **Minutes** (after the meeting).
6. **One-page rules** (if asked).

## Example of good output (short)

*Elm Park Residents' Association is a made-up group, used only to show the format. Clauses and dates are invented.*

| Rule | What the constitution says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Notice | 21 clear days, by email to members who agreed, post to others | 9.2 |
| Quorum | 15 members | 9.5 |
| Proxies | Not mentioned | Gap: flag to chair; do not offer proxies |
| Online joining | Not mentioned | Gap: CC48 says only if the document allows; hold in person |

**Timeline:** meeting Tuesday 12 May. Counting 21 clear days, notice must go by Monday 20 April. Posted notices go by Friday 17 April to allow delivery.

**Minutes extract:** "Item 5, Elections. Two nominations for chair were received. Ballot result: Ruth Okafor 31, David Lin 19. Resolved: that Ruth Okafor be elected chair for the coming year. Action: outgoing chair to hand over bank mandate forms to the treasurer by 30 May (owner: Ken Barr)."

**Note for the client:** the constitution doesn't mention proxies or online meetings. Ask your committee whether to propose a rule change at this AGM. Template: check this with local rules and your constitution before use.
