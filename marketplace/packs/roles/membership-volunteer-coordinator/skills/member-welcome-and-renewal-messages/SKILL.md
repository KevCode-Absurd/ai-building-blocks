# Member welcome and renewal messages

Renewals are won or lost on clarity: the member needs to know the fee, the deadline and how to pay, in the first line, and to hear it a sensible number of times. This skill builds the whole set, timed to the club's calendar and its membership platform, and ends with a clear point where reminders stop.

## When to use

- Renewal season is coming and the club has no message plan, or one email it sends to everyone.
- Many members lapse each year and nobody knows why.
- New members join but don't know who is who, where to go or how to book.
- The club is moving to a membership platform and needs its messages set up.

Not for: chasing debts beyond the agreed reminders, deciding fee changes, interpreting the constitution, or giving tax advice on membership fees. Check this with local rules, and ask the treasurer or a qualified accountant about fees, Gift Aid or refunds.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the member list or export, renewal dates, past messages and platform settings. Level 2 to set up draft messages, templates and automations in the membership platform or email tool. Level 3 to send approved messages to approved member groups and update renewal status and contact preferences within the client's limits. Refunds above the client's limit, payment settings and bank or payout details stay with the treasurer; ending a membership for a rules breach needs a fresh yes.

- Membership types and fees from the brief (adult, junior, family, concession), and any bursary or hardship route.
- Renewal date or dates, deadline, and any grace period the committee set.
- How members pay (online, Direct Debit, bank transfer, cash) and who confirms payment.
- The platform and its own reminder or retry timing, if any.
- Channels members agreed to (email, text, post, app) and who has opted out.
- Past messages and last year's renewal numbers.

## Steps

1. **Map the calendar.** Work back from the deadline: invitation, first reminder, second reminder, last call, deadline, lapsed. Use the committee's dates and grace period. Check the platform's automatic emails so members don't get two versions of the same reminder.
2. **Plan failed payments.** If the platform retries cards (Memberful, for example, retries up to four times 12 hours apart and deactivates after 48 hours, per its help page), write the "payment didn't go through" message to match that timing and point to the self-service update page.
3. **Write each message to one test.** The first line says what to do and by when ("Renew by 31 March to keep your court bookings"). Then the fee, how to pay, what membership gives this year, and who to contact. Texts under 160 characters where possible.
4. **Offer a route for hardship.** Mention the concession, payment-plan or confidential bursary option in the invitation and the last call, if the club has one. Sport England's club guidance (now on Buddle) suggests concessions, flexible payment and confidential bursaries so cost doesn't shut people out.
5. **Set the stop rule.** After the last reminder, stop. Move non-renewers to lapsed, send one lapsed note that keeps the door open (and asks one optional question about why), then no more renewal messages that year.
6. **Keep service and marketing apart.** Renewal notices go to members as part of their membership. Sponsor offers, appeals and event promotions go only to those who agreed to them. Whether a message counts as marketing is a legal question: check this with local rules.
7. **Write the welcome pack:** who is who, how things work (booking, kit, facilities), key dates, where to go, who to ask, the rules in one page, and how to update details or leave.
8. **Prepare the send list.** At level 3, list each message, date, group and channel, and get one yes before the first send. Log every send and status change.

## Checks

- Every fee, date and name matches the brief.
- Each message leads with the action and the deadline.
- The set has a stop rule and a lapsed note; no one is chased past it.
- The platform's own emails don't duplicate yours.
- Members who opted out of a channel are not sent messages on it.
- Hardship options are mentioned where the club offers them.
- No message contains payment card details, bank details or anything private about another member.
- The set carries "Template: check this with local rules before use."

## Output format

1. **Message map:** message, member group, channel, send date, platform automation (yes or no), stop rule.
2. **Messages:** subject or first line, body, sign-off.
3. **Welcome pack** on one or two pages.
4. **Platform set-up notes:** which automations to switch on or off.
5. **Send list and questions for the client.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Elmfield Tennis Club is a made-up club, used only to show the format. Fees and dates are invented.*

| Message | Group | Channel | Send | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Invitation | All current members | Email | 1 March | - |
| Reminder 1 | Not yet renewed | Email | 15 March | - |
| Last call | Not yet renewed | Text | 29 March | Last renewal message |
| Lapsed note | Not renewed by 7 April | Email | 8 April | No more renewal messages this year |

**Invitation (email):** "Renew by 31 March to keep your court bookings. Adult membership is £120 and junior £45. Renew online in two minutes at [link]. If the fee is hard this year, reply to this email in confidence; we have a small bursary fund. Thanks for being part of Elmfield. Priya, Membership Secretary."

**Last call (text):** "Elmfield TC: membership ends 31 March. Renew at [link] to keep booking courts. Reply STOP to opt out of texts."

**Lapsed note (email):** "We're sorry to see you go. If you'd like to come back, you can rejoin any time at [link]. If you have a minute, what made you decide not to renew? Just hit reply." Template: check this with local rules before use.
