# Volunteer roles, recruitment and induction

Most volunteer problems start before anyone signs up: a vague role, a big ask, no word on expenses, or a first day with no one to meet them. This skill fixes the front end, from the task list to the end of the first shift.

## When to use

- A group needs volunteers for tasks it can name (sorting, driving, front desk, coaching, events, admin).
- A programme recruits plenty of people but few turn up for a second shift.
- Volunteers start without a role description, a welcome or a named contact.
- A role may involve children or adults at risk and nobody has assessed checks.

Not for: deciding whether a role is eligible for a DBS or other check, approving anyone as safe, or answering employment or benefits questions. Check this with local rules, and pass those decisions to the group's safeguarding lead or a qualified adviser.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read task lists, past calls for volunteers, sign-up forms and policies. Level 2 to draft role pages, forms and welcome emails inside the group's website, volunteer database or email tool. Level 3 to publish an approved call for volunteers and send the approved sign-up reply within the limits the client sets. Accepting or turning down someone for a role that needs a check stays with the named lead.

- The cause and the tasks: what needs doing, when, where, and what went wrong before.
- Who the volunteer reports to, and who the safeguarding lead is.
- Existing policies: volunteer policy, safeguarding, expenses, data protection, code of conduct.
- Which expenses the group repays and how (form, receipts, timing).
- Country and nation (England and Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, US state, EU country).
- Who the roles might involve: children, adults at risk, money, driving, food, lone working.

## Steps

1. **Split tasks into roles.** Group tasks that one person can do in one block of time. Give each role a plain title people would search for ("Saturday food sorter", not "Logistics Associate").
2. **Write each role description** with: purpose, main tasks, time ask (with a short option where possible), place or remote, skills and training, who supports them, what they get back, limits (what they should not do), which policies apply, which expenses are repaid, and how they will know it went well. Write expectations as hopes: "we hope you can give two Saturdays a month", never "you must". NCVO warns role descriptions must not read like job descriptions.
3. **Mark people risks for the lead.** For each role, list the facts the lead needs to assess checks: contact with children or adults at risk, how often, supervised or not, overnight, personal care, money handling, driving. Write "Check level: to be confirmed by [lead]". Do not choose a level. Note for the lead: in England and Wales asking for a higher check than the law allows is unlawful; standard and enhanced checks are free for eligible volunteers; the Charity Commission says the supervision exemption for regulated work with children ended on 1 September 2026; in Scotland regulated roles need PVG membership.
4. **Remove blockers in the ask.** Offer one-off, short and remote options where the work allows; say up front that expenses are repaid; make the first step small (a 15-minute call or a taster shift). NCVO's 2023 survey found rigid commitment and cost were the main deterrents.
5. **Write the call for volunteers:** website text (100 to 200 words per role), a short social post, flyer text, and where to ask beyond the usual circle (the local Volunteer Centre, schools, colleges, community groups, employers' volunteering schemes).
6. **Write the reply to new sign-ups:** thanks, what happens next and when, who will contact them, any checks or references needed and why, and a way to say "not for me" with no guilt. Send within the time the group can keep to.
7. **Build the welcome pack:** a welcome letter from a named person, first-day checklist (where, when, who meets them, what to bring, breaks, toilets, parking), key rules in one page, how to claim expenses, who to call, an FAQ, and a box headed "Safeguarding and checks: for [lead] to confirm".
8. **Plan the induction:** role and expectations, people, tour, key policies (safeguarding, equality, data protection), how to raise a concern, shadowing an experienced volunteer, and a check-in after the first shift and after the first month.
9. **Prepare the go-live list.** At level 3, list what will be published and sent, to whom, and get one yes before going live.

## Checks

- No "must", "contract", "probation" or set weekly hours in any volunteer document.
- Expenses are real costs repaid with receipts, with no flat rates, vouchers or perks.
- Every role touching children or adults at risk has a lead-confirmation box; the agent has chosen no check level.
- The welcome pack names a real contact and a safeguarding lead.
- Sign-up forms ask only what the role needs, with marketing consent as a separate, unticked choice.
- Every name, date and policy point comes from the client's material.
- The pack carries "Template: check this with local rules before use."

## Output format

1. **Role table:** role, time ask, place, people risks noted, check level (lead to confirm), contact.
2. **Role descriptions,** one page each.
3. **Call for volunteers:** web text, social post, flyer text, where to share.
4. **Sign-up reply.**
5. **Welcome pack and induction plan,** with the lead-confirmation box.
6. **Questions for the client:** gaps, decisions for the lead, and the go-live list.

## Example of good output (short)

*Northgate Pantry is a made-up food bank, used only to show the format. Names and details are invented.*

| Role | Time ask | People risks noted | Check level | Contact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Saturday food sorter | One 3-hour Saturday shift a month, or a one-off | Works with other volunteers; no client contact | Lead to confirm | Asha, volunteer coordinator |
| Delivery driver | One weekday morning a fortnight | Visits clients at home, alone, some older or disabled | Lead to confirm (adults at risk, lone working) | Asha |

**Role description extract, Saturday food sorter:** "You'll check dates, sort donations into crates and get them ready for Monday. We hope you can join one Saturday a month, but a one-off shift helps too. We repay your bus fare or parking with a receipt. You don't need any experience: Tom will show you around on your first morning."

**Sign-up reply:** "Thanks for offering to help, Jo. Asha will call you this week to find a Saturday that suits you. There's no paperwork for this role beyond a short form on the day. If it's not for you after all, just reply 'not now' and we'll take you off the list."

**Note for the client:** the driver role involves visiting adults at home alone. Please ask your safeguarding lead to assess the check level before we advertise it. Template: check this with local rules before use.
