# Meta creative test plan

## When to use

- One or two ads carry most of the spend and the delivery column shows Creative limited or Creative fatigue.
- A new product, offer or season needs fresh concepts.
- The client asks "which ad is best?" and wants an answer they can trust.
- Monthly creative cycle.

## Why creative testing works differently on Meta now

With broad audiences, Meta's retrieval and ranking systems use the ad itself to decide who sees it, and Meta's own guidance (October 2026) is to supply more creative variety. Variety means different ideas, not different colours. Ten versions of one idea give the system one choice. So plan **concepts** first and **variations** second.

## What you need

- Last 90 days of ad-level results (spend, impressions, frequency, CTR (link), cost per result, results, ROAS) with the attribution setting named.
- The live ads and past winners, with launch dates.
- Customer language: reviews, survey answers, sales call notes, support tickets, comments on ads.
- Offer and margin: break-even cost per result.
- Weekly budget the client will put behind testing (as a share of total).
- Brand rules and claims the client can prove.

**Access:** level 1 to diagnose and read results; level 2 to build the test paused; level 3 to launch it, graduate winners and pause losers, within your budget cap and the agreed stop rule.

## Steps

### 1. Diagnose the current set
Tag every live ad with: concept name, person shown, problem, promise, proof type (review, demo, number, expert, before and after), format (video, static, carousel), length, hook (first line or first three seconds), and awareness level (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware). Count distinct concepts. Note spend share and status per concept.

### 2. Build the concept matrix
List 6 to 12 candidate concepts. For each: who it is for, the problem it opens with, the promise, the proof, the format, and the awareness level. Mix levels on purpose. Score each 1 to 5 on: strength of evidence from customer language, distance from what is already live, and production cost. Pick 3 to 5 to test this round.

### 3. Choose the test method
- **Meta A/B test (Experiments or Ads Manager):** splits the audience so nobody sees both versions. Use when the question matters (for example, "does UGC beat studio video for this product?") and you can afford equal budgets per cell. Write the hypothesis first.
- **Testing campaign:** a separate campaign with the same goal as the main campaign, holding only new concepts, often one ad set per concept with its own budget so each concept gets spend. Audiences are not split at random, so treat it as a screen, not a verdict. Graduate winners into the main campaign.
- **Add to the live campaign:** cheapest, but adding ads is a significant edit and restarts learning for that ad set. Batch new ads into one weekly drop rather than trickling them in.

Never test by switching ad sets on and off. Meta advises against it because audiences overlap and results are unreliable.

### 4. Set budget, length and stop rule before launch
- Each concept needs enough results to judge. As a working rule (my rule of thumb, not a Meta figure), aim for at least 20 to 30 conversions per concept for a cost comparison, or use a higher-volume proxy (add to cart, lead form open) only to kill clear losers early, never to crown winners.
- Budget per concept for that volume is roughly target cost per result times the conversions you want. If that is unaffordable, test fewer concepts.
- Minimum length: 7 days, to cover weekday patterns. Do not read before 3 days.
- Write the decision rule now: for example, "a concept graduates if its cost per purchase is at or below break-even after at least 25 purchases; it is cut if it spends 2 times target cost per purchase with no purchase."

### 5. Launch checklist (level 2 to build, level 3 to launch)
- Naming convention that encodes concept, format, hook and date (for example `C07_UGC-unbox_vid15_hookQ_2026-11`).
- Advantage+ creative enhancements reviewed and approved by the client.
- Vertical (9:16) and square or 4:5 versions where the placement needs them.
- Claims checked against Meta's Advertising Standards; Special Ad Category set if relevant.
- Client approval of budget, dates and the stop rule, in writing.

### 6. Read the results
- Read at the planned end, not before, unless the kill rule triggers.
- Compare cost per result, results volume and, where values exist, ROAS. Look at hook rate (3-second views divided by impressions) and hold rate for video to see where it lost people.
- Check sample size: a "win" on 6 purchases is noise.
- For A/B tests, use Meta's reported winner and confidence; for testing campaigns, say plainly that the split was not random.
- Separate the concept verdict from the execution verdict. A concept can be right and the edit wrong.

### 7. Decide and brief the next round
At level 3, I move winners and archive losers by the agreed rule and log it; at lower levels I send the list. Winners graduate to the main campaign in one batch. Losers are archived with a one-line reason. Near-misses get one more execution. Write the next brief from what you learned: what the winning concepts share, and what you still do not know.

## Checks

- Concepts are truly different: if you cover the logo, could someone tell them apart?
- Every concept is tagged, so results roll up by idea, not by file.
- Budget and stop rule were written and approved before launch.
- No concept was judged on fewer results than planned.
- The read-out names the attribution setting.
- Claims are provable and allowed.

## Output format

1. **Current state:** concepts live, spend share, fatigue status, hit rate.
2. **Concept matrix:** table of candidates with scores; chosen concepts marked.
3. **Test design:** method, cells, budget per cell, start and end dates, main metric, decision rule.
4. **Production brief per concept:** audience and awareness level, hook (first three seconds or first line), body, proof, offer, call to action, formats and sizes, must-not-say.
5. **Approval list.**
6. **Read-out template** for the end date.

## Example of good output (short)

> **Round 4 (11 to 24 Nov).** Live spend sits 71% on one founder-story video, now Creative limited. Hypothesis: problem-aware buyers respond better to a demonstration of the fix than to the founder's story.
>
> | Concept | Awareness | Hook | Proof | Format |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | C11 "The 6 a.m. leak" | Problem aware | Water on the floor, no talking | 30-second demo | 9:16 video |
> | C12 "1,900 reviews say" | Product aware | Star rating on screen | Review montage | 4:5 static carousel |
> | C13 "Plumber's verdict" | Solution aware | Plumber holds old part | Expert explains | 9:16 video |
>
> **Method:** testing campaign, same purchase goal, one ad set per concept at 75 USD a day each (about 1,050 USD per concept over 14 days, enough for roughly 27 purchases at target). **Rule:** graduate at or below 38 USD per purchase after 25 purchases; cut at 76 USD spent with no purchase. **Read on 24 Nov.** Approve? Yes or no.
