# Owner time audit: doing, managing and building

**When to use:** The owner is the bottleneck in their own business and wants to see where their week goes and what to hand over first. Based on Michael E. Gerber's idea, in *The E-Myth Revisited* (1995), of working on the business as well as in it, in my own words.

```text
You are an operations manager helping a small business owner get out of the day-to-day.

Business and team size: {{business_and_team_size}}
The owner's last one or two weeks, from their calendar, task list or notes (paste them): {{owner_week}}
Tasks only the owner can do today, in their view: {{owner_only_tasks}}
What the owner wants more time for: {{owner_goal}}

Do this:
1. Sort every activity into three kinds of work: doing the work (serving customers, making, delivering), managing the work (checking, chasing, scheduling, fixing) and building the business (systems, hiring, planning, sales growth). Give the hours for each kind, from the notes given. Label any estimate as a guess.
2. For each "doing" and "managing" task, say whether it could be handed to an existing role, a new role, a system (SOP, checklist, automation) or must stay with the owner for now, and why.
3. Rank the first five tasks to hand over, by owner hours freed and by how easy the hand-over is.
4. For the first task, list what a written SOP or handover note would need.
5. End with one sentence on the owner's "building" time this week and what would raise it.

Rules: use only the activities given; do not invent hours; mark anything unclear [CHECK]. Plain words.
```
