# Operations manual

## When to use

- The owner wants the business to run without them for weeks at a time.
- A second or third site is planned.
- The owner is getting ready to franchise and needs the manual a franchise lawyer will refer to.
- Standards differ between sites and customers notice.

Do not use it to write franchise agreements, disclosure documents or legal terms, or to recruit franchisees. Those are for a franchise lawyer in each country. Do not invent standards the business does not keep.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** existing SOPs, training notes, photos, checklists and site audit records.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft chapters, checklists and the scorecard in a drafts space.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish approved chapters to the manual space, set up the opening checklist as tasks with owners and dates, and load the scorecard into the inspection or checklist tool, within the client's limits.

## What you need

1. The business, its sites, who will use the manual (new managers, franchisees, staff) and the go-live date.
2. The best-run site, and the people who run it well.
3. Existing SOPs, training notes, photos, menus or service lists, supplier lists and checklists.
4. Brand rules: look, uniform, signage, greeting, tone.
5. The numbers each site reports and how often.
6. For a planned US franchise: the date the franchise lawyer needs the manual's table of contents, because it goes into the disclosure document.

## Steps

### 1. Draw the org chart of roles
List the positions a site needs (for example site manager, shift lead, front of house, back of house, cleaner), before deciding who fills them. Write today's names in the boxes; one person may fill several roles for now. Gaps show where the owner is still doing a job.

### 2. Write role agreements
For each role: the results it owns, its standards, who it reports to, the systems and SOPs it uses and the numbers it is judged on. Keep each to half a page.

### 3. Agree the chapter plan before writing
Share a chapter list for the client to approve. A typical plan:
1. About us and our standards (brand, promise, customer standard)
2. Roles and responsibilities
3. Opening and closing
4. Daily operations (by area)
5. Customer service and complaints
6. Health, safety and hygiene (controls set by a qualified person, with official links)
7. People: rotas, training, uniforms (HR terms point to the HR policy, not written here)
8. Stock, suppliers and ordering
9. Cash, payments and records
10. Equipment, cleaning and maintenance
11. Reporting and the weekly review
12. Emergencies and what to do when things go wrong

### 4. Pull the steps from the best site
Use existing SOPs where they work. Where sites do things differently, ask the owner which way is the standard. Mark anything with no written standard as a gap.

### 5. Write each chapter
Purpose, the standard, steps (as SOPs or links to SOPs), checks, common mistakes and places for photos. Write for the person on shift, in plain words.

### 6. Write standards with pass and fail examples
A standard that cannot be checked is a wish. "The counter is clean" fails; "No crumbs, smears or items on the counter except the till and menu stand" passes, with a photo of each.

### 7. Build the new site opening checklist
Week by week from signing the site to the end of the first month open: fit-out, suppliers, systems, hiring and training, soft opening, launch, first-month checks. Each item has an owner, a date and a sign-off.

### 8. Build the site visit scorecard
Sections for standards, service, cleanliness, safety checks done, paperwork and numbers. Each line is pass or fail against a written standard, not a score out of ten for how it feels. End with a follow-up action form: issue, owner, date, check date.

### 9. List the gaps
Tasks with no owner, standards with no written rule, steps that depend on one person. These become the next SOPs to write.

### 10. Test and publish
Ask someone who has never worked at the best site to run a shift from the manual, and fix where they got stuck. At level 3, after the client's yes, publish the chapters and set up the checklist and scorecard. Otherwise hand over the files.

### Rules by country to point to, not decide
Health and safety rules differ (HSE in the UK, OSHA in the US, the Framework Directive and national law in the EU), and so do food rules (FSA guidance in England and Wales, the locally adopted FDA Food Code in the US, Regulation 852/2004 in the EU). Link the right official source in chapter 6 and have a qualified person set the controls. In the US, the operating manual's table of contents goes into the franchise disclosure document; a franchise lawyer decides how.

## Checks

- The chapter plan was approved before chapters were written.
- Every standard can be checked as pass or fail, with an example.
- Every step comes from the client's sources or the owner's answer; gaps are listed, not invented.
- Health, safety, hygiene and legal content points to official guidance and a qualified person.
- The opening checklist has owners, dates and sign-offs.
- The scorecard uses written standards and has a follow-up form.
- Nothing was published or set up live without level 3 access.

## Output format

1. Org chart of roles and role agreements
2. Approved chapter plan
3. Written chapters (the number agreed)
4. Brand and service standards with pass and fail examples
5. New site opening checklist (table)
6. Site visit scorecard and follow-up form
7. Gap list

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bakery with one cafe, opening a second. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Standard 3.2: Opening the counter by 7:00**
> **Pass:** Display full to the photo plan, labels on every tray, allergen book on the counter, card reader tested with a zero-value test. **Fail:** gaps in the display, missing labels, allergen book in the office.
> *Photo: display plan, Monday.*
>
> | Week | Item | Owner | Sign-off |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | -6 | Book food hygiene registration with the local authority (check the official route for your area) | Owner | Owner |
> | -3 | Train new shift leads at the first cafe on chapters 3 to 5 | Head baker | Site manager |
> | -1 | Soft opening for friends and neighbours; log every problem | Site manager | Owner |
>
> **Gap:** No written standard for end-of-day waste. Next SOP to write.
