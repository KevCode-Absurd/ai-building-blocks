# Training interview: operations manager

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share names, staff details, customer data or confidential figures from real employers or clients; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of business have you run operations for (industry, size, number of sites), and what was your role?**
   Why it matters: sets the kind of business the agent is built for and the scale it should expect.

2. **When you walk into a new business, what do you look at in the first week, and in what order?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's diagnostic routine, which is the agent's first-week playbook.

3. **Which numbers do you ask for first, and which do you ignore?**
   Why it matters: shows the expert's core measures and the vanity numbers to skip.

4. **How do you find the step that limits output? Tell me about a time the real bottleneck surprised you.**
   Why it matters: bottleneck judgement is hard to write down; a real case makes it concrete.

5. **How do you get the true steps of a task when the person who does it cannot explain it well?**
   Why it matters: capture methods (watching, recording, walk-throughs) decide whether SOPs match reality.

6. **What does a good SOP look like to you? Show me one you are proud of, anonymised.**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a model of length, format and level of detail.

7. **Why do teams ignore SOPs, and what have you done about it?**
   Why it matters: adoption is where most documentation fails; the expert's fixes become decision rules.

8. **How do you run a weekly ops review so actions actually get done?**
   Why it matters: captures the meeting rhythm, the format and how the expert handles rolled-over actions.

9. **Tell me about a fix that did not stick. What went wrong?**
   Why it matters: failure stories give the agent its warning signs and follow-up habits.

10. **How do you decide whether to fix a process, automate it, or drop it?**
    Why it matters: shows the order of moves (simplify, then automate) and the expert's judgement on tools.

11. **Which tools do you rely on for SOPs, tasks, checklists and maps, and what would you never use again?**
    Why it matters: grounds the agent's tool advice in real use, not vendor claims.

12. **How do you get an owner to let go of work they have always done?**
    Why it matters: owner hand-over is a people problem as much as a process one; the expert's approach guides tone.

13. **When you wrote an operations manual for a new site, what did you include, and what did the new site still get wrong?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent what a manual must cover and where manuals usually fall short.

14. **How do you write a standard that two different people would judge the same way?**
    Why it matters: checkable standards are the core of manuals and scorecards.

15. **Where do you draw the line between your work and safety, food safety, HR or legal advice? Who do you call?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's referral rules and the qualified people to name.

16. **How do you handle a manager who blames a person for a process failure?**
    Why it matters: keeps the agent's reviews focused on causes, not blame, and gives it language for pushback.

17. **What access to a client's tools do you ask for, and what do you change yourself versus hand back to the client?**
    Why it matters: turns the three access levels into the expert's real practice and limits.

18. **Tell me about the hardest operations job you have done. What made it hard and how did it end?**
    Why it matters: a full story shows judgement under pressure that short answers miss.

19. **What do weaker operations people get wrong that you never do?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's common mistakes and quality bar.

20. **If you had one week to improve a messy small business, what would you do each day?**
    Why it matters: compresses the expert's method into a plan the agent can follow.
