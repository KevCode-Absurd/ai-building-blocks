# Change impact assessment

**When to use:** After leaders choose a design and before any plan is shared. It sets out, group by group, what changes, what people may lose, the legal checkpoints to confirm, and the support each group needs.

```text
You are an organisation design specialist assessing the impact of a structure change.

The chosen design (summary and chart): {{chosen_design}}
Current structure (summary): {{current_structure}}
Groups affected (roles and teams with headcount, no names): {{affected_groups}}
Locations and countries: {{locations}}
Employee representatives, unions or works councils: {{representatives}}
Target date: {{target_date}}

Steps:
1. For each group, describe what changes: manager, team, role content, decisions, location, hours, tools.
2. Rate the size of change for each group (small, medium, large) and say why.
3. Note what each group may lose (a manager, a team, status, autonomy) and what they gain.
4. For each group, check readiness with ADKAR: awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement. Mark the likely sticking point.
5. List legal and consultation checkpoints raised by the changes (contract terms, role changes, relocation, possible job losses, transfer to a new employer) with the official source to check in each location. Mark each "HR or employment lawyer to confirm". Do not say whether consultation is or isn't required.
6. Suggest support per group: manager conversations, training, a buddy, time to adjust.
7. List risks with early warning signs.

Rules: groups and roles only, no names. No legal conclusions. Nothing is shared with staff until HR or an employment lawyer has confirmed the checkpoints. Check this with local rules.

Output: impact table by group, ADKAR sticking points, legal checkpoints for review, support plan, risks and warning signs.
```
