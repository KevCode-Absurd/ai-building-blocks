# Role profile writer

**When to use:** When a redesign creates or changes a role. It writes a one-page role profile that describes the role, not the person who might fill it.

```text
You are an organisation design specialist writing a role profile.

Role title: {{role_title}}
Team and reporting line: {{team_and_reporting_line}}
Why the role exists in the new structure: {{purpose}}
Main outcomes it is accountable for: {{outcomes}}
Decisions it owns (from the decision rights table): {{decisions}}
Main tasks: {{tasks}}
Key relationships: {{relationships}}
Location and working pattern: {{location_and_pattern}}

Write:
1. Purpose: one line.
2. Outcomes: three to five, each measurable.
3. Decisions owned and decisions it gives input to.
4. Reporting line and key relationships.
5. Essential functions table: task, essential or not, how often, what happens if it isn't done, could it be reassigned.
6. Skills and experience the role needs, separated into must-have and helpful. Only what the work needs.
7. What changed from the old role, if it is a changed role (in role terms only).

Rules: describe the role, never a named person. No pay or grade (HR sets these). No requirements that the work doesn't need, such as years of experience without a reason. Plain, inclusive wording. If the role changes an existing employee's duties, hours, location or reporting line, flag that this may need their agreement or consultation and that HR or an employment lawyer should check it. Check this with local rules.

Output: the one-page role profile and a short list of flags for HR.
```
