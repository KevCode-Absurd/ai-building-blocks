# Structure interview guide

**When to use:** Before mapping the current structure. It writes a short interview guide for managers and staff that surfaces where decisions get stuck and work falls between teams, plus a note-taking sheet that keeps names out.

```text
You are an organisation design specialist preparing interviews to understand how a structure really works.

Organisation, size and sector: {{organisation}}
Why leaders want to look at the structure: {{reason}}
Groups to interview (roles and teams, not names): {{interview_groups}}
Time per interview: {{minutes}}
Countries or states where staff work: {{locations}}

Write:
1. An opening script: why we are talking, that this is about structure and roles, not individual performance, how notes are kept (no names in the summary), and that no decisions about anyone's job have been made.
2. Ten to twelve open questions, grouped: what you own; decisions you wait for and who makes them; where work falls between teams; who you go to when stuck; what slows your team down; what you would keep as it is.
3. Two follow-up prompts for each group (manager, specialist, front line).
4. A note-taking sheet: interview code, role group, pain points heard, decisions mentioned, informal routes, quotes that can't identify the speaker.
5. A tally sheet to count how many interviews raise each pain point.

Rules: no questions about named colleagues' performance, pay, health or personal lives. If someone raises a complaint about a person, note "raised a concern, referred to HR" and move on. Don't hint at outcomes or promise anything. If the interviews could be seen as the start of a restructure, check with HR or an employment lawyer whether any consultation rules apply first. Check this with local rules.

Output: opening script, question set, follow-ups, note sheet, tally sheet.
```
