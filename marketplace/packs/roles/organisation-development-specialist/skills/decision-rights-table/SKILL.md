# Decision rights table

## When to use

- Decisions wait for one or two people at the top.
- Two leaders both believe they own the same decision.
- After a redesign, to make the new structure work in practice.
- When merging teams that used to decide things differently.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the org chart, delegation of authority or sign-off rules, recent examples of stuck decisions.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** write the table as a draft page.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish the approved table to the page the client named and post the approved notice to the agreed channel, within their limits. Changes that alter a named person's role or authority need a fresh yes each time.

## What you need

1. The structure (current or chosen) with manager roles.
2. Any existing sign-off limits, delegation rules or approval policies.
3. A list of decisions that matter, or examples of decisions that got stuck.
4. The client's preferred format: RAPID or RACI.
5. Any legal or regulatory sign-offs (finance, safety, data protection).

## Steps

### 1. List the decisions
Aim for the decisions that matter most and recur: hiring into an approved role, pricing and discounts, budgets, product or service changes, customer exceptions, supplier choices, policy changes. Write each as a verb phrase ("approve a discount above the standard range").

### 2. Add scope limits
Where the client sets them, add value, risk or scope limits ("up to an amount the client sets", "within the approved budget"). The client sets every number; I don't invent thresholds.

### 3. Assign the roles
For each decision, using RAPID:
- **Recommend:** gathers input and proposes. One person.
- **Agree:** must sign off, used sparingly for legal, regulatory or financial checks.
- **Perform:** carries out the decision.
- **Input:** gives expertise or is affected; consulted, but doesn't block.
- **Decide:** makes the final call. Exactly one role.

If the client prefers RACI: one Accountable per decision, Responsible for doing it, Consulted and Informed.

### 4. Push decisions down where the criteria say so
Check the design criteria: if the aim is faster decisions without the founders, the decider should usually be the role closest to the information. Keep top-level decisions to the few that truly need them.

### 5. Add escalation and recording
For each decision: when it escalates (above a limit, a disagreement between Recommend and Agree), to whom, and where the decision is recorded.

### 6. Test with real cases
Take recent decisions that got stuck, with names removed. Walk them through the table. Would they have moved faster? Who would have decided? Fix the table, not the story.

### 7. Mark people decisions clearly
Hiring, promotion, pay, discipline and dismissal follow the client's HR process and employment law. The table can show who decides within that process, but it doesn't replace it. Flag these rows for HR or an employment lawyer to confirm. Check this with local rules.

### 8. Set a review date
Decision rights drift. Set a date to review the table with leaders, and a simple way for anyone to flag a decision that has no clear owner.

## Checks

- Exactly one decider per decision.
- "Agree" is used only where a real check is required.
- Every limit or threshold comes from the client.
- Escalation route and record location for each decision.
- Tested against real recent decisions.
- People decisions flagged for HR or an employment lawyer.
- No names, only roles.
- Review date set.

## Output format

1. Summary: what changes and why.
2. Decision rights table: decision, limit, Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide, escalation, record.
3. Test cases: decision, what happened, what the table says now.
4. Rows flagged for HR or legal review.
5. Rollout note for managers and review date.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a retail group, 220 staff. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> | Decision | Limit | Recommend | Agree | Input | Decide | Perform |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Approve a customer refund outside policy | Up to the limit the board set | Store manager | none | Customer service lead | Regional manager | Store team |
> | Change a product's shelf price | Within the pricing rule | Category buyer | Finance lead (margin check) | Store managers | Head of buying | Store teams |
> | Hire into an approved, budgeted role | Approved headcount only | Hiring manager | HR (process check) | Team members on the panel | Hiring manager's manager | HR |
>
> **Test case:** in September, a refund over policy waited nine days for the chief executive. Under this table the regional manager decides the same day.
>
> **Flag for HR:** the hiring row follows your recruitment policy; check it with HR. Check this with local rules.
