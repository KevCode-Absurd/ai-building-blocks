# Training interview: Organisation design specialist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share names, pay, performance or personal details of real staff, or anything that identifies a client's confidential restructure; anonymise every story.

1. **What sizes of organisation, sectors and countries do you know best?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and which employment rules and consultation steps it must flag.

2. **Walk me through your last redesign from the first conversation to the review afterwards.**
   Why it matters: shows the expert's real sequence and where they add the most value.

3. **How do you find out how a structure really works, as opposed to what the chart says?**
   Why it matters: diagnosis is the step most often skipped, and the expert's methods make it reliable.

4. **What questions do you ask in diagnostic interviews, and which answer surprises leaders most often?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent tested questions and a sense of the usual blind spots.

5. **How do you judge whether a manager's span is too wide or too narrow?**
   Why it matters: captures the factors the expert weighs instead of a single number.

6. **Show me an option you rejected. What was its "what gets harder" list?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert makes trade-offs visible and fair.

7. **When have processes, rewards or skills mattered more than the boxes?**
   Why it matters: teaches the agent to design the whole system, not just the chart.

8. **How do you get leaders to agree one decider per decision?**
   Why it matters: decision rights fail on this point, and the expert's tactics are the specialty.

9. **Tell me about a time a client wanted to design the structure around particular people.**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert keeps roles before names, kindly and firmly.

10. **What legal or consultation steps have you seen change a timeline, and how do you flag them early?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent where to stop and send the client to HR or an employment lawyer.

11. **How do you write a role profile that HR can use and that doesn't describe the current holder?**
    Why it matters: captures a reusable profile format and common traps.

12. **How do you brief managers before staff hear about a change?**
    Why it matters: managers carry the hard conversations; the briefing is what makes them work.

13. **What do you put in a staff announcement, and what do you never put in it?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent tested wording and the lines it must not cross.

14. **How do you run a team health check, and how do you stop it becoming a league table?**
    Why it matters: protects trust and keeps the tool useful.

15. **Tell me about a merger of two teams. What did people lose, and how did you handle it?**
    Why it matters: captures the people side of change in a common, hard case.

16. **When did a redesign not work? What would you do differently?**
    Why it matters: failures show the traps the agent must warn clients about.

17. **How do you tell, a few months later, whether the new structure is working?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent review measures beyond "it feels better".

18. **Which tools do you use for charts, decision tables and surveys, and what do you never put in them?**
    Why it matters: maps the expert's tools to the access levels and privacy rules.

19. **What change myths or numbers do you hear most often, and how do you answer them?**
    Why it matters: helps the agent challenge unsourced claims politely.

20. **What does a great first page of your options paper look like?**
    Why it matters: captures the expert's house format for leaders.
