# Redesign from diagnosis to review

**Trigger or cadence:** Leaders say the structure is slowing them down, a merger or acquisition closes, or the strategy changes. One redesign usually runs over several weeks to a few months, set mainly by the legal and consultation steps where staff work.

**Access:** Set at the start, per tool (see `access.md`). Steps marked level 2 or level 3 need that level in the named tool; at a lower level I hand you the work to enter or send yourself. Until you choose, I work at level 1. Anything that affects a named person's job needs your fresh yes every time, at every level.

## Steps

1. **Agree the purpose and the rules.** What the structure must make easier, who decides, who is consulted, and where staff work. Ask whether HR or an employment lawyer is involved yet. *Approval: the chief executive or sponsor agrees the purpose and the decision process.*
2. **Interview and gather.** Use the `structure-interview-guide` prompt with role groups. Read the chart and headcount export (level 1).
3. **Map today.** Use the `current-structure-map` skill: Mermaid chart, spans and layers (with the `span-and-layer-check` prompt), counted pain points, design criteria. Draw on a new board (level 2). *Approval: leaders agree the map reflects reality and sign off the design criteria.*
4. **Baseline team health.** Run the `team-health-check-session` prompt in the teams most likely to be affected. Results stay with each team.
5. **Design options.** Use the `org-design-options` skill: two or three options with trade-offs, a fair recommendation and role profiles (with the `role-profile-writer` prompt). *Approval: leaders choose an option. No staff outside the leadership group see options until HR or legal review is done.*
6. **Decision rights.** Use the `decision-rights-table` skill for the chosen design. *Approval: leaders approve the table; HR confirms the people-decision rows.*
7. **Impact and legal checkpoints.** Run the `change-impact-assessment` prompt. HR or an employment lawyer confirms consultation, contract and notice steps for each location. Check this with local rules. *Approval: the sponsor and HR or legal adviser sign off the checkpoints and timeline.*
8. **Transition plan and messages.** Use the `transition-plan-and-announcement` skill and the `staff-announcement-and-faq` prompt. *Approval: fresh yes for each staff message, each consultation or at-risk letter, and the announcement date.*
9. **Move.** Carry out the approved, non-personal actions at level 3: publish the decision rights table and role profiles to the agreed page, update the approved chart, book approved meetings. Each record change for a named person (manager, title, team, location) is prepared as a change sheet and done only with your fresh yes for that change.
10. **Review.** A few months after the change, repeat the team health check, re-test stuck decisions against the table, and report what improved and what didn't.

## Outputs

- Current-state map, spans and layers table, pain point tally and design criteria
- Options with trade-offs, recommendation and role profiles
- Decision rights table
- Change impact assessment with legal checkpoints marked for HR or an employment lawyer
- Transition plan, manager briefing, announcement and FAQ
- Team health results (owned by each team) before and after
- Action log of everything done in the client's tools (what, where, when, result)

## Needs the client's approval

**Runs at level 3 within your limits, after one confirmation.** Before the first live action, I show the list (publish approved pages, update the approved chart on the named board, send approved team health surveys to approved teams, book approved meetings) and its limits: which tools, pages, teams and dates. After your yes I do these and log each one.

**Always needs a fresh yes, every time.** Any change to a named person's manager, title, role, team, location or hours; restructure announcements and staff messages about the change; consultation, at-risk or offer letters; sharing options with anyone outside the leadership group; deleting or overwriting records. Pay and anything filed with a government body stay with you. Legal steps go to HR or an employment lawyer. Check this with local rules.
