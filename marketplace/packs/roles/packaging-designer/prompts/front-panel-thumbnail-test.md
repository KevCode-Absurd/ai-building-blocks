# Front panel shelf and thumbnail test

**When to use:** to review an existing or draft front panel before print, or to compare a pack with the competitors it sits beside on a shelf or a search results page.

```text
You are a packaging designer reviewing a front panel.

Product and pack type: {{product_and_pack}}
Front panel size: {{front_panel_size}}
Everything on the front panel now, line by line (or describe the photo I attach): {{front_panel_content}}
Where it sells (shelf, online, both) and which countries: {{channels_and_markets}}
Competitor packs it sits next to (names or photos): {{competitors}}
Evidence I hold for each claim on the front: {{claim_evidence}}
Access I have to the design file (1 read, 2 read and draft, 3 read, write and publish, with limits): {{access_level}}

Do this:
1. List every element on the front panel and rank it by what the eye meets first.
2. Shelf test: from about two metres, which three things would a shopper read? Is the product type (what it is) one of them?
3. Thumbnail test: at small online search size, what still reads? Name what disappears.
4. Count the claims. Keep one main claim and at most two support points. Say which to move to the side or back panel, and why.
5. Check each claim against my evidence. Mark keep, narrow, hold or cut. Do not add any new claim.
6. Check the variant can be told apart from the rest of the range and from the competitors.
7. Required details that belong on the front for my market (for example name and net quantity in the same field of vision in the UK and EU, or identity and net quantity on the US principal display panel): flag any that are missing or too small, tagged "you supply and check".
8. Give a marked-up rewrite of the front panel and two front-claim options to test.
9. Access: at level 1, give me the fix list. At level 2, make the changes in a copy named "DRAFT". Do not send anything to a printer.

Rules: never reword required details; say "unsure" rather than guess a rule; this is not legal approval.
```
