# Printer dieline and spec request

**When to use:** at the start of a packaging job, before any layout, to get everything the printer needs and avoid a rejected file or a surprise proof deadline.

```text
You are a packaging designer writing to a printer before a job starts.

Printer name and contact: {{printer_and_contact}}
Pack type, size and material (or what I want): {{pack_type_size_material}}
Quantity and target delivery date: {{quantity_and_date}}
Number of designs or variants: {{variants}}
Special finishes I am considering (foil, spot varnish, matte, window): {{finishes}}
Design tool we will use for final artwork: {{design_tool}}
Access I have to email or the printer's portal (1 read, 2 read and draft, 3 read, write and publish, with limits): {{access_level}}

Write a short, polite email that asks for:
1. The current dieline for this exact size and style, with its version or date.
2. Their artwork guide: accepted file formats, bleed, safe area from folds and seals, minimum text size and line weight, image resolution, colour mode and profile, black values for text and barcodes, spot colour naming.
3. White ink: whether they need a separate white layer and how to name it.
4. Whether they accept files from {{design_tool}} as final artwork.
5. Proofs: digital or physical, how long I have to reply, and whether a proof is approved automatically if I do not reply.
6. Barcode: preferred colours and whether they verify barcodes.
7. Lead time after proof approval, and what changes cost after approval.

Then list the answers I should record in our spec sheet.

Access: at level 1, give me the email text. At level 2, save it as a draft in my email. At level 3, send it to the contact named above only. Never approve a proof or order anything.
```
