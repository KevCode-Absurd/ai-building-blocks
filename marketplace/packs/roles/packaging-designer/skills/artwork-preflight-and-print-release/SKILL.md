# Artwork preflight and print release

Most packaging reprints come from a few faults: artwork off the dieline, text too close to a fold or seal, a missing white layer, an RGB file, a barcode that will not scan, or a typo in a required detail nobody re-read on the proof. This skill catches them in order, and keeps the client in control of the one step that spends the money.

## When to use

- Before uploading artwork to a box, pouch or label printer.
- After a printer rejects a file or flags problems.
- When a printer's proof arrives, and before its reply deadline.

## What you need

- **The printer's current dieline and artwork guide** (file formats, bleed, safe area, resolution, colour mode, black values, white ink, proofs). Two examples of what these guides cover: Packlane asks for 300 PPI images, CMYK, 100% black for text and scannable codes, and auto-approves its digital proof on the fourth business day without a reply; ePac asks for separate artwork, dieline and white layers, outlined text and embedded images, and does not accept Canva files as final artwork. The printer's own guide always wins.
- **The approved panel map and copy deck,** with the required details list.
- **The artwork file or exported PDF,** and later the printer's proof.
- **Access I have** per tool: level 1 for checking; level 2 to fix a draft copy or upload for a proof without ordering; level 3 to send final files to the named printer contact within the limits. **Releasing to print and approving a proof need the client's fresh yes every time, at any level.** Payment stays with the client.

## Steps

1. **Match the dieline.** Same version and size as the printer's current file; artwork on the printer's dieline layer, not redrawn; dieline layer set as the printer asks (often a named spot colour, not printed).
2. **Check edges, folds and seals.** Bleed past every cut edge as the printer asks; text and key art inside the safe area; nothing important across folds, glue flaps, pouch seals, zips, tear notches or the base gusset.
3. **Check colour.** CMYK or the printer's stated profile; spot colours named as the printer expects; small text and barcodes in 100% black where the printer asks, rich black only for large solid areas; overprint only where intended.
4. **Check white and substrate.** On clear, kraft or metallic film, a separate white layer where colours must stay bright; transparent windows left clear on purpose.
5. **Check images and type.** Raster images at the printer's minimum resolution at print size; fonts outlined or embedded; no hairlines thinner than the printer allows; minimum type sizes met for required details.
6. **Check the barcode.** Correct GTIN for this variant; not scaled from old art; quiet zones clear; dark bars on a light background, no red bars; on a flat face away from seals and curves. Scan a printed test at real size with a phone scanner app as a rough check, and ask the printer whether they verify.
7. **Check the words.** Compare every line on the artwork with the approved copy deck, character by character for required details: name, net quantity, allergens and their emphasis, address, warnings, date-mark space, recycling label.
8. **Write the preflight report** with each item marked pass, fail or unknown, and the fix for each fail.
9. **Level 2: fix a draft copy** in the connected tool and upload for a proof without ordering, where the printer allows it. Log each action.
10. **Level 3: send the files** to the printer contact the client named, within the agreed limits. Log it.
11. **Check the proof** when it arrives: dieline fit, colours against the agreed references, white layer, every word again, barcode, and anything the printer changed. Note the reply deadline.
12. **Ask for the release.** Send the client the proof, the preflight report, the open risks and the deadline, and ask a clear question: "Do you approve this proof for production, quantity X, at the quoted price?" Only after a yes for this proof do I approve it or tell the printer to proceed, if my access allows; otherwise the client approves it themselves.

## Checks

- Every printer rule checked, none marked "unknown" without a question sent.
- Required details on the proof match the approved copy exactly.
- Barcode correct for the variant and scanned on a test print.
- The proof deadline is on the plan.
- The client's yes for this specific proof is recorded with date and quantity.

## Output format

1. **Access I have.**
2. **Preflight report:** item, printer rule, status, fix.
3. **Proof check:** item, matches or differs, note.
4. **Release request:** proof version, quantity, quoted price (as the printer gave it), deadline, open risks.
5. **Action log.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Northwind Pet Treats is a made-up business used only to show the format: a 150 g kraft stand-up pouch printed digitally. Access I have: printer account level 2; design file level 1.*

| Item | Printer rule | Status | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dieline | Current printer dieline, v3 | Fail: artwork on v2 | Move artwork to v3 |
| White layer | Separate layer under colour on kraft | Fail: none | Add white under logo and photo only |
| Text near seals | Keep clear of top seal and zip | Fail: "Best before" crosses zip | Move to base gusset |
| Colour | CMYK | Pass | - |
| Barcode | Black on white, GTIN for 150 g | Unknown: GTIN is the 300 g number | Client to supply the 150 g GTIN |
| Allergens | Match copy deck | Pass | - |

**Release request (after fixes and proof):** proof v3 for 2,000 pouches, price as quoted by the printer, reply due by the printer's deadline on Thursday. Open risk: the kraft base may make the green look darker; a printed sample would settle it. Do you approve this proof for production? I will not approve it without your yes.
