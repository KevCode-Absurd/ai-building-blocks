# On-pack copy by panel

Pack copy has two jobs. The front must tell a shopper what it is and why to pick it, in about the time it takes to walk past. The back must inform, build trust and fit. Both must stay inside the rules and the space. This skill writes to the panel sizes, never to an imagined box.

## When to use

- A new product, or an existing pack that says too much or too little.
- A range moving into shops where every variant must read as one family.
- Insert cards, sleeve copy, sticker lines and thank-you notes.

Not for: legal sign-off, health or medical claims, or claims the client cannot back. Detailed claim rules (Made in USA, eco, free-from, reviews) are in the ecommerce-copywriter kit's claims-and-label-check skill; use it for any claim beyond plain product facts.

## What you need

- **Panel sizes** from the dieline or the panel map (see panel-map-and-layout-spec). Without them, ask before writing.
- **Product facts:** what it is, recipe or materials, how to use, storage, warnings, net quantity, address. Required details come from the client and are not rewritten.
- **Where it sells:** shelf, online or both, and which countries.
- **Brand voice:** a few lines the client likes, words they never use.
- **Evidence** for anything that sounds like a claim: certificates, test results, sourcing records.
- **Competitor packs:** photos of the shelf it will sit on.
- **Access I have:** level 1 for the whole skill. Pasting the copy into a draft design needs level 2. Changing required wording needs the client's fresh yes, at any level.

## Steps

1. **List the space.** For each zone on each panel, note its size and a rough character budget at the planned type size. Front zones are small; plan in words, not sentences.
2. **List the facts the label must carry** for the product type and market (see required-label-details-check). These go in first, exactly as supplied, tagged "you supply and check".
3. **Find the one reason to buy.** From the facts and the shelf photos: what is true, specific and different? Write five options, then pick one main claim and at most two support points.
4. **Write the front.** Order: brand, product name and product type (a shopper must know what it is), variant, main claim, up to two support points. Use the client's real words for the product type; a clever name without the plain type fails the thumbnail test.
5. **Write the back.** A short story (two to four sentences), how to use, ingredients or materials in the required form, storage, warnings, contact block, and any recycling line from the client's scheme. Keep the story off the US information panel cluster, so it does not sit between required details.
6. **Fit each block.** Count characters per zone. Mark any block that is over its budget and cut it, starting with adjectives and repeated ideas.
7. **Check every claim.** For each word a shopper could check (origin, "natural", "eco", "recyclable", "healthy", "free from", "handmade", "award-winning"), record the evidence or move it to "hold". Avoid vague words such as "chemical-free" and "all-natural" unless the client's adviser approves them, because they are hard to prove. In the US, "healthy" on food has its own FDA rule; check it before use.
8. **Run the thumbnail test.** Picture the front at small online size. Only the product type, variant and one claim will read. If the main claim does not, shorten it.
9. **For a range:** fix the naming pattern ("[Brand] [Variant] [Product type]"), the order of claims, a list of descriptors per variant, and a back-panel template with fixed headings.
10. **Give two front-claim options to test,** with the evidence for each.
11. **Level 2:** paste the approved copy into the draft layout, marked "DRAFT". Never send copy to a printer without the client's approval.

## Checks

- Every block fits its zone, with character counts shown.
- Product type is on the front in plain words.
- One main claim, no more than two support points.
- Required wording unchanged and tagged for the client.
- Every claim has evidence or is held.
- No word from the client's "never" list.
- Range copy follows the pattern on every variant.

## Output format

1. **Access I have.**
2. **Copy deck by panel:** zone, copy ID, text, characters used and budget.
3. **Required details** in their own list, unchanged, tagged "you supply and check".
4. **Claims log:** claim, evidence, status (keep, narrow, hold, cut).
5. **Two front-claim options to test.**
6. **Range pattern** (if relevant).
7. **Questions for the client.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a candle maker with a 200 g soy candle in a 70 x 70 x 90 mm box, sold in UK shops and online. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Front (panel A, 70 x 90 mm)**

| ID | Zone | Copy | Chars | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Top | [BUSINESS] | [count] | 14 |
| F2 | Centre | Fig & Cedar | 11 | 16 |
| F3 | Centre | Scented soy candle | 18 | 22 |
| F4 | Lower | 45-hour burn | 12 | 16 |
| F5 | Base | 200 g | 5 | 8 |

**Back (panel B):** "Poured by hand in our Leeds studio, in small batches. Ripe fig over a dry cedar base." (story, 83 chars). Burn instructions and safety warnings as supplied, unchanged, tagged "you supply and check".

**Claims log:**

| Claim | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "45-hour burn" | Client's burn test log, 3 candles | Keep |
| "Eco wax" | None | Cut: no fixed meaning, no proof |
| "Poured by hand" | Studio process | Keep |

**Two front options to test:** F4 "45-hour burn" or F4 "Poured by hand in Leeds".
