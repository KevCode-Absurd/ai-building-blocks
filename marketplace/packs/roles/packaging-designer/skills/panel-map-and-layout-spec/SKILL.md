# Panel map and layout spec

A panel map is the plan a designer or printer builds from. It says what goes on every face of the pack, in what order, at what size, and why. It is written so someone who has never met me can build it without a question. With level 2 access in a connected design tool I can also build a draft; the client's designer or printer makes the final artwork.

## When to use

- A new box, pouch, jar label, bottle wrap, tube, sleeve or bag.
- A maker moving from home-printed labels to printed packs.
- A range of flavours or scents that needs one system.
- A pack whose front panel is cluttered, or a reprint after a required-detail change.

Not for: drawing the dieline itself, structural engineering, or legal sign-off on labels.

## What you need

- **The printer's dieline** with its version or date, flat size, folds, glue flaps, seals and gussets. If missing, ask for it and stop at a rough plan.
- **Approved copy by panel** (see the on-pack-copy-by-panel skill) and the required details list (see required-label-details-check).
- **Markets and channels:** US, UK, EU or several; shelf, online or both.
- **Brand files:** logo, colours with CMYK or Pantone values, fonts, product photos.
- **The barcode** (GTIN for this variant) and any recycling label from the client's scheme.
- **Access I have:** level 1 is enough for steps 1 to 9. Step 10 (a draft in the design tool) needs level 2.

## Steps

1. **Read the dieline.** Note flat size, the size of each face, folds, glue flaps, seals, zip and tear notch on pouches, and areas hidden when closed. Mark the printer's bleed and safe area.
2. **Label the panels A to F.** For example: A front, B back, C left side, D right side, E top, F bottom. For a wrap label: front zone, back zone, overlap. Write which face the shopper sees on the shelf, which in the hand, which at home.
3. **Give each panel one job.** Front: what it is, the brand, one reason to buy. Back or information panel: details, how to use, required information. Sides: the range, the story, recycling. Top: the name again if the pack is shelved lying down.
4. **Place required details first.**
   - *US food:* statement of identity and net quantity on the principal display panel (PDP), net quantity in the bottom 30% of the PDP, parallel to the base, in metric and US units. Work out PDP area (height x width; for a cylinder 40% of height x circumference) and set the net quantity size from the FDA table. Group name and address, ingredients, nutrition and allergens on the information panel (the panel immediately right of the PDP), with nothing in between; the barcode goes elsewhere. Other required text at least 1/16 inch on the lower-case "o".
   - *UK and EU food:* name, net quantity and (for drinks over 1.2%) alcohol in one field of vision. Mandatory text x-height at least 1.2 mm, or 0.9 mm if the largest surface is under 80 cm2. Allergens emphasised in the ingredient list. If using the e-mark, place it next to the quantity, at least 3 mm high.
   - *US non-food:* identity, net contents, and name and place of business.
   - Tag each one "you supply and check".
5. **Place the barcode.** On the back, lower right, on a flat face away from folds, seals, curves and edges, with quiet zones clear. Black on white. Never inside the US information panel cluster.
6. **Place the copy.** Every line from the copy deck, in reading order, with its type style and size. Do not change required wording; suggestions go in a separate note.
7. **Set the front-panel hierarchy.** Brand, product name and product type, flavour or variant, one reason to buy. No more than two support points. Keep brand assets (logo, colour, shape) in the same place across the range.
8. **Test two distances.** Shelf: can the name and product type be read from about two metres, and the variant told apart from its neighbours? Online: shrink the front to a small thumbnail; does the product type still read? If not, cut words before shrinking type.
9. **Write print notes.** Dieline version, bleed, safe area from folds and seals, colour mode, white layer on clear or metallic film, finishes (matte, gloss, foil, spot varnish), material, and the printer's black values for text and barcode.
10. **Level 2: build a draft.** In a connected tool, build the layout on the dieline in a file named "DRAFT". Do not share, order or send it. Log what was created.
11. **For a range:** write the shared grid (fixed zones, logo spot, type sizes) and the rules that change per product (colour band, flavour name, photo), plus a side-by-side check of all variants.

## Checks

- Every panel has a job, and nothing important sits on a glue flap, seal or fold.
- Required details placed and sized for each market; US PDP and information panel rules met; UK and EU field of vision and x-height met.
- Barcode placed to GS1 advice, correct GTIN for this variant.
- Every copy line placed exactly as approved.
- Front panel passes the shelf and thumbnail tests.
- Range variants share the grid and differ only by the agreed rules.
- Nothing new claimed.

## Output format

1. **Access I have** and the dieline version used.
2. **Panel map:** a text sketch with panels A to F and when the shopper sees each.
3. **One table per panel:** zone, content (copy ID), type style and size, notes.
4. **Required details list:** item, panel, minimum size, rule, "you supply and check".
5. **Barcode and recycling label spaces.**
6. **Shelf and thumbnail test notes.**
7. **Print notes.**
8. **Range system** (if relevant).
9. **Open questions** and the action log.

## Example of good output (short)

*Kettle Hill Preserves is a made-up business used only to show the format: a 340 g glass jar of raspberry jam sold in the US, with a front label and a back label. Access I have: Canva level 1.*

**Panel map:** A front label 90 x 70 mm (shelf); B back label 90 x 70 mm (in the hand). Jar is a cylinder 85 mm tall, 255 mm round; the front label is the PDP.

**PDP area:** cylinder rule, 40% x 8.5 cm x 25.5 cm = about 87 cm2 (13.4 sq in). That is over 5 and up to 25 sq in, so the net quantity is at least 1/8 inch (3.2 mm) high. Client to confirm the measure with their adviser.

| Panel | Zone | Content | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Top band | Logo | 18 mm wide | Same spot on all flavours |
| A | Centre | C1 "Raspberry Jam" (identity) | Largest type | Variant colour band behind |
| A | Lower | C2 "Small-batch, 60% fruit" | Middle | Client holds the recipe evidence |
| A | Bottom 30% | "Net wt 12 oz (340 g)" | At least 3.2 mm | You supply and check |
| B | Top | Ingredients, Contains statement | At least 1/16 in "o" | You supply and check |
| B | Middle | Nutrition Facts, name and address | As FDA format | No barcode between these |
| B | Lower right | Barcode, GTIN for 340 g raspberry | GS1 size | Quiet zones clear |

**Thumbnail test:** "Raspberry Jam" still reads at small size; "Small-batch" does not. Keep it, it is a support point.
