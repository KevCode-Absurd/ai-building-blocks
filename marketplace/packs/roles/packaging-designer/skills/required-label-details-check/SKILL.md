# Required label details check

This skill lists what a pack is expected to carry and checks where and how big it is. It never decides whether a label is legal. Every item is tagged "you supply and check", with the official guidance to read. For anything uncertain, the client asks their regulator, trading standards office or a qualified adviser.

## When to use

- Before a panel map or copy deck, to know what must fit.
- Before a reprint, after a recipe, supplier, size or address change.
- When a product moves into a new market or channel.
- When a printed pack needs a check against the rules.

## What you need

- Product type (food, drink, cosmetic, candle, pet food, household product), recipe or materials, net quantity, and markets.
- The current pack or draft layout with sizes.
- How the product is sold: prepacked, prepacked for direct sale, online.
- Recycling scheme membership and EPR status, if known.
- **Research mode:** with web search connected, open the current official page for each market and note the date. Without it, use the rules below (checked 6 Nov 2026) and mark each as "confirm current rule".
- **Access I have:** level 1 is enough. Changing required wording on a live file needs the client's fresh yes at any level.

## Steps

1. **Pick the rule set** for each market and product type.
2. **List the items.**
   - *US food (FDA Food Labeling Guide):* statement of identity and net quantity on the principal display panel (PDP); net quantity in the bottom 30% of the PDP, metric and US units, minimum height set by PDP area; information panel with name and address, ingredient list, Nutrition Facts and allergens grouped with nothing between them; other required text at least 1/16 inch. Nine major allergens, declared in brackets or a "Contains" statement, naming the type of tree nut, fish or shellfish.
   - *US non-food (FTC Fair Packaging and Labeling Act):* identity of the product, net contents, name and place of business. Cosmetics, drugs and devices follow FDA rules; send the client to the FDA's own pages.
   - *UK food:* name; ingredients with the 14 allergens emphasised; quantitative ingredient declaration where needed; net quantity in metric; best before or use by; business name and address; alcohol over 1.2%; plus the items GOV.UK's full guidance adds for some foods (check it for this product). Mandatory text at least 1.2 mm x-height (0.9 mm on packs whose largest surface is under 80 cm2). Name, net quantity and alcohol in the same field of vision. Northern Ireland: EU rules and an NI or EU address. Food made and packed on the premises (prepacked for direct sale) needs a full ingredient list with allergens emphasised.
   - *EU food:* the same core list, with an EU business name and address, any national warnings, and the same x-height rules. Packs under 10 cm2 need only name, allergens, net quantity and date mark. Language: check the rules of each country of sale.
   - *Quantity marks:* the e-mark is voluntary; if used, at least 3 mm high, next to the quantity, and the packer follows the average quantity system.
   - *Barcode:* a GS1 GTIN for this exact variant, placed on a flat back face, lower right, with quiet zones.
   - *Recycling:* a label only from the client's scheme (OPRL in the UK, How2Recycle in the US and Canada) or a claim the client can back.
   - *Packaging data:* for UK EPR, each component's material and weight, and whether it is household packaging; for the EU, the countries where the packaging becomes waste.
3. **Place each item** on the panel map with its minimum size and rule.
4. **Check the draft or printed pack** item by item: present, right panel, right size, unchanged wording, emphasis where needed.
5. **List what is missing** and who supplies it.
6. **Name the guidance to read** for each market.

## Checks

- Every market the product sells in has its own list.
- Each item has a panel, a minimum size and a "you supply and check" tag.
- No required wording rewritten.
- Sizes measured the way the rule measures them (x-height in the UK and EU, lower-case "o" in the US).
- The note says plainly that this is general guidance, not legal approval.

## Output format

1. **Rule set used** and the date each rule was checked, or "confirm current rule".
2. **Required details table:** item, rule, panel, minimum size, status (present, missing, wrong place, too small), who supplies.
3. **Fixes** in order of risk: allergens and safety first, then quantity and identity, then the rest.
4. **Guidance to read:** FDA Food Labeling Guide and allergen page; FTC Fair Packaging and Labeling Act; GOV.UK food labelling, net quantity and FSA allergen guidance; Your Europe food labelling and e-mark pages; GS1; the client's recycling scheme; GOV.UK packaging EPR or the EU country's register.
5. **A line saying who signs off:** the client, with their regulator or adviser.

## Example of good output (short)

*Copperleaf Chai is a made-up business used only to show the format: a 100 g stand-up pouch of loose chai blend, sold in the UK and Ireland.*

| Item | Rule | Panel | Minimum size | Status | Who supplies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name "Masala Chai Loose Tea" | UK and EU food | Front | 1.2 mm x-height | Present | Client |
| Net quantity "100 g" | Same field of vision as name | Front | 1.2 mm x-height | On back: move to front | Client |
| Ingredients, allergens emphasised | 14 allergens | Back | 1.2 mm x-height | Present, no emphasis on "milk" | Client confirms |
| Best before and batch | Date mark | Base gusset | 1.2 mm x-height | Space marked | Printer or packer |
| Business address | UK address; EU or NI address for Ireland | Back | 1.2 mm x-height | UK only: add EU address for Ireland | Client |
| Barcode | GS1 GTIN for 100 g | Back, lower right | GS1 size | Over the side seal: move | Client |
| Recycling label | OPRL member? | Back | Scheme rules | Unknown | Client |

**Fixes:** emphasise "milk" in the ingredients; move "100 g" to the front; add the EU address for Ireland; move the barcode off the seal.

This is general guidance, not legal approval. Please confirm with your adviser or local trading standards before print.
