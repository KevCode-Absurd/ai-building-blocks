# Interview: packaging designer

The training coach asks these questions one at a time and writes down the answers in the expert's own words. Ask for a real example whenever an answer stays general.

1. **Walk me through the last pack you took from brief to printed stock. What happened, step by step?**
   Why it matters: shows the real order of work, where the waiting is, and which steps the expert never skips.

2. **What do you ask for before you lay out anything, and what do you do when the client cannot give it?**
   Why it matters: reveals the expert's intake list and how they handle a missing dieline or missing facts.

3. **How do you read a dieline? What do you mark first?**
   Why it matters: captures the habits that stop text landing on folds, seals and glue flaps.

4. **How do you decide what goes on the front panel and what moves to the back or sides?**
   Why it matters: the front-panel rules are the core of the craft and differ between experts.

5. **How do you test a front panel for the shelf and for a small online thumbnail?**
   Why it matters: turns a vague "make it stand out" into a repeatable check.

6. **Which required details do you place first, and how do you size them for the US, the UK and the EU?**
   Why it matters: shows whether the expert works from the rules (PDP, information panel, x-height, field of vision) or from habit.

7. **Tell me about a label error that reached print, or nearly did. How was it caught, and what do you check now because of it?**
   Why it matters: the best checklists come from real near-misses.

8. **How do you handle a client who wants a claim on the front that they cannot back up?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert protects the client without losing the relationship.

9. **What do you check on a printer's proof, and in what order?**
   Why it matters: proof checks are the last chance before money is spent.

10. **Which printer rules trip people up most: white layers, black values, bleed, resolution, file types? Give an example.**
    Why it matters: captures printer-specific knowledge an AI cannot guess.

11. **How do you set up a range so ten variants look like one family but each is easy to find?**
    Why it matters: range systems are a common job and need clear, transferable rules.

12. **How do you place and check barcodes? Has one ever failed to scan?**
    Why it matters: barcode faults cause rework and retailer complaints; the expert's checks are worth writing down.

13. **How do you decide whether a pack can carry a recycling label, and which one?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert uses schemes such as OPRL or How2Recycle and avoids decorative symbols.

14. **How do material choices affect your designs now, given packaging EPR and recyclability ratings?**
    Why it matters: design decisions now change fees and data; the expert's trade-offs are valuable.

15. **What do you hand to the printer and to the client at the end of a job?**
    Why it matters: defines the handoff pack the agent should produce.

16. **Which tools do you use for dielines, artwork and proofs, and what do you let others do in them?**
    Why it matters: maps the tools and the access levels the expert is comfortable granting.

17. **When do you say no to a job, or send the client to someone else?**
    Why it matters: sets the edges of the role (structural engineering, legal sign-off, regulated products).

18. **How do you write copy for a back panel that has very little room?**
    Why it matters: captures the cutting habits that make dense panels readable.

19. **What does a client usually misunderstand about packaging, and how do you explain it?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent ready explanations in the expert's voice.

20. **What would you check first if a pack was selling poorly in shops?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert separates pack problems from price, placement or product problems.
