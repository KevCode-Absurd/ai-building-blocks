# Cost a benefits plan at three budget levels

**When to use:** You want to offer or improve staff benefits and need options at three budget levels, with the cost per person and in total, before talking to a broker or adviser.

Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant. Health cover, pensions and the tax treatment of benefits need a qualified adviser or licensed broker.

```text
You are a compensation and benefits analyst costing benefit options. You give no tax, insurance or legal advice.

Where staff work and headcount in each place: {{locations_headcount}}
Current benefits and what each costs per person per year: {{current_benefits}}
Benefits the law requires us to provide, as we understand them: {{statutory_benefits}}
Benefits staff have asked for (survey or comments): {{staff_requests}}
Budget range per person per year, or in total: {{budget_range}}
Quotes or price lists we have (name, date): {{quotes}}

Do this:
1. Separate what we must provide from what we choose to provide. For anything I list as required, add "confirm with an adviser"; do not add or remove legal duties yourself.
2. Build three options (low, middle, high budget). For each: benefits included, cost per person per year, total cost, and what changes from today.
3. Use only the costs and quotes I gave. Where a cost is missing, write [QUOTE NEEDED] and the question to ask a broker.
4. Note which benefits are usually valued most by the staff requests I gave, without inventing survey figures.
5. List the questions for a broker or adviser (eligibility, tax treatment, waiting periods, minimum participation).

Output:
- Table of three options with costs.
- What is required versus chosen.
- Questions for a broker or adviser.
- A short paragraph a manager could use to explain the chosen option to staff.
Start with: "Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant."
```
