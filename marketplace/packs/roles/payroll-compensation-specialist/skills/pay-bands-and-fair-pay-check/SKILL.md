# Pay bands and fair-pay check

Pay that can be explained is pay people can trust. This skill groups roles into levels, sets bands from named market data, shows where each person sits, and finds gaps between similar roles before staff do. It proposes ranges; the client decides every person's pay.

Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant. Equal pay and discrimination questions go to an employment lawyer.

## When to use

- First pay structure for a growing team.
- Before an annual pay review or a round of raises.
- A manager asks why two roles, or two people, are paid differently.
- Before hiring into a place that requires pay ranges in job ads.
- Before gender pay gap reporting or EU pay transparency rules apply.

## What you need

1. A pay file: employee ID, role, level (if any), location, hours (full or part time), base pay, variable pay, start date. No names needed.
2. What each role owns and decides (a line or two per role).
3. Market data with source, date, location and sample: for example BLS OEWS in the US, ONS earnings data in the UK, or a paid survey.
4. The budget for changes, and the pay position wanted (at market, above, below).
5. Where staff work, and headcount by country.
6. For the fair-pay check, any group data the client lawfully holds and chooses to share for this purpose, kept separate and minimised.

**Access:** level 1 to read the HR or payroll system and the pay file; level 2 to build the band model and draft documents in a copy; level 3 to publish the approved band document where managers can see it, within limits. Entering any person's pay change in payroll needs the client's yes to that exact change.

## Steps

### 1. Level the roles
Group roles by scope: what the role is accountable for, the decisions it makes, the expertise it needs, and who it manages. Keep the number of levels small. Write one sentence per level that a manager can apply.

### 2. Match to market data
For each level and location, match each role to the closest occupation in each source. Record source, date, percentile used and any adjustment (for example ageing older data to the current date using a stated rate the client chooses). Do not use a source with no name, date or sample.

### 3. Set midpoints and widths
Set a midpoint per level from the matched market data and the client's pay position. Choose a band width per level and write the reason: wider where people grow a lot within the role, narrower where the going rate is clear. Check that bands for adjacent levels step up in a sensible way.

### 4. Place every person
For each person: band minimum, midpoint and maximum; compa-ratio (pay ÷ midpoint); range penetration (how far from minimum to maximum). Pro-rate part-time pay to a full-time equivalent before comparing. Flag anyone below minimum or above maximum and list likely reasons (new to role, long service, market shift, hired in a tight market) without naming a fix for any person.

### 5. Fair-pay check
- Compare people in the same role and level, and roles of similar scope across the business.
- For each gap, record the job-related factors that might explain it (experience in role, location, performance rating with evidence, hours) and whether a written reason exists.
- List gaps with no written reason for the owner to review.
- Report group figures only where groups are large enough that no one can be identified.
- Note the rules that may apply: in the US, equal pay for substantially equal work judged by job content, with "pay" including bonuses and benefits; in Great Britain, gender pay gap reporting at 250 or more employees; in the EU, the pay transparency directive as transposed in each country.

### 6. Movement rules
Write how people move within a band (for example by assessed performance and time in role) and between levels (a change in scope, not tenure alone). Write how the bands will be reviewed and when.

### 7. Cost the options
Show the cost of bringing everyone below minimum up to minimum, and of any other options the client wants, against the budget.

### 8. Write it up
A plain document managers can use to explain pay: levels, bands, how pay moves, what the bands are based on, and who to ask.

## Checks

- Every band names its market source, date and percentile.
- Compa-ratios and totals are correct, with part-time pay pro-rated.
- Gaps are listed with factors and whether a written reason exists, never with a protected trait as the reason.
- No individual is identifiable in group figures.
- No person's pay is set; options are costed for the owner to decide.
- The document carries: *Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant.*

## Output format

1. Level definitions
2. Band table: level, minimum, midpoint, maximum, width reason, market source and date
3. Placement table: ID, role, level, pay (full-time equivalent), compa-ratio, range penetration, flag
4. Fair-pay check: comparisons, gaps, factors, written reason yes or no
5. Cost of options against budget
6. Movement and review rules
7. Open questions for an adviser or employment lawyer
8. Data used and its limits
