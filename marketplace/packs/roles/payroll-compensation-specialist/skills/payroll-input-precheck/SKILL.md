# Payroll input pre-check

Most payroll mistakes are input mistakes. This skill catches them before payday by comparing this period with the last, checking every change has a source and an approval, and asking short, specific questions. The client runs payroll and pays people; this skill prepares and checks.

Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant. This skill gives no tax, pension or legal advice.

## When to use

- Before every pay run, after the change cut-off.
- After a month with several starters, leavers or raises.
- When overtime, shift or commission pay is involved.
- When the same kinds of error keep showing up on payslips.

## What you need

1. Pay period dates, pay frequency, payday and the change cut-off.
2. This period's payroll input or draft register and last period's final register (CSV or spreadsheet), with employee IDs.
3. The change log: starters (start date, rate, hours), leavers (last day, final pay items), pay changes (new rate, effective date, approver), and one-off payments.
4. Timesheets or hours for hourly staff.
5. The client's written rules: overtime, breaks, shift premiums, rounding, leave and sick pay policy.
6. Commission or bonus calculations, if paid this period.
7. The threshold for flagging changes (for example any change over a set amount or percentage the client chooses).
8. Where staff work: country, state or province.

**Access:** level 1 to read the payroll system, time tracking, HR system and commission sheet; level 2 to enter the approved changes in the unsubmitted pay run and attach notes; level 3 to apply manager-approved timesheet corrections or update named non-pay records within limits. Approving or submitting the pay run, paying anyone and changing bank details stay with the client at every level.

## Steps

### 1. Confirm the frame
Write down the period, payday, cut-off, pay frequency and the rules in use. If the rules are not written, list them as [NEEDED] and use only what the client states.

### 2. Compare person by person
Join this period and last period on employee ID. For each person show gross pay, hours, rate and main additions and deductions, with the change in amount and percent. Flag anyone over the client's threshold, and anyone paid this period but not last (should be a starter) or last period but not this (should be a leaver or unpaid leave).

### 3. Check people lists
- Every starter has a start date, rate, hours or salary, and an approval. First-period pay matches the start date (part period).
- Every leaver has a last day, and no pay after it except agreed final items. Check no leaver remains on the regular run.
- No one appears twice (same name or ID, or same bank reference flagged by the payroll system).

### 4. Check timesheets
- Gaps, overlaps and duplicate lines.
- Hours over the client's daily or weekly limits, and overtime against the client's rule.
- Breaks against the client's rule. For US staff, note that short rest breaks (usually 20 minutes or less) normally count as hours worked under federal rules, and meal breaks are unpaid only when the person is fully relieved of duty; state rules may differ.
- Entries after a leaving date or before a start date.
- Unapproved timesheets.

### 5. Check pay changes
Each change has a written approval, an effective date and the right new rate. Raises that start mid-period are split correctly. Flag any change with no approval; keep it out of the draft pay run until the client confirms.

### 6. Check variable pay
- Commissions and bonuses match the approved calculation sheet and the plan.
- Refunded or cancelled deals are handled as the plan says.
- For non-exempt US staff, flag where a non-discretionary bonus or commission falls in a period with overtime, because it may change the overtime rate.
- For UK staff, flag where commission or bonus may change pension contributions.

### 7. Rank and ask
List items in order of money at stake. One plain question per item, addressed to the person who can answer it (manager, HR, owner).

### 8. Say what was not checked
Tax codes, tax withholding, benefit deductions, garnishments, pension scheme rules, statutory pay calculations and anything outside the files received. These go to the payroll provider or adviser.

### 9. Prepare (level 2)
Enter only the changes that are approved and confirmed into the unsubmitted pay run, attach the question list, and log each entry. Tell the client the run is ready for their review and submission.

## Checks

- Totals of the comparison table tie to both registers.
- Every flagged item shows the amount at stake and the source line.
- No change without an approval went into the draft pay run.
- Questions are neutral and specific: what was found, what is needed.
- No names where IDs will do; no bank or government ID numbers in the output.
- The output carries: *Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant.*
- Nothing was submitted, approved or paid.

## Output format

1. Summary: period, headcount paid, gross total against last period, number of items to confirm, total money at stake
2. Items to confirm (ranked): ID, item, amount at stake, what I found, question, who should answer
3. Changes entered in the draft pay run (level 2), with approval reference
4. Person-by-person comparison (appendix)
5. What I could not check
6. Action log

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bakery, 23 staff, monthly payroll in England. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Summary:** October payroll, 23 people, gross £48,610 (September £46,980, +£1,630, +3.5%). 5 items to confirm, £3,046 at stake.
>
> | # | ID | Item | At stake | Found | Question (to) |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | E-019 | Leaver still on run | £1,840 | HR shows last day 30 Sep; October salary present | Should E-019 be removed except final holiday pay? (HR) |
> | 2 | E-031 | Starter rate | £860 | Start 14 Oct, rate blank, payroll default used | What is the agreed hourly rate? Offer letter not in file (owner) |
> | 3 | E-007 | Raise without approval | £176 | Rate up from £13.10 to £14.20 from 1 Oct, 160 hours | Who approved this, and from what date? (owner) |
> | 4 | E-012 | Duplicate shift | £106 | Two identical lines, 22 Oct, 06:00 to 14:00 | Was this one shift? (shift manager) |
> | 5 | E-022 | Commission and pension | £64 | £800 sales bonus; pension calculated on salary only; 8% of £800 = £64 | Does your scheme use qualifying earnings including bonus? (payroll provider) |
>
> **Not checked:** tax codes, student loan deductions, statutory pay.
