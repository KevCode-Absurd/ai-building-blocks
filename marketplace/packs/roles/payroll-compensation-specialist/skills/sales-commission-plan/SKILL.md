# Sales commission plan

A commission plan is a promise written in maths. This skill designs one that pays for the results the business wants, shows its cost in good and bad years, and explains itself to the people paid by it. It gives no tax or legal advice.

Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant. Have an employment lawyer review the final plan wording.

## When to use

- Hiring the first salespeople, or adding a new sales role (SDR, account executive, account manager).
- Reps hit target but the business loses money on discounted or refunded deals.
- Payouts surprise the owner or the reps.
- Before the new plan year.

## What you need

1. The role and what the business wants from it: new customers, renewals, expansion, margin, multi-year deals.
2. Targets: revenue or gross profit for the role, deal size, margin, sales cycle, win rate, pipeline per rep, and ramp time for new hires.
3. Pay market data for the role (named, dated) and the budget for total cost of sales.
4. Last year's deals and payouts if a plan exists, with refunds, cancellations and discounts.
5. How revenue is booked and collected, and when payroll runs.
6. Where reps work (country, state).

**Access:** level 1 to read CRM deals, billing and last year's payouts; level 2 to build the model and plan document in a copy and prepare each period's payout file for approval; level 3 to publish the approved plan document to the agreed reps' folder within limits. Paying commissions stays with the client through payroll at every level.

## Steps

### 1. Write the job of the plan
One sentence: "Pay account executives for new annual contracts at healthy margin, collected." Every later choice is tested against it.

### 2. Set on-target earnings and pay mix
OTE comes from the pay market data for the role and location. The base and variable split depends on how much the rep controls the sale: more variable where the rep finds and closes deals alone, more base where the sale is long, team-based or service-heavy. Write the reason for the split chosen; do not borrow a "standard" split without a source.

### 3. Set quota from capacity
Work out what a fully ramped rep can close: pipeline per rep × win rate × average deal size, over the plan period. Set quota so most reps can reach it, and lower it during ramp months. Show the working. If the target the owner wants is above capacity, say so and show the gap.

### 4. Choose the rate and its shape
- Base rate = variable pay at target ÷ quota (so 100% of quota pays the full variable amount).
- Threshold: whether anything is paid below a minimum attainment.
- Accelerators above quota, and whether they are limited. Explain the cost of each.
- Caps: if any, say why; caps can push reps to hold deals back.
- Measure: pay on gross profit or collected revenue instead of booked revenue where discounts or refunds are common.

### 5. Write the edge rules
Split deals (who gets what share), refunds and cancellations (clawback period and method), deals closed after a rep leaves or earned before they leave, draws for new hires, disputes and who decides, plan changes mid-year, and when commission is paid (for example the month after cash is collected).

### 6. Check the payroll links
- US: commissions are supplemental wages for federal withholding; for non-exempt reps they also feed the regular rate for overtime.
- UK: commission can count toward pension qualifying earnings.
- Everywhere: the payroll set-up must match the plan's timing and clawback rules.
Mark each as a point to confirm with the payroll adviser; do not decide treatment.

### 7. Model the payouts
For a typical rep, show earnings and cost to the business at 50%, 100% and 150% of quota: base, commission, total, and cost of sales as a share of the revenue or profit counted. Add the whole team's cost at the same levels. Test one bad case: a large deal refunded after payout.

### 8. Write the plan document
Plain words: how the rep earns, the rate, two or three worked examples with real numbers, the edge rules, when they are paid, who to ask. Leave space for acknowledgement. Whether a signed written plan is required is a question for local rules.

### 9. Review an existing plan (if asked)
Run last year's deals through the plan. Show what it rewarded and what it ignored (discounts, churn, refunds), the cost trend, and three changes with their effect on cost and on rep pay.

## Checks

- The plan's job is stated, and every choice links to it.
- Quota comes from capacity, with the working shown.
- At 100% of quota the plan pays exactly the variable part of OTE.
- The 50%, 100% and 150% model adds up and shows cost of sales.
- Every edge case in step 5 has a rule.
- Payroll links are flagged for the adviser, with no tax or legal ruling.
- The document carries: *Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant.*

## Output format

1. The plan on one page: job of the plan, OTE, pay mix, quota, rate, accelerators, threshold, caps, measure, payment timing
2. Reasons for each choice
3. Capacity and quota working
4. Payout model at 50%, 100% and 150% (rep and team)
5. Edge rules
6. Payroll points for the adviser
7. Plan document for reps with worked examples
8. Review findings and three changes (for an existing plan)

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a software firm hiring its first two account executives. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Job of the plan:** new annual contracts at 70% gross margin, paid when the customer pays.
> **OTE** $150,000, split $90,000 base and $60,000 variable, because reps run their own deals but the sale is long and needs product help.
> **Quota:** pipeline of $1.8m a year per rep × 33% win rate = about $600,000 of new annual contract value. Quota $600,000; $300,000 in the first six months of ramp.
> **Rate:** $60,000 ÷ $600,000 = 10% of collected first-year contract value; 15% on everything above quota; no cap.
>
> | Attainment | Commission | Total pay | Cost of sales (% of new revenue) |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 50% ($300k) | $30,000 | $120,000 | 40% |
> | 100% ($600k) | $60,000 | $150,000 | 25% |
> | 150% ($900k) | $105,000 | $195,000 | 21.7% |
>
> **Clawback:** if a customer cancels within 90 days, the commission on that contract comes back from the next payout.
>
> *Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified payroll adviser or accountant.*
