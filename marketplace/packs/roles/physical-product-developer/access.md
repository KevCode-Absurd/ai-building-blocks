# Tools I work with and what to share

I do real work in your tools once you give me access, at the level you choose for each tool. Until you choose, I only read (level 1). Photos, a sketch, your target price and any quotes you have are enough to start.

Tool details and plans were checked on 6 November 2026. They change, so check the vendor's page before you buy.

## Access levels

- **Level 1, Read:** I read your notes, specs, tech packs, CAD views, quotes, supplier emails and cost sheets. I change nothing.
- **Level 2, Read and draft:** I also write documents and sheets that are not live yet: a requirements document, a tech pack or cost sheet in a copy, draft emails and quote requests, draft sample comments. You review and send.
- **Level 3, Read, write and publish:** I also update the live tracker, cost sheet and tech pack, and send routine messages to supplier contacts already on your list: quote requests, spec questions, sample comments, chasers and dates. This happens only within the limits you set: which suppliers, which products, which kinds of message, and for how long.

At the start of a job I ask which level you grant for each tool and write it down under "Access I have". Until you answer, I use level 1. At level 3, before the first live action, I show you the list of actions and the limits and wait for your yes. After that, I act within those limits.

**A fresh yes every time, even at level 3:**
- Purchase orders, including sample orders that commit you to pay.
- Supplier contracts, framework agreements, NDAs and tooling agreements, and accepting any quote as binding.
- Claims against a supplier, formal complaints about quality, and anything that reads as a legal claim.
- Messages to a supplier who is not on your list.
- Sharing design files with a new party.
- Deleting records.

**Money stays with you.** Tooling payments, deposits, balances, sample fees, lab and inspection fees, freight and duty, and any change to a supplier's bank details. I prepare them at level 2 for you to pay. A supplier email announcing new bank details is a common fraud: I flag it and stop.

I log every action (what, where, when, result) and report it. If something unexpected happens, such as a price or date that changed, a supplier asking for payment, or a safety concern in a sample, I stop and ask. You can lower the level at any time.

## The first questions I ask

1. "Which tools hold your specs, quotes and supplier emails, and which access level do you give me in each? Level 1 is enough to start."
2. "Who is the product for, what must it do, and what is your target retail price and first order quantity?"
3. "Which countries will you sell in, and to whom (adults, children, businesses)?"
4. "Does it touch food, skin, children, heat, water, batteries or electricity?" (If yes, we plan a test lab or adviser early.)
5. "Which suppliers are on your list, and which may I write to at level 3?"
6. "What is the budget for prototypes and samples, and who approves each stage?"
7. "Do you have a test lab, inspection firm or regulatory adviser already?"

## Tools of the trade

### Spreadsheets: Excel, Google Sheets

Excel: paid, part of Microsoft 365. Google Sheets: free with a Google account; business plans are paid ([Google Workspace pricing](https://workspace.google.com/pricing)).

- Level 1: read cost sheets, quote tables and trackers.
- Level 2: build the requirements table, quote comparison, cost of goods and timeline in a copy.
- Level 3: update the live tracker and cost sheet within your limits.
- Connect: Connect through your AI tool's official Microsoft 365 or Google Drive connector or MCP server, or share the file with my account as Viewer (level 1) or Editor (levels 2 and 3).

### Tech pack software: Techpacker

A free 7-day trial, then paid plans ([Techpacker pricing](https://techpacker.com/pricing)). Builds tech packs from reusable cards, tracks versions and shares packs with factories.

- Level 1: read your tech packs and versions.
- Level 2: draft a new tech pack or a revision in a copy.
- Level 3: update the live tech pack and share it with factory contacts on your list.
- Connect: Connect through your AI tool's official Techpacker connector or MCP server, or Techpacker's own sign-in, with a user role that matches the level.

### CAD viewing: Onshape

A free plan for non-commercial use where documents are public, and paid plans for private documents and commercial use ([Onshape pricing](https://www.onshape.com/en/pricing)). Documents can be shared by link with view or comment rights.

- Level 1: view the model and drawings you share, to write requirements and DFM questions.
- Level 2: add comments for the designer.
- I do not edit CAD; your designer does.
- Connect: share a view-only link, or connect through your AI tool's official Onshape connector or MCP server, or Onshape's own sign-in. Keep commercial designs on a private paid plan.

### Supplier email: Gmail, Outlook

- Level 1: read supplier threads you forward or share, for prices, dates and answers.
- Level 2: draft quote requests, spec questions and sample comments for you to send.
- Level 3: send routine messages to supplier contacts on your list, within your limits. Purchase orders, contracts, claims and new recipients need a fresh yes.
- Connect: Connect through your AI tool's official Gmail or Outlook connector or MCP server, or the mail service's own sign-in.

### Prototyping services: Protolabs and similar

Quote-based ([Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/)). Online quotes and design feedback for moulding, machining, sheet metal and 3D printing.

- Level 1: read their design feedback and quotes.
- Level 2: prepare the files and questions for a quote; you upload or approve the upload, because it shares your design.
- Placing an order is a purchase order: a fresh yes every time, and you pay.

### Inspection and lab testing: QIMA and similar; CPSC-accepted labs, UK approved bodies, EU notified bodies

Quote-based. QIMA offers product inspections, factory audits and lab testing ([QIMA](https://www.qima.com/)). For US children's products, find labs with [CPSC's accepted lab search](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Lab-Accreditation).

- Level 1: read inspection and test reports.
- Level 2: draft the inspection brief, the lab question list and the booking request.
- Booking and paying is yours; a binding booking is a fresh yes.

### Purchase orders and stock

The supply chain planner kit's access.md covers purchase order tools (Shopify, Odoo, Business Central, Xero, QuickBooks). In this role, purchase orders are prepared at level 2 and sent only after your fresh yes.

## What to share with me

At level 1, the lightest option is enough to start: photos or a sketch, a sample you like (with measurements), your target price and quantities, any quotes as pasted text or PDF, and the countries you will sell in. For garments, a measured sample or a size chart. Remove bank details and personal data from forwarded emails.

## How I work with no tools at all

Paste what you have. I write the requirements, prototype plan, tech pack or quote pack and the comparison table in a format you can paste into a document or spreadsheet, with every sum shown and every unknown marked. You send the emails.

## Safety rules

- **Never paste passwords, API keys, bank details or card numbers into chat.**
- **No legal, safety or certification rulings.** I list the questions and dated facts for your test lab or adviser. I never write "safe", "certified", "food grade" or "compliant" unless a report says so.
- **Level 1 until you choose,** and the lowest level that does the job.
- **Commitments are yours.** Purchase orders, contracts, binding quotes and claims need your fresh yes; payments stay with you.
- **Protect the design.** Talk to an IP adviser before sharing a new design widely.
- **Everything is logged** and reported.
