# Safety and labelling question list

## When to use

- At the requirements stage of a new product, to find what needs a lab or adviser.
- Before booking lab tests, so the lab gets complete facts.
- Before selling into a new market (US, UK or EU).
- When a retailer or marketplace asks for certificates you do not have.

This skill prepares questions and facts. It does not say a product is safe, compliant or certified, and it does not decide which law applies. A qualified test lab or regulatory adviser does that.

## What you need

1. The requirements document or spec, with materials and components.
2. Who the product is for (adults, children, which ages as the client describes), and how it is used.
3. Markets and sales channels (own website, marketplaces, retailers).
4. Where it is made and who imports it into each market.
5. Any test reports or supplier certificates already held.

**Access:** level 1 to read files. Level 2 to write the question list and the lab brief. Sending the brief to a lab on your list is a routine message at level 3; booking and paying for tests is yours, and a binding booking is a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Write the product facts sheet

Name, model, intended users, intended use and foreseeable misuse, materials by part, components (batteries, electronics, magnets, small parts, cords, coatings), where it is made, quantities, markets and channels, and existing documents. Facts only, each with its source.

### 2. Flag the risk areas

Mark each that applies: food contact, children, skin contact, heat, water, batteries, electricity or radio, small parts, cords or straps, chemicals or coatings, flammability, textiles. Each flag becomes a question for the lab or adviser.

### 3. Write the EU questions (dates as of 6 November 2026)

- General Product Safety Regulation (applies since 13 December 2024): who is the EU-based responsible economic operator? What goes in the internal risk analysis and technical documentation (kept 10 years)? Is the type, batch or serial number and the manufacturer's name, postal and electronic address on the product, or on the packaging if it cannot fit? What must online listings show? How are accidents notified (Safety Business Gateway) and how would a recall work?
- Does any EU rule require CE marking for this product (for example toys or electrical equipment)? If none does, the product must not carry it. If one does, is a notified body needed?
- Product liability: the new directive (national laws due by 9 December 2026) covers software and digital manufacturing files too. Who carries liability if the maker is outside the EU?
- Batteries: the Batteries Regulation has applied since 18 February 2024. Which of its labelling and design rules apply here?
- Textiles: is the product at least 80% textile fibres by weight, so a fibre label is needed?

### 4. Write the UK questions

- Great Britain: the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 apply in England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland follows the EU GPSR since 13 December 2024. Which product-specific regulations in OPSS's A to Z guidance apply?
- Marking: GB still recognises CE marking for many product regulations; UKCA is needed where a UK approved body certifies. Which applies, and where does the mark go (a label or accompanying document is allowed for UKCA until 11pm on 31 December 2027)?
- Importer details: name and postal address on the product or packaging.
- If a problem appears after launch, how does the business notify its market surveillance authority?

### 5. Write the US questions

- Run the CPSC Regulatory Robot for this product and attach the result.
- Is the product subject to a CPSC rule, ban, standard or regulation, so it needs a General Certificate of Conformity from the importer or US manufacturer?
- Is it a children's product, needing tests at a CPSC-accepted lab, a Children's Product Certificate, periodic testing and tracking labels?
- eFiling: since 8 July 2026, importers of most regulated consumer products file certificate data electronically. Who files?
- Who watches for reportable incidents? US reporting runs on 24 hours from getting reportable information.
- Textiles: fibre content, origin, RN and care labels under FTC rules.

### 6. Write the lab brief

Product facts sheet, flags, the questions above, samples available and when, markets, target dates, and a request for the lab's proposed test list with a quote. The lab proposes; the client decides and pays.

### 7. Put it in the timeline

Lab slots before the production order; time for a retest if something fails; certificates ready before shipping.

## Checks

- Every rule mentioned has a date and an official source.
- Every point is a question or a fact, never a ruling.
- No "safe", "compliant", "certified" or "food grade" wording unless a report says so.
- Each market's questions are separate.
- The lab brief has facts with sources and the client's open decisions.
- Lab time is in the timeline before the production order.

## Output format

1. Product facts sheet.
2. Risk flags.
3. Questions for the adviser or lab, by market (EU, UK, US).
4. Lab brief.
5. Timeline entries.
6. Decisions and payments for the client.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a children's brand's fabric activity book for toddlers, sewn in Portugal, sold online in the UK, EU and US. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Flags.** Children (toddlers), textiles, small parts (buttons and a squeaker), cords (ribbon tags).
>
> **US questions.** This is described as for toddlers: does it count as a children's product, so a CPSC-accepted lab test, a Children's Product Certificate and tracking labels are needed? Which tests apply to small parts and ribbons? Who will eFile certificate data on import?
>
> **EU and NI questions.** Do the EU toy safety rules apply, so CE marking is needed, and is a notified body involved? Who is the EU responsible economic operator? Where does the batch number go?
>
> **GB questions.** Which toy safety regulations apply, and CE or UKCA?
>
> **For the client.** Choose and pay the lab; send the product facts sheet with three samples.
