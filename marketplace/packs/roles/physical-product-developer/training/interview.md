# Training interview: product development specialist (physical goods)

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out client names, factory names, prices and anything under a confidentiality agreement; describe products in general terms.

1. **Which kinds of products do you know best (hard goods, apparel, electronics, food-contact, children's), and which do you turn down?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and its limits.

2. **Walk me through a product you took from idea to first batch. What were the stages?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's real stage gates and order of work.

3. **How do you write a requirement so a factory and a tester agree on what "pass" means?**
   Why it matters: requirements with tests are the base of every later document.

4. **What do your looks-like and works-like prototypes usually look like, and what do they cost you in time?**
   Why it matters: grounds prototype plans in practice rather than theory.

5. **When do you bring in a test lab or regulatory adviser, and what do you hand them?**
   Why it matters: sets the line between preparing facts and making rulings.

6. **Tell me about a product that failed a test or came close to a recall. What happened next?**
   Why it matters: surfaces the checks the expert now never skips.

7. **What are the first three design-for-manufacture problems you look for, and how do you raise them with a designer?**
   Why it matters: tunes the agent's DFM questions.

8. **What goes in your request-for-quote pack, and which question has saved you the most money?**
   Why it matters: makes the agent's quote packs complete.

9. **How do you compare quotes that do not line up? Show me a comparison you were proud of.**
   Why it matters: captures the normalising method and the layout.

10. **How do you handle tooling: cost, ownership, where it is kept, and what happens if you change factory?**
    Why it matters: tooling is a common hidden cost and lock-in.

11. **How do you choose and write delivery terms, and what mistakes have you seen with them?**
    Why it matters: grounds Incoterms use in real orders.

12. **How do you check duty and landed cost, and how often do you re-check?**
    Why it matters: costs go stale; the agent needs the expert's habit.

13. **For garments: how do you define points of measure and tolerances, and who confirms grading?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's tech pack standard.

14. **How do you write sample comments so the factory fixes the right thing the first time?**
    Why it matters: shapes tone and format of factory messages.

15. **How many sample rounds is normal for you, and when do you freeze the spec?**
    Why it matters: helps the agent stop endless rounds.

16. **What does a factory do that tells you to walk away?**
    Why it matters: records red flags the agent should raise.

17. **Which messages to suppliers would you let an assistant send, and which must the owner send?**
    Why it matters: tunes level 3 limits and the fresh-yes list.

18. **How do you protect a client's design when sharing it with factories?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's advice on IP advisers and sharing.

19. **What claims do clients ask to put on products or labels that you refuse without evidence?**
    Why it matters: keeps the agent from writing unsupported claims.

20. **What does "factory-ready" mean to you? What do you check before saying it?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's personal quality bar.
