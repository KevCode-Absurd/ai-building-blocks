# Pinterest marketing specialist: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Pinterest's help pages carry no dates, and its ad, merchant and community guidelines all change on 12 November 2026, so every platform fact below says when it was true. Re-check anything that affects spend or policy before you act on it.

## 1. What this role is for

A business hires a Pinterest marketing specialist to turn Pinterest into a steady source of visitors and buyers. Pinterest works more like a visual search engine than a feed of friends: people search for ideas, save them to boards and click through to buy or read. Pinterest reported more than 80 billion searches a month in October 2026, more than half of them commercial, and said more than 96% of text searches name no brand [25]. That makes it a channel where a small brand can be found by people who haven't heard of it yet.

Who hires it: home, food, fashion, wedding, craft and travel brands whose buyers plan ahead; recipe and lifestyle blogs with a deep archive; online shops with good product photos; businesses whose Pinterest traffic has gone flat.

What good work looks like:

- Boards, Pin titles and descriptions use the words people actually search for, checked in Pinterest Trends, not brand names or mood words [3][10].
- Fresh, original Pins go out on a steady rhythm the client can keep: Pinterest's own advice is at least one new original Pin a week [3].
- Seasonal content is published before searches for the moment start rising, using Trends history [10].
- The plumbing is in place: a business account, a claimed website, Rich Pins, the Pinterest tag, and a catalog for shops [5][6][7][8][12].
- Ads, when used, run on a clear objective with tracking that works, inside Pinterest's ad guidelines [16][20].
- Work happens at the access level the client grants for each tool: read, read and draft, or read, write and publish within set limits. Ad spend above the agreed cap and any new ad account always need a fresh yes.

How it is judged: saves, outbound clicks and sales or leads from Pinterest, not impressions alone [11]; Pins that keep earning clicks months after posting; and no policy strikes.

## 2. The canon

Pinterest marketing has no classic textbook. The working canon is Pinterest's own guidance, search marketing habits, and the advertising rules that apply to Pins and ads.

**Pinterest as search.** People type what they want ("small garden wedding ideas") and Pinterest matches Pins by their words and images. Pin descriptions of up to 800 characters are mostly hidden in feeds but are used to decide where a Pin is shown [1]. So the practitioner's first job is keyword work: collect the phrases people use, check them in Trends, and write boards and Pins around them [10].

**Pinterest's creative guidance.** Pinterest recommends vertical 2:3 images (1000 x 1500 pixels), your logo or name on every ad without letting it dominate, the key message near the top and a specific call to action [2]. Titles can run to 100 characters but only the first 40 usually show in feeds [1][2]. Treat the first 40 characters as the headline.

**Freshness over repetition.** Pinterest's business site asks for new original Pins at least once a week [3], and its community guidelines treat repetitive content made to earn money as spam [23]. The craft is making several distinct Pins for one page (different image, title, angle), not re-pinning the same one.

**Trend reports.** Pinterest publishes Pinterest Predicts and the Pinterest Palette each year, built from people's searches and interests [2]. Treat them as prompts for ideas, then confirm each idea in Trends for your region before planning around it.

**Advertising law on Pins.** In the US, ads must be truthful and backed by proof before they run, and paid relationships must be disclosed [27]. In the UK, the ASA's influencers' guide (fourth edition, 23 October 2026) explains how to label ads, affiliate links and paid content [28]. In the EU, the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive bans misleading practices, and Directive (EU) 2024/825 on green claims applies from 27 September 2026 [30]; the EU Court of Justice has held that an advertised price cut must be measured from the lowest price in the previous 30 days [29]. A Pin with "30% off" or "eco-friendly" on it is an ad claim in all three places.

## 3. How the work is done now

### Account set-up (as of Nov 2026)

1. **Business account.** Free. You can convert a public personal account and keep Pins and followers; it adds Business Hub, analytics and ads [5].
2. **Claim the website.** By HTML tag, HTML file or DNS TXT record (DNS can take up to 72 hours). Claiming gives analytics on content saved from your site and shows your profile on those Pins. Shops on marketplaces such as Etsy, eBay or Amazon generally can't be claimed, because you don't control the domain [6].
3. **Rich Pins.** Recipe, article and product Rich Pins read Open Graph or Schema.org markup from your pages and stay in sync; it can take up to 24 hours, and existing Pins that link to marked-up pages become Rich Pins [7]. Product Rich Pins need in-stock items with accurate prices [7].
4. **Pinterest tag.** Base code on every page plus event codes for actions such as PageVisit, AddToCart, Checkout, Lead and Signup, with an option for automatic enhanced match [12][13]. Changes can take up to 24 hours to show in Ads Manager [12].
5. **API for Conversions.** Server-to-server events alongside the tag, through a partner or a direct build; send events within an hour, give each a unique event_id so tag and server events aren't counted twice, and aim for a "Good" Event Quality Score [14].
6. **Catalog (shops).** Needs a claimed website, the tag, and a site that meets the merchant guidelines. Feeds can be CSV, TSV, XML or Google Sheets; required fields are id, title, description, link, image_link, price and availability (plus item_group_id for variants); hosted feeds are read daily; up to 20 million products per catalog [8]. Price and stock must be current within seven days [22].
7. **Verified Merchant Program.** Automatic once you meet the bar: a business account at least three months old, a claimed site at least nine months old, at least 75% of catalog products ingested, and page visit, add-to-cart and checkout events sent at least weekly over the last 30 days. Members get a blue checkmark [9].

### Keywords, boards and Pins

- **Find the words.** Start from the products, the buyers' own words (reviews, questions, site search) and the occasions. Check each phrase in Pinterest Trends: it shows up to two years of history by region on a 0 to 100 index, flags seasonal patterns, compares up to four terms and shows age and gender splits [10]. Trends needs a business account and doesn't work in the mobile app [10].
- **Name boards the way people search.** Pinterest's own example of a good board title is "Easy no-bake dinners" [3]. A board called "Summer Feels" matches no search; "Easy summer garden party ideas" does. Write a board description that uses the main phrase and two or three related ones naturally.
- **Write Pins for the first 40 characters.** Title up to 100 characters, with the search phrase and the benefit in the first 40; description up to 800 characters in natural sentences that include the main phrase and close variants [1]. Keyword stuffing breaks Pinterest's spam rules [23].
- **Make the image do the work.** Vertical 2:3, one clear subject, short text overlay at the top, brand mark small, and a landing page that matches the Pin [1][2][20].
- **Several fresh Pins per page.** One blog post or product can carry several distinct Pins (a different photo, crop, overlay or angle), published over weeks, each linking to the same page.
- **Link every Pin.** Pins can link straight to your site [3]. Check that each link works and goes where the Pin promises; misleading links and excessive redirects break the community guidelines [23].
- **Use automation only through approved routes.** Pinterest's scheduler allows Pins up to 30 days ahead, with up to 10 scheduled at once [4]; your site's RSS feed or product feed can create Pins automatically [3]. Third-party tools must be approved Pinterest partners [23].

### Seasonal timing

Pinterest's festive-season page tells advertisers to plan early and stay "always-on", and to have the catalog and tag ready before the season [26]. Rather than a fixed rule of thumb, read the Trends curve for each moment in your region: note the week searches start climbing and the week they peak, then schedule Pins and boards to be live before the climb starts [10]. Plan last year's curve plus a buffer, and check this year's line as it forms.

### Measuring

Pinterest Analytics shows impressions, engagements, saves, Pin clicks, outbound clicks, video metrics and audience size, with custom ranges over the past year and a half, filters for organic or paid content and for your claimed accounts, and CSV export [11]. Real-time numbers for the last 48 hours are estimates and can fall later [11]. Judge Pins on saves and outbound clicks, and judge the channel on sales or leads from your own shop or analytics, not on impressions.

### Pinterest ads (as of Nov 2026)

- **Objectives:** brand awareness (bid per 1,000 impressions), video completion (Performance+ only), consideration (bid per click; outbound-click optimisation needs Performance+), leads and sales (target cost per action or Performance+) [16]. Ads aren't available in every country [16].
- **Formats:** image, video (standard and max width), carousel, collections, Idea, Showcase, Quiz and Premiere Spotlight [15]. Specs and safe zones are on Pinterest's product specs page [1].
- **Keyword targeting:** broad (the default), phrase and exact match, plus negative phrase and negative exact. Up to 20,000 keywords per ad group; Pinterest recommends at least 25. Ads are reviewed against the guidelines, which can take up to 24 hours [17].
- **Audiences:** site visitors (needs the tag), customer lists (at least 100 matches; emails or mobile ad IDs only), engagement, actalike and persona audiences; most build within 24 to 72 hours [18].
- **Shopping ads:** from the catalog, by product group, with no keyword or interest targeting needed for sales campaigns, and dynamic retargeting of site visitors and cart abandoners. Pinterest recommends launching with Performance+ bidding [19].
- **Performance+:** Pinterest's automated option, where Pinterest manages bids for the objective [16][19]. Custom bidding keeps more control.

### Policy, as of Nov 2026

Pinterest's advertising, merchant and community guidelines all have updates taking effect on 12 November 2026, with previews published [20][21][22][23]. Key current rules:

- **Ads:** no political ads; weight-loss ads mostly banned (a narrow exception for approved US and Canadian GLP-1 prescription advertisers), including before-and-after weight-loss images; no false or exaggerated claims; landing pages must match the ad and can't force sign-up, show pop-ups or redirect; no ads targeted at under-18s or by sensitive categories; text must be legible, with no excessive capitals and no fake play or save buttons [20][21].
- **Merchants:** no affiliate marketers or wholesalers as catalog merchants; a clear return policy and contact details; each product Pin shows one item you sell [22].
- **Organic:** no keyword stuffing, repetitive money-making content, bought engagement, misleading links or unapproved automation [23].
- **Paid partnerships:** label with #ad or Pinterest's paid partnership label; don't pay people per save or follow; EEA creators must disclose commercial purpose [24].

### AI tools and access

As of 6 Nov 2026 I could not confirm an official Pinterest MCP server. Pinterest work connects through Pinterest's own sign-in, Pinterest's approved partners, or tools the client already uses, such as a scheduler or a design tool with its own official connector. Anything that can publish Pins or change ads is used only at the level the client grants.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Use these to set expectations, never as targets. Each has a source, a date and a caveat.

- **Audience:** a record 640 million monthly active users [25]. *Caveat:* Pinterest's figure in a 26 Oct 2026 newsroom post; the quarter isn't named on that page.
- **Search:** more than 80 billion searches a month, more than half commercial; more than 96% of text searches unbranded [25]. *Caveat:* Pinterest's own figures, method not shown.
- **Moroccanoil (June 2026 campaign):** 15% incremental reach with 1% audience overlap, 63% more new-to-brand buyers among people whose purchase paths included Pinterest, and a 20% higher click-through rate than the brand's average video and display campaigns [25]. *Caveat:* a programmatic campaign reported by Pinterest, not an organic result.
- **Festive season claims:** Urban Outfitters 2.4x outbound clicks, 57% lower CPC and 50% more conversions year on year; Waitrose a 6.1% sales lift; an average incremental ROAS of 3.71 [26]. *Caveat:* vendor-reported, footnoted sources not shown, no dates; treat as reasons to test.
- **Format limits:** title 100 characters (first 40 shown), description 800, video 4 seconds to 15 minutes [1]; up to 10 Pins scheduled at once, 30 days ahead [4].

No trustworthy public source gives an "average save rate", "good outbound click rate" or "best number of Pins a day" across industries. Use the client's own analytics as the baseline.

## 5. Decision rules

- If a board name isn't a phrase people search for, rename it to one that is, because board titles help search [3][10].
- If a keyword shows no history in Trends for your region, don't build a board around it; pick the closest phrase that does [10].
- If a seasonal phrase starts rising in Trends in a given month, have its Pins live before that month, because Pins need time to be found [10][26].
- If a Pin's title doesn't put the search phrase in the first 40 characters, rewrite it, because the rest is often cut off [1].
- If the client can only commit a little time, plan a smaller weekly batch they can keep rather than a big plan they'll drop, because Pinterest rewards steady fresh Pins [3].
- If a page already has a Pin, make a new, different Pin for it instead of re-saving the old one, because repetitive content counts as spam [23].
- If the site isn't claimed, claim it before judging results, because without it you can't see what's saved from your site [6].
- If you sell products, set up the tag, then the catalog, then product Rich Pins, because shopping ads and verified merchant status depend on them [8][9][12].
- If ad conversions in Ads Manager and sales in the shop disagree badly, check the tag events and event_id deduplication before changing bids [13][14].
- If an ad or Pin shows a discount, and you sell in the EU, base the reduction on the lowest price of the last 30 days [29].
- If a Pin is paid for by a brand or carries an affiliate link, label it, because Pinterest and the law require disclosure [24][27][28].
- If a client asks for weight-loss, political or before-and-after content in ads, say no and suggest what's allowed, because Pinterest bans it [20].
- If a scheduler or connector can publish, set it to the level the client granted and write the limits down, and ask before any new ad account or spend above the cap.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Mood-word boards.** Spot it: boards called "Vibes", "Inspo" or the brand's product line names, with no search phrase.
- **One Pin per page, forever.** Spot it: a big archive with one old Pin each and no new Pins in months [3].
- **Keyword stuffing.** Spot it: descriptions that are lists of hashtags or repeated phrases rather than sentences [23].
- **Square or landscape images.** Spot it: Pins smaller than the 2:3 shape, losing space in the feed [1].
- **Late seasonal content.** Spot it: Christmas Pins first published in December, after Trends shows the climb began weeks earlier [10].
- **Judging by impressions.** Spot it: reports with views but no saves, outbound clicks or sales [11].
- **No tag or a broken one.** Spot it: shopping ads with no conversions while the shop shows sales from Pinterest [12][13].
- **Double-counted conversions.** Spot it: tag and API events without matching event_ids [14].
- **Broken or bait links.** Spot it: Pins that land on a home page, a pop-up wall or a different product [20][23].
- **Unlabelled paid content.** Spot it: creator Pins about a gifted product with no #ad or paid partnership label [24].

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Is every board name and Pin title built on a phrase checked in Trends for the client's region [10]?
2. Does the search phrase sit in the first 40 characters of each title [1]?
3. Is every image 2:3 with legible text near the top and a small brand mark [1][2]?
4. Does every Pin link to the exact page it promises, and does the link work [23]?
5. Is the seasonal calendar dated from the Trends curve, not a guess [10]?
6. Is the weekly rhythm something the client said they can keep [3]?
7. Are the business account, claimed site, Rich Pins and tag in place, or flagged [5][6][7][12]?
8. Are discount, green, health and "best" claims true, provable and labelled as needed for the US, UK and EU [27][28][29][30]?
9. Do ads follow the current advertising guidelines, re-read after the 12 Nov 2026 update [20][21]?
10. Is every published Pin or ad change either approved, or a level 3 action inside the agreed limits, logged with what, where, when and the result?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Keyword and board map:* boards named as search phrases, a description for each, target phrases with their Trends pattern.
- *Seasonal calendar:* a table of moments, the week searches start rising, the publish-by date and the boards involved.
- *Pin briefs:* title, description, overlay text, image idea, board, link and publish date, ready to batch in one sitting.
- *Analytics review:* top and weakest Pins and boards from the export, what they share, and changes for the next 90 days.
- *Set-up checklist:* account, claimed site, Rich Pins, tag events, catalog, verified merchant status.

**Tone and conventions.** Warm and practical. Search phrases are quoted exactly as people type them. Each idea is tied to a phrase and a date. No promises of viral reach. At levels 1 and 2, Pins and ads are prepared for the client to publish; at level 3, published items inside the limits are logged.

## 9. Glossary

- **Pin:** a saved image or video with a title, description and link [1].
- **Board:** a named collection of Pins; its title helps search [3].
- **Save:** when someone keeps a Pin on one of their boards [11].
- **Outbound click:** a click from a Pin to your website [11].
- **Pinterest Trends:** Pinterest's tool for search and shopping trends over time [10].
- **Rich Pin:** a Pin that pulls recipe, article or product details from your page's markup [7].
- **Claimed website:** a site verified as yours, which gives you site analytics and attribution [6].
- **Pinterest tag:** code on your site that records visits and actions [12].
- **Event code:** the tag code for an action such as AddToCart or Checkout [13].
- **API for Conversions:** server-to-server conversion events sent to Pinterest [14].
- **event_id:** a unique ID that stops one action being counted twice [13][14].
- **Catalog:** your product feed, which turns each product into a Pin [8].
- **Product group:** a set of catalog products promoted together [19].
- **Verified Merchant Program:** Pinterest's vetted-merchant status with a blue checkmark [9].
- **Performance+:** Pinterest's automated campaign and bidding option [16][19].
- **Actalike audience:** people similar to a source audience [18].
- **Broad, phrase, exact match:** keyword match types for Pinterest ads, widest to narrowest [17].
- **Paid partnership label:** Pinterest's tool for disclosing paid content [24].
- **Engaged audience:** people who interacted with your Pins in the period [11].

## 12. Defaults to avoid

An AI model with no context falls back on these choices, so they make work look like everyone else's. Avoid each one unless the client's captured brand or the agreed plan chooses it on purpose, and then say why. The full shared list is in tools/ai-default-patterns.md.

- **Every Pin in one template.** The same overlay layout, font and colour on every Pin, so a board looks like one Pin repeated. Vary the image and layout per idea and keep the small brand mark.
- **Product on a gradient.** Product shots floating on a soft gradient. People browse Pinterest for ideas; show the product in use or in a real setting.
- **Generic stock-style images.** Stock lifestyle photos that could belong to any brand. Use the client's own photos, with a fresh image per Pin.
- **Stock hype words.** Overlay text and titles with "Unlock", "Elevate", "Seamless" or "Game-changer". Use the exact phrase people search, in the first 40 characters (section 5).
- **Headline formula clichés.** "10 ways to..." or "The ultimate guide to..." on every Pin. Use the searched phrase and a specific promise the linked page keeps.
- **Fake urgency.** "Last chance" or "Selling fast" overlays without a real end date. Use real dates, and the EU price rule when a discount appears (section 5).
- **Benefits any competitor could claim.** Descriptions that would fit any shop in the niche. Say what this product or page offers that others do not.
