# Pinterest analytics review

## When to use

- Every quarter.
- When Pinterest traffic or sales have gone flat or dropped.
- When taking over an account from someone else.
- Before deciding whether to spend on Pinterest ads.

## What you need

- Pinterest Analytics export (CSV) for the last 90 days, and the same period a year earlier if available: Pins and boards with impressions, saves, Pin clicks, outbound clicks, engagement, filtered to organic and to your claimed accounts where possible.
- Ads Manager export if ads ran: spend, clicks, conversions by campaign.
- Shop or blog analytics for the same dates: sessions, sales or sign-ups from Pinterest.
- The board list and a note of anything that changed (new site, new theme, new tag, paused posting).

**Access:** level 1 is enough. Changes that follow the review are proposals, or level 3 actions inside agreed limits.

## Steps

### 1. Check the data
Note the date range and filters. Pinterest's real-time figures for the last 48 hours are estimates that can fall later, so leave out the last two days. Custom ranges cover the past year and a half.

### 2. Check the set-up first
- Is the website claimed? Without it, you can't see what's saved from your site.
- Do Rich Pins work on product, recipe or article pages?
- Is the tag firing PageVisit, AddToCart and Checkout (or Lead), and do Ads Manager conversions roughly match shop sales?
- For shops: catalog errors, and price and stock kept current?
A broken set-up explains many "Pinterest stopped working" stories. Fix it before judging content.

### 3. Rank Pins on what matters
Sort by outbound clicks, then saves. Impressions alone don't count. Mark a clear top group and a clear bottom group among Pins that are at least a month old (new Pins haven't had time), and say where you drew each line.

### 4. Find what winners share
Look across the top Pins for patterns: the search phrase in the title, the board, the image style (overhead, close-up, people, text overlay), the angle (how-to, list, gift, result), the season, the page type. Write the patterns as statements a designer and writer can use.

### 5. Find what the weak ones share
Same, for the bottom group: mood-word titles, square images, links to home pages, repeated images, out-of-season timing, pages that no longer exist.

### 6. Review boards
For each board: Pins added in the period, outbound clicks, saves, and whether its name is a search phrase. Flag boards with no fresh Pins and boards with mood-word names.

### 7. Connect to results
Compare outbound clicks with shop or blog sessions and sales from Pinterest. If clicks rise but sales don't, look at the landing pages, not Pinterest.

### 8. Write five changes
Each change: what to do, why (the evidence), how to measure it, and when to look again. Typical changes: rename boards to search phrases, make fresh Pins for the top pages in the winning style, fix the tag, move seasonal publishing earlier, retire a weak angle.

## Checks

- The last 48 hours are excluded, and the date range is stated.
- Pins younger than a month aren't judged.
- Winners are judged on outbound clicks and saves, not impressions.
- Set-up gaps are checked before content is blamed.
- Every change has evidence from the export and a review date.
- Ad results are kept apart from organic results.

## Output format

1. **Headline:** three sentences on what happened and why.
2. **Set-up check:** claimed site, Rich Pins, tag events, catalog, each pass or fix.
3. **Top and weakest Pins:** tables with title, board, outbound clicks, saves, and the pattern each shows.
4. **Board review:** board, fresh Pins, outbound clicks, name OK or rename.
5. **Five changes for the next 90 days:** numbered, with evidence, measure and review date.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a UK ceramics shop. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Headline:** Outbound clicks fell 31% on the same quarter last year. Most of the drop comes from 11 Christmas Pins published in late November, after the climb had started. The Pinterest tag hasn't sent a Checkout event since the shop changed theme on 2 Sep.
>
> **Set-up:** site claimed (pass); Rich Pins on products (pass); tag Checkout event (fix: missing since 2 Sep); catalog (pass).
>
> **Winners share:** overhead shots of a full table, the phrase in the first 40 characters, links to a collection page rather than one product.
>
> **Five changes**
> 1. Restore the Checkout event (developer, this week). Measure: Checkout events in Ads Manager match shop orders from Pinterest within a week.
> 2. Rename 4 mood-word boards to search phrases (list attached).
> 3. Publish Christmas Pins by early October next year, from the Trends curve.
> 4. Make 3 new overhead-table Pins for each of the top 6 collection pages.
> 5. Stop "mug on white" product shots for organic Pins; they're in the bottom group 9 times out of 10.
