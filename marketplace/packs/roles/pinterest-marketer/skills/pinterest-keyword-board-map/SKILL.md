# Pinterest keyword and board map

## When to use

- A new Pinterest account, or one being restarted.
- Boards named after moods ("Summer Feels"), brand lines or internal categories.
- Pinterest traffic has gone flat and nobody knows which searches the account serves.
- Before writing a Pin batch, so every Pin has a board and a phrase.

## Why it matters

Pinterest works like a visual search engine. Pinterest reported more than 80 billion searches a month in October 2026, with more than 96% of text searches naming no brand (see knowledge.md). People find you through the words they type, so boards and Pins named in those words get found; boards named in brand language don't. Pinterest's own example of a good board title is "Easy no-bake dinners".

## What you need

- The product list or post archive, with links, grouped as the business sees it.
- Buyers' own words: reviews, questions customers ask, site search terms, comments.
- Key occasions and seasons for the business, by country.
- The current board list and the last 90 days of Pinterest Analytics, if the account exists.
- Pinterest Trends access (business account, desktop or mobile web) for the client's region, or the client's help checking phrases.

**Access:** level 1 is enough for the map. Level 3 is needed only to create or rename boards in the account, within the agreed limits.

## Steps

### 1. Gather seed phrases
From each product or post, write the plain things a person would search to find it: the object, the use, the occasion, the style, the problem ("small balcony garden ideas", "vegetarian weeknight dinners"). Add phrases lifted from reviews and questions. Avoid brand names and mood words.

### 2. Check each phrase in Trends
For the client's region, look up each seed phrase. Note: whether it has history, the shape (steady, seasonal, rising), the peak month and the week the climb starts, and the age split if it matters to the client. Trends shows up to two years and compares up to four phrases at a time, so check close variants side by side and keep the one with more interest. If you can't access Trends, mark each phrase "to check" and say so.

### 3. Group phrases into themes
Cluster phrases that a person would see as one topic. Each cluster becomes a candidate board. Drop clusters with no history and nothing in the client's range to pin.

### 4. Name the boards
- The board name is the main search phrase, written the way people type it.
- Up to 12 boards to start, so the client can keep each one fresh.
- Seasonal boards get the season in the name ("Christmas table decorations").
- Keep one board for the brand's own range only if people search for the brand.

### 5. Write board descriptions
Two or three natural sentences using the main phrase once and two or three related phrases. No hashtag lists, no repeated phrases: keyword stuffing breaks Pinterest's spam rules.

### 6. Map pages to boards
For each board, list the products or posts that will feed it and the phrases each Pin should target. A page can feed several boards.

### 7. Plan the changes
For an existing account: which boards to rename, merge, keep or archive. Renaming keeps the Pins; deleting doesn't, so deleting always needs the client's yes.

## Checks

- Every board name is a phrase with Trends history in the client's region (or is marked "to check").
- No board name is a mood word, an internal category or a product code.
- Descriptions read as sentences, not keyword lists.
- Each board has enough pages behind it to keep publishing fresh Pins.
- Seasonal boards note when the climb starts.
- Changes to existing boards are marked as proposals or level 3 actions inside the limits.

## Output format

1. **Keyword table:** phrase, Trends pattern (steady, seasonal, rising), peak month, climb starts, region, notes.
2. **Board map:** board name, description, main phrase, related phrases, pages that feed it.
3. **Changes to existing boards:** rename, merge, keep, archive, with reasons.
4. **Phrases to check:** anything not confirmed in Trends.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a ceramics shop selling handmade mugs and plates in the UK. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. The Trends patterns only show the shape.*

> **Description (Handmade mug gift ideas):** Handmade mug gift ideas for coffee lovers, tea drinkers and anyone who likes a cup with character. Small-batch stoneware mugs, gift sets and personalised mugs, glazed by hand in our Bristol studio.
