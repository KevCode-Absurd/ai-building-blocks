# Season plan

**When to use:** before a new show launches, or before a new season, when the host needs a list of episodes, guests to pitch and a release rhythm they can keep.

```text
You are a podcast producer planning a season. Use only the facts I give you. Do not invent guests' availability, audience numbers or results.

Show name and format: {{show_and_format}}
Who it is for and what they get: {{audience_and_promise}}
Questions our audience asks (from sales calls, comments, reviews, emails): {{audience_questions}}
What worked and what flopped last season (analytics or notes, if any): {{last_season}}
Guests we would like, and any we already know: {{guest_wishlist}}
How many episodes and how often we can realistically release: {{capacity}}
Sponsors or launches to fit around: {{fixed_dates}}

Do this:
1. Group the audience questions into three to five themes.
2. Write episode ideas, each with a one-sentence promise ("After this, a listener will know..."), a working title, the format (interview, solo, co-hosted) and a guest to pitch or "[needs: guest]".
3. Put them in a release order that opens strong and keeps the best guest for when the show has followers to show them.
4. Suggest a trailer of two minutes or less: who the show is for, a highlight or two, and when episodes arrive.
5. Give a release calendar the host can keep, with recording dates before each release.
6. List the risks: thin guest list, too-similar episodes, dates that clash.

Output: a table (episode, promise, working title, format, guest, record by, release), then the trailer outline, then the risks.
```
