# Episode plan and run sheet

A good recording starts before anyone presses record. This skill fixes what the episode promises, shapes the conversation so the best stories come out, and gives the host a timed plan that keeps the session on track and covers the parts that cannot be fixed in the edit: consent on tape, sponsor reads and the call to action.

## When to use

- A host has a guest booked and needs an outline and questions.
- A solo or co-hosted episode needs structure.
- A new show or season needs a format and a list of episodes.
- Recordings run long, wander or miss the sponsor read.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read past episodes, transcripts, analytics and the guest's public work. Level 2 to save the outline and run sheet in the client's shared folder and add a tentative recording hold to the calendar. Nothing in this skill publishes anything.

- Who the show is for and what they get from it.
- The format (interview, co-hosted, solo, narrative, panel, video) and usual length.
- The topic, and the guest's bio, links and recent appearances.
- Any no-go topics, sensitive areas or legal limits the client names.
- Sponsor slots and the sponsor's approved facts, if any.
- The call to action for this episode.

With web search connected, I read the guest's recent interviews and posts so the questions find something new. Without it, I work from the bio and links the client pastes and mark "[check: recent appearances]".

## Steps

1. **Write the promise.** One sentence: "After this episode, a [listener] will know or be able to [result]." If the client cannot agree a promise, stop and agree it first.
2. **Pick the shape.** Interview: open with a story, then detail, then the turn (what changed or went wrong), then the takeaway. Solo: problem, three points, example, action. Co-hosted: named segments the audience recognises. Narrative: scene, question, the evidence in order, answer.
3. **Find the angle for this guest.** List what the guest has said often elsewhere and steer away from it. Note two or three stories only this guest can tell.
4. **Write the questions.** Around ten, open, one idea each, in conversation order. Add one or two likely follow-ups under each ("What did you try first?", "What would you tell someone in that spot now?"). Avoid yes-or-no and double questions. Mark the one question that must be asked even if time runs short.
5. **Plan the cold open and intro.** Note what kind of moment would make a good cold open, to be picked from the tape later. Write the host intro in a few spoken lines.
6. **Build the run sheet.** A table with clock times: setup and sound check, the consent line on tape, intro, segments with target lengths, sponsor read slots marked "SPONSORED", the close and call to action, and a few minutes for pickups (re-recorded lines).
7. **Add the sponsor read.** Place it where the client's sponsor agreement says, open it with a plain spoken disclosure ("This episode is sponsored by..."), and keep claims to the sponsor's approved facts and the host's real experience. US, UK and EU disclosure rules are in knowledge.md.
8. **Write the guest prep note.** Topic and angle, three sample questions, logistics, and the reminder that the release form must be signed before release.
9. **For a season,** list episode ideas tied to the audience's real questions, a guest to pitch for each, a working title, and a release rhythm the host says they can keep. Mark gaps "[needs: guest]".

## Checks

- One promise per episode, and the title idea keeps it.
- Questions are open, single and in a sensible order; none is a statement in disguise.
- Run sheet timings add up to the target length plus pickups.
- Consent line, sponsor disclosure and call to action are all on the run sheet.
- No question asks the guest to confirm a claim the client cannot back up.
- No-go topics are listed at the top of the run sheet for the host.

## Output format

1. Episode promise (one sentence).
2. Outline: cold open note, intro lines, segments with purpose.
3. Question list with follow-ups, the must-ask question marked.
4. Run sheet table: clock time, segment, length, who speaks, notes.
5. Guest prep note.
6. For a season: a table of episode, promise, guest to pitch, working title, release week.

## Example of good output (short)

*Harbour Lane Bakery Podcast is a made-up show used only to show the format. All details are invented.*

**Promise:** After this episode, a small bakery owner will know how to raise prices without losing regulars.

**Questions (extract)**

1. Take me back to the week you decided prices had to go up. What was on your desk? *(Follow-up: who did you talk to first?)*
2. What did you change first, and how did regulars react in the first week?
3. **Must ask:** If you did it again tomorrow, what would you do differently?

**Run sheet (extract)**

| Clock | Segment | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10:00 | Sound check, recording settings | 5 min | Headphones on, phone silent |
| 10:05 | Consent line on tape | 1 min | "Are you happy for us to record and publish this?" |
| 10:06 | Intro | 1 min | Host reads intro lines |
| 10:07 | Segment 1: the decision | 10 min | Q1 to Q3 |
| 10:17 | SPONSORED: flour supplier read | 1 min | Open with "This episode is sponsored by..."; approved facts only |
| 10:18 | Segment 2: what changed | 15 min | Q4 to Q7 |
| 10:33 | Close and call to action | 3 min | Q10, then "Follow the show for part two" |
| 10:36 | Pickups | 5 min | Re-record intro if needed |
