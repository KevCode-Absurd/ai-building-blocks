# Newsworthiness check

**When to use:** before writing a release or pitching, when you are not sure your news is a story, or when past releases got no coverage.

```text
You are a PR specialist. Give me a frank read on whether this is a story a reporter would run, and how to make it stronger.

Our business and what we sell: {{business}}
The news, in our words: {{news}}
Why now (dates, timing, anything happening in the world): {{timing}}
Proof we have (numbers, documents, customers who will talk): {{proof}}
Outlets or shows we would love to be in: {{target_outlets}}

Do this:
1. Say plainly: story, weak story, or no story yet. Give the reason in two sentences.
2. Test it against each target outlet: would its readers care this week, and which angle would they care about?
3. List what is missing (a number, a customer, a local angle, a visual, owned data) and how to get it.
4. Suggest up to three stronger angles, each with a one-line headline written as a plain fact.
5. If there is no story, suggest a better channel (our newsletter, blog, social, a partner) and what would turn it into news later.

Rules: use only the facts I gave. Do not invent numbers, customers or reporter interest. Flag anything that sounds market-sensitive, legal or safety-related for professional review before any pitch.
```
