# Awards, talks and podcasts

Award judges, event organisers, podcast hosts and music bookers all face the same problem: too many entries, too little time. They say yes to the ones that fit their audience, answer their questions in their order, and prove every claim. This skill picks the right targets, then writes to each one's rules.

## When to use

- The client wants to enter industry or local business awards.
- An expert wants to pitch talks to conferences or events.
- A founder, consultant or author wants to guest on podcasts.
- An artist, band, DJ or comedian needs a press kit and booking pitches to venues or festivals.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read entry rules, calls for speakers, show listings and past entries. Level 2 to save entries and proposals in the portal without submitting, and pitches as drafts. Level 3 to submit entries and proposals on the approved list and send pitches to the approved hosts, organisers or bookers, within agreed dates. Entry fees stay with the client.

- The list of awards, events, shows or venues the client is considering (or permission to search, if the AI tool has web search).
- The full entry rules or call for speakers, pasted or linked.
- Results, numbers, dates, quotes and proof, plus who can confirm each.
- Bios, past talks, past appearances, press quotes the client supplies, links.
- What the client wants: leads, authority, bookings, recruitment.

## Steps

1. **Score the shortlist.** For each target, rate fit (right audience), effort (what the entry or pitch needs), value (what a win or booking brings) and deadline. For podcasts add format, how often episodes come out and recent guests. Sort into "go now", "later" and "skip". Fewer good fits beat many random ones.
2. **Read the rules.** List every criterion, word limit, required document, eligibility rule and deadline. Note how judges score, if stated.
3. **Gather the proof.** Results, dates, numbers, quotes, before-and-after evidence. Mark anything that cannot be confirmed. Do not inflate: a true smaller number earns more trust.
4. **Find the story.** The challenge, what the client did differently, the result. Judges and hosts remember a story with a number in it.
5. **Write to the format.**
   - *Award entry:* answer each criterion in its own section, in order, inside its word limit, leading with the result. Add an evidence list for each claim and a short summary for judges.
   - *Talk proposal:* a title that promises one takeaway; an abstract for the audience, not the speaker; three takeaways; who it is for; why this speaker.
   - *Podcast one-sheet and pitch:* bio, three topics with hooks for that show's listeners, sample questions, past appearances and links. The pitch says why this show, which topic, and what listeners get.
   - *Artist press kit and booking pitch:* bios in three lengths, the story of the release, notable shows, press quotes the artist supplies, links, contacts. The booking pitch says why the act fits that venue, the draw the act can prove, dates, and the ask.
6. **Check against the rules.** Word counts, required files, eligibility, deadline, time zone.
7. **Submit or send (level 3) or hand over.** Submit only to the approved list. The client pays any fees.
8. **Prepare for yes.** For booked podcasts: talking points, three stories with numbers, a call to action for listeners. For talks: an outline and slides brief. For awards shortlisted: a short winner's statement and a release ready to go.
9. **Track.** Deadlines, submissions, results, bookings and what each led to.

## Checks

- Every criterion answered, in order, within its limit.
- Every claim has evidence or is removed.
- No invented results, quotes, press or host interest.
- Titles and hooks are about the audience's benefit.
- Bios are consistent across lengths and with the client's site.
- Submissions and pitches only went to the approved list.

## Output format

1. Scored shortlist (table): target, deadline, fit, effort, value, decision.
2. The entry, proposal, one-sheet or press kit text, with word counts.
3. Evidence list: claim, proof, who confirms.
4. Pitches (where relevant) with one follow-up each.
5. Weak spots a judge, host or booker may notice, with fixes.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a physiotherapy clinic. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Shortlist extract**

| Target | Deadline | Fit | Effort | Value | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| County Business Awards, Small Employer of the Year | 14 Feb | High | Medium | Local profile, hiring | Go now |
| National Health Innovation Awards | 2 Jan | Low (needs research data) | High | High | Skip this year |

**Criterion 2 (300 words max): "Describe the impact of your people practices."**

*Lead with the result:* "Since starting paid training days in [YEAR], staff turnover fell from [X] to [Y] a year, and all three of last year's new physios were promoted from our assistant team." [EVIDENCE: HR records, PM to confirm]

**Talk title (for a regional health conference):** "Fewer missed appointments with one text: what our clinic changed and what it cost"

**Weak spot:** the entry claims "best patient reviews in the county" with no comparison data. Fix: replace with the clinic's own review score and count, dated.
