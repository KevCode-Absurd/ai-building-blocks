# Media pitching

A pitch is one story, for one person, about what their audience needs. Reporters skim and delete generic emails; they read the ones that show the sender knows their work and offers something useful now. This skill builds the list, writes the pitches, and runs the sends and follow-ups without burning anyone.

## When to use

- A story or release is ready and needs coverage.
- The client wants to offer an exclusive or share news under embargo.
- A spokesperson wants to be a source for stories a reporter is already writing.
- Coverage is thin and the client wants to know why.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to research outlets and read replies. Level 2 to save media lists and pitch drafts in the client's mailbox or media database. Level 3 to send pitches and follow-ups, and only to reporters on the media list the client approved for this story, on the agreed dates and volumes.

- The story, release or fact sheet (see the press-release skill).
- The client's target outlets, shows and newsletters.
- What is on offer: spokesperson, customer, data, images, exclusive, embargo.
- Any market-sensitive angle (needs the securities lawyer's sign-off before any pitch).
- Past contacts and anyone who asked not to be pitched.

## Steps

1. **Pick the audiences.** Which readers or listeners need this story? Trade press, local news, national, newsletters, podcasts, creators.
2. **Build the list from recent work.** For each outlet, find the person who covered this topic recently. Note one recent piece and why this story fits them. Keep the list small and specific. A media database export helps; a spreadsheet of names and links works too.
3. **Get the list approved.** Show the client the list with the reason for each name. This approved list is the boundary for level 3 sends.
4. **Decide exclusive, embargo or open.**
   - *Exclusive:* one outlet first. Offer to one at a time, with a deadline for a reply; move on after a clear no or the deadline.
   - *Embargo:* ask first ("Would you like this under embargo until [date, time, time zone]?"). Send the material only after a yes. An embargo binds only people who agreed to it.
   - *Open:* send when the news is public.
5. **Write the subject line.** The story in plain words. Add "EMBARGOED" when it is.
6. **Write the pitch.** Three short paragraphs: the story and why now; why this reporter (their recent piece); one line of proof and what is available. End with one ask. No attachments unless asked; link to the release and images.
7. **Write one follow-up.** It adds something new (a data point, a customer, a photo) and goes a few working days later, only if there was no reply. Never more than one unless the reporter engages.
8. **Send (level 3) or hand over (levels 1 and 2).** At level 3, confirm the list, dates and volume once with the client, then send within them. Pace sends so each can be checked for errors.
9. **Handle replies.** Answer questions fast with facts. Book interviews in the slots the client gave. Note declines politely; remove anyone who asks to stop, from every list, that day.
10. **If an embargo breaks,** tell the client, lift it for everyone at once, and let the other reporters know.
11. **Log and report.** For each name: sent, follow-up, reply, outcome, coverage link.

## Checks

- Every name on the list has a recent, relevant piece noted.
- Every pitch mentions something specific to that person's work.
- One story, one ask, short enough to read at a glance.
- Embargo terms stated and agreed before material is sent.
- No exclusive offered to two outlets at once.
- No market-sensitive detail without legal sign-off.
- Sends at level 3 went only to the approved list, within the agreed dates and volume.
- Opt-outs honoured across all lists.

## Output format

1. Media list table: outlet, name, beat, recent piece (link), why this story fits, offer type, status.
2. Pitches: subject line and body per person, plus the follow-up.
3. Send plan: dates, times, embargo terms, who approves.
4. Log: sends, replies, outcomes, coverage links, opt-outs.

## Example of good output (short)

*Fieldnote Software is a made-up business used only to show the format. All details are invented for this example.*

**List entry:** *Northern Business Weekly* - [reporter name] - small-business tech - wrote last week about farm co-ops moving bookkeeping online - fits because Fieldnote's survey covers 400 co-ops - offer: data under embargo - status: approved by client.

**Subject:** EMBARGOED survey: 6 in 10 farm co-ops still close their books on paper

Hi [name],

Your piece on co-ops moving their books online last week stopped at the cost question. We surveyed 400 co-ops in the region last month, and cost was the second reason they stay on paper. The first was trust in who holds the data.

Fieldnote can share the full results under embargo until 09:00 GMT on Thursday, plus an interview with our founder and a co-op manager who switched last year.

Would you like the data under embargo?

[Sender name, phone]

**Follow-up (Tuesday, if no reply):** "One more detail from the survey: co-ops with under 50 members were twice as likely to stay on paper. Happy to send the data under embargo if useful."

*(Numbers above are invented for the example; real pitches use the client's checked figures.)*
