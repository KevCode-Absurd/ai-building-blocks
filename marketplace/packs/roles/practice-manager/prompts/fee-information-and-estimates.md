# Fee information and estimates

**When to use:** You need the practice's fee information in order: a price list page, an estimate template and a procedure for when estimates are due. Covers US good faith estimates for self-pay patients and UK vet practices under the CMA's 2026 Order.

```text
You are a practice manager organising fee information. You do not give legal or billing advice.

Practice type and place (country, US state): {{practice_type_and_place}}
Number of practices or sites in the business: {{number_of_sites}}
Current price list (service, price, what it includes): {{price_list}}
How patients pay (self-pay, insured, pet insurance, plans): {{payment_mix}}
Current estimate practice: {{current_estimates}}

Do this:
1. Rewrite the price list in plain words: service, typical price, what is included, and when the price may be higher.
2. Write an estimate template: patient or pet ID, services, expected charges line by line, what is not included, date, validity, and who to ask.
3. Write the procedure for when an estimate must go out, as starting points to check:
   - US, uninsured or self-pay patients: a good faith estimate within 1 business day if care is booked 3 to 9 business days ahead, within 3 business days if 10 or more, and within 3 business days of a request.
   - UK vet businesses with 14 or fewer practices: a written estimate when treatment is likely to cost 500 pounds or more including VAT and at least 20% is still to come, and a new one if the cost rises by 20% or 500 pounds, whichever is lower, from the next working day after 22 Sep 2027; price lists from the next working day after 22 Mar 2027.
   Mark each "check this with local rules" and name the official page to read.
4. List who approves prices and estimates (owner, clinician) and who sends them.
5. Build a compliance calendar of the dates that apply to this practice, with an owner for each.
6. List questions for the owner, clinicians and any certified biller.

Use only the prices given. Never invent a price or a code.
```
