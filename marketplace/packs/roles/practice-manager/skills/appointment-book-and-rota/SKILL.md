# Appointment book and rota

## When to use

- The diary is full of gaps on some days and overruns on others.
- Clinicians write notes at lunch because there is no protected time.
- Urgent patients are squeezed in and every later patient waits.
- New patients wait weeks while follow-ups crowd the book.
- A sick day leaves the desk or a nurse session uncovered.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** schedule exports, current templates, the rota, leave records.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** build appointment types and templates in the practice system without applying them; draft the rota in a copy.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** apply the approved template from an agreed date, and publish the approved rota and cover changes. Changing a clinician's template after it is live needs that clinician's yes.

## What you need

1. Clinicians, their days and hours, and which rooms and equipment each needs.
2. Appointment types with their lengths, as each clinician sets them (new patient, review, treatment, procedure, vaccination, emergency).
3. A schedule export for at least the last three months: bookings by type, day and hour, no-shows, overruns if recorded.
4. How far ahead new patients and urgent cases are waiting now.
5. Desk and support staff: contracted hours, skills, who can do what (phones, payments, sterilising, nursing tasks).
6. The practice's rules on breaks, leave and overtime, and where staff work (country, US state).

## Steps

### 1. Read the demand

From the export, count bookings by type, day and hour. Find when new patients ask to come, when urgent calls arrive, and which slot types run over. Show the counts. If there is no export, say so and start with the clinicians' estimates, marked as estimates.

### 2. Agree the slot rules with the clinicians

For each clinician: slot types and lengths, buffers after long or messy procedures, protected admin time, how many emergency slots per session and when they release to routine bookings if unused. These are the clinicians' calls. Write them down as a table they sign off.

### 3. Build the template

A table by day, clinician and room:
- Put emergency slots where urgent calls arrive (often early in each session) and release rules beside them.
- Put new patient slots where new patients ask to come, not only where they fit.
- Group long procedures to reduce room changes.
- Protect admin time in the same place each week so it survives.
- Check rooms and equipment never double-book.

In the practice system, build the appointment types and the template saved but not applied (level 2). If staff can book over the template in your system, add a note on who may book into protected time and when.

### 4. Build the rota around the template

- Cover every session the template opens: desk, phones, nursing or support roles.
- Put the strongest desk cover in the busiest window found in the patient flow work.
- Add breaks that fit the working time rules where staff work. In the UK, workers on shifts over 6 hours get an uninterrupted 20-minute break and 11 hours between working days; in the US, overtime applies after 40 hours in a workweek for non-exempt staff. Check this with local rules.
- Show contracted hours against rostered hours per person.

### 5. Write the cover rules

- Who covers whom, by role, in order.
- What closes first if cover fails (for example, routine phone bookings move to callback; online booking stays open).
- How leave is requested and how far ahead, and how many people can be off at once in each role.
- What happens on known busy days (school holidays, vaccination season, the first week of the month).

### 6. Test and switch on

Run the template against last month's real demand and count what would not have fitted. Adjust. With the clinicians' yes, apply it from an agreed date (level 3), leaving existing bookings untouched. Publish the rota with the cover rules on the same page.

### 7. Review after a month

Compare gaps, overruns, unused emergency slots, new patient wait and uncovered sessions with the month before. Change one thing at a time.

## Checks

- Every slot length and emergency rule was set by a clinician, and the sign-off is recorded.
- Protected admin time appears every week in the same place.
- No room or piece of equipment is double-booked.
- Every open session has desk cover and a named back-up.
- Breaks and hours follow the local rules the practice gave, with the line: *Check this with local rules before publishing.*
- The template was tested against real past demand, with the result shown.

## Output format

1. Demand summary (counts by type, day and hour)
2. Slot rules table, signed off by clinicians
3. Weekly template (day, clinician, room, slot type, length)
4. Weekly rota (person, session, role, breaks, hours)
5. Cover rules
6. Test against last month and the switch-on date

## Example of good output (short)

> **Made-up example: Linden Vets, two vets, three nurses, two receptionists (not a real practice).**
>
> **Slot rules (set by the vets):** consult 15 min; vaccination 10 min; two 15-min emergency slots at 8:30 and 14:00, released to routine at 10:00 and 15:30 if unused; 30 min protected admin at 12:30 daily.
>
> | Time | Vet 1, Consult 1 | Vet 2, Consult 2 |
> |---|---|---|
> | 8:30 | Emergency (release 10:00) | Consult |
> | 8:45 | Consult | Vaccination |
> | 12:30 | Admin (protected) | Admin (protected) |
>
> **Cover rule:** if a receptionist is off, Nurse 3 covers the desk 8:15 to 10:00; routine phone bookings move to callback until 10:00. No more than one receptionist on leave at once.
