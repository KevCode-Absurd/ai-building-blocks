# Practice KPI pack

## When to use

- The owner asks "how are we doing?" and gets a different answer each time.
- The practice system has reports but nobody reads them.
- A change was made (template, recalls, reminders) and needs measuring.
- A new practice manager needs a baseline in the first month.
- Targets exist but nobody knows how they are calculated.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the practice system's reports and exports, rotas, wait-list and recall reports. De-identified exports are enough.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft the pack in the practice's shared folder; set up saved report filters.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** schedule the monthly exports, update the live tracker and send the pack to the agreed recipients.

## What you need

1. The measures the owner cares about, and any targets, with who set them.
2. Exports for this month and last month (and the same month last year if seasonal): appointments by status and type, available clinical hours by clinician, recall report, new patients, wait-list activity.
3. The rota for the month (sessions open and covered).
4. Notes on anything unusual: a clinician on leave, a closure, a system change.

## Steps

### 1. Write the definitions first

Agree each definition with the owner before any number. For example:
- **Bookings:** appointments with a booked status in the month, by type.
- **Utilisation:** booked clinical minutes divided by available clinical minutes. Say whether protected admin time, breaks and emergency slots count as available.
- **No-show rate:** no-shows divided by appointments that should have happened (attended plus no-shows plus late cancellations, as the practice defines it).
- **Late cancellation rate:** cancellations inside the practice's cut-off divided by the same base.
- **Recall completion:** patients due in the period who booked within the agreed window, divided by patients due.
- **New patients:** first visits in the month.
- **Wait-list fills:** freed slots filled from the wait list.
- **Uncovered sessions:** open sessions with no desk or support cover.

Write the definitions on the pack itself, so next month's numbers mean the same thing.

### 2. Clean the data

List rows dropped or changed and why (test patients, duplicates, cancelled-by-practice). Remove names; use counts.

### 3. Calculate and show the arithmetic

For each measure: this month, last month, target if set, and the sum written out ("42 no-shows / 1,180 due = 3.6%"). Break the biggest movers down by clinician, appointment type, day or hour.

### 4. Explain what changed

One line per measure that moved, with the likely cause and the evidence: a clinician's leave, a new template, school holidays. If the cause is unknown, say so. Do not borrow outside averages to judge the practice; trend its own numbers. Use a published benchmark only with its source, date and setting.

### 5. Pick one action

Name one change for next month, the measure that will show whether it worked, and who owns it, in the Plan-Do-Study-Act way: small, measured, then keep, adjust or drop.

### 6. Send and file

At level 3, send the pack to the agreed people on the agreed day and file it with the exports used. Keep the definitions page stable; if a definition changes, note it and restate last month on the new basis.

## Checks

- Every measure has a written definition on the pack.
- Every figure shows its arithmetic and traces to an export.
- No patient names or details appear.
- No outside benchmark appears without a source and date.
- Targets show who set them.
- The pack ends with one action, an owner and a measure.
- Billing, coding and payer questions go to the certified biller; ask a qualified accountant about anything tax or finance.

## Output format

1. Headline (three lines: what went well, what slipped, the one action)
2. KPI table (measure, definition, this month, last month, target, arithmetic)
3. Breakdowns for the two biggest movers
4. What changed and why
5. Next month's action, owner and measure
6. Data notes (exports used, rows dropped, definitions changed)

## Example of good output (short)

> **Illustrative structure, not a real business: a therapy practice with five therapists, October. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.**
>
> **Headline:** Utilisation up after the new template; no-shows flat; recall completion down because follow-up texts were paused for two weeks.
>
> | Measure | Definition | Oct | Sep | Target | Arithmetic |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Utilisation | Booked / available clinical minutes, admin time excluded | 81% | 76% | 80% (owner) | 30,780 / 38,000 |
> | No-show rate | No-shows / (attended + no-shows + late cancels) | 4.1% | 4.0% | 3.5% (owner) | 37 / 902 |
> | Recall completion | Booked within 6 weeks / due | 58% | 66% | 65% (owner) | 70 / 121 |
>
> **Action for November:** switch the follow-up texts back on from 1 Nov; owner: practice manager; measure: recall completion.
