# Deck layout plan

A slide is a glance medium. Duarte's Glance Test asks whether the audience gets a slide's point in 3 seconds or less; Alley's assertion-evidence approach puts that point in a sentence headline backed by a visual; Mayer's research says not to put on screen what the speaker is saying. This skill applies all three to a real deck.

## When to use

- A deck with too much text for the room.
- A talk, webinar or pitch built from notes.
- A deck that will be presented and also sent on without the speaker.
- Before rebuilding a deck in a new template.

## What you need

- The deck (pasted text, an export or access to the file) or the notes.
- The audience, the room or screen (projector, big screen, video call, laptop), the time slot, and whether the deck will be sent afterwards.
- The tool that builds the final file, and the template if there is one.
- The client's numbers for any chart, with their sources.
- Brand colours (hex) and fonts.
- Access: level 1 to read; level 2 to build in a copy marked "DRAFT"; level 3 to update the live deck or share it, within the limits set.

## Steps

1. **Inventory.** Count the slides and the words on each. List every number, logo, quote and name and where it came from.
2. **Find the story.** Write the deck's one main point in a sentence. List the three to five supporting points. For a pitch, check the order against a clear spine such as Sequoia's (purpose, problem, solution, why now, market, competition, business model, team, financials, vision).
3. **Write the headlines first.** One sentence per slide stating the takeaway. Read them alone, in order: they should tell the whole story (Minto). Rewrite any label-style heading ("Results") as a claim ("Repeat orders doubled after the price change").
4. **Fit the time.** Check the slide count against the slot and the pace the speaker talks at. If it doesn't fit, cut slides with the weakest points, not the strongest evidence.
5. **Split and merge.** Split any slide with two messages. Merge or cut any slide with none.
6. **Pick a layout for each slide.** Title, section, statement, two-column, chart, image, quote, process, closing. One dominant element per slide.
7. **Spec the evidence.** For each chart, the point, the chart type chosen by the relationship (change over time: line; comparing amounts: bar or column from zero; one fact: one big number; ranking: sorted bar), the data from the brief, direct labels instead of a legend, and the takeaway as the title. For images, what the image must show and where it comes from.
8. **Move detail to notes.** Anything the speaker will say goes in speaker notes, not on the slide. If the deck will be sent on, plan a read-alone version or an appendix.
9. **Set type and colour.** Body text well above Microsoft's 18 pt accessibility floor for a room; check contrast of text on its background against WCAG's 4.5:1 (normal) and 3:1 (large, 18 pt or 14 pt bold); colour never the only cue.
10. **Accessibility.** A unique title on every slide (hidden off the slide if needed), alt text for meaningful images, decorative images marked, reading order checked, captions for any video, Accessibility Checker before PDF export.
11. **Build notes.** For the named tool: slide size (PowerPoint defaults to 16:9, 13.333 x 7.5 in; set size before building), which master layout each slide uses, fonts and fallbacks (embed if allowed), animation only where it shows a sequence.
12. **Hand over or build.** At level 1, deliver the plan. At level 2, build in a "DRAFT" copy. At level 3, after the confirmed action list, update the live deck or share it with the people named, and log each action.

## Checks

- Headlines alone tell the story in order.
- Every slide passes a 3-second glance: one point, one dominant element.
- Every number, logo and quote is from the brief, or marked [add].
- Charts start at zero where they compare amounts and carry a takeaway title.
- Contrast and type size checked; titles, alt text and reading order done.
- Slide count fits the slot.
- All content from the original deck is placed, moved to notes, or listed as cut with a reason.

## Output format

1. **Story:** main point, supporting points, headline list.
2. **Layout plan table:** slide number, headline, layout type, zones and contents, visual or chart spec, speaker notes.
3. **Cut and moved list:** what left the slides and where it went.
4. **Style notes:** type sizes, colours with contrast results, image rules.
5. **Build notes** for the named tool.
6. **Open questions** and any [add] items.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bike hire firm giving an 18-slide talk in a 20-minute slot at a regional tourism conference. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

**Main point:** Small rental firms can win repeat visitors by selling the second day, not the first.

| # | Headline | Layout | Zones and contents | Visual | Speaker notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Half our riders asked about a second day, and we had nothing to sell them | Statement | Headline centred; source line bottom left | None | The counter log from last summer; how we noticed |
| 5 | A two-day pass tripled second-day rentals in one season | Chart | Headline top; chart full width; source bottom | Column chart: second-day rentals by month, 2025 vs 2026, from client's sheet, axis from zero, direct labels | Price of the pass; what we stopped doing |
| 6 | Three things made the pass sell | Three-column | Headline top; three icons with four-word labels | Icons for map, locker, café voucher | One sentence on each; detail moved from old slides 6 to 8 |

**Cut and moved:** old slides 6 to 8 (bullet lists) merged into slide 6, detail moved to notes. Old slide 14 (partner logos) cut: two logos have no permission in the brief, marked [add if approved].

**Style notes:** headlines 40 pt, body 28 pt, labels 24 pt; text #1B2A38 on #FFFFFF (14.64:1, passes); accent #E07A1F is 3.01:1 on white, so it is used for shapes and chart highlights, and as text only at 40 pt headline size.
