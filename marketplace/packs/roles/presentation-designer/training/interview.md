# Interview: presentation and layout designer

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you show me an example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work (slides, workbooks, covers or forms).

## How you approach a job

1. **Walk me through your last project from the brief to the files you handed over.**
   Why it matters: shows the real method, not the textbook one.
2. **What do you ask before you open any design tool?**
   Why it matters: intake decides whether the layout fits the room, the printer or the person filling it in.
3. **How do you decide what a slide or page is for before you lay it out?**
   Why it matters: the one-point habit is the core of the craft.
4. **When a client sends a wall of text, what do you cut, what do you move, and how do you tell them?**
   Why it matters: captures their editing judgement and how they keep clients on side.

## Systems and templates

5. **Show me a template or style system you built. What did you decide once so nobody had to decide again?**
   Why it matters: templates are the reusable value this role adds to a team.
6. **How do you pick type sizes for a big room, a video call and a printed workbook?**
   Why it matters: reveals how they test legibility in practice.
7. **How do you handle brand colours that fail contrast?**
   Why it matters: a common conflict between brand rules and readability.
8. **What goes wrong with fonts when files move between people, and how do you prevent it?**
   Why it matters: font embedding and licensing break many handoffs.

## Charts and evidence

9. **How do you choose a chart, and what do you strip out of the ones clients send?**
   Why it matters: chart choice and honesty are a distinct skill.
10. **Tell me about a time the client's numbers didn't support the point they wanted to make. What did you do?**
    Why it matters: tests their line on misleading visuals and invented proof.

## Covers and print

11. **How do you work out a book's spine and wrap size, and where has it gone wrong?**
    Why it matters: print errors are expensive and specific to each printer.
12. **How do you brief a cover artist, or write AI prompts, so the title has room and the genre reads at a glance?**
    Why it matters: briefs decide the quality of the art.
13. **What do you tell authors about AI art, disclosure and rights?**
    Why it matters: platform disclosure and copyright limits now shape cover decisions.
14. **What's on your preflight checklist before a file goes to a printer?**
    Why it matters: a reusable quality bar.

## Forms and workbooks

15. **How do you fix a form that keeps coming back half-filled?**
    Why it matters: form redesign is evidence-led, and their method is reusable.
16. **How do you size writing spaces and decide between tick boxes and free text?**
    Why it matters: small decisions that make paper forms work.
17. **How do you handle legal or consent wording and personal data on forms?**
    Why it matters: the agent must leave legal text to the client and ask only for what is needed.

## Accessibility

18. **What do you check for accessibility on decks and PDFs, and with which tools?**
    Why it matters: titles, alt text, reading order and contrast are part of a professional finish.

## Access and handoff

19. **Do you work inside clients' Canva, Figma, Google or Microsoft files? What do you need before you change a shared template?**
    Why it matters: sets the trained agent's level 2 and level 3 habits.
20. **What does your handoff include so the client's team can edit the work without you?**
    Why it matters: build notes and usage guides decide whether the work lasts.

## Judgement

21. **How do you know a deck or form worked after it shipped?**
    Why it matters: separates outcomes (understood, completed, accepted by the printer) from looks.
22. **Tell me about your hardest layout job: the brief, what went wrong, and what you would do differently.**
    Why it matters: the richest source of judgement for the trained agent.
