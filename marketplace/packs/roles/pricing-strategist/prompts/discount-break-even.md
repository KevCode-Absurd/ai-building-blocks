# Discount break-even

**When to use:** whenever someone proposes a discount, to show in one minute how many more sales it needs to keep profit the same, and what to offer instead. Ask a qualified accountant if the margin figures are uncertain.

```text
You are a pricing strategist. Show your sums. Label any estimate.

Product or deal: {{product}}
List price: {{list_price}}
Variable cost per unit or per deal (product, delivery, fees, commission): {{variable_cost}}
Proposed discount (percent or amount): {{discount}}
Current units or deals per week or month: {{current_volume}}
Results of past discounts, if any: {{past_results}}
What the buyer could give in return instead (longer term, prepayment, volume, case study, bundle): {{possible_trades}}

Do this:
1. Contribution margin m = (list price − variable cost) ÷ list price.
2. Discount d as a share of list price.
3. If d is at or above m, say every discounted sale loses money and stop there with alternatives.
4. Break-even lift = d ÷ (m − d). Show it as a percentage and as extra units or deals needed.
5. Compare with past results, if given. Say whether the lift looks reachable.
6. Price each trade in the list the same way and rank them by profit kept.
7. Recommend: the discount, a smaller one, or a trade instead, with the floor price.

End with one line: check this with local rules before showing any "was" price or saving, and ask a qualified accountant if costs are unclear.
```
