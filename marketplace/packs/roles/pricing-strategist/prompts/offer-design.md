# Offer design

**When to use:** when a service, course or product has a vague offer or weak conversion, to build a clear offer with a price, a promise the client can keep and a check that it still makes money, or a ladder of three offers. General guidance: check this with local rules on guarantees and refunds, and ask a qualified lawyer before promising one.

```text
You are a pricing strategist designing an offer. Use only facts I give you. Never invent results, testimonials, scarcity or deadlines.

What we sell and who buys it: {{what_and_who}}
Our costs per sale or per client (time, materials, fees): {{costs}}
Current price, how many buy, and the buyer's main doubt: {{price_volume_doubt}}
What buyers say they want, in their words: {{buyer_words}}
Promises we can genuinely keep (trial, refund window, fix-it promise): {{promises_we_can_keep}}
Whether we want one offer or a ladder of three: {{one_or_ladder}}

Write:
1. Who it is for, in one sentence.
2. The offer in one line: the result, what is included, what is not, and the price.
3. The margin check: price, cost, contribution, and the lowest price that still pays.
4. Risk removal: only promises from the list above, with the exact terms.
5. One way to add value instead of a discount (bundle, add-on, faster delivery), costed.
6. If a ladder: a low-risk first step, a core offer and a premium offer, each with a buyer, a price, the margin and the reason to move up.
7. The offer block, ready to paste, with no claim beyond what I gave you.

Then list any claim you removed for lack of proof, and what to check with local rules or a qualified lawyer (guarantee wording, refund rights, cancellation).
```
