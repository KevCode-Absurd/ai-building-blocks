# Discount and promotion rules

## When to use

- Before a sale, seasonal event or launch offer.
- When salespeople give discounts to close and margin is leaking.
- When a "was" price, percentage, deadline or "free" offer needs checking before it goes live.
- After a promotion, to set rules for the next one.

This is general practice, not legal advice. Price display rules differ by country and sector: check this with local rules, and ask a qualified lawyer before a claim you are unsure of.

## What you need

1. Products or plans in scope, current prices and contribution margin for each (price minus variable costs).
2. Price history for at least the last 30 days for each product, longer if prices moved, and the dates of past promotions.
3. The planned discount, its dates, and what happens to the price afterwards.
4. For sales-led businesses: the discounts given in the last few months, by rep and deal, and why.
5. Mandatory fees (delivery, booking, joining, service) and how prices are shown today.
6. Markets: which countries see the offer.

**Access:** level 1 to read prices, discounts and orders. Level 2 to set up discount codes switched off, a scheduled sale or draft quote rules. Level 3 to start and end approved promotions on approved dates and apply approved quote rules. Any new "was" price or deadline outside the approved plan, and any discount below the floor, needs a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Work out the break-even lift

For each product, with contribution margin m and discount d as shares of the original price:

- Break-even lift = d ÷ (m − d).
- If d is at or above m, every discounted sale loses money.

Show a small table for the depths being discussed. Then ask: is that lift plausible from the client's own past promotions? If they have readouts, use them; if not, say so.

### 2. Count the hidden costs

Pull-forward (sales taken from the weeks after), sales to buyers who would have paid full price, extra returns, and training buyers to wait. If past data exists, measure the dip after the last promotion.

### 3. Prefer trades and value over price cuts

Bundles, a gift with purchase, free delivery above a threshold, a longer term, prepayment or volume. Each keeps the list price intact. Cost each one the same way.

### 4. Write the discount policy (sales-led businesses)

One table: discount type, reason, cap, what the buyer gives in return, floor price, end date or review date, approver. Add a stacking rule (how discounts combine) and a log so every discount given is recorded.

### 5. Check the display for each market

- **US:** a "was" or "regular" price must be a bona fide price openly offered for a reasonably substantial period in the recent regular course of business; a trivial reduction is not a "Sale"; "price elsewhere" and list prices need substantial sales at that price; "free" and multi-buy offers cannot raise the regular price or cut quantity or quality, and terms are clear at the outset; limited offers must be limited (FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing). Live-event tickets and short-term lodging must show the total price with known mandatory fees (FTC fees rule, since 12 May 2025). California requires most advertised prices to include mandatory fees except government taxes and fees and reasonable shipping.
- **UK:** the comparison price must be a genuine, realistic usual selling price you can demonstrate; not one charged briefly, rarely sold at, flip-flopping, undercut since, a short-lived hike or an old price (CMA advice, 2023). Deadlines must be real and not followed by the same deal; falsely limited-time offers are banned under the DMCC Act. Headline prices include mandatory fees and taxes; no drip pricing (CMA209). "Free" means the buyer pays nothing beyond unavoidable costs.
- **EU:** a percentage or "price highlight" is measured from the lowest price in at least the 30 days before the reduction (Court of Justice, Aldi Süd, 2024); the total price includes taxes and charges; extras need consent, not a pre-ticked box.

### 6. Set the end and the after

Write the end date and time, what the price returns to, and when the next offer may run. Make sure ads, emails and pages stop on time.

### 7. Plan the readout

Baseline weeks, the measure (contribution, not units), the comparison products, and when to read it (including the weeks after).

## Checks

- Break-even lift shown for every depth.
- No discount takes a product below its floor.
- Every discount in the policy has a reason, cap, trade, floor, end date and approver.
- Every "was" price has price history behind it for each market.
- Deadlines are real and offers end on time.
- Mandatory fees are in the headline price where required.
- New "was" prices, deadlines or below-floor discounts are listed for the client's fresh yes.
- The deliverable says to check this with local rules.

## Output format

1. **Break-even table:** product, margin, discount, break-even lift.
2. **Promotion plan:** offer, dates, end rule, price after, cost, expected lift (labelled as an estimate), readout plan.
3. **Discount policy table** (if sales-led).
4. **Display check:** per market, each claim marked pass, fix or cut, with the fix.
5. **For your fresh yes:** anything outside the plan.

## Example of good output

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a skincare brand that sells in the UK in pounds and in Ireland through a euro store. Contribution margin 62%. Black Friday options. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

| Discount | Break-even lift | Sum |
|---|---|---|
| 15% | 32% | 15 ÷ (62 − 15) |
| 20% | 48% | 20 ÷ (62 − 20) |
| 30% | 94% | 30 ÷ (62 − 30) |

Last year's 30% sale lifted units by about 60% for the week (client data), short of the 94% needed. **Recommendation:** a gift-with-purchase (a travel-size cream costing £2.10) on orders over £35, plus 15% off the hand cream only.

**Display check:**
- UK "Was £18, now £15.30" on the hand cream: **pass**, £18 is the usual selling price, charged since March with steady sales.
- Ireland "Was €21, now €17.85": **pass** only if €21 was the lowest price in the 30 days before the sale; history shows €21 since March, confirmed.
- "Ends Monday midnight" with a timer: **pass** only if the offer switches off at midnight Monday and no near-identical offer follows; scheduled to end, confirmed.
- "Free gift": **pass**, no extra charge beyond normal delivery.
- Delivery £3.95 on orders under £35: **fix**, show it beside every price in the email and ads, not only at checkout.

Check this with local rules for Ireland and the UK, and ask a qualified lawyer if the gift has conditions not shown here.
