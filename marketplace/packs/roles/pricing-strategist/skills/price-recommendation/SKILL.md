# Price recommendation

## When to use

- A new product, service or package needs a price.
- A price has not moved in years, or margins are slipping.
- An owner wants to move from hourly or cost-plus pricing to packages priced on value.
- Before a price rise, to know how many customers can leave before profit falls.

This is general pricing practice, not legal, tax or accounting advice. Check this with local rules for how prices must be shown, and ask a qualified accountant how tax (VAT or sales tax) belongs in the displayed price.

## What you need

1. What is being priced and who buys it.
2. Costs: direct cost per unit or job, time and its loaded cost, platform and payment fees, and overheads with how to share them.
3. The client's minimum margin, or permission to suggest one.
4. Current price and sales volume, if it exists.
5. Rival prices the client found or that are public, with what each includes. Never anything a rival shared privately.
6. Buyer evidence: interview or call notes, survey answers, win and loss reasons, reviews. If none, I write the questions first (the buyer-pricing-questions prompt).
7. Markets: which countries, for how the price is shown.

**Access:** level 1 is enough (exports, pasted figures, accounting reports). Level 2 lets me build the model in a copy of the client's sheet or prepare a new price unused in billing. Level 3 lets me apply an approved price for new buyers within the agreed limits. Raising the price for existing customers needs a fresh yes every time.

## Steps

### 1. Build the cost and the floor

Cost per unit = direct cost + time × loaded hourly cost + fees + overhead share. Show each line. Floor = cost per unit ÷ (1 − minimum margin). Nothing is offered below the floor.

### 2. Put rival prices on the same basis

For each rival: headline price, what it includes, what it leaves out, contract length, fees, delivery. Adjust to the client's offer using the rival's own add-on prices where they exist; otherwise mark it "not comparable" rather than guess. Use prices only; never contact a rival about prices.

### 3. Read the buyer evidence

Sort comments into "too expensive" (the buyer values it but has a budget limit), "not worth it" (the value is not clear or not there) and "worth it". Count each. Note the outcomes buyers name in their own words; they shape the package and the price story. If a survey used the Van Westendorp questions, give the acceptable range and say its limits: no rivals in view, no volume forecast, no costs.

### 4. Set the options

Give three price points across a range: near the low end of rivals on the same basis, at the suggested price, and a stretch price. For each: margin per unit, margin %, and the volume needed to match current profit.

For a price rise: maximum share of customers you can lose before profit falls = 1 − (old contribution per customer ÷ new contribution per customer). Show it.

### 5. Recommend one price

One suggested price with three reasons: the floor it clears, where it sits against rivals on the same basis, and what buyers said. State the risk and what would change your mind.

### 6. Plan a small test

New buyers only, a set period, one measure (profit per visitor or per deal), and a stop rule. Existing customers keep their price until the client decides on a rise separately.

### 7. Check the display

How the price will be shown in each market: total price including mandatory fees where the law asks (UK; California for most consumer prices), no "was" price unless it is genuine for that market. See the discount-and-promotion-rules skill.

## Checks

- Every cost line is shown and adds up.
- The floor is above cost with the minimum margin.
- Rival prices are on the same basis, or marked not comparable.
- Buyer evidence is real and counted, or marked missing.
- Each option shows margin and the volume needed.
- The suggested price clears the floor.
- The test is small, has a stop rule and leaves existing customers alone.
- The deliverable says to check this with local rules and when to ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.

## Output format

1. **Cost build table** and the floor.
2. **Rivals on one basis** table.
3. **Buyer evidence** summary with counts.
4. **Options table:** price, margin per unit, margin %, volume needed, risk.
5. **Recommendation:** one price, three reasons, the risk.
6. **Test plan:** who, how long, measure, stop rule.
7. **Display note** per market, and questions for a qualified adviser.

## Example of good output

*Harbour Lane Bookkeeping (a made-up business). Monthly bookkeeping for small firms, now billed at $75 an hour, about 6 hours a client a month ($450).*

**Cost build:** 6 hours × $38 loaded staff cost = $228; software $25; overhead share $60. Cost per client = **$313**. Minimum margin 30%: floor = $313 ÷ 0.70 = **$447**.

**Rivals on one basis** (up to 100 transactions a month, monthly reconciliation, no payroll):

| Rival | Headline | Adjustment | Same basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | $450 | Includes payroll for 5 staff; rival's own payroll add-on is $60 | $390 |
| B | $520 | None | $520 |
| C | $600 | Includes a quarterly review call; no add-on price | Not comparable |

**Buyer evidence (12 client calls):** worth it 6, too expensive 3, not worth it 3. Eight mentioned a fast month-end close.

| Price | Margin per client | Margin % | Customers you can lose vs today |
|---|---|---|---|
| $480 | $167 | 34.8% | 18% |
| **$520** | **$207** | **39.8%** | **34%** |
| $560 | $247 | 44.1% | 45% |

(Today's contribution is $450 − $313 = $137. At $520: 1 − 137 ÷ 207 = 34%.)

**Recommendation:** $520 a month as a fixed package with a month-end close by the 5th. It clears the floor by $73, matches rival B on the same basis, and sells the outcome eight clients named.

**Test:** offer it to new clients for eight weeks; keep it if profit per proposal sent beats the last eight weeks at hourly rates (the decision rule you approve before starting). Existing clients stay on hourly until you decide on a rise.

Check this with local rules for how fees are shown, and ask a qualified accountant whether sales tax applies to the package.
