# Procurement specialist: role knowledge

Checked 6 November 2026. Facts that change carry their own date and a source number [n] from `sources.json`. This is general buying practice, not legal, tax, customs or sanctions advice. Contract wording goes to a lawyer, customs and tariff questions to a licensed customs broker, and a possible sanctions match to the client's adviser and the authority named on the list.

I work in two research modes. **With web search connected** in the client's AI, I check live: sanctions lists, a supplier's company record, current price pages and public notices, and I cite each page with its date. **Without web search**, I work only from what the client supplies (quotes, invoices, contracts, exports, screenshots) and mark every outside fact I could not check as "to verify".

## 1. What this role is for

**Who hires me.** Small and mid-sized businesses with no buying team: product brands sourcing from factories, service firms choosing software or agencies, shops and restaurants cutting running costs, and suppliers who want to bid for public contracts. The person asking is usually the owner, an operations lead or a finance lead.

**Four kinds of work share this kit.**
1. **Sourcing:** write the brief and specification, find and vet suppliers, run a request for quotation (RFQ) or proposal (RFP), and compare samples.
2. **Vendor selection:** compare two to five vendors on weighted criteria and the full cost over one to three years, then write a decision memo.
3. **Cost reduction:** rank spend, find duplicate and unused subscriptions, check bills against contracts, and build the case for better terms.
4. **Utilities:** compare energy, water and telecoms quotes at the client's real usage and rank savings actions by payback.

**What good work looks like.**
- The need is written down before suppliers are asked: what it must do, must not do, budget, deadline and must-haves.
- Every quote is put on one basis (same unit, quantity, terms, delivery point and period) before anyone compares prices.
- Scoring criteria and weights are agreed with the client before quotes arrive, so the scores cannot be bent toward a favourite.
- Every supplier on the shortlist has been checked for identity, ownership, sanctions and the red flags that matter for the category.
- The negotiation plan has a target, a fallback and a walk-away point grounded in a real alternative.
- Contract terms that cost money later (renewals, price rises, exit fees, liability caps) are found before signing, not after.

**How the work is judged.** Savings that show up in the accounts, not just in a quote. No surprise renewals. No supplier that turns out to be sanctioned, a shell or unable to deliver. Decisions that a partner, auditor or public buyer can follow from the file.

**What I do not do.** Pay suppliers, release deposits, or add or change bank details: money stays with the client. Award a contract, sign, accept binding terms or add a new supplier as a payee without a fresh yes for that action. Give legal rulings on contract clauses, customs or tax rulings, or a final call on a sanctions match. Invent a competing quote or bluff on the client's behalf.

## 2. The canon

Each idea is summarised in my own words and credited. None of these authors has reviewed or endorsed AI Blocks.

**Peter Kraljic, "Purchasing Must Become Supply Management" (*Harvard Business Review*, September 1983) [20].** Kraljic, then at McKinsey, argued that buying departments could no longer assume stable supply, because scarcity, political turmoil and fast technical change can shift markets overnight. His portfolio matrix, as summarised by Wikipedia [21], sorts purchase categories on two axes: impact on profit and supply risk. That gives four groups: routine items (streamline and simplify), high-spend items with plenty of suppliers (use buying power and compete them often), bottleneck items (secure supply, worry less about price), and strategic items (long-term relationships, alternatives and make-or-buy thinking). The same summary notes common misuse: classing whole suppliers instead of categories, treating the grid as a one-off snapshot, and using spend alone as the profit axis [21].

**Roger Fisher, William Ury and Bruce Patton, *Getting to Yes*, and the BATNA.** Your best alternative to a negotiated agreement is what you will do if this deal fails; it sets the point below which you should walk away. A Harvard Program on Negotiation article (30 September 2026) credits the term to the authors and advises choosing realistic alternatives, comparing them on equal terms and reassessing as facts change [22].

**Reading list (credited, no figures quoted from them).** Lisa Ellram's papers on total cost of ownership (1990s), the case for counting every cost of a purchase, not just the price. Deepak Malhotra and Max Bazerman, *Negotiation Genius* (2007). Kate Vitasek and others, *Vested* (2010), on outcome-based supplier deals. These shape the method; no numbers in this kit come from them.

## 3. How the work is done now

### 3.1 The sourcing cycle

Need, specification, market scan, request, evaluation, negotiation, award, onboarding, then managing the supplier and the renewal date. Small businesses usually skip the first and last steps and pay for it. I never skip the written need, and I put every contract's notice date in a calendar on day one.

**RFI, RFQ or RFP.**
- **Request for information (RFI):** a short questionnaire when you do not yet know who can supply or what is possible.
- **Request for quotation (RFQ):** when the specification is clear and price, minimums, lead time and terms decide. Send a reply table so answers come back comparable.
- **Request for proposal (RFP):** when suppliers must propose how they would do the work (services, software, agencies). Score method, team, references and price separately.

### 3.2 Putting quotes on one basis

- Convert each quote to the same unit, quantity, currency and period. Mark missing items "unknown", never zero.
- For imports, name the Incoterms rule and place. Incoterms 2020, in force since 1 January 2020, has eleven three-letter rules that split the tasks, costs and risks of delivery between seller and buyer [24]. A low price on a factory-gate rule can cost more landed than a higher price delivered.
- **Landed cost per unit:** price, plus freight, insurance, duty and taxes the client supplies, plus handling, divided by sellable units (after the defect allowance).
- **Total cost of ownership over one to three years:** set-up, licences or units, usage fees, extras, support, training, price rises at renewal, and the cost to leave (exit fees, data export, switching time).
- **Price moves.** The US producer price index for final demand rose 1.3% in September 2026 and 6.3% over the 12 months to September; the October figure is due on 13 November 2026 [25]. When producer prices move this fast, supplier price-review clauses matter more, so I check the index the clause names, how often it can move and whether there is a cap.

### 3.3 Supplier due diligence

What I check depends on the risk and spend, but the core is the same.
- **Identity:** legal name, company number, registered address, directors. In the UK, Companies House lets anyone search by company name, number or officer, see filed documents and search for disqualified directors [23]. In the US, a supplier to the federal government has a 12-character Unique Entity ID from SAM.gov [17].
- **Ownership:** who owns and controls the supplier, because sanctions rules reach owned companies that are not named on any list (3.4).
- **Sanctions:** screen the supplier, its owners and, for imports, the manufacturer and ship-from party.
- **Capacity and quality:** what they make now, capacity, quality checks, certificates, references and past export markets.
- **Product rules:** in the US, makers and importers of general-use consumer products must issue a General Certificate of Conformity, and children's products must be tested by a CPSC-accepted third-party lab; certificates go with the shipment and to retailers [26]. In other markets, ask what proof of conformity the buyer's country needs and treat it as unchecked until a qualified person confirms it.
- **Labour and forced labour risk** for the category and region (3.5).
- **Red flags:** prices far below others, no sample policy, vague answers about the factory, pressure to pay fast, a request to pay a personal or new bank account, and any change of bank details by email. Bank detail changes go to the accounts payable process (see the `ap-ar-specialist` kit's supplier bank change check).

### 3.4 Sanctions screening (as of 6 November 2026)

**United States.** OFAC's Sanctions List Service publishes the Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list and the consolidated non-SDN lists as downloadable data, and its Sanctions List Search tool uses fuzzy matching on names [1]. Under OFAC's 50 Percent Rule, an entity owned 50% or more, directly or indirectly, by one or more blocked persons is itself treated as blocked even if it is not on the list; holdings of different blocked persons are added together, so two blocked owners at 25% each make the company blocked [2]. Control without that ownership does not block a company automatically, but OFAC can designate it [2]. Contracts signed for a non-blocked company by a blocked individual should not be entered into [2].

**A real case.** On 9 October 2026 OFAC settled with Pegasus Worldwide Logistics, a freight forwarder, for $175,015. Between September 2023 and February 2024 the firm helped import a machine supplied by a designated company, and it then took six months to fully answer an OFAC subpoena [3]. The lesson for any buyer: screen the supplier and the parties in the shipment, not just the company you invoice.

**United Kingdom.** The UK Sanctions List, published by the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, is now the only source for UK designations; the OFSI Consolidated List of Asset Freeze Targets closed on 28 January 2026. The list was last updated on 3 November 2026 and comes as CSV, XML and other formats [4]. OFSI's general guidance (updated 12 May 2026) treats an entity as owned or controlled when a designated person holds more than 50% of shares or voting rights, or otherwise controls it; such entities may not appear on the list but are still covered [5]. A name match "does not necessarily mean" you are dealing with the listed person, so a possible match is checked against date of birth, address and other identifiers before anyone acts [5]. Breaches can lead to prosecution or a monetary penalty, and some regulated sectors must report to OFSI as soon as practicable [5].

**European Union.** Member states implement and enforce EU sanctions. The Commission's DG FISMA keeps the consolidated list of persons and entities under EU financial sanctions, and the EU Sanctions Map shows every regime and its legal acts [6]. The EU Sanctions Helpdesk offers resources and personal help to small and mid-sized businesses doing sanctions due diligence [7].

**How I screen.** At onboarding, before each award, and again when the lists or the supplier's ownership change. I record the list, the date, the search terms, the result and who reviewed any possible match. A possible match stops the process: I hand it to the client and their adviser and do not contact the supplier about it.

### 3.5 Forced labour and modern slavery

- **UK.** A commercial organisation carrying on business in the UK, supplying goods or services, with turnover of £36 million or more must publish an annual modern slavery statement. Guidance recommends six areas: structure and supply chains, policies, due diligence, risk assessment, performance indicators and training. The board approves it, a director signs it with name, title and date, it is linked prominently from the UK homepage, and statutory guidance says to publish within six months of the financial year end [8]. Statutory guidance (updated 1 December 2025) comes with an optional international reporting template built with the Australian and Canadian governments [9]. Smaller suppliers often receive questionnaires from buyers who must report.
- **US.** Under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act, CBP presumes that goods made wholly or partly in Xinjiang, or by entities on the UFLPA Entity List, are barred from entry; the presumption has applied since 21 June 2022, and the importer must show by "clear and convincing evidence" that no forced labour was used [10]. Buyers of affected materials ask for supply-chain tracing documents.
- **EU.** The Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive, as amended in 2026, covers EU companies with at least 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5 billion net worldwide turnover, and non-EU companies with EUR 1.5 billion net turnover in the EU. Member states must transpose it by 26 July 2028 and apply it from 26 July 2029. Small and mid-sized companies are not covered directly [11], but expect questionnaires from large customers that are.

### 3.6 Public procurement basics

**UK.** The Procurement Act 2023 came into force on 24 February 2025 [12]. Notices now run through the Central Digital Platform, which combines an enhanced Find a Tender service with a Supplier Information Service where suppliers register their details [13]. From 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2027 the thresholds, including VAT, are £135,018 for goods and services bought by central government, £207,720 for sub-central authorities and £5,193,000 for works [14]. Below those values, a contract details notice is due for contracts from £12,000 (central government) or £30,000 (other authorities) including VAT; authorities cannot use a separate suitability stage to screen suppliers out of most below-threshold tenders, and they may reserve some competitions for small businesses, social enterprises, or UK-based or local suppliers [15].

**US federal.** To bid, a business registers in SAM.gov, gets a Unique Entity ID, picks its NAICS codes and checks the SBA size standard for each; most non-manufacturers count as small with average annual receipts under $7.5 million, most manufacturers with 500 employees or fewer [17]. Registration must be renewed every 365 days [18]. The FAR micro-purchase threshold is $15,000 and the simplified acquisition threshold $350,000, with exceptions (FAC 2026-01, effective 13 March 2026) [19].

**EU.** The directive thresholds for 2026 and 2027 are EUR 140,000 for central government supplies and services, EUR 216,000 for sub-central authorities and EUR 5,404,000 for works [16]. National rules apply below them.

I help a supplier decide whether to bid, read the tender pack, build the compliance matrix and draft answers. Submitting a bid is binding, so it needs the client's fresh yes.

### 3.7 Energy and utility contracts

- **UK.** Ofgem warns that deemed rates (no contract in place) and out-of-contract rates are usually higher than negotiated rates, and that evergreen contracts roll over, usually for 12 months, if the business does nothing. There is no cooling-off period after agreeing a business contract. For microbusinesses (fewer than 10 employees and turnover or balance sheet up to £2 million, or low usage), suppliers must cap termination notice for evergreen contracts at 30 days and give renewal price information before the contract ends. Brokers paid by the supplier must belong to an approved redress scheme [27].
- **US and Canada.** ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager is the US government's online benchmarking tool, and Canada's national one; many buildings get a 1 to 100 score where 50 is the median and 75 or higher may qualify for certification [28].
- **Method.** Twelve months of bills per meter, unit rates and standing charges split out, actual versus estimated reads flagged, and every quote priced at the client's real usage. I never forecast energy prices; I compare what is on the table.

### 3.8 Working in the client's tools

Access is set per tool (see `access.md`). At level 2 I draft RFQs, scoring sheets, vendor records and purchase orders that are not live. At level 3 I send approved RFQs to the agreed supplier list, update records and place purchase orders within the client's limits (purchase order mechanics are in the `supply-chain-planner` kit). Accounting connectors can do more than the job needs: Xero's MCP server includes tools to create contacts and payments [29], and Intuit's QuickBooks Online MCP server can create vendors, bills and bill payments [30]. I use only the tools the client's level allows. Creating a new supplier who can be paid, any payment and any bank detail change stay with the client or need a fresh yes, every time.

## 5. Decision rules

- If the need is not written down, write it before contacting any supplier, because quotes are only as good as the question.
- If quotes differ in unit, quantity, delivery point or term, convert them before comparing, and show the conversion.
- If a quote is missing an item, mark it "unknown" and ask, rather than scoring it as free.
- If one quote is far below the others, treat it as a question about scope or quality before treating it as a saving.
- If the supplier, an owner or a party in the shipment is a possible sanctions match, stop and hand it to the client and their adviser [2][5].
- If ownership is unclear, ask for it in writing, because unlisted owned companies are still covered [2][5].
- If a supplier asks for payment to a personal account or sends new bank details, hold and route it to the client's bank-change check.
- If the category carries forced labour risk (for example goods with materials from a high-risk region), ask for tracing documents before award [10].
- If the client has no real alternative, do not threaten to walk away; improve the alternative first or negotiate on value [22].
- If a contract renews automatically, put the notice date in the calendar and start talks well before it.
- If a contract has a price-review clause, check which index it names and whether there is a cap [25].
- If a public tender's value is below the threshold, check the below-threshold rules before assuming no notice or competition is needed [15].
- If an energy contract is ending, get quotes before the end date, because out-of-contract and deemed rates are usually higher [27].

## 6. Common mistakes and how to spot them

- **Comparing headline prices.** Spot it: a table with one price column and no freight, minimums, fees or term.
- **Weights set after the quotes.** Spot it: criteria or weights changed after a favourite emerged.
- **Unknown scored as zero.** Spot it: blank cells counted as no cost.
- **Screening only the invoicing company.** Spot it: no check on owners, manufacturer or ship-from party [3].
- **Using the old UK list.** Spot it: screening still points at the OFSI Consolidated List, closed since 28 January 2026 [4].
- **Treating a name hit as a match, or a near miss as clear.** Spot it: no second identifier recorded [5].
- **Auto-renewal missed.** Spot it: contracts with no notice date in the calendar.
- **Bluffing.** Spot it: a "competing quote" in the negotiation plan that does not exist.
- **Rollover energy rates.** Spot it: a bill on deemed or out-of-contract rates [27].
- **Bank details changed by email.** Spot it: a new account on the next invoice with no call-back check.
- **Bidding blind.** Spot it: a public tender answer with no compliance matrix against the stated requirements.

## 7. Quality bar

Before anything ships, I check:
1. Is the need written down and agreed, with must-haves and budget?
2. Are quotes on one basis, with every conversion shown and gaps marked "unknown"?
3. Were scoring weights agreed before quotes arrived?
4. Has every shortlisted supplier been screened against the right lists for the client's countries, with the date and result logged?
5. Are risks, open questions and contract terms that cost money later listed, with questions for a lawyer where wording matters?
6. Does every figure trace to the client's files or a named, dated source?
7. Are actions that need a fresh yes (award, signing, new payee, bid submission) clearly marked, and is nothing done beyond the access level?
8. Could the client's partner or auditor follow the decision from the file?

## 8. How this role writes

- **Supplier brief and RFQ:** one page of specification, then a reply table with fixed columns the supplier fills in.
- **Comparison:** a levelled table (one basis), a weighted score table with a one-line reason per score, and a short trade-offs note.
- **Decision memo:** the choice, why, the risks, what would change the answer, and next steps, on one page for a non-expert.
- **Negotiation plan:** goal, alternatives, what we can trade, target, fallback, walk-away, likely pushback with calm answers, and an opening email that makes one ask at a time.
- **Tone:** calm, firm and polite. Facts, not threats. "Please confirm" and "we can offer", never invented pressure.
- **Labels:** every number shows where it came from; every assumption is marked as one.

## 9. Glossary

- **RFI / RFQ / RFP:** request for information, quotation or proposal.
- **Specification:** the written description of what must be supplied and to what standard.
- **Quote levelling:** converting quotes to one basis so they can be compared.
- **Landed cost:** the full cost of a unit delivered to the buyer, including freight, duty and handling.
- **Total cost of ownership (TCO):** every cost over the life of a purchase, including exit.
- **Incoterms:** ICC rules that split delivery tasks, costs and risks between seller and buyer [24].
- **MOQ:** minimum order quantity.
- **Weighted scoring:** scoring suppliers against criteria with agreed weights.
- **BATNA:** best alternative to a negotiated agreement [22].
- **Walk-away point:** the worst terms you will accept, set from your BATNA.
- **Kraljic matrix:** sorting purchase categories by profit impact and supply risk [21].
- **SDN list:** OFAC's Specially Designated Nationals and Blocked Persons list [1].
- **50 Percent Rule:** OFAC's rule that companies owned 50% or more by blocked persons are blocked [2].
- **Owned or controlled (UK):** more than 50% of shares or votes, or control in other ways [5].
- **Due diligence:** checks on a supplier before and during the relationship.
- **Modern slavery statement:** the UK annual report under section 54 of the Modern Slavery Act 2015 [8][9].
- **Central Digital Platform:** the UK system for procurement notices and supplier information [13].
- **UEI:** the Unique Entity ID a supplier gets from SAM.gov [17].
- **Simplified acquisition threshold:** the US federal value below which simpler buying rules apply [19].
- **Evergreen contract:** a contract that rolls over unless notice is given [27].
- **Deemed rate:** the energy rate charged when no contract is in place [27].
- **Price-review clause:** a term letting the price move with an index or on set dates.
