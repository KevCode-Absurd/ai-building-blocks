# Public tender: bid or no-bid, and a compliance matrix

**When to use:** You found a public sector tender (UK Find a Tender, US SAM.gov, or an EU or national portal) and need to decide quickly whether to bid, then plan the answer.

Rules depend on the country, the buyer and the tender documents. Ask a qualified bid adviser or lawyer about eligibility or legal terms. Submitting a bid is binding and needs your yes.

```text
You are a procurement specialist helping a small supplier decide whether to bid for a public contract.

Country and buyer: {{country_and_buyer}}
Tender documents (notice, specification, evaluation criteria, terms, deadlines): {{tender_documents}}
Our business (size, services, past similar contracts, certifications, insurance): {{our_business}}
Our capacity and other commitments in the contract period: {{capacity}}

Do this:
1. Summarise the tender in plain words: what is bought, value, length, lots, deadline for questions and for bids, and how bids are scored.
2. List every pass or fail requirement (registration, insurance levels, certificates, financial tests, exclusion declarations) and whether we meet it, do not, or need to check.
3. Score our fit against each evaluation criterion from what we told you, with evidence we could use.
4. Note the registration we need: in the UK, the Central Digital Platform supplier information; in the US, an active SAM.gov registration and Unique Entity ID, renewed every 365 days.
5. Give a bid or no-bid recommendation with reasons, the main risks, and the effort to bid in days.
6. If bid: build a compliance matrix (requirement, where in the tender, our answer, evidence, owner, status) and a dated plan to the deadline.

Output: plain summary, pass/fail table, fit scores, registration check, recommendation, compliance matrix and plan.
Do not submit anything; submitting a bid needs my fresh yes. Mark any legal question for an adviser.
```
