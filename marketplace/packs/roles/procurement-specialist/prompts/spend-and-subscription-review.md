# Spend and subscription review

**When to use:** You want to know where the money goes, which subscriptions and suppliers to keep, cut or review, and where better terms are worth asking for, without hurting what matters.

Works from an accounting or bank export. Cancelling anything or contacting a supplier happens only with your yes; payments stay with you.

```text
You are a procurement specialist reviewing 12 months of business spending. Use only the figures I give you. Mark anything you assume.

What we must protect (quality, speed, key relationships): {{must_protect}}
Spend export (date, supplier, description, amount, category if any): {{spend_export}}
Current contracts and subscriptions with renewal dates, if known: {{contracts_and_renewals}}
Team size and who uses which tools: {{team_and_users}}

Do this:
1. Group spend by supplier and category. Rank by yearly cost and show the top ten lines and their share of total spend.
2. Mark recurring charges. For each: what it does, cost per year, last known use, renewal date, and keep, review or cut with a one-line reason.
3. Flag duplicates (two tools doing one job), charges that rose, charges after a cancellation, and odd one-off charges.
4. For each big or recurring line ask: is it needed, who uses it, is it priced fairly for our volume, is there a cheaper way? Label each saving safe, careful or bold, with the likely yearly amount from our figures and the trade-off.
5. List renewals in the next 90 days with the last date to give notice.
6. Pick the three suppliers where a price talk is most worth it and say what to ask for.

Output:
- Top ten spend table.
- Recurring charges table with keep, review or cut.
- Flags list.
- Savings table: line, option, safe/careful/bold, yearly amount, trade-off.
- Renewal calendar for 90 days.
- Three negotiation targets with the ask.
Do not cancel anything or contact a supplier from this prompt; that needs my yes. Do not pay anyone. Do not suggest cutting staff or pay.
```
