# Negotiation and contract terms check

Most small businesses negotiate on price alone, once, under deadline pressure. This skill prepares the talk on facts and real options, and reads the terms that decide what the deal really costs over its life.

Ask a qualified lawyer about what any clause means or whether it is enforceable. This skill flags terms and writes the questions; it gives no legal opinion.

## When to use

- Before a price talk with a new or existing supplier.
- Well before a renewal or notice date, while there is still time to get other quotes.
- When a draft contract or order form arrives for signature.
- When prices have crept up and the client wants a better deal without losing the supplier.

## What you need

1. The current contract or draft, last 12 months of invoices, and usage.
2. The levelled quotes and scores, if a sourcing exercise was run.
3. The client's real alternatives: other quotes received, doing it in-house, or waiting.
4. What the client must protect: quality, a delivery date, a relationship, cash.
5. What the client could offer: volume, longer term, faster payment, a case study, flexible delivery dates.
6. Who signs and who can approve concessions.

## Steps

### 1. Establish the alternative
Write down what the client will do if this deal fails, priced on the same basis as the current offer. If there is no real alternative, say so: the plan then focuses on improving the alternative (one more quote, a smaller order) or on value rather than threats. Never invent a competing quote.

### 2. Set target, fallback and walk-away
- **Target:** the result to ask for, backed by the evidence (market quotes, volume, price index the contract names).
- **Fallback:** an acceptable result if the target fails.
- **Walk-away:** the worst terms worth accepting, set from the alternative.
Write each in money and terms, not just "a discount".

### 3. Widen the table
List what matters beyond unit price: payment terms, delivery dates, minimum order, price-hold period, service levels, free samples or training, exit terms. Rank them for the client and guess the supplier's ranking. Trades work best where the two rankings differ.

### 4. Plan concessions and pushback
For each ask, what the client can give back and what it costs. Predict the supplier's likely answers ("costs have gone up", "this is our best price", "we need a longer term") and write a calm, factual reply to each.

### 5. Draft the opening email
Short and polite. State the relationship, the facts, one clear ask, and a date to talk. Hold the second ask for the call. At level 2 it sits as a draft in the client's mailbox. At level 3 I send it to the existing supplier contact after the client approves the text. Anything that offers or accepts binding terms needs a fresh yes.

### 6. Check the contract terms
Read the draft or current contract against this list, quote the clause number for each finding, and mark it fine, ask, or lawyer:
- **Price:** fixed for how long; review dates; the index named and any cap; currency and exchange risk.
- **Term and renewal:** start, length, auto-renewal, the notice period and the exact last date to give notice.
- **Volume:** minimums, take-or-pay, forecasts that become commitments.
- **Payment:** terms in days, deposits, late payment interest, early payment discount.
- **Service levels:** measures, how they are reported, credits and whether credits are the only remedy.
- **Liability and insurance:** caps, exclusions, the supplier's insurance.
- **Termination and exit:** for convenience, for breach, fees, data return and format, help with handover.
- **Data and IP:** who owns the work and the data, where data is held, subprocessors.
- **Changes:** whether the supplier can change terms or prices by notice.
- **Law and disputes:** which country's law and courts.
For a full plain-words reading of a long contract, use the `contract-plain-summary` skill in the `compliance-specialist` kit.

### 7. Write the renewal or negotiation brief
One page: what changed since signing, the cost now and after the proposed change, the asks in order, what we can give, target, fallback and walk-away, the terms to fix, and the dates (notice deadline, talk date, decision date).

### 8. Record the outcome
After the talk, log what was agreed, what is still open, and the new notice date. Signing, accepting a binding offer or giving notice to end a contract needs the client's fresh yes.

## Checks

- The alternative is real and priced on the same basis.
- Target, fallback and walk-away are written in money and terms.
- No invented quotes, deadlines or threats.
- Every contract finding cites a clause and is marked fine, ask or lawyer.
- The last date to give notice is in the calendar.
- Nothing binding was sent or signed without a fresh yes.

## Output format

1. Negotiation brief (one page): facts, alternative, target, fallback, walk-away, trades, pushback and answers.
2. Opening email draft.
3. Contract terms table: topic, clause, what it says, status (fine, ask, lawyer), question.
4. Key dates.
5. Outcome log after the talk.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a 20-person design agency renewing a project management tool. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Facts:** 20 seats; price rose from $14 to $19 per seat per month at the last renewal; renews 1 June; notice due by 2 May (clause 9.2).
> **Alternative:** a quote from a rival tool at $15 per seat, plus about two days of migration time.
> **Target:** $15 per seat for two years, price hold until June next year. **Fallback:** $16.50 with a 5% cap on the next rise. **Walk-away:** above $17, or no cap: switch.
> **We can offer:** a two-year term, annual payment up front, a short case study.
>
> | Topic | Clause | Says | Status | Question |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Renewal | 9.2 | Auto-renews 12 months; 30 days' notice | ask | Can notice move to 60 days with a reminder? |
> | Price changes | 4.3 | May change price on renewal by notice | ask | Add a cap tied to a named index |
> | Data on exit | 12.1 | Export "on request", no format | lawyer | Is a free export in CSV within 30 days enforceable as written? |
>
> **Opening email:** "We have valued the tool for three years. With the last rise our cost is up by more than a third, and we are reviewing options before our 2 May notice date. Could we talk this week about a two-year renewal?"
