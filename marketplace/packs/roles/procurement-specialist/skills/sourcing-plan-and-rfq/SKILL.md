# Sourcing plan and RFQ

Quotes are only as good as the question. This skill makes sure the need is written down, the criteria are fixed before prices arrive, and every supplier answers in the same format so the answers can be compared.

## When to use

- A new product, part, packaging or material needs a supplier.
- The business is choosing software, an agency, a contractor or a service provider.
- A contract is ending and the client wants to test the market before renewing.
- Quotes keep coming back in different shapes and cannot be compared.

## What you need

1. **The need:** what the purchase must do, what it must not do, quantities or users, where it ships or who uses it, timing.
2. **Must-haves:** certificates, materials, data location, service hours, insurance, anything that disqualifies a supplier.
3. **Budget or target price**, and the first order size or contract length.
4. **Suppliers to ask:** the client's list, or permission to search (with web search connected) and propose a long list.
5. **Constraints:** who must approve, deadline, existing contracts and notice dates.
6. **Access:** level 1 is enough to plan; level 2 to draft emails in the client's mailbox; level 3 to send the approved request to the agreed list.

## Steps

### 1. Write the need on one page
Restate the need in the client's words, then list must-haves, nice-to-haves and deal-breakers. If the specification is vague, say so: a vague brief returns quotes that mean little. Ask the two or three questions that matter most and stop there.

### 2. Choose the request type
- **RFI** when you do not yet know who can supply or what is possible. Short questions, no price.
- **RFQ** when the specification is clear and price, minimums, lead time and terms will decide.
- **RFP** when suppliers must propose how they would do the work. Ask for method, team, references and price separately.

### 3. Set criteria and weights before anyone quotes
Five to eight criteria the client agrees, each with a weight that adds to 100 and a plain description of what scores high and low. Typical families: total cost (not unit price), quality or fit to spec, delivery or lead time, service and support, risk (financial, sanctions, single source), terms (payment, exit, liability). Write the weights into the plan and do not change them after quotes arrive without telling the client why.

### 4. Write the brief or specification
For goods: materials, sizes, tolerances, colours, quality rules, packaging, labels, what a good sample looks like, target price, order size and timing, Incoterms rule and named place for imports. For services and software: outcomes, volumes, service levels, data and security needs, integrations, exit and data return.

### 5. Build the reply table
Fixed columns the supplier fills in, so answers come back comparable: unit price by quantity break, currency, minimum order, lead time from order to ship and to arrival, sample cost and time, payment terms, what the price includes and excludes, validity date, warranty, set-up and exit costs, and named contact. Add "Anything not included above?" as the last row.

### 6. Add the supplier questions
Identity and ownership (legal name, company number, owners), what they make or deliver now, capacity, quality checks, certificates, references, past export markets, subcontractors. These answers feed the `supplier-due-diligence-and-sanctions-check` skill.

### 7. Plan samples and timeline
What samples to ask for, how many, at whose cost, and how each will be scored against the spec. Then dates: request out, questions closed, quotes due, samples due, scoring, negotiation, decision. Work backward from the date the client needs supply.

### 8. Draft and send
At level 2, draft one email per supplier in the client's mailbox, each with the brief and reply table attached. At level 3, after the client approves the text and the list, send to the agreed suppliers only, from the agreed mailbox, and log each send. A supplier not on the list needs a fresh yes.

### 9. Record the file
Keep the plan, the brief, the list of who was asked, the date sent and every reply in one folder. This is the trail a partner, auditor or public buyer will ask for.

## Checks

- The need, must-haves and deal-breakers are written and agreed.
- Criteria and weights were fixed before any quote arrived.
- The reply table forces like-for-like answers, including what is excluded.
- Every supplier received the same brief and the same deadline.
- Nothing in the request commits the client to buy.
- Sending happened only at level 3, to the agreed list, after the client approved the text.
- No passwords, bank details or card numbers appear anywhere.

## Output format

1. Sourcing plan (one page): need, must-haves, request type, criteria and weights, supplier list, timeline, approvals.
2. Supplier brief or specification.
3. Reply table (spreadsheet tab or table in the email).
4. Supplier questions.
5. Sample request and scoring sheet (goods only).
6. Draft emails, or a send log at level 3.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a UK online candle shop. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

> **Need:** 3,000 amber glass jars, 200 ml, with bamboo lids, for a spring range. Delivered to Leeds by 15 February.
> **Must-haves:** food-safe glass certificate, lids fit within 0.5 mm, jars packed in dividers. **Deal-breakers:** no samples, or payment to a personal account.
> **Request type:** RFQ to five suppliers.
>
> | Criterion | Weight | Scores high when |
> |---|---|---|
> | Landed cost per sellable jar | 35 | Lowest after freight, duty and breakage |
> | Sample quality against spec | 25 | Lid fit and glass clarity pass every check |
> | Lead time to Leeds | 15 | Arrives by 1 February with buffer |
> | Supplier risk | 15 | Identity, owners and sanctions check clear; references given |
> | Terms | 10 | No more than 30% upfront; clear defect policy |
>
> **Reply table columns:** price at 1,000 / 3,000 / 5,000; minimum order; ship and arrival dates; Incoterms rule and place; sample cost; payment terms; what is excluded; quote valid until.
> **Timeline:** RFQ out 6 Jan, questions close 10 Jan, quotes due 14 Jan, samples due 21 Jan, decision 24 Jan.
