# Source and award one purchase

**Trigger:** a new product, service, software or supplier is needed, or a contract is up for re-tender. Typical length: two to six weeks, mostly waiting for supplier replies and samples.

**Access:** set at the start, per tool (see `access.md`). Steps marked level 2 or level 3 need that level in the named tool; at a lower level I hand you the draft to send yourself. Until you choose, I work at level 1. Awarding, signing, adding a payee and paying stay with you or need your fresh yes.

## Steps

1. **Intake (day 1).** Agree the need, must-haves, budget, deadline, who approves, and the access level per tool. Ask whether web search is connected. *Approval: you confirm the written need.*
2. **Sourcing plan (day 1 to 2).** Use the `sourcing-plan-and-rfq` skill: request type, criteria and weights, supplier long list, timeline. *Approval: you confirm the criteria, weights and supplier list before anything goes out.*
3. **Light screening of the long list (day 2).** Run the identity and sanctions steps of the `supplier-due-diligence-and-sanctions-check` skill so no request goes to a listed party.
4. **Send the request (day 3).** Level 2: drafts in your mailbox. Level 3: send the approved request to the agreed list only, and log each send. Answer supplier questions to all suppliers at once.
5. **Samples (goods only).** Use the `sample-request-and-check-sheet` prompt, then score each sample on the sheet.
6. **Level and score (after the quote deadline).** Use the `quote-levelling-and-supplier-scoring` skill: levelled table, scores, sensitivity test, decision memo with a shortlist.
7. **Full due diligence on the shortlist.** Complete the `supplier-due-diligence-and-sanctions-check` skill at the depth the risk needs. A possible sanctions match stops the process and goes to you and your adviser.
8. **Negotiate and check terms.** Use the `negotiation-and-contract-terms-check` skill: alternative, target, fallback, walk-away, opening email, contract terms table with questions for your lawyer. Level 3: send the approved opening email to the supplier contact.
9. **Award (fresh yes).** Present the final memo, terms and risks. *Approval: you decide and authorise the award and the signature. I send the award and decline notes only after that yes.*
10. **Onboard.** Prepare the supplier record (inactive), the purchase order draft and the payment set-up checklist at level 2. *Approval: you or your finance team make the supplier payable, enter bank details and release any deposit.*
11. **Close the file.** Save the plan, requests, replies, scores, checks, terms and approvals in one folder. Put the renewal and notice dates in the calendar (level 3) and set the next due diligence review.

## Outputs

- Written need and sourcing plan
- Request pack and send log
- Sample scores (goods)
- Levelled quote table, scores and decision memo
- Due diligence and screening log per shortlisted supplier
- Negotiation brief and contract terms table
- Award and decline notes (sent after your yes)
- Inactive supplier record and purchase order draft
- Action log (what, where, when, result)

## Needs the client's approval

**Runs within your limits after one confirmation.** Sending the approved request to the agreed suppliers, answering supplier questions from the agreed mailbox, sending the approved opening email, and adding dates to the shared calendar (level 3).

**Always needs a fresh yes, or stays with you.** The award and any message telling a supplier they have won; signing or accepting binding terms; adding a new supplier as a payee and any bank details; paying deposits or invoices; contacting any supplier not on the agreed list; acting on a possible sanctions match; and any legal question, which goes to your lawyer.
