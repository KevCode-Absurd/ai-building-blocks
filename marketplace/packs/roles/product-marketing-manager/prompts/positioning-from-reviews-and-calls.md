# Positioning from reviews and calls

**When to use:** at the start of positioning work, to turn raw buyer material into the alternatives, values and words the message sheet is built from. Use before writing any homepage or launch copy.

```text
You are a product marketing manager finding positioning from buyer evidence, using April Dunford's order: competitive alternatives, unique capabilities, value, best-fit buyers, market category. Quote only what is in the material below. Never invent a quote, a number or a review. Mark anything you infer as "Inference:".

Product and price: {{product_and_price}}
Who the team thinks buys it: {{assumed_buyer}}
Customer reviews (with source and date for each): {{reviews}}
Sales or onboarding call notes: {{call_notes}}
Reasons for recent won and lost deals: {{win_loss_reasons}}
Current homepage text: {{homepage_text}}
Is web search connected? If yes, also read these rival URLs and record the date you read each: {{rival_urls_or_none}}

Do this:
1. List every competitive alternative buyers mention, including doing nothing or a spreadsheet, with how often each appears and a quote for each.
2. Flag any rival the team fears that buyers never mention ("phantom competitor").
3. For the top three alternatives, list what our product does that each does not, using only evidence above.
4. For each difference, write the value in buyer terms and the proof we hold, or "No proof yet: test it".
5. Describe the best-fit buyer by situation and trigger, citing the reviews or calls that show it.
6. Suggest two market frames (an existing category, and a sub-segment of it) and say which makes the value most obvious and why.
7. List 10 words or phrases buyers use that our homepage never does.
8. Write a draft one-line positioning statement: "For [who] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [value]. Unlike [alternative], it [difference]."
9. List the three switching interviews you most want next, and five questions to ask (about what happened, not opinions).
```
