# Competitor comparison and claims check

## When to use

- A "vs" page, "alternatives to" page or comparison table is planned or live.
- An ad, pricing page or sales sheet says "best", "fastest", "cheapest", "number one" or "save X% versus".
- Sales wants a comparison sheet to send to buyers.
- A rival, a customer or a regulator has questioned a claim.
- A quarterly check of live comparison content, because rivals change pages and prices.

Internal battlecards are in the sales enablement specialist kit. This skill covers anything a buyer or the public will see.

## What you need

1. The draft or live page, ad or sheet.
2. The rivals to be named, and the markets the business sells to (US, UK, EU, others), and whether to consumers, businesses or both.
3. Evidence for each claim: test results with method and date, the client's own prices, published specs, independent reviews or reports.
4. Rival facts: read live with web search (URL and capture date for each), or pasted by the client and marked "as supplied on [date]". A rival's own marketing alone is not enough to support a claim about it.
5. Who signs off: the owner, a lawyer, or both.

**Access:** level 1 to read pages and evidence. Level 2 to draft the page in your CMS, unpublished. Level 3 does not cover publishing anything that names a competitor: that needs your fresh yes each time, even at level 3, after you have seen the claims file.

## Steps

### 1. Build the rival map

Table: alternative (include the status quo), main claim, target buyer, price model and current price, proof they show, capture date, source. Note claims many rivals share.

### 2. Choose what to compare

Pick features that matter to the buyer's decision, that meet the same need, and that can be checked: material, relevant, verifiable and representative (the CAP Code test). Drop rows where rivals' data is missing or unclear rather than guessing. Keep to about five options and the same attributes with comparable values in every column; give a "show differences" view on long tables.

### 3. List every claim

Write each claim on its own line, including implied ones ("switch and save" implies a saving; a tick and a cross imply a gap). Sort into: objective (can be measured), comparative (names or points to a rival), establishment ("tests show"), and obvious exaggeration nobody would take literally.

### 4. Match evidence to each claim

For each objective or comparative claim: the evidence held, its date, whether it covers the exact claim and the markets, and whether a reader can check it. Rules to apply:
- **US:** a reasonable basis before the claim runs; "tests show" needs that test. Truthful comparisons naming rivals are encouraged; deception is not. Fine print cannot fix a misleading headline; qualifiers sit beside the claim. A rival can sue under the Lanham Act or file an NAD challenge.
- **UK:** documentary evidence before publication; comparisons must not mislead, must be like for like and verifiable, and must not denigrate a rival or confuse buyers; an unqualified "best" compares with every rival. Savings against rivals must use real prices of named, comparable products (see the Nectar Sleep ruling, Aug 2026). B2B ads fall under the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008.
- **EU:** the comparative advertising conditions apply to consumer and business ads alike; unfair practices rules apply to consumer ads; environmental and durability claims face stricter rules from 27 September 2026.

### 5. Fix or cut

For each weak claim, offer a fix: narrow it ("faster to set up than [rival] in our test of 20 users, May 2026, method linked"), add the basis next to it, swap it for a provable difference, or cut it. Never fill a gap with invented data.

### 6. Make it verifiable

Add a "how we compared" note: what was compared, when, how, and where to check (a method page, a dated screenshot, the rival's pricing page URL). Date every rival fact on the page and set a re-check date.

### 7. Flag for the lawyer

List the claims a lawyer should review: any claim about a rival's product quality, any savings claim, any use of a rival's logo or trade mark, health, safety, environmental or financial claims, and anything aimed at a market not yet checked.

### 8. Ask for the fresh yes

Present the page, the claims file and the lawyer flags. Ask: "This names [rivals]. May I publish it, as shown, on [page], from [date]?" Record the answer, who gave it and the date.

### 9. After publication

Log the action. Set a re-check (rivals' prices and features, at least every review cycle). If a rival or regulator complains, stop, gather the evidence and draft a reply for the client; a formal reply needs its own fresh yes.

## Checks

- Every comparative claim has evidence the client holds, beyond the rival's own marketing.
- Like for like: same need, same plan level, same date.
- The reader can check each comparison from the page.
- Qualifiers sit next to the claim, not in a footnote.
- No denigration, no rival logo without lawyer review, no confusion about who makes what.
- Prices are real, current and named, with a date.
- Markets and audience (consumer or business) are stated and checked.
- The fresh yes is recorded before publication.
- The work says it is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Output format

1. Rival map (dated).
2. The comparison table or page draft.
3. Claims file: claim, type, evidence, source, date, markets checked, verdict (keep, fix, cut), fix wording, lawyer review (yes or no).
4. "How we compared" note for the page.
5. Approval record: who, when, what was approved.

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real business: shift-scheduling software for UK cafes, comparing itself with two named rivals. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> | Claim | Type | Evidence | Verdict | Fix |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | "The best rota app for cafes" | Comparative (superlative) | None | Cut | "Built for cafes: shift swaps in one tap" |
> | "Half the price of [Rival A]" | Comparative, price | Our Team plan vs Rival A Team plan, both monthly, captured 3 Nov 2026 | Fix | "£X less per month than [Rival A] Team, prices checked 3 Nov 2026" with link |
> | "Set up in 10 minutes" | Objective | Median set-up time 11 min, 40 new accounts, Oct 2026 | Fix | "Most cafes set up in about 11 minutes (Oct 2026 data)" |
> | "[Rival B] loses your data" | Comparative, denigrating | A forum post | Cut | None |
>
> **Lawyer review:** price comparison row. **Approval needed:** this page names two rivals; fresh yes required before publishing.
>
> *General guidance, not legal advice. Check comparative and price claims with a qualified lawyer in each market.*
