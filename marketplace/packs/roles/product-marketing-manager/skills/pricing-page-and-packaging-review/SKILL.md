# Pricing page and packaging review

## When to use

- A new product or plan structure needs a pricing page.
- Buyers or sales keep asking which plan fits.
- A sale, discount or "save X%" claim is planned.
- Fees, delivery or tax are added at checkout.
- The business starts selling in a new country.

Setting the price level itself (margins, unit economics) is in the growth marketer kit; price-test design is in the market research analyst kit. This skill covers packaging, the page and the claims on it.

## What you need

1. The current page (or plan list) and checkout flow, with screenshots of each step.
2. Plans, limits, add-ons, fees, taxes and delivery charges, with the markets each applies to.
3. Who buys each plan today (counts by plan, upgrades, downgrades, cancellations) if available.
4. Top questions from sales and support about plans and price.
5. Any planned sale or discount, with the prices charged before it and for how long.
6. Rival pricing pages if comparisons are planned: live with web search (URL and date), or pasted and marked "as supplied".

**Access:** level 1 to read the page, checkout and plan data. Level 2 to draft a new pricing page or table in your CMS, unpublished. Level 3 to publish approved wording and layout changes within limits; any change to the prices, fees, plan limits or guarantees customers see needs your fresh yes, and anything that names a competitor needs its own fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Map segments to tiers

List the buyer segments and what each values most. Check each tier serves one segment, has a "who it's for" line, and differs from the next by something that segment cares about (a limit, a feature, support). A common shape is a plain tier, a recommended middle tier and a top tier; use it only if three real segments exist.

### 2. Check limits and the reason to upgrade

Each tier states what is in, what is not, and the limit that moves a buyer up (seats, volume, features). Buyers should be able to pick a plan without a call, unless the business sells only through sales.

### 3. Test the price range if new

For a new product with no sales history, gather a first range from interviews or the four Van Westendorp questions, labelled as directional; it ignores rivals and does not predict volume. Do not present it as the answer.

### 4. Review the table

Plans as columns, features as rows; every cell has a comparable value; plain labels with short definitions; a way to see only differences on long tables; about five options at most; headers stay visible when scrolling; a mobile view that still allows comparison.

### 5. Check the total price for each market

- **UK consumers:** show the total including unavoidable fees and taxes up front; no fees first revealed at checkout; "from" prices include mandatory charges; subscriptions show the monthly or total cost and term clearly.
- **EU consumers:** prices include taxes and delivery charges; say when a price is personalised by automated decision-making.
- **US:** general deception law applies to fees and prices; live-event tickets and short-term lodging must show the total with mandatory fees most prominently (since 12 May 2025).
- **Business buyers:** say clearly whether prices exclude VAT or sales tax.

### 6. Check savings, sale and "free" claims

- **Sale prices:** in the EU, show the lowest price of the previous 30 days as the prior price. In the UK, the comparison basis must be clear and must not overstate the saving.
- **"Save versus other brands":** compare with a real, current price of a named, comparable product, not an average (ASA Nectar Sleep ruling, Aug 2026); this names rivals, so it also goes through `competitor-comparison-and-claims-check`.
- **"Free":** in the UK, nothing beyond the unavoidable cost of responding and delivery; "free trial" is not a money-back offer. In the US, "free" with a purchase must not hide a raised price, and terms sit near the price.
- **Annual discounts:** show the per-month figure and the amount charged up front side by side.

### 7. Check the guarantees and cancellation wording

Refund, cancellation and renewal terms are clear, near the price, and match the terms of service. Subscription and auto-renewal law differs by market and state: flag for the client's lawyer rather than ruling.

### 8. Write the recommendations

Rank fixes by risk first (legal and trust), then by buyer confusion, then by revenue upside. For each: the problem, the evidence, the fix, the access level needed, and whether it changes what customers pay.

## Checks

- Each tier maps to a real segment with a "who it's for" line.
- Every price shows what the buyer will really pay, per market.
- No fee appears first at checkout for UK or EU consumers.
- Every saving has a stated, real basis; EU reductions show the 30-day prior price.
- "Free" means free under the rules of each market.
- Comparison table values are consistent and checkable.
- Price changes customers see and anything naming a rival are marked for fresh approval.
- The review says it is general guidance and lists questions for the lawyer.

## Output format

1. Segment-to-tier map.
2. Findings table: issue, evidence, market, risk (high, medium, low), fix, access level, needs fresh yes (yes or no).
3. Redrafted tier cards and table (draft).
4. Claims to check with a lawyer.
5. What to measure after the change (plan mix, upgrade rate, support questions about plans).

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real business: an online booking tool for yoga studios selling in the UK and Ireland. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> | Issue | Market | Risk | Fix | Fresh yes |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | £3 "booking fee" first shown at checkout | UK, IE | High | Include in plan prices or show the total from the first price shown | Yes (price display) |
> | "Black Friday: was £49, now £29" but £49 charged for only 5 days in October | IE | High | Show the lowest price of the past 30 days as the prior price | Yes |
> | "Free forever" plan needs card details and auto-upgrades | UK | High | Rename "Free plan"; state upgrade terms beside it | Yes |
> | Middle and top tiers differ only by "priority support" | All | Medium | Move class-pack sales into the middle tier, the feature studios ask for most (support log, 41 requests) | No (wording) |
>
> *General guidance, not legal advice. Check this with a qualified lawyer in each market before publishing.*
