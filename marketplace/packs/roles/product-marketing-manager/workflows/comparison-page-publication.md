# Comparison page publication

**Trigger:** the client wants a "vs" page, an "alternatives to" page, a comparison table with rivals' names, or a sales sheet that compares with a named rival and will go to buyers. Also when a live comparison page reaches its re-check date.

**Cadence:** one to two weeks per page; then a re-check of rival facts at every review cycle (at least monthly for price comparisons).

**Access:** steps are marked L1 (read), L2 (read and draft) or L3 (read, write and publish within the client's limits). Publishing anything that names a competitor needs the client's fresh yes every time, even at L3.

## Steps

1. **Scope, L1.** Which rivals, which page type, which markets, consumer or business buyers, and who signs off (owner, lawyer or both). Record access levels.
2. **Rival facts, L1 (prompt: rival-claim-map).** With web search connected, read rivals' current pages and pricing and record URL and date; without it, use what the client supplies and mark it "as supplied on [date]". A rival's own marketing alone does not support a claim about it.
3. **Evidence, L1.** Gather the client's proof for each row: tests with method and date, own prices, specs, independent reviews.
4. **Draft the comparison, L1 (skill: competitor-comparison-and-claims-check; library: Comparison page set planner).** Like-for-like rows, consistent values, about five options at most, a "how we compared" note, dated rival facts.
5. **Claims file, L1.** Every claim typed (objective, comparative, establishment, obvious exaggeration), matched to evidence, with keep, fix or cut, and the markets checked against US, UK and EU rules.
6. **Lawyer flags, L1.** List claims for the client's lawyer: quality claims about a rival, savings claims, any use of a rival's logo or trade mark, regulated claims (health, environmental, financial), new markets.
7. **Build the page, L2.** Draft or pending page in the CMS, unpublished, with the method note and dates.
   **Approval point 1:** the client (and lawyer, where flagged) reviews the page and the claims file.
8. **Fresh yes, then publish, L3 or the client.** Ask: "This page names [rivals]. May I publish it, as shown, at [URL], from [date]?" Record who said yes and when. Publish only the approved version and log the action.
9. **Monitor, L1.** Set a re-check date for rival prices and features. Watch for complaints, rival responses, and ASA, NAD or regulator contact.
10. **If challenged, L1 then L2.** Stop changes to the page, gather the evidence trail, and draft a factual reply for the client. Sending a formal reply to a rival, the ASA, NAD or a regulator needs its own fresh yes; the client's lawyer should see it first.
11. **Re-check cycle, L1 then L2.** Refresh rival facts; if anything changed, update the draft and repeat the claims check and the fresh yes before republishing.

## Outputs

- Dated rival map and evidence log.
- Comparison page draft with a "how we compared" note.
- Claims file with verdicts and lawyer flags.
- Approval record (who, what, when).
- Action log for publication and edits.
- Re-check schedule.

## Needs the client's approval

- Access levels and limits for each tool.
- The draft and claims file (approval point 1), with lawyer review where flagged.
- A fresh yes before publishing or republishing any page, ad or sheet that names a competitor, every time.
- A fresh yes before any formal reply to a rival or regulator.
- Deleting or unpublishing a page, unless the client asked for it.
