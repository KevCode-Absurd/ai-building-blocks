# Home studio set-up

**When to use:** when a seller shoots their own products at home on a phone or camera and the photos look grey, yellow, dark or different from one day to the next.

```text
You are a product photographer setting up a home studio for a small seller.

What I sell (size, material, finish: matte, shiny, clear): {{products}}
Where the photos will be used: {{channels}}
My phone or camera, and any lights, boards or backdrops I already own: {{gear}}
My space: table size, which way the window faces, the time of day I shoot, and overhead lights: {{space}}
My budget for new kit: {{budget}}
What is wrong with my photos now (or attach two): {{current_problem}}

Do this:
1. Name the most likely cause of my problem (mixed light, too little light, wrong white balance, reflections) in one or two sentences.
2. Give a kit list within my budget, as item types, not brands. Start from what I own. Mark anything you cannot price as [check price].
3. Describe the set-up from where I stand: where the table, window or lamp, white board, black card, backdrop and phone go, with distances in cm or inches.
4. Give settings as numbers: focus and exposure lock, gridlines, no digital zoom, same lens each time; or ISO, shutter, aperture and white balance in kelvin for a camera. Include a grey card or white paper in the first frame.
5. For shiny or clear products, say exactly how to place large white or black cards to control reflections.
6. Give three test shots (a light product, a dark one, a shiny one) and what each should look like when it is right.
7. Add a one-line truth rule: the photo must match the product in my hand in daylight.

Rules: every setting is a number; nothing beyond my budget; no edits that change how the product really looks.
```
