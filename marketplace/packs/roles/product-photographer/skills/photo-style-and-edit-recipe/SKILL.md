# Photo style and edit recipe

Most "bad" product photos come from light, not from the camera. This skill fixes the light first, then locks a set-up and an edit so the hundredth photo looks like the first, and the product looks like itself.

## When to use

- Photos look grey, yellow, flat or too dark.
- A catalogue looks like several people shot it.
- A freelancer's shots and the seller's own phone shots need to match.
- A seller is setting up a corner of a room to shoot at home.

## What you need

- **What is sold** and where the photos will be used.
- **Gear:** phone or camera model, any lights, stands, boards or backdrops already owned, and the budget for anything new.
- **Space:** which way the window faces, the time of day they shoot, the size of the table, and any overhead lights.
- **The look they want**, in their words, and two or three photos they like (their own or described).
- **Current photos** and the editing app they use.

**Access:** level 1 to read photos and settings the client shares. The client applies the set-up and recipe themselves; with level 2 or 3 access to the store, edited files can be uploaded as drafts or live (see `channel-image-spec-and-ai-check`).

## Steps

1. **Describe the look in words.** Light (soft or hard, from which side), colour temperature (warm, neutral or cool), background (white, colour, texture), shadow (none, soft, defined), angle family (straight on, three-quarter, top-down), crop and space around the product, props and their rules. Add three do and don't pairs, such as "do: soft shadow to the right; don't: no shadow at all, the product floats".
2. **Start from what they own.** Plan around a window or one lamp. Add items only within the budget, named as item types (diffusion fabric, white foam board, black card, a light stand) rather than brands. Mark anything without a budget as "[check price]".
3. **Lay out the set from where they stand.** For example: "Table against the wall, window on your left, white board standing on your right 30 cm from the product, phone on a stand 60 cm in front at product height." Give distances in centimetres or inches. For shiny products, add a large white card above or a black card to the side to shape reflections, and say which.
4. **Give the settings as numbers.** Phone: lock focus and exposure on the product, gridlines on, no digital zoom (step back and crop instead), the same lens every time, flash off. Camera: ISO, shutter speed, aperture and white balance in kelvin, on a tripod. Grey card or white paper in the first frame of each session.
5. **Kill mixed light.** Overhead bulbs off when using window light; the window covered or the shoot moved to one light if lamps are used. Mixed warm and cool light is the most common cause of yellow-grey photos.
6. **Write the edit recipe.** A table of steps in order, with values: white balance (pick the grey card), exposure, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, contrast, texture or clarity (light touch), saturation (zero or near zero), crop ratio per channel, and export settings (size, sRGB, quality). Say how to save it as a preset and apply it to a batch in their app.
7. **Set the truth rule.** The finished photo must match the product held next to the screen in daylight. No changes to colour, shape, size, finish or flaws. If the recipe makes a colour drift, fix the light or the white balance, not the saturation.
8. **Write three test shots.** One white or light product, one dark product, one shiny or clear product. For each, what it should look like: whites white but not blown, details visible in dark areas, reflections soft and placed.
9. **Plan the check-in.** After the first batch, compare ten photos side by side for consistency, and adjust one thing at a time.

## Checks

- Every setting is a number with a unit; nothing says "a bit brighter".
- The set-up is described from the person's position.
- Spending stays within the stated budget.
- The recipe has been tried on a light, a dark and a shiny product.
- Colour matches the product in daylight; saturation has not been pushed.
- Export is in sRGB at the sizes each channel needs.

## Output format

1. **Style guide:** the look in words, with three do and don't pairs.
2. **Kit list** within budget.
3. **Set-up plan** from the person's position, with a simple top-down sketch in words.
4. **Settings table.**
5. **Edit recipe table** and how to save and batch-apply it.
6. **Three test shots** and what each should look like.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a maker of silver rings, shot on a phone at a kitchen table by a north-facing window. Budget: about the price of two foam boards and a fabric diffuser. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Look:** soft daylight from the left, neutral white, light grey shadow to the right, rings at a three-quarter angle, 15% space around each ring.

**Set-up:** table side-on to the window, window on your left. Hang thin white fabric over the window. White foam board on your right, 25 cm from the ring, angled toward it. Second white board curved behind as a sweep. Black card above the phone if the ring shows the phone's reflection. Phone on a stand 35 cm away, at ring height. Overhead light off.

**Settings:** tap and hold on the ring to lock focus and exposure, then lower exposure until the brightest silver edge just keeps detail. Gridlines on. Main lens only.

| Step | Setting | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | White balance | Pick on the white board |
| 2 | Exposure | +0.3 |
| 3 | Highlights | -40 |
| 4 | Shadows | +20 |
| 5 | Whites | +15 |
| 6 | Saturation | 0 |
| 7 | Crop | 1:1 for shop and Etsy first photo; export 2000 px, sRGB |

**Test shots:** plain band (silver should look grey-white, not blue); oxidised ring (dark recesses still show detail); stone ring (the stone shows its true colour next to the real ring in daylight).
