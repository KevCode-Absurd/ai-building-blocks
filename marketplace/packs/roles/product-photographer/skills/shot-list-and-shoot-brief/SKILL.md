# Shot list and shoot brief

A shoot day is expensive and short. The images that matter are decided before it starts. This skill turns a product list into a plan a photographer can quote from and shoot from without calling back.

## When to use

- New products or a new range need a full image set.
- A reshoot after listings were rejected or buyers kept asking questions the photos did not answer.
- Briefing a freelance photographer or videographer, or planning your own phone shoot.
- A short product video for a product page or marketplace.

## What you need

- **Channels:** every place the images will appear (store, Amazon, Etsy, Google Shopping, ads, social, email).
- **Products:** names, SKUs, sizes, colours, variants, what comes in the box, and samples available on the day.
- **Current photos** and the brand guide or style guide, if there is one.
- **Buyer questions:** reviews, return reasons and pre-sale questions.
- **Practical limits:** budget, number of shoot days, location, models, props on hand, deadline.

**Access:** level 1 to read listings and current images. Level 2 or 3 is only needed later, to upload the results (see `channel-image-spec-and-ai-check`).

## Steps

1. **Channel list first.** For each channel, note the image rules that shape the shoot: main-image background and fill, minimum and suggested size, aspect ratio, and what may not appear (text, props, other variants). Date each rule and link its source; check the knowledge file's table and the platform's own page on the day.
2. **Read the buyer questions.** Turn each repeated question into a shot: "is it big enough for a laptop?" becomes a scale shot with a laptop; "what's inside?" becomes an open shot.
3. **Build the core set per product.** Main image, angles, scale, detail, in use, what's in the box, packaging if it matters, and each variant. Add lifestyle shots only where a channel has a slot for them and a buyer question needs them.
4. **Give every shot one job and at least one channel.** Write the job in a few words ("shows thickness", "shows fit on a size 12 model"). Cut any shot that has no job or no channel. If the list does not fit the day, cut lifestyle before scale and detail.
5. **Specify each shot.** Angle and lens height, background, props (only included items for marketplace main images), crop and orientation per channel, and whether it is a still, a short clip or both.
6. **Write the brief.** Goal in one line; the look in plain words (soft or hard light, warm or neutral, background colour, shadow or no shadow); colour accuracy (a grey card or colour chart in the first frame of each set-up; product held next to the screen when reviewing); must-have shots; what to avoid; references described in words; model and location needs.
7. **Plan any video.** Scenes in order, shot sizes, length per channel, where on-screen text will sit, and spare footage (close-ups, hands, pours). Mark which clips double as stills.
8. **Build the day schedule.** Group shots by set-up and light, not by product, to cut changes. Put white-background sets together, then detail, then in-use and lifestyle. Add buffer time for set changes, product prep and reviewing a test frame on a big screen.
9. **Write the delivery spec.** File types and sizes per channel, colour space (sRGB for web unless a channel asks otherwise), how many edited picks per shot, file-name pattern (for Amazon, include the product identifier), masters kept unedited, alt text needed, and the delivery date.
10. **Settle rights in writing.** Who owns the images or what licence the client gets, for which channels and for how long; releases for models and locations; and whether the photographer may use the images in their own portfolio. This is general practice, not legal advice; the client checks contracts with a qualified adviser.
11. **List open questions** with a default for each, so the photographer can quote now.

## Checks

- Every channel has its rules written down, dated and sourced.
- Every shot has one job and at least one channel; nothing is there just because it looks nice.
- Main images follow each marketplace's rules: real product, correct background, no extra props or text.
- Scale and detail are covered for every product where buyers ask about size or quality.
- The schedule groups by set-up and includes buffer time.
- The delivery spec covers sizes, colour space, file names, masters and alt text.
- Rights and releases are covered in writing before the day.

## Output format

1. **Channel list** with rules, dates and sources.
2. **Shot list table:** product, shot number, shot, job, channel, background, props, crop, notes.
3. **Brief:** goal, look, light, colour accuracy, must-haves, avoid, references, people and places.
4. **Video plan**, if any.
5. **Day schedule** in time blocks.
6. **Delivery spec and rights terms.**
7. **Open questions** with defaults.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a backpack brand with eight backpacks, one studio day, sold on Shopify and Amazon. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Buyer questions from reviews:** "Does a 16-inch laptop fit?" (14 mentions), "How many pockets inside?" (9), "Is the grey more blue or green?" (6).

| Product | # | Shot | Job | Channel | Background | Crop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trail 22 L, grey | 1 | Front, straight on | Main image | Amazon, Shopify | Pure white | Square, product fills about 85% |
| Trail 22 L, grey | 2 | Open, laptop half inserted | Answers "does it fit a 16-inch laptop?" | Amazon, Shopify | White | Square |
| Trail 22 L, grey | 3 | Inside, pockets fanned out | Shows the 5 inner pockets | Shopify | White | Square |
| Trail 22 L, grey | 4 | Fabric close-up with grey card in frame | True colour of the grey | Shopify, Amazon | White | Square |
| Trail 22 L, grey | 5 | Worn by a 178 cm model, side view | Scale on a person | Shopify, Amazon | Light grey studio | Portrait 4:5 for Shopify, square for Amazon |

**Schedule (extract):** 08:30 set and light the white table, test frame, grey card. 09:00 to 11:30 shots 1 to 4 for all eight packs. 11:30 to 12:00 switch to model set. 12:00 to 14:00 shots 5 for all eight packs, with a 30-minute buffer.

**Delivery:** JPEG, sRGB, 2048 x 2048 for Shopify and Amazon squares; file names `SKU_shot-number`, with the product code as Amazon asks; three edited picks per shot; unedited masters delivered too.

**Rights:** client owns all images from the day; model release signed before shooting.
