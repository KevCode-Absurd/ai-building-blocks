# Client updates and reminders

Clients of expertise firms often feel lost between meetings. Clear updates cut the "just checking" calls, reminders get documents in on time, renewals keep clients, and a useful newsletter keeps the firm in mind for referrals. This skill writes those messages so each one does one job, in words a busy person understands.

These are general communications. Dates, rules, rates and cover details come only from the firm, for its jurisdiction. Nothing here is advice. Ask your regulator or a qualified adviser about anything rule-based, and have the firm's reviewer sign off.

## When to use

- New clients need onboarding: what to send, what happens when, who to contact.
- The firm wants a year of deadline or document reminders (accounting, tax, legal or compliance dates it supplies).
- A design or consulting project needs stage-by-stage updates.
- An insurance agency needs a renewal sequence.
- The firm wants a client newsletter, or to re-engage past clients and referral partners.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read current templates, lists and results. Level 2 to build sequences and newsletters as unsent drafts in the email tool or CRM. Level 3 to switch on approved sequences and send newsletters and reminders to the client lists the firm approved, after sign-off.

- The firm's process stages and what the client must do at each.
- The dates and deadlines the firm wants cited, for its jurisdiction, with the source it relies on.
- For insurance: the carrier-approved wording and any phrases that are required or banned.
- Client lists and segments, and how people opted in.
- The firm's tone, and a past email it liked.

## Steps

1. **List the moments.** Onboarding, each project stage, each deadline, renewal points, and natural check-ins between jobs.
2. **Give each message one job.** One action per email: send documents, approve a stage, book a review, reply with changes.
3. **Write with the standard frame.** What has happened; what comes next; what we need from you, by when, and how to send it; who to contact; when you will hear from us next.
4. **For reminders, use only the firm's dates.** State the date, what to send, how to send it, and what happens if it is late, using the firm's wording. Mark every date [FIRM TO CONFIRM] until checked.
5. **For renewals,** use the timing the firm sets. Invite a review of needs, say what to send, make replying easy, and use the carrier's approved wording. Never say what is or is not covered for a specific person; say "check your policy documents" or "talk to us".
6. **For newsletters,** pick one useful topic, use sources the firm provides, state the jurisdiction and date checked, label it as general information, and end with "speak to us about your own situation".
7. **For referrals and reactivation,** write short, honest messages: a thank-you, a specific reason to get in touch now, and an easy way to reply. No pressure, no incentives the profession's rules do not allow, and no messages to people who did not opt in where rules restrict contact.
8. **Check for advice and promises.** Mark anything that reads as advice, a promise of savings, refunds, outcomes or cover with [REVIEW].
9. **Get sign-off and schedule.** At level 3, switch on only approved sequences and send only to approved lists. Log each send.

## Checks

- Each message has one clear action and a named contact.
- Every date and rule comes from the firm, with jurisdiction and [FIRM TO CONFIRM] cleared.
- No advice, promises or guesses about cover, savings or outcomes.
- Insurance messages use approved wording.
- Newsletters are labelled general information and end with "speak to us".
- Lists are opted in and match the profession's contact rules.
- Plain words, short sentences, terms explained on first use.

## Output format

1. Message map: moment, audience, timing, job, channel.
2. Each message: subject line, body, call to action, sender.
3. Review list ([REVIEW] and [FIRM TO CONFIRM] items).
4. Sending plan: lists, dates, who signs off, what is switched on at level 3.

## Example of good output (short)

*Bramble Cover Agency is a made-up insurance agency, used only to show the format. All details are invented for this example.*

**Renewal reminder (timing set by the agency):**

Subject: Your home insurance renews on [date]

"Hi [first name],

Your home insurance with [carrier] renews on [date]. Before then, it's worth a quick check that your cover still fits your home.

Has anything changed this year? For example, building work, a new lodger, or expensive items you've bought. If so, reply to this email or call [name] on [number], and we'll go through it with you.

If nothing has changed, you don't need to do anything. Your renewal documents will arrive by [date]. Please check them carefully, because they set out exactly what your policy covers.

[Name], Bramble Cover Agency"

**Review list:** "'make sure you're fully covered' removed - [REVIEW] implied promise of cover; replaced with 'check that your cover still fits your home'."
