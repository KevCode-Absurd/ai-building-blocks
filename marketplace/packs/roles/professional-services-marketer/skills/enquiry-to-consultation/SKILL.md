# Enquiry to consultation

Most expertise firms lose good clients between the enquiry and the first meeting: slow replies, long forms, or a receptionist who says too much or too little. This skill builds a calm, quick path that collects what the firm needs, sets expectations, and gets the right people booked in.

This is marketing and process design, not advice. What the firm may ask, record and say before it agrees to act depends on its profession's rules. Ask your regulator or a qualified adviser, and have the firm's reviewer sign off scripts and templates.

## When to use

- Enquiries wait too long for a reply, or many never book.
- Staff answering calls are unsure what to ask or what they may say.
- A coach or consultant needs a discovery call flow that feels helpful, not salesy.
- A firm is setting up a new intake form, booking tool or email sequence.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read enquiry sources, response times and outcomes (client details removed). Level 2 to draft forms, scripts and email templates in the intake tool, switched off. Level 3 to turn on approved sequences, send approved replies and reminders, update contact stages and book consultations within the slots and lists the client set. Nothing that gives advice, quotes a binding fee or confirms the firm will act.

- The services offered and the clients the firm wants (and does not want).
- What the firm must know before a first meeting, and what it must check first (for law firms, the conflict check process).
- Who replies to enquiries, how fast today, and the reply time the firm can promise.
- The booking tool and the slots for consultations.
- Whether first consultations are free or paid, and how long they last.
- The firm's rules on what staff may say before the firm agrees to act.

## Steps

1. **Map today's path.** From each source (website form, phone, email, referral, directory) to booking. Note the delays and drop-off points.
2. **Write the acknowledgement.** Sent at once: thank you, what happens next, when a person will reply, what to do if it is urgent (for example, emergency services or a crisis line where relevant), and a privacy line. No advice.
3. **Design the questions.** Only what the firm needs to decide whether it can help, in the order a stressed person can answer: who they are, what kind of help, key dates or deadlines, how they heard of the firm. For law firms, ask only what the conflict check needs before the check is done.
4. **Write the call script.** Greeting; what this call can and cannot do ("I can't give advice on your situation, but I can book you with someone who can"); the questions; the next step; a polite close for people the firm cannot help, with a pointer elsewhere.
5. **Write the form.** Short, plain labels, optional fields marked, a privacy note, and the same "what happens next" message.
6. **Set the booking step.** A booking link with real slots, a confirmation that says what to bring or send, and reminders before the meeting.
7. **Write the follow-up.** For those who do not book: a short, helpful nudge with one new reason, then a polite close. No pressure tactics, and no follow-up where the profession restricts unsolicited contact.
8. **For coaches and consultants: shape the discovery call.** Questions about the client's goal and situation, a clear explanation of how working together looks and costs, and a respectful "this isn't a fit" when it is not.
9. **Add tracking.** Enquiry source, reply time, booked, attended, became a client, and reason lost.
10. **Get sign-off.** The firm's reviewer approves scripts and templates. At level 3, switch on only approved versions.

## Checks

- First reply time is set and achievable.
- Every script and template states what staff cannot advise on.
- Questions are the minimum needed; sensitive details are not collected before the firm's process allows.
- Urgent or safety situations have a clear pointer.
- Privacy line present; data goes only into the firm's approved tools.
- Follow-ups respect the profession's limits on unsolicited contact.
- Tracking shows where enquiries are lost.

## Output format

1. Current path map with delays and drop-offs.
2. Acknowledgement email and text.
3. Call script and intake form.
4. Booking confirmation and reminders.
5. Follow-up messages and close.
6. Tracking sheet columns.
7. Review list and sign-off record.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: an accounting firm. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Acknowledgement (sent at once):**
"Thanks for getting in touch with [Business]. A member of our team will reply by the end of the next working day. If you have a deadline in the next 7 days, reply with the date and we'll look at your message first. We can't give advice by email before we've agreed to work together, but we'll tell you if we can help and what it would cost. [Privacy line]"

**Call script (extract):**
"Before we start: I can't advise on your tax position today, but I can find out what you need and book you with one of our accountants. Is that OK?
1. Are you contacting us for yourself or a business?
2. What do you need help with: bookkeeping, payroll, a tax return or something else?
3. Is there a deadline coming up?
4. How did you hear about us?"

**Close for a poor fit:** "That's outside what we do, but [type of adviser] can help. You could start with [professional body's 'find an adviser' page]."
