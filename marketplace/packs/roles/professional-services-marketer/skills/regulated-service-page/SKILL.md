# Regulated service page

Buyers of expertise cannot test the work before they hire, so the page must earn trust on one read: it shows the firm understands their situation, explains what will happen and what it costs, and proves it plays by its profession's rules. This skill writes that page and builds the compliance check into the draft, not after it.

This skill writes marketing. It does not decide what a profession's rules allow. For that, ask your regulator or a qualified adviser, and get the firm's named reviewer to sign off.

## When to use

- A firm needs a new service, practice-area or "work with me" page.
- An existing page is vague ("we offer a full range of services") or reads like a brochure.
- Before a page goes live in a regulated profession, to run the claims check.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the current site, enquiries and rules. Level 2 to save the page as a draft in the website editor. Level 3 to publish it in the sections the client named, and only after the firm's reviewer has signed it off.

- The profession, the country or state of regulation, and the firm's reviewer.
- Who the service is for and the problems clients bring, in their words (forwarded enquiries with names removed are ideal).
- How the process works, step by step, and what the client sends and receives.
- Fees: actual prices, ranges or the fee basis, and what is included and extra.
- The people who do the work and their real qualifications.
- Proof the firm may use: reviews, testimonials with permission, case or project stories with permission.
- Required regulatory details (for example a regulator number, badge, complaints route).

## Steps

1. **Write the positioning line.** "We help [who] with [problem] so they can [result]." If it is vague, ask questions until it is not.
2. **List the client's questions.** From enquiries: what they worry about, what they ask before hiring, what confuses them. Each becomes a section or an FAQ.
3. **Check the rules that apply.** Find the profession's advertising rules for this country or state (see knowledge.md, "Rules by profession"). List what the page must include (for example price information for listed legal services in England and Wales) and what it must avoid (guarantees, misleading results, unsupported comparisons, restricted titles). Ask your regulator or a qualified adviser about anything unclear.
4. **Open with the problem.** First lines in the client's words, then one sentence on how the firm helps.
5. **Explain who it is for and not for.** This saves both sides time.
6. **Describe the process.** Numbered steps from first call to finish, with what the client does at each step and roughly how long it takes, using the firm's own figures.
7. **Explain fees.** Prices or ranges where the firm can give them, otherwise the fee basis (fixed, hourly, stage, commission) and what changes the cost. Say what is included and what is extra.
8. **Show the people.** Names, real qualifications and what each does. Use protected titles only for people who hold them.
9. **Add fair proof.** Reviews and testimonials the firm has permission to use, presented without implying everyone gets the same result. Where results vary, say so.
10. **Add required information.** Regulatory numbers or badges, complaints route, jurisdiction, and "general information, not advice" where the page explains rules.
11. **Write the next step.** One clear action (book a call, send documents, request a quote) and when the client will hear back.
12. **Run the claims check.** Mark every line that could read as advice, a promise, an outcome, a comparison or a superlative with [REVIEW] and a note on why.
13. **Hand to the reviewer.** Record who signed off and when. At level 3, publish only after sign-off.

## Checks

- The first lines describe the client's situation, not the firm.
- No guarantees, implied outcomes, or "best" and "leading" claims without proof.
- Fees or fee basis are stated where the rules or buyers expect them.
- Required regulatory information is present.
- Every testimonial has permission and fair context.
- Titles and qualifications are accurate.
- General information is labelled, with the jurisdiction.
- [REVIEW] marks are resolved and sign-off is recorded.
- A stressed first-time client could understand it on one read.

## Output format

1. Positioning line.
2. The page: headline, opening, who it is for and not for, process, fees, people, proof, FAQs, required information, next step.
3. Rules checked: profession, jurisdiction, the rules looked at, and what the page includes because of them.
4. Review list: each [REVIEW] line with the reason.
5. Sign-off record: reviewer, date.

## Example of good output (short)

*Lindqvist & Okafor is a made-up two-solicitor firm in England, used only to show the format. All details are invented for this example.*

**Positioning:** "We help families in Northshire sort out a loved one's estate when there is no dispute, so they can get on with grieving."

**Headline:** Help with probate when someone has died

**Opening:** "When someone close to you dies, the paperwork can feel endless. We handle the legal steps of sorting out their estate, explain each stage in plain English, and tell you what it will cost before we start."

**Fees (extract):** "Our fixed fee for uncontested probate where there is a will starts at [£X] plus VAT, for estates with [conditions]. Typical extra costs are the court fee and [disbursements]. [REVIEW: check against the SRA Transparency Rules list of required price information before publishing.]"

**Proof:** "'Patient and clear from the first call.' (Client review, Google, March [YEAR], shared with permission.)"

**Review list:** "'We make probate stress-free' - [REVIEW] implied outcome; suggest 'We explain every step so you know where things stand'."

**Required information:** SRA number and digital badge; link to complaints procedure.
