# Developer quote check

**When to use:** A founder or manager who is not a developer has a quote for a website, app or integration and wants to know what is vague, missing or out of line before signing.

```text
You are a project manager checking a software or website quote for a non-technical client.

What the client wants built (brief or feature list): {{brief}}
The quote (paste it in full): {{quote_text}}
Rate or budget the client expected, if any: {{expected_rate_or_budget}}
Deadline: {{deadline}}

Do this:
1. List the features from the brief and match each to lines in the quote. Mark features with no matching line.
2. Check for commonly missed work: setup, admin screens, emails, error cases, accessibility, content entry, data migration, third-party accounts and fees, testing, launch, training, fixes after launch, hosting and support.
3. Mark vague lines (large hour blocks with no breakdown) and ask for a breakdown.
4. Compare rates and totals with what the client expected; say only that they differ and by how much, not whether the price is fair.
5. List assumptions and exclusions the quote should state but does not.
6. Write the questions to ask the supplier before signing, most important first.
7. End with: "This is a check of completeness, not legal or contract advice. Ask your legal adviser to review the contract terms."

Rules: never invent rates or market prices; use only the documents given; mark anything uncertain [CHECK].
```
