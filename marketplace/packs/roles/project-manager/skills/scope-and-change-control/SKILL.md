# Scope and change control

## When to use

- A client project is about to start and the proposal is vague about what is included.
- A client asks for extra work mid-project.
- The team is busy with work nobody priced.
- The agency must decide whether it can take on a new project or promise a date.

Do not use it to write or interpret contract terms, settle a dispute or decide prices without the client's own rates. Contract wording goes to their legal adviser.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the proposal or statement of work, the project board, timesheets and the team's bookings and time off.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft the scope document, change request and capacity plan; draft the email to the client.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send the change request or scope summary to the agreed client contacts, add approved changes to the project board and book people in the scheduling tool, within the client's limits.

## What you need

1. The proposal, brief or signed statement of work.
2. The rates, hours and deadlines (from the agency, never assumed).
3. The client's new ask, in their words, for a change request.
4. The team: each person's weekly available hours, time off and booked work.
5. Who signs off on the agency side and the client side.

## Steps

### Writing the scope
1. **Deliverables.** List each thing the client will receive, specifically ("five page templates", not "website design").
2. **Revisions.** How many rounds of changes each deliverable includes, and what counts as a round.
3. **What the client provides.** Content, images, logins granted through the tool's own user settings, feedback, sign-offs, each with a date. Late inputs move the timeline; say so.
4. **Not included.** The things clients often assume are included: copywriting, stock photos, hosting, ongoing support, extra languages, accessibility audits, data migration.
5. **Assumptions.** What the price and timeline depend on.
6. **Timeline.** Milestones with the client's sign-off points.
7. **Change process.** What counts as a change, how it is requested, how it is priced and that work starts only after written approval.

### Handling a change request
1. **Name the ask in one sentence** and check it against the scope. If it is in scope, say so and plan it. If not, it is a change.
2. **Size it.** Tasks, hours as a range, cost at the agency's rate, and the effect on the deadline and other work.
3. **Offer options.** Do it as a paid change; swap it for something of similar size in scope; or park it for a later phase.
4. **Write the note.** Friendly, short, no blame: what was asked, what it involves, the options with cost and time, and what you need from them (a choice and a written yes).
5. **Send (level 3) or hand over.** At level 3, send to the agreed client contacts after the agency's yes to the note. Otherwise hand the draft to the account lead.
6. **Log it.** Add every change, approved or not, to a change log with date, cost and decision.

### Checking capacity
1. For each person, by week: available hours (after time off and non-project work), booked hours, spare hours.
2. Mark weeks where anyone is booked above their available hours.
3. For a new project or date, show whether it fits, and if not, which weeks and people are the problem.
4. Offer options: a later start, a phased delivery, freelance help, or moving another project, each with its knock-on effects.
5. Show every step of the arithmetic.

## Checks

- Every deliverable is specific, and exclusions are listed.
- The client's inputs have dates, and the effect of late inputs is stated.
- Change requests show hours as a range, cost at the given rate and the deadline effect.
- Work on a change starts only after a written yes.
- Capacity numbers come from the agency's data; arithmetic is shown.
- The tone is calm and blame-free.
- No contract wording; legal questions are referred.
- Nothing went to the client without level 3 access inside the agreed list of contacts.

## Output format

1. Scope document (deliverables, revisions, client inputs, not included, assumptions, timeline, change process)
2. Change request note and change log entry
3. Capacity table by person and week, with overbooked weeks marked and options
4. Draft emails to the client
5. Action log (level 3)

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Northgate Studio, a six-person design agency. Rate given by the agency: GBP 75 an hour.*

> **Change request 3: Spanish version of the site**
> Hi Marta, thanks for the idea of a Spanish version. It isn't in our current scope (section 4 lists one language), so here is what it would involve:
> - Set up a second language on all 12 pages and the contact form: 14 to 20 hours, GBP 1,050 to 1,500.
> - Translation is not included; you would supply the Spanish text, or we can recommend a translator.
> - Launch would move from 6 March to 20 March, because the same designer is on both.
>
> **Your options:** (1) Add it now as above. (2) Launch in English on 6 March and add Spanish in phase two. (3) Swap it for the blog templates, which are a similar size.
> Let us know which you prefer. We will start only after your written yes.
