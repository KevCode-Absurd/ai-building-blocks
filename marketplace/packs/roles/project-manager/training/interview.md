# Training interview: project manager

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client names, contract details, rates or confidential figures from real projects; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of projects have you managed (type, size, team, budget range), and for whom?**
   Why it matters: sets the kind of project the agent is built for and the scale it should expect.

2. **When a new project lands, what do you do in the first week, step by step?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's start-up routine, which becomes the agent's playbook.

3. **How do you get a client to say what "done" looks like when they cannot?**
   Why it matters: a clear finish line is the base of every plan; the expert's questions make it concrete.

4. **How do you break work down? How small is small enough, and why?**
   Why it matters: decomposition habits decide whether plans can be tracked.

5. **How do you estimate? Tell me about an estimate that was badly wrong and what you changed.**
   Why it matters: real misses teach the agent where estimates go wrong and how to range them.

6. **What work do quotes and plans most often leave out, in your experience?**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's forgotten-work checklist.

7. **How do you find and protect the critical path in practice?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert turns theory into weekly decisions.

8. **How do you run a pre-mortem or risk session, and which risks do teams never mention?**
   Why it matters: surfaces the hidden risks the agent should ask about.

9. **Walk me through how you handle a client asking for "just one small thing".**
   Why it matters: change control wording and timing protect margin without hurting the relationship.

10. **Show me a status update you were proud of, anonymised. What makes it work?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a model for tone, length and how bad news is framed.

11. **When did you last have to deliver bad news on a date or budget? How did you do it?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent when and how to escalate.

12. **How do you work out a team's real capacity, and what numbers do you trust?**
    Why it matters: capacity maths decides whether promises can be kept.

13. **Which project tools do you use, and what do you change in them yourself versus leave to the team?**
    Why it matters: turns the three access levels into real practice and limits.

14. **For website projects: how do you protect search traffic and handle redirects?**
    Why it matters: redesigns are a common job; the expert's checks become launch rules.

15. **How do you handle late content or late sign-offs from a client?**
    Why it matters: client-side delays are a frequent cause of slips; the agent needs a plan for them.

16. **When do you use a dated plan, short cycles or a board, and when a mix?**
    Why it matters: method choice depends on the work; the expert's judgement guides the agent.

17. **Where does your job end and the lawyer's, accountant's or engineer's begin?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's referral rules.

18. **Tell me about the hardest project you have run. What made it hard and how did it end?**
    Why it matters: a full story shows judgement under pressure that short answers miss.

19. **How do you run a wrap-up review so people tell the truth?**
    Why it matters: blame-free reviews are how the agent's clients improve between projects.

20. **What do weaker project managers get wrong that you never do?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's common mistakes and quality bar.
