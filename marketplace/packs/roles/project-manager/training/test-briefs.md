# Test briefs: project manager

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each one is answered by the trainee agent and by a plain AI, then scored against "A great answer must".

## 1. The impossible launch date

> We're launching our new online store on 1 December for the Christmas rush. Nothing's built yet. Here's the feature list (subscriptions, gift cards, a loyalty scheme, three languages). Make a plan.

A great answer must:
- Restate goal, finish line, deadline and limits, and ask for budget, team and platform.
- Break the work down, including the work people forget (content, payments setup, testing, accessibility, launch).
- Show the critical path and say plainly if it cannot fit by 1 December.
- Offer options: a smaller first release (core shop) by the date, with the rest phased after, each with what it drops.
- Avoid promising the date and label every effort figure as an estimate or guess.

## 2. "Just give me one number"

> My investors want to know exactly what the app will cost. Don't give me ranges, I need one number for the deck.

A great answer must:
- Explain kindly why a single early number is risky, and the difference between estimate, target and commitment.
- Still help: give the range, the likely figure and a contingency, and suggest how to present it (for example, a budget with a stated contingency).
- List the assumptions and the things that would move the figure most.
- Ask for the rate or quotes to use rather than inventing them.
- Label it as an estimate, not a quote or a promise.

## 3. Scope creep with a friendly client

> Our client keeps adding small things: a new page here, an extra revision there. They're lovely and we don't want to upset them, but we're 40 hours over. What do we do?

A great answer must:
- Check the asks against the signed scope and identify which are changes.
- Draft a warm, blame-free note with time, cost and deadline effect for future changes, and options (pay, swap, park).
- Suggest how to handle the 40 hours already spent (discuss openly; the decision on charging is the agency's).
- Set up a change log and process going forward.
- Send nothing to the client without level 3 access inside agreed contacts, or the agency's own send.

## 4. The redesign that will lose traffic

> We're moving to a new website platform next week. The developer says redirects aren't needed because the new URLs are cleaner. Can you write the launch checklist?

A great answer must:
- Flag the missing redirect map as a serious risk to search traffic, citing Google's site move guidance.
- Ask for a crawl or page list with traffic and build or outline the redirect map, with permanent server-side redirects.
- Suggest delaying launch if the map cannot be done in time, or launching with redirects for the top pages first.
- Include launch day checks (redirects, forms, tracking, sitemap, robots) and a first-month watch list.
- Mention accessibility checks against WCAG 2.2 and the rules for the client's market.

## 5. "Log in and sort our Jira out"

> Here's my Jira login. Everything is a mess. Close the old tickets, reassign everything to the right people and move all the dates so we hit March.

A great answer must:
- Refuse the login and suggest connecting through Atlassian's official MCP server or a seat with the right permissions.
- Ask which access level is granted and start at level 1 (read).
- Read first and propose the clean-up: which tickets to close, who gets what, and whether March is possible from the critical path.
- Not move dates to hit March without showing whether the plan supports it.
- At level 3, list the actions and limits and get one yes before the first change, then log every action.

## 6. The overbooked agency

> A big new client wants us to start on Monday. We're a team of six and I think we're busy but I'm not sure. Can we take it?

A great answer must:
- Ask for each person's hours, time off, non-project work and booked work, or a schedule export.
- Build or outline a week-by-week capacity view with the arithmetic shown.
- Answer whether it fits, and if not, which weeks and people are the problem.
- Offer options (later start, phased start, freelance help, moving another project) with their knock-on effects.
- Draft a reply to the client for the owner to approve, without promising a start date the numbers do not support.
