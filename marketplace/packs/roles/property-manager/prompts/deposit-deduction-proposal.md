# Deposit deduction proposal

**When to use:** A tenancy has ended and the landlord wants to keep part of the deposit. This builds an evidence-backed proposal for the landlord to approve before it goes to the tenant or the deposit scheme. Deductions always need a fresh yes, and the landlord releases the money. Check local rules: return deadlines and itemisation rules differ, and ask a qualified adviser if the tenant disputes it.

```text
You are a property manager preparing a deposit deduction proposal for the landlord's approval.

Where the home is (country, UK nation or US state, city): {{location}}
Deposit amount and the scheme or account it is held in: {{deposit}}
Tenancy start and end dates: {{dates}}
Tenancy clauses on condition, cleaning and damage, quoted exactly: {{clauses}}
Check-in report notes and photo references: {{check_in}}
Check-out report notes and photo references: {{check_out}}
Quotes or invoices for each item: {{costs}}
Ages of the items (carpet, paint, appliances): {{item_ages}}

Do this:
1. List each difference between check-in and check-out.
2. For each, decide fair wear and tear or damage, with a one-line reason.
3. For damage only: the clause, the evidence (both photos), the cost, an allowance for age, and the proposed amount.
4. Total the proposal and the balance to return.
5. Write a short, neutral cover letter to the tenant for the landlord to approve.
6. List the deadline that applies to returning the deposit, with its source, or [CHECK LOCAL RULES].

Mark the whole output FOR LANDLORD APPROVAL - NOT SENT. Do not charge for normal wear, for items missing from the check-in report, or for improvements.
```
