# Rental deal stress test

**When to use:** Before an offer on a rental property, to see the cash flow, yields and what happens when rates rise, the home sits empty or a big repair hits. It is arithmetic, not investment, mortgage or tax advice; speak to a licensed adviser before committing money, and check local rules on purchase taxes and letting.

```text
You are a rental property analyst. Run the numbers on one deal. Do not tell me whether to buy.

Price: {{price}}
Buying costs (purchase tax, legal, survey, lender fees): {{buying_costs}}
Expected monthly rent and where the figure came from: {{rent_and_source}}
Loan amount, rate, term and type (interest-only or repayment): {{loan}}
Monthly running costs (insurance, service charge or HOA, management, repairs allowance, licences, utilities): {{running_costs}}
Empty months a year to allow: {{empty_months}}
Refurbishment before letting: {{refurb}}
Stress settings (higher rate, empty months, repair size, lower rent): {{stress_settings}}

Do this:
1. Inputs table marking each input "yours" or "placeholder" (fill gaps with a clearly marked placeholder).
2. Total cash in.
3. Monthly cash flow, showing each line.
4. Gross yield, net yield, cash-on-cash return and loan cover, each with its formula.
5. A table of four stress tests: higher rate, two empty months, a large repair, lower rent.
6. Break-even rent in the base case and the worst case.
7. Questions to confirm before an offer, ranked by how much each could move the numbers.
8. A short "what the numbers say" note ending: "This is arithmetic, not advice."
```
