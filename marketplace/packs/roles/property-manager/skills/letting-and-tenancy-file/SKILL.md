# Letting and the tenancy file

## When to use

- A home is coming empty and needs an advert and a fair way to choose between applicants.
- The landlord screens "by feel" and wants written criteria.
- A new tenant is moving in and the paperwork must be complete on day one.
- A tenancy is ending and the landlord wants to keep part of the deposit.

Do not use it to decide who gets the home (the landlord decides), to give a legal view on a deposit dispute, or to run credit checks yourself.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** listings, applications, tenancy documents and certificates in the property software.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft adverts, criteria, application summaries, move-in packs, condition reports and deduction proposals.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish an approved advert, update the tenancy file and checklists, send viewing confirmations from approved templates. Choosing or rejecting an applicant, offering or signing a tenancy, adverse action notices, deduction proposals sent to a tenant or deposit scheme, and deposit dispute evidence need a fresh yes each time. Taking, holding, returning or refunding money stays with the landlord.

## What you need

1. Where the home is (country, UK nation, state, city). Check local rules: letting rules differ widely.
2. The home's facts: rooms, rent, deposit, bills, parking, furnishing, accessibility, rules on pets and smoking.
3. The landlord's screening criteria, or their goals so you can propose criteria for their approval.
4. The tenancy agreement, house rules, and the deposit scheme used.
5. Certificates: gas record, electrical report, EPC, and any licence; for US homes built before 1978, lead records.
6. For move-out: the check-in inventory and photos, check-out photos, the tenancy clauses on condition, and quotes or invoices.

## Steps

### 1. Write the advert about the home, not the person
Describe rooms, rent, deposit, bills, location, transport, rules and when it is available. In England, show the asking rent; inviting or accepting bids above it is illegal from 1 May 2026, and refusing people because they have children or receive benefits is illegal. In the US, adverts must not show a preference about race, color, religion, sex, national origin, familial status or disability, and this rule applies even to small owners exempt from other parts of the Fair Housing Act. Remove phrases like "ideal for a professional couple", "no kids", "no DSS" or "mature tenants only".

### 2. Write the screening criteria before the first viewing
Income or affordability measure, references, rental history, guarantor rules, pet policy, number of occupants (within local limits), and the order applications are handled. Apply the same criteria to every applicant, in the order received, and record each decision against them. The FTC warns that a blanket ban on anyone with a criminal record may breach the Fair Housing Act; any criminal record rule should be specific and approved by the landlord's adviser. Disability-related requests (an assistance animal, a ground-floor unit, a ramp) go to the landlord promptly as reasonable accommodation or modification requests.

### 3. Handle checks lawfully
- **England:** Right to Rent checks on every adult aged 18 or over before the tenancy starts, not only people you suspect; if permission is time-limited, check within the 28 days before the start. No rent before the agreement is signed, and no more than one month in advance.
- **US:** if a screening report played any part in a no, a co-signer request, a bigger deposit or higher rent, draft the adverse action notice: the reporting company's name, address and phone, a statement that it did not make the decision, and the applicant's right to dispute and get a free report within 60 days. The landlord sends it after a fresh yes. Dispose of reports securely.
- Never paste screening reports or ID documents into chat; work from the landlord's summary.

### 4. Build the move-in pack
- Tenancy agreement, house rules and, in England, the government's Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet.
- Condition report: room by room, item by item, with dated photos, signed by both sides.
- Deposit: in England, protect it in a government-backed scheme within 30 days and give the prescribed information within the same 30 days. Put the day-30 date in the calendar the day the money arrives.
- Safety: gas safety record before move-in, electrical report, EPC, alarms checked on the day.
- US homes built before 1978: the EPA lead pamphlet, disclosure and Lead Warning Statement before the lease is signed; keep signed copies for three years.
- Contacts, how to report repairs, rent payment details (from the landlord), bins and meter readings.

### 5. Run the move-out
Send move-out instructions in good time. At check-out, photograph every room in the same order as check-in. Compare item by item and list differences, separating fair wear and tear from damage.

### 6. Write the deduction proposal
For each item: the check-in condition and photo, the check-out condition and photo, the tenancy clause, the cost with a quote or invoice, and an allowance for age and wear. Total it, and list what the tenant gets back. In England, the deposit is returned within 10 days of agreeing the amount, and disputed money stays in the scheme. The proposal goes to the tenant only after the landlord's yes; the landlord releases the money. In the US, check the state's deadline and itemisation rule (New York, for example, requires an itemised statement within 14 days).

## Checks

- The advert describes the home and passes a fair housing read; English adverts show the rent and invite no bids.
- Criteria are written, approved and applied the same way to everyone, with a decision record.
- Right to Rent (England) or adverse action (US) steps are done or drafted as needed.
- Deposit protection and prescribed information are dated within 30 days (England).
- Every certificate in the pack is current.
- Every deduction line has two photos, a clause and a cost.
- Nothing about choosing a tenant, deductions or money went out without the landlord's yes.

## Output format

1. Advert text with a fair housing check note.
2. Screening criteria and an applicant decision table.
3. Move-in checklist with dates and owners.
4. Condition report template.
5. Deduction proposal table and a cover letter, marked "for landlord approval".

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a letting business with a two-bed flat in Nottingham, England. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Advert:** Two-bedroom first-floor flat on Mapperley Road, 10 minutes' walk to the tram. Gas central heating, new kitchen (2025), separate bathroom with shower over bath, on-street parking with permit. Rent 950 pounds a month. Available from 1 December 2026. Pets considered on request. EPC rating C. Viewings Tuesday and Saturday.
>
> **Fair housing check:** describes the home only; asking rent shown; no wording about who should apply.
>
> **Deduction proposal (for landlord approval):**
>
> | Item | Check-in | Check-out | Clause | Cost | Proposed |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Lounge carpet | Good, photo L3 | Burn mark 4 cm, photo L7 | 12.1 (damage) | Patch repair quote 85 pounds | 85 pounds |
> | Hall walls | Fresh paint, photo H1 | Scuffs, photo H4 | 12.2 (fair wear excluded) | - | 0 (fair wear and tear) |
>
> Proposed deduction: 85 pounds. Balance to return to tenant once agreed: to be confirmed by landlord from the scheme record.
