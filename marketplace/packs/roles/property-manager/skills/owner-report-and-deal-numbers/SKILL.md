# Owner report and deal numbers

## When to use

- An owner asks "how is the flat doing?" and the answer is a pile of statements.
- A small agent needs the same monthly report for every landlord.
- A landlord is looking at a purchase and wants to see the risks in the numbers.
- Two or three deals need comparing on the same rules.

Do not use it to value a property, recommend a purchase, advise on a mortgage or decide tax treatment. Ask a qualified accountant or a regulated adviser for those, and check local rules on tax.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** rent ledger, bills, bank feed categories, the repair log and owner statements in the property or accounting software.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft the owner report and the deal worksheet.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** build and send the agreed monthly report to the owners on the list. Paying owners, contractors or anyone else, transfers and changing bank details stay with the client.

## What you need

**For the owner report:** the month's rent due and received by unit, arrears by age, bills paid, management fees, the repair log, vacancies and viewings, compliance due dates, and anything the owner asked about last month.

**For a deal:** price; buying costs (tax on purchase, legal, survey, lender fees); expected rent and where it came from; loan amount, rate, term and type; running costs (insurance, service charge or HOA, ground rent, management, repairs allowance, licences, utilities you pay); allowance for empty months; and any refurbishment. Anything missing becomes a marked placeholder for the client to replace.

**Research mode.** With web search connected, I can look up the purchase tax calculator or the lender's published rate the client names, and I say what I checked and when. Without it, I use only what the client gives me. Either way I do not look up asking prices or "achievable rents" and present them as fact; the client confirms them with a local agent.

## Steps

### Owner report
1. **Reconcile rent.** Rent due versus received by unit; list late payers by days late, from the ledger. Note payment plans the landlord agreed.
2. **Money out.** Bills and repairs paid this month, by property and category, matched to invoices.
3. **Repairs.** Open and closed jobs from the log, with cost, and anything over the promised time.
4. **Lettings.** Vacancies, viewings, applications, move-ins and move-outs.
5. **Compliance.** Certificates due in the next three months; anything overdue in red.
6. **Risks and decisions.** One line each: what it is, what it might cost, what the owner needs to decide and by when.
7. **Write it short.** Three sections (money in, money out, repairs), then risks and decisions. Keep the same tone whether the news is good or bad. No forecasts unless asked, and then labelled as estimates.

### Deal numbers
1. **Inputs table.** Every input with its source: "yours" (from the client or a document) or "placeholder" (my assumption for the client to replace).
2. **Total cash in.** Deposit plus buying costs plus refurbishment.
3. **Monthly cash flow.** Rent, less an allowance for empty months, less running costs, less loan payments.
4. **Measures, each with its formula shown.**
   - Gross yield = annual rent / price.
   - Net yield = (annual rent - annual running costs) / (price + buying costs).
   - Cash-on-cash return = annual cash flow after loan payments / total cash in.
   - Loan cover = monthly rent / monthly loan interest (or payment, say which). Lenders set their own tests and stress rates; ask the lender for theirs.
5. **Four stress tests, run on every deal.** Higher interest rate; two empty months a year; a large repair in year one; rent lower than expected. Show cash flow under each.
6. **Break-even rent.** The monthly rent at which cash flow is zero, under the base case and the worst stress test.
7. **Compare deals** on the same rules and the same placeholders, side by side.
8. **Questions before an offer,** ranked by how much each could move the numbers: rent evidence, condition and survey, lender terms, service charges, licensing, local rules on letting, and tax for the client's accountant.
9. **Close with "what the numbers say".** Plain words, no verdict to buy. "This is arithmetic, not advice. Speak to a licensed adviser before you commit money."

## Checks

- Every figure in the owner report ties to the ledger or an invoice; totals add up.
- Arrears are by tenant unit reference, not personal detail, if the report goes beyond the owner.
- Every deal input is marked yours or placeholder; no hidden assumptions.
- Every formula is shown and works on the numbers given.
- The same four stress tests ran on every deal.
- No buy, sell, value or mortgage recommendation; tax questions are listed for an accountant.
- Reports sent at level 3 went only to the owners on the approved list, and the send is logged.

## Output format

**Owner report:** heading with property and month; money in; money out; repairs; lettings; compliance due; risks and decisions; attachments list.

**Deal worksheet:** inputs table; total cash in; monthly cash flow; measures with formulas; stress test table; break-even rent; comparison table (if more than one deal); questions before an offer; "what the numbers say".

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up figures for illustration: a two-bed flat, price 180,000, rent 1,000 a month.*

| Input | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Price | 180,000 | yours |
| Buying costs | 9,000 | placeholder: confirm with your solicitor |
| Loan | 135,000 interest-only at 5.0% | yours (lender quote) |
| Rent | 1,000 a month | placeholder: confirm with two local agents |
| Running costs | 220 a month | placeholder |
| Empty months | 1 a year | placeholder |

- Total cash in = 45,000 + 9,000 = **54,000**.
- Loan interest = 135,000 x 5.0% / 12 = **562.50 a month**.
- Monthly cash flow = (1,000 x 11 / 12) - 220 - 562.50 = **134.17**.
- Gross yield = 12,000 / 180,000 = **6.7%**. Cash-on-cash = 1,610 / 54,000 = **3.0%**.

| Stress test | Monthly cash flow |
|---|---|
| Rate rises to 7.0% | -90.83 |
| Two empty months | 50.83 |
| 6,000 repair in year one (spread over 12 months) | -365.83 |
| Rent 900 | 42.50 |

**What the numbers say:** at 5% with one empty month the flat earns a small surplus; at 7% it loses about 91 a month. Break-even rent at 7% is about 1,099 a month. This is arithmetic, not advice.
