# Tenant notices and letters

## When to use

- Rent is late and the landlord wants a friendly reminder, then a firmer letter.
- A contractor, inspector or viewer needs to get in, and the tenant needs notice.
- A house rule is being broken (bins, parking, noise, smoking, pets) and the landlord wants to raise it fairly.
- The landlord plans a rent increase and needs the cover letter and the prescribed form filled in for review.
- A tenancy is ending and the tenant needs move-out instructions or a deposit letter.
- The landlord is thinking about possession and needs the facts laid out for a solicitor or attorney.

Do not use it to decide whether a landlord can evict, to choose a possession ground, or to give a legal view on a tenancy. Check local rules, and ask a qualified solicitor or attorney before any possession step.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the tenancy, the rent ledger, the message history and the repair log in the client's property software.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save notices and letters as drafts, and fill in prescribed forms for review.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send repair updates, appointment confirmations the tenant agreed, and the friendly "rent is due" reminder from approved templates. Every formal notice, rent change, deposit deduction letter and possession step needs a fresh yes each time, even at level 3.

## What you need

1. Where the home is: country, UK nation, US state and city. This decides the forms and notice periods.
2. The tenancy agreement, and the exact clause the letter relies on.
3. The facts: dates, amounts, what happened, what was already said and when (the message history).
4. The landlord's goal: payment, access, a change in behaviour, a new rent, a clean move-out.
5. The landlord's own notice periods and procedures, or an official source for them. If these are missing, ask before writing rather than guess.
6. With web search connected, check the official page for the rule (for an English home, the GOV.UK Renters' Rights Act overview and the assured tenancy forms page) and record what you checked and when. Without it, mark each rule [CHECK LOCAL RULES].

## Steps

### 1. Name the letter type and its risk
Sort the request into: routine update, friendly reminder, formal notice, rent change, deposit letter, or possession step. The last four are formal: they need the landlord's yes on the final text every time. Possession steps also need a solicitor or attorney.

### 2. Check the rule before writing
For England from 1 May 2026: rent increases use Form 4A with at least two months' notice, once a year; possession uses a section 8 notice (Form 3A) on a legal ground, and section 21 no longer exists; entry for repairs needs at least 24 hours' notice except in emergencies. Prescribed forms must not be reworded. For anywhere else, use the client's local source; in New York, for example, large rent increases need 30, 60 or 90 days' notice depending on how long the tenant has lived there. Check local rules for each one.

### 3. Write to the fixed order
Date at the top. Then: what is happening, when, why, what we need from you and by when, and who to contact. One fact per sentence for dates and money. Quote the tenancy clause word for word when you rely on it, with its clause number.

### 4. Keep the tone even
No "failure to comply", "immediately", "as you are aware" or "unfortunately you have". No threats, no blame, no capital letters. Offer a choice where you can (two appointment slots, a payment plan for the landlord to consider). Read it aloud: would a fair person feel cornered?

### 5. Build the late-rent sequence (if asked)
- Friendly reminder: rent was due on [date], the amount, how to pay, a contact if something is wrong. Can be an approved level 3 template.
- Second letter: the arrears to date, the clause on paying rent, a request to pay or talk by a date, and where to get free money advice. Formal: fresh yes.
- Third letter: the arrears schedule, what the landlord intends to consider next, and a meeting offer. Formal: fresh yes, and a solicitor's view if possession is in the landlord's mind. Do not charge or mention a late fee unless the tenancy and local rules allow it.

### 6. Prepare, don't serve, possession steps
For a possession question, produce a facts pack: tenancy start date and type, rent and arrears schedule from the ledger, deposit protection status and prescribed information date, safety certificates on file, every notice and message with dates, and the questions for the solicitor. In England, a court will look at whether the deposit was protected or returned, so flag any gap. Never draft a notice that suggests the tenant must leave without a court order: harassment and illegal eviction can be crimes.

### 7. Send or hand over
At level 2, save the draft and send the landlord a one-line summary and the risk type. At level 3, send routine items from approved templates and log them. For anything formal, show the final text, the method of service and the date, get the landlord's yes, then send or let the landlord send, and log it.

## Checks

- Every date, amount and clause matches the tenancy and the ledger.
- The country, nation, state and city are named, and every rule is sourced or marked [CHECK LOCAL RULES].
- Prescribed forms are the current ones and unchanged in wording.
- The letter follows what, when, why, what we need, who to contact.
- No threats, no legal conclusions, no wording that implies the tenant must leave without a court process.
- Formal notices, rent changes, deposit letters and possession steps have the landlord's yes on the final text.
- Every sent item is logged with date, method and copy.

## Output format

1. Letter type and risk level (routine or formal).
2. The letter or notice, ready to send, with [CHECK] marks on anything unconfirmed.
3. The rule used and its source, or [CHECK LOCAL RULES].
4. Method and date of sending, and what to keep as proof.
5. Log entry.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Hollis Lettings, eight flats in Leeds, England.*

> **Notice of entry for a repair (formal: waiting for landlord's yes)**
>
> 6 November 2026
>
> Dear Ms Okafor,
>
> Flat 3, 14 Carr Road: boiler repair
>
> Our heating engineer, Brightwell Gas, needs to replace the pressure valve on your boiler. You reported the fault on 4 November.
>
> We would like to visit on Monday 9 November between 9 am and 12 noon. If that does not suit you, Tuesday 10 November between 1 pm and 4 pm is also free.
>
> Please reply by 6 pm on Saturday 7 November to confirm a time. If you are out, the engineer can use the spare key we hold, as set out in clause 7.2 of your tenancy: "The Landlord may enter at reasonable times on at least 24 hours' written notice to carry out repairs."
>
> If you have any questions, please call Dan Hollis on the office number.
>
> **Rule used:** at least 24 hours' notice to enter for repairs, England (GOV.UK, making repairs). Checked 6 Nov 2026.
> **Send by:** email and text to the numbers on file; keep a copy in the tenancy file.
