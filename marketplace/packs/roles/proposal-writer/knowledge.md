# Proposal and bid writer: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Procurement rules and portals change often: every rule here carries its source and the date it was true. This is general bid practice, not legal advice. Contract terms, liability, eligibility rulings and certifications go to the client's own lawyer, accountant or the buyer's published contact.

## 1. What this role is for

A business hires a proposal and bid writer when a buyer has asked for something in writing and the answer decides whether money changes hands. The request might be a council tender, a federal solicitation, a company's RFP, a grant call, a hospital's security questionnaire, or a two-line job post on a freelance marketplace. The job is to give that buyer exactly what they asked for, in the order they asked for it, backed by facts the client can prove, at a price and scope the client can deliver.

**Who hires it.** Agencies and consultants who send a proposal a week from scratch. Contractors, IT, cleaning and facilities firms bidding for public contracts. Software firms answering RFPs and vendor security forms. Charities, researchers and small firms applying for grants. Freelancers who need a profile, packages and short bid replies.

**What good work looks like.**

- It starts with a decision: is this worth bidding? A good writer kills weak bids early.
- Every requirement in the buyer's documents is listed, owned and answered. Nothing is missed because it sat in an appendix.
- Answers follow the buyer's structure and scoring, not the writer's favourite order.
- Every claim traces to the client's own evidence: a past contract, a number, a certificate, a named reference who agreed.
- Scope says what is in, what is out and what the buyer must supply. Price is easy to compare.
- Nothing is promised that the client cannot deliver, and gaps are shown, not hidden.

**How it is judged.** By how many qualified bids win, by scores and debrief feedback, by never being ruled out on a technicality, and by whether delivery later matches what the proposal promised. A win that the team cannot deliver is a loss that arrives later.

**What an AI proposal writer delivers.** Bid or no-bid scorecards, compliance matrices, win themes, draft answers, executive summaries, price tables built from the client's own prices, answer banks, grant narratives and freelance bids. With access, it also drafts inside the client's proposal or RFP software. Submitting a bid, tender, grant or binding quote always needs a fresh yes from the client, every time, even at level 3 (see section 3, "Access and submission").

## 2. The canon

**Shipley Associates on reviews [28].** Shipley's colour team model is the most widely used review cycle in bid teams. In my words: a Blue team tests the win strategy early; a Black Hat team plays the main rivals; a Pink team reviews the outline or storyboard before drafting, which Shipley says is where many proposals fail; a Red team reads the near-final draft and predicts how the buyer would score it, without the writers in the room; a Green team checks that the price matches the technical offer; a Gold team gives the final executive sign-off; a White Hat review captures lessons after submission and again after the buyer's feedback. Small firms do not need seven meetings, but they do need the three questions behind them: is the strategy right, does the outline answer the scoring, and would an evaluator score this well?

**APMP [29].** The Association of Proposal Management Professionals is the field's professional body for bid, proposal, capture and business development staff. Its certifications start at Foundation level. APMP co-developed the yearly Loopio benchmark report cited in section 4 [30].

**The evaluator's view, in the buyer's own rules.** Public buyers publish how they will score. US federal agencies must evaluate only on the factors in the solicitation and must document each proposal's strengths, weaknesses, deficiencies and risks [4]. EU buyers score against criteria and weights they publish in advance, such as price 40%, technical 50% and environment 10% in the Your Europe example [22]. The lesson: the scoring scheme is the brief. Write so an evaluator can find, tick and quote each point.

**Books practitioners return to.** Credited as a reading list; this kit makes no specific claim from them.

- Larry Newman, *Shipley Proposal Guide*. Read it for the full proposal process and house rules.
- Tom Sant, *Persuasive Business Proposals*. Read it for building a proposal around the client's needs and outcomes.
- Alan Weiss, *Million Dollar Consulting Proposals*. Read it for options-based pricing and short consulting proposals.
- Blair Enns, *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto*. Read it for when not to pitch and how to avoid free work.
- Neil Rackham, *SPIN Selling*. Read it for the discovery questions that make a proposal specific.
- Ellen Karsh and Arlen Sue Fox, *The Only Grant-Writing Book You'll Ever Need*. Read it for grant structure and funder relations.

## 3. How the work is done now

### The bid cycle

1. **Find.** Watch the portals that matter to the client (listed below and in access.md), plus buyers' own sites and framework lists.
2. **Qualify.** Score bid or no-bid against pass or fail gates first (mandatory certificates, turnover, insurance, experience, deadline), then fit, capacity, price position and win odds.
3. **Shred.** Pull every requirement, instruction, word limit, format rule, deadline and scoring criterion into a compliance matrix with its section number and an owner.
4. **Plan.** Set two to five win themes that match the scoring and are true for the client. Outline each answer (the Pink team stage [28]).
5. **Draft.** Answer each question in the order asked, from the client's evidence. Mark missing facts as `[NEED: ...]`.
6. **Review.** An evaluator-style read (Red team), a price-to-offer check (Green team), and a final sign-off (Gold team) [28].
7. **Submit.** By the client, or by the agent with a fresh yes, through the buyer's portal, well before the deadline.
8. **Learn.** Ask for feedback or a debrief and update the answer bank (White Hat) [28].

### United States: federal contracts

- **Register.** Bidding as a prime needs a SAM.gov entity registration. SAM assigns the Unique Entity ID during registration, and a UEI-only record cannot apply directly. Registration must be renewed every 365 days [1]. The SBA adds the other basics: pick NAICS codes, check SBA size standards for each, and for DoD work meet the required CMMC level [3].
- **Find.** SAM.gov Contract Opportunities lists pre-solicitation, solicitation, award and sole source notices. Anyone can search without an account [2].
- **Thresholds.** As of FAC 2026-01 (effective 13 March 2026), the general micro-purchase threshold is $15,000 and the simplified acquisition threshold is $350,000 [5]. Below these, buying is simpler and often less formal.
- **How negotiated bids are scored (FAR Part 15, as of FAC 2026-01) [4].** The solicitation must say how important non-price factors are compared with price. In a *tradeoff*, the agency may pay more for a better proposal and must document why. In *lowest price technically acceptable* (LPTA), the cheapest acceptable proposal wins and nothing earns extra credit; agencies should avoid LPTA for knowledge-based services such as IT. Past performance is evaluated on most larger negotiated buys, and a bidder with no relevant record is rated neither up nor down. Oral presentations can replace or add to written parts, but anything meant to become a contract term must be in writing. Bidders still in the competitive range get a final proposal revision, and eliminated bidders can ask for a debriefing.
- **The rules are being rewritten.** The Revolutionary FAR Overhaul is rewriting the FAR in plain language and removing most non-statutory rules; acquisition.gov shows press releases from August 2025 and June 2026 [6]. Read the clauses each solicitation actually cites, and check the overhaul page for the part you bid under.

### United States: federal grants

- Organisations register in SAM.gov first; Grants.gov says to allow up to 10 business days for SAM to become active, longer if details cannot be verified. The E-Business Point of Contact then sets up the organisation in Grants.gov and gives people roles, including the Authorized Organization Representative (AOR) who submits [7].
- Most opportunities are for organisations, not individuals, and none provide personal financial help. The eligibility section of each Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) decides who may apply [8].
- Teams prepare applications in Grants.gov Workspace. Several people can fill forms at once, online or offline, and the Workspace Owner can give external users such as consultants access to forms [9].

### United Kingdom: public contracts

- **The Procurement Act 2023 came into force on 24 February 2025** [10]. Notices now run through the central digital platform: an enhanced Find a Tender plus a Supplier Information Service, with one supplier account [10]. Registration is free, and you only need to register when you want to bid. Expect to give your Companies House number, address, website, the last two years of accounts if you have them, connected persons and any convictions [11].
- **Where to look.** Find a Tender lists notices and lets suppliers save searches and set alerts [14]. Contracts Finder is still live for searching opportunities by stage and location [15]. Framework opportunities are listed by the government's commercial bodies [16].
- **What changed for bidders.** Buyers must give bid assessments for final tenders [11]. The competitive flexible procedure lets a buyer design its own stages, and negotiation can happen at any stage if the documents say so [13]. Implied 30-day payment terms cover more contracts [11][12]. From 1 April 2026, a supplier awarded a notifiable below-threshold contract (£12,000 or more for central government including NHS bodies, £30,000 or more for sub-central bodies) must register and share its unique identifier [12]. Contracts above £5m carry KPIs, and the three most material are published yearly [12].
- **Social value.** PPN 026, published 5 August 2026, applies to central government procurements started on or after 1 January 2027 worth £1m or more. Social value must carry at least 10% of the total score on contracts from £1m to under £5m, and at least 20% from £5m, with outcomes on good jobs and skills and at least one social value KPI on the larger contracts [18]. For procurements started before then, read which social value note the tender names.
- **Cyber Essentials.** PPN 014 (17 February 2025) says central government contracts where the supplier handles citizens' or staff personal data, ICT holding OFFICIAL data, or government business information should require Cyber Essentials or Cyber Essentials Plus, with proof before data passes and yearly renewal [19]. Cyber Essentials covers five controls; Plus adds independent testing; IASME runs certification [20].
- **AI in bids.** PPN 017 (17 February 2025) says suppliers' use of AI is not prohibited, but buyers may ask suppliers to disclose it, using unscored example questions. It warns that AI text can be inaccurate or misleading, and suggests clarification questions, presentations or site visits to test claims [17]. So an AI-drafted bid must be true line by line, and the client must be ready to explain how it was written.
- **Grants.** Find a grant lists UK government grants with eligibility checks and an apply route [21].

### European Union

- **Tenders.** EU rules apply above set thresholds; below them, national rules apply but EU principles of transparency and equal treatment still count. Above-threshold tenders are published on TED, the Tenders Electronic Daily. Buyers may publish a prior information notice 35 days to 12 months ahead, which can shorten bid deadlines. Procedures include open, restricted, competitive with negotiation, competitive dialogue and innovation partnership. Losing bidders are entitled to an explanation [22].
- **Thresholds for 2026-2027.** Central government supplies and services €140,000; sub-central bodies €216,000; works €5,404,000; social and other specific services €750,000; utilities have higher figures [23].
- **ESPD.** The European Single Procurement Document is a self-declaration that replaces certificates at bid stage. Only the winner must then produce the documents. Use your country's ESPD service; the Commission's own closed on 2 May 2019 [24].
- **Grants.** EU grants answer calls for proposals for projects that serve EU policy; tenders answer calls for tender when the EU buys [25]. Applications go through the Funding and Tenders Portal: find a call, find partners if needed, register the organisation, and submit on time. Late or incomplete proposals can be rejected, and ignoring the call documents can make a proposal inadmissible [26]. Commission staff or independent specialists evaluate against the criteria in the call, in the order eligibility and exclusion, selection, then award. Unfunded proposals may go on a reserve list [27].

### Commercial proposals and freelance bids

Private buyers set their own rules, so the brief, the call notes and the RFP are the rule book. The working pattern: restate the buyer's goal in their words, set scope as deliverables with a "not included" list, show a timeline with what the buyer must supply, give two or three priced options built from the client's own price list, add only proof the client supplied, and end with one next step and a date the offer stays open. Freelance marketplaces each have their own rules on proposal length, fees, off-platform contact and payment. Read the platform's current terms before writing; never suggest paying off the platform.

### Security and vendor questionnaires

Buyers send long forms about security, privacy, uptime and policies. Answer each item from the client's real policies and certificates, mark each answer confirmed, partial or gap, and flag security, privacy, legal and uptime claims for a named human reviewer. A wrong "yes" here can become a contract warranty.

### Access and submission

Access is granted per tool at three levels: read (level 1), read and draft (level 2), and read, write and publish within the client's limits (level 3). Until the client answers, I work at level 1. Even at level 3, some steps need a fresh yes every time because they commit the client: submitting any bid, tender, RFP response or grant application; sending a quote or proposal the client treats as binding; signing or accepting terms, declarations or certifications; and anything that names a new recipient for money. Bank and payment details stay with the client. Personal seats such as a freelancer's marketplace account or a Grants.gov AOR role are used only by their holder.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Use these to frame expectations, never as targets.

- **Bid volume and AI use.** Loopio's 2026 trends report, its seventh, built with APMP from 1,500+ participants: teams submitted an average of 166 RFPs a year; management consulting teams the most, at 229; advertising teams spent the least time per RFP, at 22 hours; 92% of software teams use AI in their RFP process [30]. *Caveats:* a vendor survey of its own market, self-reported, full method behind a sign-up form.
- **Win rates.** No public source I checked gives a trustworthy average win rate across industries. Use the client's own record: bids sent, won, lost and why, by buyer type.
- **Thresholds that change the work.** US: $15,000 micro-purchase and $350,000 simplified acquisition thresholds [5]. EU: €140,000 to €5,404,000 depending on buyer and contract type, for 2026-2027 [23]. UK: notifiable below-threshold contracts from £12,000 (central) and £30,000 (sub-central) [12]; social value at least 10% or 20% of the score from 1 January 2027 [18].
- **Lead times.** SAM.gov activation: allow up to 10 business days [7]. SAM.gov renewal: every 365 days [1]. EU prior information notices: 35 days to 12 months before the tender [22].

## 5. Decision rules

- If a mandatory gate fails (a missing certificate, turnover below the minimum, no insurance at the level asked), recommend no-bid unless the gap can be closed and proved before the deadline, because a failed gate ends the bid before scoring.
- If the scoring is price-only or LPTA, write short, compliant answers and put the effort into price, because quality earns no extra marks [4].
- If the scoring is a tradeoff or quality-weighted, spend the effort on the highest-weighted questions first, because marks follow weights [4][22].
- If a question is unclear, send a clarification question before the buyer's clarification deadline, because a guess can be scored as a non-answer.
- If the client cannot prove a claim, cut it or narrow it to what they can prove, because evaluators and PPN 017-style checks test claims [17].
- If the client has no relevant past performance for a US federal bid, say so plainly and show capability another way, because no record is rated neutral, not negative [4].
- If an answer has a word or page limit, write to it and stop, because text past the limit may be ignored, and ignoring the documents' rules can make a bid inadmissible [26].
- If the price does not match the effort the technical answer describes, fix one of them before submission, because evaluators compare the two (the Green team's job) [28].
- If the buyer asks how AI was used, answer honestly and describe the human checks, because the question is allowed and the answer must be true [17].
- If a tender touches personal data or government systems in the UK, check Cyber Essentials needs first, because certification may be needed before contract award [19].
- If a grant's eligibility section excludes the client's organisation type, stop, because no narrative fixes eligibility [8].
- If the client wants to promise an outcome they do not control, rewrite it as a commitment to actions and measures they do control.
- If a freelance post asks for a free sample of real work, offer a short plan or a past example instead, unless the client chooses otherwise.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Bidding on everything.** Spot it: no scorecard, and a low win rate the team blames on price.
- **Missing a mandatory item.** Spot it: no compliance matrix, or one without section numbers and owners.
- **Answering a different question.** Spot it: the answer's first sentence does not repeat the question's key words.
- **Writing in the client's order, not the buyer's.** Spot it: the evaluator would need to hunt across sections to score one criterion.
- **Unproven claims.** "Market-leading", "best-in-class", "fully compliant" with no evidence next to them.
- **Copy-paste errors from old bids.** Spot it: another buyer's name, old dates, a product the client no longer sells.
- **Price and offer out of step.** Spot it: the technical answer promises a dedicated manager the price never paid for.
- **Leaving submission to the last hour.** Spot it: no upload test, portal registration not checked, files not named as instructed.
- **AI filler.** Lines that could sit in any rival's bid, rule-of-three padding and invented case studies.
- **Ignoring feedback.** Spot it: the same weakness in three debriefs and no change to the answer bank.

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Is there a recorded bid or no-bid decision, with gates passed?
2. Is every requirement in the compliance matrix answered, with its section number?
3. Do answers follow the buyer's order, headings and limits?
4. Does each answer open with a direct reply, then proof?
5. Is every claim backed by the client's evidence, and every `[NEED: ...]` closed or removed?
6. Are names, numbers, dates and product names the same in every section?
7. Does the price match the scope, staff and timeline in the answers?
8. Are win themes visible in the executive summary and the top-weighted answers?
9. Have security, legal, privacy and uptime claims been signed off by a named person?
10. Has someone outside the writing team scored it against the buyer's scheme?
11. Are file names, formats, signatures and portal steps checked, with time to spare?
12. Has the client given a fresh yes to submit this specific version?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.** Bid or no-bid scorecard (gates table, weighted scores with reasons, the call in one line). Compliance matrix (requirement, section, mandatory or scored, owner, status, page reference). Win themes (theme, buyer need it answers, proof). Answer drafts in the buyer's order. Executive summary written last. Client proposals of three to six pages. Freelance bids under 150 words. Answer banks with each answer marked confirmed, partial or gap and dated.

**Tone and conventions.** Plain, direct and specific. Mirror the buyer's terms and headings. Lead with the answer, then the evidence. Short paragraphs, numbered lists where the buyer numbers, tables for scope and price. "We will", not "we aim to". No superlatives without proof. Notes to the client stay out of the bid text and are marked clearly.

## 9. Glossary

- **RFP (request for proposal):** a buyer's formal request for proposals against stated requirements and scoring.
- **Tender:** a formal bid for a contract, often public; also the buyer's whole process.
- **Bid or no-bid:** the go decision, made before writing.
- **Gate:** a pass or fail requirement; failing it ends the bid.
- **Compliance matrix:** a table of every requirement with its section, owner and status.
- **Win theme:** a short, provable reason the buyer should choose you, tied to what they score.
- **Discriminator:** something you offer that rivals do not, which the buyer values.
- **Colour team review:** staged reviews such as Pink (outline), Red (scored read) and Gold (sign-off) [28].
- **Executive summary:** the one or two pages a decision maker reads first; written last.
- **Answer bank:** reusable approved answers, each dated and marked confirmed, partial or gap.
- **Best value tradeoff:** US award method where a better proposal may beat a cheaper one [4].
- **LPTA:** lowest price technically acceptable; the cheapest acceptable bid wins [4].
- **Past performance:** evidence of how well you did similar work before [4].
- **SAM.gov and UEI:** the US federal registration system and the 12-character ID it assigns [1][3].
- **NOFO:** Notice of Funding Opportunity, the rule book for a US grant [8].
- **AOR:** Authorized Organization Representative, the person who submits on Grants.gov [7].
- **Central digital platform:** the UK's enhanced Find a Tender and supplier information service [10].
- **Competitive flexible procedure:** a UK procedure whose stages the buyer designs [13].
- **Social value:** wider benefits a UK contract delivers, scored in the award [18].
- **TED:** Tenders Electronic Daily, where above-threshold EU tenders are published [22].
- **ESPD:** the EU self-declaration used at bid stage [24].
- **Clarification question:** a question to the buyer before its clarification deadline; the answer may be shared with every bidder.
- **Debrief:** feedback from the buyer after award, used to improve the next bid.
