# Bid or no-bid and compliance matrix

Most lost bids were lost before anyone wrote a word: the firm could not meet a mandatory requirement, did not know the buyer, or had no time to do it well. This skill makes the go decision on evidence and then makes sure nothing in the buyer's documents is missed.

## When to use

- A tender pack, RFP, US solicitation, UK or EU notice, or grant notice has arrived.
- The team wants a yes or no before committing days of work.
- The team bids on everything and wins rarely, and wants a scoring sheet for every future bid.
- Before writing any answers: the compliance matrix is the plan for the whole response.

Not for: legal review of contract terms (flag them for the client's lawyer) or deciding prices (the client sets them).

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the documents and the client's facts. Level 2 to build the matrix and calendar in the client's RFP tool, shared drive or project tool. Level 3 to assign owners and dates in those tools within the client's limits. No step here submits anything.

- The full buyer pack: notice, specification, instructions to bidders, evaluation criteria and weights, pricing schedule, contract terms, appendices, Q&A log.
- The client's facts: services, size, turnover, insurance, certificates, past contracts like this one, staff who would deliver, current workload.
- The client's record: past bids, won or lost, and any debrief notes.
- Key dates: clarification deadline, submission deadline, any site visit or presentation date.

## Steps

1. **Read everything once, fast.** Note the buyer, scope, value, contract length, procedure and where submission happens (SAM.gov, Find a Tender, the EU portal, the buyer's own system or email).
2. **Find the gates.** List every pass or fail item: registrations (SAM.gov and UEI for US federal work, central digital platform registration in the UK), minimum turnover, insurance levels, certificates such as Cyber Essentials for UK government work that handles personal data, mandatory experience, exclusion grounds, eligibility for grants. Mark each pass, fail or unknown, with the section number.
3. **Stop or continue.** If any gate fails and cannot be closed and proved before the deadline, recommend no-bid now. If a gate is unknown, put it at the top of the questions for the client.
4. **Score the rest.** Use six criteria the client agrees, each scored 1 to 5 with a one-line reason: fit with what the client does; relevant past work and proof; capacity to deliver and to write the bid; price position against likely rivals; relationship with or knowledge of the buyer; and how the buyer scores (price-only and LPTA favour the cheapest; quality-weighted favours the best answer). If the client has past bid results, set the weights from what predicted their wins; otherwise weight equally and say so.
5. **Make the call.** One line: "Bid", "Bid if [condition] by [date]" or "No-bid", then the three reasons that decided it.
6. **Shred the documents into the matrix.** For every "must", "shall", "will", "should", "provide", "describe", "confirm", word or page limit, format rule, form, signature and date: one row. Columns: ID, section and page, requirement in the buyer's words, mandatory or scored, weight if scored, evidence needed, owner, status (not started, drafting, needs input, done), and where it is answered.
7. **Map scoring to effort.** Sort scored questions by weight. The top-weighted questions get the strongest writer and the most proof.
8. **Draft win themes.** Two to five themes, each tied to something the buyer scores and backed by a fact the client can prove. Write them as "Because [buyer need], we [offer], proved by [evidence]."
9. **List gaps.** Every missing fact, certificate or reference, with how to close it and by when, or how to answer honestly without it.
10. **Build the bid calendar.** Back from the deadline: final upload with a buffer the client chooses, sign-off, Red team review, drafts, outline review, clarification questions before the buyer's deadline, and kick-off.
11. **Write the clarification questions.** Only where the documents are unclear or conflict, phrased neutrally, because answers may be shared with all bidders.

## Checks

- Every gate has a section number and a pass, fail or unknown mark.
- The call is one line, and its reasons are facts, not hopes.
- Every row in the matrix quotes the buyer's words and cites a section.
- Word limits, formats, file names and signatures are in the matrix, not just the questions.
- Win themes each name a proof the client has today.
- No legal reading of terms: risky clauses are listed for the client's lawyer.
- No experience, certificate or reference is assumed that the client did not confirm.

## Output format

1. **Gates table:** gate, section, pass, fail or unknown, evidence.
2. **Scorecard:** criterion, score, weight, reason.
3. **The call** in one line, then three reasons.
4. **Compliance matrix** as a table (or a spreadsheet at level 2).
5. **Win themes** with proof.
6. **Gaps** with fixes and dates.
7. **Bid calendar** with owners.
8. **Clarification questions**, if any.
9. **Questions for the client.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Harbourline Cleaning is a made-up business used only to show the format: a 60-person commercial cleaning firm looking at a made-up council tender for 14 buildings.*

**Gates**

| Gate | Section | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public liability cover at the level stated | 3.2 | Pass | Certificate on file, renews in March |
| Two similar contracts in the last three years | 4.1 | Pass | Two hospital sites, references agreed |
| Registered on the central digital platform | 1.6 | Unknown | Ask who holds the account |
| ISO 14001 | 4.4 | Fail | Not held; no time to certify before the deadline |

**Call:** Bid only if the council confirms in its clarification answer that ISO 14001 "or equivalent" accepts a documented environmental policy. Otherwise, no-bid.

**Reasons:** the 4.4 gate is the only blocker; fit and references are strong; quality carries most of the score, which suits Harbourline's record.

**Matrix extract**

| ID | Section | Requirement (buyer's words) | Type | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R14 | 5.3 | "Describe your approach to mobilisation within 4 weeks" | Scored, 15% | Ops lead | Needs input |
| R15 | 5.3 | Max 750 words, Arial 11 | Mandatory | Writer | Done |

**Win theme:** Because the council's specification stresses continuity for building users, Harbourline offers the same named supervisor per cluster of buildings, shown by the hospital contracts where supervisors stayed in post through the term.
