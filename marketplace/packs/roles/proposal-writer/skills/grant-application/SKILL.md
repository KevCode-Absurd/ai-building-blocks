# Grant application

A grant is scored against the funder's own criteria, by reviewers reading many applications. The narrative matters, but eligibility, registration and the rules in the call decide whether it is read at all. This skill checks those first and then writes to the criteria.

## When to use

- A client has found a grant call or Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) and wants to apply.
- A client wants to know whether they can apply, and what it will take, before committing time.
- A draft application needs checking against the call before submission.

Not for: deciding eligibility in law, tax status or audit questions (point to the funder's contact or a qualified accountant), or inventing figures for a budget.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the call and the client's documents. Level 2 to draft the narrative and budget notes in a shared document, or in Grants.gov Workspace forms if the client's Workspace Owner adds the agent as a participant. Level 3 to file final versions and track the checklist within the client's limits. Submission needs a fresh yes every time, and in Grants.gov it is made by the client's Authorized Organization Representative (AOR) from their own account.

- **The full call:** the NOFO, call document or funder guidance, with eligibility, criteria, weights, page limits, forms, budget rules and deadlines.
- **Registrations:** whether the organisation is registered where the funder requires (SAM.gov and Grants.gov for US federal grants; the funder's own system in the UK; the Funding and Tenders Portal for EU calls).
- **The project:** need and evidence, aims, activities, who benefits, partners, timeline, how success is measured.
- **Capacity:** past projects, staff, governance, accounts if the funder asks.
- **Budget:** the client's own cost figures and any match funding. Never invent a figure.

## Steps

1. **Eligibility first.** Quote the call's eligibility section and check the organisation type, location, size and any partner rules against it. If the client is not eligible, stop and say why. For US federal grants, remember most opportunities are for organisations and none give personal financial help.
2. **Registration and lead times.** For US federal grants: SAM.gov registration must be active (allow up to 10 business days, longer if details cannot be verified), the E-Business Point of Contact sets up Grants.gov and assigns the AOR role. For EU calls: the organisation is registered on the Funding and Tenders Portal. Put each lead time in the calendar, backward from the deadline.
3. **Shred the call.** Every required section, form, attachment, page or character limit, font rule, budget rule and criterion, with its weight, into a checklist table.
4. **Map criteria to sections.** For each evaluation criterion, say which section answers it and what evidence a reviewer will look for.
5. **Write the need.** The problem, for whom, with sourced evidence (the client's own data or public statistics with the source named). No invented numbers.
6. **Write aims and outcomes.** Few, specific and measurable: what changes, for how many, by when, measured how.
7. **Write the method.** Activities in order, who does them, why this approach suits these people, and the risks with how they are managed.
8. **Write capacity.** Past delivery like this, staff, partners with their agreed roles, governance and financial controls.
9. **Write the budget narrative.** Explain each line from the client's figures: what it buys and why it is needed. Check every line is allowed by the call's budget rules; flag doubtful costs for the funder's contact or the client's accountant.
10. **Write the summary last.** Usually the most-read part: need, approach, outcomes and cost, in the funder's terms.
11. **Self-review against the criteria.** Score each section as a reviewer would, fix weak spots, and check every limit.
12. **Build the submission checklist.** Forms, attachments, signatures, file formats, who submits, the target date ahead of the deadline, and how to confirm the funder received it.

## Checks

- Eligibility is quoted from the call and matches the organisation.
- Registrations and roles are active, with lead times in the calendar.
- Every criterion maps to a section with evidence.
- Outcomes are measurable and within the client's control to deliver.
- Every budget line comes from the client's figures and fits the call's rules.
- Every statistic has a named source; no invented beneficiaries, partners or letters of support.
- Limits, formats and attachments match the call.
- The client has given a fresh yes to submit this version, and the right person will press submit.

## Output format

1. **Go or no-go note:** eligibility, registrations, lead times, effort, and the call.
2. **Checklist table:** item, call section, limit or rule, owner, status.
3. **Criteria map:** criterion, weight, section, evidence.
4. **Draft narrative** in the funder's section order.
5. **Budget narrative** with each line explained, plus any lines to check.
6. **Submission plan:** who submits, target date, confirmation step.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real organisation: a reading charity. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

**Go or no-go:** Go. Section 2.1 of the call allows registered charities with income under the stated limit; the charity qualifies on its latest filed accounts. The online form needs the trust's account holder to submit; target submission is five working days before the deadline.

**Criteria map**

| Criterion (weight) | Section | Evidence a reviewer will look for |
|---|---|---|
| Need (25%) | 1 | Local reading data with source; waiting list numbers from the charity's records |
| Approach (30%) | 2 | Weekly sessions, trained volunteers, why small groups suit these children |
| Outcomes (25%) | 3 | Reading age measured at start and end with the same test |
| Value for money (20%) | 4 | Cost per child from the budget; volunteer time shown separately |

**Outcome as written:** By July, 60 children on the current waiting list will have completed 30 weekly sessions, with reading age measured at the start and end using the same test. [NEED: confirm the test name and who scores it, from the programme lead.]

**Budget line to check:** "Volunteer travel" is not named in section 5.4 of the call's eligible costs. Ask the funder's contact before including it.
