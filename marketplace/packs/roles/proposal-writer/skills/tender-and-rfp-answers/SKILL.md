# Tender and RFP answers

Evaluators read dozens of answers against a scoring guide. They give marks for what they can find, tick and quote. This skill writes answers that make that easy and never claims what the client cannot prove.

## When to use

- A bid decision is made and the compliance matrix exists (see the bid-no-bid-and-compliance-matrix skill).
- Questions need drafting: method statements, technical answers, case studies, social value, quality, staffing, mobilisation.
- A security, privacy or vendor questionnaire has arrived.
- The client wants an answer bank so the next bid starts from approved text.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the buyer's documents and the client's sources. Level 2 to write drafts in the client's RFP tool (such as Loopio or Responsive), shared drive or the buyer's portal draft area. Level 3 to update the answer library and file final versions within the client's limits. Uploading or submitting the bid needs a fresh yes every time, even at level 3.

- The compliance matrix and win themes.
- The scoring guide: criteria, weights and any descriptors of what earns top marks.
- Source material: past bids, policies, CVs, case studies, product docs, certificates, accounts, references who agreed to be named.
- A named reviewer for technical, security, legal and price claims.

## Steps

1. **Map sources to questions.** For each question, list which source passages answer it. Where nothing does, write `[NEED: what fact, from whom]` and add it to the gaps list now.
2. **Read the scoring descriptors.** Write down, for each question, what a top-mark answer must show. That becomes the answer's checklist.
3. **Mirror the question.** Open with a direct reply that reuses the question's key words: "We will mobilise all 14 sites within four weeks of contract start, as follows."
4. **Structure for scoring.** If the question has parts (a, b, c), answer in that order with matching sub-headings. If not, use: answer, how, proof, benefit to the buyer.
5. **Put proof beside claims.** Each claim gets one piece of evidence next to it: a past contract, a number with its source, a certificate, a named reference. Cut claims with no proof, or narrow them to what is proved.
6. **Thread the win themes.** Each top-weighted answer carries at least one theme, stated in the buyer's terms.
7. **Keep to limits.** Count words or pages as the buyer counts them. Stop at the limit.
8. **Keep facts consistent.** Same names, numbers, dates, product names and staff roles in every answer. Keep a fact sheet and check against it.
9. **Security and vendor questionnaires.** Answer each item only from the client's policies, certificates and notes. Mark it confirmed (the policy says so and it is done), partial (true in part, explain), or gap (not true today). Flag security, privacy, legal and uptime claims for the named reviewer. Never turn a gap into a yes.
10. **Social value and similar sections.** Give specific commitments the client will measure, with who, what and when, because larger UK contracts attach KPIs to them.
11. **If the buyer asks about AI use**, answer truthfully: what was drafted with AI and how humans checked every fact.
12. **Write the executive summary last.** One page: the buyer's goal in their words, the client's offer, the win themes with proof, and why the client is a safe choice.
13. **Self-score.** Score each answer against the descriptors as an evaluator would, fix anything weak, then hand over for the client's Red team read.
14. **Save to the answer bank.** Each approved answer, dated, with its sources and its confirmed, partial or gap status.

## Checks

- Every question in the matrix is answered in the buyer's order.
- Every answer opens with a direct reply.
- No claim lacks proof; no `[NEED: ...]` is left in a final draft.
- Word and page limits are met as the buyer counts them.
- Names, numbers and dates match the fact sheet.
- Security, legal, privacy and uptime claims are signed off by a named person.
- No copied text from another buyer's bid (search for old buyer names and dates).
- No invented client, result, reference, certificate or policy.

## Output format

1. **Answer document** in the buyer's order and template, each answer headed with its question ID and limit ("Q5.3, 412 of 750 words").
2. **Evidence notes** beside each answer, for the client only.
3. **Gaps list** with owners.
4. **Questionnaire table** where relevant: question, answer, status, source, reviewer.
5. **Executive summary.**
6. **Self-score sheet:** question, weight, expected score, what would raise it.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a six-person IT support firm. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Q4.2 (15%, max 300 words): Describe how you will protect the authority's personal data.**

We will keep the authority's personal data on systems covered by our Cyber Essentials Plus certificate, renewed every year and in force before any data is passed to us [NEED: certificate number and expiry date, from Priya]. Three controls do most of the work:

- **Access only for named staff.** Two engineers will have access, each with multi-factor sign-in, reviewed monthly. (Access policy v3, section 2.)
- **Updates within our patch window.** We apply security updates on the schedule in our patch policy and log each one. (Patch policy, section 4.)
- **Encrypted laptops and backups.** All devices and backups are encrypted, checked in each quarterly audit. (Audit log, Q3.)

If a breach happens, we will tell the authority's named contact within the time set in the contract and follow our incident plan, tested in [NEED: date of last test].

*Status for the client:* two NEED items; claims 1 to 3 confirmed against the policies sent; the incident notice time must match the contract, so check it with your lawyer.
