# Interview: proposal and bid writer

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you give me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work: a freelancer will skip public tender questions, and a tender writer may skip marketplace ones.

## How you decide and plan

1. **Walk me through the last bid you decided not to write. What made it a no?**
   Why it matters: the no-bid habit is what separates experts from people who bid on everything.
2. **What are your pass or fail gates, and which one catches firms out most often?**
   Why it matters: becomes the gate list in the bid or no-bid skill.
3. **How do you score an opportunity, and did your scores ever predict wins? Show me the sheet.**
   Why it matters: turns gut feel into a scorecard the agent can reuse.
4. **How do you build a compliance matrix from a long tender pack? What do people miss?**
   Why it matters: captures the shredding method and the hiding places for requirements.
5. **How do you set win themes, and how do you know one is true?**
   Why it matters: shows how they tie strategy to proof.

## Writing and reviewing

6. **Take one scored question you answered well. Why did it score well?**
   Why it matters: reveals their answer structure in practice.
7. **What do you do when the client has no proof for something the buyer weights heavily?**
   Why it matters: tests honesty under pressure.
8. **How do you run reviews: who reads, when, and what do they score against?**
   Why it matters: becomes the review steps in the workflow.
9. **How do you keep answers consistent across a 200-page response?**
   Why it matters: catches the fact-sheet and naming habits.
10. **How do you write an executive summary, and when?**
    Why it matters: the most-read page deserves a method.
11. **How do you handle security and vendor questionnaires, and who signs off risky answers?**
    Why it matters: a wrong yes there becomes a contract promise.

## Price, scope and deals

12. **How do you lay out options and scope in a commercial proposal so the buyer can say yes fast?**
    Why it matters: sets the client-proposal pattern.
13. **What goes in your "not included" list every time?**
    Why it matters: scope edges prevent disputes later.
14. **Tell me about a proposal that won but went badly in delivery. What would you write differently?**
    Why it matters: captures the line between persuasive and over-promising.

## Platforms, rules and tools

15. **Which portals do you use (SAM.gov, Grants.gov, Find a Tender, TED, buyer portals, freelance marketplaces), and what has gone wrong at submission?**
    Why it matters: real submission failures become checklist items.
16. **Which tools do you use for answer libraries, proposals and tracking, and what do you ask clients to share?**
    Why it matters: fills access.md with what really works.
17. **Has a buyer ever asked how AI was used in your bid? What did you say?**
    Why it matters: UK buyers may ask this (PPN 017), and the answer must be honest.
18. **What do you insist a client approves before anything is sent or submitted?**
    Why it matters: sets the approval points and the fresh-yes habits.

## Grants and freelance work

19. **How do you check grant eligibility and registrations before writing, and what lead times have bitten you?**
    Why it matters: eligibility and registration failures waste whole applications.
20. **For marketplace bids, what does your best opening line look like, and what gets ignored?**
    Why it matters: short bids win or lose on the first line.

## A hard job

21. **Tell me about the hardest bid you've written: the deadline, the gaps, the team, the result.**
    Why it matters: stories show judgement that checklists miss.
22. **What feedback from a buyer changed how you write?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert learns from debriefs.
