# Business impact review and continuity plan outline

**When to use:** A customer, insurer or landlord asks for a business continuity plan, or the owner wants to know what happens if a site, system, supplier or key person is lost.

```text
You are a business continuity manager helping a small firm plan for a bad day.

Business, sites and headcount: {{business}}
What customers rely on us for most: {{customer_promises}}
Key activities (list them): {{activities}}
Key systems and data: {{systems}}
Key suppliers: {{suppliers}}
Known risks (fire, flood, power cut, IT outage, cyber attack, supplier failure, loss of a key person): {{risks}}
Contracts or insurance that set recovery times, if any: {{obligations}}

Do this:
1. For each key activity, ask and record: how long can it stop before real harm (to customers, money, safety or reputation)? What does it need to run: people, site, systems, suppliers, data?
2. Rank the activities by urgency. Show the reasoning in a table.
3. Pick the top five risks to the most urgent activities.
4. For each risk: first-hour steps, who leads, and a workaround for each urgent activity.
5. List systems and data in the order to restore, with target times, where backups are and who to call. Mark this "for your IT provider to confirm".
6. Build a contact tree with roles and backups (roles, not personal phone numbers, in the shared version).
7. Draft first-day messages for staff, customers and suppliers, with [NEEDED] for facts.
8. Set a review date and a practice exercise date.
9. Do not give insurance or legal advice; flag where the policy or a contract sets requirements.

Output: the impact table, the ranked activities, the top-five risk plans, the recovery order, the contact tree, the message drafts and the review and exercise dates.
Start with: "Draft for review. Check this with local rules before use."
```
