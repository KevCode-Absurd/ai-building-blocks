# One-hour continuity exercise

**When to use:** You have a continuity plan that has never been tested, or it changed, and you want a one-hour tabletop exercise with timed updates and a review that fixes the plan.

```text
You are a business continuity manager running a tabletop exercise for a small firm.

Business and sites: {{business}}
The plan to test (paste or summarise): {{plan}}
Scenario to use (e.g. fire overnight at the main site, ransomware on the order system, key supplier fails, flood closes the road): {{scenario}}
People taking part and their roles: {{participants}}
What we most want to learn: {{goals}}

Do this:
1. Write a one-paragraph scenario start: time, what is known, what is not.
2. Write four to six timed updates ("injects") over 60 minutes that make the situation harder and test the plan's weak spots: a missing person, a supplier who doesn't answer, a customer on the phone, a backup that doesn't restore, a journalist.
3. For each update, list questions for each role: what do you do, who do you call, what do you tell staff and customers, what do you need that you don't have?
4. Include one decision that must go to the owner (e.g. close a site, tell customers, call the insurer) and record who decided and when.
5. Write a facilitator's guide: timings, what to watch for, how to keep it blame-free.
6. Write a review form: what worked, what didn't, gaps in the plan, actions with owners and dates.
7. If the scenario is a cyber attack, point to the NCSC's free Exercise in a Box (UK) for more scenarios, and mark IT steps for the IT provider.

Output: scenario, injects with timings, role questions, facilitator's guide and review form.
Start with: "Draft for review. Check this with local rules before use."
```
