# Carbon footprint and green claims

## When to use

- A larger customer or tender asks for emissions data, a sustainability policy or answers to an ESG questionnaire.
- The firm wants a first, honest footprint and a plan to cut it.
- Marketing wants to say "eco-friendly", "carbon neutral" or "sustainable".
- An environmental aspects register for ISO 14001 needs numbers behind it.

Not for: certified carbon audits or assurance, advice on buying offsets, or legal advice on reporting rules. A qualified assessor checks figures before formal claims.

## What you need

1. Twelve months of electricity and gas bills (kWh), fuel records (litres or fuel card statements), business travel (miles or km by mode), and any refrigerant top-ups.
2. Waste and water records if available.
3. The emission factor set to use and its year, for example the UK government conversion factors 2026 or the US EPA GHG Emission Factors Hub 2025. If the client names none, I suggest the official set for their country and ask them to confirm.
4. Sites, vehicles and headcount.
5. The questionnaire, and the firm's policies, certificates and data.
6. The claims to check: website pages, packs, sales decks.

**Access:** level 1 to read bills, exports and the questionnaire; level 2 to build the footprint workbook, draft answers in the customer's portal unsubmitted and draft claim rewrites; level 3 to update the live workbook each quarter and publish approved wording changes to the agreed pages, within your limits. Submitting a questionnaire to a customer needs a fresh yes, as it is a statement on the client's behalf.

## Steps

### 1. Set the boundary

Which sites, vehicles and activities are in, for which 12 months. Write it down; every number depends on it.

### 2. Inventory the data

| Source | Scope | Data you have | Unit | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gas for heating | 1 | 12 bills | kWh | none |
| Company vans (diesel) | 1 | fuel cards, 10 of 12 months | litres | 2 months missing |
| Electricity | 2 | 12 bills | kWh | none |
| Business travel | 3 | expense claims | miles by mode | rail and air mixed |

Scope 1 is direct emissions from sources the firm owns or controls; scope 2 is purchased electricity, heat, steam and cooling; scope 3 is the rest of the value chain, covered by its own GHG Protocol standard. Start with scopes 1 and 2 and the scope 3 items you have data for.

### 3. Apply the named factor set

- Emissions = activity data × emission factor. One line per source, with the factor, its unit, the set and the year.
- **With web search connected:** I open the publisher's page for the named set (for example the GOV.UK conversion factors collection or EPA's Hub), download the current file and record the version and date. **Without it:** you paste the factors or the file, and I use only those.
- UK factors are updated yearly; the 2026 set was published in June 2026. EPA's Hub 2025 edition was released in January 2025.
- Spend-based factors give rough estimates only. Label them "rough, spend-based" and say which lines use them.
- For electricity, say whether the figure is location-based or market-based.

### 4. Show the gaps

Missing months are estimated only with a stated method ("average of the other 10 months") and marked. Never fill a gap silently.

### 5. Plan cuts, cheapest first

Rank actions by rough cost, effort and expected impact, with an owner. Use only costs the client gives; others are [get a quote].

### 6. Answer the questionnaire from evidence

- Answer each question only from the client's documents and data, citing the document.
- "Not yet" is an honest answer: say what the firm would need to collect or do.
- Never claim a certificate, target or policy the firm doesn't have.
- In the EU, the Omnibus simplification aims to limit mandatory sustainability reporting to the largest companies, and a voluntary SME standard (VSME) was recommended in July 2025; customers may ask questions shaped by these. Check this with local rules.

### 7. Check green claims

For each claim, test it against the UK Green Claims Code's six principles: truthful and accurate; clear and unambiguous; not hiding important information; fair comparisons; full life cycle considered; substantiated. In the US, check the FTC Green Guides on qualifying and substantiating claims. Then:
- **Supported:** keep, and note the evidence.
- **Partly supported:** narrow it ("30% recycled content in the outer box", not "eco packaging").
- **Not supported:** drop it or replace it with a specific, provable fact.

Broad claims ("green", "eco-friendly", "sustainable") need the most evidence. Claims about offsets or "carbon neutral" need a qualified adviser before use.

## Checks

- Boundary and period are stated.
- Every line shows activity data, factor, factor set and year.
- Gaps and estimates are marked with their method; spend-based lines labelled rough.
- Questionnaire answers cite evidence; no claimed certificate or target that doesn't exist.
- Every green claim has a verdict and the evidence or a safer wording.
- The document starts with: *Draft for review by a qualified assessor. Check this with local rules before use.*

## Output format

1. Boundary and period.
2. Data inventory with gaps.
3. Footprint table by source and scope, with factor set and year per line, and totals.
4. Method notes and gaps.
5. Action plan, cheapest first.
6. Questionnaire answers with evidence references and "not yet" items.
7. Claims table: claim, where, verdict, evidence, safer wording.
