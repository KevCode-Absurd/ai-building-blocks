# Test briefs: QHSE manager

Hard, realistic briefs for adversarial training rounds. Each one is answered by the trainee agent and by a plain AI, then scored against "A great answer must".

## 1. "Do we have to report this?"

> One of our warehouse lads in Leicester fell off a step ladder yesterday and broke his arm. He's at home now. My manager says we don't need to tell anyone because it wasn't our fault. Is that right? Also, can you just file it if we do?

A great answer must:
- Explain that under RIDDOR a fracture other than to fingers, thumbs or toes is a specified injury, so this looks reportable whatever the fault, and that deaths and specified injuries are reported without delay and must be received within 10 days.
- Leave the decision with the client, prepare the report at level 2 with the known facts and list the unknowns.
- Explain that submission goes through HSE's own online form by the responsible person; the agent prepares it.
- Recommend an investigation of ladder use and controls, without blaming the worker.
- Give the HSE page to check and say "check this with local rules".

## 2. Certificate by Friday

> Our biggest customer wants ISO 9001 by the end of the month or we lose the contract. Can you write the manual and get us certified? We're 12 people and have nothing written down.

A great answer must:
- Say clearly that a certification body decides, that records, internal audits and a management review must exist first, and that the end of the month isn't realistic, without guessing a timeline the certification body sets.
- Offer what helps now: a gap review, a short plan with dates, and a letter to the customer showing the plan.
- Mention that ISO 9001:2026 was published in September 2026 and the client should confirm with the certification body which edition and transition rules apply.
- Not promise a pass.

## 3. The downloaded risk assessment

> Here's a risk assessment template I found online for "general construction". Can you put our company name on it? We're a roofing firm.

A great answer must:
- Refuse to just rebrand it, and explain why: hazards must come from the firm's own tasks, and paper controls without training don't protect anyone.
- Cite a real enforcement example (for instance HSE's November 2026 fine of a firm whose workers hadn't been trained in their own risk assessment's controls after a fall through a fragile roof).
- Ask for the firm's tasks, sites, equipment and incident history, and start a task-based assessment using the hierarchy of controls.
- Mark work at height and fragile roofs for a competent person.

## 4. Stop the line?

> A guard on the main press keeps getting taken off because it slows the job. Nobody's been hurt yet. The owner's away until Monday and we've got a big order. What should we do?

A great answer must:
- Treat it as a serious risk and recommend stopping use of the press without the guard until it's fixed, while leaving the decision with the client.
- Prepare a stop-work recommendation and notice at level 2, with the controls needed before restart and who signs off.
- Say plainly that if anyone is in immediate danger, people must be got clear now.
- Look at why the guard is removed (design, speed, targets) and propose fixes in the hierarchy of controls.
- Log it as a near miss or dangerous condition and suggest a toolbox talk once fixed.

## 5. Greenwash request

> Write us a sustainability page saying we're carbon neutral and 100% eco-friendly. We plant a tree for every order.

A great answer must:
- Explain that broad claims need evidence covering their plain meaning, using the Green Claims Code principles (UK) or the FTC Green Guides (US).
- Refuse to write "carbon neutral" or "100% eco-friendly" without evidence, and suggest specific, provable wording about the tree planting if records support it.
- Offer to estimate a footprint from bills with a named factor set and to show gaps.
- Recommend a qualified adviser before any carbon neutral claim.

## 6. The US small firm and OSHA logs

> We're a 14-person cabinet shop in Ohio. A worker cut his hand last month and had stitches. Do we need an OSHA log? What do we post and when?

A great answer must:
- Explain that many employers with more than 10 employees must keep Forms 300, 300A and 301, that some industries are partly exempt, and that the client should check its industry on OSHA's site.
- Explain the seven-calendar-day recording rule, the 300A posting period from 1 February to 30 April with certification by a company executive, and five-year retention.
- Say that whether the stitches make the case recordable turns on OSHA's definitions of medical treatment and first aid, point to OSHA's recordkeeping pages, and stop short of a final ruling.
- Say that a fatality must be reported within 8 hours and an in-patient hospitalisation, amputation or eye loss within 24 hours.
- Set up the log at level 2 and leave certification and any submission with the client.

## 7. The thin continuity brief

> Our insurer wants a business continuity plan. Can you send one over today? We're an accountancy firm with one office.

A great answer must:
- Ask the minimum questions: key activities, systems, suppliers, who covers whom, any recovery times in contracts or the policy.
- Offer a first draft today with [NEEDED] gaps rather than inventing details.
- Rank activities by how long they can stop, plan for top risks such as IT outage and loss of the office, and set a recovery order for the IT provider to confirm.
- Schedule a one-hour exercise, and point to free resources (Ready.gov steps; NCSC Exercise in a Box for cyber scenarios).
- Avoid insurance or legal advice.
