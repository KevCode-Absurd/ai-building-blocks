# After an incident or near miss

**Trigger:** Any injury, ill health, near miss, dangerous event or damage is reported. Runs from the first report to a checked close, usually two to six weeks.

**Access:** Set at the start, per tool (see `access.md`). Until you choose, I work at level 1. Steps marked level 2 or level 3 need that level in the named tool. Reporting to a regulator and stopping work are your decisions at every level.

## Steps

1. **Immediate safety (minute 0).** If anyone is in immediate danger: get people clear, call the emergency services and tell the person in charge. I don't wait for access to say this.
2. **Log it (same day).** Record the facts with the `incident-log-and-reportability-check` skill: what, where, when, who (IDs in shared records), injuries, treatment, immediate action. At level 3, I log it in the agreed incident tool; otherwise I hand you the entry.
3. **Reportability check and clock (same day).** Check against RIDDOR, OSHA or the national rules for the site, write the result and reason, and show the deadline in hours or days. With web search connected I check the official page and record the date; without it I mark the result "check before use".
4. **Regulator report (level 2, within the deadline).** If it looks reportable or is unclear, I prepare the report with every known field and list the unknowns. *Approval: you or your competent person decide whether to report and submit it through the regulator's own sign-in. I log who decided and when.*
5. **Stop-work decision (level 2, same day if needed).** If a serious risk is still present, I prepare a stop-work recommendation and notice with the controls needed before restart and who signs off the restart. *Approval: you decide whether to stop and when to restart.*
6. **Investigate (week 1).** HSG245's four steps: gather information, analyse (conditions, not just the person), identify controls with the hierarchy, action plan. Draft at level 2 in the agreed folder.
7. **Corrective actions (weeks 1 to 4).** Use the `nonconformance-and-corrective-action` prompt. At level 3, I create and assign the actions in the agreed tracker. *Approval: you approve any spending and any change to a published procedure.*
8. **Update documents (weeks 2 to 4).** Revise the risk assessment and checklists with the `risk-assessment-and-checklists` skill (level 2 drafts; level 3 publishes the approved versions and marks old ones as replaced).
9. **Tell the crew (week 2).** A toolbox talk on the lesson with the `toolbox-talk-and-sign-in-sheet` prompt. At level 3, I post the approved reminder to the agreed channel.
10. **Check and close (weeks 4 to 6).** Run the effectiveness checks. Close actions only when they pass. Update the log.
11. **US annual duties.** If the case is recordable, keep the 300 log and 301 current within seven calendar days, and include it in the 300A summary and any electronic submission.

## Outputs

- Log entry with reportability result, reason, source and date checked
- Draft regulator report and decision record (if needed)
- Draft stop-work notice and restart sign-off (if needed)
- Investigation summary and action plan
- Updated risk assessment, checklists and toolbox talk
- Action log of everything done in your tools (what, where, when, result)

## Needs the client's approval

**Runs at level 3 within your limits, after one confirmation.** Before the first live action, I show the list (log incidents in the agreed tool, create and assign actions in the tracker, publish approved document updates, post the approved toolbox reminder) and its limits: which tools, sites, people and until when. After your yes, I act and log each step.

**Always needs your own decision or a new yes.** Whether to report to a regulator and the submission itself; whether to stop work and when to restart; any spending; deleting or changing incident records; anything that names a person outside the restricted record; legal or medical questions, which go to a qualified professional. Check this with local rules.
