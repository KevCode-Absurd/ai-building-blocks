# Get ready for a certification audit

**Trigger:** The firm decides to certify to ISO 9001, 14001 or 45001, or to move a certificate to a 2026 edition, usually because a customer or tender asks. Plan for several months; records must build up over time before the audit.

**Access:** Set at the start, per tool (see `access.md`). Until you choose, I work at level 1.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the target (week 1).** Standard, edition, scope, sites, certification body and audit date. Check the edition and any transition dates with the certification body; BSI's pages listed no transition deadline for ISO 9001:2026 on 6 November 2026. *Approval: owner confirms scope and target date.*
2. **Gap review (weeks 1 to 2).** Run the `iso-gap-review-and-document-set` skill: process map, clause 4 to 10 gap table, ranked gaps.
3. **Write the core documents (weeks 2 to 6).** Scope, policy, process map, document register, procedures and forms, in order of need (level 2 drafts). *Approval: owner and process owners approve each document.* At level 3, I publish approved versions and update the register.
4. **Start the records (from week 4).** Inspections, training records, calibration, supplier reviews, incident and nonconformance logs. At level 3, I set up recurring inspections and reminders in the agreed tools.
5. **For ISO 45001:** risk assessments for every task (`risk-assessment-and-checklists`), the incident log (`incident-log-and-reportability-check`) and worker consultation records. **For ISO 14001:** the aspects register and footprint (`carbon-footprint-and-green-claims`), legal and other requirements list, and emergency plans for all potential emergencies.
6. **Train people (weeks 4 to 8).** Short briefings on the policy, their procedures and how to report problems; toolbox talks for safety topics. Record attendance.
7. **Internal audit round (months 2 to 4).** Audit every process with the checklists; auditors don't audit their own work. Raise nonconformances with the `nonconformance-and-corrective-action` prompt.
8. **Management review (before the audit).** Inputs: audit results, incidents, customer feedback, objectives, actions. Record decisions in minutes. *Approval: top management holds and signs the review.*
9. **Pre-audit check (two weeks before).** Sample the evidence an auditor would ask for, clause by clause. List anything thin, with a fix or an honest explanation.
10. **Audit days.** Prepare the evidence index and who speaks to which process. I don't attend as the auditor or answer on the firm's behalf.
11. **After the audit.** Log findings as nonconformances, plan corrective actions to the certification body's deadlines, and check effectiveness.

## Outputs

- Gap table and ranked plan with dates
- Approved document set and register
- Records running for each process
- Internal audit reports and nonconformance log
- Management review minutes
- Pre-audit evidence index
- Action log of everything done in your tools (what, where, when, result)

## Needs the client's approval

**Runs at level 3 within your limits, after one confirmation.** Before the first live action, I show the list (publish approved documents, update the register, set up recurring inspections and reminders, create audit actions in the tracker) and its limits. After your yes, I act and log each step.

**Always needs a new yes.** Scope and target date, each document's approval, any spending (including certification fees), anything sent to the certification body, and deleting or overwriting records. A certification body decides whether the firm passes; no promise of a pass is ever made. Check this with local rules.
