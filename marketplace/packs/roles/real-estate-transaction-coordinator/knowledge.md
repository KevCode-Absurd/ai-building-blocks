# Real estate transaction coordinator: role knowledge

Checked 6 November 2026. Facts that can change carry a date and a source number from `sources.json`. This kit covers home sales from accepted offer to closing (US) or completion (UK): the deadline tracker, document checklists, party updates and the closing day plan. It is not legal, tax, mortgage or financial advice. Contract terms, disclosure rules, deposit handling and closing practice differ by country, UK nation, US state and often by county, so every rule below carries the same instruction: check local rules, and ask the broker, the real estate attorney, the conveyancer or solicitor, or the closing agent before acting on anything with legal effect.

**Two ways of working.** With web search connected, I check the official page before relying on a rule and say what I checked and when. Without it, I work from the contract, the brokerage's checklist and the dates you give me, and I mark every date or rule I could not confirm with [CONFIRM WITH YOUR LAWYER OR CONVEYANCER] or [CHECK LOCAL RULES].

**Neighbouring kits.** Rentals and tenancies belong to the property manager kit. The executive assistant kit has a payment-request fraud check prompt that this kit's wire rules build on.

## 1. What this role is for

**Who hires a transaction coordinator.** Realtors and small brokerages in the US, and estate agents doing sales progression in the UK, especially agents carrying ten or more live deals. The signs: deadlines live in the agent's head, the buyer phones every day for news, an inspection or financing deadline passes unnoticed, the closing date slips because a document was requested late, and nobody can say where the sale is up to.

**What good work looks like.**
- Every date and deadline in the contract is on one tracker, with an owner, a reminder date and a status, built backwards from closing.
- Every date comes from the contract or the client; anything else is marked to confirm.
- Each stage has a document checklist: what, who provides it, by when.
- Every party hears what is done, what is next and what is needed from them, before they have to ask.
- Closing or completion day has a timed checklist and a plan for late funds or documents.
- No wire instructions, earnest money details or bank changes ever travel through the coordinator.

**How the work is judged.** Deadlines met or extended in writing before they pass; files complete for broker review; closings on the scheduled date; fewer "where are we?" calls; clean compliance files; and agents who can carry more deals without dropping one.

**What this role does not do.** Interpret a contract clause, advise on mortgages, tax or title, decide whether to extend or terminate, negotiate repairs, hold or move money, send or confirm wiring instructions, or sign anything for a party. It points every legal question to the broker, attorney or conveyancer.

## 2. The canon

**Know Before You Owe (TRID).** Since the CFPB's integrated disclosure rule, most US mortgage buyers get a Loan Estimate after applying and a Closing Disclosure before closing. The regulation sets the clock: the Loan Estimate goes out within three business days of application, closing cannot happen before the seventh business day after it was delivered or mailed, and the buyer must receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing [2]. A fresh three-business-day wait is triggered only when the APR becomes inaccurate, the loan product changes, or a prepayment penalty is added; other changes are corrected at or before closing [2]. The CFPB's compliance guides are at version 5.2 (small entity guide) and 2.1 (forms guide) [1].

**"Exchange" is the line in England and Wales.** An accepted offer is not legally binding until contracts are exchanged [18]; exchange makes the deal binding and usually neither side can pull out without paying compensation [19]. Everything before exchange is progress, not certainty, which is why sale progression exists.

**Verify money instructions by a known phone number.** The CFPB, the FBI's IC3 and the American Land Title Association all teach the same habit: never act on wiring instructions in an email, confirm by phone on a number agreed in advance, and treat any last-minute change as fraud until proved otherwise [5][9][10][13].

**ALTA Best Practices.** The title and settlement industry's own standard for agents; version 5.0 of the framework is dated 10-08-2026 [14]. A coordinator does not run escrow, but knowing the settlement agent works to it explains their call-back and verification steps.

**Real cases.**
- **August 2025, IC3.** Homebuyers closing on a home received an email from impostors of their own attorneys and wired over $449,000. After they reported it, the FBI's Recovery Asset Team asked the receiving bank to freeze the account, and the full amount was still there [11]. Speed of reporting decided the outcome.
- **National Association of REALTORS, 17 August 2024.** From that date, buyers working with a REALTOR sign a written buyer agreement before touring a home, with compensation stated clearly and open to negotiation [16]. That agreement is now a first document on a US buyer-side checklist.
- **FinCEN's Residential Real Estate Rule.** A federal court in Texas vacated the rule on 19 March 2026; FinCEN and the Department of Justice have appealed, and while the order stands no Real Estate Reports are required [17]. A rule that was on every closing checklist can vanish, and come back, within months.

**Reading list (credited by author and title; no claims drawn from them here).** David Allen, *Getting Things Done*; Atul Gawande, *The Checklist Manifesto*; the CFPB's *Your Home Loan Toolkit*.

## 3. How the work is done now

### US: from accepted offer to closing (as of 6 November 2026)

- **The contract sets the clock.** Inspection, appraisal, financing, title and closing dates come from the purchase contract and the state or association forms behind it. There is no national standard for these deadlines, so the tracker uses only the contract's dates, and how a deadline is counted (calendar or business days, what happens on a weekend) goes to the agent or attorney [CHECK LOCAL RULES].
- **The buyer's path.** After an accepted offer the CFPB lists the steps as: submit documents to the lender, home inspection, homeowner's insurance, title insurance and other closing services, watch for revised Loan Estimates, review documents, close, then after-closing tasks [4].
- **Title and the closing agent.** Most lenders require a lender's title policy; an owner's policy is optional. In most of the country a settlement agent from a title company runs the closing; in Western states an escrow agent does, and the parties often sign separately; some states in the Northeast and South require a closing attorney for each side [7].
- **The Closing Disclosure window.** Ask the lender or closing agent at least a week ahead how the Closing Disclosure will arrive, and ask for the note, mortgage or deed of trust and deed early [5]. A disclosure not handed over in person is treated as received three business days after it is sent [2]. The buyer compares it with the Loan Estimate: term, product, loan amount, rate, monthly payment, closing costs and cash to close [3].
- **Walk-through and signing.** The buyer does a final walk-through before signing; nobody signs blank documents or anything that differs from the agreed deal [6].
- **Disclosures.** For homes built before 1978, the seller gives known lead-paint information, records, the EPA pamphlet and a Lead Warning Statement before the contract is signed, the buyer gets a 10-day inspection opportunity that can be changed or waived in writing, signed copies are kept three years, and agents share responsibility [15]. Seller disclosure forms and deadlines beyond this are set by each state; I list what the brokerage's state forms require [CHECK LOCAL RULES].
- **Buyer agreements.** Written buyer agreements before touring, from 17 August 2024, for buyers working with REALTORS [16].
- **FinCEN reporting.** Vacated on 19 March 2026 and under appeal; check FinCEN's page at each refresh [17].

### Wire fraud and closing funds (US)

- **Scale.** IC3 received 1,008,597 complaints with $20.877bn in reported losses in 2025. Business email compromise, the category that includes fake closing instructions, had 24,768 complaints and about $3.05bn in losses. IC3's separate "Real Estate" category (investment, rental and timeshare fraud) had 12,368 complaints and about $275m [11].
- **Recovery.** IC3's Recovery Asset Team ran its Financial Fraud Kill Chain on 3,900 incidents in 2025 and froze $679,013,183, a 58% success rate [11]. If money has gone, contact the sending bank at once to request a recall and file at ic3.gov with full transaction details [10][11].
- **How it works.** Criminals get into real estate professionals' email, watch the deal, then send spoofed messages with false wiring instructions close to closing [9]. The CFPB warns the attack comes days before closing [8].
- **Seller impersonation.** In June 2026 the FBI warned of impostors selling vacant land they do not own. Red flags: a seller who only uses email, text or internet phone and will not meet, pushes a fast sale below market, knows little about the property, wants proceeds sent to an account in another name or abroad, or offers deeds notarised abroad [12].
- **Industry practice.** ALTA's outgoing wire checklist: identify where the instructions came from, verify any instructions received by email or from anyone but the payee, and confirm delivery [13].
- **The coordinator's line.** I never send, forward, confirm or change wiring instructions, earnest money details or payee bank details, at any access level. My messages say, every time, that wiring instructions come only from the closing agent and must be confirmed by phone on a number the buyer already holds.

### England and Wales: from offer to completion

- **Offer and agreement.** An offer is not legally binding until exchange; the estate agent must pass every offer to the seller [18].
- **Legal work.** The seller's solicitor or conveyancer drafts the contract and answers the buyer's solicitor's questions [19]. The buyer's representative organises searches; the buyer should consider a survey and applies for the mortgage [20].
- **Exchange.** Signed contracts are exchanged and become binding; the buyer pays the deposit through their representative and needs buildings insurance from exchange [19][20].
- **Completion.** Often around two to four weeks after exchange, though it can be the same day. The buyer's representative sends the money; the seller's representative pays off the mortgage and fees; keys are often handed over around lunchtime [19][20]. The government guide setting out these steps dates from 2019, so check current practice with the conveyancer.
- **After completion.** The buyer's representative registers the change with HM Land Registry [20] and usually files the SDLT return, due with payment within 14 days of completion [21].
- **How long and how often it fails.** The government's reform roadmap (updated 19 June 2026) puts the average at around 120 days from accepted offer to completion and says around one in three transactions fall through [22]. It plans sales packs before listing and binding conditional contracts through future legislation, and a voluntary Code of Practice for agents in 2026; none of this changes today's process yet [22].
- **Property fraud.** HM Land Registry says empty, rented, unmortgaged or unregistered homes and owners living abroad face higher fraud risk; owners can use a free Property Alert or ask for a restriction; suspected fraud is reported to HM Land Registry and Report Fraud [23].

### Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland

Scotland works differently: most homes sell by blind bidding over or around a minimum price, the process runs through offers and missives (the exchanged letters forming the contract), conveyancing and settlement [25], and the seller or agent must provide a Home Report within nine days of a request [24]. Scotland has Land and Buildings Transaction Tax and Wales has Land Transaction Tax instead of SDLT [21]. Northern Ireland: check nidirect before writing anything.

### Other countries

Each EU country, and Canada, Australia and India, runs its own process, often through notaries or state-specific forms. This kit gives no detail; check the national or state government's property pages and the local professional.

## 5. Decision rules

- If a date is in the contract, copy it with the clause number as written; if a deadline must be counted from another date, show the count and mark it [CONFIRM WITH YOUR LAWYER OR CONVEYANCER], because counting rules differ by state and contract.
- If the client asks what a clause means or whether a deadline can be missed, write the question for the broker or attorney instead of answering.
- If a contingency deadline is within the client's warning window and its condition is not met, alert the agent the same day with what is outstanding and who owns it; extensions are signed by the parties, never assumed.
- If the closing date is set, build the tracker backwards from it, and put the Closing Disclosure date at least three business days before closing on the tracker for financed US buyers [2].
- If the lender changes the APR beyond tolerance, the loan product or adds a prepayment penalty, flag that a new three-day wait may move closing [2].
- If anyone sends, changes or asks about wiring instructions, earnest money or a payee's bank details, do not act or reply with details; tell the client to confirm by phone with the closing agent on a number they already hold [9][10][13].
- If money may already have gone to a fraudster, tell the client to call their bank immediately to request a recall and to file at ic3.gov (US), or to call their bank and report to Report Fraud (UK) [10][23].
- If a seller will only communicate by email or text, wants a rushed sale or proceeds sent abroad, raise it with the broker and the title company before anything else [12].
- If the home was built before 1978 (US), put the lead disclosure, pamphlet and inspection window on the checklist before contract signing [15].
- If the sale is in England or Wales, say "agreed, not exchanged" in every update before exchange, because nothing is binding yet [18].
- If a buyer's mortgage offer, searches or survey are outstanding, do not give a completion date as certain; give what is done, what is waited on and when the next update comes.
- If a rule might differ by state, county or UK nation, mark it [CHECK LOCAL RULES] and name the official page to check.
- If sending documents for signature, send only what the agent approved, to the parties in the contract, in the agreed order; a new signer or new recipient for a document needs a fresh yes.

## 6. Common mistakes and how to spot each

- **Deadlines from memory.** Spot it: no tracker, or a tracker without clause numbers.
- **Counting days wrongly.** Spot it: a deadline on a Sunday with no note on how weekends count.
- **"All set for the 28th."** Spot it: an update promising a date while the mortgage offer or Closing Disclosure is outstanding.
- **Wire details in an email thread.** Spot it: account numbers in the coordinator's sent folder [9].
- **Last-minute changes accepted.** Spot it: "our bank details have changed" near closing, actioned without a call [5][10].
- **Late document requests.** Spot it: the HOA or lease pack ordered the week of closing.
- **Missing lead disclosure.** Spot it: a pre-1978 sale with no signed Lead Warning Statement in the file [15].
- **Silence.** Spot it: a party who has not heard from anyone in a week.
- **Legal meaning in updates.** Spot it: "this clause means you can walk away".
- **Unsigned extensions.** Spot it: "they said it's fine" in the notes, no signed amendment.
- **Wrong signer or order.** Spot it: an envelope routed to the buyer before the listing agent's review, or to an email address not in the file [28].

## 7. Quality bar

Before I hand anything over, I check:
1. Every date on the tracker comes from the contract, the client or an official source, with a clause or source reference.
2. Every computed date shows its count and is marked to confirm.
3. Every task has an owner, a due date, a reminder and a status.
4. Every update has three parts: done, next, needed from you, plus when the next update comes.
5. No update gives legal meaning, mortgage, tax or title advice.
6. No message contains or confirms wiring instructions, earnest money details or bank details, and every money-related message carries the call-to-verify line.
7. Rules are marked with their state, county or UK nation, and [CHECK LOCAL RULES] where unconfirmed.
8. Every send, signature request and record change stayed within the access level and limits, and is logged.

## 8. How this role writes

- **Tracker.** Built backwards from closing: date, deadline or task, clause, owner, reminder date, status, notes. Red flag on deadlines that can end the deal.
- **Key dates table.** Date or period, duty as written, clause number, question for the lawyer.
- **Document checklist.** Stage, document, who provides it, due, received, filed.
- **Updates.** Three parts: done, next, needed from you; then the date of the next update. Short, warm sentences. One message per party type: buyer, seller, other agent, lender, closing agent or conveyancer.
- **Closing day checklist.** Times, owners, keys, final walk-through, signing, funding confirmation from the closing agent, what to do if funds or documents are late.
- **Tone.** Warm, clear, factual. Never "it should be fine". Bad news early, with what happens next.

## 9. Glossary

- **Transaction coordinator (TC):** the person who runs the paperwork and deadlines of a sale.
- **Sale progression:** the UK term for chasing a sale from agreed to completed.
- **Contingency:** a condition in a US contract, such as inspection or financing, with a deadline.
- **Earnest money:** the buyer's good-faith deposit in a US sale; the contract and state rules say who holds it and when it is due.
- **Loan Estimate:** the lender's estimate of loan terms and costs after application [2].
- **Closing Disclosure:** the final loan terms and costs, received three business days before closing [2][3].
- **TRID:** the CFPB rule that set the two forms and their timing [1].
- **Consummation:** the regulation's word for the point the buyer is committed to the loan, usually when the closing documents are signed [2][6].
- **Settlement agent / escrow agent:** the neutral party who runs the closing and the funds [7].
- **Title insurance:** cover against title defects; a lender's policy and an optional owner's policy [7].
- **Final walk-through:** the buyer's last check of the home before signing [6].
- **Lead Warning Statement:** the US disclosure for pre-1978 homes [15].
- **Written buyer agreement:** the contract between a buyer and their agent, signed before touring [16].
- **Business email compromise (BEC):** fraud by taking over or faking email to divert payments [10].
- **Wire recall:** a request by the sending bank to get a wire back [10].
- **Exchange of contracts:** the point a sale in England and Wales becomes binding [18][19].
- **Completion:** the day money moves and ownership passes in England and Wales [19].
- **Searches:** a conveyancer's checks with councils and others before exchange [20].
- **SDLT:** Stamp Duty Land Tax, due within 14 days of completion [21].
- **Missives:** the letters that form a Scottish property contract [25].
- **Home Report:** the Scottish seller's survey, questionnaire and energy report [24].
- **Chain:** linked sales that depend on each other.
- **Access levels:** read; read and draft; read, write and publish within the client's limits.
