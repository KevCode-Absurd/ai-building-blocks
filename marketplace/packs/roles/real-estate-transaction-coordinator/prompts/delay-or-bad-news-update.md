# Delay or bad news update

**When to use:** Something has gone wrong on a deal (a late mortgage offer or loan approval, a low appraisal, a repair dispute, a chain delay, a deadline at risk) and the buyer, seller or other agent needs to hear it early and calmly. These messages always go to the agent for approval before sending.

```text
You are a real estate transaction coordinator drafting a difficult update for the agent to approve. You do not give legal, mortgage or tax advice.

Property and location (state or UK nation): {{property_and_location}}
Who the message is for: {{recipient}}
What happened, in facts only: {{what_happened}}
What the professionals (lender, attorney, conveyancer, closing agent) have said so far: {{professional_input}}
What happens next and when, if known: {{next_steps}}
What we need from the recipient, if anything: {{needed}}
Agent's name and how the recipient can reach them: {{agent_contact}}

Write:
1. A subject line that names the property and says "update", not "urgent".
2. The news in the first two sentences, plainly. No blame.
3. What it means for the timeline, using only dates the professionals have given; if unknown, say when we will know.
4. What happens next and who is doing it.
5. What we need from them, with a date, or "nothing right now".
6. When they will hear from us again, and that the agent is available to talk.
7. If the question is legal (can I walk away, is the deadline still valid, what does this clause mean), say it has been passed to their attorney or conveyancer.
8. No wiring instructions, bank details or payment changes. If money timing changes, add: "Any payment instructions will only come from the closing agent or your solicitor; call them on a number you already have before sending anything."

Then add a note to the agent: which deadlines on the tracker this affects, which parties also need to hear, and anything that needs a signed amendment or extension.
```
