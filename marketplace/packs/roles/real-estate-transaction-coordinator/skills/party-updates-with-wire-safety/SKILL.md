# Party updates with wire safety

## When to use

- Buyers or sellers keep phoning to ask where the sale is up to.
- A stage has changed: inspection done, appraisal in, mortgage offer received, exchange, Closing Disclosure sent, closing booked.
- A document or action is needed from a party.
- An agent wants a set of approved templates for every stage.
- Something has gone wrong and the parties need to hear it early.

## What you need

1. The deal tracker and checklist (from the `deal-deadline-tracker` and `stage-document-checklists` skills).
2. The parties by role, their preferred channel, and any language or access needs.
3. The agent's tone and sign-off, and anything they never want said.
4. The name and published phone number of the closing agent, escrow officer or conveyancer, as the agent gives it (never a number taken from an incoming email).
5. Where the property is. Check local rules: what can be shared with whom differs, and some updates belong to the lawyer.
6. Access: level 1 to draft in chat; level 2 to save drafts in the inbox or transaction tool; level 3 to send approved templates to parties already on the file, at agreed times, within the client's limits.

## Steps

### 1. Write in three parts

Every update has:
- **Done:** what has happened since the last update, in plain words.
- **Next:** what happens next and roughly when, from the tracker.
- **Needed from you:** the specific action, document or decision, with a date, or "nothing right now".
Then: "I'll update you again on [day]."

### 2. Fit each party

- **Buyer:** reassurance and plain explanations of steps (not of legal meaning); first-time buyers get one line on what each step is for.
- **Seller:** what the buyer side has done, what is still waited on, moving and key handover planning.
- **Other agent:** short, factual, deadline-led.
- **Lender, closing agent or conveyancer:** document status and questions only.

### 3. Never promise what is not certain

Before a mortgage offer, the Closing Disclosure (US) or exchange (England and Wales), write "planned for", not "all set for". In England and Wales, say "agreed, not yet exchanged" until exchange.

### 4. Add the wire-safety line to anything about money

Use the client's wording or this default: "Wiring instructions will only come from [closing agent name]. Before you send any money, call them on [the number you already have for them], never a number in an email, and confirm the details. We will never send you bank details or ask you to change them." Never put wiring instructions, account numbers, earnest money payment details or payoff figures in an update, and never confirm or forward them, at any access level.

### 5. Handle bad news early (fresh yes)

A delayed loan, a failed appraisal, a repair dispute, a missed or at-risk deadline, or a party who wants out: draft the update with what happened, what it means for the timeline in plain words, what happens next and who will call them. The agent approves each one before it goes; deadline and deal-ending messages are never sent from a template.

### 6. Answer questions without giving meaning

If a party asks what a clause means, whether they can walk away, or anything about the loan, tax or title: "That's a question for [your attorney / your conveyancer / your lender]. I've passed it on and they'll come back to you by [date]." Then add it to the lawyer questions list.

### 7. Send and log (level 3)

Within the confirmed limits: send approved templates to parties on the file, at the agreed times and through the agreed channel; log what, to whom, when and the result. A new recipient, a new template, or a message outside the agreed hours needs a fresh yes. If anyone replies with new or changed bank details, or asks to change where money goes, stop, do not reply with details, and alert the agent at once.

## Checks

- Every update has done, next, needed, and the next update date.
- Every date matches the tracker.
- No promise of a date that depends on an outstanding item.
- No legal, mortgage, tax or title meaning.
- No wiring instructions, account numbers or payment details anywhere; the wire-safety line is on every money message.
- The closing agent's phone number came from the agent, not from an email.
- At level 3, only parties on the file, approved templates and agreed times; every send logged.

## Output format

1. **Template set** by stage and party, each with a subject line.
2. **This week's updates**, ready to approve or send.
3. **Questions passed to professionals**, with who and when.
4. **Send log** (level 3).

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real deal: first-time buyers Priya and Tom, 22 Linden Way, England. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Subject: 22 Linden Way, your weekly update**
>
> Hi Priya and Tom,
>
> **Done:** The seller's solicitor has sent the draft contract and property forms to your solicitor. Your survey happened on Tuesday.
>
> **Next:** Your solicitor will check the forms and the searches, then send questions to the seller's side. Your lender is waiting for the valuation report before issuing the mortgage offer.
>
> **Needed from you:** Please send your solicitor the signed client care letter by Friday 13 November.
>
> You're "agreed, not exchanged" right now, so nothing is binding on either side yet. That's normal at this stage.
>
> Money note: your deposit details will only come from your solicitor. Before sending anything, call them on the number on their engagement letter, not one in an email.
>
> I'll update you again next Thursday.
