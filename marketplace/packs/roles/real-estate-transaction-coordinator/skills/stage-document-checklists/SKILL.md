# Stage document checklists

## When to use

- A new file opens after an accepted offer.
- A brokerage wants one standard checklist for every coordinator.
- A broker's compliance review keeps finding missing documents.
- A UK agent wants a sale progression checklist to stop buyers phoning for news.

## What you need

1. The brokerage's or agency's own checklist and compliance rules, if any.
2. The contract and addenda, and which side the client represents.
3. Where the property is (state and county, or UK nation), its age, whether it is in a homeowners' association, condominium or leasehold, and how the buyer is paying. Check local rules: required disclosures and forms differ.
4. The transaction tool used (Docusign Rooms, dotloop, SkySlope, a shared folder).
5. Access: level 1 to draft; level 2 to create tasks and draft requests in the tool; level 3 to keep the live checklist, upload received documents and send approved document requests to parties on the file.

## Steps

### 1. Start from the client's checklist

Use the brokerage's names and order for documents, so the file passes their review. Add missing items from the stages below only as suggestions marked "add?".

### 2. Lay out the stages

**US (adapt to the state's forms):**
1. *Agreement:* listing agreement or written buyer agreement (for buyers working with REALTORS, signed before touring since 17 August 2024); agency disclosures the state requires [CHECK LOCAL RULES].
2. *Contract:* purchase contract, all addenda and counter-offers, fully signed and dated; earnest money receipt from the holder (the receipt only, never payment instructions).
3. *Disclosures:* the state's seller disclosure forms [CHECK LOCAL RULES]; for homes built before 1978, the lead disclosure and records, the EPA pamphlet (given before the buyer signs the contract), the Lead Warning Statement in or attached to the contract, and the buyer's choice on the 10-day inspection opportunity; HOA or condominium documents where they apply.
4. *Inspections and appraisal:* inspection reports, repair requests and responses, appraisal (if financed).
5. *Loan and title:* loan application status, homeowner's insurance, title commitment, survey if required, the Closing Disclosure (buyer receives it at least three business days before closing).
6. *Closing:* final walk-through confirmation, signed closing documents, settlement statement, recording and funding confirmation from the closing agent, keys.
7. *After closing:* final copies to the file, commission paperwork for the broker, file closed for compliance.

**England and Wales:**
1. *Sale agreed:* memorandum of sale with both parties' conveyancers; the agent's anti-money-laundering and ID checks as the agency requires.
2. *Conveyancing:* draft contract and property information forms from the seller's conveyancer; searches, survey and mortgage valuation for the buyer; enquiries raised and answered.
3. *Ready to exchange:* mortgage offer, signed contracts, buildings insurance arranged by the buyer from exchange, deposit arranged through the buyer's conveyancer, completion date agreed.
4. *Completion:* funds received by the seller's conveyancer, keys released, meter readings.
5. *After completion:* SDLT return and payment within 14 days (by the buyer's conveyancer), Land Registry registration.

**Scotland:** Home Report (due within nine days of a request), offer through the solicitor, missives, settlement. Northern Ireland: check nidirect.

### 3. For each document, write four things

| Stage | Document | Provided by | Due | Received | Filed where |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

"Due" comes from the contract or the agent; anything else is marked to confirm.

### 4. Mark what only professionals provide

Title commitments, searches, settlement statements, contracts and Closing Disclosures come from the title company, conveyancer or lender. The coordinator requests and files them; it never drafts or edits them.

### 5. Send requests (level 2 or 3)

Draft each request with what is needed, why, and by when. At level 3, send approved request templates to parties already on the file and log them. A request to someone new needs a fresh yes.

### 6. Pre-review check

Before broker review or closing: every item received or explained, every signature and initial present, dates consistent, no document in the wrong file.

## Checks

- The client's own checklist names and order are kept.
- Every document has a provider, a due date and a filing place.
- Every item that depends on local rules is marked [CHECK LOCAL RULES].
- No payment instructions, bank details or wiring information appear on any checklist or request.
- Professional documents are requested, not drafted.
- At level 3, requests went only to parties on the file, from approved templates, and are logged.

## Output format

1. **Checklist by stage** (table per stage).
2. **Missing or at-risk items**, oldest first.
3. **Request drafts** for each missing item.
4. **Local rules to confirm**, with the official page or person to ask.
