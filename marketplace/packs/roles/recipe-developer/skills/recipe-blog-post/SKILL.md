# Recipe blog post

## When to use

- A tested recipe needs to go on the client's website or blog.
- An old post needs updating: clearer card, better tips, fixed markup.
- A food brand wants recipes that use its products.

Do not use it to publish a recipe that has not been tested, copy another site's text or photos, add health or nutrition claims, or hide a sponsorship.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the card, the client's past posts, search data from Google Search Console, reader comments.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** a draft post and an unpublished recipe card in the website and recipe plugin.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish or schedule the post in the agreed categories and update the card, within the limits. Changing or removing a sponsor disclosure, or deleting a post, needs a fresh yes.

## What you need

1. The tested recipe card (use `recipe-card-writing` and `recipe-testing-plan` first).
2. The search phrase or topic the client wants the post to answer.
3. The client's story or reason for the recipe, in their words.
4. Real reader questions: comments, emails, messages about this or similar recipes.
5. Whether the post is sponsored, gifted or uses affiliate links.
6. The source of any recipe it was adapted from.
7. Photos the client owns, described in words, for the alt text.

## Steps

### 1. Check the disclosure
If anything was paid for, gifted or earns a commission, write a clear line at the top of the post near the first mention, for example "This post is sponsored by [brand]" or "I earn a commission on purchases through links in this post". A bio or footer note alone is not enough.

### 2. Write the headnote
Two to four short paragraphs: why to make it, what makes this version work (the technique or ratio you tested), and who it is for. First-hand: what you saw in testing. No padding and no word count target.

### 3. Place the card
Put the recipe card where readers can reach it fast, with a jump link. Keep the card identical to the tested master.

### 4. Tips and swaps
Short headed sections: key tips from testing, tested swaps, untested swaps labelled "not tested", make-ahead and storage. Allergen and dietary notes exactly as the client gave them. Any cooking-safety point gives the official temperature and "check this with your local food safety authority".

### 5. FAQ from real questions
Answer the questions readers actually asked, briefly. Do not invent questions to repeat the search phrase.

### 6. Fill the markup from the card
In the recipe plugin: name, image, description, ingredients, instructions (no step numbers or "Directions" labels inside the steps), prep and cook time or total time, yield, category and cuisine. Add nutrition calories only if the client's qualified person approved the figures, and only with a yield.

### 7. Check originality
If adapted, the method and headnote are written fresh from the client's own test, the source is credited ("adapted from ... "), and no text or photos are copied.

### 8. Alt text and readability
Describe each photo plainly. Short sentences, plain words, units with every number.

### 9. Publish or hand over
At level 3, publish or schedule in the agreed category and log it. Otherwise hand over the draft and the card fields.

## Checks

- Disclosure present at the top if anything was paid, gifted or affiliate.
- The card matches the tested master exactly.
- Every swap is marked tested or not tested.
- Allergen and dietary notes copied exactly; no health or nutrition claims added.
- Safety temperatures are from the official source: check this with your local food safety authority.
- Markup fields match the card; no step numbers inside instructions.
- Adapted recipes credited; no copied text or photos.
- Published only with level 3 access and within limits.

## Output format

1. Disclosure line (if needed)
2. Title and meta description
3. Headnote
4. Recipe card (or link to the card fields)
5. Tips, swaps, storage
6. FAQ
7. Markup field list
8. Alt text for each photo
9. Originality note

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: The Copper Pan, a home baking blog. Search phrase: "cardamom buns recipe".*

> **Disclosure:** none (no sponsor, no affiliate links).
> **Title:** Soft cardamom buns with a knotted twist
> **Headnote:** I tested these buns six times to get a soft crumb that still holds a knot. The trick is a cooked flour paste in the dough, which keeps them soft for two days. If you have made cinnamon rolls, you can make these.
> **Tip (tested):** grind cardamom from whole pods just before mixing; pre-ground gave a flatter flavour in my tests.
> **Swap (not tested):** plant butter and oat milk.
> **FAQ (from reader comments):** "Can I make the dough the night before?" Yes: shape, cover and chill overnight, then prove at room temperature until puffy, about 1 hour in my kitchen.
> **Markup:** name, image (3 ratios), description, 9 ingredients, 7 instructions, prepTime PT40M, cookTime PT15M, recipeYield 12 buns. No nutrition (not approved).
> **Allergens (as you gave them):** wheat (gluten), milk, eggs.
