# Recipe card writing

## When to use

- A baker, cafe or blogger has recipes in notes, on cards or in their head.
- A kitchen needs every cook to make a dish the same way (standard recipes).
- A cookbook or website needs recipes in one consistent house style.

Do not use it to invent recipes the client has not made, copy another person's recipe text, write nutrition panels or health claims, or sign off allergens or food safety.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the client's notes, old cards, photos described in words, and their house style if they have one.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** cards in a drafts folder or unpublished recipe cards on the website.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** replace the master card with a dated new version, or publish the card on the website, within the agreed limits.

## What you need

1. The notes for each recipe, with every amount, time, temperature and pan size the cook gave.
2. Who will cook from it (home cooks, a kitchen team, readers abroad) and which units they use: metric weights, US cups, or both; Celsius, Fahrenheit or both.
3. Yield and portion size, if known.
4. Allergen and dietary notes from the client, and who confirms them.
5. The house style, if any: how the client writes temperatures, abbreviations and ingredient states.

## Steps

### 1. List everything given
For each recipe, list every ingredient, amount, time, temperature, pan size and cue in the notes, in the order the cook mentions them. Do not fill gaps.

### 2. Mark what is unclear
Anything vague ("a bit of flour", "bake until done", no oven temperature, no pan size) gets [check] and a question. Do not guess.

### 3. Order the ingredients
In the order they are used. Group by component (dough, filling, glaze) with a subheading. Write the state at the time of measuring: "100 g walnuts, chopped" (weigh, then chop) or "100 g chopped walnuts" (chop, then weigh).

### 4. Give exact amounts in the units asked for
Weights first for baking. Where the reader uses cups, give both, using the client's own weighing where possible; otherwise flag that cup weights vary by ingredient and how the cup is filled. Kitchen equivalents are approximate (1 cup about 240 mL, 1 tablespoon about 15 mL, 1 teaspoon about 5 mL).

### 5. Write the method
Numbered steps with one or two actions each, a time and a cue the cook can see, smell, hear or measure: "until the edges are golden and the centre wobbles slightly", "until it reaches the safe temperature". For meat, poultry, fish, eggs or reheated food, give the safe temperature for the reader's country from the official source and add "check this with your local food safety authority".

### 6. Add the frame
Yield and portion size; prep, cook and total time; equipment and pan size; make-ahead, storage and reheating notes from the client's notes.

### 7. Copy allergen and dietary notes
Exactly as the client gave them. If they gave none, list the allergens the ingredients suggest as a draft for the client to confirm, labelled "draft, not a claim".

### 8. For kitchen standard recipes
Add batch yield and portion weight, critical points from the business's food safety system, plating standard with a photo slot, hot or cold holding and shelf life from the business's own rules, and the allergen line from its signed-off record.

### 9. Final check and hand over
Read it as a cook who has never made it. At level 3, save or publish within the limits and log it.

## Checks

- Nothing added, removed or swapped without a [suggested] label.
- Every [check] has a question for the client.
- Ingredients are in order of use, amounts in the requested units.
- Every step has a time and a cue.
- Yield, times, pan size and storage are present.
- Safety temperatures come from the official source for the reader's country: check this with your local food safety authority.
- Allergen and dietary notes are copied exactly; no claims added.
- Words are plain; abbreviations follow the house style.

## Output format

1. The card: title, description, yield, times, equipment, ingredients by component, numbered method, storage, notes, allergen and dietary notes
2. A list of [check] questions
3. A list of [suggested] changes, if any

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bakery; website card, metric and US cups. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Brown butter blondies**
> Makes 16 squares. Prep 20 min, bake 25 to 30 min, cool 1 hour. 20 cm (8 in) square tin, lined.
>
> - 170 g (3/4 cup) unsalted butter
> - 200 g (1 cup packed) light brown sugar
> - 2 large eggs, at room temperature
> - 2 tsp vanilla extract
> - 190 g (1 1/2 cups) plain flour [check: your note says "about 190"; please confirm]
> - 1/2 tsp fine salt
>
> 1. Melt the butter in a light-coloured pan over medium heat, stirring, until it foams, smells nutty and the bits on the bottom turn golden brown, 5 to 7 minutes. Pour into a large bowl and cool for 10 minutes.
> 2. Whisk in the sugar, then the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla, until glossy, about 1 minute.
> 3. Fold in the flour and salt until no dry flour shows.
> 4. Spread in the tin. Bake at 175°C (350°F) [check: fan or conventional?] until the edges are golden and set and the centre wobbles slightly, 25 to 30 minutes.
>
> **Storage:** airtight, room temperature, up to 4 days (from your notes).
> **Allergens (as you gave them):** milk, eggs, wheat (gluten).
