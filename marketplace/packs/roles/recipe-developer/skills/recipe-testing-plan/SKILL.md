# Recipe testing plan

## When to use

- Before a recipe goes on a website, in a cookbook or into a kitchen binder.
- A recipe works for its author but fails for others.
- A recipe has been scaled, converted or changed.
- Readers report the same problem in comments.

Do not use it to test food safety controls for a business (that is the business's food safety system and its trained manager), or to make nutrition or health claims.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the draft card, earlier test notes and reader comments.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** the test plan and test sheets in a drafts folder; a changed card saved as a draft.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** replace the master card with the tested version and a dated change note, or update the published card, within the agreed limits.

## What you need

1. The draft recipe card (use the `recipe-card-writing` skill first if it is still notes).
2. Who it is for and what equipment they will have (home oven, combi oven, stand mixer, none).
3. What "success" looks like, in the client's words: texture, colour, taste, yield, time.
4. Who can test: the author, and at least one other cook who has not made it.
5. Any reader comments or kitchen notes about failures.

## Steps

### 1. Write success criteria
Three to six things anyone can check: yield (number or weight), size, colour, texture, taste, total time. Add the safe internal temperature for the reader's country where the recipe cooks meat, poultry, fish, eggs or reheats food, from the official source: check this with your local food safety authority.

### 2. Build the test sheet
For each step: the amount actually used (weighed), the time taken, the temperature (oven set and, where useful, measured), the cue observed, and notes. At the end: yield, photos described in words, taste notes, what was unclear in the card.

### 3. Test 1: the author or developer
Make it exactly as written. Weigh everything, including volume measures, to record real weights. Note every point where you did something the card does not say.

### 4. Fix the card, not the cook
Add every unwritten step or cue found in test 1. Mark changes [suggested] for the client.

### 5. Test 2: a cold test
A different cook makes it from the card alone, with no help. They record on the same sheet. Their questions are the card's weak points.

### 6. Change one variable at a time
If something fails, change one thing (temperature, time, an amount, a technique) per test and record the result, so you know which change worked.

### 7. Test the edge cases that matter
The scaled size, the other unit system, fan and conventional ovens, a common swap the client wants to offer. Each gets its own short test or a note "not tested".

### 8. Sign off
The client approves the final card against the success criteria. At level 3, replace the master or published card with a dated change note and log it.

## Checks

- Success criteria are written before testing and are checkable.
- Real weights are recorded, not only the card's amounts.
- At least one cold test by someone who has not made the dish.
- One variable changed per test, with results recorded.
- Safe temperatures come from the official source: check this with your local food safety authority.
- Untested variations are labelled "not tested".
- The final card shows a version date and what changed.

## Output format

1. Success criteria
2. Test sheet template
3. Test log: test number, cook, change made, result, decision
4. Card changes, each marked [suggested] until approved
5. Sign-off note with version date

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Lark Lane Kitchen, sourdough focaccia for a cafe website.*

> **Success criteria:** 1 tray 30 x 40 cm; deep golden top; open, even crumb; crisp base; ready in under 24 hours including the cold prove.
>
> | Test | Cook | Change | Result | Decision |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | Developer | As written | Pale base; dough stuck to tin | Card: oil the tin more; add "bottom shelf" |
> | 2 | Cafe baker (cold) | Bottom shelf, more oil | Base crisp; baker unsure when dough was "ready" | Card: add cue "doubled, domed, jiggles when tin is shaken" |
> | 3 | Cafe baker | Same card, fan oven at 210°C instead of 230°C conventional | Top golden at 22 min | Card: give both oven settings |
>
> **Not tested:** wholemeal flour swap. **Sign-off:** version 6 Nov, approved by owner.
