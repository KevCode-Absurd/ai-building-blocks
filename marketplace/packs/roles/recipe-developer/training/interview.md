# Training interview: recipe developer

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share a client's unpublished recipes, a publisher's confidential terms or other people's recipe text; describe them instead.

1. **Who have you developed recipes for (home cooks, restaurants, bakeries, brands, publishers), and what did a typical job look like?**
   Why it matters: sets the audience and the kind of work the agent is built for.

2. **When someone hands you rough notes, what do you do first, and what questions do you always ask?**
   Why it matters: captures the intake routine that stops guessing.

3. **Show me a recipe card you are proud of, anonymised. Why does it work?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a model of format, length and voice.

4. **How do you write a doneness cue? Give me three you use often.**
   Why it matters: cues are what separate a usable recipe from a list; real examples teach the agent the craft.

5. **Do you write by weight, volume or both? How do you handle readers who only use cups?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's unit and conversion habits.

6. **How do you scale a recipe up for a kitchen or down for a home cook? What has gone wrong when you scaled?**
   Why it matters: scaling failures give the agent its "does not scale straight" list from experience.

7. **Walk me through how you test a recipe. Who tests, how many times, and what do you record?**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's testing plan.

8. **Tell me about a recipe that failed for readers or cooks after it was published. How did you find and fix the cause?**
   Why it matters: a real failure story teaches the agent where cards break.

9. **How do you handle allergen and dietary notes? Who confirms them?**
   Why it matters: marks where the agent's job ends and the client's responsible person begins.

10. **How do you include food safety points, such as safe temperatures, without scaring readers?**
    Why it matters: shows how to give the authority's figure in plain words.

11. **What is your house style for abbreviations, temperatures, ingredient states and pan sizes?**
    Why it matters: a style sheet keeps every card consistent.

12. **How do you adapt a recipe from a book or another site and keep it your own?**
    Why it matters: originality and credit habits protect the client.

13. **What makes a recipe blog post useful to you, and what do you cut?**
    Why it matters: sets the line between helpful content and padding.

14. **How do you handle sponsored recipes and brand products?**
    Why it matters: grounds disclosure practice in real jobs.

15. **What goes into a cookbook proposal you would send, and what do agents or editors ask for most?**
    Why it matters: captures the proposal know-how the agent cannot get from rules alone.

16. **When a client asks for nutrition facts or a health claim, what do you say and who do you send them to?**
    Why it matters: keeps the agent on the right side of labelling rules.

17. **Which tools do you use (scales, thermometers, spreadsheets, recipe plugins, nutrient databases), and which do you avoid?**
    Why it matters: grounds tool advice in real use.

18. **What do new recipe writers get wrong most often?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent its common mistakes.

19. **Tell me about the hardest recipe you ever had to get right. What finally made it work?**
    Why it matters: a hard story shows judgement and persistence that plain AI lacks.

20. **When a client wants to publish a recipe you think is not ready, how do you handle it?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent to push back usefully.

21. **What do you check in the last five minutes before you send a recipe?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's quality bar.
