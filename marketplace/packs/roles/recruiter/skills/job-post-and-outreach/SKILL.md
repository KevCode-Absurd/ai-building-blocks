# Job post and outreach

## When to use

- The role brief is agreed and the post needs writing.
- An old post brings many applicants who do not fit, or very few at all.
- A recruiter or agency needs first messages for people who have not applied.
- Candidates keep asking the same questions, or hear nothing after applying.

Do not use it to decide who may apply or to set pay; the client decides those.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** old posts, board results and past messages, to learn what worked.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the posts unpublished in the ATS, board or careers page, and the messages as drafts or templates.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish posts and send outreach, follow-ups, updates and declines, within the limits the client confirmed (which roles, boards, templates, daily volume and spend). Log every action.

Without access, everything below comes back as text to paste.

## What you need

1. The agreed role brief: mission, outcomes, must-haves, learnables, pay range, location, hours and hiring steps with dates.
2. The one honest reason this job is worth a look (the work, the team, the schedule, the pay, the growth).
3. The hard parts of the job, said plainly (early starts, weekend cover, heavy lifting).
4. Benefits that are real and written in a policy.
5. For outreach: the person's public professional facts that the client has gathered, such as current role and a piece of work they shared. Never guess facts about a person.
6. The client's tone: two or three past messages they liked.

## Steps

### 1. Write the long post

In this order:
- **Opening (two sentences):** what the job is for and the one reason it is worth a look.
- **What you will do:** the outcomes as plain verbs. "Fix and service 15 to 20 bikes a week" beats "dynamic technical role".
- **A normal week:** hours, place, who you work with, the hard parts.
- **What you need:** must-haves only. Learnables go under "what we will teach you".
- **Pay and benefits:** the range, then the benefits backed by a policy.
- **How hiring works:** each step, how long it takes and when people will hear back.
- **Adjustments:** one line on how to ask for an adjustment in the process.
- **Scam warning:** "We will never ask you for money or bank details to apply."

### 2. Write the short post

Under 150 words for boards with tight limits. Same title, pay, location, must-haves and steps. Never let the versions disagree on a fact.

### 3. Check the wording

- Cut hype titles such as "guru" or "superstar" and coded words such as "dominant" or "aggressive". Describe the task instead.
- Remove age and life-stage hints: "digital native", "young team", "recent graduate", "energetic".
- Remove degree or years rules the job does not truly need.
- Remove anything that asks for a photo, date of birth or health details.
- Keep it at a reading level a busy person can scan on a phone: short sentences, short paragraphs.
- List every change you made and why, so the client can see the reasoning.

### 4. Check search and board rules

If the post will sit on the client's careers page, note the `JobPosting` fields Google needs (title, date posted, description, hiring organisation, location) and that "remote" is only for fully remote jobs with the countries applicants may live in. Once the role is filled the post comes down or is marked expired: at level 3 I do this, otherwise I remind the client.

When posting at level 3, publish only the version the client approved, on the boards in the confirmed list, and check the live post shows the pay, location and steps correctly.

### 5. Write first messages

Three versions, each under 100 words:
- **Why you:** one real, specific reason from the facts provided.
- **What it is:** role, pay range, location or remote, one line on the work.
- **One easy next step:** "Would a 15-minute call next week be useful?"
No flattery you cannot back up. No fake urgency.

### 6. Write follow-ups

Two follow-ups, each shorter than the last, a few working days apart, easy to say no to: "If now is not the time, no reply needed." Stop after the second. At level 3, send them on schedule from the approved templates, and stop at once for anyone who replies or asks not to be contacted.

### 7. Answer common questions

Short, true replies on pay, remote work, hours, the steps, how long it takes and who decides. Where the answer is not known, write [CLIENT TO CONFIRM].

### 8. Write decline notes

One for each stage: after applying, after screening, after interview, after final round. Thank them, give the decision in the first two lines, and offer feedback only if someone will really give it. Keep it short and final.

## Checks before handing over

- The long and short posts agree on every fact.
- Pay range is present, or the gap is marked and the client is told it may be required where the role is based.
- No wording hints at age, gender, origin, health, religion or family.
- Every claim about the job is in the brief or the client's policies.
- Every outreach message uses only facts the client supplied.
- Nothing went live without level 3 access and the client's yes to the action list; every live post and message is in the action log.
- The document carries: *Check this with local rules before use.*

## Output format

1. Long post
2. Short post
3. Table of wording changes (old, new, why)
4. Careers page notes for Google for Jobs
5. Three first messages, two follow-ups
6. Answers to common questions
7. Decline notes by stage
8. Open questions for the client

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a two-surgery dental practice in Columbus, Ohio, hiring a receptionist. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Template: check this with local rules before use. Applies to: Columbus, Ohio, on site.**
>
> **Front desk coordinator, [Business]. $19 to $22 an hour, full time, Monday to Friday.**
>
> You will be the first person our patients meet and the reason their visit runs on time. You will book and confirm about 60 appointments a day, take payments and explain costs before treatment.
>
> **What you need:** you have worked a busy front desk, phone line or shop counter, and you can explain a bill calmly.
> **What we will teach you:** our booking system and dental insurance basics.
> **How hiring works:** a 20-minute phone call, then a 45-minute interview with a short booking task. You will hear from us within five working days at each step.
> **Need an adjustment to apply or interview? Email the practice manager.**
> **We will never ask you for money or bank details to apply.**
>
> | Old | New | Why |
> |---|---|---|
> | "Young, energetic team" | "A team of six who cover for each other" | Age hint removed |
> | "3+ years' experience" | "You have worked a busy front desk, phone line or shop counter" | Describes the skill, not the years |
