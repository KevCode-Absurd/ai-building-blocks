# Role brief and search plan

## When to use

- A role is opening, new or replacement, and the only input is "we need someone".
- A staffing agency takes on a client brief and needs the facts pinned down before sourcing.
- A search has run for weeks with few good applicants and nobody knows why.
- The hiring manager and the owner describe the job differently.

Do not use it to decide who is hired, whether a requirement is lawful, or whether someone can work in the country. Those go to the client and, where the law decides, to a qualified adviser.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the ATS or spreadsheet for past roles, sources of past hires and time in each stage.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** create the role, its stages and the tracker in the ATS without publishing anything, and add tentative interview holds to calendars.
- **Level 3:** not needed for this skill. Publishing starts in `job-post-and-outreach`.

## What you need

Ask for these. Work with what you get and mark each gap [NEEDED].

1. Title, team, manager, and where the person will work: country, state or city, remote, hybrid or on site.
2. Why the role exists now: the work piling up or the problem to solve.
3. What success looks like at 30, 90 and 180 days.
4. Pay range and benefits, and who can change them.
5. The latest start date and the reason for it.
6. Who will interview and decide, and how many hours each can give.
7. Past job posts, past hires who did well, and where they came from (as raw material only).
8. For agencies: the client's fee terms are not needed, but the client's decision makers and feedback deadlines are.

## Steps

### 1. Write the mission and outcomes

One sentence on why the role exists. Then three to five outcomes for the first six months, each one observable by someone else. "Every new customer is set up and trained within five working days" beats "great customer focus". Read them back to the hiring manager and get a yes before anything else.

### 2. Derive the criteria

From the outcomes, list four to six criteria. Mark each as must-have (needed on day one) or learnable (can be taught in about 90 days). Replace every years-of-experience rule with what the person must be able to do; years of experience predict job performance weakly. Replace "culture fit" with the behaviours you mean, such as "writes up decisions so others can act on them".

### 3. Fix the pay and the rules check

Write the pay range into the brief. If the role is in a place with a pay range law, or you are not sure, the range goes in the ad. List the location rules to check: pay range posting, any AI hiring tool rules, background check steps and right to work steps. Do not decide them; name the official source and the person who will confirm.

### 4. Choose channels

Pick two to four channels for this role and say why:
- **Referrals and the team's own networks.** Often the strongest channel, but never the only one, because it copies the current team.
- **Job boards** that people in this field actually use.
- **Your careers page**, marked up so it can show in Google's job search.
- **Direct outreach** to people who have not applied, from a target list.
- **Communities, schools, trade groups and events** where the people gather.

### 5. Build the search brief

For direct sourcing write: the profile in plain words, job titles people with this skill use, companies or sectors they come from, search terms including a simple Boolean string, places to look, and a three-line pitch on why this role is worth a look. Use only public professional information. Never scrape sites against their terms.

### 6. Set stages, owners and dates

List each stage (apply, screen, interview, work sample, references, offer) with its owner, the date it happens and the reply time promised to candidates at that stage. Work back from the start date. Flag any stage with no owner.

### 7. Set up the tracker

One row per candidate: ID, source, stage, date entered, next step, owner, and the reason for any decline in plain words. Names stay in the ATS; a tracker shared outside it uses IDs. With level 2 access, set the stages and decline reasons up in the ATS itself.

### 8. First-week task list

Five to ten tasks with an owner and a day, such as "Monday: manager approves brief" and "Wednesday: post live on two boards; 20 outreach messages drafted for approval".

## Checks before handing over

- Every criterion traces back to an outcome.
- No requirement is a proxy for age, origin, health or family, such as "digital native", "recent graduate" or "must be local" where that is not a real job need.
- The pay range is in the brief, or the gap is marked [NEEDED] with who decides.
- Every stage has an owner, a date and a reply time.
- More than one channel is planned, including at least one open channel.
- The brief names the location rules to check and carries the line: *Check this with local rules before use.*

## Output format

One markdown document:

1. Role brief (mission, outcomes, criteria table with must-have or learnable, pay, location, start date)
2. Rules to check (topic, place, official source, who confirms)
3. Channel plan (channel, why, owner, expected volume if the client has past data)
4. Search brief (profile, titles, sectors, Boolean string, pitch)
5. Stages and dates (stage, owner, date, reply time)
6. Tracker columns
7. First-week task list
8. Open questions for the client

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a 14-person bike shop and repair workshop in Bristol, UK. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Template: check this with local rules before use. Applies to: Bristol, UK, on site.**
>
> **Mission:** Keep repair turnaround short as the workshop grows.
>
> **Outcome 1:** By month three, standard services are back to the customer within two working days (now five).
>
> | Criterion | Must-have or learnable | How we will see it |
> |---|---|---|
> | Diagnoses common bike faults and fixes them to standard | Must-have | 30-minute paid workshop task |
> | Explains a repair and its price to a customer in plain words | Must-have | Interview question 2 |
> | Uses our booking system | Learnable | Trained in week one |
>
> **Removed:** "5+ years' experience" (replaced by the workshop task).
>
> **Channels:** staff referrals; a cycling trade job board; the shop's careers page; a local college's cycle mechanics course.
>
> **Boolean string:** ("bike mechanic" OR "cycle mechanic" OR "bicycle technician") AND (Bristol OR Bath)
>
> **Stage reply times:** every applicant hears back within five working days.
