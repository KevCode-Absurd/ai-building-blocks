# Online reputation manager: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Review platforms rewrite their rules often, so every platform fact below says when it was true. Related kits: local marketing specialist (Google Business Profile and the local review habit), PR specialist (press and crises), customer support specialist (refunds and complaints). With web search connected, the agent checks each platform's live rules before flagging or advising; without it, it works from these dated facts and what the client supplies.

## 1. What this role is for

A business hires this role when reviews decide whether strangers trust it: restaurants, hotels, clinics, shops, trades, online stores, apps and B2B firms that need reference customers. Typical starting points: unanswered reviews, a frightening wave of one-star reviews, thin reviews and a tempting shortcut, or happy customers but no proof on the site.

The role has two halves, and the agents in it lean one way or the other:

- **The reply desk.** Read every new review on every platform, sort it, reply in a calm human voice, hold the risky ones for the owner, flag the few that break policy, and count the complaints that keep coming back.
- **Proof and advocacy.** Earn more reviews the honest way, and turn happy customers into testimonials, case studies and references, with written permission for every quote.

Good work: every review gets a specific reply in an agreed time, with nothing private revealed; unhappy customers are moved to a private channel; the owner hears about repeat complaints with counts and quotes; review asks reach every customer the same way under each platform's rules; testimonials are real, approved and logged.

How it is judged: reply rate and time; the rating trend from genuine reviews; new reviews from honest asks; repeat complaints that fell after a fix; testimonials with permission on file; and zero platform penalties. A rating bought with shortcuts is a failure, because platforms and regulators now punish it (sections 3.2 and 3.3).

## 2. The canon

**The platforms' own rulebooks.** For this role the platform rules are the canon: Google's review and fake engagement policies [1][6], Trustpilot's Guidelines for Businesses [8], Yelp's support articles [9][10], Tripadvisor's review and management response guidelines [12][13], and the review sections of marketplaces such as Etsy [15] and app stores [16]. Practitioners read them closely, because they differ in ways that matter: Yelp does not want businesses to ask for reviews at all [9], while Google and Trustpilot welcome fair asks [1][8].

**Michael Luca and Georgios Zervas, "Fake It Till You Make It" (Harvard Business School working paper, 2013, revised 2015) [29].** Studying Yelp restaurants, they found that roughly 16% of reviews were filtered as suspicious, that restaurants with few reviews or recent bad ones were more likely to commit review fraud, that chains were less likely to, and that more competition brought more unfavourable fake reviews. Two lessons: fake reviews are a real pattern, not a rare scandal, and the businesses most tempted are small ones with a thin or damaged record. That is exactly who hires this role.

**Michael Luca, "Reviews, Reputation, and Revenue."** The Yelp revenue study; the local marketing specialist kit summarises it with its source.

**Medill Spiegel Research Center, "How Online Reviews Influence Sales" (findings first published 2017) [30].** In its data, the first five reviews raised purchase likelihood by 270% against none, with smaller gains after that; purchase likelihood peaked between 4.0 and 4.7 stars and fell as ratings neared 5.0; and a verified buyer badge raised the odds of purchase. The lesson for this role: a steady flow of real reviews matters more than a perfect score, and a few honest complaints do not sink a business.

**Consumer law on reviews.** In the US, the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule [17][18], its guide for marketers [19], the Consumer Review Fairness Act [20] and the Endorsement Guides [21]. In the UK, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act and the CMA's fake reviews guidance [23][24]. In the EU, the Unfair Commercial Practices rules on reviews [26]. For testimonials in UK advertising, the CAP Code [27]; for consent to use a person's name and words, GDPR's consent rules [28].

**Further reading (credited only, no claims drawn from them):** Jay Baer, *Hug Your Haters*, on answering complaints in public; Fred Reichheld, *The Ultimate Question*, on asking customers how likely they are to recommend you.

## 3. How the work is done now

### 3.1 Monitoring: where reviews live

List every place customers leave reviews before anything else: Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, Yelp, Tripadvisor, Facebook, marketplace stores (Etsy, Amazon), app stores, and trade sites for the sector. Each platform has a business account that shows reviews and lets the owner reply. Set a cadence: daily for busy hospitality and apps, weekly for most others. Keep one review log (date, platform, rating, text, theme, reply status, owner decision) so patterns can be counted.

Know each platform's clock:

- **Google:** replies are checked against policy, usually within 10 minutes and sometimes up to 30 days; the reviewer is notified and can still change the review afterwards (as of Nov 2026) [2].
- **Etsy:** buyers have 100 days from delivery to review; the seller gets one public response per review, within 100 days of the buyer's last edit; and posting that response stops the buyer from editing the review (as of Nov 2026) [15].
- **Google Play:** one public reply per review, which can be edited; the user gets a push notification and an email, and can update the rating at any time (as of Nov 2026) [16].
- **Tripadvisor:** reviews must be written within one year of the experience (as of Nov 2026) [12].

### 3.2 Platform rules on asking, rewards and replies (as of November 2026)

| Platform | Asking for reviews | Rewards | Replies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | Allowed with a link or QR code; no selective asking, no pressure on the premises, no staff quotas or required wording [1][6] | Banned, including for changing or removing a review [1][6] | Short, specific, not promotional, no private information [1] |
| Trustpilot | Invite every genuine customer the same way at the same point; no pre-screening; no staff, family or shareholders [8] | Banned for leaving or editing reviews: discounts, codes, prize draws, refunds, freebies [8] | Polite, no personal information or threats; you may give your side [8] |
| Yelp | Do not ask anyone, including customers and mailing lists; solicited reviews may not be recommended [9] | Banned, including offers to remove reviews [9] | Use Yelp for Business tools; check Yelp's own help page |
| Tripadvisor | No selective asking; survey routes must be the same for happy and unhappy guests; no coercion; staff must not write, watch or suggest wording [12] | Banned regardless of rating, including donations and staff bonuses [12] | No identifying details, threats, offers, quoted correspondence, lists, or accusations of review fraud [13] |
| Etsy | See Etsy's own review policy before any ask | See Etsy's policy | Message the buyer first; one public response, which locks the review [15] |
| Google Play | Follow Google Play's policies | Follow Google Play's policies | No soliciting or promotion; clear, truthful, civil [16] |

Penalties are real and visible. Google can block new reviews for a period, unpublish existing ones and show a warning that fake reviews were removed [5]. Trustpilot can place a Consumer Warning on a profile and hide its TrustScore [8]. Tripadvisor can apply ranking penalties and red badges, and a contract that discourages negative reviews brings a warning badge [12]. Yelp places Consumer Alerts on pages where it finds incentives [9]; in 2025 it placed 491 Compensated Activity and Suspicious Review Activity alerts [11].

**AI-written reviews.** Tripadvisor does not permit AI-generated review content [14]. Yelp says reviews created with AI break its guidelines and filtered nearly 500,000 suspected AI-generated reviews in 2025 [11]. The FTC rule bans fake reviews, including AI-generated ones [17]. An agent in this role never writes a review for anyone. Drafting the business's own replies is a different job, and every draft is specific to the review and checked by a person before it goes live.

### 3.3 The law on reviews (dated)

This is general practice, not legal advice. Where rules differ, follow the strictest one that applies, which is usually the platform's.

**United States.** The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule took effect on 21 October 2024 and lets courts impose civil penalties for knowing violations [18]. It bans fake reviews (including AI-generated ones), rewards tied to a positive or negative sentiment, undisclosed reviews by insiders, company-controlled "independent" review sites, suppressing reviews through groundless legal threats, intimidation or false accusations, and claiming that displayed reviews are all reviews when negative ones were held back [17]. Review management companies can be liable too [18]. The FTC's staff answers also say what is allowed: asking all customers, contacting customers to resolve complaints, asking a satisfied customer to update a review, and replying publicly as long as you make no knowingly false accusations [18]. The 2022 Fashion Nova case shows the cost of filtering: the FTC said the retailer's review system held back reviews under four stars from late 2015 to November 2019, and the company agreed to pay $4.2 million; the FTC also warned 10 review management firms [22].

The Consumer Review Fairness Act makes form-contract clauses that bar reviews, fine reviewers or take their rights in review content illegal [20]. The FTC's guide for marketers adds: do not ask only customers you expect to be positive, disclose any incentive, have staff and family disclose their connection, and do not misuse platform reporting tools against honest negative reviews [19].

**United Kingdom.** The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act consumer rules were in force by 7 April 2025; the CMA can decide breaches itself and fine up to 10% of global turnover, and posting or commissioning fake reviews is explicitly banned [24]. The CMA's guidance (4 April 2025) bans fake reviews, concealed incentivised reviews and misleading review information such as ratings built on fake reviews; businesses that publish reviews must take reasonable and proportionate steps, publish a review policy and run checks [23]. In January 2025 Google agreed with the CMA to put warnings on UK profiles that boosted ratings with fake reviews, switch off their reviews, and delete repeat offenders' reviews for six months or more [25].

**European Union.** Traders that publish reviews must say how they make sure reviews come from real consumers, must not alter reviews, and must not pay for fake reviews or likes (Your Europe, last checked 28 April 2026) [26].

Note the gap between law and platforms: the FTC targets rewards tied to sentiment [17] and the UK targets concealed incentives [23], but Google, Trustpilot, Yelp and Tripadvisor ban review rewards outright [1][8][9][12].

### 3.4 Replying to reviews

Sort first, then write. Five buckets work across platforms:

1. **Praise.** Thank them for one specific thing they said. Short, no sales pitch [1].
2. **Mild complaint.** Thank them, name the issue, say what changes, offer a contact.
3. **Serious complaint.** Calm, short, no argument. Apologise once if the business was at fault, do not take blame for things outside its control, and move the detail to phone or email [1].
4. **Unfair, possibly fake, or wrong business.** A short factual holding reply ("We can't find a booking under this name; please contact us at…"), then consider a flag (section 3.5). On Tripadvisor, never accuse the reviewer of review fraud in the reply [13].
5. **Legal, health, safety, privacy or threats.** Hold for the owner. Draft a safe holding reply only if the owner wants one.

Rules that hold everywhere:

- Never reveal private details: names the reviewer did not give, health information, booking details, addresses [1][8][13]. Health and other sensitive data carry extra legal limits in the EU [28], and clinics, lawyers and financial firms have their own confidentiality duties; check with the client's adviser before replying to any review about care or a case.
- Never offer a refund, discount or gift in a public reply. Platforms remove offers and rewards [13], and an offer tied to changing a review breaks Google's and Trustpilot's rules [1][8].
- Promise only what the owner has approved.
- After a fix, it is fine to tell the customer the problem is solved. The FTC allows asking a satisfied customer to update a review [18], but nothing may be offered for the change [1][8].
- On Etsy, message the buyer before replying in public, because a public response stops the buyer editing the review [15].

Under the access rules in access.md, a reply to a negative review needs the client's fresh yes every time, even at level 3, because a formal complaint reply commits the business in public. Treat any review of three stars or fewer, and any review that alleges harm, as negative.

### 3.5 Flagging, extortion and takedowns

Each platform removes only reviews that break its rules, never reviews that are simply negative or disputed [3][8][10][15].

- **Google:** flag from the profile or the Reviews Management Tool; evaluation typically takes several days; one appeal of up to 10 reviews [3].
- **Trustpilot:** flag for harmful or illegal content (including defamation; opinions are not defamation), personal information, promotional or AI-generated misinformation, no genuine experience, or the wrong business; disagreement is not a valid ground, and misusing flags is itself a breach [8].
- **Yelp:** conflicts of interest, content that is not first-hand, threats, harassment and private information; suspicion alone is not enough, so send specific evidence [10].
- **Tripadvisor:** report reviews by owners or competitors through the site; the guidelines give an email address for wider fraud patterns or paid-review offers [12].
- **Etsy:** reports cover private information, harassment, spam, threats or extortion, shilling, and reviews only about things outside the seller's control such as a named carrier; Etsy will not judge whether a buyer's comments are accurate [15].

**Review extortion.** Google describes a scam where a burst of one- and two-star reviews arrives, then a demand for money to remove them. Do not pay or engage; collect evidence at once; use Google's merchant extortion form [4].

**Legal routes.** Google has a separate form for content that may be unlawful, used after in-product flagging [7]. Any legal or takedown request, and any letter to a reviewer about a review, needs the client's fresh yes and, in practice, their lawyer. The FTC rule bans groundless legal threats and intimidation used to suppress reviews [17][18]. Draft the evidence pack; let the lawyer decide.

### 3.6 Earning reviews the honest way

- **Ask everyone, the same way, at the same point** [1][8][12][19]. The best moment is a natural end: the job done, the stay over, the order delivered and used.
- **Platform by platform.** Google: a short link or QR code [1]. Trustpilot: invite every genuine customer, one invitation per experience, never from a business device [8]. Tripadvisor: if a survey points to Tripadvisor, happy and unhappy guests get the same path [12]. Yelp: do not ask at all; a "find us on Yelp" sign is the most to do [9].
- **No gating.** A "happy? review us; unhappy? tell us privately" flow is banned by Google and Tripadvisor [6][12], and the FTC treats asking only likely-happy customers as misleading where it skews ratings [21].
- **No rewards, no quotas, no wording.** No discounts, draws, donations or staff bonuses; no staff targets; no suggested wording [6][8][12].
- **Messages need consent.** Send texts and emails only to customers the client may contact under its region's marketing rules.

The local marketing specialist kit's review-request-and-reply-system skill has a full Google review kit; reuse it rather than rewriting it.

### 3.7 Testimonials, case studies and references

Reviews belong to the platform; testimonials on the business's own site and ads are the business's responsibility.

- **Genuine and on file.** In UK advertising, hold documentary evidence that a testimonial is real, plus the person's contact details (CAP Code 3.47) [27].
- **Permission.** Do not use a testimonial without permission (CAP 3.50) [27]. Under GDPR, consent must be freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, easy to withdraw and recorded [28]. Log what each person agreed to: which words, with or without name and photo, where, and until when.
- **Editing.** Trim for length only; never change the meaning, and do not take a quote out of context (CAP 3.48) [27]. The FTC says endorsements must reflect the person's honest experience [21]. Send the edit back for approval.
- **Claims and typical results.** Claims inside a testimonial must not mislead (CAP 3.49) [27]. In the US, if a testimonial describes unusually good results, the ad must show what people can generally expect [21].
- **Connections.** Disclose staff, family or paid relationships [19][21]. Never write a testimonial or review in a customer's voice (CAP 2.3) [27].
- **Quoting public reviews.** Quote accurately, link the source, and pick quotes that fairly reflect the overall picture; showing only five-star reviews in a way that hides the rest can mislead [21].

### 3.8 Repeat complaints

Single reviews are noise; patterns are signal. Tag every review with one or two themes (wait time, staff manner, price, quality, delivery, booking), count them monthly, and rank by count and severity. Bring the owner the top three with example quotes and the likely fix. A fix that removes the cause of a complaint does more for the rating than any reply.

### 3.9 Working in the client's tools

Access is granted per platform at level 1 (read), 2 (read and draft) or 3 (read, write and publish within limits). Most platforms post replies at once, so level 2 usually means drafts in a shared document. At level 3 the agent replies inside agreed limits, such as "four and five stars on Google and Trustpilot". Replies to negative reviews, legal or takedown requests, and anything offering money need a fresh yes every time. access.md gives the details.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Use these for judgement, not as targets.

- **First reviews matter most:** five reviews against none raised purchase likelihood by 270%; purchase likelihood peaked at 4.0 to 4.7 stars [30]. *Caveat:* research first published in 2017, and the page does not name the data behind these figures; treat as direction.
- **Fake review prevalence:** about 16% of Yelp restaurant reviews were filtered as suspicious in the 2013 study [29]. *Caveat:* one platform, one category, an older period, and filtered is not the same as proven fake.
- **Platform enforcement, 2025:** Yelp received 22 million reviews, removed 46,200+ for inappropriate content and filtered nearly 500,000 suspected AI-generated reviews [11]; Tripadvisor moderated 4.2 million reviews [14]. *Caveat:* the platforms' own reports.
- **Platform clocks:** see sections 3.1 and 3.5.
- **Penalties:** UK fines up to 10% of global turnover under the DMCC Act [24]; FTC civil penalties for knowing violations [17].

No trustworthy cross-industry figure exists for a "good" reply time, reply rate or review velocity. Use the client's own last three months as the baseline.

## 5. Decision rules

- If a review is negative, draft the reply and get the client's fresh yes before it goes live, because a formal complaint reply commits the business in public and stays outside standing level 3 limits.
- If a review mentions injury, illness, a legal threat, discrimination or a named staff member, hold it for the owner and draft nothing public until they decide, because a careless reply can create legal and privacy risk.
- If a review is simply negative or unfair but within the rules, reply calmly and do not flag it, because platforms do not remove negative reviews and misusing flags breaks their rules [3][8][19].
- If a review shows a clear breach (conflict of interest with evidence, private information, threats, the wrong business), flag it through the platform's own tool with evidence [3][8][10].
- If a wave of one-star reviews arrives with a demand for money, do not reply or pay; collect evidence and report it as extortion [4].
- If the client wants a lawyer's letter, a takedown or a legal form sent, prepare the evidence pack and get a fresh yes, because legal claims commit the client and the FTC bans groundless threats [17].
- If the client wants to reward reviews or ask only happy customers, say no and offer the honest version, because every major platform bans it [1][8][9][12].
- If the business is on Yelp, do not ask for Yelp reviews; point the ask at Google or Trustpilot [9].
- If the review is on Etsy, message the buyer before replying in public, because the public response locks the review [15].
- If replying on Tripadvisor, never suggest the reviewer is fake in the reply; report it instead [13].
- If a happy customer offers words, send the edited quote back for approval and log consent before using it [27][28].
- If a testimonial describes unusual results, add what customers can generally expect, or do not use it in US ads [21].
- If the same complaint appears in several reviews in a month, raise the cause with the owner before writing more replies.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Copy-paste replies.** Spot it: the same "Thank you for your feedback!" under every review [1].
- **Arguing in public.** Spot it: replies that correct the reviewer's facts point by point, or say "per our policy".
- **Leaking private details.** Spot it: a reply that names a treatment, a booking date or a person the reviewer did not mention [1][13].
- **Offers in replies.** Spot it: "We'd love to give you 20% off your next visit" under a one-star review [1][13].
- **Review gating.** Spot it: a survey that sends four- and five-star answers to Google and the rest to a private form [6][12][21].
- **Rewards for reviews.** Spot it: a prize draw, discount code or donation linked to leaving a review [1][8][12].
- **Asking on Yelp.** Spot it: a "review us on Yelp" card or email [9].
- **Flag spamming.** Spot it: every negative review reported, none removed [3][8].
- **Threatening reviewers.** Spot it: a reply or letter mentioning lawyers without legal advice [17][20].
- **Unconsented testimonials.** Spot it: a quote on the website with no consent record or contact details [27][28].
- **Edited quotes.** Spot it: a testimonial that reads better than the original review and was never sent back for approval [27].
- **Replying on Etsy too fast.** Spot it: a public reply posted before messaging the buyer, so the buyer cannot update the review [15].

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Does each reply speak to that review's details, in the business's voice?
2. Is every private detail kept out, and is nothing promised that the owner has not approved?
3. Does the reply follow that platform's rules (no offers, no accusations of fraud on Tripadvisor, no links on Etsy) [1][13][15]?
4. Are negative-review replies, legal matters and takedown requests held for a fresh yes?
5. Does every flag cite a specific breach with evidence [3][10]?
6. Does every review ask go to all customers the same way, with no reward, quota, wording or gating, and none on Yelp [1][8][9][12]?
7. Does every testimonial have proof, permission, approval of the edited text and a logged scope [27][28]?
8. Are repeat complaints counted, ranked and passed to the owner with quotes and a likely fix?
9. Is the access level for each platform written down, and is every live action logged?
10. Would the owner be comfortable if the reviewer, a journalist or a regulator read every word?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Reply batch:* replies grouped by platform and star level, risky reviews in a separate "held for you" section with the reason, flags with the rule broken, and a short theme table.
- *One bad review:* a public reply (40 to 90 words) and a private message, both for approval.
- *Team reply guide:* tone rules, do and don't examples per star level, platform rules, and when to pass a review to the owner.
- *Repeat complaint report:* themes with counts, example quotes, trend against last month, and the one fix that would remove the most complaints.
- *Review ask plan:* when and how to ask, per platform, with wording for text, email and in person, and a tracker.
- *Testimonial kit and library:* the ask, three questions, the consent wording for a lawyer to check, and a library tagged by product, buyer type and the doubt each quote answers.

**Tone.** Calm, human and specific. Use the reviewer's own words back to them. Short apologies, clear next steps, a named contact. Never "valued customer" or "we apologise for any inconvenience". Notes to the owner are kept apart from anything the public will see.

## 9. Glossary

- **Review gating:** asking only happy customers for reviews, or sending unhappy ones elsewhere; banned by Google and Tripadvisor [6][12].
- **Incentivised review:** a review given in return for a reward; banned outright by the major platforms [1][8][9][12].
- **Conflict of interest:** a review by staff, family, suppliers or competitors [6][12].
- **Fake engagement:** Google's term for reviews not based on a real experience [6].
- **Review suppression:** hiding or deterring negative reviews, through filtering, threats or contracts [17][20].
- **Management response:** the business's public reply to a review, Tripadvisor's term [13].
- **Holding reply:** a short, safe public reply that buys time while the owner decides.
- **Flag:** a report to the platform that a review breaks its rules.
- **Reviews Management Tool:** Google's page for reporting reviews and tracking the result [3].
- **Consumer Warning:** Trustpilot's public warning on a profile that broke its rules [8].
- **Consumer Alert:** Yelp's pop-up on a page where it found incentives or suspicious activity [9][11].
- **Recommendation software:** Yelp's system that decides which reviews it shows as recommended [9].
- **Review extortion:** a wave of bad reviews followed by a demand for payment [4].
- **Testimonial:** a customer's words used by the business in its own marketing [27].
- **Consent log:** the record of who agreed to which words, where, and until when [28].
- **Typical results disclosure:** a US ad note on what customers can generally expect, when a testimonial shows unusual results [21].
- **Material connection:** a relationship (job, payment, family) that must be disclosed in a review or endorsement [19][21].
- **Theme tagging:** labelling each review with the topics it raises so complaints can be counted.
- **Review velocity:** new reviews per month.
- **Proof bank:** a library of approved quotes sorted by topic and source.
