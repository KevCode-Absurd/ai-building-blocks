# Policy flags and takedowns

Platforms remove reviews that break their rules, not reviews that are negative or unfair. A flag that cites a real rule with evidence has a chance; a flag that says "this is untrue" usually fails, and misusing flags is itself a breach on Trustpilot and a warning sign to the FTC. This skill sorts what can be flagged, what should simply get a calm reply, and what is a legal matter for the owner's lawyer.

Access levels for this skill: reviewing and building the evidence pack is level 1. Writing the flag text for the owner to submit is level 2. Filing flags and one appeal through the platform's own tool is level 3, within the limits in "Access I have". Any legal or takedown request, any report resting on legal grounds such as defamation, and any message to a reviewer about their review need the owner's fresh yes every time, even at level 3. These usually involve the owner's lawyer.

## When to use

- A review may come from staff, a competitor, or someone who was never a customer.
- A review contains private information, threats, hate speech or harassment.
- A review is about a different business.
- A sudden wave of one- and two-star reviews arrives, with or without a demand for money.
- The owner says "get this review taken down".

## What you need

- **Access I have:** level and limits per platform.
- The review: link, screenshot with date, reviewer name as shown, full text.
- The business's records: booking, order or visit search results for that name and date (shared privately, never posted).
- For a wave: a list of every new review with times, and any messages, emails or calls demanding money.
- Any history: earlier flags, appeals and their results.
- **Research mode.** With web search connected, open the platform's current rules page before writing the flag, because grounds change. Without it, work from the kit's dated rules and screenshots the owner sends, and say which rules you could not recheck.

## Steps

1. **Read the platform's grounds** for this review (knowledge section 3.5):
   - Google: fake engagement, conflicts of interest, off-topic, offensive, spam; one appeal of up to 10 reviews through the Reviews Management Tool.
   - Trustpilot: harmful or illegal (including defamation, though opinions are not defamation), personal information, promotional or AI-generated misinformation, no genuine experience, the wrong business.
   - Yelp: conflicts of interest, not first-hand, threats, harassment, hate speech, lewd content, private information; suspicion alone is not enough.
   - Tripadvisor: reviews by owners or competitors, through the site; wider fraud patterns by email to the address in its guidelines.
   - Etsy: private information, harassment, spam, threats or extortion, shilling, or reviews only about things outside the seller's control, such as a named carrier.
2. **Sort the review.**
   - *Clear breach with evidence:* flag it.
   - *Negative, unfair or disputed but within the rules:* do not flag; send it to the review reply desk for a calm reply.
   - *Possible breach, weak evidence:* reply calmly now; flag only if more evidence appears.
   - *Extortion:* follow step 5.
   - *Possible legal issue* (serious false statements of fact, threats, a privacy breach): follow step 6.
3. **Build the evidence pack.** The rule broken, quoted from the platform's page in a few words; the specific lines in the review that break it; supporting facts (no booking under that name on that date, the reviewer works for a named competitor, the review names a different business). Keep customer records out of anything public.
4. **File or draft the flag.** At level 2, write the flag text and steps for the owner. At level 3, file it through the platform's own tool within the limits, record the date and status, and check back after a few days. If Google finds no breach, decide with the owner whether to use the single appeal, grouping up to 10 reviews.
5. **Extortion or attack.** If a burst of bad reviews arrives with a demand for money, tell the owner at once. Do not pay, reply to the extorter or post public replies arguing with the wave. Capture screenshots with dates and times, links to each review, and every name, email, phone number or profile used in the demand. On Google, use the merchant extortion report form. On other platforms, use their report tools and fraud contacts. Draft one short neutral holding reply for the owner to approve if customers are asking about it.
6. **Legal and takedown requests.** Prepare a factual pack for the owner and their lawyer: the content, the URLs, what is false as a matter of fact (not opinion), and the harm. Point to the platform's legal route, such as Google's legal removal form. Do not threaten the reviewer: groundless legal threats used to suppress reviews break the FTC rule, and form-contract bans on reviews break the Consumer Review Fairness Act. Nothing is sent without the owner's fresh yes.
7. **Report.** Summarise flags filed, outcomes, appeals used, and what was left to a reply.

## Checks

- Every flag names a specific rule and points to specific evidence.
- No flag rests only on "this is untrue" or "this is unfair".
- No private customer data in any public reply or public flag text.
- Extortion: nothing paid, no engagement, evidence captured early.
- Legal requests, defamation reports and any contact with a reviewer about the review have the owner's fresh yes on record.
- The appeal limit (Google: one appeal) is not wasted on weak cases.
- Every action is logged with date, platform, review link and status.

## Output format

1. **Triage table:** review, platform, bucket (flag, reply only, watch, extortion, legal), reason.
2. **Flags:** the rule, the evidence, the flag text, status.
3. **Extortion or attack file** (if any): timeline, evidence list, reports filed, holding reply draft.
4. **Legal pack for the lawyer** (if any): content, URLs, facts disputed, harm, platform legal route.
5. **Action log.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Northgate Physio is a made-up clinic, used only to show the format.*

**Triage**

| Review | Platform | Bucket | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Rude receptionist, waited 20 mins" (2 stars) | Google | Reply only | Negative but within the rules; send to the reply desk |
| "Avoid! Go to [rival clinic] instead, much better" (1 star) | Google | Flag | Promotes a competitor; the reviewer's public profile lists that rival clinic as employer (screenshot saved) |
| Five 1-star reviews in 40 minutes, no text, plus an email asking for £300 to "make them go away" | Google | Extortion | Do not pay or reply; report through Google's merchant extortion form |
| "Dr Shah misdiagnosed me and I'm suing" | Google | Legal | Held for you and your lawyer; no public reply until you decide |

**Flag text (rival clinic review):** "Conflict of interest: the reviewer appears to work for the competing clinic named in the review, as shown on their public profile (screenshot attached), and the review promotes that clinic."
