# Review reply desk

A reply is written for the next customer, not only the reviewer. This skill turns a pile of reviews into specific, safe replies, keeps private details out, and makes sure nothing risky goes live without the owner.

Access levels for this skill: reading reviews is level 1. Drafting replies in a shared document is level 2, because most platforms post a reply as soon as it is sent. Posting replies yourself is level 3, only within the limits written in "Access I have" (for example, four- and five-star reviews on Google and Trustpilot). Replies to negative reviews (three stars or fewer, or any review alleging harm) need the owner's fresh yes every time, even at level 3.

## When to use

- The weekly or daily review batch.
- A backlog of unanswered reviews.
- One bad review the owner is upset about.
- A new team member needs examples of good replies.

## What you need

- **Access I have:** level and limits per platform. Until the owner answers, level 1.
- The reviews: platform, date, star rating, reviewer name as shown, full text, and any existing reply.
- The owner's notes on each complaint: was there a booking or order, what happened, what has been fixed.
- What the business may promise without asking (for example, "a call from the manager"), and what it may not (refunds, discounts, free items).
- A named contact and channel for complaints.
- Three or four past replies the owner liked, for tone.
- Whether the business is in health, law, finance or another field with confidentiality duties.
- **Research mode.** With web search connected, check each platform's reply rules page for changes before a big batch. Without it, use the kit's dated rules and say so in the batch notes.

## Steps

1. **Read and summarise.** For each review, write what the customer actually said in under ten words. Read the hard ones twice before drafting.
2. **Sort into buckets.**
   - *Praise* (usually four or five stars).
   - *Mild complaint.*
   - *Serious complaint* (three stars or fewer, or a clear failure).
   - *Unfair, possibly fake or wrong business* (no record of the customer, details that do not match, a review about another firm).
   - *Hold* (injury, illness, safety, legal threat, discrimination, a named staff member, privacy, press interest).
3. **Check the platform's reply rules** before writing:
   - Google: short, specific, not promotional, no deals, no private information (knowledge section 3.4).
   - Trustpilot: polite, no personal information or threats; you may give your side.
   - Tripadvisor: no identifying details, no offers, no quoting correspondence, no lists, and never accuse the reviewer of review fraud.
   - Etsy: message the buyer first, because a public response stops the buyer from editing the review; no tracking numbers, links or private details.
   - App stores: clear, truthful, civil; no soliciting or promotion.
4. **Write praise replies.** Thank the reviewer for one specific thing they mentioned, add one human detail, and stop. No sales pitch.
5. **Write complaint replies.** Name the exact problem, apologise once if the business was at fault, do not take blame for things outside its control, say what has changed if something has, and give the named contact. Promise only what the owner approved. Never offer money, discounts or gifts in public.
6. **Write a private message** for each serious complaint where the platform allows it, or where the business holds the customer's contact details and may use them: a sincere apology, one question to understand what went wrong, and the way to put it right. Any refund or credit is the owner's decision.
7. **Handle unfair or possible fakes.** Write a short factual holding reply ("We can't find a booking under this name; please contact Sam at …") and pass the review to the policy flags skill if it may break the platform's rules. Do not call the review fake in public.
8. **Hold the risky ones.** For each review in the hold bucket, write why it is held and what decision the owner must make. Draft a holding reply only if the owner wants one.
9. **Count themes.** Tag each review with one or two themes and pass the counts to the repeat complaint report.
10. **Get approvals.** Send negative-review replies and private messages to the owner for a fresh yes. At level 3, post approved replies and replies inside the standing limits; log each one.
11. **Check status.** After posting, check each reply went live; Google checks replies against policy, usually within minutes but sometimes much longer. Report anything removed or rejected.

## Checks

- Every reply mentions something the reviewer actually said.
- No names, dates, treatments, order details or addresses the reviewer did not give.
- No offers, discounts, gifts or refunds in any public reply.
- No arguing and no point-by-point corrections.
- No public suggestion that a review is fake.
- Every negative-review reply has the owner's fresh yes on record.
- Held reviews carry a reason and the decision needed.
- At level 3, only replies inside the written limits went live, and each is logged.

## Output format

1. **Ready to post** (inside limits), grouped by platform and star level.
2. **Needs your yes:** negative-review replies with private messages.
3. **Held for you:** risky reviews, the reason, and the decision needed.
4. **Possible policy breaches:** to pass to the policy flags skill.
5. **Themes this batch:** a short table of themes with counts.
6. **Action log** (level 3): what was posted, where, when, result.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a harbourside restaurant. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Ready to post (Google, 5 stars, "The crab linguine was perfect and Mia looked after us"):**
"Thank you, Daniel. We'll tell the kitchen the linguine landed well, and Mia will be pleased you mentioned her. See you next time you're on the harbour."

**Needs your yes (Tripadvisor, 2 stars, "Waited 50 minutes for mains, nobody told us why"):**
Public: "Thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry. Fifty minutes without an update is not the evening we want any guest to have. On busy Saturdays we now check every table when mains pass 25 minutes. I'd like to hear more, so please contact our manager, Priya, at the restaurant."
Private message (if the platform allows it): "I'm sorry about the wait on Saturday. Could you tell me roughly what time you ordered, so I can see what went wrong in the kitchen? I'd like to put it right."

**Held for you (Google, 1 star, "Got food poisoning after the mussels"):** health claim. Decide whether to contact the reviewer and whether your food safety process needs a check. No public reply drafted until you say.

**Themes:** slow mains on Saturdays (4), friendly staff (6), noise (2).
