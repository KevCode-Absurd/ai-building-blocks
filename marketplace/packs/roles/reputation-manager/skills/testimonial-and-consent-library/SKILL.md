# Testimonial and consent library

A testimonial is only useful if it is specific, real and usable. "Great service" persuades no one, and a quote with no consent record is a risk. This skill gets specific words from the right customers at the right moment, keeps their meaning, and keeps a clear record of what each person agreed to.

Access levels for this skill: reading feedback, surveys and reviews is level 1. Drafting requests, question forms and the library is level 2. Sending requests from the client's email tool to customers the client has confirmed, and adding approved quotes to the shared library, is level 3, within the limits in "Access I have". Using a customer's name, photo or words in a new place (website, ad, sales deck) needs the client's fresh yes each time.

## When to use

- Plenty of happy customers, no proof on the website.
- Buyers keep asking for references, and the same three customers are asked every time.
- The owner wants to put review quotes into ads or pages.
- A case study is planned.

## What you need

- **Access I have:** level and limits for the email tool, review exports and the library folder.
- Signs of happy customers: renewals, survey scores, thank-you messages, recent wins, five-star reviews.
- Which customers the business may contact, and through which channel.
- Where testimonials will appear (website, ads, sales deck, print) and in which countries.
- The business's own consent wording, if any. Consent forms should be checked by the client's lawyer.

## Steps

1. **Pick who to ask.** Customers with a recent result, a renewal or a thank-you. Spread asks across customer types, so the library covers different buyers and doubts. Do not ask anyone in an open complaint or right after a problem.
2. **Pick the moment.** Just after a win: the project delivered, the first month's result, the renewal, the thank-you email.
3. **Write the request.** Short, warm, and honest about use: what you are asking for, where it may appear, that they can say no or change their words, and that nothing is used without their final yes. No reward tied to what they say. If anything is given as a thank-you, it goes to everyone who replies whatever they say, and the client checks it against the rules of any review site involved and discloses it where required.
4. **Ask three questions, never ten.**
   - "What was going on before you came to us?"
   - "What changed, and roughly when did you notice?"
   - "Is there a number, a moment or a detail that sums it up?"
5. **Edit lightly.** Cut for length; fix typos; keep their words and meaning. Do not combine answers from different people, change a claim, or move a quote to a newer product it was not about.
6. **Send the edit back for approval.** Show the exact text, the name and photo you plan to use, where it will appear, and for how long. Record the yes.
7. **Check claims.** If the quote states a result ("cut our bills by 40%"), the business must be able to stand behind it. In US ads, if the result is better than customers can generally expect, add what they can generally expect or do not use it. Remove health or financial claims the business cannot support.
8. **Disclose connections.** If the person is staff, family, a partner or was paid or gifted, the testimonial says so.
9. **Log consent.** One row per quote: person, contact details, the approved words, name and photo use, where, until when, date and how they agreed. Keep their contact details on file privately.
10. **Tag and store.** Tag each approved quote by product, buyer type and the doubt it answers (price, speed, trust, fit). Set a review date to re-confirm or retire quotes.
11. **Reuse public reviews carefully.** Quote accurately, name the platform and date, do not edit the meaning, and do not present only five-star quotes in a way that hides the overall picture. Ask permission before using a reviewer's name in an ad.
12. **Thank them** promptly and personally.

## Checks

- Every quote is from a real, identifiable customer, with evidence and contact details on file.
- Every quote has a recorded yes to the exact words and the planned use.
- No quote is edited in a way that changes its meaning or context.
- Results quoted are supportable, with a typical-results note for US ads where needed.
- Connections and gifts are disclosed.
- Nobody writes a testimonial or review in a customer's voice.
- Consent can be withdrawn as easily as it was given, and the library shows how.

## Output format

1. **Ask plan:** who, why them, when, channel.
2. **Request message** and the three questions.
3. **Approval message** showing the edited quote and planned use.
4. **Consent log** (table).
5. **Library:** approved quotes tagged by product, buyer type and doubt answered.
6. **Notes for the owner:** claims to check, consent wording for the lawyer.

## Example of good output (short)

*Ledgerline Bookkeeping is a made-up accounting firm, used only to show the format.*

**Request (email, sent the week after the client's year-end filing):**
"Hi Amara, now that your year-end is filed, would you be willing to share a few words about working with us? We'd like to use them on our website. Three quick questions are below; answer as briefly as you like. We'll send you the final wording before anything is used, and you can say no at any point."

**Edited quote sent back for approval:**
"Before Ledgerline, I spent most Sunday evenings on receipts. Now I send a photo and it's done. Our year-end went in three weeks early." Amara Okafor, owner, Okafor Florals.
"Is this OK to use on our website, with your name and business name, for two years?"

**Consent log**

| Person | Approved words | Name/photo | Where | Until | Agreed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amara Okafor | As above | Name and business, no photo | Website | Nov 2028 | Email reply, 12 Nov |

**Tags:** product: monthly bookkeeping; buyer: florist, sole owner; doubt answered: "Will this save me time?"
