# Interview: online reputation manager

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you give me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work.

## Replies

1. **Show me a reply to a one-star review you are proud of. What made it work?**
   Why it matters: their best reply becomes the agent's model for tone and structure.
2. **What do you do in the first ten minutes after reading a nasty review?**
   Why it matters: reveals their habits for slowing down and avoiding replies written in anger.
3. **Which reviews do you never answer yourself, and who decides instead?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's hold list and escalation path.
4. **How do you reply when the reviewer is simply wrong about the facts?**
   Why it matters: the hardest tone call in the job, and where most owners go wrong.
5. **How do you stop replies to five-star reviews sounding the same?**
   Why it matters: generic replies are the most common weakness; their tricks carry over.
6. **What do you change when you reply on Tripadvisor, Trustpilot, Yelp, Etsy or an app store instead of Google?**
   Why it matters: platform differences decide what is allowed and what works.

## Rules and judgement

7. **Tell me about a time an owner wanted to reward reviews or ask only happy customers. What did you say?**
   Why it matters: shows how they say no and what honest alternative they offer.
8. **When do you flag a review, and when do you leave it with a reply? What evidence have you seen work?**
   Why it matters: separates real policy breaches from wishful flags.
9. **Have you handled a review extortion attempt or a coordinated attack? Walk me through it.**
   Why it matters: rare, high-stakes events where a clear sequence protects the client.
10. **When has a review turned into a legal matter, and how did you hand it to a lawyer?**
    Why it matters: sets where the agent's work stops and the lawyer's begins.
11. **Which sectors you work in have extra privacy or confidentiality rules, and how do they change your replies?**
    Why it matters: health, legal and financial replies carry risks a general reply does not.

## Patterns and proof

12. **How do you tag and count complaints? Show me a theme list you use.**
    Why it matters: their theme list becomes the agent's starting set.
13. **Tell me about a repeat complaint you spotted that led to a real change in the business.**
    Why it matters: the best evidence that the role fixes causes, not only reviews.
14. **How do you choose which customers to ask for testimonials, and when?**
    Why it matters: timing and choice decide whether answers are specific.
15. **What questions get you specific testimonial answers instead of "great service"?**
    Why it matters: their questions become the agent's defaults.
16. **How do you record consent for a quote, and how long do you keep using it?**
    Why it matters: consent records protect the client and the customer.

## Tools and clients

17. **Which tools do you use to watch reviews across platforms, and what do you do without them?**
    Why it matters: shapes access.md and the no-tools fallback.
18. **What limits do clients usually give you for posting replies yourself?**
    Why it matters: real limits make the agent's level 3 defaults sensible.
19. **How do you report to an owner each month so they actually act?**
    Why it matters: the report format decides whether fixes happen.
20. **What is the hardest client you have had on reviews, and what did you learn?**
    Why it matters: hard stories teach judgement that rules do not.
21. **What do people new to this work get wrong in their first month?**
    Why it matters: turns into the agent's common mistakes list.
