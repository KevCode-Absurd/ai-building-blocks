# Review attack response

**Trigger:** a sudden burst of one- and two-star reviews, a demand for money to remove reviews, reviews that look coordinated, or a complaint going viral after press or social media attention.

**Cadence:** start within the hour the spike is spotted; check twice a day until reviews return to normal.

**Access:** L1 (read), L2 (read and draft), L3 (read, write and publish within limits). Most of this workflow is L1 and L2; nothing public goes out without the owner's yes.

## Steps

1. **Tell the owner, L1.** One message: what is happening, on which platforms, since when, how many reviews. Pause any level 3 posting on the affected platforms until the owner says otherwise.
2. **Capture evidence, L1.** Screenshots with dates and times, links to each review, reviewer names as shown, and every message, email, call note or profile linked to any demand. Do this first, because reviews and profiles can disappear.
3. **Sort the cause, L1.**
   - *Extortion:* a demand for money or favours. Do not pay or engage.
   - *Coordinated or fake:* no matching customers, similar wording, accounts created recently.
   - *Real backlash:* a genuine incident or a viral post; reviews describe real experiences or reactions to news. Yelp, for example, places Media Attention Alerts on pages that draw sudden public attention.
4. **Report through each platform, L2 or L3 (skill: policy-flags-and-takedowns).** Google: the merchant extortion form for demands, and flags for reviews that break policy. Other platforms: their report tools and fraud contacts. At L3, file within the limits; otherwise draft for the owner.
5. **Holding reply, L2.** Draft one short, calm public statement or holding reply for the owner to approve, if customers are asking. No accusations, no arguing, no private details.
6. **Real incident, L1 and L2.** If the backlash comes from something that really happened, pass it to the owner and the PR specialist kit's crisis-response skill. Reviews get replies only after the owner has decided what the business will say and do.
7. **Legal steps, fresh yes only.** If the owner wants a lawyer involved or a takedown request filed, prepare the evidence pack for the lawyer. Nothing is sent to a platform's legal route or a reviewer without the owner's fresh yes.
8. **Track, L1.** Twice a day: new reviews, flags' status, removals, alerts placed on the page. Log everything.
9. **Close and review, L1.** When reviews are back to normal, write a short summary: what happened, what was removed, what replies went out, and what to change.

## Outputs

- An evidence file with timeline.
- Platform reports filed or drafted, with status.
- An approved holding reply, if needed.
- A lawyer's pack, if the owner chose legal steps.
- A closing summary and action log.

## Needs the client's approval

- Every public reply or statement during the attack.
- Restarting level 3 posting on the affected platforms.
- Any legal or takedown request, and any contact with a reviewer or extorter.
- Any payment of any kind: the answer is no, and the owner decides with their adviser.
