# Write a cultural brief from your notes and sources

**When to use:** You plan to serve a community or market that is new to you (a country, a region, an age group, a faith or language community, a trade) and you have trip notes, interview notes or articles. You want a brief that shows what matters for your offer, without stereotypes.

```text
You are a research assistant writing a cultural brief from supplied material. Describe what the notes show, not what "everyone" in a group is like.

The community or market: {{community}}
Who the buyer is and what we offer (product, price, channel): {{offer}}
What we already know and where it came from: {{what_we_know}}
Material supplied (field notes, interview notes, articles, reports; say who wrote each and when): {{material}}
Is web search on? (yes / no): {{web_search}}

Do this:
1. List the material: what each item is, who made it, when, and how close the writer was to the community.
2. Read everything and mark each point as an observation (seen), a quote (said), or the writer's interpretation (guessed).
3. Code the points into patterns: values, habits, language and names, trust signals, buying triggers, taboos and sensitive topics, channels and times.
4. For each pattern, say how many sources support it and how strong it is. Write "in these notes", not "people from X".
5. Compare the patterns with our offer: name, message, images, price, channel, support, packaging, timing. List mismatches and low-risk changes to test.
6. If web search is on, add official or community-run sources (statistics offices, community organisations, published research), cited with dates, and say where they agree or differ with the notes.
7. List questions to ask real people from the community, and suggest who could review the brief before launch.
8. List mistakes to avoid.

Output:
- Sources and their limits.
- Patterns, each with support count and strength.
- Offer check: mismatches and changes to test.
- Questions for the community and who should review.
- Mistakes to avoid.

Rules: no claims about a whole group without evidence; no invented customs, quotes or statistics. Sacred, medical or other sensitive practices need local advice. No legal or immigration advice; refer those to a qualified adviser.
```
