# Training interview: Research assistant

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share client names, unpublished findings, confidential documents or anyone's personal details; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of questions do clients bring you most: fact checks, study summaries, literature reviews, histories, cultural briefs, something else?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and which skills it should reach for first.

2. **When a request is vague ("can you look into this?"), what do you ask to turn it into a question you can answer?**
   Why it matters: framing decides the scope and the stopping point; the expert's questions become the agent's intake.

3. **Walk me through your last fact check, claim by claim. How did you decide which claims to check?**
   Why it matters: captures the real method for extracting claims and what the expert treats as checkable.

4. **What status labels do you use for claims, and where do you draw the line between "partly supported" and "not supported"?**
   Why it matters: consistent labels are what clients act on; the expert's edge cases teach judgement.

5. **How do you trace a claim back to its original source? Tell me about one that turned out different upstream.**
   Why it matters: going upstream is where most errors are caught; a real story shows the pattern.

6. **Which sources do you trust by default, which do you never use, and why?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a tested source hierarchy and the expert's warnings.

7. **How do you check whether a reference is real and correct, especially one written by an AI tool?**
   Why it matters: made-up and garbled references are common; the expert's checks become the agent's routine.

8. **How do you check for retractions, corrections and doubtful journals?**
   Why it matters: citing retracted or hijacked work is a quiet but serious failure.

9. **When you read a study, what do you look at first, and what makes you trust it less?**
   Why it matters: the reading order and red flags are the core of judging evidence strength.

10. **How do you explain to a business reader that a study shows a link but not a cause?**
    Why it matters: this is the most common overstatement; the expert's wording is reusable.

11. **How do you handle relative versus absolute numbers, and p-values, in summaries for non-experts?**
    Why it matters: these are where readers are most often misled; the agent needs plain phrasings.

12. **Tell me about a time sources flatly disagreed. How did you work out which to trust?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent to look for the reason before picking a side.

13. **How do you plan and log a literature search so someone else could repeat it?**
    Why it matters: repeatability separates a review from a reading list.

14. **What do you do when you have no web access and only the client's files?**
    Why it matters: the agent must work honestly in supplied-only mode and say what is missing.

15. **For histories: how do you weigh documents made at the time against later memories and family stories?**
    Why it matters: the expert's labels keep tradition and fact apart in published histories.

16. **For cultural briefs: how do you avoid turning a few notes into claims about a whole community?**
    Why it matters: protects clients from stereotypes and the agent from overreach.

17. **How long a quote do you allow yourself, and when do you tell a client to get permission?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's practical copyright habits without giving legal rulings.

18. **What does your finished deliverable look like? Walk me through the layout of one you are proud of.**
    Why it matters: captures formats, ordering and tone the agent should copy.

19. **What do you check in the last ten minutes before you send anything?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's quality bar.

20. **Tell me about a hard job: a client who wanted the evidence to say more than it did. What happened?**
    Why it matters: shows how the expert holds the line kindly, which the agent must learn.

21. **What mistakes do newer researchers make that you have stopped making?**
    Why it matters: turns experience into rules the agent can follow from day one.

22. **Which tools do you use (reference managers, databases, archives, AI tools), and what does each do well or badly?**
    Why it matters: fills in access.md with tested tools and the expert's cautions.
