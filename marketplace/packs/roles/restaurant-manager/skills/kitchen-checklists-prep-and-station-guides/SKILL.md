# Kitchen checklists, prep lists and station guides

## When to use

- A new opening or a new menu.
- A new manager or a team with high turnover that needs written standards.
- Dishes run out on busy days and prep goes in the bin on quiet days.
- Shifts do not pass on what happened.

Do not use it to set food safety limits, write the business's food safety management system, or sign off allergen information. Those come from the business's own system (SFBB, Safe Catering or the health department's forms) and its trained manager.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** menu, recipes, covers by day and time from the booking tool or till, the current lists, the floor plan, the food safety pack or diary.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft checklists, prep lists and guides in the team's wiki, checklist app or a shared folder, not yet assigned.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish approved checklists and guides, assign them to areas and roles, and set recurring checklist runs. I never tick or sign a check: the person who does it records it.

## What you need

1. The menu and recipes, with prep items for each dish.
2. Covers by day of week for the last four to eight weeks, and any booked events.
3. Opening hours, areas (kitchen, bar, floor, front of house) and who works each.
4. The business's food safety system and its daily checks. In England and Wales that is often the FSA's Safer food, better business pack and diary.
5. Current lists, even rough ones, and the problems the owner sees.

## Steps

### 1. Walk a full day on paper
From first arrival to last lock-up, area by area: deliveries, prep, set-up, service peaks, breaks, changeover, close. Note who does what and when.

### 2. Write opening and closing checklists
One list per area, in the order the work happens, each line an action with a time and a role. Copy the food safety checks from the business's own system, word for word. The SFBB caterers pack, for example, opens with checks that fridges and freezers work, staff are fit for work, surfaces are clean, there are no signs of pests, hand-washing supplies and hot water are there, the probe works and allergen information is accurate; and it closes with food covered and labelled, use-by food thrown away, waste out and the day's checks recorded. Mark every food safety line "[confirm with your trained manager: check this with your local food safety authority]".

### 3. Set prep pars from covers
For each prep item: forecast covers for the day, times the item's use per cover (from recent sales of the dishes it goes into), times the amount per portion, plus the owner's chosen buffer. Prep = par minus what is on hand and still in date. Do this by day of week. Show the rule so the owner can change the buffer.

### 4. Write the prep list
One list per station: item, unit, par for today, on hand, to prep, who, done. Order items by when they are needed and by shelf life, so short-life items are prepped closest to service. Note the business's own date-labelling rule.

### 5. Write a station guide for each station
One page: what the station makes, set-up (tools, small wares, mise en place with amounts), the steps for each dish, the plating standard (a photo slot), timing at the pass, common mistakes and fixes, and the close-down. Allergen-sensitive steps (separate boards, clean utensils, separate fryer) come from the business's allergen procedure.

### 6. Design the shift log
Columns: time, area, what happened (86'd items, breakages, equipment faults, complaints, near misses), action taken, who must follow up. A short handover box at the end: what the next shift must know first. Food safety issues go at the top.

### 7. Test as a new starter
Read every list as someone on day one. Cut vague words ("as needed", "properly"). Each line should be something you can see done.

### 8. Publish or hand over
At level 3, publish the approved documents and set the recurring runs. Otherwise hand over print-ready pages.

## Checks

- Lines are in task order, each with a time or trigger and a role.
- Food safety checks are copied from the business's own system and marked for the trained manager: check this with your local food safety authority.
- Pars show the rule and change by day of week.
- Station guides fit one page and match the current menu and recipe cards.
- The shift log puts food safety and allergen issues first.
- Nothing vague: every line can be seen done.
- Nothing published without level 3 access; no check ticked on someone's behalf.

## Output format

1. Opening and closing checklist per area
2. Prep list per station with the par rule
3. Station guide per station
4. Shift log and handover template
5. Open questions marked "Needs answer"

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bistro, Saturday prep. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Par rule:** forecast covers x uses per cover (last four Saturdays) x portion, plus the owner's 10% buffer.

> | Item | Covers | Uses per cover | Portion | Par | On hand | Prep | Who |

>
> **Kitchen opening (8:30, first chef in):** fridges and freezers working and at the temperatures your system sets [confirm with your trained manager: check this with your local food safety authority]; probe working and wipes out; hand basin stocked; allergen folder matches today's specials.
> **Needs answer:** who cleans the extraction canopy, and how often?
