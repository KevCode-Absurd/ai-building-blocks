# Weekly restaurant review

## When to use

- Every week, on the same day, as the operating rhythm of the business.
- The same complaints, run-outs or waste keep coming back.
- A new manager needs a simple routine to take over.

Do not use it to judge individual staff, decide pay or discipline, rule on employment law or handle a food poisoning or allergic reaction report. Those go straight to the owner.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** shift logs, till reports, booking summaries, waste log, reviews, rota and timecard totals.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** the review, draft review replies, an unpublished rota for next week, draft menu and hours updates.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** post the review to the agreed channel; reply to reviews within the limits the owner set; publish the approved rota and notify affected staff; update online menus, sold-out items, hours and special hours. Replies to formal complaints, illness or allergy reports, or legal threats need a fresh yes each time.

## What you need

1. This week's and last week's covers and sales by day, from the till or booking tool.
2. The shift logs, including 86'd items, equipment faults and complaints.
3. The waste log (item, amount, reason, value at cost if known).
4. Ticket times if the kitchen display or till records them.
5. New reviews on Google and the booking platform.
6. Labour hours by day from timecards, and next week's forecast covers and events.

## Steps

### 1. Check last week's fixes
Done, not done, or done but did not work. A fix not done twice is dropped or given to someone with time.

### 2. Read the numbers
Covers, sales and average spend by day; labour hours per cover by day; waste value and top three items; 86'd items and when; ticket times at the peak. Compare with last week and say whether each change is real or noise (a holiday, a big party, a closed day).

### 3. Read the logs and reviews for themes
Group complaints and review comments: wait times, food temperature, portion, a named dish, noise, booking problems, staff manner, price. Count each theme. Anything about illness, foreign bodies or allergic reactions goes to the owner at once, not into a theme count, and is handled through the business's food safety procedure: check this with your local food safety authority.

### 4. Find the cause behind the top issues
For each issue that matters, ask why until you reach something the team can change: a prep par, a station set-up, a rota gap at the peak, a missing step in the allergen procedure. If unclear, the fix is to measure it next week.

### 5. Choose three fixes
Each with one owner, one date and how you will know it worked. Use the `kitchen-checklists-prep-and-station-guides` skill for par or checklist fixes and the `menu-engineering-and-food-cost-check` skill for cost fixes.

### 6. Draft next week's rota against covers
Place hours where forecast covers are, by day and time. Flag any line that may touch break, rest, notice or minor-worker rules for the owner to check against the official source for their area; check local rules. Do not change anyone's contracted hours or pay.

### 7. Housekeeping online
List what must change on the till, Google Business Profile and booking or delivery pages: dishes added or removed, prices, sold-out items, holiday hours. Check allergen notes match the signed-off record.

### 8. Replies to reviews
Draft a specific, calm reply to each new review: thank, name the detail they mentioned, own what went wrong, say what changed, invite them back or to get in touch. Never offer a reward tied to a better review. Hold for the owner any review that mentions illness, allergies, injury, staff by name, or a legal threat.

### 9. Publish or hand over
At level 3 and within the limits: post the review, publish the rota, post the routine replies, make the online changes and log each one. Otherwise hand over the page, the rota draft and the reply drafts.

## Checks

- Every fix from last week has a status.
- Numbers come from the till, logs or bookings, with dates; guesses are labelled.
- Illness and allergy reports went to the owner and are not in a theme count: check this with your local food safety authority.
- Fixes name processes, not people's character.
- Rota flags say "check local rules" and name the official page; no ruling on what is lawful.
- Review replies are specific, offer no reward for praise and hold risky reviews.
- Every live change was within level 3 limits and is in the action log.

## Output format

One page:
1. Last week's fixes: status
2. Numbers table: this week, last week, change, note
3. Themes from logs and reviews, with counts
4. Three fixes: owner, date, measure
5. Rota draft summary with flags
6. Online housekeeping list
7. Review replies: posted, drafted, held for the owner
8. Action log (level 3)

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Saltmarsh Kitchen, week to Sunday 2 Nov.*

> | Number | This week | Last week | Note |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Covers | 612 | 588 | Half-term Friday |
> | Labour hours per cover (Sat) | 0.21 | 0.24 | One fewer runner |
> | Waste at cost | £214 | £168 | Bread £71, risotto base £48 |
> | Items 86'd | 5 | 2 | Hake out at 8:10 pm Fri and Sat |
>
> **Themes:** slow mains on Saturday 7 to 8 pm (4 comments, 2 reviews); cold chips (2).
> **Held for the owner:** one review mentions a nut allergy reaction. Not answered. Logged for the allergen procedure.
> **Fixes:** (1) Hake par Friday and Saturday from 18 to 24 portions, owner: head chef, date: 7 Nov, measure: 86 time. (2) Add a second fryer basket at the pass 7 to 9 pm, owner: sous chef, measure: chip complaints. (3) Bread: bake in two batches, owner: pastry, measure: bread waste.
> **Rota flag:** Saturday close-to-open for one chef leaves under 11 hours between shifts: check local rules before publishing.
