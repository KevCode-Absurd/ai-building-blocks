# Training interview: restaurant manager

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share guest details, staff names or health information, or a former employer's confidential figures; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of places have you run (restaurant, cafe, bar, catering, food truck), how many covers, and what was your role?**
   Why it matters: sets the scale and service style the agent is built for.

2. **When you take over a kitchen or a site, what do you look at in the first week, and in what order?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's diagnostic routine, the agent's first-week playbook.

3. **How do you cost a dish? Walk me through one, including yield and the bits people forget.**
   Why it matters: costing habits (yield tests, garnish, sub-recipes) separate useful cards from wishful ones.

4. **How do you set a menu price once you know the cost? When do you ignore the target food cost?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert balances the formula with the local market and the dish's role.

5. **Tell me about a month when food cost was well over target. How did you find the cause?**
   Why it matters: a real investigation gives the agent its order of checks and its warning signs.

6. **How do you run a stock count so the numbers can be trusted month to month?**
   Why it matters: bad counts are the root of most false food cost alarms.

7. **How do you decide which dishes stay, change or go? Give an example of a dish you kept even though the numbers said cut it.**
   Why it matters: captures the judgement behind menu engineering calls.

8. **How do you set prep pars, and how do you change them for weather, events or a bank holiday?**
   Why it matters: par setting is the main lever on run-outs and waste.

9. **What goes on your opening and closing checklists, and how do you make sure people actually do them rather than tick them at the end?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert makes checklists real on a busy shift.

10. **Show me a station guide you were proud of, anonymised. What made it work for new starters?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a model of length, layout and level of detail.

11. **How do you handle allergen questions from guests, and who signs off your allergen information?**
    Why it matters: the agent must know where its job ends and the trained manager's begins.

12. **Tell me about a food safety inspection or a near miss. What changed afterwards?**
    Why it matters: real stories show which checks matter most and how fixes stick.

13. **How do you build a rota around covers, and which local rules do you check before publishing it?**
    Why it matters: grounds the rota drafts in practice and in the official sources the expert trusts.

14. **How do you reply to reviews? Which ones do you never answer yourself without the owner?**
    Why it matters: sets the reply voice and the limits for level 3 replies.

15. **What do you do about no-shows and late cancellations?**
    Why it matters: booking policies affect covers, prep and guest goodwill.

16. **How do you work with suppliers on prices, substitutions and deliveries? What would you never agree to without the owner?**
    Why it matters: sets the line between preparing orders and committing the owner's money.

17. **Which tools do you rely on (till, stock, rota, bookings, checklists), and what would you never use again?**
    Why it matters: grounds tool advice in real use, not vendor claims.

18. **What does your weekly review look like, and how do you make the fixes stick?**
    Why it matters: captures the rhythm that keeps a site improving.

19. **What do new managers get wrong most often in their first three months?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent its common mistakes from experience.

20. **Tell me about the hardest service or opening you have run. What did you do in the moment, and what did you change after?**
    Why it matters: a hard story shows priorities under pressure, which plain AI does not have.

21. **When an owner wants something you think is wrong (cutting staff at the peak, buying cheaper fish, a review incentive), how do you push back?**
    Why it matters: teaches the agent how to disagree usefully and when to hold the line.

22. **What do you check before you say a menu, checklist or rota is ready?**
    Why it matters: becomes the agent's quality bar.
