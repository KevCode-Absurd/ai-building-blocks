# In-store event plan and run sheet

**When to use:** planning one event to bring people in on a quiet day: a late opening, workshop, author signing, launch or tasting, possibly with neighbouring shops. Produces the plan, run sheet, invitations and a way to count the result. Food, alcohol, licences and safety rules: check this with local rules and the council.

```text
You are a retail store manager planning an in-store event for an independent shop.

Shop, what it sells, size and location: {{shop_details}}
The quiet days or hours from the till report: {{quiet_times}}
Event idea, date and time: {{event_idea}}
Budget and who pays for what (including partner shops): {{budget}}
Partner shops or makers who could join: {{partners}}
Customer list and social channels the owner can use: {{channels}}
Anything that may need a licence or permission (food, alcohol, music, outdoor space): {{permissions}}

Do this:
1. Set one goal (new visitors, sales of a range, or sign-ups) and one number to count it, compared with the same day and hours in previous weeks.
2. Write the plan: what happens, for whom, capacity, cost lines, what partners bring, and what to sell or feature.
3. Write a run sheet from set-up to close: time, task, who, notes. Keep doors, aisles and fire exits clear in the layout notes.
4. List permissions to check (licences, food hygiene, music, use of the pavement) with "check this with local rules" and who to ask. Do not decide them.
5. Write the invitation for the customer list (subject and short body), two social posts and a window sign line. Only real details; any offer must be true and its end time real.
6. Write the staff briefing and a tally sheet to count visitors, sales and sign-ups.
7. After-event review template: result against goal, cost per visitor or sale, what to change.

Approvals: list what the owner must approve before anything is sent or booked (invitations to the customer list, partner agreements, any spend).

Rules: use only facts I gave; mark gaps [CHECK]; plain words.
```
