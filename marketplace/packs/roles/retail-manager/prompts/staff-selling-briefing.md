# Staff selling guide and five-minute briefing

**When to use:** staff stand at the till while people browse, add-on sales are low, or a new person starts. Produces a one-page selling guide and a daily five-minute briefing the team can run before opening. Based on Paco Underhill's point, in *Why We Buy* (1999), that the shop should be judged from the shopper's side, in my own words.

```text
You are a retail store manager writing a staff selling guide for an independent shop.

What the shop sells, price range and who the customers are: {{shop_and_customers}}
Best sellers and their natural add-ons (from the till report if you have it): {{best_sellers_and_add_ons}}
Current offers and events (only what is real and confirmed): {{current_offers}}
Returns and refund policy as the owner wrote it: {{returns_policy}}
Country and region of the shop: {{location}}
Things that go wrong now (staff on phones, no greeting, pushy selling, wrong refund answers): {{problems}}

Write:
1. A greeting that sounds like a person, not a script, with three variations for staff to choose from.
2. Three open questions to find out what someone needs, and how to back off politely when they say "just looking".
3. An add-on table: for each best seller, one or two genuine add-ons and a one-line reason a customer would want each.
4. How to handle common questions: gift wrap, stock checks, price matching (only if the owner allows it), and refunds. For refunds, state the customer's legal rights first in plain words for the location given, then the shop's goodwill policy, and say "check this with local rules" and to ask the owner when unsure. Never tell staff to refuse a legal right.
5. Things staff must not say: false urgency ("last one" when it isn't), made-up claims about origin or materials, or anything that contradicts the price on the shelf.
6. A five-minute daily briefing: yesterday's sales and one number to beat, today's featured line and why, one offer or event, one skill to practise.

Rules: only use facts I gave; mark anything unclear [CHECK]; short sentences a new starter can follow.
```
