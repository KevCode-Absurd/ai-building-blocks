# Pop-up and market plan

A market day is a small shop that opens once. It has fixed costs paid in advance, a few hours to earn them back, and no back room. This skill decides whether the event is worth it, then plans it so the owner turns up ready and goes home knowing what worked.

## When to use

- Before applying for or paying for a pitch, to check the break-even.
- Three weeks to a few days before an event, to plan stock, layout, staff, float and kit.
- The day after, to review sales and profit and plan the next one.

Not for: food hygiene, food labelling, electrical safety or licence decisions (ask the organiser and the local council or environmental health team), booking pitches or paying fees (the client does that), or trade shows for business buyers.

## What you need

- **Event details:** date, hours, set-up and take-down times, pitch size, cover (indoor, gazebo needed), power, expected visitors, the fee and what it includes, organiser rules (insurance, permits, gazebo weights, payment rules).
- **Products:** list with prices, cost prices, stock on hand, and sales from past events or the shop.
- **People:** who can work, their hours, travel time.
- **Payments:** card reader model and app, whether it works offline, phone signal at the venue.
- **Where:** country and council area. In England and Wales, street trading outside an organised market may need a council licence.
- **Access I have:** level 1 to read sales and stock; level 2 to draft the application, a price sheet or a stock transfer to an "event" location; level 3 to move stock to the event location or set event prices in the till within the client's limits. Submitting the application, booking and paying stay with the client.

## Steps

1. **Break-even.** Fixed costs = pitch fee + travel + parking + staff pay + kit hire + insurance share. Contribution per sale = average sale price minus average cost of goods and card fees. Break-even number of sales = fixed costs divided by contribution per sale, rounded up. Compare with past events of a similar size. If break-even needs far more than past sales, say so before the fee is paid.
2. **Stock list.** Start from past sales per hour at similar events, scaled to the hours and expected visitors. Bring best sellers in depth, a few low-price items for impulse buys, gift options in the season, and only a small taster of slow lines. Note the quantity, the space each takes, and a reserve kept in the van or under the table.
3. **Layout.** Draw the pitch in words: table front, sides and back. Best sellers at the front edge and at eye level for someone walking past; prices on every item or group; one clear sign readable from a few metres saying what you sell; a mirror for wearables; a payment spot where the queue doesn't block browsers. Keep walkways and the organiser's fire routes clear, and weight any gazebo as the organiser requires.
4. **Staffing and breaks.** At least two people for busy events, so one can sell while the other restocks or takes a break. Plan breaks into quiet times. Check breaks and rest against local rules (in the UK, a 20-minute break for shifts over 6 hours; in the US, federal law does not require breaks but many states do; in the EU, national rules under the Working Time Directive). Check this with local rules.
5. **Payments.** Card reader charged, a power bank, the app updated, a second device or reader if possible. Know the offline rules: with Square, offline payments should be uploaded within 24 hours and expire after 72; the seller carries any decline. Set a per-payment cap for offline payments. Have a QR code or payment link as a third option if the venue allows.
6. **Float.** Work out the float by note and coin from your price points: prices ending in round numbers need fewer coins. List the starting float and keep a cash log.
7. **Counts.** Start count of every line before leaving (see the stock-count-and-shrink skill for the count sheet). End count at pack-down.
8. **Checklist.** Packing list (stock, display, signs, price tags, bags, reader, float, cash log, weights, ties, tape, first aid kit, water), weather and power backups, timings for travel, set-up and take-down.
9. **Application (if needed).** Answer the organiser's questions from the client's facts only: product description, price range, what makes the stall different, photos to send (a shot list). The client submits.
10. **Review the day.** Sales from the reader export plus cash; units sold = start count minus end count, checked against the export; profit = sales minus fixed costs and cost of goods sold. Sales by hour, best and worst lines, sell-outs and when they happened. End with three changes for next time.

## Checks

- Break-even uses the client's real costs and past sales, with any estimate labelled.
- Stock quantities fit the pitch and the van.
- The layout keeps walkways and fire routes clear and follows organiser rules.
- Breaks are planned and the local rules named, with "check this with local rules" where unsure.
- A card backup exists and offline limits are known.
- The application uses only facts the client gave.
- Nothing is booked, paid or submitted without the client.

## Output format

1. **Go or think again:** break-even sales against past sales, with the working.
2. **Stock list:** line, price, quantity, reserve, space.
3. **Layout:** a text sketch of the pitch and a placement list.
4. **Rota for the day,** with breaks.
5. **Payments and float plan.**
6. **Packing and set-up checklist** with timings.
7. **After the event:** sales, profit, hourly pattern, three changes.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a jewellery maker at a Christmas market. Access I have: Shopify POS level 1. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context. Figures only show the shape.*

**Go or think again:** fixed costs £190 (pitch £120, travel and parking £40, helper £30). Average sale £24; average cost of goods and card fees £9; contribution £15 a sale. Break-even = 13 sales. Past two markets averaged 31 sales. **Go.**

**Stock:** best-selling hoops 40 (10 in reserve); studs 30; necklaces 15; gift boxes 60; slow brooches 6 as a taster.

**Layout (2 m table):** front edge, hoops on a riser at eye level for passers-by; left, studs on cards with one price sign; right, gift-boxed sets and the "Gifts from £12" sign; back corner, mirror and card reader so the queue forms to the side.

**Rota:** you 9:00 to 17:00, break 13:00 to 13:20; helper 10:00 to 16:00, break 12:30 to 12:50.

**Payments:** reader charged, power bank, phone hotspot as backup; offline cap £60 a payment; upload the same evening.

**Float:** £60 in £5 notes and £1 and £2 coins; prices set at whole pounds.
