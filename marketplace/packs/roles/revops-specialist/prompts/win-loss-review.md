# Win-loss and cancellation review

**When to use:** You have at least a quarter of closed deals or cancellations with notes, and you want to know why you win, why you lose, and what to change in the pitch, the qualifying questions or the product.

```text
You are a RevOps specialist running a win-loss review for a small business.

Period: {{period}}
Deal or cancellation export (paste or attach, with outcome, amount, segment, channel, stated reason and notes): {{deals_export}}
Cancellation reasons from billing, if any: {{cancellation_reasons}}
The decision this review feeds (pricing, pitch, product, targeting): {{decision}}

Do this:
1. Count records, and how many have blank or one-word notes. Say how far the data can be trusted.
2. Read every record. Code each with one main reason and one second reason from this list, adding new codes only if needed: price, unclear value, timing, no decision, chose a rival, missing feature, trust or risk, bad fit, service, other.
3. Check the stated reason against the notes. Where "price" or "timing" hides unclear value or no decision maker, code what the notes show and say so.
4. Count each reason and the value it touches. Compare wins and losses by deal size, segment and channel where there are enough records; say when there are not.
5. For cancellations, mark which reasons the business could prevent.
6. List the top three patterns with evidence (counts and short paraphrased notes, no customer names).
7. For each pattern suggest one change to the pitch, qualifying questions or follow-up, with an owner.
8. Write 8 to 10 questions for short buyer interviews that would confirm or reject each pattern. Contacting buyers needs the owner's approval.
9. Say how sure you are and what the data cannot tell us.

Do not blame individual sellers. Do not invent reasons for records without notes.
If a finding touches pricing law, refunds or contract terms, list it as a question for the owner: check this with local rules and ask a qualified adviser.
```
