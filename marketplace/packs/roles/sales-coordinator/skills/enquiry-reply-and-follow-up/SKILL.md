# Enquiry reply and follow-up

## When to use

- A venue, supplier, studio, travel agent, estate agent or order desk gets enquiries faster than it can answer them well.
- Replies go out late, or go out fast but ignore the question asked.
- Buyers hold dates or options and go quiet, and nobody acts before the hold ends.
- The client wants one set of replies by enquiry type that anyone on the team can send.

Do not use it to set prices, write contracts or reply to complaints. Complaints go to the client.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the inbox or enquiry form, the calendar or booking system, the CRM, the rate card and two or three of the client's best past replies.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save replies and follow-ups as drafts, pencil tentative holds and tours, add next-step tasks in the CRM.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send replies and follow-ups from approved templates, book tours and calls, place and release holds by the client's rules, update the CRM. Only within the confirmed limits; log every action.

## What you need

1. The enquiry types the client gets most, with a real example of each.
2. The rate card, packages and what is included, as they stand today.
3. The facts buyers ask about most: capacities, dates free, lead times, travel areas, turnaround.
4. The hold rules: how long, what happens at the end, who may extend.
5. The next step the client wants: a call, a tour, a viewing, a questionnaire, a quote.
6. The client's voice: their best past replies and words they never use.

## Steps

### 1. Sort the enquiries

Group recent enquiries by type and note what each type asks first. A wedding couple asks "is our date free and what does it cost?"; a corporate planner asks about capacity, AV and invoicing; a trade buyer asks about stock, lead time and account terms. Each type gets its own reply.

### 2. Check before you promise

Before any reply names a date, a room, stock or a lead time, check the calendar, booking system or order system. If you cannot check, write "I'll confirm the 14th by 3 pm today" and set a task. Never offer what you have not checked.

### 3. Write the first reply

For each type, in this order:
- Thank them by name and answer the question asked in the first line.
- One or two facts that matter to their event, shoot, trip or order (not a brochure).
- One or two qualifying questions (date, guest count, budget range, location, what matters most).
- One next step with a time: "Would Tuesday at 4 pm or Thursday at 11 am suit for a walk-round?"
- Sign-off in the client's voice.

Prices come from the rate card only. If you show a price to a UK consumer, it is the total with mandatory fees, not a base price plus extras. If you cannot price it from the card, say the quote will follow and flag it to the client.

### 4. Write the out-of-hours and poor-fit replies

The out-of-hours reply says when a person will answer. The poor-fit reply is kind and clear: the reason (date taken, outside the area, below the minimum) and, if the client allows, a referral.

### 5. Set up holds

When a buyer asks to hold, record the hold in the calendar and the CRM with its end date. Write three messages: the hold confirmation (what is held, until when, what secures it), a reminder before the hold ends, and the release message.

### 6. Write the follow-up series

Three to four follow-ups for enquiries that go quiet after a reply or a quote. Each adds something real: a sample timeline, a past event like theirs (described without inventing praise), an answer to a common worry, a genuine deadline such as a hold ending. The last one closes the file politely and leaves the door open. Stop at once when the buyer replies or says no.

### 7. Log and hand over

Log each reply, hold and follow-up in the CRM with the next step and date. When the buyer says yes, start the quote or the handoff.

## Checks

- The first line answers the buyer's question.
- Every date, room, stock level and lead time was checked or marked "to confirm".
- Every price is from the rate card and shown as the total.
- One or two questions, one next step, no more.
- Holds have an end date, a reminder and a release message.
- Follow-ups each add something new and stop on a reply or a no.
- No invented reviews, numbers or urgency.
- At level 3, only approved templates were sent, within the confirmed limits, and every send is logged.

## Output format

1. Reply set: one first reply per enquiry type, plus out-of-hours and poor-fit replies
2. Hold messages: confirmation, reminder, release
3. Follow-up series with the gap between each and the stop rule
4. CRM notes: fields to fill and the next-step rule
5. Open questions for the client, marked [NEEDED]

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a wedding venue for up to 120 guests. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **First reply: wedding enquiry**
>
> Hi Priya and Tom,
>
> Thank you for your message. Saturday 12 June 2027 is free as I write this, and I can hold it for you for 7 days at no cost.
>
> For 90 guests, most couples use the Threshing Barn for the ceremony and dinner and the orchard for drinks, so you would not need to move rooms if it rains. Our 2027 Saturday package is £9,850 in total for up to 90 guests, including VAT, tables, chairs and the evening bar staff.
>
> Two quick questions so I can send the right details: is your ceremony here or elsewhere, and do you have a caterer in mind?
>
> Would you like to see the barn? I have Thursday 19 November at 5 pm or Saturday 21 November at 11 am.
>
> Best wishes,
> Ellie, [Business]
>
> **Hold reminder (day 6):** "Your hold on 12 June ends tomorrow at 5 pm. If you'd like to keep the date, the £1,000 deposit secures it. If you need a few more days, just reply and I'll check with the team."
>
> **Close-the-file (day 21 after the last message):** "I haven't heard back, so I'll assume you've found the right place. If anything changes, I'd be glad to help."
