# Order and booking handoff

## When to use

- A booking or order is confirmed and passes from sales to the people who deliver it.
- The delivery team learns key facts late: dietary needs, access times, shot lists, delivery addresses, special terms.
- Trade buyers chase order status, or stock problems are handled differently each time.
- A buyer changes a confirmed order or booking and nobody re-confirms it.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the signed quote or proposal, the email thread, the CRM deal, the order or booking system, stock and lead times.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** create the handoff sheet, draft acknowledgments, change notes and backorder messages, create tasks for the delivery team.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send acknowledgments, change confirmations and status updates from approved templates; move the deal to its won or handed-over stage; enter orders in the order system only within the limits the client set. Log every action.
- **Stays with the client:** credit decisions, refunds above the set limit, changes to payment or bank details, and promising a date the warehouse or supplier has not confirmed.

## What you need

1. The final signed or accepted quote, proposal or purchase order.
2. Everything promised in writing during the sale: emails, call notes, chat.
3. The delivery team's own checklist or run sheet format, if they have one.
4. For trade orders: stock, lead times, cut-off times, delivery terms and the buyer's PO number.
5. Who in the delivery team receives the handoff, and how (task, email, shared folder).

## Steps

### 1. Pull every promise into one place

Read the whole thread, not only the final quote. List every commitment: inclusions, times, names, special requests, discounts the client approved, anything said in passing ("we can keep the orchard lit until 11"). Each one gets a source (email date, call note).

### 2. Spot the gaps and conflicts

Mark anything missing (final guest count, delivery address, access time, a dietary list) and anything that conflicts (the quote says 80 guests, the latest email says 95). Conflicts go to the client before the handoff, never to the delivery team as a guess.

### 3. Write the handoff sheet

One page, in the delivery team's language: who, what, when, where, how many, special needs, what was promised in writing (with sources), money (deposit paid, balance due date), open questions with owners, and contacts on the day. The aim: the delivery team needs nothing more from sales.

### 4. Confirm to the buyer

Send the buyer a short confirmation that matches the handoff: what is booked or ordered, key dates, what they still need to send and by when, and who their contact is now. For trade orders, this is the order acknowledgment: PO number, lines, quantities, prices, delivery terms and the confirmed or "to confirm" date. Businesses using EDI send the same moments as standard documents (purchase order acknowledgment, change acknowledgment, ship notice).

### 5. Handle changes after confirmation

Every change gets written down, priced from the rate card, acknowledged to the buyer with the new totals and dates, and accepted by the buyer before anyone acts on it. Update the handoff sheet and tell the delivery team what changed.

### 6. Handle stock and date problems

For a trade order with a problem line, send one message with the options: wait for the new date (if confirmed), part-ship what is in stock, take a named substitute, or cancel the line. Never give a date the warehouse or supplier has not confirmed. Log the buyer's choice.

### 7. Close the loop

Move the CRM deal to its handed-over stage, attach the handoff sheet, and set the next step (balance reminder, pre-event call, dispatch check, review request after delivery).

## Checks

- Every promise in the thread appears in the handoff, with its source.
- Conflicts were settled with the client, not guessed.
- The buyer's confirmation matches the handoff sheet line for line.
- Dates are confirmed or marked "to confirm"; no stock or lead time is promised without a check.
- Changes were acknowledged and accepted before work changed.
- Credit, refunds and payment details were left with the client.
- At level 3, only approved templates went out, within limits, all logged.

## Output format

1. Handoff sheet (one page)
2. Buyer confirmation or order acknowledgment
3. Change log, if any
4. Stock or date problem message with options, if any
5. CRM update and next steps
6. Open questions for the client, marked [NEEDED]

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a catering equipment wholesaler confirming a trade order. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Order acknowledgment: PO 44718, [Customer]**
>
> Thank you for your order, received 5 November 2026. Here is what we have confirmed:
>
> | Line | Item | Qty | Status |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | 1/1 gastronorm pan, 65 mm | 40 | In stock, ships 7 Nov |
> | 2 | Combi oven rack, 10-grid | 2 | In stock, ships 7 Nov |
> | 3 | Heated pass shelf, 1.5 m | 1 | **Out of stock** |
>
> **Line 3, your choice:** (a) wait: our supplier has not confirmed a new date yet, and we will tell you as soon as they do; (b) take the 1.2 m shelf, in stock now, at the price on your trade list; or (c) cancel this line. Lines 1 and 2 ship either way. Reply with a, b or c.
>
> Delivery: DAP, your Leith kitchen, as on your account. Your contact is Sam on the order desk.
>
> **Handoff note to warehouse:** ship lines 1 and 2 on 7 Nov; hold line 3 for the buyer's choice; delivery to the rear door before 10 am (buyer's email, 4 Nov).
