# Rep onboarding and certification

## When to use

- The client is hiring two or more reps and wants them selling the client's way within a month.
- New reps learn by shadowing whoever is free, and results vary.
- A playbook exists but nobody practises it.
- A retail team needs a quick refresh before a busy season.

Do not use it to write the playbook itself (use `sales-playbook-builder`) or to judge a rep's performance for pay or review.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the playbook, product sheets, CRM stages, a few anonymised call notes or approved recordings, existing training material.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** build modules, quizzes, role-plays and the certification checklist as unpublished drafts in the training tool or drive.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish approved modules, assign them to the named reps with due dates, book practice and certification sessions in the agreed calendars, and log progress, within the confirmed limits.

## What you need

1. The playbook or, if none, the sales stages and the best seller's habits.
2. Product facts and proof points the client supplies.
3. How many reps, their experience, and who coaches and certifies them.
4. How leads are handed out and when a rep may work alone.
5. Tools the reps must learn (CRM, dialer, quoting).

## Steps

### 1. Set the certification goals

Four to six goals a manager can hear on a call, written as behaviours: "asks at least three questions about the problem before mentioning price", "states the next step and a date before ending the call", "logs the call with the buyer's words within the hour". Avoid goals that only test memory.

### 2. Plan the month

- **Week 1: product, process and tools.** Short sessions, each ending with a quiz on the key facts.
- **Week 2: role-plays.** One stage per day, with feedback.
- **Week 3: shadowing and supervised calls.** Reps listen to approved recordings and live calls, then lead parts of calls with a coach.
- **Week 4: certification.** A scored call or role-play per goal, with a retry plan.

Spread practice over the month and bring key facts back in short quizzes; the US Department of Education's practice guide rates quizzing to re-expose key content as strong evidence and spacing as moderate. One long induction day followed by nothing is the pattern to avoid.

### 3. Write the role-plays

For each stage: the buyer's profile, their opening lines, two tempting mistakes, what good sounds like, and a 1-to-4 scoring sheet tied to the certification goals. Include at least one difficult buyer per stage (silent, hostile, price-first, "send me something").

### 4. Write the cheat sheets

One page per product: who it is for, the problem it solves, three proof points from the client's files with sources, the top three buyer questions and honest answers.

### 5. Write the quizzes

Five to eight questions per session, mixing recall ("what are the three exit criteria for Stage 2?") with judgement ("the buyer asks for a discount on the first call: what do you do?"). Repeat the key ones in later weeks.

### 6. Write the certification checklist

Each goal, what the manager listens for, the pass line (for example, every goal scored 3 or more), and a retry plan: one targeted drill, a second role-play within a week, then a re-certification call.

### 7. Plan the evaluation

Using the four Kirkpatrick levels: a short reaction survey, quiz and role-play scores, behaviour on real calls in the month after, and the business result the client cares about (first won deal, meetings held, average order).

### 8. Retail floor version

Turn the same goals into daily 10-minute huddles: one skill, one short role-play in pairs, one thing to try on the floor that day.

## Checks

- Every goal is a behaviour a manager can hear, practised before it is certified.
- Every claim on a cheat sheet comes from the client's proof.
- No tactic that misleads or pressures buyers.
- Role-plays use made-up buyers, never real buyer names or details.
- The plan spaces practice and repeats key facts.
- Certification results are for development; they are not used for pay or review decisions by this agent.
- At level 3, only the named reps were assigned, within the agreed dates, and every assignment is logged.

## Output format

1. Certification goals
2. 30-day plan, week by week, with sessions, practice and checks
3. Role-play scenarios with scoring sheets
4. Product cheat sheets
5. Quizzes
6. Certification checklist with pass line and retry plan
7. Evaluation plan (four levels)
8. Retail huddles, if needed
9. Gaps and decisions for the client

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a home solar installer hiring three new sales advisers. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Certification goal 2:** Before talking about price, the adviser asks about the last 12 months of energy bills, roof direction and how long the family plans to stay in the home.
>
> **Role-play: Stage 2, the price-first buyer**
> - **Buyer opening:** "Just tell me the price. I've had three quotes already."
> - **Tempting mistake:** giving a number before knowing the roof or the bills.
> - **Good sounds like:** "Happy to. So it's a real number and not a guess, can I ask three quick questions first?"
> - **Score 4:** asks all three questions, gives a range tied to the answers, books a survey. **Score 1:** gives a price with no questions.
>
> **Week 1 quiz, question 4:** "A buyer asks if panels work on a north-facing roof. What do you say, and which sheet has the answer?"
>
> **Retry plan:** one drill on the opening question set, a second role-play within five working days, then re-certification.
