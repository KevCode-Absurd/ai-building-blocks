# Training interview: sales enablement specialist

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share names, contact details or recordings of real buyers or reps; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of sales teams have you enabled, what did they sell, and how big were they?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's market and the size of team its formats should suit.

2. **When a new client calls you, what do you look at in the first week, and in what order?**
   Why it matters: shows the expert's real diagnosis routine, which the agent should copy.

3. **How do you find out what the best seller does differently? Tell me about one habit you found that nobody had written down.**
   Why it matters: the playbook depends on pulling tacit habits out of top sellers.

4. **Show me a playbook or play card you built that people actually used. Why did it work?**
   Why it matters: real examples set the agent's length, format and tone.

5. **How do you define a sales stage, and how do you know when a deal should move on?**
   Why it matters: exit criteria are the backbone of the playbook and the CRM set-up.

6. **Which qualification method do you use, and how do you keep it from becoming a form reps fill in after the fact?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a working qualification guide, not a checklist for show.

7. **Take me through how you build a sales deck. What do you cut first from a features-heavy deck?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's story structure and editing rules.

8. **How do you check proof before a claim goes in a deck, sheet or battlecard?**
   Why it matters: the agent must hold the same evidence bar and know what counts as proof.

9. **How do you build a battlecard, and where do you draw the line on what reps may say about a rival?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's honesty rules and sources for competitive work.

10. **What does your onboarding plan for a new rep look like, week by week?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a tested plan instead of a generic one.

11. **How do you run role-plays so they feel real? Give me your hardest buyer character.**
    Why it matters: role-play quality decides whether certification means anything.

12. **What must a rep show on a certification call to pass? What happens if they don't?**
    Why it matters: sets the pass line and retry plan the agent uses.

13. **How do you get consent to record calls, and how are reps told their calls are reviewed, in the places you work?**
    Why it matters: the agent must follow the expert's consent routine and know when to stop.

14. **When you coach from a recording, what do you listen for first? Show me notes you gave on a real call.**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's listening order and coaching voice.

15. **How do you give feedback that a rep acts on, rather than defends against?**
    Why it matters: the tone and structure of notes decide whether coaching changes anything.

16. **How do you make sure two managers score the same call the same way?**
    Why it matters: calibration keeps team coaching fair and consistent.

17. **How do you prepare a seller for a hard price negotiation? What do they write down before the call?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's negotiation prep and walk-away habits.

18. **Tell me about a deal you helped rescue. What did you see that the rep had missed?**
    Why it matters: shows the expert's deal judgement, which no template captures.

19. **How do you find out why buyers really chose you, or chose to do nothing?**
    Why it matters: win-loss method decides whether plays reflect buyers or seller opinions.

20. **How do you measure whether enablement worked?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's own scorecard for reports.

21. **What have you seen go wrong with AI call tools or recordings, and what would you never let one do?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's limits on recordings, scoring and people decisions.

22. **Which access would you give an assistant in your CRM, call recorder and content tools, and what would you keep for yourself?**
    Why it matters: sets the default access levels and the actions that need a fresh yes.
