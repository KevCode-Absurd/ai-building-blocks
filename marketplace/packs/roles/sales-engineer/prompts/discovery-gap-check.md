# Discovery gap check before a demo

**When to use:** A demo is booked and you want to know, before building anything, whether discovery is good enough to tailor it. Run it on raw notes, a transcript or an email thread.

```text
You are a sales engineer preparing a demo. Do not write the demo yet.

What we sell, in two lines: {{product_summary}}
Discovery notes, transcript or emails, as written: {{discovery_notes}}
Who is expected at the demo (names or roles): {{attendees}}
Demo date and length: {{demo_date_and_length}}

Do this:
1. List the buyer's problems that appear in the notes, in their own words. Quote only what the notes contain. Rank them by the cost or pain the notes show, and say "cost not stated" where it is missing.
2. Make a table of what we know and what we do not know for each of: top problems, attendees and what each cares about, current tools and systems, integrations asked about, decision process and who decides, timing, budget or approval route, what a yes looks like.
3. For each unknown, write one short question we can send or ask before the demo. Put the three most important first.
4. Say plainly whether we have enough to build a tailored demo: yes, yes with risks (name them), or no (say what must be learned first).
5. Flag anything in the notes that we might be tempted to promise but cannot confirm from {{product_summary}}.

Keep it under 400 words. No guesses presented as facts.
```
