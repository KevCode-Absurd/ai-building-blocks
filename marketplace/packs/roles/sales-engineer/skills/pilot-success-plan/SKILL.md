# Pilot success plan

A pilot without agreed success criteria does not end in a decision. It drifts, the champion moves on and the deal fades. This skill writes the plan both sides sign before day one, and the readout that closes it.

## When to use

- A buyer asks for a trial, pilot or proof of concept.
- A pilot is running with no end date or no agreed measures.
- The pilot has ended and the decision memo is needed.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read the deal, discovery notes and product docs. Level 2 to save the plan, the success criteria table and the readout as drafts, and to draft CRM tasks. Level 3 to log the plan in the CRM, set the pilot stage's required fields, update progress and send approved pilot updates to the named buyer contacts within the client's limits. Sending the pilot agreement, any discount or any binding quote needs a fresh yes from the client every time.

- The buyer's problems and what a yes looks like (from discovery).
- Who sponsors the pilot on each side, and who decides at the end.
- Users, sites, systems and data the pilot would touch.
- What the product can do today in that setup, and what needs configuration.
- Any baseline numbers the buyer has, with their source.
- The client's rules on pilot length, cost and support.

## Steps

1. **Write the goal in one sentence.** The business result the buyer wants, in their words: "Cut first-time-fix failures for the North region engineers."
2. **Set the scope.** In: users, sites, use cases, integrations, data. Out: everything else, listed by name. A short list of what the buyer must supply (people's time, data, system access) and by when.
3. **Choose a short list of success criteria.** Each must be measurable within the pilot. For each, write the baseline (from the buyer's own records, or "to be measured in week one"), the target the buyer agrees to, how it is measured, who measures it and where the evidence will sit. Prefer the buyer's own measures over the vendor's.
4. **Test the criteria.** Could each be met by luck or missed for reasons outside the product? If so, rewrite it. Is any target set by the vendor without the buyer's agreement? If so, it is not agreed.
5. **Plan the timeline.** Set-up, training, a run period long enough to show the measure, a mid-point check, the readout meeting and the decision date. Name both sides' owners for each step.
6. **Agree the decision for each result.** All criteria met: the buyer proceeds to purchase on the terms discussed. Some met: a short extension with a named fix, or a narrower purchase. None met: stop, and both sides record why. Write this before day one.
7. **Flag data and paper.** If the pilot needs real personal data, list it and flag it for the client's lawyer and the buyer's data protection lead: in the EU, a processor must work under a contract with the terms GDPR Article 28 lists, including instructions, security, sub-processors, deletion at the end and audits. Prefer sample or test data. Note any pilot agreement or NDA needed; sending it needs a fresh yes.
8. **Set the risks.** Low user uptake, missing data, IT approval delays, a champion leaving. For each, an early sign and what you will do.
9. **Get sign-off.** Both sponsors approve the plan in writing before set-up starts. [L3] Record the signed criteria in the CRM pilot stage.
10. **Run the mid-point check.** Progress against each criterion, issues, changes requested. Any scope change goes in a change log with both sponsors' approval and a note on whether the end date moves.
11. **Write the readout.** Results against each criterion with the evidence, what was learned, the decision the plan named, and next steps. Draft it so the buyer's sponsor can forward it to whoever approves the spend.

## Checks

- Every criterion has a baseline, target, method, owner and sign-off.
- The buyer agreed the targets; none are vendor wishes.
- The end date and the decision for each result are written before day one.
- Scope "out" is as specific as scope "in".
- Personal data, contracts and NDAs are flagged for the right people, not decided here.
- Nothing promises a business outcome the product does not control: commit to actions and measures, not to the buyer's results.
- The readout reports misses as plainly as hits.

## Output format

1. Goal (one sentence).
2. Scope: in, out, what the buyer supplies.
3. People: sponsors, decision maker, day-to-day owners on both sides.
4. Success criteria table: criterion, baseline, target, how measured, who measures, evidence location, signed off by.
5. Timeline with owners.
6. Decision for each result.
7. Data and paperwork flags.
8. Risks with early signs.
9. Change log (empty at start).
10. Readout template.

## Example of good output

*Made-up business for illustration: Fieldquill, a field service app, piloting with a made-up 40-engineer heating and cooling contractor for six weeks.*

**Goal:** Fewer return visits for the 12 North region engineers.

**Scope.** In: 12 North engineers, job scheduling, parts lookup, photo notes. Out: invoicing, the South and East regions, the accounting system link, customer portal. Buyer supplies: 12 engineers' time for a 2-hour training (week 1), last quarter's job export (by day 3).

**Success criteria**

| Criterion | Baseline | Target | How measured | Who measures | Signed off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jobs needing a return visit | From last quarter's export, week 1 | Agreed in week 1 by the ops director | Job records, weeks 3 to 6 | Buyer's ops coordinator | Both sponsors |
| Engineers logging jobs in the app | 0 | 10 of 12 active in week 4 | App usage report | Fieldquill | Both sponsors |
| Time to find a part number | Timed sample of 20 lookups, week 1 | Agreed in week 1 | Same timed sample, week 5 | Buyer's North supervisor | Both sponsors |

**Decision for each result.** All three met: the buyer moves to a North region purchase on the quoted terms. Two met: two-week extension with a named fix. One or none: stop and record why.

**Data flag:** the job export includes customer names and addresses. Flagged for Fieldquill's lawyer and the buyer's data lead before it is shared; a version with customer details removed is offered instead.
