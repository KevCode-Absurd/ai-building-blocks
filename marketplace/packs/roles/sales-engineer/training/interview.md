# Training interview: sales engineer

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share customer names, contract terms, security reports or credentials; anonymise every story.

1. **What do you sell, to which kinds of buyers, and who is usually in the room on the technical side?**
   Why it matters: sets the products, buyers and objections the agent must know.

2. **Walk me through how you prepare a demo, from the moment it is booked to the moment you start sharing your screen.**
   Why it matters: shows the expert's real order of work and where they spend time.

3. **What do you need to know from discovery before you will build a demo? What do you do when you do not have it?**
   Why it matters: the agent must know when to stop and ask instead of guessing.

4. **Show me a demo script or outline you are proud of. Why is it in that order?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a model of good structure in this market.

5. **What is on your "do not show" list, and why?**
   Why it matters: knowing what to leave out is a large part of the craft.

6. **Tell me about a demo that went badly. What happened and what would you change?**
   Why it matters: failure stories teach the warning signs the agent should look for.

7. **How do you decide whether a buyer needs a pilot, or whether a reference call or technical session would do?**
   Why it matters: pilots are costly; the agent needs the expert's threshold.

8. **How do you write pilot success criteria? Give me an example of good ones and bad ones.**
   Why it matters: measurable, agreed criteria decide whether pilots end in a decision.

9. **What do you do when a buyer wants to add scope in the middle of a pilot?**
   Why it matters: pilot creep is a common way deals stall.

10. **Which technical objections do you hear most, and how do you answer each honestly?**
    Why it matters: builds the agent's objection sheet from real experience.

11. **When the true answer is "no, we can't do that", how do you say it, and what happens next?**
    Why it matters: honest "no" answers protect trust; the agent needs the expert's phrasing.

12. **Which security questionnaires do you see (CAIQ, SIG, sector forms, buyers' own)? How long do they take, and where do they get stuck?**
    Why it matters: shows which frameworks and bottlenecks matter in this market.

13. **How do you decide whether an answer is confirmed, partial or a gap? Who reviews before anything goes out?**
    Why it matters: the agent must copy the expert's evidence standard and sign-off chain.

14. **What do you share with a buyer's security team, on what terms, and what never leaves the building?**
    Why it matters: sets the rules for reports, NDAs and restricted documents.

15. **How do you handle questions about AI features: models used, training on customer data, where data is processed?**
    Why it matters: these questions are new and easy to answer wrongly.

16. **Tell me about a time a sales colleague promised something technical that was not true. How did you handle it?**
    Why it matters: the agent needs a way to push back on overclaims.

17. **What goes in your hand-off to the delivery team, and what goes wrong when it is missing?**
    Why it matters: the hand-off decides whether the buyer gets what they were shown.

18. **Which CRM fields, stages or notes do you keep up to date, and which do you think are a waste of time?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent what to log at level 3 and what to skip.

19. **How do you work with the account executive on a deal? Where do your jobs overlap or clash?**
    Why it matters: the agent must fit into an existing team without stepping on the closer.

20. **What would you check in an AI-written demo script or questionnaire answer before trusting it?**
    Why it matters: turns the expert's quality bar into the agent's self-check.
