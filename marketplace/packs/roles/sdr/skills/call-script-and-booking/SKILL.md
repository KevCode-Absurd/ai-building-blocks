# Call script and booking

## When to use

- A team books meetings by phone for a closer (site visits, demos, consultations).
- New enquiries need a call-back script that books the next step.
- Reps sound scripted, or stall at the gatekeeper.
- Meetings get booked but do not happen.

Do not use it to make calls, find phone numbers, or call people on do-not-call lists.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** call logs, outcomes and any transcripts recorded with consent; the calendar of the person who takes meetings.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** build call lists in the dialer, paused; draft confirmation and reminder messages and calendar event types.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** start screened call lists for the human callers, send booking links, book meetings into the named calendar, send confirmations and reminders, and log outcomes and follow-up emails, within the confirmed limits (which lists, which calendar, which templates, how many bookings a day). Log every action. No AI-voiced calls at any level.

## What you need

1. Who we call: role, company type, size, country and any US states.
2. What we sell, and what the meeting is for (who attends, how long, what the buyer gets from it).
3. Who books and who closes; what a good meeting looks like to the closer.
4. A believable reason to call this buyer now: a trigger, a seasonal problem, a change in their sector, or the enquiry they sent.
5. The five brush-offs the team hears most, in the buyer's words.
6. The calling rules the client follows: do-not-call screening, recording and consent, calling hours.

## Steps

### 1. Write the opener

Name, company and the reason for the call in the first ten seconds, then ask for a short moment. Vendor data from recorded calls found that stating the reason early helps and that opening with "Did I catch you at a bad time?" hurts. Treat that as a starting point to test.

> "Hi Maria, it's Tom from Northgate Cleaning. I'm calling because you've just taken the second floor at Quay House, and offices that expand usually find their cleaning contract no longer fits. Can I take 30 seconds to say why I called, and you tell me if it's worth a proper chat?"

### 2. Two fit questions

Short, open, about their situation and one problem. Skip anything the research already answers.

### 3. The meeting ask

A clear time, length and purpose, two options, and who will be there. "Would Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2 work for a 20-minute walk-round with our operations manager? You'd get a written quote the same week."

### 4. Brush-off replies

One short, honest reply to each of the five common brush-offs, then either ask once more or let go. No arguing, no pressure tricks, no guilt.

| They say | Reply | Then |
|---|---|---|
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to. So I send the right thing: is it price, how we work, or references?" | Send it; ask for a time to talk it through |
| "We already have someone." | "Most of our clients did. Is it working well, or is there one thing you'd change?" | If all is fine, thank them and log for later |
| "Not interested." | "Understood. Is that the topic, or just today's timing?" | Let go if it is the topic |

### 5. Gatekeeper lines

Honest and brief: who you are, why you are calling, who you need. Ask for help, not a way past them. "Could you tell me who looks after the cleaning contract? I'd rather not waste the wrong person's time."

### 6. Voicemails and matching emails

Three voicemails under 25 seconds: name, reason, one useful detail, number said slowly twice. Each has a short follow-up email sent the same day with the same reason.

### 7. Call-back script for inbound enquiries

Thank them for the enquiry, confirm what they asked about, ask the qualifying questions agreed for inbound, book the next step, and confirm by email while still on the call if possible.

### 8. Booking, confirmation and reminders

- Confirmation within minutes: date, time, place or link, who attends, agenda, how to reschedule.
- A reminder the day before with one useful line (what to have ready).
- A reschedule message that offers two new times.
- A no-show message the same day that offers to rebook, once.

### 9. The calling block plan

- Screen the list first: the client's own do-not-call list; the US National Do Not Call Registry for consumer numbers, refreshed at least every 31 days; TPS and CTPS for UK numbers. Record when screening ran.
- Calling hours that respect the buyer's local time and any legal limits.
- Order: A tier first, then call-backs due, then B tier.
- Attempts: how many over how many working days, mixing calls, voicemails and emails, then stop.
- Logging after every call: outcome, next step, opt-out if asked, notes in the buyer's words.
- Anyone who asks not to be called again goes on the do-not-call list at once, across every tool.

### 10. Flag the rules to check

Do-not-call screening, call recording consent, calling hours, and any automated or prerecorded messages (which need consent). Point to the official pages in `knowledge.md`. Do not rule on whether the plan is lawful.

## Checks

- The opener says who, where from and why within ten seconds.
- Every claim in the script is true and in the brief.
- Each brush-off reply is short and lets the buyer say no.
- Voicemails are under 25 seconds when read aloud.
- Confirmation, reminder, reschedule and no-show messages exist.
- Screening, recording and opt-out steps are in the block plan.
- At level 3, only screened lists ran, bookings went to the named calendar, and every action is logged.

## Output format

1. One-page script: opener, fit questions, meeting ask, brush-off table
2. Gatekeeper lines
3. Three voicemails with matching emails
4. Inbound call-back script (if needed)
5. Booking messages: confirmation, reminder, reschedule, no-show
6. Calling block plan with screening and logging rules
7. Rules for the client to check

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Northgate Cleaning, an office cleaning firm in Manchester booking site visits for its sales manager.*

> **Voicemail 1 (about 20 seconds):** "Hi Maria, Tom from Northgate Cleaning. I'm calling about your move into the second floor at Quay House. We clean six offices in that building, so we know the lifts and access times. I'll send a short email now. My number is 0161 496 0000, that's 0161 496 0000. Thanks."
>
> **Confirmation (sent during the call):** "Thanks Maria. Confirmed: walk-round at Quay House, Tuesday 18 November, 10:00 to 10:20, with Jo Patel, our operations manager. You'll get a written quote by Friday. To move it, reply with a time that suits."
>
> **Block plan, Tuesday 9:30 to 11:30:** list screened against CTPS and our own list on Monday; A tier first; three attempts over eight working days; log outcome within one minute of each call.
