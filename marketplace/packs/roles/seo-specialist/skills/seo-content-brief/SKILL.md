# SEO content brief

A brief is the contract between the SEO and the writer. A good one makes the page useful to the reader first and easy for search engines to match to the right searches. A weak one lists keywords and a word count. This skill writes the good kind on one page.

## When to use

- A new page for a query group from the keyword map.
- A refresh of a page stuck in positions 5 to 20, or one losing clicks to newer pages.
- Briefing a freelance writer, an agency or an AI drafting tool.

Not for: landing pages built mainly to convert paid traffic (hand those to a conversion copywriter), or news posts that must ship within the hour.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 for every step, because the brief is a document. Level 2 to save it, or a draft page built from it, in your shared folder or CMS.

1. **The target query group** and its primary query, from the keyword-intent-map skill or the client.
2. **The business goal** of the page: the action you want and the product or service it supports.
3. **Customer language:** sales call notes, support tickets, reviews or survey answers that mention the topic (10 or more lines if you can get them).
4. **The client's expertise:** who will write or review, their credentials, first-hand experience, data or cases you are allowed to use.
5. **Existing page** (for refreshes): URL plus its Search Console queries for the last 3 months.
6. **Sitemap or page list** for internal links.

## Steps

### 1. Confirm intent from the results page
Search the primary query (signed out, the client's main country). Record: the type of pages ranking (guides, product pages, category pages, tools, forums, videos), the formats (lists, comparisons, calculators), whether an AI Overview, map pack, shopping units or "People also ask" appear, and how fresh the top results are. If the results are mostly product pages and the client wants a blog post, say so: the format must match the intent.

### 2. Define the reader and the job
One sentence: who searches this, what they already know, and what they need to do next. For example: a homeowner who has had one quote for a heat pump and wants to know whether the price is fair before signing.

### 3. Find the information gain
List what the top five results all cover (the table stakes), then what none of them offers. The angle must add something real: first-hand testing, the client's own data or prices, a worked example, a decision tool, original photos, an expert who has done the job. If the client has nothing new to add, say so and recommend a smaller page or no page. Google's people-first guidance asks whether the content gives original information or analysis and goes beyond the obvious.

### 4. Build the outline
- H1 that matches the main intent in plain words.
- An opening that answers the main question in the first two or three sentences, then says who it is for.
- H2s in the order the reader needs them, phrased as the reader would ask. Each H2 gets one line on what it must say and the evidence for it.
- A comparison table or step list where the results page shows people want one.
- The call to action, placed where the reader is ready for it.

### 5. Questions and subtopics
List 5 to 10 questions from "People also ask", the client's support inbox and the query report. Mark each as "answer in a section" or "answer in one line". Skip questions that belong to a different intent.

### 6. Evidence and experience
For every claim that needs proof, name the source to cite (primary sources first: official guidance, standards, original studies). Name the author and reviewer, their relevant experience and the bio page to link. For health, money, legal or safety topics, require a qualified reviewer.

### 7. On-page elements
- Title tag draft (aim for about 50 to 60 characters so it is not cut off; put the main words first).
- Meta description draft (a clear promise and the next step; about 150 characters).
- URL slug (short, readable, no dates unless the page is dated news).
- Image needs with alt text guidance.
- Schema type: Article or BlogPosting with author; Product, Service or LocalBusiness where relevant; BreadcrumbList. No FAQ markup for rich results unless the site is a well-known government or health site.

### 8. Internal links
Three to six links out to related pages with descriptive anchors, and two to four strong existing pages that should link in, with the anchor text to use.

### 9. Success measure
Name what will show it worked and when: impressions and clicks for the query group in Search Console after 6 to 12 weeks, the conversion event in GA4, and (if an AI Overview appears) impressions in the Generative AI report.

## Checks before you ship

- The format matches what Google ranks for this intent.
- The angle adds something the top results lack, and the client can actually supply it.
- No target word count. Length follows what the reader needs.
- Every factual claim has a named source or the expert who will vouch for it.
- The brief does not ask for keyword repetition, hidden text or fake experience.
- One primary intent per page; no overlap with another page in the keyword map.

## Output format

A one-page brief with these headings: Target and intent, Reader and job, Angle and information gain, Outline, Questions, Evidence and experts, On-page (title, meta, slug, schema, images), Internal links, Success measure, Notes for the writer.

## Example of good output (made for this kit, not a real client)

> **Target and intent:** "heat pump installation cost" plus 14 close variants. Commercial investigation. Results: cost guides, two installer pages, a government grant page; an AI Overview shows a price range.
>
> **Reader and job:** A homeowner with one quote who wants to know if it is fair and what drives the price.
>
> **Angle:** The top five give national ranges only. We add 37 anonymised quotes the client issued in 2026, broken down by house size and system type, and a checklist of what a fair quote must include.
>
> **Outline:** H1: What a heat pump installation costs in 2026 (with real quotes). Opening: the typical range from our 37 quotes in two sentences. H2: What our last 37 installs cost, by house size (table). H2: The five things that move the price. H2: Grants you can claim (link the official page; check dates before publishing). H2: How to check your quote (checklist). H2: When a cheap quote is a warning sign. CTA: book a free survey.
>
> **Evidence:** the client's quote data (owner approves the table); the official grant page; the installer's certification shown in the author bio.
>
> **Success:** impressions for the 15 queries up from 1,200 to 4,000 a month within 12 weeks; 20 survey requests a month from the page.
