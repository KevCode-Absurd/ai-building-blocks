# Test briefs: hard SEO jobs

Each brief is a realistic, difficult request. The coach runs it against your agent and against a plain AI answer, then scores both against "A great answer must".

## 1. The thin brief

> Our Google traffic is down. Can you fix it? Site: a 60-page plumbing business site.

A great answer must:
- Ask for the minimum data before diagnosing: Search Console exports with dates, GA4 organic sessions, and the change log.
- Explain that the first step is confirming the drop is real (tracking, seasonality, reporting delay).
- Lay out the causes it will test, in order, and what evidence would point to each.
- Not recommend any site changes before seeing data.
- Offer a useful first step the owner can do today (for example, screenshots of Search Console Performance and Page Indexing).

## 2. The client who wants the wrong thing

> We want 500 city pages, one for every town in the country, written by AI, live next month. Our competitor did it.

A great answer must:
- Explain scaled content abuse in plain words and why near-identical pages risk the whole site.
- Ask where the business actually serves customers and what it can say that is unique about each area.
- Propose a smaller, stronger plan: real service-area pages with local detail, reviews, photos and prices.
- Not promise the competitor's tactic will keep working, and not insult the client.
- Give a way to test demand first (Search Console impressions, a handful of pilot pages).

## 3. Conflicting goals

> Marketing wants more blog traffic. Sales says blog visitors never buy. The CEO wants both teams happy by next quarter.

A great answer must:
- Reframe the goal around non-branded visits that lead to sales or qualified leads.
- Use GA4 and Search Console to show which content types already assist conversions.
- Propose a keyword map that links informational content to money pages, and cuts or merges posts that serve neither.
- Set measures both teams accept, with a timeline that is honest about SEO lag.

## 4. Tricky data

> Search Console says clicks are up 12%, GA4 says organic sessions are down 20%, and our rank tracker says we dropped on 30 keywords. Which is right?

A great answer must:
- Explain why the sources differ: Search Console counts clicks, GA4 counts sessions with JavaScript and consent, rank trackers sample positions.
- Check for a GA4 tracking or consent change and for channel grouping changes.
- Note that a rank tracker's 30 keywords may not be the queries that bring traffic.
- Give a step-by-step way to reconcile the numbers and say which source answers which question.

## 5. The rushed migration

> We are moving from WordPress to Shopify next Friday. The developer says redirects are handled. Anything we should check?

A great answer must:
- Say clearly that one week is tight, and list the checks that cannot be skipped.
- Ask for the full old URL list and the redirect file, and explain how to test it.
- Cover Shopify-specific URL changes (fixed paths for products, collections and pages) and the need to map every old URL.
- Include launch-day checks (robots.txt, noindex, canonicals, sitemaps, analytics) and post-launch monitoring.
- Suggest moving the date if the redirect map is not ready.

## 6. The core update panic

> We lost 45% of our traffic in the last core update. A consultant says we need to disavow 3,000 links and delete half our blog. Should we?

A great answer must:
- Not agree to mass disavows or deletions without evidence.
- Explain what core updates assess and how to compare losing pages with the pages that replaced them.
- Check for a manual action before talking about links.
- Propose a page-by-page review with refresh, merge or remove calls, starting with the pages that lost most.
- Set honest expectations: recovery is not guaranteed and often waits for a later core update.

## 7. The link offer

> A site owner has offered us 20 guest posts on high-authority sites for $2,000, with keyword links. Good deal?

A great answer must:
- Identify this as buying links that pass ranking credit, which breaks Google's spam policies.
- Explain the risk (manual action, wasted money) without moralising.
- Offer real alternatives: original data for digital PR, expert comment, partnerships, sponsored placements marked rel="sponsored" for referral value only.

## 8. The AI Overview question

> An AI Overview now answers our main how-to query and our clicks have halved, even though we still rank first. What do we do?

A great answer must:
- Confirm with Search Console: impressions and position steady, CTR down, and check the Generative AI performance report.
- Explain that this is a results-page change, not a penalty.
- Suggest realistic actions: improve the title and snippet, add formats that earn clicks (tools, video, comparison data), make sure the page is cited in the AI Overview, and shift targets toward queries closer to a purchase.
- Change the reporting so the client sees visibility and conversions, not only clicks.
