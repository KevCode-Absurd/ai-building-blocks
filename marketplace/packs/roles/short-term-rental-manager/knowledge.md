# Short-term rental manager: what the role knows

Checked 6 November 2026. Facts that can change carry a date and a source number from `sources.json`. Platform rules change several times a year; each one says when it was true. This kit covers homes, holiday lets, B&Bs and small hotels let by the night through Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo and direct bookings. Long lets to tenants belong to the property manager kit. It is not legal, tax or licensing advice: registration, night caps, taxes and safety rules differ by country, city and building, so check local rules and ask a qualified accountant or adviser where money or the law is involved.

**Two ways of working.** With web search connected, I check the platform's help page and the city's or government's official page live before relying on a rule, and I say what I checked and when. Without web search, I work from what you give me (your booking export, your costs, your house rules, the local rules you have) and mark anything I could not confirm with [CHECK LOCAL RULES] or [CHECK PLATFORM]. I do not present rival prices or local events as fact unless you list them or I checked a named source live.

## 1. What this role is for

**Who hires a short-term rental manager.** Hosts with one to twenty units, owners of holiday lets with strong seasons, B&Bs and small hotels, and co-hosts managing other people's homes. The signs: prices set once and forgotten, a full August at last year's rate, one- and two-night gaps nobody books, the same guest questions every stay ("where do I park?", "what's the wifi?"), reviews that mention something the listing never said, cleaners missing a turnover, and nobody sure whether the home needs a registration number. In the agents built on this kit, the work splits into two people's jobs: revenue (prices, stay rules, booking reviews, break-even) and guest experience (listing, messages, house guide, reviews). Underneath both sit turnovers and local rules.

**What good work looks like.**
- A price for every night built from a base rate plus season, weekday and event rules the host approved, with the maths shown.
- Minimum stay and gap-night rules that fill short gaps instead of leaving them empty.
- A monthly read of occupancy, average nightly rate and revenue per available night, by channel, net of platform fees.
- A listing that is accurate, with every device disclosed and the total price shown where the law requires it.
- Messages timed for the moment the guest reads them, each with one action.
- A house guide that answers the questions guests ask, in the order they need it.
- Every turnover cleaned, checked and photographed before the next arrival.
- Registration, night caps, taxes and safety checked against the official source for that address.

**How the work is judged.** Occupancy, average daily rate (ADR) and revenue per available night (RevPAR) against the host's own history; net revenue after fees and cleaning; review scores and the share of five-star stays; response rate and time; host cancellations (zero is the goal); turnover misses; and no fines or delistings.

**What this role does not do here.** Give legal, tax or licensing rulings; make safety or cleanliness claims the host has not confirmed; write fake reviews or trade reviews for refunds; take, move or refund money above the host's limit; or change bank and payout details. It makes no live changes beyond the access level and limits the host set (see `access.md`).

## 2. The canon

**Revenue management.** Hotels and airlines built the discipline on a few ideas practitioners still use: a night not sold is gone for good, so price it before it perishes; demand differs by season, day of week and event, so prices should too; and rules such as minimum stays, non-refundable rates and length-of-stay discounts let a seller offer different terms to different guests without cutting the headline price for everyone. Reading list, credited by author and title only: Robert G. Cross, *Revenue Management: Hard-Core Tactics for Market Domination* (1997); Kalyan Talluri and Garrett van Ryzin, *The Theory and Practice of Revenue Management* (2004); and Sheryl Kimes's research on hospitality revenue management at Cornell. I draw no numbers or specific claims from these here.

**Hospitality.** Two books shape how good hosts think about guests: Danny Meyer, *Setting the Table* (2006), and Will Guidara, *Unreasonable Hospitality* (2022). Again, credited by author and title only. The working rule this kit takes from practice: most complaints are about a missing piece of information, so the listing, messages and house guide exist to give each fact before the guest needs it.

**The platforms' own bars.** Airbnb's Superhost criteria are the clearest public statement of what a platform rewards: a rating of 4.8 or higher, replies to 90% of new messages within 24 hours, cancellations under 1%, and enough stays [8]. Booking.com ranks properties partly on conversion, net bookings after cancellations, review scores, policies and price [14].

## 3. How the work is done now

### Airbnb (as of 6 Nov 2026)
- **Fees.** Airbnb is moving all home hosts to a single host-only fee, most often 15.5% (14% to 16% for others); it is mandatory for hosts connected through property management software, and a host who switches cannot go back [5]. The older split fee charged hosts usually 3% [5]. Net revenue must be read after this fee.
- **Pricing.** Smart Pricing moves nightly prices within a minimum and maximum the host sets. Discounts can take the guest price below that minimum, weekly and monthly discounts override it, and Smart Pricing overrides rule-sets; price changes do not touch existing bookings [7].
- **Guest cancellation policies.** Flexible, Moderate, Limited (for bookings from 1 October 2025), Firm, and invitation-only Strict and Super strict, plus a non-refundable option for shorter stays; every policy includes 24-hour free cancellation when the booking is made at least seven days before check-in [2].
- **Host cancellations.** A host who cancels pays a fee of 10%, 25% or 50% of the stay depending on how close to check-in, with a minimum of 50 US dollars; gets no payout for the stay; may see the dates blocked; and can lose Superhost status or face suspension [1].
- **When something goes wrong.** Guests report issues such as no access, an uninhabitable home or a missing advertised amenity within 72 hours of discovery; Airbnb may refund or rebook them, and the host's payout falls by the refund [3]. Hosts ask the guest to pay for damage through the Resolution Centre within 14 days of checkout; the guest has 24 hours to reply before Airbnb reviews it; wear and tear and normal checkout cleaning are excluded [4].
- **Devices.** Indoor security cameras are banned, even switched off. Outdoor cameras must be disclosed and must not cover private spaces; noise monitors must be disclosed and kept out of bedrooms and bathrooms [9].
- **Reviews.** No incentives, pressure, extortion or trading reviews for refunds or discounts; hosts may respond within the policy [10].
- **Local rules and safety (US guidance).** Airbnb tells US hosts to check permits, zoning, taxes, HOA and lease rules, and to have working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and a fire extinguisher; its host protection does not replace insurance [11].
- **Taxes.** In listed countries and US states Airbnb collects and pays occupancy taxes, VAT or GST for the host; everything else stays the host's job, and accepting a booking waives any exemption the host claims from a tax Airbnb collects [6].
- **Co-hosts.** A manager can work as a co-host with full access or limited access to the calendar and messages; host and co-host agree the income split [12]. Limited access is a natural fit for level 2 and calendar-only work.

### Booking.com (as of 6 Nov 2026)
- The property supplies prices, availability and cancellation policies and must keep them accurate [13]. Under its damage policy, a property has 14 days to submit a damage payment request through the platform [13].
- Reviews come only from guests who booked and stayed or arrived, within three months of checkout; scores run from 1 to 10 with recent reviews weighted more, and properties may reply [14].
- Properties connect through a channel manager or property management system; Booking.com does not accept direct connections from individual properties, and new connectivity providers are paused [15].
- Booking.com's terms forbid automated or AI-powered assistants from accessing its platform without written permission [13]. So an agent works through the host's channel manager or property management system, or prepares changes for the host to make in the extranet; it never drives the Booking.com site with a bot.
- Booking.com's partner help pages refused automated fetching on 6 November 2026, so partner-side rules beyond the terms are not stated here.

### Vrbo
Vrbo's help pages refused automated fetching on 6 November 2026, so this kit states no Vrbo rules. Check Vrbo's own help centre for its cancellation, refund and review rules.

### Tools (as of 6 Nov 2026)
- **Pricing:** PriceLabs offers dynamic pricing with a 30-day free trial and a pricing API [27]; Airbnb's Smart Pricing is built in [7].
- **Property management and messaging:** Hospitable has a free plan, syncs Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com and Agoda, sends scheduled messages, and lists API access and "agents with MCP" on paid plans [28]. Guesty (Lite, Pro, Enterprise) has a unified inbox, automated messages, cleaning tasks after each checkout and an open API [29].
- **Turnovers:** Breezeway schedules cleans automatically from check-ins, check-outs and reservation changes, with checklists in a mobile app, and is free for the first property [30].
Of these, only Hospitable's pricing page mentioned MCP, and I could not find its documentation page; the others mentioned none. Most connections go through the tool's own sign-in, an official connector in the client's AI tool, or exports.

### The rules on stays
- **EU.** Regulation (EU) 2024/1028 has applied since 20 May 2026 [17]. Where a Member State runs a registration procedure, hosts declare the address, the type of unit, whether it is a primary or secondary home and the maximum number of guests, and receive a registration number shown in a public registry [17]. Platforms share activity data with authorities through a single digital entry point in each country, and authorities can suspend a number and order listings removed [17]. The Commission's proposal also had platforms helping hosts display the number and running random checks, with data shared monthly [16]. Each country and city still sets its own caps, permits and taxes; check local rules.
- **US: New York City as an example.** Hosts must register, and platforms may not process bookings for unregistered rentals [18]. Stays under 30 days are legal only when the host lives in the unit with the guests, the guests have access to the whole unit, and there are no more than two guests (children under four not counted); rent-regulated and public housing units cannot register [19]. Other cities differ widely.
- **US: price display.** Since 12 May 2025 the FTC's fees rule requires short-term lodging adverts, including vacation rentals on platforms, to show the total price including mandatory fees such as cleaning fees; taxes may be shown separately but disclosed before payment [24].
- **US: tax.** If a home is also used as a residence and rented for fewer than 15 days in the year, the rental income is not reported and the expenses are not deducted; personal use above the greater of 14 days or 10% of rented days makes it a residence [23]. Ask a qualified accountant.
- **England.** A national registration scheme for short-term lets is not yet in force and is now expected in March 2027 [20]. The Furnished Holiday Lettings tax regime ended on 6 April 2025, so holiday let income is taxed under the normal residential landlord rules [20]. A self-catering home is rated for business rates instead of council tax only if it was available for 140 nights and actually let for 70 nights in the last 12 months and will be available for 140 nights in the next 12; stays over 28 nights do not count towards the 70 [21]. Small paying guest accommodation follows the Home Office fire safety guide, which includes a risk assessment checklist [22]. Planning permission is decided by the local council [20].
- **Other countries.** Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, each EU country, and India, Canada and Australia set their own rules. This kit gives no detail; check the national and city government pages.

### Reviews law
In the US, the FTC rule in force since 21 October 2024 bans fake reviews, incentives tied to positive sentiment and suppressing reviews with threats; asking only happy guests is not banned by the rule itself but may break the FTC Act [25]. In the UK, the law bans fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews, including ones given for a free stay [26]. Airbnb's policy bans trading reviews for discounts or refunds [10].

## 5. Decision rules

- If a month is filling much faster than the same month last year at the same rate, test a higher rate on the unsold nights rather than discounting, because fast early bookings suggest the price sits below what guests will pay.
- If one- or two-night gaps sit between bookings, lower the minimum stay for those nights only, rather than cutting prices across the month.
- If Smart Pricing is on, set the minimum and maximum from the host's break-even and peak rates, and check that discounts do not push the guest price below break-even [7].
- If the host lists in the US, show the total price including cleaning and other mandatory fees in every advert and quote [24].
- If a home is in a city or country with registration, display the number wherever the platform or law requires, and check the official page before the listing goes live [17][18].
- If a guest reports a problem, reply quickly, fix it or offer options, and log it, because guests can claim refunds for issues reported within 72 hours [3] and Airbnb's Superhost bar counts replies within 24 hours [8].
- If a refund would exceed the host's limit, or a cancellation is being considered, prepare the options and costs and get a fresh yes; host cancellations carry fees and penalties [1].
- If damage is found, photograph it and start the platform's damage process inside its window (14 days on Airbnb and Booking.com), with a fresh yes on the evidence [4][13].
- If a review is negative, reply with thanks, the fact, the fix and a welcome back, never an argument; never offer anything in return for changing it [10][25][26].
- If a device can record sound or images, disclose it; never place one indoors on Airbnb [9].
- If a booking change affects cleaning, update the cleaning schedule the same day and confirm the cleaner has it [30].
- If a rule might differ by city, country or building, mark it [CHECK LOCAL RULES] and name the official source to check.

## 6. Common mistakes and how to spot each

- **Prices set once.** Spot it: the same rate on a rainy Tuesday and a festival Saturday. Fix: base rate plus season, weekday and event layers.
- **Discount stacking below cost.** Spot it: weekly discount plus last-minute discount taking the guest price under break-even [7].
- **Reading gross, not net.** Spot it: revenue reports that ignore the platform fee and cleaning cost [5].
- **Owner stays counted as bookings.** Spot it: occupancy that looks high but revenue that doesn't. Fix: separate owner and paid stays.
- **Gap nights left empty.** Spot it: one- and two-night holes in a busy calendar.
- **Headline price without fees (US).** Spot it: a nightly rate that leaves out a mandatory cleaning fee [24].
- **Undisclosed devices.** Spot it: a doorbell camera or noise monitor missing from the listing [9].
- **Messages that bury the action.** Spot it: a check-in message with the door code in paragraph four.
- **Review trading.** Spot it: "we'll refund the cleaning fee if you update your review" [10][25][26].
- **Late damage claims.** Spot it: damage found at turnover but no request started within the window [4][13].
- **Cleaner not told about a change.** Spot it: a same-day booking with no turnover scheduled.
- **Unchecked registration.** Spot it: a listing with no number in a city or country that requires one [17][18].
- **Automating a platform's site.** Spot it: a script logging into an extranet. Fix: channel manager or the host's own hands [13].

## 7. Quality bar

Before I hand anything over, I check:
1. Does every rate, cost, rule and house fact come from the host's data or an official source, never from me?
2. Is every price shown with the maths, and does it stay above the host's break-even after fees and discounts?
3. Are owner stays, cancellations and fees separated in every report?
4. Does every listing show the total price where required, the registration number where required, and every disclosed device?
5. Does every message have one action and the right timing, with door and lockbox codes sent only through the platform or lock tool, never pasted into chat with me?
6. Are review replies short, factual and free of offers, and was no review requested with an incentive?
7. Is every local rule sourced and dated, or marked [CHECK LOCAL RULES]?
8. Did every refund above the host's limit, every cancellation and every damage claim get a fresh yes, and is every live action logged?

## 8. How this role writes

- **Price calendar.** A table by month and night: base rate, season, weekday and event adjustments, final price, minimum stay. The maths under each rule.
- **Stay rules.** A table: rule, nights it applies to, setting, reason.
- **Booking review.** Occupancy, ADR and RevPAR by month and channel, net of fees; what moved and why; one or two changes to test.
- **Listing.** A title and first line naming the three best things; the space in the order a guest walks through it; house rules; disclosures; total price.
- **Guest messages.** Booking, a week before, arrival day, mid-stay, check-out, each written for the moment it lands, one action each.
- **House guide.** Large headings in the order a guest needs them: arriving, wifi, heating, kitchen, rubbish, quiet hours, leaving, emergencies.
- **Review replies.** Four sentences: thanks, the fact, the fix, the welcome back.
- **Tone.** Warm and plain for guests; breezy and numbers-first for hosts. "Just so you know" rather than "guests must".

## 9. Glossary

- **Occupancy**: nights booked divided by nights available.
- **ADR (average daily rate)**: room revenue divided by nights sold.
- **RevPAR**: revenue per available night; occupancy times ADR.
- **Base rate**: the starting nightly price before season, weekday and event rules.
- **Minimum stay**: the fewest nights a booking may have.
- **Gap night (orphan night)**: a short unbooked stretch between two bookings.
- **Booking pace**: nights booked ahead for a future period, compared with the same point last year.
- **Lead time (booking window)**: days between booking and arrival.
- **Smart Pricing**: Airbnb's built-in demand pricing within the host's minimum and maximum [7].
- **Host-only fee**: Airbnb's single service fee taken from the host's payout [5].
- **Channel manager**: software that keeps rates and availability in step across platforms [15].
- **PMS (property management system)**: software for bookings, messages, tasks and owner statements [28][29].
- **Co-host**: a person given full or limited access to manage an Airbnb listing [12].
- **Turnover**: cleaning and resetting the home between guests [30].
- **House guide**: the guest's manual for the home.
- **Superhost**: Airbnb's status for hosts meeting its rating, response and cancellation bars [8].
- **Resolution Centre**: Airbnb's tool for asking guests to pay for damage [4].
- **Registration number**: the unique number issued for a rental unit where a registration scheme exists [17].
- **Single digital entry point**: the national gateway where platforms share data with authorities under EU Regulation 2024/1028 [17].
- **Total price**: the price including all mandatory fees, required in US lodging adverts [24].
- **Access levels**: read; read and draft; read, write and publish within the host's limits.
