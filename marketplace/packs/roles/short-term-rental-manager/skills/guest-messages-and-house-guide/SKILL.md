# Guest messages, listing and house guide

## When to use

- Guests keep asking where to park, how the heating works or what the wifi is.
- A review complains about something the listing did not mention (stairs, noise, a shared entrance).
- A new listing or a newly furnished home is about to go live.
- Messages go out late, all at once, or with the door code buried in a long paragraph.

Do not use it to make claims about safety, cleanliness, licences or accessibility the host has not confirmed, or to write fake reviews.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the listing, past guest messages, reviews and current templates in the platform or property management system.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft listing text, message templates saved but not switched on, and the house guide.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** switch on approved templates, answer guest questions from the house facts, and publish approved listing edits. A new template, a change to house rules or the cancellation policy, and any statement about safety or licences need the host's yes.

## What you need

1. Property facts: rooms, beds, bathrooms, sizes, floor level and stairs, parking, outdoor space, what is shared, distances the host has measured.
2. Check-in method (lockbox, smart lock, key handover), check-in and check-out times, quiet hours, pets, smoking, party rules, maximum guests.
3. The questions guests ask most, from past messages and reviews.
4. Every camera, doorbell camera or noise monitor and where it is.
5. Registration or licence number if the address needs one, from the host.
6. Two or three of the host's own messages, so new ones sound like them.
7. Local tips the host wants to share (with web search connected, I can check opening hours or official pages the host names and say when I checked).

## Steps

### 1. Mine the questions
List every question and complaint from the last 12 months of messages and reviews, grouped by moment: before booking, before arrival, arrival, during, leaving. Each one is a fact missing from somewhere.

### 2. Write the listing
- **Title and first line:** the three best things, in plain words with real details ("10 minutes' walk to the beach", not "close to the beach").
- **The space:** in the order a guest walks through it, from the front door.
- **What to know:** stairs, shared areas, street noise, parking limits, anything that surprises guests. Saying it here prevents the complaint later.
- **Disclosures:** on Airbnb, indoor cameras are banned even when switched off; outdoor cameras and noise monitors must be disclosed, and noise monitors kept out of bedrooms and bathrooms. Use the listing's safety devices section.
- **Price:** for US listings, the total price including mandatory fees such as cleaning must be shown (FTC fees rule, since 12 May 2025).
- **Registration number:** where the city or country requires one, shown where the platform or law says. Check local rules.

### 3. Write the message set
Five messages, each for the moment it lands, each with one action:
1. **Booking confirmed:** thanks, dates, what happens next, one question (arrival time).
2. **A week before:** directions, parking, what to bring, a link to the house guide.
3. **Arrival day:** check-in time, how to get in. Send door or lockbox codes through the platform's scheduled message or the smart-lock tool, never pasted into chat with me.
4. **Mid-stay (morning after arrival):** a short check that all is well, and how to reach the host.
5. **Check-out:** time, the few things to do (bins, dishwasher, keys), thanks.
Add quick replies for the top ten questions. Keep texts short; check each tool's character limits and variables.

### 4. Write the house guide
Large headings in the order a guest needs them: Arriving; Wifi; Heating and hot water; Kitchen and appliances; Rubbish and recycling; Quiet hours and neighbours; Local tips; Leaving; Emergencies (emergency number, the host's contact, where the fuse box, water stop tap, fire extinguisher and first aid kit are). For US homes, Airbnb asks hosts to have working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and a fire extinguisher; for small UK paying-guest accommodation, the Home Office fire safety guide applies. Do not state that equipment exists unless the host confirms it.

### 5. Tune the tone
Warm and plain. "Just so you know" rather than "guests must". No "per policy", "no exceptions" or "we regret to inform". One action per message.

### 6. Set up and test
At level 3, switch on approved templates, send a test to the host's own account if the tool allows, check that variables (name, dates, code) fill correctly, and log it. At level 2, hand over the set with timing settings.

## Checks

- Every fact comes from the host; anything missing is marked [CHECK].
- Every recording device is disclosed; nothing indoors on Airbnb.
- US listings show a total price with mandatory fees.
- Registration numbers are shown where required, or [CHECK LOCAL RULES].
- Each message has one action and lands at the right moment.
- Codes are sent only by the platform or lock tool.
- No claims about safety, cleanliness, licences or accessibility beyond what the host confirmed.

## Output format

1. Question map by moment, with the fix for each.
2. Listing: title, first line, the space, what to know, disclosures, house rules.
3. Message set with timing and character counts, plus quick replies.
4. House guide.
5. Setup log (level 3).

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business for illustration: Heron Cabin, a lakeside cabin in Ontario, Canada.*

> **Title:** Lakeside cabin with private dock, wood stove and 2 kayaks
>
> **What to know:** The cabin is down 28 steps from the parking area. The road is gravel for the last 3 km. Phone signal is weak; wifi is good.
>
> **A week before (email):** "Hi {first name}, your stay at Heron Cabin starts on {arrival date}. The last 3 km are gravel, so allow extra time. Park in the bay marked H and take the steps down to the cabin. Here's the house guide: {guide link}. What time do you expect to arrive?"
>
> **Arrival day (platform scheduled message, 10 am):** "Check-in is from 3 pm. Your door code is {lock code}. The code works from 3 pm today until 11 am on {departure date}."
>
> **Mid-stay:** "Morning! Just checking the stove and hot water are working. Reply here any time and Ana will help."
