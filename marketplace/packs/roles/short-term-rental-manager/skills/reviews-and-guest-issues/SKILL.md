# Reviews and guest issues

## When to use

- A guest messages that something is wrong: no hot water, the code doesn't work, the home isn't clean, an advertised amenity is missing.
- A guest asks for a refund, or the host is thinking about cancelling a booking.
- The cleaner finds damage at turnover.
- New reviews need replies, or a bad review has landed.

Do not use it to offer anything in return for a review, to ask only happy guests for reviews in a way that hides unhappy ones, or to argue with a guest in public.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** messages, reservation details, reviews, payout reports and turnover notes.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** draft replies, refund and cancellation options, damage claim packs and review replies.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** reply to guests from house facts and approved wording, issue refunds up to the host's limit through the platform's own tool, and reply to positive and neutral reviews. Cancelling a booking, any refund above the limit, damage claims and their evidence, and replies to negative reviews or formal complaints need a fresh yes each time. Payouts and bank details stay with the host.

## What you need

1. The reservation: dates, guests, channel, amount paid, cancellation policy.
2. The guest's words, photos and the time they reported the problem.
3. The house facts and the turnover record (checklist, photos, cleaner's notes).
4. The host's refund limit and what they are willing to offer.
5. For damage: check-in condition photos, turnover photos, repair or cleaning quotes, and the checkout date.
6. For reviews: the review text and rating, and what actually happened.

## Steps

### 1. Live problem: reply fast, fix first
Acknowledge within the response time the host promises (Airbnb's Superhost bar counts replies within 24 hours). Restate the problem in the guest's words, say what happens next and when, and offer a practical fix (a fresh code, a cleaner sent back, a heater dropped off). If anything is unsafe (gas smell, smoke, no heat in freezing weather, a fall hazard), tell the guest to call the emergency number first and tell the host at once. Log the time of report and every step.

### 2. Know what the platform will do
On Airbnb, guests can report covered issues (no access, not habitable, significantly different from the listing, a missing advertised amenity) within 72 hours of discovery; Airbnb may refund or rebook them, and the host's payout falls by the refund. A fast fix often settles it before then. On Booking.com and Vrbo, check the platform's own rules; Vrbo's pages could not be checked when this kit was written.

### 3. Refunds and cancellations: lay out the options and their cost
For each option (partial refund, a refund of nights not used, moving the dates, cancelling), show the cost to the host and the guest's likely next step. Under Airbnb's host cancellation policy, a host who cancels pays 10%, 25% or 50% of the booking depending on timing (minimum 50 US dollars), loses the payout and may see dates blocked and Superhost status lost. A refund within the host's limit can go at level 3; above it, or any cancellation, goes to the host for a fresh yes.

### 4. Damage: evidence, then the platform's process, inside the window
Photograph the damage at turnover with the date, collect a quote or receipt, and compare with the check-in condition photos. On Airbnb, ask the guest for payment in the Resolution Centre within 14 days of checkout; the guest has 24 hours to reply before Airbnb reviews it, and wear and tear and normal checkout cleaning are not covered. On Booking.com, a damage payment request must be submitted within 14 days. Prepare the pack; the claim goes only after the host's yes.

### 5. Review replies: four sentences
Thanks; the fact (one line, no argument); the fix (what changed); the welcome back. Keep personal details out. Future guests are the real audience. Never offer a refund, discount or gift in return for a review or for changing one: Airbnb's policy bans it, the US FTC rule bans incentives tied to positive reviews, and UK law bans concealed incentivised reviews, including ones given for a free stay.

### 6. Asking for reviews
Ask every guest the same way, after every stay. Do not filter who gets the request by how happy they seemed; in the US the FTC says asking only happy customers may break the FTC Act.

### 7. Close the loop
Turn each problem into a fix: a line in the house guide, a turnover checklist item, a listing change. Log it.

## Checks

- Every guest problem has a time of report, a reply time and an outcome in the log.
- Unsafe situations went to the emergency number and the host first.
- Refunds above the limit and every cancellation had a fresh yes.
- Damage claims were started inside the platform's window, with dated photos and a quote.
- Review replies are four sentences or fewer, factual, polite, and offer nothing for the review.
- Review requests go to every guest the same way.
- Each problem produced a fix somewhere (guide, checklist, listing).

## Output format

1. Guest reply, ready to send, with the next step and time.
2. Options table for refund or cancellation: option, cost to host, platform rule, recommendation for the host to decide.
3. Damage pack: photos list, check-in comparison, quote, platform and deadline, draft claim text.
4. Review replies, marked "send" (positive and neutral) or "needs your yes" (negative).
5. Fix list and log.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a one-bed let in Galway, Ireland. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Guest reply (sent at level 3, 7:12 pm):** "Sorry the hot water has gone, Ms Brady. Our plumber, Ciaran, can be there by 8:30 tonight. Until then, the immersion switch by the bathroom door gives hot water in about 40 minutes. I'll message you the moment he's on his way."
>
> **Options for you to choose:**
>
> | Option | Cost to you | Note |
> |---|---|---|
> | Refund tonight's night | 95 | Within your 100 limit, so I can issue it at level 3 if you pick it |
> | No refund, fix tonight | 0 plus call-out | Guest may still report a covered issue within 72 hours |
>
> **Review reply (negative, needs your yes):** "Thank you for staying, Tom. You're right that the street can be noisy on Friday nights. We've added this to the listing and fitted heavier curtains in the bedroom. We'd love to welcome you back on a quieter weekend."
