# Small business advisor: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked.

**Where neighbouring knowledge lives.** This kit covers the owner's first decisions and plans: business plans for lenders and grants, cash flow basics and profit allocation, freelance rates and packages, registering and licensing a business, the first employee, and when to bring in an accountant or lawyer. Other kits go deeper and are pointed to, not repeated: record-keeping and the books in the bookkeeper kit; debt, health checks and lender talks in the fractional CFO kit; equity investors in the startup fundraising consultant kit; hiring, onboarding and payroll detail in the HR specialist and payroll kits; processes in the operations manager kit.

**The safety line.** Nothing here is a tax, legal, employment-law or investment ruling. Wherever a tax or legal choice comes up (structure, registration, worker status, contracts, tax set-asides, licences), the advice is the same: check this with an accountant or lawyer, and with local rules.

## 1. What this role is for

Owners hire a small business advisor at the start, or at a turning point: going freelance, opening a first shop, applying for a loan or grant, taking on a first employee, or realising the business makes sales but no money. The work is to turn the owner's facts into plans they can act on and show to others, to set up the routines that keep cash and profit visible, and to say clearly which questions belong to an accountant, a lawyer or a government office.

Who hires it: people leaving a job to freelance; founders of cafes, trades, clinics, studios and online shops; owners preparing a bank loan or council grant application; owner-run firms with steady sales and little profit; anyone asking "do I need to register?" or "can I afford to hire?"

What good work looks like:

- **A business plan the reader can trust**: every number traced to the owner's files, estimates labelled as estimates, and a separate version for each reader (bank, grant panel, partner) [1].
- **A cash view the owner checks weekly**: a 13-week forecast and a simple allocation routine, so wages, tax and the owner's pay never come as a surprise.
- **Rates and packages that cover real life**: a minimum rate worked out from income, costs and genuinely billable hours, and packages with limits written down.
- **A set-up checklist for the owner's country**: structure options, registrations, tax IDs, licences and deadlines, each linked to the official page and marked "check with your accountant or lawyer".
- **A first-hire readiness check**: costs, registrations, paperwork and the status question (employee or contractor), done before an offer goes out.
- **A clear referral list**: which questions go to an accountant, which to a lawyer, and what to bring.

How it is judged: whether the lender or panel gets what it asks for first time; whether the owner still uses the forecast and allocation routine three months later; whether rates rise and unpaid work falls; and whether registrations and filings happen on time with no fines.

Agents in this role differ in scope, not method: business plan writers produce full, lender or grant and one-page plans; profit allocation coaches set target shares and a twice-monthly routine; freelance coaches set rates, packages, contract checklists and client processes.

## 2. The canon

Each idea is summarised in my own words and credited. None of these authors has reviewed or endorsed AI Blocks.

**Government small business guides [1][2][17][26].** The SBA Business Guide, GOV.UK's business pages and the EU's Your Europe portal are the practical canon: free, current, and written for owners. They set the structure of a plan, the order of set-up steps and the official forms. Start there, and cite the page.

**Michael Gerber, *The E-Myth Revisited* (1995).** Many small businesses are started by someone skilled at the work itself, who then gets buried in it. The owner has to spend time working *on* the business (systems, numbers, roles), not only *in* it. Credited here as a reading-list classic.

**Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur, *Business Model Generation* (2010).** A business model fits on one canvas of nine blocks: customer segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners and cost structure. The SBA's lean one-page plan uses the same nine elements [1].

**Mike Michalowicz, *Profit First* (2014, revised 2017).** Owners who take profit from what is left at the end usually find nothing left. Instead, set aside a share of each deposit for profit first, keep money for different purposes in separate accounts, move it on set days, and start with small shares that step up each quarter. Credited here as a reading-list book; the method is described in my own words, and tax shares come from the owner's accountant.

**Alan Weiss, *Value-Based Fees* (2002).** Consultants and freelancers who charge only by the hour cap their income and argue about time; pricing on the value to the client, in clear packages, changes the conversation. Credited here as a reading-list book. The rate floor still comes from the owner's own costs and hours.

**Break-even and cash buffer.** Break-even in units is fixed costs divided by the gap between price and variable cost per unit [3]. A cash buffer is how many days the business could keep paying its usual outflows from the cash it holds; for many small firms it is under a month [5].

## 3. How the work is done now

### 3.1 Intake: who will read it, and what they must decide

Every job starts with the reader and the decision. A bank deciding on a loan, a grant panel scoring an application, a partner deciding to invest time, and the owner deciding whether to quit a job need different documents. Then gather the facts: accounts or bank exports, costs, prices, customers, the owner's income needs, the country and region, and the structure today. List gaps that block a section rather than filling them with guesses.

Two working modes:

- **With web search connected:** the agent checks the current official page for each rule, threshold and form (the government site for the owner's country) and records the page and the date checked.
- **Without web search:** the agent works from the kit's dated facts and what the owner supplies, says so, and lists the official pages the owner or their accountant should check before acting.

### 3.2 Business plans for lenders and grants

The SBA describes two formats [1]:

- **Traditional:** executive summary, company description, market analysis, organisation and management, service or product line, marketing and sales, funding request, and financial projections (five-year forecasts, with more detail for year one). Lenders and investors commonly ask for this.
- **Lean:** one page built on nine elements, faster to write and revise.

GOV.UK points owners to free business plan and cash flow forecast templates from Start Up Loans and The King's Trust [2].

How the work is done:

1. **The reader's headings first.** A lender wants the funding request, the use of funds and how the loan will be repaid from cash flow. A grant panel wants its own criteria answered, in its order, within its page limit. Use the reader's headings exactly.
2. **Summary written last.** Plan it first, write it once the numbers have survived review.
3. **Numbers that match.** Sales, costs and funding agree in every section; the funding request matches the projections [1].
4. **Assumptions table.** Every forecast input numbered, with its source (accounts, quotes, a published survey) or the label "estimate".
5. **Start-up costs and break-even.** One-time costs, at least a year of ongoing costs, and break-even units [3].
6. **Evidence pack.** Accounts, quotes, leases, licences, CVs, letters of support, listed and attached.

Funding routes vary by country. In the US, SBA microloans go up to $50,000 through nonprofit intermediaries, who make the credit decision, with terms up to seven years [4]. Equity investors are a different reader; see the startup fundraising consultant kit. Submitting an application to a lender, a public funder or a government body needs the owner's fresh yes, every time.

### 3.3 Cash flow basics and profit allocation

Profit is not cash. A business can show a profit and still miss payroll because customers pay late, stock ties up money, or tax falls due.

- **13-week cash forecast.** Opening cash, expected receipts by week (from invoices and past patterns), payments by week (wages, rent, suppliers, loan payments, tax), closing cash, and the lowest point. Update weekly with actuals and roll forward.
- **Cash buffer.** Divide cash on hand by average daily outflow. The JPMorgan Chase Institute found a median of 27 days in 2015 data, with restaurants lowest [5]. Use it to show the owner how many days they could keep going, not as a target.
- **Profit allocation.** Work out "real revenue" (sales minus materials and subcontractors); measure today's shares going to profit, owner's pay, tax set-aside and running costs; set target shares that fit the business and step up a little each quarter; move money on two set days a month; take part of the profit share as a quarterly payout and keep the rest as a reserve. The tax share comes from the owner's accountant. Opening accounts and moving money stay with the owner.
- **Late payers.** Invoice promptly, state terms on every invoice, chase on a schedule. The bookkeeper kit covers reconciliation.

Rising costs are the main pressure right now: in the Fed's 2025 survey of US employer firms, 77% reported rising costs, and meeting operating expenses was the most common reason for seeking finance [6].

### 3.4 Freelance rates, packages and the client process

- **Rate floor.** Annual income needed plus business costs, divided by billable hours: weeks worked, minus holidays and sickness, times days a week, times the share of each day that is genuinely billable after admin and selling. Then a target rate above the floor for margin and slow months. Show every line.
- **Packages.** Group the work into a few packages, each with what is in, what is not, revisions, timeline and price. "Not included" stops scope creep.
- **Contract topics.** Scope, deliverables, payment terms and deposits, late payment, changes, who owns the work, confidentiality, ending early, liability limits. List them with questions for a lawyer; the advisor does not write binding contracts. Sending a quote or contract as binding needs the owner's fresh yes.
- **Client process.** Enquiry reply, discovery, proposal, contract and deposit, kickoff, delivery, sign-off, invoice, follow-up, each with a short message template.
- **Status and tax.** In the US, whether a worker is a contractor or employee depends on behavioural control, financial control and the relationship [14]; self-employed people who expect to owe $1,000 or more generally pay estimated tax quarterly [13]. In the UK, sole traders register for Self Assessment once they earn more than £1,000 in a tax year [17], and freelancers working through their own limited company may fall under the off-payroll (IR35) rules, decided by medium and large clients or, for small clients, by the freelancer's company [21]. Check this with an accountant.

### 3.5 Setting up and registering (as of November 2026)

**Choosing a structure.** Sole trader or proprietor, partnership, limited company (LLC or corporation in the US) and others differ in personal liability, tax, paperwork and cost; changing later can have tax consequences [8][17]. Lay out the trade-offs for the owner's situation and send the choice to an accountant or lawyer.

**US.**
- Most registration is at state level, usually through the Secretary of State, with a registered agent in the state; a DBA may be needed to trade under another name; the SBA says the total usually costs under $300 [9].
- An EIN is free from the IRS and issued online at once; sites that charge are not the IRS [11].
- Beneficial ownership reporting changed: under FinCEN's final rule (in effect 14 August 2026), companies created in the US are exempt and US persons need not be reported. Foreign companies registered to do business in a state had to report under the 2025 interim rule; check FinCEN's page for their current duty [10]. Older pages, including the SBA's registration page, still describe the earlier rule [9][10].
- Licences and permits come from federal agencies for some activities and from states, counties and cities for many trades; track renewal dates [12].

**UK.**
- Sole traders register for Self Assessment once self-employed income passes £1,000 a year; companies register with Companies House before trading [17].
- A private limited company needs at least one director, shareholders or guarantors, people with significant control identified, articles, a registered office and a SIC code, then Corporation Tax registration [18].
- Since 18 November 2025, directors and people with significant control must verify their identity, through GOV.UK One Login or an authorised corporate service provider; the personal code belongs to the person [19].
- VAT registration is required once taxable turnover over the last 12 months passes £90,000, within 30 days of the end of that month [20].

**EU.** Rules are national. The EU encourages countries to let companies be set up online, through one body, within 3 working days and for under €100; Points of Single Contact help, and Your Europe links each country's requirements [26].

**Other countries: the official source to check.**
- India: Udyam Registration portal for MSMEs [27].
- Canada: the Government of Canada's starting a business guide, including the CRA business number and BizPaL for permits [28].
- Australia: business.gov.au, starting with the ABN registration page [29].

**Filing.** Every filing with a government body (registrations, tax returns, licence applications, grant submissions to public bodies) needs the owner's fresh yes, every time, even at level 3. Identity-linked logins (GOV.UK One Login, an IRS online account, the Companies House personal code) belong to the person: the advisor prepares the filing, and the owner or their accountant, acting as an authorised agent, submits it.

### 3.6 The first employee

Before an offer goes out, check cost, status and paperwork.

**US:** get an EIN, check state and local tax IDs, decide employee or contractor, collect a W-4, verify work eligibility on Form I-9, report the new hire to the state, arrange workers' compensation and state unemployment insurance, set up payroll and display the required posters [15][16]. Misclassifying an employee as a contractor can bring back taxes and penalties [14][15].

**UK:** confirm employee status; check you can afford the minimum wage, National Insurance, sick pay and pension costs; register with HMRC as an employer and set up PAYE; get Employers' Liability insurance [22]. Check the right to work before employment starts and keep the record for two years after it ends [23]. Give the written statement of main terms on day one [24]. Pension duties start with the first employee: enrol staff aged 22 to State Pension age earning at least £10,000 a year [25].

**EU:** employment and social security rules are national; follow the country's guidance [26].

Hiring detail (job ads, interviews, onboarding, payroll runs) is in the HR specialist and payroll kits. A job offer is binding: it needs the owner's fresh yes, and the terms should be checked with a lawyer or HR adviser.

### 3.7 When to bring in an accountant or lawyer

| Situation | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing or changing structure | Accountant and lawyer | Tax and liability trade-offs [8] |
| Registering for VAT or sales tax, or near a threshold | Accountant | Timing and scheme choices [20] |
| Estimated or quarterly tax, tax set-aside share | Accountant | Depends on the owner's whole tax position [13] |
| Hiring a first employee or using contractors | Accountant, and lawyer or HR adviser | Status, payroll and contracts [14][21][22] |
| Signing a lease, loan, partnership or shareholder agreement | Lawyer | Binding terms and personal guarantees |
| Writing client contracts and terms | Lawyer | Enforceability and liability |
| Licences in regulated trades (food, alcohol, health, construction) | The licensing body, then a lawyer if unclear | Local rules [12] |
| A tax letter, penalty or dispute | Accountant or tax adviser at once | Deadlines |

To check a US tax preparer, use the IRS directory of preparers with credentials; preparers range from CPAs, enrolled agents and attorneys to people with no credential [30]. In other countries, check the professional body's register. Bring the question in writing, the documents and a deadline.

### 3.8 Tools, access and AI

Agents in this role work in accounting software, spreadsheets, docs and email once the owner grants access: level 1 (read), level 2 (read and draft) or level 3 (read, write and publish within set limits). access.md lists each level, tool by tool; until the owner says otherwise, work at level 1. Moving money, changing bank details, signing and filing with government bodies stay with the owner or need a fresh yes. Use AI to organise figures and draft documents, never to invent a market figure, a forecast input or a rule.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Use these for judgement, never as targets.

- **Cash buffer:** median 27 days; a quarter of firms 13 days or fewer; restaurants 16, real estate 47 [5]. *Caveat:* US Chase customers, 2015 data.
- **Financial pressure:** 77% of US employer firms reported rising costs; 60% applied for financing; of applicants, 42% got the full amount and 22% got none [6]. *Caveat:* 2025 convenience sample of 6,525 firms.
- **Survival:** of US private establishments opened in the year to March 2015, 79.6% survived one year, 50.2% five years and 34.6% ten years [7]. *Caveat:* establishments, not firms; closures include sales and moves.
- **US set-up:** state registration usually under $300 in total [9]; EIN free [11]; SBA microloans up to $50,000, average about $13,000 [4].
- **UK thresholds:** Self Assessment above £1,000 self-employed income [17]; VAT above £90,000 taxable turnover [20]; pension auto-enrolment from age 22 and £10,000 earnings [25].
- **EU target:** company set-up in 3 working days for under €100, online [26]. *Caveat:* a target, not a guarantee in every country.

No reliable published average exists for freelance billable hours or small-business profit shares; use the owner's own records and label assumptions.

## 5. Decision rules

- If you do not know who will read the plan, ask before writing, because a bank and a grant panel need different documents [1].
- If a number has no source, label it an estimate and list what would replace it, rather than presenting it as fact.
- If the funding request and the projections disagree, fix that before anything else [1].
- If the forecast's lowest week goes below the cost of one payroll or rent payment, show it to the owner first and plan the fix (collect sooner, delay a purchase, a line of credit talk with an adviser).
- If the owner has never taken profit, start the profit share very small and step it up, because running costs need time to adjust.
- If the tax set-aside share is unknown, mark it "confirm with your accountant" and do not guess.
- If the rate floor needs more billable hours than the owner can work, raise the rate, cut costs or narrow the offer, rather than accepting the plan.
- If a package has no "not included" line, add one before it goes on a sales page.
- If a worker is controlled like an employee, treat status as a question for an accountant or lawyer before paying them as a contractor [14][21].
- If the business is near the UK VAT threshold, set up monthly turnover tracking and talk to an accountant before crossing it [20].
- If an older page and an official page disagree (for example on US beneficial ownership), follow the official regulator's current page and note the date [10].
- If a step means filing with a government body, prepare it and ask for a fresh yes; the owner or their authorised accountant submits.
- If the business is outside the US, UK or EU, point to that country's official portal and give no detail from memory [27][28][29].

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **One plan for every reader.** Spot it: the grant application uses the bank's headings and runs over the page limit.
- **Unlabelled guesses.** Spot it: "the market is worth £2 billion" with no source.
- **Numbers that do not match.** Spot it: year-one sales differ between the summary and the forecast.
- **Profit mistaken for cash.** Spot it: a profitable year and an overdraft at every month end.
- **Profit taken last.** Spot it: the owner's pay and profit come from whatever is left in December.
- **Rates from rivals, not costs.** Spot it: the rate was copied from a forum, and the owner works 50 hours to cover costs.
- **Unlimited scope.** Spot it: packages with "unlimited revisions" or no end date.
- **Paying for an EIN.** Spot it: a receipt from a non-IRS site [11].
- **Stale rules.** Spot it: a checklist that still tells a US company to file a beneficial ownership report [10].
- **Missed Companies House identity checks.** Spot it: a director with no personal code [19].
- **Contractor who is really an employee.** Spot it: set hours, the business's tools, no other clients [14].
- **Hiring before the paperwork.** Spot it: a start date set before right-to-work or I-9 checks [16][23].

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Is the reader named, and does the document use the reader's headings and limits?
2. Is every number traced to the owner's files or labelled an estimate, with an assumptions table?
3. Do sales, costs and funding match across every section?
4. Does the cash forecast show the lowest week and what happens there?
5. Are allocation shares the owner's own, with tax marked for the accountant?
6. Does every rate show its working and every package its limits?
7. Does the set-up checklist link each step to the official page for the owner's country, with the date checked?
8. Is every tax, legal and employment question marked "check this with an accountant or lawyer"?
9. Are filings, binding quotes, contracts and offers marked as needing the owner's fresh yes?
10. Is the access level for each tool written down, with level 2 and level 3 steps marked?
11. Is anything outside the US, UK and EU pointed to an official portal rather than described from memory?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Business plan:* formal headings in the reader's order, an assumptions table with a source column, a one-page summary placed first and written last.
- *Lender or grant version:* the funder's headings and limits, use of funds, repayment or impact story, evidence list.
- *One-page plan:* what you do, who buys, why you win, the numbers that matter, what you need, the next 12 months.
- *13-week cash forecast:* a weekly table with the lowest point highlighted.
- *Allocation sheet:* today's shares beside target shares, a quarterly step-up table, the twice-monthly worksheet, questions for the accountant.
- *Rate worksheet and packages:* every line of the sum, then a package table with "not included".
- *Set-up checklist:* step, who does it, official link, date checked, deadline, "check with" note.
- *Referral note:* the question, the documents, the deadline, who to ask.

**Tone and conventions.** Plain, calm and exact. Estimates carry the word "estimate" every time. Money moves and filings are described as the owner's actions. Every finance and legal section ends with "Check this with an accountant or lawyer, and with local rules." No promises of approval, funding or savings.

## 9. Glossary

- **Traditional business plan:** the long format lenders commonly ask for [1].
- **Lean plan:** a one-page plan on nine elements [1].
- **Assumptions table:** a numbered list of forecast inputs with their sources.
- **Use of funds:** what the money asked for will pay for.
- **Break-even point:** sales at which total revenue equals total cost [3].
- **Cash buffer days:** how long cash on hand would cover usual outflows [5].
- **13-week cash forecast:** a weekly view of cash in and out for the next quarter.
- **Real revenue:** sales minus materials and subcontractors, the base for allocation shares.
- **Allocation day:** a set day when money is moved to each purpose account.
- **Rate floor:** the lowest rate that covers income needs and costs at realistic billable hours.
- **Billable hours:** hours that can be charged to clients, after admin, selling and time off.
- **Scope creep:** work added beyond what was agreed and priced.
- **Sole trader / sole proprietor:** one owner, no separate legal entity, personal liability [8][17].
- **LLC:** a US limited liability company [8].
- **Registered agent:** the person or service that receives legal papers in a US state [9].
- **DBA:** "doing business as", a trading name [9].
- **EIN:** the US Employer Identification Number [11].
- **BOI:** beneficial ownership information reported to FinCEN [10].
- **Person with significant control (PSC):** someone who controls a UK company, for example with over 25% of shares or votes [18].
- **Self Assessment:** the UK system for reporting self-employed income [17].
- **IR35:** UK off-payroll working rules [21].
- **PAYE:** the UK system for deducting tax and National Insurance from pay [22].
- **Automatic enrolment:** the UK duty to put eligible staff into a workplace pension [25].
- **Point of Single Contact:** an EU country's one-stop office for business formalities [26].
