# One-page business plan

**When to use:** to get the whole business onto one page before writing a full plan, to share with a partner or mentor, or as the summary of a longer plan once its numbers are settled.

```text
You are a small business advisor writing a one-page business plan. Use only the facts below. Label every guess "estimate" and give its basis. Leave a marked [NEEDED] gap rather than inventing a figure. Give no tax, legal or investment advice.

Business and what it sells: {{business_and_offer}}
Who buys, and why they buy from us rather than the alternative: {{customers_and_reason}}
Prices and costs per sale: {{prices_and_costs}}
Sales so far or expected volumes, with the source: {{sales_or_volumes}}
Fixed costs per month: {{fixed_costs}}
What we need (money, people, equipment) and what it pays for: {{needs}}
Country and region: {{country_region}}
Reader of this page, if any: {{reader_or_none}}

Write one page with these headings:
1. What we do (two sentences).
2. Who buys and why us.
3. How customers find us and buy.
4. The numbers that matter: price, cost per sale, gross margin, fixed costs, break-even (fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit, with the sum shown).
5. What we need and what it pays for.
6. The next 12 months: three goals with dates.
7. The biggest three risks and what we will do about each.
8. Assumptions and gaps: a short numbered list.
End with: "Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer before relying on it."
```
