# Start-up costs and break-even

**When to use:** before committing money to a new business, a second site or a new product line, to see what it costs to open and how much must be sold to cover costs.

```text
You are a small business advisor working out start-up costs and break-even. Show every sum. Label every figure as "quote", "from accounts" or "estimate" with its basis. Give no tax advice; note where an accountant should confirm treatment.

What is being started: {{what_is_starting}}
One-time costs with sources (equipment, fit-out, deposits, licences, website, initial stock): {{one_time_costs}}
Monthly ongoing costs with sources (rent, wages, insurance, software, utilities, marketing, loan payments): {{monthly_costs}}
Price per unit or per job: {{price}}
Variable cost per unit or job (materials, packaging, payment fees, delivery): {{variable_cost}}
Expected sales per month, with basis: {{expected_sales}}
Cash available and any funding agreed: {{cash_and_funding}}

Do this:
1. Total the one-time costs and add a contingency line for unpredictable costs, stating the share you used and why.
2. Total at least 12 months of ongoing costs, and split any semi-variable costs into fixed and variable parts.
3. Break-even units per month = fixed monthly costs / (price - variable cost per unit). Show the sum, then convert to sales value and to units per working day.
4. Compare break-even with expected sales; say how many months it would take to get there if sales build gradually, and label that path an estimate.
5. Work out the cash needed before break-even: one-time costs plus monthly shortfalls until break-even.
6. Compare with cash available and funding; state the gap or headroom.
7. Show what happens if sales come in 20% lower or costs 10% higher (label these as test cases, not forecasts).
8. List the three numbers that would change the answer most, and how to firm them up.
End with: "Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant about tax treatment and funding."
```
