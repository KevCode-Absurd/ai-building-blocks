# Business plan for lenders and grants

## When to use

- A bank loan, microloan or government-backed loan application needs a plan.
- A council, government or foundation grant asks for a business plan or project plan.
- A partner, landlord or key supplier wants to see the plan.
- The owner has a draft and wants a send-ready check (prompt `business-plan-send-ready-check`).
- The owner wants a one-page plan to think clearly (prompt `one-page-business-plan`).

Equity investors are a different reader: see the startup fundraising consultant kit. The financial forecast itself is built in the skill `cash-flow-forecast-and-profit-allocation`, or brought by the owner.

## What you need

1. Who will read the plan, what they will decide, and their headings, page limit and deadline (the lender's or funder's guidance notes).
2. The last two years of accounts, if trading; otherwise bank statements and any sales so far.
3. Costs and quotes for what the money will pay for (equipment, fit-out, stock, hires).
4. A forecast, or the inputs for one: prices, volumes, costs, payment terms.
5. Notes on the business: what it sells, to whom, how it wins customers, who runs it.
6. Market and competitor research with sources. If web search is connected, I can look up official statistics and record each source and date; if not, I leave a marked gap and say what kind of source would fill it.
7. Supporting documents: CVs, leases, licences, letters of support, quotes.

**Access:** level 1 to read files. Level 2 to write drafts in your shared folder. Level 3 to update the live plan files within your limits. Submitting to a lender, funder or government portal is never covered by level 3: it needs your fresh yes each time, and you or your adviser submit through your own login.

## Steps

### 1. Name the reader and the decision

Write at the top: "Reader: [bank / grant panel / partner]. Decision: [lend X / award Y / join]." Collect the reader's required headings and limits. If there is a scoring sheet, keep it open while writing.

### 2. List facts and gaps

Read every file. Make two lists: facts I have (with file names) and gaps that block a section. Ask for the gaps before drafting; do not fill them with guesses.

### 3. Build the assumptions table

Number every forecast input: price, volume, growth, costs, payment days, staff. Give each a source (accounts, a quote, a published survey with its date) or the label "estimate" and the reasoning. Use the same label wording every time.

### 4. Draft the full plan

Use the SBA's traditional sections unless the reader sets others: executive summary (plan now, write last), company description, market analysis, organisation and management, products or services, marketing and sales, funding request, financial projections (five years, with year one in most detail), appendix. Plain English for non-expert readers. Market sizes and rival facts only with sources.

### 5. Add start-up costs and break-even

One-time costs, at least 12 months of ongoing costs, and break-even in units: fixed costs divided by (price minus variable cost per unit). Show the sum.

### 6. Cross-check the numbers

Sales, costs, staff, funding and cash agree in the summary, the narrative, the forecast and the funding request. The funding request equals the use of funds. Any mismatch is fixed before anything else.

### 7. Cut the lender version

Lead with the request: amount, purpose, term sought, and how repayments are covered from forecast cash flow, with the lowest cash month shown. Add the owner's own contribution, security offered, the track record, and the risks with what the owner will do about each. Attach the evidence list.

### 8. Cut the grant version

Answer the funder's criteria in its order, in its words, within its limit. Show outcomes the funder cares about (jobs, local impact, community benefit) with how they will be measured. Cut anything the funder does not ask for.

### 9. Write the one-page summary last

What you do, who buys, why you win, the numbers that matter, what you need and what it pays for, the next 12 months.

### 10. Mark adviser questions

List anything that turns on tax, law or how a figure must be reported (structure, VAT or sales tax, grant tax treatment, personal guarantees, lease terms). Check this with an accountant or lawyer before submitting.

### 11. Prepare the submission pack

The plan version, the attachments, the portal or address, the deadline. Ask for the owner's fresh yes; the owner or their adviser submits.

## Checks

- Reader and decision named; reader's headings and limits followed.
- Every number traced to a file or labelled "estimate" in the assumptions table.
- Funding request equals use of funds and matches the projections.
- The lowest cash month is shown and covered.
- No invented market data, quotes or letters of support.
- One-page summary matches the full plan.
- Adviser questions listed; submission marked as needing a fresh yes.
- The plan ends with: *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer before you submit.*

## Output format

1. Reader and decision line.
2. Facts and gaps list.
3. Full plan with assumptions table.
4. Lender or grant version.
5. One-page summary.
6. Evidence list and submission pack.
7. Questions for the accountant or lawyer.

## Example of good output (short)

Made-up business for illustration: Crumb and Co, a bakery seeking a £60,000 bank loan to open a second site.

> **Reader:** high-street bank, small business lending team. **Decision:** lend £60,000 over five years.
>
> | # | Assumption | Value | Source |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | A1 | Site 2 weekly sales, year 1 | £5,400 | Estimate: 75% of site 1's year-1 average (accounts 2024) |
> | A2 | Ingredient cost | 31% of sales | Accounts 2024 and 2025 |
> | A3 | Fit-out | £38,500 | Two contractor quotes, Oct 2026 |
> | A4 | Lease | £21,000 a year | Heads of terms, 14 Oct 2026 |
>
> **Use of funds:** fit-out £38,500 (A3), ovens £14,200 (quote), opening stock and working capital £7,300. Total £60,000.
>
> **Repayment:** forecast cash from operations covers loan payments in every month; lowest point month 3 after opening, with £4,100 headroom (estimate, depends on A1).
>
> **Gap:** footfall for the new street. A council footfall count or the landlord's data would fill it.
>
> *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer before you submit.*
