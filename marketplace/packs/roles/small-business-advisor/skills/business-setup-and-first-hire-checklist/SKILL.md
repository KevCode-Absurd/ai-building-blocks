# Business set-up and first-hire checklist

## When to use

- Someone is starting to trade, or has started and is not sure what they should have registered.
- An owner is thinking about becoming a company or adding a partner.
- A business is about to hire its first employee, or is paying someone as a contractor and wonders if that is right.
- A business is expanding into another state or country.

## What you need

1. Country and region (state, county, city), and where customers are.
2. What the business sells, and any regulated activity (food, alcohol, childcare, health, construction, finance, transport).
3. Who owns it, the structure now or the options being considered, and expected first-year turnover.
4. The trading name, and whether it differs from the owner's or company's legal name.
5. For a first hire: role, hours, pay, start date, and whether the person will be controlled like an employee.
6. The owner's accountant and lawyer, if any.

**Access:** level 1 to read official pages (live if web search is connected) and any documents you share. Level 2 to prepare each filing: completed answer sheets, document lists and deadlines, saved for you to check. Level 3 does not cover filings: every registration, tax return, licence application or other government submission needs your fresh yes each time, and identity-linked logins (GOV.UK One Login, a Companies House personal code, an IRS online account) are used only by you or your authorised accountant. A job offer needs your fresh yes too.

## Steps

### 1. Confirm the mode and the date

With web search connected, check each rule on the official page and record the URL and the date. Without it, use the kit's dated facts, say so, and list the pages for the owner or accountant to check before acting.

### 2. Lay out the structure options

Compare the options that fit (for example sole trader, partnership, limited company; in the US, sole proprietorship, LLC, corporations) on personal liability, how profit is taxed, paperwork and cost, and how hard it is to change later. Do not pick for the owner. Check this with an accountant or lawyer.

### 3. Build the registration list for the country

**US:** state registration (usually the Secretary of State) with a registered agent for LLCs and corporations; a DBA if trading under another name; a free EIN from the IRS (never pay a third-party site); state and local tax IDs; federal registration only for trade marks, tax-exempt status or an S corporation election. Beneficial ownership: under FinCEN's final rule effective 14 August 2026, companies created in the US are exempt; foreign companies registered in a state should check FinCEN's page for their current duty. Older pages may say otherwise; follow FinCEN's current page.

**UK:** sole traders register for Self Assessment once self-employed income passes £1,000 in a tax year; companies register with Companies House before trading (director, shareholders or guarantors, people with significant control, articles, registered office, SIC code), then Corporation Tax; directors and people with significant control verify their identity; VAT registration once taxable turnover over 12 months passes £90,000.

**EU:** use Your Europe's country page and the country's Point of Single Contact; structure, registration and VAT rules are national.

**India, Canada, Australia:** point to the official portal only: Udyam Registration (India), the Government of Canada's starting a business guide (Canada), business.gov.au and its ABN page (Australia). Give no detail from memory.

### 4. Licences and permits

List federal or national licences for the activity, then state or regional, then local (city or county) ones. Note renewal dates. Ask the licensing body when unclear.

### 5. Tax set-up flags

Note how the owner will pay tax on profits (for example US estimated tax if $1,000 or more is expected to be owed; UK Self Assessment), sales tax or VAT, and record-keeping (see the bookkeeper kit). These are flags for the accountant, not rulings.

### 6. First-hire readiness

- **Status:** is this an employee or a contractor? US: behavioural control, financial control, relationship. UK: employment status, and the off-payroll rules where a contractor works through their own company. Check this with an accountant or lawyer.
- **Cost:** pay plus employer taxes, insurance, pension or benefits, equipment and time to train; check it against the 13-week forecast.
- **US steps:** EIN, state tax IDs, W-4, Form I-9, new-hire report to the state, workers' compensation, state unemployment insurance, payroll, posters, records.
- **UK steps:** register as an employer with HMRC before the first payday and set up PAYE; minimum wage; right-to-work check before the start date (keep the record for two years after employment ends); written statement on day one; Employers' Liability insurance; workplace pension duties from the first employee.
- **Other countries:** the official employer guidance for the country.
Hiring process detail is in the HR specialist kit; payroll runs in the payroll kit.

### 7. Order the checklist

Sequence by dependency and deadline: structure decision, name checks, registration, tax IDs, bank account (the owner opens it), licences, insurance, tax and records set-up, then hiring steps. Each row: step, why, official link, who does it (owner, accountant, lawyer, me at level 2), deadline, check-with note, status.

### 8. Prepare filings and ask

For each filing, prepare the answers and documents at level 2, list what the owner must confirm, and ask for a fresh yes. The owner or their authorised adviser submits through their own login. Log what was prepared and when.

## Checks

- Every step links to the official page for the owner's country, with the date checked or "not checked live".
- Rules that changed recently (US beneficial ownership, UK identity verification) reflect the official regulator's current page.
- No structure, tax or employment-status ruling; each is marked "check with an accountant or lawyer".
- Nothing outside the US, UK and EU is described beyond the official portal.
- Every filing and job offer is marked "fresh yes", with the owner or adviser submitting.
- Hiring steps come before the start date, not after.
- The checklist ends with: *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.*

## Output format

1. Mode and date line (checked live, or kit facts as of a date).
2. Structure options table with the questions for the adviser.
3. Set-up checklist table: step, why, official link, who, deadline, check-with, status.
4. Licences list with renewal dates.
5. First-hire readiness table, if hiring.
6. Filings prepared and awaiting a fresh yes.

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real business: a sole trader in England starting a catering business and hiring a part-time kitchen assistant in March. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> **Mode:** official pages checked live, 6 Nov 2026.
>
> | Step | Who | Deadline | Check with |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Register for Self Assessment (income will pass £1,000) | Owner, online | Before the first tax return is due | Accountant |
> | Ask the local council about food business registration and hygiene checks | Owner | Well before trading (ask for its lead time) | Council food team |
> | Watch turnover against the £90,000 VAT threshold monthly | Me (L1), from bank exports | Ongoing | Accountant |
> | Register as an employer and set up PAYE | Owner or accountant | Before the first payday | Accountant |
> | Right-to-work check for the assistant | Owner | Before the start date | None; follow GOV.UK |
> | Written statement of main terms | Me (L2 draft) | Day one | Lawyer or HR adviser |
> | Employers' Liability insurance | Owner | Before the start date | Broker |
> | Workplace pension: check eligibility (age 22+, £10,000+) | Me (L1) | From the first employee's start | Accountant |
>
> **Fresh yes needed:** every registration above and the job offer.
>
> *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.*
