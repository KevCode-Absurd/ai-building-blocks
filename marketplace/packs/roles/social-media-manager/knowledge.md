# Social media manager: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. Platform facts come only from the platforms' own pages, and each says when it was true. Platforms change ranking and formats often, so this kit is reviewed every 14 days.

## 1. What this role is for

A small business hires a social media manager to show up steadily where its customers already spend time, sound like itself while doing it, and turn attention into enquiries, bookings and sales. The other half of the job is the conversation: answering comments and messages quickly and well, and spotting trouble early.

Who hires it: local shops, cafés, salons, studios and clinics; online shops; coaches, consultants and creators; B2B firms whose buyers are on LinkedIn; any owner who posts in bursts and then goes quiet for months.

The work covers four jobs:

- **Planning:** content pillars, a calendar, and a rhythm the owner can keep with the time and camera confidence they have.
- **Writing posts:** captions, carousels, text posts and threads, bios and pinned posts, written for each platform rather than pasted across all of them.
- **Short video:** ideas, hooks, shot lists, on-screen text and batch shoot plans for Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, plus edit notes from a transcript or description.
- **Community:** replies to comments and messages in the brand's voice, with risky ones held for a person, and holding statements when something goes wrong.

What good work looks like:

- Every post has one point, a first line that earns the next, and one clear ask.
- The content is original: filmed, photographed or written by the business, not reposted.
- The plan fits the owner's real week, so posting does not stop in month two.
- Replies are short, human and accurate, never promise what the business has not agreed, and move private matters to private messages.
- Results are read by shares, saves, sends, profile visits, clicks, enquiries and sales, and by reach to non-followers, more than by likes.

How it is judged: steady output; growth in reach beyond current followers; actions that lead to business (link clicks, messages, bookings, orders); reply speed and tone; and no avoidable incidents (undisclosed ads, unchecked claims, public arguments).

**This agent posts only with level 3 access.** It plans, drafts and checks. With level 3 access it publishes approved posts and sends routine replies within the limits the client set, and logs each one. Otherwise the client, or a scheduling tool the client controls, publishes every post and sends every reply.

## 2. The canon

**Jonah Berger, *Contagious: Why Things Catch On* (Simon & Schuster, 2013) [27].** Berger studied why some ideas spread by word of mouth and sets out six principles, often remembered as STEPPS. In my own words: people share what makes them look good to others (social currency); what everyday cues bring to mind (triggers); what stirs a strong feeling, especially high-energy ones (emotion); what is visible to others (public); what is useful enough to pass on (practical value); and ideas carried inside a story. For social posts this becomes a question to ask of every draft: why would someone send this to a friend?

**Gary Vaynerchuk, *Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook* (HarperBusiness, 2013) [29].** Give value many times (the jabs) before you ask for anything (the right hook), and make content native to each platform: what works on one network, in its format and culture, often fails when pasted onto another. The book's platform details have dated; the two principles have not.

**Les Binet and Peter Field, *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA, 2013) [28].** Their analysis of advertising effectiveness data warns about the tension between short-term response and long-term brand building, and about using very short-term online metrics as the main measure of success. For social, that means likes and views are not the goal; being remembered and chosen when the customer is ready to buy is.

**The platforms' own explanations.** Social is unusual: the platforms publish a lot about how they rank. Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, explained Instagram's ranking signals in 2023 [1]; Meta publishes system cards for Facebook Feed and Threads [8][9]; X publishes its ranking code [20]; LinkedIn's engineers describe their feed [18]; TikTok and YouTube explain their recommendation systems [11][16]. Read these first-hand. Third-party "algorithm hacks" are usually guesses.

**Disclosure guidance as the honesty canon.** The FTC's guide for influencers [21], the ASA and CMA's influencers' guide [23] and the European Commission's Influencer Legal Hub [24] set the standard for making clear when a post is an ad.

## 3. How the work is done now

### What the ranking systems have in common (as of Nov 2026)

- **They predict what each person will do.** Meta's Facebook Feed card lists predictions such as whether you will scroll past, engage, open, or click a link and stay [8]. Threads predicts likes, replies, profile taps and time spent [9]. X's published code predicts the probability of likes, replies, reposts, shares, clicks, dwell time and follows, and of negative actions such as "not interested", mute, block and report, then adds them up with weights [20]. LinkedIn predicts clicks, skips, long dwells, likes, comments and shares [18]. TikTok gives each video a prediction score for how likely a person is to interact with it [11].
- **Negative signals count.** Scrolling past, hiding, "not interested", muting and reporting push a post down for that person and similar people [8][9][20].
- **Recommendations to non-followers matter.** Instagram Explore and Reels, TikTok's For You feed, Threads, X's For You and LinkedIn all mix in posts from accounts people do not follow [1][9][11][18][20]. Followers alone no longer decide reach.
- **Originality is rewarded and copying is punished.** Instagram is less likely to recommend reposts, watermarked content and accounts that mostly re-share others' work [1][2]; Meta says 75% of Instagram recommendations in the US came from original posts by Q4 2025 [4]. Facebook announced cuts to reach and monetisation for repeated reuse in July 2025, rolled out over the following months, and tightened its definition of original in March 2026 [5][6]. TikTok may remove unoriginal content from the For You feed [12].
- **Bait is demoted.** Facebook demotes engagement bait and clickbait links [7]. "Comment YES if you agree" style posts work against you.

### Platform by platform

**Instagram.** Each surface ranks differently. In Feed, the strongest signals are your activity, information about the post, information about the poster and your history with them; for Reels, your activity and your history with the poster come first, then the reel itself and the poster [1]. Since January 2025, US creators can post reels up to 3 minutes and those reels can be recommended to non-followers; "trial reels" let you test a reel with non-followers before showing it to followers; adding audio to photo posts and carousels can place them in the Reels tab [3]. The creators' tips page says reels of 3 minutes or less can be recommended, and to use Collab posts and searchable words in captions, bios and hashtags [2]. Account Status in the app shows whether an account is eligible for recommendations [1]. Meta's Edits app is now a real share of production: nearly 10% of daily Reels views came from content made in Edits in Q4 2025 [4]. *Hashtags:* Instagram describes them as a way to make content searchable [2]. In December 2025, trade press reported a cap of five hashtags per post; I could not confirm this from an Instagram page I could open, so check the limit in the app.

**Facebook.** Feed gathers posts from friends, followed Pages and groups, narrows them to about 500 with a lightweight model, then scores each one by predicted actions and varies formats so people do not see several videos in a row (card updated 6 October 2026) [8]. People can switch to a chronological Following view [8]. Meta's own caption advice for creators (July 2025): no links in captions, minimal capitals, and no more than five hashtags tend to perform best [5]. Original means content the account filmed or produced itself; reaction, watch-along and stitched reposts without real added value are deprioritised, and so are re-uploads with borders, captions or speed changes [6].

**Threads.** The feed blends followed and recommended posts and ranks them on predicted likes, replies, profile taps and time spent, with a chronological Following view available (card updated 7 March 2025) [9]. Meta reports a 20% lift in Threads time spent from Q4 2025 changes [4]. Replies are one of the predicted actions, so write posts people can answer.

**TikTok.** The For You feed scores each video on predicted interaction, runs a final similarity check on the top-ranked videos before recommending them, and makes content unsuitable for a broad audience ineligible [11]. Unoriginal content (copied, mostly repurposed without creative edits, or low-value compilations) may be removed from the For You feed [12]. In the US, a joint venture set up under a September 2025 executive order is now responsible for US user data, the app and the algorithm [14]; news reports say the recommendation system will be retrained on US data, so watch US reach for shifts.

**YouTube Shorts.** Since 15 October 2024, square or vertical videos up to 3 minutes count as Shorts [15]. A Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim is blocked worldwide and cannot be recommended, so use licensed or library music on longer Shorts [15]. Recommendations draw on viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and satisfaction surveys, and on channel reputation [16].

**LinkedIn.** Since a March 2026 rebuild, LinkedIn's feed finds posts with large language model embeddings that match posts to members by topic and meaning, then ranks them with a model of each member's past interactions that predicts clicks, skips, long dwells, likes, comments and shares; posts can reach people outside your network if they match their professional interests, and can surface within minutes [18]. For company Pages, LinkedIn recommends posting at least weekly, using images and video, 3 to 5 relevant hashtags, and notifying employees of key posts no more than once a week [19]. Many small B2B firms also plan posts for the founder or staff to publish from their own profiles; each person posts and approves their own words.

**X.** The For You feed draws on posts from accounts you follow and on recommended posts, which are discounted against followed ones; it predicts positive actions (likes, replies, reposts, quotes, shares, clicks, dwell, follows) and negative ones (not interested, mute, block, report); repeat posts from the same author are decayed [20]. Updates in October 2026 trained the system to predict time spent and to surface posts people read through [20]. Write posts that are worth reading to the end and that invite real replies.

### Formats and limits

Formats and length limits change several times a year. This kit gives only limits confirmed on official pages: Instagram reels up to 3 minutes can be recommended [2][3]; YouTube Shorts up to 3 minutes [15]. For everything else (caption length, carousel slide count, video length on TikTok, character limits on X, Threads and LinkedIn), check the app on the day and write to that limit.

### How the work runs week to week

1. **Pillars.** Two to four recurring themes, each with a job: attract (useful or entertaining for strangers), teach, prove (results, reviews, behind the scenes), sell. Offers are a minority of posts.
2. **Calendar.** Posts by platform, pillar, format, hook, point and ask, sized to the owner's real time.
3. **Batch production.** Group video ideas by location, outfit and props so one session gives weeks of posts.
4. **Drafting.** One point per post. A first line that stands alone. One idea per line. One ask. Platform-native format.
5. **Approval and posting.** The client approves, then posts or schedules in their own tools, or the agent does it at level 3 within the limits.
6. **Community.** Replies daily or on the client's schedule; risky messages held for a person.
7. **Review.** Monthly read of the last 30 posts by shares, saves, sends, profile visits, clicks and non-follower reach.

### Ads, endorsements and honesty (US, UK, EU)

- **US:** disclose any material connection (payment, free product, discount, employment, family) in the post itself, hard to miss, in plain words such as "ad" or "sponsored"; in videos, say it and show it; platform labels may not be enough on their own [21]. The FTC's 2024 rule bans buying or selling fake indicators of influence, such as bot followers or views, to misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose, and bans fake reviews including AI-generated ones [22].
- **UK:** the ASA and CMA published the fourth edition of their influencers' guide on 23 October 2026, covering when content is an ad, affiliate links, labelling, fake reviews and CMA powers under the DMCC Act [23].
- **EU:** consumer law requires brand partnerships and affiliate marketing to be disclosed; the Commission's Influencer Legal Hub explains how [24].
- **Platform tools:** TikTok's commercial content disclosure setting labels a post "Promotional content" (your own brand) or "Paid partnership" (another brand), and the label cannot be changed after posting [13]. Use the platform's label and a plain-words disclosure.
- **AI-made content:** YouTube requires creators to disclose realistic altered or synthetic content [17]; Meta labels AI-generated content as "AI info" when it detects it or people disclose it [10]. In the EU, the Commission's AI Act page gives August 2026 as the start of the transparency rules, which include labelling deepfakes; a voluntary code of practice and Commission guidelines (July 2026) explain how [25].

This is general guidance, not legal advice. For a specific campaign, the client should check the official guidance for each country or ask a qualified adviser.

### Community management

Sort every comment and message: praise, question, complaint, spam, risk. Reply briefly and warmly, with a detail from the comment. Answer the same question the same way every time, from a fact sheet the client approves. Move anything personal (orders, health, accounts) to a private message in one line, and never ask for payment details or personal data in public. Hold safety, legal, medical, press and angry threads for a person, with a holding reply. Do not argue; state one fact or one next step. Never promise refunds, dates or fixes the client has not agreed.

### AI in the work

AI is useful for turning one idea into platform versions, drafting hooks and captions, sorting comments, and reading a stats export. It must not invent stories, results, reviews, offers or trends, must not pretend to have watched a video it has not seen, and cannot check what is trending live. Every draft needs the client's eye before it goes out.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Use these to frame expectations, never as targets.

- **Where US adults are:** YouTube 84% and Facebook 70% of US adults say they use them, the two most widely used platforms (Pew, survey of 5,511 adults, January to June 2026, published 19 October 2026) [26]. *Caveat:* US only; use counts, not time spent or buying intent. Check Ofcom or national statistics for the UK and EU.
- **Original content on Instagram:** 75% of US Instagram recommendations came from original posts in Q4 2025, up 10 percentage points [4]. *Caveat:* Meta's own figure.
- **Original Reels on Facebook:** views and watch time of original Reels roughly doubled in the second half of 2025 compared with the same period in 2024 [6]. *Caveat:* Meta's own figure, across all creators.
- **LinkedIn Pages:** weekly posting doubles engagement; one image roughly doubles comments; video gets 5 times and live video 24 times the engagement; complete Pages get 30% more weekly views [19]. *Caveat:* LinkedIn's own, undated figures; "engagement" is not defined.
- **Disclosure gap:** the Commission's hub cites a 2025 study that found only 5.4% of influencer posts were labelled as advertising [24]. *Caveat:* cited by the Commission, study details not on the page.

No official source gives a trustworthy "good engagement rate" for small businesses. Vendor benchmark reports vary in method and sample. Use the client's own last 30 to 90 posts as the baseline.

## 5. Decision rules

- If a post's main purpose is to be shared, write it so the reader looks good or helps a friend by sending it, because sharing follows social currency and practical value [27].
- If content was made by someone else, do not repost it; remix, react with real added value, or credit through a Collab post with permission, because reposts and watermarked clips are demoted [1][2][5][6][12].
- If a video carries another app's watermark, re-export a clean version before posting [1][5].
- If a caption is for Facebook, keep links out of the caption and keep hashtags few, because Meta's own caption advice says so [5].
- If a business has limited time, pick one or two platforms where its buyers are and post steadily there, rather than thinly everywhere.
- If the audience is B2B and on LinkedIn, write posts worth reading to the end and worth a real comment, because LinkedIn's ranking predicts long dwells and comments [18]; post on the Page at least weekly [19].
- If a longer Short or reel uses music, use the platform's licensed library within its limits, because a claimed Short over 1 minute is blocked [15].
- If a post is paid for, gifted or affiliated, disclose it in the post and use the platform's label [13][21][23][24].
- If content made or changed with AI looks real, use the platform's AI disclosure [10][17][25].
- If the client wants follower numbers fast, refuse bought followers and engagement pods, because buying fake indicators can break US law [22] and fake engagement trains the ranking systems wrongly.
- If a comment raises safety, legal, medical, privacy or press issues, hold it for the client and post only a holding reply.
- If a post underperforms, change one thing next time (hook, format, length or topic), not five, so you can learn.
- If the client asks the agent to post or schedule, check the access level first: at level 3, post only the approved posts within the limits and log each one; at level 1 or 2, hand over ready-to-post files and a checklist. Never take a password in chat; connect through the platform's official connector or a scheduler the client controls.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Posting the same thing everywhere.** Spot it: a landscape video with a TikTok watermark on Instagram, or hashtags pasted onto LinkedIn.
- **Chasing likes.** Spot it: monthly reports with likes and follower count only, no shares, saves, sends, clicks or enquiries [28].
- **Engagement bait.** Spot it: "Comment YES", "Tag 3 friends", "Like if you agree" [7].
- **Reposting other people's content.** Spot it: a feed of memes and clips the business did not make [2][6].
- **Bursts and silence.** Spot it: ten posts in a week, then nothing for a month.
- **No point.** Spot it: you cannot say what the post wants the reader to think or do.
- **Weak first line.** Spot it: the post opens with "Happy Monday!" or the business name.
- **Undisclosed ads.** Spot it: gifted products or affiliate links with no "ad" label [21][23].
- **Arguing in public.** Spot it: a reply thread that grows longer and angrier.
- **Promising in replies.** Spot it: "We'll refund you today" with no approval from the owner.
- **Bought followers or pods.** Spot it: a sudden jump in followers from unrelated countries, or the same handful of accounts commenting on every post [22].
- **Invented trends.** Spot it: "This sound is trending" with no check in the app that day.

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before handing over

1. Does each post have one point and one ask, and could a stranger say what it is about from the first line?
2. Is it native to the platform: format, length, tone and caption style? Limits checked in the app?
3. Is it original, with no other app's watermark and nothing reposted without permission?
4. Are all facts, prices, dates, offers and results confirmed by the client?
5. Are paid, gifted or affiliate links disclosed in the post and with the platform's label?
6. Is any realistic AI-made or AI-altered content disclosed?
7. Does it avoid engagement bait and fake urgency?
8. Does the video have captions, and do images have alt text where the platform allows?
9. For replies: short, accurate, no promises the client has not approved, private matters moved to messages, risky ones held?
10. Is there a measure: what this post should earn (shares, saves, clicks, messages) and when it will be read?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Content plan:* pillars with their jobs, platforms and why, rhythm per week, and a 30-day calendar (date, platform, pillar, format, hook, point, ask, asset needed).
- *Post pack:* for each post, the first line, the full caption or text, on-screen text or slide copy, hashtags if used, alt text, disclosure if needed, and facts to confirm.
- *Video brief:* hook (first line and first shot), shot list, on-screen text, script lines, caption, length, and a batch shoot order.
- *Reply sheet:* a table of comment, category, draft reply, flag; plus a fact sheet of approved answers.
- *Monthly review:* top and bottom posts by shares, saves, sends, clicks and non-follower reach, what they share, what to do more and less of.

**Tone and conventions.** Sound like the business, not like marketing. Learn the voice from posts the client likes. First lines that stand alone. One idea per line. Plain words, short sentences, numbers as numerals. Emojis only if the brand uses them. No "Happy Monday" openers, no rage bait, no begging for likes.

## 9. Glossary

- **Feed:** the main scrolling stream of posts in an app.
- **For You feed:** a mostly recommended stream chosen by the platform, as on TikTok and X [11][20].
- **Recommendation:** a post shown to someone who does not follow the account [1].
- **Non-follower reach:** how many people outside your followers saw a post.
- **Ranking signal:** a piece of information the system uses to order posts, such as past interactions with the poster [1].
- **Prediction:** the system's estimate of how likely a person is to take an action on a post [8][20].
- **Negative feedback:** actions such as hide, not interested, mute or scroll past that push a post down [8][20].
- **Original content:** content the account filmed, photographed, wrote or produced itself [6].
- **Engagement bait:** asking for likes, comments or shares as the point of the post; demoted on Facebook [7].
- **Sends (shares to messages):** when someone sends your post to a friend privately.
- **Saves:** when someone bookmarks your post to come back to it.
- **Dwell time:** how long someone spends on a post [18][20].
- **Content pillar:** a recurring theme with a job in the plan, such as teach or prove.
- **Hook:** the first line or first seconds that make someone stop and keep going.
- **Carousel:** a post of several images or slides people swipe through.
- **Trial reel:** an Instagram reel shown first to non-followers as a test [3].
- **Collab post:** an Instagram post shared by two accounts at once, crediting both [2].
- **Account Status:** Instagram's in-app page showing whether your content can be recommended [1].
- **Paid partnership label:** a platform label marking a post as sponsored by another brand [13].
- **Material connection:** any link between a poster and a brand (payment, free product, job, family) that must be disclosed [21].
- **Holding statement:** a short public reply that acknowledges an issue while the business finds out more.
- **Content ID claim:** YouTube's automatic copyright match, which blocks Shorts over 1 minute [15].

## 12. Defaults to avoid

An AI model with no context falls back on these choices, so they make work look like everyone else's. Avoid each one unless the client's captured brand or the agreed plan chooses it on purpose, and then say why. The full shared list is in tools/ai-default-patterns.md.

- **Format sameness.** Every post in the same template, layout and length on every platform. Make each post native to its platform (section 7) and vary the format by the post's job.
- **One hook formula reused.** Every first line opens the same way, with "Stop scrolling", a question or a stat. Write each first line to stand alone for that post's one point.
- **Stock hype words.** Captions with "Unlock", "Elevate", "Seamless", "Game-changer" or "In today's fast-paced world". Sound like the business, not like marketing (section 8).
- **Rule-of-three everywhere.** Captions built as three bullets or three benefits by habit. One idea per line, one point per post.
- **Emoji icons.** Emoji as bullets and decoration by default. Use emojis only if the brand does (section 8).
- **Fake urgency.** "Last chance" or "Don't miss out" without a real deadline. Section 7 already checks for it; use real dates.
- **Benefits any competitor could claim.** Posts that would fit any business in the niche. Use the client's own facts, people and work.
- **Generic stock-style images.** Stock photos in place of the client's own pictures and video. Use real assets, or brief a shoot.
