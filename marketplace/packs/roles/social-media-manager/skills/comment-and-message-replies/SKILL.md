# Comment and message replies

Comments and messages are where followers become customers, and where small problems become public ones. Good replies are quick, short, human and accurate. This skill drafts them, sends the routine ones when the client grants level 3 within set limits, and decides which ones a person must handle.

## When to use

- A backlog of comments and messages to answer.
- The same ten questions arriving every week (prices, stock, delivery, booking, opening hours).
- A complaint, a rumour, an outage or a bad review getting attention.
- Writing a community and comment policy for the first time.

## What you need

- **The comments and messages,** pasted as text with the post they belong to. Remove personal details that are not needed (phone numbers, order numbers, addresses).
- **Voice:** three replies or posts the client likes, and words to avoid.
- **A fact sheet,** or the facts to build one: prices, opening hours, delivery times, booking steps, returns policy, contact routes. Only what the client confirms.
- **Escalation contacts:** who handles complaints, refunds, legal, press, safety and health questions, and how fast.
- **Anything off limits:** topics the business will not discuss publicly, and current issues (a delay, a recall, a closure).

**Access:** level 1 to read comments and messages; level 2 to leave replies as drafts in the inbox; level 3 to send routine replies from the approved fact sheet and hide spam, within your limits. Complaints, refunds and risky items always go to the person you named.

## Steps

1. **Sort every item.**
   - *Praise:* thank, with a detail from their comment.
   - *Question:* answer from the fact sheet, or say who will answer and when.
   - *Complaint:* acknowledge, apologise for the experience where fitting, move to private messages for details, no admissions of legal fault and no promises.
   - *Spam or abuse:* recommend hide, delete or report under the community policy; do not engage.
   - *Risk:* safety, injury, health or medical questions, legal threats, discrimination, press, anything involving a child, threats, or a post going viral. Hold for a person.
2. **Draft short replies.** One to three lines. Use their name or a detail from their comment. One fact or one next step. Match the brand's voice and the platform's tone.
3. **Move private matters to messages.** One public line ("Sorry to hear this, Sam. I've sent you a message so we can sort it out.") and the private message itself. Never ask for card numbers, passwords or full addresses in public, and only ask for what is needed in private.
4. **Never promise what is not approved.** No refunds, replacements, dates, discounts or fixes unless the client has approved them for this case. Write "[CLIENT: approve refund?]" where a decision is needed.
5. **Do not argue.** If someone is wrong on a fact, state the correct fact once, kindly, with a source if there is one. Do not reply a second time to bait.
6. **Write holding statements for a bad moment.** A short public reply that says you know, you are looking into it, and when you will update; a pinned post or story if it affects many people; a private message template. No speculation about causes. The client approves every word, even at level 3.
7. **Build or update the fact sheet** from repeat questions, for the client to confirm.
8. **Write or update the community policy:** what you remove (spam, abuse, hate, personal data, scams), what you reply to, response times, and how to escalate. Plain words; the client publishes it if they choose.
9. **Spot patterns.** List repeat questions (content ideas), repeat complaints (business problems to pass on), and any account that needs reporting.

## Checks

- Every reply is short, accurate and in the brand's voice.
- No promise of refunds, dates, discounts or fixes without approval.
- No request for payment or personal details in public.
- Risky items are held, with a holding reply drafted, not answered in full.
- No arguing, no sarcasm, no blaming the customer.
- Facts come from the approved fact sheet; anything else is marked to confirm.
- Replies go out only at level 3 and only within the limits; every reply sent is in the action log, and every held item went to the client.

## Output format

1. **Reply table:** comment or message, category, draft reply, flag (send, hold, client decision needed), notes.
2. **Held items:** why each is held, who should handle it, a holding reply.
3. **Private message drafts** where needed.
4. **Fact sheet updates** for the client to confirm.
5. **Patterns:** repeat questions, repeat complaints, accounts to report.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: an online candle shop. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

| Comment | Category | Draft reply | Flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Do you ship to Ireland?" | Question | "We do, Aoife. Delivery to Ireland usually takes 3 to 5 working days (client to confirm). Everything's in the shipping page linked in our bio." | Send after fact check |
| "Ordered 2 weeks ago, nothing. Scam?" | Complaint | "Sorry about the wait, Tom. That's not the experience we want. I've sent you a message so we can track it down today." | Send; move to DM |
| "My candle flame was huge and the jar cracked." | Risk (safety) | Holding reply: "Thank you for telling us, Rhea. Please stop using the candle. We've sent you a message and the owner will contact you personally today." | Hold for owner |
| "Love the fig one!!" | Praise | "So glad. Fig is the one we burn in the studio too." | Send |

**Private message (order delay, draft):** "Hi Tom, thanks for your patience. Could you send your order number here? We'll check with the courier and update you by 5pm today (client to confirm). We won't ask for card details."

**Pattern:** three shipping questions this week, so add delivery times to the bio link page and post a short "how shipping works" story.
