# Short video scripts

Short video now reaches people who do not follow you, on every major platform. The ranking systems watch what people do: whether they keep watching, share, save or swipe away. A small business wins with original, specific videos it can make again and again, not with copied trends. This skill plans and scripts those videos. It cannot watch video or check live trends, and it uploads or schedules videos only with level 2 or 3 access.

## When to use

- A month of video ideas for Reels, TikTok and Shorts.
- A batch shoot plan so one session covers several weeks.
- Edit notes for footage the client describes or transcribes.
- Turning a post, blog or FAQ into short videos.

## What you need

- **The business and buyer:** what it sells, to whom, and the questions customers ask most.
- **On camera:** who will appear, or whether it is hands, products and places only.
- **Places, props and time:** where filming can happen, what is visual (a process, a before and after, a reveal), and how long the session can be.
- **Proof the client can show:** real results, real reviews with permission, real process.
- **Past videos:** what did well and badly, by shares, saves, watch time and non-follower reach, if the client has insights.
- **Music:** whether the account will use the platform's licensed library (needed on many business accounts) or original sound.

**Access:** level 1 to read past video insights. Scripts need no access. Level 2 or 3 applies only if I also upload or schedule the finished videos.

## Steps

1. **Pick four repeatable formats.** Formats make filming easy and teach the audience what to expect. Common ones for small businesses:
   - *Demo:* how the product or service works, close up.
   - *Myth fix:* "You've been told X. Here's what actually happens."
   - *Process or day-in-the-life:* the making, the prep, the behind the scenes.
   - *Before and after:* only if real and permissioned.
   - *Answer a question:* one customer question per video, on screen.
   - *List:* three mistakes, three tips, three things we never do.
2. **Write the hook for each idea.** Two parts: the first line (spoken or on screen) and the first shot. The first second should show the subject, not a logo or an intro. Write three hook options and pick one. The hook must be paid off in the video; bait-and-switch hooks drive people away.
3. **Write the shot list.** Numbered shots with framing (close, medium, wide), what happens, and roughly how long. Vertical framing. Keep important text and faces away from the edges where app buttons sit.
4. **Write on-screen text and script lines.** Short lines that make sense with the sound off. One idea per video. End with one ask that fits (follow for part two, save, visit, book).
5. **Write the caption.** Short, with words people would search for. Disclosure if paid, gifted or affiliated. AI disclosure if any realistic footage or voice was made or changed with AI.
6. **Set the length.** As long as the idea needs and no longer. Instagram can recommend reels up to 3 minutes and YouTube counts vertical videos up to 3 minutes as Shorts; check other limits in the app. On YouTube, a Short over 1 minute with a copyright claim on its music is blocked, so use library music within its limits or original sound.
7. **Plan the batch shoot.** Group ideas by location, outfit and props; order shots so set-ups change as little as possible; list props and a shot checklist; plan B-roll that can fill several videos.
8. **Edit notes (from a transcript or description).** Mark the strongest opening line and move it to the start; cut pauses and repeated points; mark where on-screen text appears; note the best frame for a cover; keep captions on. Say clearly that the notes come from the description, not from watching the video.
9. **Set the measures.** For each video: shares, saves, average watch time or completion, non-follower reach, and the business action (profile visits, clicks, messages). Change one thing at a time between versions.

## Checks

- Every idea is original to the business; no other creator's footage, no other app's watermark.
- Each hook is paid off, and the subject appears in the first second.
- On-screen text works with the sound off; captions are on.
- Claims, results and before-and-afters are real and permissioned.
- Music is licensed for the account or original.
- Disclosure and AI labels planned where needed.
- The batch fits the time the client has.
- The skill says what it could not do (watch video, check live trends).

## Output format

1. **Formats:** four formats with a one-line description each.
2. **Idea table:** number, format, hook line, first shot, point, length, ask.
3. **Scripts:** for each idea, the shot list, on-screen text, script lines and caption.
4. **Batch shoot plan:** set-ups in order, props, shot checklist.
5. **Measures** and what to change next round.
6. **Facts and permissions to confirm.**

## Example of good output (short)

*Brightwell Bikes is a made-up business used only to show the format: a bike repair shop whose mechanic will show his hands but not his face. All details are invented.*

**Formats:** myth fix; 30-second fix; what we found inside; customer question.

| # | Format | Hook line | First shot | Point | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Myth fix | "More air doesn't make you faster on these roads." | Thumb pressing a rock-hard tyre | Lower pressure grips better on broken tarmac | Save for your next ride |
| 2 | What we found inside | "This came out of a bottom bracket last week." | Gloved hand opening on a pile of grit | Why winter riding needs a spring service | Book a spring service (client to confirm price) |

**Script, idea 1**

1. Close: thumb presses a hard tyre (1 second). On screen: "More air ≠ faster."
2. Close: gauge reading (2 seconds). On screen: "What most people run."
3. Medium: wheel rolling over a pothole (3 seconds). Voice: "On rough roads, a rock-hard tyre bounces instead of gripping."
4. Close: letting air out, gauge again (3 seconds). On screen: "Try a little less. Check the range printed on your tyre."
5. Close: the tyre sidewall with the pressure range (2 seconds). On screen: "Save this for Saturday."

Caption: "Tyre pressure for rough city roads, explained in 12 seconds. Check the range on your own tyre's sidewall."

*Why this works:* the subject is in the first second, the hook is paid off, it works with the sound off, and the advice points to the tyre's own printed range rather than inventing a number.
