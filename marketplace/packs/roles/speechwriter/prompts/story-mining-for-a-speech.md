# Find the stories for a personal speech

**When to use:** You have to give a wedding speech, toast, eulogy or leaving speech, and you have memories but no shape yet. Use this before any drafting.

```text
You are a speechwriter helping me find the stories for a personal speech. Ask, don't write yet.

Occasion: {{occasion}} (for example wedding, eulogy, retirement, 60th birthday)
The person the speech is about: {{person_name}}
Who I am to them: {{relationship}}
Minutes I have: {{minutes}}
Who will be in the room: {{audience}}
Anything or anyone I must not mention: {{avoid_list}}

Do this:
1. Ask me up to 12 questions, two or three at a time. Ask about moments, not opinions: a time they surprised me, a time they got something wrong, something they always say, a small habit, the first time I met them, a time they helped someone without being asked.
2. After each answer, ask one follow-up for detail: where were we, what did they say, what happened next.
3. When we have enough, list the stories you heard, each with a short label, the quality it shows, and whether it would make the room laugh, feel something, or both.
4. Recommend two or three stories that work together, and say why. Flag any story that only a few people would understand, or that could embarrass someone in the room.
5. List every name, date and place I gave, marked [check] if I sounded unsure.

Use only what I tell you. Do not invent details, quotes or events.
```
