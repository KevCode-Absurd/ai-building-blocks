# Interview: Speechwriter

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you give me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the expert's work.

## The work and the clients

1. **What kinds of speeches do you write most: personal occasions, business talks, pitches, remarks? Who asks you?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and the speakers it serves.
2. **What does a nervous first-time speaker usually need from you that has nothing to do with the words?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert handles confidence, not just copy.
3. **How do you learn how someone talks before you write for them?**
   Why it matters: voice capture is what makes a speech sound like the speaker.

## Finding the material

4. **Walk me through your first conversation with a client. Which questions get the best stories?**
   Why it matters: the expert's interview questions become the agent's.
5. **How do you choose two or three stories from twenty? What makes you drop a good one?**
   Why it matters: selection is the core judgement in personal speeches.
6. **What do you do when a client has no stories, only adjectives?**
   Why it matters: thin briefs are common, and the fix is a method.
7. **How do you check that a story is true and that people named in it are happy to be mentioned?**
   Why it matters: protects the speaker and the room from embarrassment.

## Structure and writing

8. **Show me a speech you're proud of. Why does the ending work?**
   Why it matters: a real example teaches structure and closing better than rules.
9. **How do you write differently for the ear than for the page?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's specific habits for spoken language.
10. **How do you handle humour in a room with children, grandparents and a boss?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's humour boundaries.
11. **What's different about writing a eulogy? What do you leave out?**
    Why it matters: eulogies need care the agent must copy exactly.
12. **For pitches, what's the most common reason a pitch fails, and how do you fix it without rewriting everything?**
    Why it matters: turns the expert's pitch-fix judgement into a method.

## Timing and rehearsal

13. **How do you time a speech before the speaker has read it? What rate do you use, and why?**
    Why it matters: replaces a general figure with the expert's tested habit.
14. **What do you cut first when a speech runs long?**
    Why it matters: cut order is a repeatable decision.
15. **How do you rehearse with a speaker? What do you listen for?**
    Why it matters: rehearsal steps become the agent's plan.
16. **Which marks do you put in a delivery version, and how do you lay it out?**
    Why it matters: the format speakers find easiest to read under pressure.

## Access and the room

17. **When have captions, an interpreter or a livestream changed how you wrote a speech?**
    Why it matters: brings real accessibility practice into the agent's work.
18. **How do you brief a captioner or interpreter?**
    Why it matters: a good brief prevents errors on the day.

## Limits and hard jobs

19. **What will you not write, even if the client insists?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's boundaries (invented stories, cruel jokes, false claims).
20. **Have you written for someone at a listed company? How did you handle new figures?**
    Why it matters: market rules can make a speech a legal risk.
21. **Tell me about the hardest speech you've written. What went wrong, and what did you do?**
    Why it matters: real recovery stories teach judgement under pressure.
22. **How do you know a speech worked?**
    Why it matters: defines the measures the agent uses to judge its own work.
