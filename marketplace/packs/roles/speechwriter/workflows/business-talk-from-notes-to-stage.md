# Business talk or pitch: from notes to stage

**Trigger:** a speaking slot is confirmed (conference, meetup, award, launch, investor or buyer meeting), or a pitch keeps getting a no.

**Cadence:** once per talk. For a pitch, repeat steps 3, 5 and 6 after every round of feedback.

## Steps

1. **Intake and access (level 1).** Audience, setting, slot, the one thing they should remember or do, the speaker's notes and slides, and whether the speaker works for a listed company. Ask which access level the speaker grants for documents, slides and the event platform, and write it down.
2. **Settle the point and the case.** One sentence for the point; one true case with permission for any named client.
3. **Draft (skill: talk-or-pitch-script).** Talk: hook, story, up to three points, ask. Pitch: problem, solution, proof, ask, with a 60-second and a 3-minute version. Remarks: a few thanks by name, one story, the close.
   **Approval point:** the speaker approves the point, the case and every number before slides are changed.
4. **Market rules check.** If the speaker works for a listed company and the talk includes new figures, forecasts or deals, send the draft to investor relations or legal and wait for clearance. See the PR specialist kit for disclosure timing.
5. **Questions (prompt: likely-questions-and-answers).** Five likely questions, honest answers, "I don't know yet" where true.
6. **Slides and notes.** Hand slide changes to the presentation designer kit, or at level 2 add speaker notes and time marks to a copy of the deck; at level 3 update the live deck's notes within the files the speaker named.
7. **Access (skill: accessible-speech-and-captions).** Spoken descriptions of every meaningful slide, caption choice, and the captioner or interpreter brief.
8. **Time and rehearse (skill: timing-and-rehearsal-plan).** Timed read, cut to fit, Speaker Coach or Presenter View if useful, and a room or platform run. For large online events, schedule the dry run with the organiser.
9. **After the talk or pitch.** Collect feedback and every reason for a no. For a pitch fix, change the part that caused most noes and keep the rest. If the text or recording will be published, label it as prepared or as delivered and add captions.

## Outputs

- One-sentence point and chosen case.
- Script with time marks; 60-second and 3-minute pitch versions where needed.
- Speaker notes per slide.
- Likely questions and answers.
- Access plan and captioner brief.
- Timing sheet and rehearsal plan.
- After a pitch: the no-reasons table and the revised version.
- Log of every action taken in the speaker's tools.

## Needs the client's approval

- The point, the case and every number before slides change.
- Clearance from investor relations or legal for any new figures at a listed company.
- Live changes to the deck or event at level 3, within the limits set at the start.
- A fresh yes every time before the script, slides or recording are published, sent to press, or shared with someone new.
