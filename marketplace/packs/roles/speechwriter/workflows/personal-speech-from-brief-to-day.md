# Personal speech: from brief to the day

**Trigger:** someone asks for a wedding speech, toast, eulogy, retirement or leaving speech, or a written tribute.

**Cadence:** once per occasion. Allow time for at least one round of edits and four rehearsals; for a eulogy the time is often short, so compress the rounds but keep the read-aloud.

## Steps

1. **Intake and access (level 1).** Gather the occasion, slot, running order, who is in the room, the avoid list and the speaker's relationship to the person. Ask which access level the speaker grants for their documents and write it under "Access I have".
2. **Find the stories (prompt: story-mining-for-a-speech).** Ask about moments, not opinions. Log every name, date and place with [check] where unsure.
3. **Draft (skill: speech-from-stories).** Two or three stories, what each shows, the turn, the close. Word count and planned minutes on top.
   **Approval point:** the speaker reads the draft aloud and approves the stories, names and tone before any polishing. For a eulogy, the family approves what is included.
4. **Check the humour (prompt: humour-safety-check).** Keep, soften or cut each joke for a mixed room.
5. **Time and cut (skill: timing-and-rehearsal-plan; prompt: cut-to-time).** Use the speaker's timed read, add time for laughter, cut whole units, keep the close.
6. **Access (skill: accessible-speech-and-captions).** If anyone in the room is deaf or hard of hearing, or the event is streamed or recorded, agree captions with the organiser and send the brief (prompt: captioner-and-interpreter-brief).
7. **Delivery version and cue cards.** Large type, one thought per line, pause marks, story labels.
8. **Rehearse.** Alone aloud, timed, standing to a listener, and in the room if possible. Change any word the speaker trips on.
9. **Hand over.** At level 1, send the files for the speaker to print. At level 2, put them in a copy in the speaker's drive. At level 3, update the master version and share it with the people the speaker named (for example the officiant or the MC).
10. **After the day.** If the speech will be printed in an order of service or posted online, get the speaker's fresh yes on the final text, check any quoted poem or lyric for print or online use, and add a transcript or captions to any recording.

## Outputs

- Fact list and story list.
- Approved full script.
- Delivery version and cue cards.
- Timing sheet and cut list.
- Captioner or interpreter brief, if needed.
- Rehearsal plan and day-of checklist.
- Log of every action taken in the speaker's tools.

## Needs the client's approval

- The stories, names and tone, before polishing (the family, for a eulogy).
- The final script, after the speaker's read-aloud.
- Sharing files with anyone new, such as an officiant, MC or captioner.
- A fresh yes every time before publishing the text, a recording or a transcript under anyone's name.
