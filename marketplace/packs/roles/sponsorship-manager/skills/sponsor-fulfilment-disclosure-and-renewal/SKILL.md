# Sponsor fulfilment, disclosure and renewal

Sponsors renew when they can see that every promise was kept and the audience noticed. This skill tracks each promise to proof, makes sure every paid mention is labelled so it can't land the client or the sponsor in trouble, and turns the season's record into a report that opens the renewal conversation.

## When to use

- A sponsor has signed and the deliverables need tracking.
- Sponsored podcast reads, social posts, videos or newsletter slots are about to go out.
- A partnership has ended and logos or "partner" wording are still live.
- The season is ending and renewals are due.

Not for: legal rulings on whether content is an ad, contract changes, issuing refunds or credits, or tax advice. Ask the client's lawyer or a qualified accountant.

## What you need

**Access:** level 1 to read contracts, the deliverables schedule, analytics and past content. Level 2 to build the tracker, draft labelled content, the report, renewal proposal, make-good proposals and invoice drafts. Level 3 to update the tracker, publish approved sponsored posts and newsletter slots with the agreed labels, and send approved reports to the sponsor contact, within the client's limits. Refunds, credits above the client's limit, invoices and payment details stay with the client; a formal reply to a sponsor's complaint or claim needs a fresh yes.

- Signed contracts or the agreed deliverables schedule for each sponsor.
- Content calendars: events, episodes, issues, posts.
- Analytics and proof sources (host stats, newsletter reports, gate scans, photos).
- Sponsor scripts, talking points and creative, with any claims the sponsor wants made.
- Logo permissions and end dates.

## Steps

1. **Build the tracker.** One row per promise: sponsor, deliverable, quantity, due date, owner, status, proof link, notes. Pull every line from the contract schedule; flag anything vague ("social support") for the client to define.
2. **Set the disclosure wording before anything airs.**
   - *UK:* label paid content "Ad", "Advert", "Advertising" or "Ad feature" at the start; "Sponsored", "Supported by", "Gifted" or "Thanks to X" alone are not recommended; on YouTube, put the label in the title or thumbnail (ASA/CAP advice, 6 August 2026). Advertorials need a clear label such as "advertisement feature".
   - *US:* disclose in the same channel as the endorsement: say it in an audio read, show it in a video; "Sponsored by [brand]" is clearer than "Sponsored"; repeat in live streams; not only in the description (FTC).
   - *EU:* sponsored content must be made clear by images, words or sound.
   - *Platforms:* use YouTube's paid promotion setting and similar tools as well as, not instead of, a clear label.
3. **Check scripts and claims.** Read every sponsor script for claims (prices, "best", health, environmental or results claims). Ask the sponsor to confirm they can back each one up. A host should not present a paid read as an independent opinion: in 2016 the FTC acted against Warner Bros. after paid gameplay videos hid disclosures below the fold.
4. **Collect proof as it happens.** Screenshots with the label visible, episode timestamps, newsletter reports, photos of signage, scan counts. File them by sponsor.
5. **Keep partnership claims current.** When a deal ends, remove logos, "partner" wording and sponsor pages, as the ASA's 2024 ruling against The BLAC Awards shows an expired partnership can't be implied.
6. **Handle misses.** If a deliverable is missed, record why, and draft a make-good proposal (an extra read, a bonus slot) for the client to offer. The agent does not change terms or issue credits.
7. **Write the wrap-up report:** a one-page summary, then each promise with proof, audience numbers with sources, standout moments, anything missed and the make-good, and two ideas for next season. Send before the renewal conversation.
8. **Write the renewal proposal** from the report: what worked, what to change, the package and price for next year, and a date to decide.
9. **Prepare money steps for the client.** Draft invoices on the contract's payment dates and any credit or refund requests at level 2; the client sends and issues them.

## Checks

- Every contract promise is a tracker row with a due date, owner and proof.
- Every paid mention has the agreed label at the start, in the same channel.
- Sponsor claims are confirmed by the sponsor before airing.
- No expired partner appears as current anywhere.
- Misses are recorded honestly with a proposed make-good.
- The report's numbers have sources and dates; no results are claimed for the sponsor's sales.
- Invoices, refunds and credits are drafts for the client.

## Output format

1. **Fulfilment tracker** (sheet).
2. **Disclosure sheet:** channel, label wording, placement, who checks.
3. **Proof folder index.**
4. **Wrap-up report** and **renewal proposal**.
5. **Make-good and invoice drafts** for the client.
