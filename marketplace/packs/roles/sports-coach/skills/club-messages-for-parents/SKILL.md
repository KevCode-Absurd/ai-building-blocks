# Club messages for parents

## When to use

- Start of season: who, what, when, where, what to bring, how to contact the club.
- A session is moved, cancelled or changes venue.
- Kit, fees reminders (payment links prepared only), festival or match day details.
- Asking for consent to photos or filming.
- End of a block or season: what the squad worked on and what comes next.
- Sharing the club's head-injury steps at the start of a season.

Do not use it for a message about one child's behaviour, welfare, injury, health, selection or a complaint. Write a short factual note for the coach instead and tell them it goes to the club welfare officer (welfare or safeguarding) or the club secretary (complaints), with a fresh yes before anything is sent.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** past messages, the club handbook and policies, the team schedule.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save messages unsent in the club app or email.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** send the approved messages to the agreed team groups (parents included), on the agreed schedule and within agreed hours, within the confirmed limits. A new audience, a single family, a payment request or any photo needs a fresh yes. Log every action.

## What you need

1. The club's name, team, coach names and how parents should contact the club.
2. The facts: dates, times, venues, kit, costs (if any), deadlines.
3. The club's voice: a past message or two.
4. The channel (club app, email, text) and character limits if any.
5. The club's welfare officer contact, photo consent process and parent code of conduct, or [placeholders].
6. Whether the club is under a governing body, and which country's rules apply.

## Steps

### 1. One message, one job

Decide the single thing the message must do (turn up at the new time, bring shin pads, return the consent form). Put it in the first line.

### 2. Write the facts block

What, when, where, what to bring, who to contact. Use the club's own words for places and teams. Double-check every date and time against the schedule.

### 3. Add the safety parts that belong to this message

- **Start of season:** the club's ethos and rules, the coaching approach, the welfare officer's name and contact [placeholder if not given], how to raise a concern, the parent code of conduct, and how the club will message (group messages with parents included, within set hours). The NSPCC CPSU lists these as the start-of-season basics.
- **Photo consent:** say what may be taken, by whom, on which devices, where it may be used, that names will not be published with images, that the child is asked too, and that consent can be withdrawn at any time. For under-16s in the UK, consent comes from a parent or carer.
- **Head-injury note:** the club's steps only, in plain words: "If in doubt, sit them out"; a player with a suspected head injury stops for the day; the coach tells the parent; get the player checked by a healthcare professional (UK: NHS 111 within 24 hours; call 999 or 911 for danger signs); return follows the official graduated steps and a healthcare professional's advice. Link the official page. Do not give medical advice or dates for a named child.

### 4. Keep it safe

- Send to the team group with parents and carers included. No message goes privately to a young person.
- No photos with names, school, or training times. No child's health or personal details in any message.
- Send within the club's agreed hours (US clubs under MAAPP: 8 am to 8 pm is the recommended window).

### 5. Match the voice and trim

Short sentences, warm and clear, no jargon. Cut anything that does not help the parent act.

### 6. Prepare for sending

Give the message, the channel, the audience and the send time for approval. At level 3, send only what was approved, to the agreed group, and log it.

## Checks

- First line says what the parent must do.
- Dates, times and venues match the schedule.
- Safety parts present where they belong: contacts, consent wording, head-injury steps.
- No medical, injury, diet or load advice; no clearance for any player.
- No child's full name, health detail or photo with a name.
- Group audience with parents included; agreed hours.
- Anything about a named child's welfare went to the welfare officer, not into a message.
- At level 3, only approved messages were sent, and the log is updated.

## Output format

1. Message purpose and audience
2. The message (subject line or first line, body, sign-off)
3. Channel, send time and who approves
4. Placeholders still to fill
5. For start of season: a checklist of the safety parts included

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up club for illustration: Northfield Swim Club, level 3 group, Thursday sessions.*

> **Subject: Thursday swim moves to 5.30 pm from 12 November**
>
> Hi everyone,
>
> From Thursday 12 November, level 3 sessions start at **5.30 pm** (not 5 pm) at the same pool, for the rest of the term.
>
> Please arrive 10 minutes early with a swimsuit, hat, goggles and a water bottle. Poolside changes stay the same.
>
> Questions about the session: [coach email]. Any worries about a child's welfare: our welfare officer, [name and phone].
>
> Thanks,
> [Head coach], Northfield Swim Club
>
> *Audience: level 3 parents group. Send: Monday 6 pm. Approval: head coach.*
