# Season plan and progress sheet

## When to use

- Before a new season, term or block.
- When sessions feel random and players are not getting better at the basics.
- When a club wants all coaches in one age group working to the same plan.
- When parents ask how progress is tracked.

Do not use it to set individual training loads, plan a return from injury, or track weight, growth or diet. Those questions go to a qualified medical professional, and for a child to the parents too.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the fixture list, the training calendar, past plans and the current progress sheet.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the season plan as a draft document; add a new progress sheet tab; create unpublished events.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** publish the agreed weekly themes to the club app events, share the plan with the age group's coaches, and update the agreed progress columns after each block, within the confirmed limits. Log every action.

## What you need

1. Sport, age group and the governing body's format for that age (pitch or court size, match format, contact or heading rules).
2. Season start and end dates, weekly slots and length, holidays and known gaps.
3. Match, festival or gala dates.
4. Squad size and ability spread; any players new to the sport.
5. What the club wants by the end of the season (skills, enjoyment, retention).
6. The current progress sheet, if any.

## Steps

### 1. Place the squad on the development stages

Use Sport for Life's long-term development stages as a guide, not a rule: Active Start (up to about 6), FUNdamentals (boys about 6 to 9, girls about 6 to 8), Learn to Train (to the start of the growth spurt), Train to Train (the growth spurt). Children of the same age can be at different stages, so plan for the spread. For FUNdamentals groups, keep competition informal with no recorded results, and keep a wide range of movement skills. Avoid over-specialising before the early teens, except in early-specialisation sports such as gymnastics, diving or figure skating, where the governing body's pathway applies.

### 2. List the skills for the season

Pull a short list of core skills or game ideas for the sport and age (from the governing body's syllabus if there is one). Put them in a sensible order: individual control before passing, passing before passing under pressure, attacking ideas before defending them.

### 3. Lay out the weeks

- Each week: one theme, one key skill, one game that rewards it.
- Group weeks into blocks of a few weeks on related skills.
- Add a review week at the end of each block: replay favourite games, check progress, adjust the next block.
- Put lighter, fun weeks before holidays and after long breaks.
- Mark match or festival weeks, and keep training the week before light on new content.
- Leave rest: holidays and breaks are part of the plan. Overuse injuries come from repeated stress without recovery, so ask a medical professional before adding sessions for any player.

### 4. Write the progress sheet

- Rows: the season's core skills, plus attitude skills the club values (effort, being a good sport, helping teammates).
- Columns: player first name or initials, then three plain levels per skill: working towards, can do, can teach a teammate.
- A short notes column for "next step" only.
- No date of birth, address, school, photo, medical, injury, growth or weight columns. If the club needs medical or emergency information, it lives in the club's own secure registration system, managed by the club, not in the coach's sheet.

### 5. Plan how to use it

Assess at the review weeks, not every session. Share each player's next step with the player and parent in positive terms. Use the sheet to adjust the next block, not to rank or select young children.

### 6. Check the plan is runnable

Can a volunteer see, in one line per week, what to coach? Does each block link to sessions built with `session-plan-builder`?

## Checks

- Themes follow a sensible order and fit the age group's development stage.
- Every block ends with a review week.
- Breaks, holidays and match weeks are in.
- The format matches the governing body's rules for the age group.
- The progress sheet holds skills, levels and next steps only.
- No load, injury or nutrition advice.
- At level 3, only the agreed events and sheet columns were changed, and the log is updated.

## Output format

1. Squad summary and development stage notes
2. Core skills in order, with the reason for the order
3. Week-by-week grid: date, theme, key skill, game, notes (review, match, break)
4. Progress sheet template with levels and an example row
5. How and when to assess, and how to share next steps

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real organisation: a netball club's under-11s, 14 players, Saturday mornings, 75 minutes, September to December, festival days in October and December. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*
