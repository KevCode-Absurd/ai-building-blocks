# Session plan builder

## When to use

- A coach needs next week's session, or the first sessions of a block.
- A plan exists but players queue, get bored or fail the drill.
- A backup plan is needed for rain, a smaller space, fewer coaches or more players than expected.
- A new volunteer needs a plan they can run from one page.

Do not use it to plan around an injury, a return after a head knock, a player's weight or diet, or extra load for one player. Say plainly that those need a qualified medical professional (and the parent, for a child) and leave them out.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the team schedule, past plans and the squad's first names or initials.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the plan as a draft on the club app event or in the planning tool.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** attach the plan to the published session in the club app, update session notes, and share it to the coaches you named, within the confirmed limits. Log every action.

## What you need

1. Sport and the skill or theme for the session (or the season plan week).
2. Age group, usual and worst-case numbers, and the ability spread.
3. Space (for example half a pitch, one court, the shallow end, a sports hall) and the surface.
4. Kit: balls, cones, bibs, goals, mats, floats.
5. Session length and the number of adults, and whether one is first-aid trained.
6. Any access needs, described by what the player needs to take part, with no medical detail.
7. The governing body's age-group rules if they limit contact, heading or format.

If numbers, space or kit are missing, ask before planning. Drills fall apart with the wrong numbers.

## Steps

### 1. Check the set-up is safe to run

Compare adults present with the CPSU minimum ratios (1:6 for ages 4 to 8, 1:8 for 9 to 12, 1:10 for 13 to 18, and at least two adults) and the governing body's rules. If short, say so and plan smaller groups or ask the club for help. Note the first aider, where the first aid kit is, and the emergency plan. For any contact sport, add the concussion line: remove anyone with a suspected head injury for the rest of the session, tell the parent, and point them to NHS 111 or a healthcare provider (US: keep them out that day and until a healthcare provider clears them).

### 2. Set one goal

Write one sentence: what players should do better by the end, in observable terms ("pass to a moving teammate with the inside of the foot").

### 3. Build the blocks

- **Warm-up (short):** a moving game that previews the skill, not static stretches in a line.
- **Drill 1:** the skill in a simple form, lots of touches, low pressure.
- **Drill 2:** the same skill with a decision or a defender.
- **Drill 3 (if time):** the skill under game-like pressure.
- **Game:** a small-sided or modified game where using the skill pays off (a bonus point, a rule, a zone).
- **Cool-down:** easy movement and two questions to the players ("When did the pass work best?").

Times must add up to the session length, with time for set-up, water and moving between areas.

### 4. Write each drill properly

For each: purpose, set-up (area size, cones, groups), rules, three coaching points as short cues, and a text diagram with cones and players marked. Then an easier and a harder version, changing one thing at a time: the space (bigger is easier), the task (fewer rules or more time), the equipment (bigger or softer ball, lower net), or the people (more attackers than defenders, or the reverse).

### 5. Count activity

Estimate how many players are waiting at any moment. If more than a few are queuing, split into more groups, add balls or turn it into a game.

### 6. Check inclusion

Make sure every player can take part in each block, with the easier version or a role change. Keep any access needs off the shared plan; write the adjustment, not the reason.

### 7. Add the coach notes

What to look for, when to step up or down, and one question to ask players. Keep talk short: one coaching point, then play.

## Checks

- Every drill fits the space, the usual and worst-case numbers, and the kit.
- Times add up.
- Each drill has purpose, set-up, rules, three coaching points, a diagram, and easier and harder versions.
- Players are active most of the time; the session ends with a game.
- The safety line is there: ratios, first aider, emergency plan, concussion steps.
- No injury, rehab, nutrition or load advice; no punishment running.
- No child's full name or health detail on the plan.
- At level 3, only the agreed events and folders were changed, and the log is updated.

## Output format

1. Header: team, date, length, goal, adults and first aider, safety line
2. Timed table: time, block, activity, set-up, coaching points
3. Per drill: text diagram, rules, easier version, harder version
4. Coach notes: what to look for, one question for players
5. Backup: what to change if numbers drop or rain arrives

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up club for illustration: Riverside Juniors under-9s football, 16 players, a quarter of a pitch, 10 balls, 20 cones, 60 minutes, 3 adults (one first aider).*

> **Goal:** pass to a moving teammate with the inside of the foot.
> **Safety:** 3 adults for 16 players (CPSU minimum 1:8, at least two adults: met). First aider: Coach B, kit by the gate. Suspected head knock: off for the rest of the session, tell the parent, NHS 111.
>
> | Time | Block | Activity | Coaching points |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 0-8 | Warm-up | Traffic lights dribbling | Small touches; head up; stop on red |
> | 8-22 | Drill 1 | Pass through the gates in pairs | Inside of foot; plant foot beside the ball; pass to the front foot |
> | 22-36 | Drill 2 | 3v1 rondo in 10x10 squares | Move to an open angle; pass early; call a name |
> | 36-55 | Game | 4v4, a goal counts double after 3 passes | Look up first; pass and move |
> | 55-60 | Cool-down | Walk and talk | "When was it easy to pass?" |
>
> ```
> Drill 1 (each pair)
>  P1 ->  [ ]  <- P2      [ ] = cone gate, 1 m wide
>        5 m apart
> ```
> **Easier:** gates 2 m wide, stop the ball before passing. **Harder:** one touch, move to a new gate after each pass.
> **If 10 turn up:** two 3v1 squares only, then 3v3 plus a floater.
