# Volunteer coach handbook

## When to use

- Before a season, so every volunteer coach works the same way.
- When new parent helpers or young leaders start.
- After something went wrong and nobody knew what to do.
- When a club joins a governing body or updates its safeguarding policy.

Do not use it to write or change the club's safeguarding policy itself, to decide what happens after a concern, or to set first aid or medical procedures beyond "who to call". The welfare officer, the governing body and qualified medical professionals own those.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the club's safeguarding policy, codes of conduct, governing body rules, past handbook.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the new handbook as a draft beside the current one, with tracked changes.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** share the approved handbook with the coaches you named and post it in the club app's coach group, within the confirmed limits. Any change to safeguarding wording needs the welfare officer's yes each time. Log every action.

## What you need

1. The club's safeguarding policy, codes of conduct (coaches, parents, players) and photo and messaging rules. If there are none, say so; the handbook will point to the NSPCC CPSU or U.S. Center for SafeSport templates.
2. Names and contacts of the welfare officer, the club lead and the first aiders, or [placeholders].
3. Whether the club is under a governing body and which country's rules apply (England and Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, US, EU country).
4. Venue details: address for emergency services, first aid kit, defibrillator if any, nearest A&E or emergency department.
5. How sessions are run now: arrival, registration, typical plan, pick-up.
6. Any recent incidents (described without names) that the handbook must answer.

## Steps

### 1. Map what the club already has

List each policy and what it says about coaches. Mark gaps: no welfare officer named, no photo consent, no messaging rule, no concussion steps.

### 2. Write "How we coach here"

One page: the club's values, the session shape (warm-up, drills, game, cool-down), keep players active, easier and harder versions, praise effort, no punishment running, ends with a game. Link to the season plan and `session-plan-builder`.

### 3. Write "Keeping everyone safe"

- **Ratios and adults:** at least two adults; CPSU minimum ratios of 1:6 (ages 4 to 8), 1:8 (9 to 12) and 1:10 (13 to 18), or the governing body's rule if stricter; at least one first aider, as far as possible.
- **Vetting:** volunteers complete the club's checks before working with children (UK: the right DBS, PVG or AccessNI check; US: SafeSport Core training where the club is in the Olympic and Paralympic Movement, and the club's background check).
- **Positions of trust (England and Wales):** adults who regularly coach under-18s are in a legal position of trust.
- **Messaging:** group messages with parents included, no private messages or social media connections with players, agreed hours.
- **One-to-one time:** in sight and open to others (US clubs under MAAPP: observable and interruptible).
- **Photos and video:** only with consent, on club devices, never in changing areas, no names with images.
- **Changing and toilets:** supervision rules from the club policy.
- **Lifts:** the club's transport rule (US MAAPP: written parent consent or another adult or two other minors).

### 4. Write "When things go wrong"

Short numbered steps for each:
- **Injury:** stop the activity, first aider assesses, call 999 or 911 if serious, tell the parent, record it on the club's form. Coaches do not diagnose or advise on treatment or return; that is for a qualified medical professional.
- **Suspected head injury:** "If in doubt, sit them out." Remove for the rest of the day, tell the parent, advise NHS 111 within 24 hours in the UK or a healthcare provider in the US, 999 or 911 for danger signs. The player returns only through the official graduated return and a healthcare professional's advice; no return to competition before day 21 under the UK grassroots guidelines.
- **A child tells you something worrying:** listen calmly, do not promise to keep it secret, do not investigate or question, write down what was said in their words, tell the welfare officer as soon as possible. Immediate danger: 999 or 911.
- **A concern about an adult's behaviour:** tell the welfare officer; in England this can lead to the LADO.
- **A missing child:** the club's missing child procedure [placeholder].
- **Bad weather or an unsafe venue:** cancel or move; message parents through the club group.
- **Touchline trouble:** calm, remind of the parent code, involve the club lead; never argue with a parent in front of children.

### 5. Add the contact card

One box at the front: welfare officer, club lead, first aiders, venue address, NSPCC Helpline 0808 800 5000 and Childline 0800 1111 (UK), U.S. Center for SafeSport 833-587-7233 (US), local police non-emergency number. Every club-specific contact is a [placeholder] until the club fills it in.

### 6. Keep it short

Aim for a handbook a volunteer can read in one sitting. Put templates (incident form, consent form) in an appendix.

## Checks

- Every safeguarding contact is the club's own or a clear [placeholder].
- Steps match the club's policy and governing body rules; gaps are listed, not invented.
- No medical advice beyond "stop, first aid, call, tell the parent, record".
- No promise of confidentiality to a child; no investigating.
- Messaging and photo rules match the CPSU or MAAPP standard.
- Country-specific parts are labelled (UK nations, US, EU).
- At level 3, only the approved version was shared, to the named coaches, and the log is updated.

## Output format

1. Contact card
2. How we coach here
3. Keeping everyone safe
4. When things go wrong (one box per situation)
5. Gaps the club must fill, with who should own each
6. Appendix: templates and links to official guidance

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real organisation: a judo club in England, under a national governing body. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **When a child tells you something worrying**
> 1. Stay calm and listen. Let them talk; do not ask leading questions.
> 2. Say you are glad they told you and that you will pass it to someone who can help. Do not promise to keep it secret.
> 3. Do not investigate or speak to anyone the child mentions.
> 4. Write down what they said, in their words, with the date and time. Sign it.
> 5. Tell our welfare officer, [name, phone], as soon as possible, the same session if you can. If the child is in immediate danger, call 999 first.
> 6. Do not post or message about it to anyone else.
>
> **Gap found:** the club has no written missing child procedure. Owner: welfare officer, before the first session.
