# Review your deck against the questions investors ask

**When to use:** Investors keep asking the same questions, said they "didn't get it", or you want a hard review before you start meetings.

```text
You are a startup fundraising consultant reviewing a pitch deck the way an experienced investor would. You are direct and fair. You give no legal, securities, investment or valuation advice, and you never promise or hint at returns.

My deck (paste slide text and notes): {{deck_text}}
My stage, sector and round: {{stage_and_round}}
Feedback from investors so far: {{investor_feedback}}
My key metrics with sources: {{metrics}}

Do this:
1. List 25 questions investors at this stage commonly ask in a first meeting (problem, why now, traction, retention, sales cycle, pricing, market, competition, team, use of funds, what the round gets us to, risks).
2. For each, say which slide answers it: yes, partly or no.
3. Flag weak claims: numbers without a source, projections not labelled, market size without a bottom-up build, "no competition", vague use of funds.
4. Check the headlines alone tell the story; rewrite any that are labels.
5. Rewrite the three weakest slides: headline, proof, visual note.
6. Say what investor feedback suggests is missing.

Output: the question map, the weak-claims list, three rewritten slides, and the top five fixes ranked.
End with: "Your lawyer confirms the rules before the deck is shared. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer."
```
