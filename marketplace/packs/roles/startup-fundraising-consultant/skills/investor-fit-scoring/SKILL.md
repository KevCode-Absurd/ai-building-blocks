# Investor fit scoring and outreach plan

## When to use

- A founder has a list of investors (from their network, a database export or advisers) and doesn't know where to start.
- A raise is about to begin and the founder needs an outreach order and first messages.
- Outreach has stalled and the founder wants to know whether the list or the approach is wrong.

Who may be approached, and what may be said, depends on securities rules (in the US the SEC's Regulation D and Crowdfunding pages; in the UK the FCA financial promotion rules; in the EU the prospectus and crowdfunding rules) and on email rules. The founder's lawyer confirms the route before outreach starts. I plan outreach; I don't judge whether an investment is good for anyone. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.

## What you need

1. The raise: amount, instrument, stage, sector, location and timing.
2. What the founder wants from investors besides money (introductions, hiring help, sector knowledge).
3. The investor list with whatever is known: type (angel, fund, corporate), stage, typical cheque, sectors, location, recent investments, partner, notes.
4. The founder's network notes: who could introduce whom.
5. The lawyer's confirmation of the route, and any limits on who may be approached.
6. The founder's approved message templates, once written.

**Access:** level 1 to read the list and CRM; level 2 to load investors and draft stages, notes and messages unsent; level 3 to send approved messages to investors on the approved list and update stages, within the founder's limits. Any new investor, new template or message about terms needs a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Write the fit criteria

Five to eight criteria with weights that add to 100, for example: invests at this stage (25), cheque size fits the round (20), sector focus (20), location (10), leads rounds (15), adds the help the founder wants (10). Mark must-haves: an investor who fails one goes to "not a fit" whatever the score.

### 2. Score each investor on given facts only

For each criterion: met, partly, not met or unknown, with the fact and its source (the founder's note, the investor's own website, a public filing). Unknown scores zero and is listed, so the founder knows what to find out. Never guess a cheque size or focus from reputation.

### 3. Sort into tiers

- **Top fit:** passes must-haves, high score, few unknowns.
- **Good fit:** passes must-haves, some gaps.
- **Long shot:** partial fit or many unknowns.
- **Not a fit:** fails a must-have.
Write one line under each top-fit score explaining it.

### 4. Plan the order

Practice meetings first, with good-fit investors, to sharpen the story. Top fits next, close together so meetings cluster and decisions overlap. Ask each investor about next steps and record actions, not mood; treat "maybe" as "not yet" until there is a commitment.

### 5. Write outreach notes for each top fit

Why they fit (one line), the angle to lead with (one line tied to their focus), the warm introduction path from the founder's network, and a short first message: who, what, traction in one number, the ask, no attachments unless asked. No promise or hint of returns.

### 6. Write the forwardable intro blurb

Three or four sentences a contact can forward: what the company does, one traction number with its date, the round, and why this investor.

### 7. Set up the pipeline tracker

Columns: investor, tier, stage (researching, intro requested, first meeting, follow-up, diligence, term sheet, committed, passed), owner, next step, date, last action, notes. A weekly 20-minute review: what moved, what is stuck, who to follow up.

### 8. Send within the limits (level 3 only)

Before the first send, show the founder the list of recipients and templates and get a yes. Send only to that list. Log each message. Stop on any reply that raises a legal question, an unsubscribe or a bounce spike, and report.

## Checks

- Criteria and weights agreed with the founder.
- Every score cites a fact; unknowns marked, not guessed.
- No personal contact details collected.
- Top-fit scores each have a one-line reason.
- Messages carry no return promises and match approved templates.
- Level 3 sends only to the approved list, logged.
- The lawyer has confirmed the route before outreach. *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.*

## Output format

1. Fit criteria table with weights and must-haves.
2. Scored list: investor, scores by criterion, total, unknowns, tier, reason.
3. Outreach order with dates.
4. Outreach notes and first messages for top fits.
5. Forwardable intro blurb.
6. Pipeline tracker and weekly review steps.
7. Facts to find out, ranked by how much they would change the tiers.
