# Investor update

## When to use

- Every month, on a fixed date, for existing investors and advisers.
- Quarterly, as a longer letter with the plan changes and risks.
- After a big miss or a big win, when investors need to hear it from the founder first.
- Before a raise, so prospective investors can see a record of regular, honest updates (shared only with the approved list).

An update to existing investors is a report, not an offer; if it is also used to raise money, or shared beyond existing investors, the founder's lawyer confirms the rules first. Forecasts in the update are labelled as forecasts. Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.

## What you need

1. Last month's update, so the format stays the same.
2. The KPI sheet: revenue, growth, customers, the business's own key number.
3. Profit and loss and cash for the month; burn and runway as the founder calculates them.
4. Notes on wins, misses, hires, product, customers and risks.
5. What the founder needs: introductions, hires, advice, decisions.
6. Who receives it: existing investors, advisers, board.

**Access:** level 1 to read the KPI sheet and books; level 2 to draft the update in email or Visible, unsent; level 3 to send the approved update to the existing investor list, within the founder's limits. Adding a new recipient needs a fresh yes.

## Steps

### 1. Pick the headline

The one fact an investor most needs this month, in one sentence. If the month was bad, the bad news is the headline.

### 2. Show five numbers plus one

Revenue (and growth on last month and last year), cash at month end, net burn, runway in months (cash divided by net burn, with the method stated), and one number special to the business (for example active customers, gross margin or pipeline). Same order and definitions every month. If a definition changes, say so.

### 3. Write wins

One line each, with a number: "Signed two hospital groups, 6,200 a month together."

### 4. Write misses

One line each: what happened, the cause, the fix and when the founder will know if it worked. No softening words. A miss stated plainly costs less trust than a miss discovered.

### 5. Name the top risk

The biggest risk to the plan and what the founder is doing about it.

### 6. Write up to three asks

Specific and easy to act on: "An introduction to a finance director at a care group with over 20 sites." Under each, a two-line note the investor can forward.

### 7. Check every number

Against the KPI sheet and the books. Mark anything not verified. Runway is consistent with cash and burn.

### 8. Send (level 3 only, within limits)

Show the founder the final update and the recipient list. After the yes, send to the existing investor list only. Log the send.

## Checks

- Headline first; bad news not buried.
- Five numbers plus one, same definitions as last month.
- Runway equals cash divided by net burn, stated.
- Misses have cause, fix and a date.
- Asks are specific, with forwardable notes.
- No promise or hint of returns; forecasts labelled.
- Sent only to the approved recipients. *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.*

## Output format

1. Subject line: company, month, one-line headline.
2. Headline paragraph (two sentences).
3. Numbers table: this month, last month, change.
4. Wins (one line each).
5. Misses (one line each: cause, fix, date).
6. Top risk.
7. Asks with forwardable notes.
8. Unverified items list (for the founder only).

## Example of good output (short)

Illustrative structure, not a real company: a startup making software for physiotherapy clinics. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.

> **Subject:** [Business], October: our largest clinic group won't renew; runway 14 months
>
> Clinic Group A (9% of revenue) told us it won't renew in December; the reason is our price for small sites. Revenue grew 6% this month from new clinics.
>
> | Number | October | September | Change |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Revenue | 63,400 | 59,800 | +6% |
> | Cash | 712,000 | 763,000 | -51,000 |
> | Net burn | 51,000 | 48,000 | +3,000 |
> | Runway | 14 months | 16 months | -2 |
> | Active clinics | 212 | 205 | +7 |
>
> **Miss:** Clinic Group A will leave; our per-site price is too high for groups of small clinics. Fix: a group price tested with two prospects in November; we'll know by 15 December.
>
> **Ask:** an introduction to an operations lead at a clinic group with over 30 sites. *Forwardable:* "[Business] runs bookings and notes for 212 physio clinics; they want to learn how large groups buy."
>
> *Check this with local rules and ask a qualified accountant or lawyer.*
