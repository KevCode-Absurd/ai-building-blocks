# Training interview: Startup fundraising consultant

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share founder or investor names, round terms that could identify a company, or personal data; anonymise every story.

1. **Which founders do you work with (stage, sector, country), and which parts of a raise do you handle?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's defaults, its regional rules and which of the four jobs it leads with.

2. **How do you decide whether a founder should raise now, later or not at all?**
   Why it matters: the expert's readiness test keeps the agent from writing decks for raises that shouldn't happen yet.

3. **Walk me through how you build a deck story, from the founder's notes to the final headlines.**
   Why it matters: captures the real order of work behind a deck that holds together.

4. **Which investor questions does a seed deck most often fail to answer, and how do you fix that?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a tested question map instead of a generic one.

5. **How do you check the numbers in a deck, and what do you do when a founder insists on a number they can't support?**
   Why it matters: records how the expert holds the line on claims without losing the founder.

6. **How do you build a market size slide that survives diligence?**
   Why it matters: market size is where decks most often overreach.

7. **How do you build an investor list and decide who fits?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's criteria and the facts they trust.

8. **What order do you approach investors in, and why?**
   Why it matters: sequencing decides whether meetings cluster and the story is sharp when it counts.

9. **What makes a first message or intro blurb work, and what makes one get ignored?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent tested patterns for short, specific outreach.

10. **How do you work with the founder's lawyer on what may be said in public and who may be approached?**
    Why it matters: the agent must know when to stop and wait for the lawyer.

11. **Tell me about a raise where outreach went wrong. What happened and what did you change?**
    Why it matters: real failures become checks in the agent's outreach plan.

12. **How do you build a cap table from messy records, and what conflicts do you see most often?**
    Why it matters: the expert's reconciliation habits stop wrong percentages reaching founders.

13. **Walk me through converting SAFEs or notes in a priced round. Where do people go wrong?**
    Why it matters: conversion mistakes are common and costly; the expert's method is reusable.

14. **How do you show a founder the effect of an option pool top-up or a liquidation preference in plain words?**
    Why it matters: captures how the expert explains dilution without jargon.

15. **When a founder asks "should I take this term sheet?", what do you say and do?**
    Why it matters: keeps the agent inside its role and gives it tested wording.

16. **How do you write an investor update after a bad month?**
    Why it matters: the expert's honesty pattern is what makes updates trusted.

17. **What do investors actually read in an update, and what do they skip?**
    Why it matters: shapes the agent's update format.

18. **How do you prepare a founder for a board meeting with bad news?**
    Why it matters: board prep is where honest numbers and calm wording matter most.

19. **Which tools do you use for decks, data rooms, cap tables, pipelines and updates, and what access do you ask for?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's default tools and access levels.

20. **What do you check at closing, and what can go wrong with the money or the documents?**
    Why it matters: closing risks, such as wire fraud or unrecorded shares, need the agent's attention.

21. **What do founders ask you for that you refuse, and how do you say no?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent tested wording for out-of-scope requests such as valuations or return promises.

22. **Tell me about the hardest raise you worked on and what made it succeed or fail.**
    Why it matters: one detailed story teaches judgement that rules can't.
