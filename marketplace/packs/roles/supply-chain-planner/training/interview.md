# Training interview: supply chain planner

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers and asks for a real example each time. Remind the expert to leave out client and supplier names, prices that are under agreement, and any personal data.

1. **What kinds of businesses and products do you plan for: shops, makers, importers, food, seasonal goods, spare parts?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's specialty and where its advice stops.

2. **Walk me through the last reorder or production plan you built, from the first file to the orders placed.**
   Why it matters: shows the real order of work and where the time goes.

3. **When a client sends a sales export and says "tell me what to order", what do you ask before you open it?**
   Why it matters: captures how the expert pins the decision, cash floor and lead times first.

4. **How do you spot and fix sales history that was capped by stockouts?**
   Why it matters: the most common hidden error in demand planning; the agent needs the expert's method.

5. **How do you decide which forecasting method each product gets?**
   Why it matters: records the expert's grouping rules for steady, seasonal, lumpy and new products.

6. **How do you forecast a product that has never been sold?**
   Why it matters: new lines are where plain AI guesses most; the expert's analogue method is the value.

7. **How do you measure whether a forecast was any good, and what do you do when it was not?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent the expert's accuracy measures and the action that follows.

8. **How do you agree a service level with an owner who says "I never want to run out"?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert turns a wish into a choice with a cost.

9. **Where do your lead times come from, and how do you handle a supplier who is often late?**
   Why it matters: lead-time spread drives safety stock; the expert's habits here change the numbers.

10. **Tell me about a time the textbook safety stock formula gave the wrong answer. What did you do instead?**
    Why it matters: captures the limits of the formula for lumpy, short-life or promotional items.

11. **How do you handle supplier minimums that are far above what you need?**
    Why it matters: records the trade-off between cash, shelf life and unit price that the expert weighs.

12. **How do you plan a one-time seasonal buy, and how do you set the quantity?**
    Why it matters: separates the newsvendor logic from reorder points in the agent's practice.

13. **How do you show an owner what each order does to their cash?**
    Why it matters: the cash view is what owners act on; the expert's format is worth keeping.

14. **When you have permission to place orders, what limits do you ask for, and what do you always check before sending?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's level 3 habits and the moments to stop and ask.

15. **What would make you stop and call the client before placing an order you were allowed to place?**
    Why it matters: records the red flags, such as changed bank details, price jumps or odd quantities.

16. **How do you find the bottleneck in a workshop, and how do you schedule around it?**
    Why it matters: gives the production planner the expert's capacity method.

17. **How do you cost a product from its parts, and what do owners usually leave out?**
    Why it matters: captures scrap, set-up and overhead habits that make costs real.

18. **What quality checks do you set at goods-in, during production and at final inspection, and how do you pick sample sizes?**
    Why it matters: records the expert's quality plan and where they rely on standards.

19. **What do you do with slow stock, and how do you convince an owner to let it go?**
    Why it matters: captures the options and the conversation that frees cash.

20. **Tell me about the hardest planning job you have had. What went wrong and what did you change?**
    Why it matters: a real story shows judgment under pressure that briefs cannot.

21. **What do you see plain AI tools get wrong in stock and supply planning?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent the mistakes to beat in adversarial rounds.

22. **Which tools do you use, and what do you check in each before trusting its numbers?**
    Why it matters: records tool-specific traps, such as reorder rules double counting forecasts.

23. **How do you write the weekly note to an owner, and what goes at the top?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's voice and the layout owners actually read.
