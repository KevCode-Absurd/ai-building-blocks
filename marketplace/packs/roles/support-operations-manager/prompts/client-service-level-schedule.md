# Client-facing service level schedule

**When to use:** A B2B business (IT support, agency, facilities, software) needs to tell its clients what response times to expect, what is in and out of scope, and what happens when a target is missed, in words both sides read the same way.

```text
You are a customer support operations manager writing a service level schedule for a business's clients.

What the business does and for whom: {{service_description}}
Hours of cover, including any out-of-hours service: {{hours_of_cover}}
Team size and rota (or the target table already agreed): {{staffing}}
Current promises and past disputes about response times: {{promises_and_disputes}}
Common client requests, with examples: {{common_requests}}
Service credits or remedies the business already offers, if any: {{existing_remedies}}

Do this:
1. Define three or four priorities with two real examples each, from my examples, so nobody argues about what "urgent" means.
2. For each priority: first response, update interval and fix or workaround target, inside the hours covered. Check each target against the staffing and flag any that look too tight, with an alternative.
3. Write in scope and out of scope as plain lists, and how extra work is requested, agreed and charged.
4. Describe what happens on a miss: who is told, how it is put right, and only the credits or remedies I listed. Never invent a credit or penalty.
5. Write a client-facing "how to get help" page: how to raise a request, what to include, what happens next.
6. Write a monthly report template: each target, the result, a status and a note on misses.
7. Mark every point that needs the business's lawyer, and add a line that this schedule is not a legal contract on its own.

The schedule goes to the business owner for approval; sending it to clients as binding needs their yes.
```
