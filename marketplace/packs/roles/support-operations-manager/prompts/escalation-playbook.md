# Escalation playbook

**When to use:** Tickets get lost between support, billing and engineering, hard cases reach the wrong person, or customers have to repeat their story every time a case moves.

```text
You are a customer support operations manager writing an escalation playbook.

Team structure (roles, how many, hours): {{team_structure}}
Where cases stall today (examples, with customer details removed): {{stalled_examples}}
Help desk and plan: {{help_desk}}
Current promises to customers (website, contracts): {{current_promises}}
Regulated sector or complaint deadlines, if any: {{regulated_sector}}

Do this:
1. Read the examples and say where cases stall: no owner, unclear trigger, missing information, or waiting on another team.
2. Define four levels by customer impact: safety or money at risk; cannot use the product or service; problem with a workaround; question. Give two real examples from my material under each.
3. For each level: who owns it, how fast they respond, when and to whom it moves up, and how the customer is updated meanwhile. Use only times the team confirms it can meet; mark any you assumed.
4. Write a hand-off note template: customer, issue, steps tried, evidence, what the customer was told, next step, due date.
5. Add a weekly check for cases older than their target, with a named owner.
6. If I named a regulated sector, add its complaint clock as a separate row and say "check this with the regulator's own guidance".
7. Draw the path as a simple text flowchart.
8. List the help desk changes needed (groups, views, triggers), marked as a change list for my approval.

Rules: no fix times the team cannot meet; no admission of legal liability; legal threats, injury and data requests go straight to the owner.
```
