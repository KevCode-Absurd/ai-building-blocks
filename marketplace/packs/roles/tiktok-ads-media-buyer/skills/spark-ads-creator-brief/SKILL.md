# Spark Ads creator brief

## When to use

- A brand wants creators to film content that can run as Spark Ads.
- A creator has already posted about the brand and the brand wants to promote that post.
- Creator ads have run before without clear rules, and something went wrong (a claim, a missing disclosure, an unhappy creator).

## What you need

- Product facts and proof the brand can stand behind: ingredients, results with sources, reviews it may quote, awards.
- Words and claims the brand must not use, and any restricted-product rules.
- The creators: links to their profiles and any posts already made about the brand.
- The fee range the client is willing to pay, and who signs off fees and posts.
- Usage terms the client wants: how long the post may run as an ad, and in which countries.

**Access:** level 1 to read posts and pick candidates. Level 2 to write briefs, scripts and fee proposals. Level 3 to promote an approved post as a Spark Ad within the limits, after the creator's agreement and the client's yes for that post.

## Steps

### 1. Pick the angle
From the brand's best-selling reasons and customer words, choose one problem and one promise for this brief. One brief, one idea.

### 2. Write what to film
- Setting and props, in the creator's own space.
- The product shown in use, with the brand visible in the first 2 seconds.
- Length: most ads under 25 seconds.
- Three hook options for the first two seconds, each written as its own line. Let the creator say them in their own voice.
- Shots list: hook, problem, product in use, proof, call to action.

### 3. Write the rules
- **Say:** the facts and proof listed, in the creator's words.
- **Don't say:** claims the brand cannot prove, medical or financial promises, comparisons the brand has not checked, anything about competitors by name unless approved.
- **Don't show:** under-18s using restricted products, unsafe use, other brands' logos.
- **Music:** only music cleared for ads; Smart+ Music Autofix flags music that can't be used, but check before filming.

### 4. Disclosure
- The creator says and shows that it is an ad, in the video itself, in plain words such as "ad" or "sponsored". The US FTC says a platform tool alone may not be enough, and the UK ASA's influencer guide (fourth edition, October 2026) sets out how to label. Use TikTok's own commercial content setting as well.
- If the creator received free product, that counts as a connection to disclose.

### 5. AI content
- If any part is made or changed with AI (a Symphony avatar, an AI voiceover, a generated scene), it carries the AIGC disclosure when it runs as an ad, and realistic AI content is labelled on organic posts.
- Keep the original file so its Content Credentials stay intact.

### 6. Agreement and fee
- The creator agrees in writing to the use: how long, which countries, whether edits are allowed.
- The creator authorises the post through TikTok's own Spark Ads flow. Never ask for their login.
- Prepare a fee proposal (flat fee, or floor plus commission) for the client to approve and pay. The agent does not pay creators.

### 7. Client approval check
Before any post runs as an ad, the client sees: the final video, the caption, the disclosure, the claims list with proof, the AI disclosure if any, and the fee. The client replies yes or no for each post.

### 8. Track it
Name each creator ad in the test log with the hook name, so results compare cleanly with the brand's own ads.

## Checks

- Every claim in the brief has proof from the client.
- Disclosure is in the video, spoken and on screen.
- AI elements are marked for disclosure.
- The creator's agreement, the usage period and the client's yes are recorded before launch.
- No step asks for a creator's password or pays a creator.

## Output format

1. Brief: angle, what to film, three hooks, shot list, length.
2. Rules: say, don't say, don't show, music.
3. Disclosure and AI-label instructions.
4. Agreement terms and fee proposal (for the client to approve and pay).
5. Approval checklist per post.

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up business: Climbworks, an indoor climbing gym.*

> **Angle:** "First time climbing? You don't need to be strong."
> **Hooks (first two seconds):**
> 1. "I can't do a single pull-up. Watch this."
> 2. Wide shot of the wall, text on screen: "Day 1 vs Day 30"
> 3. "Things nobody tells you about your first climb"
>
> **Say:** taster session includes shoes and an instructor (client confirmed). **Don't say:** "anyone can climb the hardest wall in a week"; no weight-loss claims.
> **Disclosure:** say "This is an ad for Climbworks" in the first five seconds and keep "Ad" on screen; switch on TikTok's commercial content setting.
> **Agreement:** run as a Spark Ad for 60 days in the UK; no edits without the creator's OK. Fee proposal for your approval: flat fee per post, paid by you.
