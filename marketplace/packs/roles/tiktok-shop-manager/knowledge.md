# TikTok Shop manager: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-07. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. This kit is general practice, not legal advice. Claims, disclosure and consumer law questions that matter go to the client's lawyer.

**A note on TikTok's own rules.** TikTok's seller pages refuse automated page reads, so the TikTok Shop rules below were confirmed through search results that show each exact Seller University page, dated 7 Nov 2026. Most are US rules; UK and EU rules differ, and TikTok changes them often. So before each job I check them where they live:

- **With web search connected:** I open the Seller University and policy pages for the client's market, note the date, and quote what I used.
- **Without web search:** I work from the client's Seller Center fee page, policy pages, exports and screenshots. Anything I could not see is marked "[check in Seller Center]".

**Other kits hold the detail I lean on.** Creator programme design, reward maths and disclosure depth: the affiliate and influencer marketing manager kit. Posting, short video scripts and comment replies: the social media manager kit (it also covers TikTok's content disclosure setting). Product data, markdowns and its price reduction check prompt: the ecommerce manager kit. Setting prices and price display rules: the pricing strategist kit. Test design: the growth marketer kit.

## 1. What this role is for

A business hires this role to sell through TikTok Shop: products bought inside TikTok from short videos, LIVE sessions and product pages. The job is to pick products that still make money after every fee, list them well, plan videos and LIVE sessions that show the product working, bring in creators who sell on commission, ship on time, handle returns, and read the numbers every week.

**Who hires it.** Beauty, fashion, gadget, snack and home goods brands; Shopify stores adding TikTok Shop as a channel; makers new to LIVE selling; and brands whose products already get talked about on TikTok without a shop.

**What good work looks like.**

- Two or three hero products picked from a margin table that includes TikTok's fees, creator commission, shipping, packaging, returns and the planned discount.
- Listings with the product type first in the title, plain facts, sizes and care, and only claims the client can prove.
- A posting and LIVE rhythm the team can keep, with every video built on one hook, one proof shot, the price and the product pin.
- LIVE run sheets timed to the minute, with true offers, true stock calls and answers to common comments.
- Creators chosen for fit, briefed with disclosure rules for each market, and checked after they post.
- Orders dispatched within the promised time, delays told to buyers, and returns handled under the law and TikTok's rules.
- A weekly review by product, video, LIVE and creator that ends in three actions.

**How it is judged.** Sales and contribution margin after all fees, not sales alone. Return and refund rate. On-time dispatch. Any policy warnings or violations in Seller Center. Share of sales from creators against the commission paid. Clean disclosure on every paid or gifted post. And whether the formats that work can be repeated.

**What an AI manager delivers.** Margin tables, hero picks, launch plans, listing sheets, video briefs, LIVE run sheets, creator briefs, sample rules, claim and disclosure checks, fulfilment and returns checks, and weekly reviews from exports. With access to Seller Center or a connected store, it can also draft and, within the client's limits, publish listings, set approved promotions, post approved videos and send approved creator invitations. The client hosts the LIVE. New paid creators and money stay under the client's control (section 3).

## 2. The canon

**TikTok's own rules are the working manual.** The Seller University, the Seller Center policy pages and the TikTok Community Guidelines set what can be sold, how listings and videos must look, how creators are paid and how fast orders ship [2][9][14][25][26][29][30]. They change often, so I re-read them at every job.

**The regulators' guides are the honesty canon.** The FTC's Endorsement Guides FAQ (Guides revised 2023) has TikTok-specific advice on where a disclosure goes and how to disclose in live streams [1]. The ASA's advice on recognising ads [8] and the CMA's guidance for brands [13] are the UK standard. The European Commission's Influencer Legal Hub covers the EU [21].

**Claims and selling pressure.** The FTC's Health Products Compliance Guidance sets the evidence bar for health, beauty and wellness claims [4]. The CMA's advice on urgency and price reduction claims (29 March 2023) shows, example by example, which countdowns, stock calls and "was" prices mislead [16]. LIVE selling uses all of these at speed.

**Fulfilment.** The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule guide is the clearest statement of what a shipping promise commits a seller to in the US [5].

**Reading list** (credited only; this kit makes no specific claim from them): Robert Cialdini, *Influence* (new and expanded edition 2021), for why live demos, social proof and scarcity move people, and why faking them backfires. The affiliate kit's reading list covers creator marketing.

## 3. How the work is done now

### The shop and its connections (as of 6 Nov 2026)

- **Seller Center** is the seller's TikTok Shop dashboard. Rules can differ by market, so check the Seller Center for each country the client sells in before applying any rule.
- **Shopify stores** can connect through TikTok's own app on the Shopify App Store. TikTok Inc. lists it as free to install with other charges possible. It syncs Shopify products to TikTok, syncs TikTok Shop to Shopify so products show in video and LIVE, and lets the seller manage TikTok Shop orders and inventory in Shopify admin [27].
- **No official TikTok Shop MCP server** was confirmed on 6 Nov 2026. I connect through the client's own Seller Center sign-in, the Shopify app, or exports.

### Who sees it

Pew's 2026 survey (30 Jan to 18 Jun 2026, 5,511 US adults) found TikTok used by 65% of US adults aged 18 to 29, 45% of those 30 to 49, 31% of those 50 to 64 and 11% of those 65 and over [28]. Younger adults use TikTok far more, so a product aimed at people over 65 is a weaker fit for TikTok Shop as a first channel.

### Hero products and margin after fees

Every plan starts with a margin table per product:

contribution per order = price after discount − TikTok's fees for that order − creator commission (if a creator sold it) − product cost − packaging − shipping the seller pays − returns allowance

**TikTok's fees (as of 7 Nov 2026).** In the US, TikTok's fee page set the referral fee at 6% per order from 1 April 2024, with some categories lower. When an order is refunded, TikTok keeps a refund administration fee of 20% of the referral fee, capped at $5 per item [2]. Some 2026 trade reports describe a higher US rate that TikTok's public pages did not show on 7 Nov 2026. In the UK, the standard commission is 9% including VAT, with lower rates for some categories and new sellers [9]. EU rates differ again [check in Seller Center].

The fee terms used in a plan come from the client's Seller Center fee page on the day, never from memory or a blog. A hero product needs a clear result on camera, a margin that survives the planned discount and commission, and stock for the plan. Drop products that cannot pay for a discount. The hero-product-margin-pick skill holds the method.

### Listings

- **Title:** product type first, then the main feature, size or count. Plain words, no claims the client cannot prove.
- **Body:** five selling points, a description, sizes, materials, care, what is in the box, warnings.
- **Images:** product on plain background first, then in use, scale, detail, contents.
- **Rules:** TikTok's prohibited and restricted product lists and listing rules for the market [check in Seller Center].
- **US:** under the INFORM Consumers Act (effective 27 June 2023), marketplaces must collect and verify bank, tax and contact details from high-volume sellers: 200 or more sales and $5,000 or more in gross revenue in any continuous 12 months within the last 24. Sellers with $20,000 or more a year on the platform must have their name, physical address and contact shown to buyers. Sellers certify their details at least once a year [6].
- **EU:** under the Digital Services Act, marketplaces must verify and display sellers' contact details and make reasonable efforts to check products at random [24]. The Commission tells creators who sell to check products on Safety Gate and never to sell recalled ones [21]; the same check suits any seller.

### Shoppable videos

Each video brief names the product, the hook in the first seconds, the proof shot (the product working), the price moment and when to pin the product. One hook can carry three versions. Posting and scripts follow the social media manager kit.

**On the brand's own account,** the commercial intent is usually plain, but anyone with a connection who posts from a personal account (owner, staff, family) must label it as an ad [8].

### Creators, commissions and samples

Creators can sell a shop's products for commission and may ask for samples. **TikTok's US sample rules (as of 7 Nov 2026):** samples are free or refundable (the creator buys and is refunded after meeting the seller's sales target). Sellers answer a free sample request within 7 days or it is cancelled, then ship within 7 days of approval. Creators are expected to post within 14 days of receiving it [29]. UK and EU terms [check in Seller Center]. The law around samples is settled:

- **US (FTC).** A free product given with an expectation of promotion is a material connection that must be disclosed, even if the product is returned [1]. On TikTok, a disclosure only in the text description is "very unlikely" to be clear; the FTC suggests larger text over the video, and a spoken disclosure too when the endorsement is spoken [1]. Platform tools may not be enough on their own [1]. Brands need a reasonable programme to explain allowed claims, instruct on disclosure, check what creators post and act on problems, and to keep checking for a few months after a contract ends [1]. Discount-code reviewers must disclose the discount [1].
- **UK (ASA and CMA).** Content is an ad if the creator got payment or any incentive, has a connection to the brand, or earns commission through an affiliate link or code [8]. "Ad", "Advert", "Advertising" or "Ad Feature" work; "sponsored", "gifted", "aff", "spon", a brand tag or a discount code alone are likely not enough. On TikTok the commercial intent should be clear before people click to watch where possible, or from the outset [8]. Brands should send gifts with clear instructions to label any resulting post "Ad", even when nothing is asked in return; gifts count as payment [13]. Brands check posts, act on unlabelled ones, and keep working with a creator who keeps failing at their own risk [13].
- **EU.** Brand partnerships and affiliate marketing must be disclosed. Creators who regularly sell or advertise are treated as traders, with the duties that brings [21].

**Approvals.** Inviting a new creator into a paid commission offer names a new recipient of money, so it needs the client's fresh yes every time (a named list can be approved at once). So does any change to commission rates. Sending samples to creators on the approved list, within a sample budget, is routine work at level 3. The creator-affiliates-and-samples skill holds the method; the affiliate kit holds reward maths.

### LIVE selling

A LIVE is a timed show: warm-up, product order, demo steps, offer moments, stock calls, comment answers and a close. The client or their host goes live; I write the run sheet, the host notes and the comment answers, and at level 3 can set up the approved offers.

- **TikTok's US LIVE rules (as of 7 Nov 2026).** No AI-generated voices, pre-recorded clips or screen recordings in place of a live host. No links, phone numbers or QR codes that send viewers off TikTok Shop. Giveaways only through TikTok's own LIVE Giveaway feature, free to enter, and no raffles or chance games [30]. Other markets [check in Seller Center].

- **Urgency must be true.** The CMA's examples of misleading claims include offers that do not end when the timer says, a new offer with the same deal straight after, "only 5 left" when more stock is coming, and "10 people are viewing" used to rush buyers who need not hurry [16]. Under the DMCC Act, falsely claiming an offer is only available for a limited time is banned outright [17]. On 14 October 2026 the CMA fined Wayfair £4.8 million because many "Daily Deals" and 24-hour "Flash Deals" stayed at the same or a lower price after they ended [18].
- **"Was" prices must be real.** In the EU, a stated reduction is measured from the lowest price in at least the 30 days before it [23]. In the UK, the comparison price must be a genuine, usual selling price, not a short-lived hike, an old price or one at which few sold [16]. In the US, a former price must be one openly offered in good faith for a reasonably substantial period [7]. The pricing strategist kit has the full rules.
- **Disclosure repeats.** When a creator sells a brand's product live, the FTC asks for disclosures repeated through the stream or shown all the time, and in the stream description [1].
- **Restricted products stay off.** In September 2023 the ASA ruled against ZOVOO (Shenzhen) Technology after an influencer promoted its unlicensed nicotine vape on a public TikTok account; e-cigarette ads are not allowed in that kind of public online media in the UK [12].

### Claims on camera

- **Health, beauty and wellness.** Health claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence, with randomised controlled human trials as the standard; anecdotes never suffice. Testimonials showing results better than typical users get are likely deceptive, and "results not typical" does not fix it. Before-and-after images can imply claims that need proof [4].
- **Borrowed claims count.** In September 2025 the ASA ruled against Simmer for a TikTok ad that reused a TV clip saying its meals were curated by Michelin-starred chefs; the chef it relied on had been junior staff for nine months [11].
- **Reviews.** The FTC's rule (in force 21 October 2024) bans fake reviews, incentives that require a positive (or negative) review, undisclosed insider reviews, review suppression and fake followers or views. Free products for reviews are allowed if no sentiment is required, but undisclosed incentives can still break the law [3]. In the UK, fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews are banned, and businesses that publish reviews need a policy, a risk assessment and reasonable steps to stop them [15].

### Fulfilment and returns

TikTok Shop sets its own dispatch, delivery and return rules for each market. **US rules (as of 7 Nov 2026):**

- **Dispatch.** Regular orders must be shipped (marked In Transit) within 2 business days. TikTok asks sellers to keep their late dispatch rate at 4% or below and may act above 10%. Valid tracking must stay at 95% or more and on-time delivery at 80% or more [14].
- **Returns.** Buyers can ask for a return within 30 days of delivery, started in the app [25]. Sellers have 4 business days to answer a return or refund request, or TikTok may approve it automatically [26].

UK and EU dispatch and return rules [check in Seller Center]. The law sits underneath:

- **US.** Any shipping time a seller states needs a reasonable basis. With no stated time, the seller must expect to ship within 30 days. If an order will be late, the buyer gets a delay notice with a new date and the right to cancel for a full refund, paid within seven working days. Sellers stay liable when a fulfilment house or drop shipper causes the breach [5].
- **UK.** Before an order, buyers get the total price, delivery costs and expected delivery time. Goods are delivered within 30 days unless agreed otherwise. Buyers can cancel within 14 days of delivery without a reason; if not told of this right, they get up to 12 months [20]. Prices must include mandatory delivery fees wherever they appear, and the CMA's 2 November 2026 letter says marketplaces must include each trader's delivery charge in that product's total [19].
- **EU.** Buyers can withdraw within 14 days of delivery for goods, with exceptions such as perishable goods, made-to-order or personalised items, and unsealed audio, video or software; buyers pay return postage unless the seller offers to pay or did not tell them before purchase, and traders pay for returning defective goods [22].

### Real businesses, real lessons

- **Jemella (GHD) with Emily Canham, ASA, 4 Nov 2020.** A TikTok video of GHD hair tools with a "TODAY ONLY" 20% off code was ruled an ad because it came from a paid contract and used the contract's code; nothing like an upfront "#ad" made that clear [10].
- **Simmer, ASA, 17 Sep 2025.** A claim borrowed from TV still had to be true [11].
- **ZOVOO (Shenzhen) Technology, ASA, 13 Sep 2023.** A restricted product promoted on public TikTok; the seller also failed to answer the ASA [12].
- **Wayfair, CMA, 14 Oct 2026.** £4.8 million for deals that did not end when they said [18].
- **Aldi Süd, Court of Justice, 26 Sep 2024.** A banana price shown as 23% off was the same as its lowest price of the previous 30 days; the reduction had to be counted from that lowest price [23].

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Each figure has a source, a date and a caveat.

- **TikTok use by age, US:** 65% (18 to 29), 45% (30 to 49), 31% (50 to 64), 11% (65+), surveyed 30 Jan to 18 Jun 2026 [28]. *Caveat:* use of TikTok, not of TikTok Shop.
- **Disclosure gaps in the EU:** Ireland's CCPC found 48.4% of commercial content it reviewed unlabelled (2022); Spain's preliminary result found on average 77.75% of content investigated non-compliant (investigation from July 2023) [21]. *Caveat:* national sweeps, mostly Instagram, cited by the Commission.
- **US marketplace seller thresholds:** 200 sales and $5,000 in 12 months for verification; $20,000 a year for disclosure to buyers [6].
- **US shipping default:** 30 days when no time is stated; refunds within seven working days after cancellation [5].
- **UK delivery and cancellation:** 30 days to deliver unless agreed; 14 days to cancel from delivery [20].
- **UK fines:** Wayfair £4.8 million (October 2026) [18]. DMCC fines reach 10% of global turnover or £300,000, whichever is higher [18].

- **TikTok Shop fees:** US referral fee 6% per order (from 1 April 2024), refund administration fee 20% of it, capped at $5 per item [2]; UK commission 9% including VAT [9]. *Caveat:* check the client's Seller Center; trade reports describe 2026 changes.
- **TikTok US service levels:** ship within 2 business days; late dispatch at or below 4% advised, action possible above 10%; valid tracking 95%; on-time delivery 80%; returns asked within 30 days of delivery; sellers answer in 4 business days [14][25][26].

## 5. Decision rules

- If the fee terms for this market are not in front of me, ask for the Seller Center fee page before naming any hero, because a margin built on a guessed fee is wrong.
- If a product's contribution after fees, commission and the planned discount is at or below zero, drop it from hero picks or change the offer, because volume only multiplies the loss.
- If a product needs a health, before-and-after or "clinically proven" claim to sell, get the evidence first or cut the claim [4].
- If a video or LIVE shows a "was" price, check the price history first: lowest 30-day price for EU buyers [23], a genuine usual price for UK buyers [16], a bona fide former price for US buyers [7].
- If a LIVE uses a countdown or a stock call, make sure the offer ends and the stock count is real, or drop the line [16][18].
- If a creator gets anything of value (sample, commission, fee, discount code), require an upfront "Ad" label in the video and a spoken one when they speak about the product, for every market it reaches [1][8][13].
- If a creator's past sponsored posts are unlabelled, treat it as a red flag before sending samples [13].
- If the client wants positive reviews in return for samples, refuse the condition [3].
- If inviting a creator into a paid commission offer, or changing a commission rate, prepare it and get a fresh yes, because it names a new recipient of money or changes what is paid.
- If stock or dispatch capacity cannot meet a LIVE plan, cut the plan, because a late order needs a delay notice and may end in a refund [5].
- If a rule in this kit and a rule in Seller Center differ, follow the stricter one and tell the client.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **Hero by hype, not margin.** Spot it: a hero product with no margin table, or a table with no fee or commission line.
- **Fees from memory.** Spot it: a fee rate with no date and no screenshot from Seller Center.
- **Fake urgency on LIVE.** Spot it: "last chance" every week, timers that reset, "only a few left" with full stock [16].
- **Invented "was" prices.** Spot it: a strikethrough price that was charged for a few days, or never [7][16][23].
- **Disclosure in the caption only.** Spot it: a creator video with "#ad" lost in hashtags or only in the description [1][8].
- **Gifts with no instructions.** Spot it: samples sent with no note on labelling [13].
- **Claims the client cannot prove.** Spot it: "cures", "clinically proven", dramatic before-and-after with no study [4][11].
- **Over-promising dispatch.** Spot it: "ships tomorrow" with stock at a supplier [5].
- **Restricted products slipped in.** Spot it: vapes, supplements or regulated goods with no check against TikTok's lists and ad codes [12].
- **Reading sales, not profit.** Spot it: the weekly review ranks by GMV only.

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Does every hero product have a margin table using the client's current fee terms, commission and discount?
2. Is every claim in listings, videos and LIVE scripts backed by evidence the client holds?
3. Are "was" prices and percentages checked against price history for each market?
4. Are LIVE countdowns, stock calls and deadlines true?
5. Does every creator brief carry market-correct disclosure wording and gift instructions?
6. Are new paid creators and commission changes with the client for a fresh yes?
7. Can stock and dispatch meet the plan, and is there a delay-notice template?
8. Do returns follow both the law and TikTok's rules for the market?
9. Is every TikTok rule I relied on dated and sourced from Seller Center or the Academy?
10. Does the weekly review end in three actions, ranked by profit?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *30-day launch plan:* hero products with a one-line why, prices and offers, posting and LIVE calendar, who does what, and the numbers to watch weekly.
- *Margin table:* one row per product, every cost on its own column, contribution per order and at the planned discount.
- *Listing sheet:* title, five selling points, description, specs and care, image order, compliance notes.
- *Video brief:* product, hook, show, price, pin, on-screen text, disclosure line.
- *LIVE run sheet:* a minute-by-minute table with product order, talking points, offer timing, stock calls, comment answers and the host checklist.
- *Creator brief and sample rules:* who qualifies, what they get, message, must-nots, disclosure by market, link and code.
- *Weekly review:* sales and contribution by product, video, LIVE and creator; what to cut, repeat and test; three actions.

**Tone and conventions.** Short, energetic and plain. Tables first. Every TikTok rule carries "[as of date, from Seller Center]" or "[check in Seller Center]". Prices and claims marked "[client to confirm]" until confirmed. Never "viral" or "guaranteed".

## 9. Glossary

- **TikTok Shop:** TikTok's in-app shopping, where products are shown and sold through videos and LIVE sessions [27].
- **Seller Center:** the seller's TikTok Shop dashboard, where fees, rules and exports live.
- **Hero product:** one of two or three products the plan leads with.
- **Contribution per order:** price after discount minus all costs and fees for that order.
- **Shoppable video:** a short video with a pinned product link.
- **LIVE selling:** a live stream where the host demonstrates and sells products.
- **Run sheet:** the minute-by-minute plan for a LIVE.
- **Affiliate creator:** a creator who sells a shop's products for commission.
- **Sample:** free product sent to a creator; a material connection that must be disclosed [1][13].
- **Material connection:** any payment, gift, job or tie that must be disclosed [1].
- **Ad label:** "Ad" or similar shown upfront on paid or gifted content [8].
- **Was price (reference price):** a higher comparison price shown beside a sale price [7][16].
- **Prior price:** in the EU, the lowest price in at least the 30 days before a reduction [23].
- **Urgency claim:** a timer, scarcity, popularity or "act fast" message [16].
- **Drip pricing:** showing part of the price first and adding mandatory charges later [19].
- **High-volume seller:** a US marketplace seller over the INFORM Act thresholds [6].
- **Trader:** in EU law, anyone selling or advertising in the course of business, including many creators [21].
- **Delay notice:** the message a US seller must send when an order will ship late [5].
- **Right to cancel (withdrawal):** the buyer's 14 days in the UK and EU [20][22].
- **Substantiation:** the evidence that backs a claim [4].
- **GMV:** gross merchandise value, total sales before costs and returns.

## 12. Defaults to avoid

An AI model with no context falls back on these choices, so they make work look like everyone else's. Avoid each one unless the client's captured brand or the agreed plan chooses it on purpose, and then say why. The full shared list is in tools/ai-default-patterns.md.

- **Every video in one look.** Product videos all filmed in the same set-up and edit template. Vary the creator, setting and format per video.
- **One hook formula reused.** Every video and LIVE segment opens with the same line pattern. Write hooks per product and per creator.
- **Product on a gradient.** Listing images and video covers with the product floating on a gradient. Show the product in use and at real size.
- **Fake urgency.** Countdowns, "only 3 left" and "today only" that are not true. Section 5 already says to drop them unless real.
- **Stock hype words.** Titles and selling points with "Elevate", "Seamless" or "Game-changer". Use plain product facts the client can back.
- **Benefits any competitor could claim.** Selling points that fit every rival listing. Lead with the specific difference.
- **Rule-of-three everywhere.** Video scripts built around three benefits for every product by habit. Lead with the one point that sells this product.
