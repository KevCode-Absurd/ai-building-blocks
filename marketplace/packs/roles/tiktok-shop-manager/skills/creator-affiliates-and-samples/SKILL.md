# Creator affiliates and samples

## When to use

- Starting creator selling on TikTok Shop.
- Sample requests are piling up and the client does not know whom to say yes to.
- Creator sales are up but margin, returns or labelling are a worry.
- Before a creator hosts a LIVE with the brand's products.

For deeper reward maths, partner terms and creator search tools, use the affiliate and influencer marketing manager kit.

## What you need

1. The margin table from the hero-product-margin-pick skill, with contribution on creator-sold orders.
2. The client's target: the most they will give up per order to a creator, and a sample budget per month (units and cost).
3. TikTok's current creator, commission and sample rules for the market. In the US (as of 7 Nov 2026): answer free sample requests within 7 days, ship within 7 days of approval, and expect a post within 14 days of delivery. Other markets [check in Seller Center]. With web search connected I read the Seller University pages and note the date; without it, the client sends screenshots.
4. Current creators and their results (orders, returns, posts, labelling), if any.
5. The approved claims list and the proof behind it.
6. Markets each creator's audience is in.

**Access:** level 1 to read creator performance and requests. Level 2 to prepare invitation lists, sample decisions and messages for the client to send. Level 3 to send approved messages, and approve sample requests from creators on the approved list within the sample budget. Inviting a new creator into a paid commission offer, or changing a rate, needs the client's fresh yes every time.

## Steps

### 1. Set the commission ceiling

For each hero: commission ceiling = contribution on an own-video order − the client's minimum profit per order. Show it as a percentage of the base TikTok's terms use. Offer below the ceiling; the gap is room for a later bonus or a better rate for proven sellers.

### 2. Set the sample rules

Write a one-page rule the client approves once:

- **Who qualifies:** audience in the client's markets and age range; content that shows products working, not just unboxing; past paid or gifted posts labelled properly; no claims the brand could not back; no restricted products promoted carelessly.
- **What they get:** which products, how many units, shipping method.
- **What we ask:** a video or LIVE within a stated time if they choose to post, the "Ad" label upfront, a spoken disclosure, the approved claims only. No requirement for a positive review.
- **Budget:** units and cost per month; stop when reached.

### 3. Judge requests

For each request: fit (yes, maybe, no with the reason), audience market, past labelling, expected orders (from their past sales if TikTok shows them), and the cost of the sample against the contribution of the orders needed to pay it back. Approve, decline or ask a question. At level 3, approvals within the rule and budget go ahead for creators on the approved list; others wait for the client.

### 4. Write the creator brief

One page: the product, the one result to show, two or three approved facts, the must-nots, the price and offer as approved, the deadline, and the disclosure rules:

- **US:** say and show the connection; on TikTok put larger text over the video, not just in the caption; in a LIVE, repeat it or keep it on screen and put it in the description. A free sample counts even if returned.
- **UK:** "Ad" upfront, before people click or from the first second; "gifted", "sponsored", a tag or a code alone is not enough. Any gift comes with clear instructions, in writing, to label resulting posts "Ad".
- **EU:** disclose the partnership clearly; creators who regularly sell are traders with their own duties.

Use TikTok's own content disclosure setting as well, never instead of a plain label.

### 5. Check every post

Within a few days of each post or LIVE: label present and upfront, claims within the approved list, price and offer correct, product correct. Log it. If a label is missing, ask for a fix the same day. If a creator fails twice, pause their samples and tell the client. Keep checking for a few months after a creator stops.

### 6. Read the results monthly

By creator: orders, returns, contribution after commission and sample cost, posts, labelling record. Keep, grow, pause or stop. Suggest rate changes for the client to approve.

## Checks

- Commission offers sit below the ceiling for every hero.
- The sample rule is approved and dated.
- No sample is conditional on a positive review.
- Every brief carries disclosure wording for each market the creator reaches.
- Gifts carry clear labelling instructions.
- New paid creators and rate changes are listed for the client's fresh yes, never sent on my own.
- Posts are checked and logged; failures followed up.

## Output format

1. **Commission ceiling table:** product, contribution (own video), minimum profit, ceiling, suggested offer.
2. **Sample rule** (one page, for approval).
3. **Request log:** creator, fit, market, past labelling, decision, reason, status (approved within limits / waiting for client).
4. **Creator brief** (one page).
5. **Post check log:** creator, date, label, claims, offer, action.
6. **Monthly creator review** with keep, grow, pause or stop.
