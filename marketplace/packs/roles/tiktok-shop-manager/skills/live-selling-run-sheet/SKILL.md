# LIVE selling run sheet

## When to use

- Before every LIVE selling session, from a first 30-minute test to a two-hour sale event.
- When a creator will sell the brand's products live and needs host notes.
- After a LIVE that sold little, to rebuild the order and timing.

## What you need

1. Date, start time, planned length, market (US, UK or an EU country), and who hosts.
2. Products in the session: price, any offer, stock that can ship on time, margin after fees at the offer price (from the hero-product-margin-pick skill).
3. The offers: what, when they start and end, and what happens to the price after. Price history if any "was" price or percentage will be said or shown.
4. Demo props and the proof shots for each product.
5. Claims the host may make, with the client's evidence.
6. Common questions and complaints from past comments, reviews or support.
7. TikTok's current LIVE and promotion rules for the market [check in Seller Center or the Academy].

**Access:** level 1 to read past LIVE results. Level 2 to set up the session's promotions without starting them. Level 3 to start and end approved promotions at the approved times and prices. The host runs the stream.

## Steps

### 1. Set the goal and limits

One goal (orders of the hero, bundle take-up, or first-time buyers) and the limits: lowest price per product, total stock per product for the session, and the latest dispatch date the client can meet.

### 2. Check every offer before writing

- Margin after fees and commission at the offer price is above the client's floor.
- Any "was" price or percentage is valid for the market: EU, measured from the lowest price in the previous 30 days or more; UK, against a genuine usual selling price, not a short-lived or rarely-used one; US, a bona fide former price offered openly for a reasonably substantial period.
- Each deadline is real: the price goes back up when it ends, and no near-identical offer follows straight after (the CMA treats that as misleading).
- Stock calls will be true counts, not scripted lines.

Anything that fails is rewritten or cut before the run sheet is built.

### 3. Order the products

Open with the strongest demo, not the cheapest item. Put the hero in the first third and repeat it near the end for late joiners. Place the bundle or add-on right after the product it pairs with. Keep a backup product for if stock runs out.

### 4. Time the blocks

A typical block per product: hook and demo (2 to 4 minutes), proof and questions (2 to 3 minutes), price and offer (1 minute), pin and call to action (1 minute). Add a warm-up at the start and a recap every 15 minutes for people who join late.

### 5. Write the host lines

For each block: the hook line, demo steps, the two or three facts to say, the price line exactly as approved, and the pin cue. Keep claims to the approved list.

### 6. Add disclosure lines

If a creator is hosting for the brand, or anyone with a connection to it: a spoken and on-screen "Ad" at the start, repeated through the stream or shown all the time, and in the stream description. US guidance asks for periodic or continuous disclosure in live streams; UK guidance asks for labels upfront.

### 7. Prepare comment answers

Ten to fifteen short answers: sizes, ingredients, shipping times (true ones), returns, how to use, price questions. Include a holding line for anything the host does not know: "Great question, we'll check and reply after the LIVE."

### 8. Write the host checklist

Stock counted, promotions set up and checked, products pinned in order, props ready, lighting and sound tested, moderator briefed, the approved claims list on screen, the "Ad" label ready if needed.

## Checks

- Every price line matches the approved offer and the product's price floor.
- Every "was" price has price history for the market.
- Every deadline really ends and the price goes back up.
- Stock calls come from a live count.
- No claim outside the approved list.
- Disclosure lines present and repeated where a creator or connected person hosts.
- Dispatch promises match what the client can ship.
- Restricted products are not in the session.

## Output format

1. **Session card:** date, time, length, market, host, goal, limits.
2. **Offer check table:** product, offer, price after offer, margin, "was" price status, deadline status.
3. **Run sheet table:** minute, segment, product, host lines, on-screen text, pin cue, offer action, stock note.
4. **Comment answers.**
5. **Host checklist.**
6. **After the LIVE:** the numbers to pull (orders, views, average watch time, product clicks, orders by product) and the one thing to change next time.

## Example of good output

*Fernleaf Skin (a made-up business). 45-minute LIVE, UK, Friday 7 pm. Host: the founder. Goal: 60 orders of the hand cream. Offer: hand cream and lip balm bundle at £14 instead of £17 bought separately, during the LIVE only; prices return to normal at 7:45 pm.*

**Offer check:** Bundle margin after fees £3.10 (above the £2.50 floor). No "was" price used; the saving is shown against the two current prices bought separately. Deadline real: bundle switched off at 7:45 pm.
