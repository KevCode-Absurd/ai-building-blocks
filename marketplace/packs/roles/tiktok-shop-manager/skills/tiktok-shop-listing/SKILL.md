# TikTok Shop listing

## When to use

- New products going onto TikTok Shop.
- Listings rejected, taken down or flagged in Seller Center.
- Listings with views but few orders.
- A Shopify catalogue synced through TikTok's app that needs TikTok-ready text.

## What you need

1. Product facts: name, type, size or count, materials or ingredients, colours and variants, dimensions and weight, care, what is in the box, warnings, country of origin if the client wants it shown.
2. Proof for every claim the client wants to make (tests, certificates, studies, reviews).
3. Current price and any planned offer, with 30 days or more of price history if a "was" price will show.
4. Photos and videos available, or a shot list.
5. TikTok's listing rules and restricted product rules for the market, from Seller Center or the Academy. With web search connected I open them and note the date; without it, the client sends screenshots.
6. Markets: US, UK, EU countries.
7. Up to five competitor listings the client admires (optional).

**Access:** level 1 to read current listings and rejection notices. Level 2 to put the new text in a bulk upload sheet or saved drafts. Level 3 to publish approved listings and fixes within the limits set (which products, no price changes unless approved).

## Steps

### 1. Check the product can be listed

- Is it on TikTok's prohibited or restricted list for this market, or does it need approval first? [check in Seller Center]
- Does the client's advertising code restrict it (in the UK, for example, the ASA ruled e-cigarette ads are not allowed on public TikTok accounts)?
- EU: is it on Safety Gate as recalled? The client should check before listing.
- US: if the client is or will soon be a high-volume seller under the INFORM Consumers Act, are their bank, tax and contact details verified with TikTok?

Stop and report if any answer is a problem.

### 2. Write the title

Product type first, then the key feature, then size, count or variant. Example shape: "Reed Diffuser, Fig and Cedar, 200 ml, 8 Reeds". Plain words; no all-caps, no claims, no prices or offers in the title unless TikTok's rules for the market allow it [check in Seller Center]. Use the length limit shown in Seller Center that day.

### 3. Write five selling points

Each one a benefit tied to a fact: "Lasts about 8 weeks in a medium room (client's own test, 2026)". Order them by what the buyer cares about most. If a point needs proof the client lacks, cut it or soften it to a fact ("200 ml of oil" rather than "lasts for months").

### 4. Write the description

Three short parts: what it is and who it is for; how to use it; specs, care and what is in the box. Add warnings (allergens, age, safety). Match the language of each market.

### 5. Set the image order

1. Product on a plain background, whole and clear.
2. In use, showing the result.
3. Scale (in a hand or room).
4. Detail or texture.
5. What is in the box.
6. Size or variant chart if there are variants.

No added claims in images that the text could not carry.

### 6. Check claims and prices

- Health, beauty and wellness claims need competent and reliable scientific evidence; testimonials must reflect typical results (FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance).
- Claims borrowed from press or TV still need proof (ASA, Simmer, 2025).
- A "was" price follows the pricing rules for each market: the EU's 30-day lowest prior price, a genuine usual price in the UK, a bona fide former price in the US. See the pricing strategist kit.
- UK prices shown must include mandatory delivery charges, including on marketplaces (CMA letter, 2 Nov 2026).

### 7. Write the compliance note

One line per listing: claims checked, proof file, restricted status, price history checked, markets.

## Checks

- Title starts with the product type.
- Every selling point is a fact or has proof on file.
- No banned or restricted product without approval.
- No "was" price without price history for each market.
- Variants clearly named and pictured.
- Warnings present where the product needs them.
- Text fits Seller Center's current limits (checked that day).
- Nothing in images contradicts the text.

## Output format

For each product:

1. **Title**
2. **Selling points** (five bullets)
3. **Description** (three short paragraphs)
4. **Specs and care** (table)
5. **In the box**
6. **Image order** (numbered, with shot notes)
7. **Compliance note**
8. **Open questions** for the client

Then a summary table for upload: product, title, price, variants, status (ready, needs proof, needs approval).

## Example of good output

*Fernleaf Skin (a made-up business).*

**Title:** Hand Cream, Oat and Shea, 75 ml Tube, Fragrance Free

**Selling points**
- Fragrance free, for people who avoid scented creams.
- 10% shea butter (from the client's formula sheet).
- Absorbs in about a minute in the client's own wear test (12 people, 2026) [proof on file].
- 75 ml tube fits a coat pocket or bag.
- Vegan formula, certified by [certifier named on client's certificate].

**Description**
A rich, unscented hand cream made with oat and shea for dry hands.
Rub a pea-sized amount into clean hands as often as needed.
75 ml. Store below 25°C. For external use only. Patch test if you have sensitive skin.

**Specs and care:** 75 ml / 2.5 fl oz; ingredients list from label; made in [client to confirm].

**In the box:** one 75 ml tube.

**Image order:** 1. Tube on white. 2. Cream squeezed on a hand. 3. Tube in a hand for scale. 4. Close-up of texture. 5. Ingredients panel.

**Compliance note:** "Heals cracked skin" removed: no clinical proof on file. "Dermatologist approved" removed: no study supplied. No "was" price shown (no 30-day history yet). Category check: cosmetics, allowed in US and UK [as of 6 Nov 2026, from client's Seller Center screenshot].

**Open questions:** Can you send the vegan certificate? Which countries are you listing in first?
