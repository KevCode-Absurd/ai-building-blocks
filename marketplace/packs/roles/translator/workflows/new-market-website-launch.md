# New market website launch

**Trigger:** a business decides to sell or serve customers in a new language or country and wants its website (or shop) ready in that language.

**Cadence:** once per new market. The timeline depends on the volume of text and how quickly the native reviewer can work, so agree it at step 1.

## Steps

1. **Brief and access (prompt: translation-brief-intake).** Target locale, readers, purpose, tone, reviewer, deadline. Record the access level granted per tool under "Access I have"; level 1 until the client answers.
2. **Risk triage (prompt: translation-risk-triage).** Sort pages into machine translation with full post-editing, human translation, transcreation (headlines, slogans) and refer out (terms and conditions, returns policy, regulated claims for a qualified local reviewer).
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves the page list, methods and reviewer.
3. **Glossary and style sheet (skill: glossary-and-style-sheet).** Harvest terms, agree form of address and formats, get native sign-off, load the glossary into the engine or plugin.
4. **URL and search plan.** Choose the URL structure (country domain, subdomain or subdirectory), list the hreflang set for each page with return links and an x-default, and remove any automatic language redirect.
5. **Translate and localise (skill: translate-and-localise-content).** Product pages, category pages, checkout and emails first, because they carry money. Then help and about pages.
6. **Post-edit any machine output (skill: machine-translation-post-editing).** Full post-editing for everything published; sample error log per page type.
7. **Locale check (prompt: locale-format-check)** on checkout, prices, delivery and contact pages; **UI strings (prompt: ui-strings-and-placeholders)** for the theme, apps and transactional emails.
8. **Native review.** The reviewer works from the bilingual table and the notes. Log every change and feed it back to the glossary.
   **Approval point 2:** the client (or their reviewer) signs off the translations and the transcreated lines.
9. **Load into the site.**
   - Level 2: save translations unpublished in the plugin or platform (for example Weglot, WPML, Shopify Translate & Adapt) and send preview links.
   - Level 3: publish the languages and pages in the confirmed action list, within the client's limits. Log each action.
   **Approval point 3:** the client says yes to the action list before anything goes live.
10. **Post-launch check (week 1).** Click through every translated page on a phone, test the checkout in the new currency, check hreflang with a crawler or Search Console, and read the first customer messages in the new language for confusion.
11. **Report.** What went live, the action log, open questions, glossary changes and the date of the next content review.

## Outputs

- Brief, triage table and approved page list.
- Glossary, do-not-translate list and style sheet, in engine-ready files.
- URL and hreflang plan.
- Translated and reviewed pages, UI strings and emails.
- Error logs and reviewer notes.
- Action log and launch report.

## Needs the client's approval

- Page list, methods and reviewer (point 1).
- Final translations and every transcreated headline or slogan (point 2).
- The level 3 action list and limits before anything goes live (point 3), and a new yes before anything outside them, such as a new language, overwriting approved translations or deleting a glossary.
- Legal pages and regulated claims, which go to a qualified local reviewer chosen by the client.
