# Language practice set

## When to use

- A teacher or tutor needs practice for one lesson topic at a set level.
- A workplace course needs role-plays for real situations (reception, phone calls, complaints).
- A learner wants extra practice on a unit of their course book, using their own word list.
- A class needs a short level or progress check.
- Learners need short daily practice between classes.

Do not use it for certified translation, official level certificates, real exam papers, or to complete a learner's homework or graded task.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the syllabus, word lists, unit topics and past learner errors.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the set and answer key as documents; draft daily practice messages; build a deck file.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** post the practice set to the named class, and send the approved daily practice messages to learners who opted in (with a parent's consent for children), within the confirmed limits. Stop messaging anyone who asks. Log every action.

## What you need

1. Language, CEFR level (A1 to C1), topic and situation.
2. Class size, lesson length, adult or young learners, and the learners' first language if instructions should use it.
3. The course book unit or word list to work from, if any.
4. Grammar points the unit covers.
5. What learners find hard (common errors).

## Steps

### 1. Fix the level

Keep grammar and vocabulary inside the level for every item. Mark any word above it with an asterisk and a gloss. Use the CEFR's can-do descriptions to set the task: an A1 learner handles familiar everyday phrases; a B1 learner copes with most situations while travelling and can describe experiences; a C1 learner uses language flexibly for social and work purposes.

### 2. Choose the words

Pick 12 to 25 key words or phrases from the word list or the topic. Give each a short example sentence at the level. For reading tasks, keep unknown words few: when learners know nearly every word in a text, they can read it without help.

### 3. Write the dialogue

A short, natural dialogue that uses the words in a real situation. Name the speakers, keep turns short, and include one or two of the unit's grammar points.

### 4. Write the drills, easy to hard

- **Recognise:** match words to pictures, meanings or situations.
- **Choose:** gap fill with options, or pick the right form.
- **Produce:** complete sentences, rewrite, answer questions in full sentences.

Six to ten items each. Each item has one right answer or a clear model answer.

### 5. Add a speaking task

A role-play with a clear goal for each person, useful phrases on each card and a challenge card for stronger learners. Example goals: "book a table for four at 8 pm", "politely refuse the first room offered".

### 6. Balance the lesson

Check the set covers more than drills: listening or reading for meaning (the dialogue), speaking or writing for meaning (the role-play), focused study of words and grammar (the drills), and a quick fluency round with known language under a little time pressure.

### 7. Write the answer key

All answers, model answers for open tasks, and a note on the common errors to listen for.

### 8. Optional extras

- **Level check:** a short test across the skills taught, with answers and what each score range means for the next unit. It is a teaching check, not an official certificate.
- **Vocabulary deck:** the words with examples as an import file, both directions if wanted, tagged by unit (see `flashcard-deck-builder`).
- **Daily practice messages:** a run of short messages, each with one small task and the answer the next day.

### 9. Final check

Instructions in simple words (or the learners' first language if asked); nothing copied from a published book; every item answerable at the level.

## Checks

- Every word and structure fits the level, or is marked and glossed.
- Drills go from recognise to choose to produce.
- One right answer or a model answer per item.
- The role-play has a goal for each person.
- The set includes meaning-focused and fluency work, not only drills.
- No published book text copied; no learner homework done for them.
- At level 3, only the named class and opted-in learners were contacted, and the log is updated.

## Output format

1. Level, topic and goals (can-do statement)
2. Key words with examples
3. Dialogue
4. Drills 1 to 3
5. Role-play cards
6. Fluency round
7. Answer key and common errors
8. Optional: level check, deck file, daily messages

## Example of good output (short)

*Made-up class for illustration: adult beginners in Spanish, CEFR A1, ordering in a cafe.*

> **Can-do:** order a drink and a snack and ask for the bill.
>
> **Key words:** un café con leche (a white coffee): *Un café con leche, por favor.* / la cuenta (the bill): *La cuenta, por favor.* / ¿Cuánto es? (How much is it?)
>
> **Dialogue**
> Camarero: Hola, ¿qué quiere tomar?
> Ana: Un café con leche y un cruasán, por favor.
> Camarero: Muy bien. ¿Algo más?
> Ana: No, gracias. ¿Cuánto es?
> Camarero: Son cuatro euros.
>
> **Drill 2 (choose):** 1. ___ café con leche, por favor. (un / una) 2. La ___, por favor. (cuenta / cuánto)
> **Answers:** 1. un 2. cuenta
>
> **Role-play:** Card A: you want a tea and a sandwich, and you have five euros. Card B: you are the waiter; the sandwich is finished, offer a croissant instead.
>
> **Listen for:** *una café* (café is masculine).
