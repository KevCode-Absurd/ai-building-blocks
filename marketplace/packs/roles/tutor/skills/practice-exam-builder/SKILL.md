# Practice exam builder

## When to use

- A learner or team is preparing for a certification or a course exam and wants realistic practice.
- A training provider needs a question bank sorted by topic and difficulty.
- A learner's practice scores show weak domains and they need targeted drills and a plan.

Do not use it to supply real exam questions, leaked papers or "brain dumps", to sit a test for someone, or to promise a pass. Exam bodies treat unauthorised materials as a breach of their rules.

## Access needed

- **Level 1 (read):** the study guide, exam outline and past practice results.
- **Level 2 (read and draft):** save the exam, key and bank as documents, or as an unpublished quiz in the class tool.
- **Level 3 (read, write and publish):** post the practice quiz to the class or group named in the limits, set as practice and not counted towards any mark, and add study sessions to the calendar. Log every action.

## What you need

1. The study guide, notes or course material the questions must come from.
2. The exam outline: domains or topics and their weights, question formats, number of questions, time limit, pass mark if published.
3. The learner's level and weakest areas, and past practice scores if any.
4. How many questions, and the mix of difficulty wanted.
5. For a study plan: exam date and hours per week.

## Steps

### 1. Build the blueprint

List domains and weights from the outline. If there are no weights, use the share of the material each topic takes, and say so. Set questions per domain, the difficulty mix (by default about a quarter easy, half medium, a quarter hard) and the formats.

### 2. Write each question from one part of the material

- Tag it with the domain and the exact place in the material (section, page or slide).
- Use the exam's style: best answer, scenario ("what should you do next?"), or multiple response, as the outline says.
- Write original wording and situations. Never copy or rebuild a real exam item, a past paper you were not given by the course, or a question bank you do not own.
- One clearly right answer. Distractors are plausible mistakes a learner really makes, not jokes or trick wording.
- Keep option lengths similar and avoid "all of the above" unless the exam uses it.

### 3. Explain every option

For the right answer: why it is right, citing the material. For each wrong option: why it is wrong or what it would be right for. This is where most of the learning happens.

### 4. Check difficulty and independence

- Easy questions test recall; medium ones apply a rule; hard ones combine ideas or pick between two good options.
- No question gives away the answer to another.
- Count questions per domain against the blueprint.

### 5. Lay it out

Learner copy first, with no answers and the time limit. Then the answer key with explanations. Then a score guide: score bands by domain and what to review for each.

### 6. Turn results into a plan

When the learner returns their answers: score by domain, find the two or three weakest, and plan the days to the exam with practice on those first, mixed review of the rest, and one more full practice exam near the end. Keep it to the hours they have.

### 7. For a question bank

Group by domain and difficulty, with the same tags and explanations, so the learner can drill one area. Offer a CSV for a quiz or flashcard tool.

## Checks

- Every question traces to a named place in the learner's material.
- One right answer each, with reasons for every option.
- Domain counts match the blueprint.
- No real, leaked or recreated exam items; no claims about what "will be on the exam".
- Difficulty mix as agreed.
- At level 3, the quiz was posted only to the named group, set as practice, and the log is updated.

## Output format

1. Blueprint table: domain, weight, questions, difficulty mix
2. Learner copy
3. Answer key: question, answer, why, why not each other option, source in the material
4. Score guide by domain
5. Study plan (when scores are in)
6. Gaps: topics in the outline with no material, marked [not in your material]

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a software firm's internal product certification, with five domains from its own study guide. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

> **Blueprint (40 questions):** Setup 20% (8), Billing 25% (10), Reports 20% (8), Users 15% (6), Troubleshooting 20% (8). Difficulty: 10 easy, 20 medium, 10 hard.
>
> **Q14 (Billing, medium; study guide section 3.2)**
> A customer on the annual plan adds three seats in month five. According to the billing rules, what happens to their invoice?
> A. They pay for three seats for a full year from today
> B. They pay a prorated amount for the seven months left in their term
> C. Nothing changes until renewal
> D. Their whole plan restarts with a new 12-month term
>
> **Answer: B.** Section 3.2: added seats are prorated to the end of the current term.
> - A is wrong: that would extend the seats past the customer's term.
> - C is wrong: section 3.2 says seats are billed when added, not at renewal.
> - D is wrong: adding seats does not change the term dates (section 3.4).
>
> **Score guide:** under 6 of 10 in Billing, reread section 3 and redo the Billing drill set.
