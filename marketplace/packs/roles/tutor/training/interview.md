# Training interview: tutor

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share names or personal details of real learners, especially children; anonymise every story.

1. **Who do you tutor or teach (ages, levels, subjects or languages, exams), and in which countries?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's level range, exam rules and safeguarding duties.

2. **Walk me through a first session with a new learner. What do you find out before you teach anything?**
   Why it matters: captures the expert's intake and diagnosis, the part plain AI skips.

3. **How do you tell what a learner really does not understand, as opposed to what they forgot?**
   Why it matters: diagnosis drives everything; the agent needs the expert's tells and questions.

4. **Show me revision notes you made that worked. What did you leave out?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent a real model and the cutting decisions behind it.

5. **How do you build a revision timetable when the learner has too little time?**
   Why it matters: real plans involve trade-offs; the agent should copy how the expert makes them.

6. **What makes a good flashcard, and what is the worst card you have seen a student make?**
   Why it matters: card-writing rules are easy to state and hard to apply; examples teach them.

7. **How do you write a practice question that teaches, not just tests? Show me one with its explanation.**
   Why it matters: the explanation habits are what make practice exams worth using.

8. **How do you pitch difficulty so a learner is stretched but not crushed?**
   Why it matters: the agent needs the expert's signals for raising or lowering the level.

9. **When a learner is stuck, what do you say first? What do you never say?**
   Why it matters: gives the agent its hint ladder in the expert's own words.

10. **For language learners: how do you fix the level, and how do you balance speaking, listening, reading, writing and drills?**
    Why it matters: captures the expert's lesson shape and level judgement.

11. **Tell me about a time a learner asked you to write or finish work that would be marked. What did you say?**
    Why it matters: the agent must refuse kindly and offer real help; the expert's wording is the template.

12. **Which exam board or course rules about outside help and AI do you check, and where?**
    Why it matters: tells the agent which official sources the expert trusts for their learners.

13. **How do you keep children safe in your tutoring: who knows what, what you record, and what you do if something worries you?**
    Why it matters: the agent must follow a clear safeguarding routine that matches the expert's.

14. **How do you keep in touch with parents or managers, and what do you report?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's reporting habits and boundaries.

15. **How do you motivate a learner who has given up, without empty praise?**
    Why it matters: tone and encouragement are craft; the agent should avoid flattery yet keep learners going.

16. **Which tools do you use (Anki, Quizlet, class platforms), and what goes wrong with them?**
    Why it matters: grounds the agent's tool advice in real problems.

17. **Tell me about a learner who did not improve. What would you do differently?**
    Why it matters: reveals the checks and changes the expert now builds in.

18. **What do you measure to know tutoring is working?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's progress checks and stops it relying on feel.

19. **What study advice do you hear often that you think is wrong?**
    Why it matters: the agent should avoid popular myths the expert has seen fail.
