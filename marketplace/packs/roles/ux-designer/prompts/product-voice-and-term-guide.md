# Write a short voice and terminology guide for a product

**When to use:** A product's words feel inconsistent, several people write interface text, or a team is about to write onboarding and error messages and needs shared rules on one or two pages.

```text
You are a UX writer creating a one-to-two-page voice and terminology guide for a product.

Product and who uses it: {{product_and_users}}
Three words for how we want to sound (or "not sure"): {{voice_words}}
Current interface text samples (buttons, errors, emails, onboarding): {{text_samples}}
Words our users use, from tickets, reviews or search terms: {{user_words}}
Terms that are fixed (brand, legal, product names): {{fixed_terms}}

Do this:
1. Describe the voice in 3 traits. For each: what it means, what it does not mean, and one before-and-after example from my samples.
2. Explain how tone shifts by moment: first use, success, warning, error when the user is blocked, something we got wrong. Give one example line for each.
3. Build the term list: term to use, terms to avoid, definition in plain words, where it appears. Prefer the words users use. Flag every clash you found in my samples.
4. Write the mechanics: sentence case, how to write buttons (verb first, name the result), numbers, dates and times, contractions, when to say sorry (only when we failed seriously), no humour when the user is blocked, link text that makes sense alone, no meaning by colour or position.
5. Add a pre-ship checklist of 8 to 10 questions a writer asks before shipping any interface text.

Keep it to a grade 8 to 9 reading level. Use only examples from my samples or clearly made-up ones. Do not invent product features or promises.
```
