# Site structure and navigation

## When to use

- A service firm, nonprofit, school or software site has grown page by page and visitors cannot find things.
- A redesign is starting and the menu is being decided by opinion.
- Support keeps answering questions that the site already answers somewhere.

Not for store category and filter design (that has its own patterns), visual design, or building the menu in code.

## What you need

1. **The page list:** a sitemap export, a crawl, or a list of URLs and titles.
2. **Evidence of what visitors want:** analytics (top pages, entry pages, exits), site search terms, common support and sales questions. Exports are enough.
3. **What the business needs visitors to do:** enquire, book, donate, apply, buy, sign in.
4. **Audiences:** the main visitor groups and their tasks (for example parents, students, staff at a school).
5. **Constraints:** pages that must exist (legal, policies), CMS limits on menu depth.
6. **Access I have:** the level the client grants in each tool (1 read, 2 read and draft, 3 read, write and publish). Steps 1 to 8 need level 1 only (reading exports or a read-only seat). Step 9 needs level 2 or 3 depending on what is changed. Until the client answers, work at level 1.

## Steps

1. **List and rank the top tasks.** From search terms, analytics and support questions, list what visitors come to do, in their words ("see prices", "book a consultation", "check term dates"). Rank by how often it happens and how much it matters to the visitor and the business. Mark the evidence for each: a count from the client's data, or "from interviews", or "judgement".
2. **Inventory the pages.** For each page: title, URL, what task it serves, traffic if known, last updated. Mark duplicates, outdated pages and pages that serve no task.
3. **Group pages by visitor task, not by internal teams.** "Services" grouped by what the visitor needs done, not by which department delivers it. If you cannot decide the groups, plan an open card sort (step 7) before going further.
4. **Draft the page tree.** Keep most pages within three levels of home. Put the top tasks one click from home. Every page has one parent. Write it as an indented list with each section tagged with the task it serves.
5. **Write the labels.** Plain nouns or short verb phrases in visitors' words ("Prices", "Book a visit", "Term dates"). Avoid clever names and vague words ("Solutions", "Explore", "Resources") unless research shows visitors use them. One name per thing, used the same way everywhere.
6. **Design the menus.**
   - **Main menu:** the top tasks and main sections, in order of importance. Keep it short enough to scan; note every item you cut and where it now lives.
   - **Footer:** contact, legal, policies, accessibility statement, secondary pages.
   - **Mobile menu:** how it folds, what stays visible outside it (for example the main action button), and touch targets at platform sizes.
   - **Breadcrumbs** for anything below level two.
   - **Search:** where it sits and what the empty and no-results states say.
   Check that every page has a home and at least one link to it, and that no top task is reachable only through search.
7. **Write the test plan.**
   - **Open card sort** (to find groups): 30 to 50 cards from real page titles; at least 15 participants for a qualitative read, 30 to 50 for a quantitative one; a few moderated sessions to hear the reasons.
   - **Tree test** (to check the chosen tree): the tree as text only; one task for each top task and each label you are unsure of (usually ten to fifteen in all), written as short scenarios that do not use the label words; an easy warm-up task first; the correct answer or answers defined for each task. Report success, directness and time per task, and where people went wrong.
   Name who to recruit (real or likely visitors from each audience) and how to read the results: a task with low success or low directness points to the label or group to change.
8. **Note the trade-offs.** What you chose, what you rejected, and the questions only testing can answer.
9. **Make changes only at the right level.**
   - Level 1: hand over the tree, menu spec, label list and test plan.
   - Level 2: build the tree test or card sort in the client's research tool as an unlaunched draft; draft the new menu in the CMS without publishing (where the CMS supports drafts or a staging site).
   - Level 3: after the client confirms the action list and limits (which site, which menus, which redirects, when), launch the study to the audience and budget the client set, or publish the menu change and redirects. Log each action. Ask again before deleting pages or changing URLs that are not on the agreed list.

## Checks

- Every top task is one click from home or clearly signposted on the home page.
- Every page has a parent and at least one inbound link; no orphans.
- Labels come from visitors' words, with the evidence noted.
- No label is used for two different things, and no thing has two labels.
- Tree test tasks do not contain the answer's label.
- Changed or removed URLs have redirects listed.
- Any level 2 or 3 action is in the action log.

## Output format

1. Top tasks table: task, audience, evidence, rank.
2. Page inventory summary: keep, merge, rewrite, remove.
3. Page tree (indented list, task tags).
4. Menu specs: main, footer, mobile, breadcrumbs, search.
5. Label changes: old, new, reason.
6. Test plan: card sort or tree test, tasks, recruits, how to read results.
7. Trade-offs and open questions.
8. Redirect list (if URLs change) and action log.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real organisation: a community choir run as a nonprofit. Evidence: site search export (three months) and 40 enquiry emails. Access I have: level 1 (exports). Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Top tasks:** 1. Find out how to join (most frequent search term). 2. See concert dates and buy tickets. 3. Check rehearsal times and venue. 4. Donate or sponsor.

**Main menu (old → new):** "About us" → "About"; "Get involved" → "Join the choir"; "What's on" → "Concerts and tickets"; "Members area" stays; "Support us" → "Donate".

**Tree test task 2:** "You want to take your aunt to the next Christmas performance. Where would you go to get seats?" Correct answer: Concerts and tickets > Christmas concert. (Avoids the words "concerts" and "tickets".)
