# Fix a website's navigation

**Trigger:** visitors cannot find key pages, support answers questions the site already covers, a redesign is starting, or the team is arguing about the menu. Usually three to six weeks of elapsed time.

## Steps

1. **Intake and access (access.md questions).** Confirm audiences, the top business goals, the CMS, and the deadline. Ask which access level the client grants for analytics, search console or site search logs, the CMS and the research tool; record it as "Access I have". Until they answer, work at level 1.
2. **Gather evidence.** Page list or crawl, top pages and entry pages, site search terms, the most common support and sales questions. Exports are enough at level 1. Remove personal data from any support export before sharing.
3. **Audit the current navigation (skill: site-structure-and-navigation, steps 1 and 2).** Top tasks ranked with their evidence; page inventory; dead ends, buried pages, duplicates and confusing labels, ranked by how many visitors they affect.
4. **Card sort if the groups are unclear (skill: site-structure-and-navigation, step 7).** Open sort with real page titles.
   - Level 2: set up the sort in the client's research tool as an unlaunched draft.
   - Level 3: launch it to the audience and within the budget the client set; log it.
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves recruiting, any panel spend and the study wording.
5. **Draft the new tree, labels and menus (skill: site-structure-and-navigation, steps 3 to 6).** Page tree, main menu, footer, mobile menu, breadcrumbs and search states.
6. **Tree test the draft.** Tasks that do not use the label words, a warm-up task, correct answers defined. Same level rules as step 4. Revise labels or groups where success or directness is low, and retest the changed parts.
   **Approval point 2:** the client approves the final tree and labels.
7. **Write the words (skill: interface-microcopy).** Menu labels, page titles, breadcrumb names and the search no-results message, all matching one term list.
8. **Plan the change.** Redirects for every changed or removed URL, a list of internal links to update, and what to watch after launch (site search exits, task-page visits, support contacts on the same topics).
9. **Make the change.**
   - Level 1 or 2: the client or their developer publishes; at level 2 I prepare the new menu in the CMS as a draft or on a staging site.
   - Level 3: after the client confirms the action list and limits (which menus, which redirects, which pages, when), publish the menu and redirects, then check every link. Log each action. Ask again before deleting any page or changing a URL that is not on the list.
   **Approval point 3:** the client approves the launch date and the redirect list.
10. **Check after launch.** Compare the watch list after four to six weeks with the weeks before the change, and report.

## Outputs

- Top tasks table and navigation audit.
- Card sort and tree test plans, and their results.
- Final page tree, menu specs and label list.
- Redirect list and launch checklist.
- Before-and-after report on the watch list.
- Action log of every change made in the client's tools (level 2 or 3).

## Needs the client's approval

- The access level for each tool, and the limits for level 3 (sites, menus, redirects, studies, panel budget, dates).
- Recruiting, panel spend and study wording (point 1).
- The final tree and labels (point 2).
- The launch date and redirect list (point 3).
- Deleting pages or changing URLs not on the agreed list, asked again each time.
