# Cue notes and chapters

Once the cut is decided, the editor needs to know what to show over it, where to slow down, where music comes in, and how the video is labelled. Every note here is tied to a timecode, uses only footage the client has, and uses only music the client can use on that platform.

## When to use

- The edit decision list is approved and the editor needs build notes.
- A talk-led video needs B-roll and pacing to hold viewers.
- A long video needs YouTube chapters and a description.

## What you need

- The approved edit decision list with timecodes in the cut (not the source).
- Shot notes listing every B-roll clip, graphic and logo the client has.
- Music the client wants, with licences or the source library.
- Platform, audience and brand style.
- **Access I have.** Notes, chapters and descriptions are level 1. Adding them to a draft or private upload is level 2. Adding chapters and descriptions to a live video, or publishing, is level 3 within the client's limits. Buying or accepting a music licence needs the client's fresh yes every time.

## Steps

1. **Map the cut.** List the sections of the approved cut with their timecodes in the cut.
2. **B-roll cues.** For each section, note where a picture would show what the speaker describes: product shots, screen recordings, place shots, documents. Use only footage from the shot notes; anything else is "[needs: ...]". Give in, out and length for each cue. Cover every jump cut flagged in the edit list.
3. **Pacing cues.** Mark where to hold (a key line, a laugh, a reveal), where to tighten, and where a short pause helps. Note J-cuts or L-cuts where sound should lead or trail a picture change.
4. **Graphics cues.** Lower thirds for each speaker's first appearance, key numbers on screen (only numbers the speaker said and can back up), and section titles. Exact text in quotes with character counts. Burned-in text in vertical cuts stays inside the safe zone (Meta's Reels ad spec: 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides clear, checked 6 Nov 2026).
5. **Music cues.** In, out, level (under speech or full), and the track. Check each track's licence against the platform and use:
   - A recording usually involves two works, the composition and the sound recording, licensed separately (U.S. Copyright Office).
   - No set number of seconds is automatically fair use (U.S. Copyright Office fair use index).
   - YouTube Audio Library tracks are copyright-safe on YouTube; Creative Commons tracks need credit in the description; YouTube does not vouch for "royalty-free" music from elsewhere.
   - Meta's Music Guidelines say commercial or non-personal use needs the right licences.
   - Creative Commons NC is non-commercial only; ND cannot be edited.
   - CapCut's terms page was unreachable to automated checks on 6 Nov 2026; confirm in-app licence labels.
   Anything without a licence covering this use is marked "replace". Ask the rights owner or a qualified adviser about any licence question.
6. **Chapters.** From the cut's sections, write chapter titles from what was said. YouTube's rules: first timestamp 00:00, at least three in ascending order, each at least 10 seconds; manual chapters replace automatic ones; channels with active strikes cannot use chapters.
7. **Description.** Two or three plain sentences on what the viewer will learn, drawn from the transcript; then chapters; then credits (including any Creative Commons credit required); then links the client provides. No claims beyond what was said.
8. **Check.** Every cue timecode is inside the cut; every track has a status; chapters meet the rules.

## Checks

- Every cue uses footage the client has, or is marked "[needs: ...]".
- Every on-screen number and claim was said in the video.
- Every track is cleared for this platform and use, or marked "replace".
- Chapters start at 00:00, number at least three, each at least 10 seconds.
- Description credits any Creative Commons music.

## Output format

1. **Cue sheet:** time in cut, type (B-roll, pacing, graphic, music), what, length, source, status.
2. **Music clearance table:** track, where used, source, licence, allows, status.
3. **Chapters** ready to paste.
4. **Description** ready to paste.
5. **Needs list** and questions.
