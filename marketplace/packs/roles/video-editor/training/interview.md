# Interview: video editor

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you show me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the editor's work (for example, multicam questions for someone who only cuts screen recordings).

## How you approach a cut

1. **Walk me through your last edit from receiving the files to the video going live.**
   Why it matters: shows the real method, step by step, not the textbook one.
2. **What do you ask for before you start, and what makes you say "I can't plan this yet"?**
   Why it matters: an expert's intake list prevents most re-edits.
3. **How do you decide what to cut from a long recording? Show me an edit list you made.**
   Why it matters: the reasons behind cuts are the core skill to capture.
4. **How do you work from a transcript, and when do you have to look at the picture?**
   Why it matters: sets the honest limits of transcript-led planning.
5. **How do you keep a cut from changing what someone meant? Tell me about a time it nearly happened.**
   Why it matters: fairness to speakers is the editor's first duty and plain AI often misses it.

## Clips and pacing

6. **How do you find a moment that works as a short clip? What do you look for in the first seconds?**
   Why it matters: clip choice is the most valued and least teachable part of the job.
7. **How do you write hook text without putting words in the speaker's mouth?**
   Why it matters: separates strong hooks from misquotes.
8. **How do you plan vertical crops from a 16:9 recording, and what has gone wrong?**
   Why it matters: reframing failures are common and costly to fix late.
9. **How do you cover jump cuts when you only have one camera?**
   Why it matters: practical cover habits become rules the agent can apply.
10. **How do you decide pacing: where to hold and where to tighten?**
    Why it matters: turns taste into notes another editor can follow.

## Captions, music and delivery

11. **What are your caption rules: line length, reading speed, line breaks, speaker names?**
    Why it matters: grounds the agent's caption checks in real practice.
12. **What goes wrong with captions most often in your work?**
    Why it matters: the common faults become the agent's validation list.
13. **How do you check music is cleared, and what do you do when a claim arrives?**
    Why it matters: claims and blocked Shorts are the most common post-upload problem.
14. **How do you write chapters and descriptions, and who approves them?**
    Why it matters: shows how the editor keeps labels true to the content.
15. **What export settings do you use for each platform, and where do you check them?**
    Why it matters: specs change; the habit of checking matters more than any number.

## Tools and access

16. **Which editing tools do you use, and how do you hand a cut from one tool to another?**
    Why it matters: real handover habits (EDL, XML, manual rebuild) make plans buildable.
17. **What access do you ask for in a client's editing and publishing tools, and what do you never do without asking?**
    Why it matters: grounds the agent's access levels in real practice.
18. **How do you organise files, names and versions so nothing gets lost?**
    Why it matters: version control prevents posting the wrong cut.

## Judgement and limits

19. **When has a client asked for an edit you would not make? What happened?**
    Why it matters: captures the editor's red lines on meaning, rights and voices.
20. **How do you take feedback from a client who wants everything kept?**
    Why it matters: shows how an expert defends a cut kindly.
21. **Tell me about the hardest edit you have done.**
    Why it matters: a full story shows judgement under pressure better than any rule.
