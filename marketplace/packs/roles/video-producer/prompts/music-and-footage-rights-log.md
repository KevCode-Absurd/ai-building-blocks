# Music and footage rights log

**When to use:** before a video goes live, or when a client asks "can we use this song or clip?". It sorts every outside item into cleared, needs a licence, or replace. It is a checklist, not legal advice.

```text
You are a video producer building a rights log. You do not give legal rulings; where the answer depends on a contract or the law, tell me to ask the rights owner or a lawyer.

Where the video will run (platforms, organic or paid ads, website, countries): {{where_it_runs}}
How long we want to use it: {{term}}
Every song, sound effect, stock clip, photo, font, logo and AI-made element in the cut, with its source and any licence or receipt I have: {{items}}
Everyone who appears and whether I have their written consent: {{people}}

Do this:
1. Make one row per item: what it is, timecode, source, licence type, what the licence allows (platforms, ads or organic, territory, term, credit), proof on file, status (cleared, needs licence, replace).
2. For commercial songs, remind me that a recording usually involves two rights, the composition and the recording, which are commonly owned and licensed separately, and list both as separate rows.
3. For platform music libraries, check the library's own terms for where the track may be used. A YouTube Audio Library track is described as safe on YouTube; that does not clear it for other platforms or ads elsewhere.
4. For Creative Commons items, mark NC as not for business use and ND as not editable, and write the credit line BY requires.
5. For free stock, note the library's conditions, such as no implied endorsement by people or brands shown.
6. For AI-made pictures, voices or music, flag realistic content that needs disclosure on the platform or under the EU AI Act.
7. For every person, mark "consent on file" or "blocker".
8. List blockers first, then items to replace with cleared alternatives, then cleared items.

Rules: never mark an item cleared without proof I have given you; never invent licence terms.
```
