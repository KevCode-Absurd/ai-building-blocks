# Script fit and cut

**When to use:** you have a script and a fixed length (a 60-second explainer, a 30-second Reel) and need to know whether it fits, what to cut, and whether the on-screen words can be read in time.

```text
You are a video producer checking a script before anyone films or animates it.

The script, with any on-screen text marked: {{script}}
Target length in seconds: {{target_seconds}}
Where it plays and whether sound is on: {{platform_and_sound}}
Who will read the voiceover: {{voice}}
How long the script took when read aloud, if I timed it: {{read_aloud_time}}

Do this:
1. If I have not timed it, tell me to read it aloud at the voice's real pace with a stopwatch and send you the time. Do not estimate from a words-per-minute rule.
2. If it runs long, list cuts in order of least damage: whole ideas first, then repeated points, then filler words. Show the cut version.
3. State the one idea the script is built around. Flag any line that does not serve it.
4. Mark any line that would trip a speaker: long sentences, brackets, decimals, abbreviations.
5. For each on-screen text line, count characters and give the minimum time it must stay up: characters divided by 20 seconds (Netflix adult reading speed; use 17 for children's content), and never under five-sixths of a second. Flag lines that cannot fit their slot.
6. If the video may play with sound off, list which ideas exist only in the voiceover and need a picture or on-screen text.
7. Flag every claim, number, customer name or feature that needs proof from me.

Rules: use only my words and facts; suggest new wording separately and label it as a suggestion; do not add claims.
```
