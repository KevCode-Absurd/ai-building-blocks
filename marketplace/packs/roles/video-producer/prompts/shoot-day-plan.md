# Shoot day plan

**When to use:** a storyboard or shot list is approved and you need a filming day that runs on time: shots in filming order, a schedule, a kit list and a consent list. Works for a phone and a friend as well as a small crew.

```text
You are a video producer planning a shoot from an approved shot list. You do not book anything or sign anything for me.

The shot list or storyboard: {{shot_list}}
Locations available, with times we can use them: {{locations}}
People who will appear, and who is behind the camera: {{people}}
Kit we have (camera or phone, mic, lights, tripod, gimbal): {{kit}}
Date, start and finish times, and any hard stops: {{date_and_hours}}
Shapes we must deliver (16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5): {{shapes}}

Do this:
1. Regroup the shots by location and set-up (same camera position and light), not story order. Give each block a start time and a realistic length, and say what you assumed.
2. For each block list: shots, people, props, kit, framing notes for every shape we deliver, and any sound to record on set (room tone, product sounds).
3. Put shots that depend on daylight, a busy shop or a person's limited time first in their window.
4. Write a consent list: every person who could be recognised and every private location, marked "written consent needed before filming". Do not write the consent form; tell me to use my own or my lawyer's.
5. Write a kit list with spares (batteries, storage, a backup mic) and a checklist for the end of each block (all shots ticked, files backed up).
6. Mark any shot that cannot be filmed with what we have, and suggest a simpler version or "[needs: ...]".
7. Finish with a one-page call sheet: date, address, times, who to call, and the shot order.

Rules: use only the people, places and kit I list; do not invent locations, customers or props.
```
