# Storyboard and shoot plan

A storyboard is a promise about every second of the video. Written as a shot table, it lets a founder with a phone, a freelance filmer or an animator build the video without guessing. The shoot plan then turns story order into filming order, so the day runs on time.

## When to use

- Live-action videos: product demos, founder videos, customer stories, how-tos.
- Animated explainers: scenes matched line by line to the voiceover.
- Short vertical videos (Reels, Shorts and similar) with a hook in the opening seconds.
- Any job where one shoot must feed several shapes.

Not for: writing the script from nothing (use the two-column script skill first), drawing frames, or editing.

## What you need

- The approved script, transcript or rough idea with its spoken words.
- Target length, shapes (16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:5) and platforms.
- People, places, products and existing footage available. Nothing else may appear.
- Brand rules: colours, fonts, logo, any style frames.
- Shoot constraints: date, budget, crew, kit, location access.
- **Access I have.** Steps 1 to 9 need level 1. Step 10 (adding the shot list to the client's project or review tool as a draft) needs level 2. Booking crew, locations or kit commits the client and needs their fresh yes, even at level 3.

## Steps

1. **Check the fit.** Read the script aloud with a stopwatch and compare with the target length. If it runs long, say what to cut before planning shots.
2. **Break into beats.** Mark the hook, problem, show, proof and call to action (or the beats the script uses). For short vertical video, the first shot must make a viewer stop: movement, a face, or the result first.
3. **Write the shot table.** One row per shot: number, timecode in and out, length, what we see, shot size and angle, camera move or motion, on-screen text (exact, with character count), audio (voiceover line, sound effect, music cue) and the transition to the next shot. Use the shot-size abbreviations defined at the top.
4. **Use only what exists.** People, places, products and footage come from the brief. Anything missing is marked "[needs: ...]" with who could supply it. Do not invent customers, offices or features.
5. **Place text inside safe areas.** For 16:9, keep titles inside EBU R 95's 5% graphics-safe margin. For 9:16, keep text and logos out of the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% at each side, per Meta's Reels ad spec (checked 6 Nov 2026). Check each line against a reading-speed floor of 20 characters per second (Netflix, adult content).
6. **Plan every crop.** If one shoot feeds several shapes, note for each shot where the subject sits so a 9:16 and a 16:9 crop both work; mark shots that need a separate vertical take.
7. **Add up the time.** The shot lengths must total the brief's length; if they do not, cut or trim shots here, not in the edit, and note any agreed tolerance from the client.
8. **Write the style frame note.** Colour, light, type, pace and music feel, in words, from the brand rules. Describe qualities; never ask to copy another brand's video.
9. **Build the shoot plan.** Regroup shots by location and set-up. For each block: time, location, shots, people, props, kit, and lighting notes. Add a consent list (every recognisable person and any private location, with "written consent needed"), a kit list, and an asset list: shots to film, graphics and animation to make, music and sound effects to license, stock clips to buy, each with its source and licence status.
10. **Level 2: draft in the client's tool.** With level 2 access, add the shot list to the client's project or review tool as a draft for the team. Log what you created.

## Checks

- Every voiceover line has at least one shot; every shot has a reason.
- Shot lengths add up to the target.
- Every on-screen line fits the time and sits inside the safe area for each shape.
- Nothing appears that the brief does not have; gaps are marked "[needs: ...]".
- Every recognisable person and private location is on the consent list.
- Every music cue and stock clip has a licence status.
- The shoot plan has no shot that appears in the table but not in the schedule, or the reverse.

## Output format

1. **Header:** title, length, shapes, platforms, frame rate, shot-size key, access I have.
2. **Shot table** (as in step 3), with a total at the bottom.
3. **Crop notes** per shot for each shape.
4. **Style frame note.**
5. **Shoot plan:** schedule by location, people, kit, consent list.
6. **Asset list** with source and licence status.
7. **Open questions** and an action log for any level 2 work.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a bakery with a 20-second Reel for a new sourdough, 9:16, filmed on a phone, 30 fps. Key: WS wide, MS medium, CU close-up, ECU extreme close-up. Access I have: none yet, so level 1. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

| # | Time | Length | We see | Size and move | On-screen text (chars) | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00-0:02 | 2 s | Knife breaks a crackling crust | ECU, static, top-down | none | Crust crackle (recorded on set) |
| 2 | 0:02-0:06 | 4 s | Baker shapes dough at dawn | MS, slow push in | "Shaped at 5 am" (14) | Low music bed [needs: licensed track] |
| 3 | 0:06-0:12 | 6 s | Loaves go into the oven, door closes | CU, handheld | "Fermented 24 hours" (18) | Oven door |
| 4 | 0:12-0:17 | 5 s | Customer takes the bag at the counter | MS, static | "New: Rye sourdough" (18) | Music |
| 5 | 0:17-0:20 | 3 s | Loaf on counter, shop logo | CU, static | "Fridays from 8 am" (17) | Music out |
| | | **20 s** | | | | |

*Text sits between 15% and 60% of frame height, inside the side margins. Shot 4 needs a written consent form from the customer or a staff member standing in. "24 hours" and "5 am" are "[proof needed]" until the baker confirms them.*
