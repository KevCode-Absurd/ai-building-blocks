# Interview: video producer

The training coach asks these questions one or two at a time, follows up on vague answers with "Can you show me a real example?", and writes what it learns into playbook.md, knowledge.md ("My specialty") and soul.md. Skip any question that does not fit the producer's work (for example, shoot questions for someone who only writes motion specs).

## How you approach a job

1. **Walk me through your last video from the first message to the file going live.**
   Why it matters: shows the real method, step by step, not the textbook one.
2. **How do you find the one idea a video is about when the client gives you five?**
   Why it matters: focus is the hardest call in short video, and how an expert makes it is reusable.
3. **How do you time a script? Do you read it aloud, use a rate, or something else, and how often are you wrong?**
   Why it matters: timing errors cause most re-edits, and real habits beat rules of thumb.
4. **Show me a script where the picture column adds something the words do not say. How did you decide what to show?**
   Why it matters: captures the core skill of writing for picture.
5. **What does your storyboard or shot table look like, and what columns do filmers or animators ask you to add?**
   Why it matters: the deliverable format is what makes a spec buildable.

## Shoots, motion and edit

6. **How do you plan a shoot day so it finishes on time? What usually runs late?**
   Why it matters: real schedules teach the agent what to allow for.
7. **How do you plan one shoot for several shapes (16:9, 9:16, square)? What has gone wrong with crops?**
   Why it matters: multi-shape delivery is now normal, and crop failures are costly.
8. **How do you spec motion for an animator: keyframes, references, or something else? What gets lost in handoff?**
   Why it matters: shows how to make motion specs exact enough to build.
9. **How long do you hold on-screen text, and how do you check it?**
   Why it matters: unreadable text is a common failure the agent must catch.
10. **What do you check in a cut before the client sees it?**
   Why it matters: becomes the agent's internal review list.

## Platforms, captions and sound

11. **Which platforms do you deliver to most, and which specs or safe zones have caught you out recently?**
   Why it matters: fills the delivery checklist with what really happens, and dates it.
12. **How do you make captions, and how do you check they match the final audio?**
   Why it matters: caption quality is an accessibility duty and a frequent complaint.
13. **How do you handle loudness and sound for videos watched with the sound off?**
   Why it matters: shows how the expert designs for real viewing conditions.

## Rights, people and AI

14. **How do you clear music? Tell me about a claim or takedown you had to fix.**
   Why it matters: music is the most common rights failure in business video.
15. **What is in your rights log, and when do you start it?**
   Why it matters: an early rights log prevents late replacements.
16. **How do you get consent from people on camera, including staff and customers?**
   Why it matters: consent habits protect the client and must be built into plans.
17. **Where do you use AI tools in production, and where do you refuse to? How do you handle disclosure?**
   Why it matters: sets the agent's boundaries for AI-made pictures, voices and music.

## Clients and judgement

18. **Tell me about a client who wanted something that would not work, such as a famous song or ten messages in 30 seconds. What did you do?**
   Why it matters: teaches how to say no while keeping the client.
19. **How do you know a video worked? Which numbers do you look at, and when?**
   Why it matters: links the craft to results the client cares about.
20. **What is the hardest video job you have done, and what would you do differently?**
   Why it matters: a real story carries judgement no checklist holds.
