# Brief to delivered video

**Trigger:** a client needs a video made (an explainer, a demo, a product video or a short series) and someone (a filmer, editor, animator, AI video tool or the client) will build it.

**Time:** usually one to four weeks, most of it waiting for approvals, footage, voiceover and licences.

## Steps

1. **Intake and access.** Ask the access.md questions: where it plays, who watches, sound on or off, length, shapes, the one idea, the one action, who builds it, the deadline. Ask which access level the client grants in each tool (editor, review tool, YouTube, Meta, LinkedIn) and record it as "Access I have". Until they answer, work at level 1.
2. **Script (skill: two-column-explainer-script; prompt: script-fit-and-cut).** Write the two-column script, time it by reading aloud, check on-screen text against the reading-speed floor, and list claims that need proof. Level 1, or level 2 to draft it in the client's tool.
   **Approval point 1:** the client approves the script, the claims and the length. Nothing is filmed or animated before this.
3. **Storyboard (skill: storyboard-and-shoot-plan).** Shot table with timecodes, safe areas and crop notes for every shape, a style frame note and an asset list. Level 1.
4. **Rights plan (prompt: music-and-footage-rights-log).** Start the rights log now: music, stock, fonts, logos, people. Flag anything that needs a licence or consent, and replace what cannot be cleared.
   **Approval point 2:** the client approves the storyboard, the style and the rights plan, including any licence to buy. Buying or accepting a licence needs the client's yes each time; the client pays.
5. **Shoot or build.**
   - Live action: shoot plan and call sheet (prompt: shoot-day-plan); consent forms signed before filming.
   - Animation and titles: motion spec (skill: motion-and-titles-spec; prompt: lower-thirds-and-titles-pack).
   - Level 2: build drafts in a connected tool (for example a Canva design or a Descript project) named "DRAFT"; do not share or export; log what was made.
6. **Review the cut.** Check the cut against the script and shot table: length, every line, every claim, text on screen long enough, nothing flashing more than three times a second. Collect the client's notes in one round where possible.
   **Approval point 3:** the client approves the final cut.
7. **Deliver (skill: delivery-and-rights-check; prompt: vertical-cutdown-plan for extra shapes).** Dated delivery spec per platform, captions from the final audio, rights log complete, AI disclosure planned.
   - Level 1: hand over files, captions and upload notes.
   - Level 2: upload as private, unlisted or draft with captions, titles and credits.
   - Level 3: show the action list and limits (accounts, files, dates, placements) and get a yes, then publish or schedule within them. Ask again before anything outside them. Log each action and result.
8. **Hand over and measure.** Deliver the script, storyboard, specs, rights log, caption files, final files (or their location) and the action log, plus which numbers to watch (audience retention, average view duration, clicks) and when to review them.

## Outputs

- Approved two-column script and timing note.
- Storyboard as a shot table, with crop notes and asset list.
- Shoot plan and consent list, or motion spec.
- Rights log with proof for every item.
- Delivery spec and caption files per platform.
- Final files, drafts uploaded (level 2) or published videos within the client's limits (level 3).
- Action log of every change made in the client's tools.

## Needs the client's approval

- The access level for each tool, and the limits for level 3.
- The script, claims and length (point 1).
- The storyboard, style and rights plan (point 2).
- The final cut (point 3).
- Every licence bought or accepted, and every payment, each time.
- Written consent from everyone recognisable on camera.
- Anything outside the level 3 limits, asked again each time.
