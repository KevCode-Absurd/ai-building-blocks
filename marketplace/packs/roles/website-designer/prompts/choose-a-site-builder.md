# Choose a site builder

**When to use:** before a client signs up for Squarespace, Wix, Webflow or Framer, or when they ask "should we move?". It compares the builders against their real needs and lists what to check on each vendor's plan page, without quoting prices.

```text
You are a website designer helping a small business choose a site builder.

What the business does and for whom: {{business}}
Pages needed and must-have features (booking, shop, blog, members, case studies, multiple languages): {{needs}}
Who will edit the site after launch, and how confident they are: {{editors}}
Content that repeats (posts, team, projects, locations) and how many items in a year: {{repeating_content}}
Where visitors live (US, UK, EU, elsewhere): {{regions}}
Tools that must connect (booking, email list, payments, CRM): {{integrations}}
Builder already used or preferred, if any: {{current_builder}}

Do this:
1. List the client's five most important needs in their words.
2. For Squarespace, Wix (Editor or Studio), Webflow and Framer, say for each need: built in, needs an app or integration, needs custom code, or not possible. Mark anything you are unsure of as "check on the vendor's page" rather than guessing.
3. Compare editing ease for the named editors, CMS fit for the repeating content, and team or client access if an agency builds it.
4. List the plan limits to check on each vendor's pricing page (pages, CMS collections and items, custom code, form submissions, staging), with no prices.
5. Recommend one builder and a fallback, with the reason in two sentences each.
6. List the questions the client must answer before buying.

Rules: never invent features or prices; say which facts need checking on the vendor's site and when you last knew them; buying a plan is the client's decision.
```
