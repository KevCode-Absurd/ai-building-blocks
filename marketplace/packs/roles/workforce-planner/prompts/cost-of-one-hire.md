# The full cost of one hire

**When to use:** You are thinking about one new role and want to know what it really costs per month and in the first year, not just the salary, and how it compares with part-time, contractor or agency options.

Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified employment adviser or accountant. Tax, pension and benefit rates must come from your payroll provider or accountant.

```text
You are a headcount planning analyst costing one role. Use only the rates I give and label every one.

Role, hours and location (country, state or province): {{role_and_location}}
Pay (salary or hourly rate and expected hours, any overtime): {{pay}}
Employer tax rates confirmed by payroll or our accountant: {{employer_tax_rates}}
Pension, benefits and insurance costs: {{benefits}}
Equipment, software seats and space: {{equipment}}
Recruiting cost and training time: {{recruiting_and_training}}
Weeks until the person works at full output: {{ramp_up}}
Other options to compare (part-time, contractor, agency, tool) with their prices: {{options}}

Do this:
1. A monthly cost sheet: pay, employer taxes, pension and benefits, equipment and software, one-off costs spread over the start months. Show each sum.
2. First-month, steady-month and first-year totals.
3. The cost of the ramp-up: months paid before full output.
4. A 12-month cost for each other option, with notes on control, availability, knowledge kept and risk.
5. If any contractor option would work like an employee (fixed hours, our tools, no end date), flag worker status for an adviser instead of treating it as the cheaper choice.
6. Any rate I did not give, shown as a labelled placeholder to confirm.
Output: cost sheet, totals, options table, placeholders list.
End with: "Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified employment adviser or accountant."
Do not recommend a specific person or pay level for a named person.
```
