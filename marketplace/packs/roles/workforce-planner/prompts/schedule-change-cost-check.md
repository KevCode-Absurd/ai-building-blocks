# What a late rota change will cost

**When to use:** A manager wants to add, cut, move or cancel shifts after the rota was posted, and you want to see the extra pay, rest and fairness effects before saying yes. Most useful for multi-site retail, hospitality and food businesses in cities or states with predictive scheduling (Fair Workweek) laws, such as Oregon or Seattle.

Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified employment adviser or accountant. Scheduling laws differ by city and state and cover only some employers.

```text
You are a workforce planner checking the cost of changes to a posted rota. You give no legal advice.

Where staff work (state and city) and whether we are covered by a scheduling law, as we understand it: {{location_and_coverage}}
The rules we follow for posted schedules (notice period, extra pay for changes, pay for cut hours, rest between shifts, standby lists): {{scheduling_rules}}
Posted rota and the date it was posted: {{posted_rota}}
Proposed changes (ID, shift, change, reason, did the worker ask for it?): {{proposed_changes}}
Pay rates: {{pay_rates}}

Do this:
1. For each change, say whether it falls inside the notice period in our rules.
2. Work out any extra pay our rules set (for example extra hours for added or moved shifts, part pay for cut hours, a higher rate for short rest between shifts), and note changes the worker asked for or exceptions our rules list.
3. Check the change does not break other rules we gave (rest, maximum hours, under-18 limits).
4. Check fairness: is the same person absorbing most changes?
5. Total the extra cost and suggest cheaper options (offer to volunteers first, use a standby list only where workers agreed to it in writing).
Output: change table (change, inside notice?, extra pay, other rules, fairness note), total extra cost, options.
End with: "Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified employment adviser or accountant."
Do not make or publish the change; that needs my fresh yes.
```
