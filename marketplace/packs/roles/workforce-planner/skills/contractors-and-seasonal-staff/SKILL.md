# Contractors and seasonal staff

Flexible staff make peaks affordable, but they bring their own risks: thin records, access left on, status questions, and hiring that starts too late. This skill keeps the records straight and plans the season backwards from the first busy week.

Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified employment adviser or accountant. Worker status and contract terms are decided by the client with an employment lawyer or tax adviser; this skill lists the facts and never decides them.

## When to use

- The business uses several freelancers or contractors and has no single list.
- An owner cannot say what each contractor costs, what they signed or what access they hold.
- A contractor relationship has started to look like a job.
- A peak season (retail, tourism, events, harvest, warehouse) is coming and there is still time to advertise, hire, train and roster before the first busy week.

## What you need

**Contractors:**
1. The list of contractors with what they do, how they are paid and through what (sole trader, own company, agency).
2. Contracts, statements of work, invoices for the last 12 months, and the tools and systems each can access.
3. The client's approval rules for engaging and paying contractors.

**Seasonal staff:**
1. Last year's weekly sales or bookings, staff hours, hires, start dates, drop-outs and rehires.
2. Staffing rules (from the `staffing-levels-from-demand` skill) and the dates of this year's peak.
3. Pay rates and contract terms the client has set (any gap is a [placeholder]).
4. Whether any seasonal staff will be under 18.

**Access:** level 1 to read the HR system, accounting and shared drives; level 2 to draft trackers, briefs and job posts; level 3 to update the roster and remove access on the dates the client sets.

## Steps: contractors

### 1. Build the roster
One row per contractor: role, how engaged, rate and basis, contract start and end, notice, documents held (contract, statement of work, insurance, confidentiality, data terms), access given, spend to date, owner in the business, review date. Fill in what the files show and leave blanks visible.

### 2. Rank the gaps
Missing contracts, expired end dates, shared passwords, access to systems not needed, spend above what was agreed. Order by risk and money.

### 3. Flag status facts
For each contractor, note the facts that point towards employment or self-employment: who controls how and when the work is done, whose tools, fixed hours, length and end date, working alongside staff on core work, freedom to work for others, who bears financial risk. In the UK, status for employment rights can differ from tax status, and under the off-payroll rules the client usually decides and gives a status determination statement for contractors working through their own company (small private-sector clients excepted); HMRC's CEST tool can help. In the US, the IRS looks at behavioural control, financial control and the type of relationship, and the Department of Labor sets out its own test. List the facts for the adviser; do not decide.

### 4. Write the brief template
From the work bought most often: goal, scope, deliverables, deadline, budget, files, contacts, how work will be checked, who owns the result. Fill one in as an example.

### 5. Onboarding and offboarding checklists
Day one: documents to collect, access to grant (named accounts, least access), contacts, house rules. Last day: access to remove, files and equipment returned, final invoice checked, feedback. At level 3, remove access on the date the client set and log it. Ending an engagement early needs a fresh yes.

### 6. Spend review
Spend by contractor, project and month against budget; rates for similar work compared; keep, renegotiate or replace noted for the owner.

## Steps: seasonal staff

### 7. Size the season
Staff needed by week and role from last year's weekly numbers and the staffing rules, adjusted for this year's calendar and growth. Add a drop-out allowance from last year's actual drop-outs, or a labelled placeholder.

### 8. Work the timeline backwards
From the first busy week: rota published (with the notice the rules need), training done, contracts signed, offers made, hiring day, adverts out. Give each step a date and an owner. Hiring kits, fair questions and scorecards come from the `hr-specialist` kit's `structured-hiring-kit` skill.

### 9. Check youth and holiday rules
If staff under 18 are hired, apply the limits for their location before any rota is built (in the US, federal limits for 14- and 15-year-olds, and state rules; in the UK, the under-18 hours, breaks and night rules). Part-year workers build up holiday in the UK; plan for it.

### 10. Rehire list and review
After the season: a rehire list with fair, job-related criteria, a short survey, and the numbers to keep for next year (hires, start dates, drop-outs, hours, cost).

## Checks

- Every contractor row shows blanks as blanks; nothing is invented.
- Status facts are listed for an adviser with no ruling given.
- Access removal has a date and an owner for every leaver.
- Seasonal numbers use last year's data and the client's rules, with sums shown.
- Youth limits were applied before rostering.
- Offers, contracts and ending engagements were left for the client's fresh yes.
- The output carries: *Check this with local rules, or ask a qualified employment adviser or accountant.*

## Output format

1. Contractor roster (spreadsheet) and ranked gap list.
2. Status facts table per contractor, for the adviser.
3. Brief template with one filled example.
4. Onboarding and offboarding checklists.
5. Spend review.
6. Seasonal plan: staff by week and role, timeline with owners, youth and holiday notes.
7. Rehire list template and end-of-season survey.
