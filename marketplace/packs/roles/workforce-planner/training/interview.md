# Training interview: Workforce planner

The coach asks these one at a time, follows up on short answers, and asks for a real example each time. Answers fill in the expert's knowledge, playbook and specialty. Remind the expert never to share names, pay, health details or dates of birth of real staff or contractors; anonymise every story.

1. **What kinds of businesses, sites and countries have you planned staff for?**
   Why it matters: sector and location decide which rules and methods the agent points to.

2. **Which demand measure do you use to set staffing, and how did you choose it?**
   Why it matters: the driver decides whether the staffing numbers mean anything.

3. **Walk me through how you turn last month's data into next week's staffing by hour.**
   Why it matters: captures the real sequence an expert follows, step by step.

4. **How do you handle days that were short-staffed or sold out when you forecast?**
   Why it matters: stops the agent reading capped sales as low demand.

5. **What service rules or ratios do you use, and how do you agree them with managers?**
   Why it matters: the agent needs the client's rule, not a borrowed one, and a way to get it.

6. **How do you build a rota so it feels fair to the team?**
   Why it matters: fairness is the expert's judgement the agent must copy, not guess.

7. **Which rule breaks do you see most often on draft rotas, and how do you spot them fast?**
   Why it matters: turns experience into the agent's first-pass rule check.

8. **How far ahead do you post rotas, and what happens when you have to change one?**
   Why it matters: late changes cost money and trust, and some laws put a price on them.

9. **How do you plan for breaks, holiday and sickness in the hours?**
   Why it matters: the allowance must come from the client's history, and the expert knows how to measure it.

10. **Tell me about a rota that went badly wrong. What would have caught it?**
    Why it matters: real failures show which checks matter most.

11. **How do you work out the full cost of a new hire for an owner?**
    Why it matters: captures the cost lines owners forget and how the expert explains them.

12. **How do you decide whether a need is best met by a hire, a part-timer, a contractor or an agency?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent a sound options method instead of defaulting to the cheapest.

13. **What signs make you think a contractor relationship has become a job, and what do you do then?**
    Why it matters: sets where the agent stops and hands over to an adviser.

14. **How do you plan a seasonal peak, and when do you start?**
    Why it matters: most seasonal problems come from starting too late.

15. **How do you set a drop-out allowance for seasonal hiring?**
    Why it matters: a judgement call the agent must base on data, not a rule of thumb.

16. **What changes when some staff are under 18?**
    Why it matters: youth rules come first, and the agent must not miss them.

17. **Where do you draw the line between checking a rota against rules and giving employment-law advice?**
    Why it matters: sets the agent's safety boundary in the expert's own words.

18. **Which rota, HR and planning tools do you use, and what access would you give an assistant in each?**
    Why it matters: grounds the agent's access levels in real practice.

19. **How do you show an owner that a plan is not affordable without losing their trust?**
    Why it matters: gives the agent tone and structure for hard news.

20. **Tell me about the hardest staffing job you have done. What made it hard and what did you decide?**
    Why it matters: a full story shows trade-offs the checklists miss.
