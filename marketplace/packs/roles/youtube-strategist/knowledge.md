# YouTube channel strategist: what the role knows

Checked 2026-11-06. Sources are numbered [n] and listed in sources.md with the date each was checked. YouTube's help pages carry no dates of their own, so each platform fact here is "as of 6 Nov 2026". YouTube is rolling out an updated YouTube Studio from July 2026 [4], so menu names may differ on your screen. This kit is general practice, not legal advice.

This kit covers the channel: strategy, search, titles and thumbnails, scripts that hold viewers, analytics and YouTube's rules. Upload encoding, captions, music licensing and AI copyright are in the video producer kit; cross-platform short video is in the social media manager kit; finding and paying sponsors and creators is in the affiliate and influencer marketing manager kit. Read those instead of repeating them here.

## 1. What this role is for

A business hires a YouTube strategist when it wants the channel to bring in customers, students, leads or sponsors, not just views. Typical clients: software and service firms with things to explain; experts, coaches and teachers; shops with products that need showing; channels stuck at a few thousand subscribers; brands starting from zero.

The role holds three jobs that often sit in one person:

- **Channel strategy:** what the channel promises, which two or three series it runs, which searches it should own, how playlists and end screens move viewers on, and the mix of Shorts and long videos.
- **Scripts:** openings that pay off the title in the first seconds, sections with a payoff each, and rewrites at the points where viewers leave.
- **Titles and thumbnails:** the promise of each video, shown as a picture and a few words, tested in YouTube's own tool.

**What good work looks like.**

- A one-line channel promise: who it is for and what they get from every video.
- Series, not one-offs. A viewer who likes one episode knows what the next one gives them.
- Topics chosen from real searches and real buyer questions, not guesses.
- Each title and thumbnail pair makes one promise that the video keeps early.
- A plan the client can keep up with the time and camera confidence they have.
- Reports read by series and traffic source, with a short list of changes.
- No fake engagement, no misleading thumbnails, and every sponsorship and realistic AI use disclosed.

**How it is judged.** By growth in the numbers that match the goal: returning viewers, search traffic, watch time on the series that sell, clicks to the site, enquiries and sales. By whether the client keeps publishing. By a clean channel: no strikes, no lost monetisation. A strategist never promises views; YouTube decides who sees what.

**What an AI strategist delivers.** Plans, topic maps, scripts, title and thumbnail concepts and analytics reviews from exports or screenshots. With level 2 or 3 access to YouTube Studio or a connected tool it can also draft uploads and, within the client's limits, update titles, descriptions, chapters, playlists and tests.

## 2. The canon

**YouTube's own explanations come first.** YouTube publishes how recommendations and search work [1][2], what its analytics mean [4]-[8], and its policies [15]-[21]. They are the most reliable guide to how the system behaves, and third-party "algorithm secrets" are mostly guesses. Read them first-hand.

**Books practitioners return to.** Credited as a reading list; this kit makes no specific claim from them.

- Derral Eves, *The YouTube Formula* (Wiley, 2021). Read it for a whole-channel view: audience, analytics and repeatable formats.
- Chip Heath and Dan Heath, *Made to Stick* (Random House, 2007). Read it for why simple, concrete and unexpected ideas are remembered; useful for titles and openings.
- Jonah Berger, *Contagious* (Simon & Schuster, 2013). Read it for why people share; useful for deciding which videos get sent to a friend.

**Search-led channels.** Ahrefs, a search software firm, published how it grew its channel by making one video a week on topics people search for, and found that a product-focused series not built around searched topics drew little search traffic [30]. The method (find the search, make the best answer, measure search traffic) is the backbone of a business channel.

**Videos in Google Search.** Google's video guidance explains watch pages, structured data and key moments; YouTube chapters feed Google's key moments [23]. A business that embeds its videos on its own site should follow it.

**Disclosure as the honesty canon.** The FTC's Endorsement Guides FAQ [24], the ASA's advice on recognising ads [25] and the European Commission's Influencer Legal Hub [26] set the standard for sponsored videos.

## 3. How the work is done now

### How YouTube picks videos (as of 6 Nov 2026)

- **Recommendations** (Home and Up Next) draw on each viewer's viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback from satisfaction surveys, plus the reputation and quality of the channel; YouTube also uses outside evaluators to judge how an average viewer may see content [1]. Viewers can pause or delete their watch and search history [1].
- **Search** ranks on three things: relevance (how well the title, tags, description and the video itself match the query, and watch time for that query), engagement (such as how long people watch for that query) and quality (signals of expertise, authority and trust on the topic). Results are personalised, and YouTube says it takes no payment for better organic placement [2].
- **Tags play a minimal role** except for commonly misspelled subjects; the title, thumbnail and description matter more. Stuffing tags into a description breaks the spam policy [3].

The practical reading: make the video people meant to find, make the title and thumbnail promise exactly that, and keep the viewer satisfied after the click.

### Channel strategy

1. **Goal and time.** Leads, sales, sponsors or reach; hours a week; who appears on camera.
2. **Channel promise.** One line: who it is for and what every video gives them.
3. **Two or three series.** Each with a format the team can repeat (a tutorial series, a myth fix, a customer question, a teardown). Series build a viewing habit; one-off videos do not.
4. **Topic map.** Ideas grouped by series and buyer stage, each with a working title and a reason it can rank or be recommended.
5. **Paths between videos.** Playlists in viewing order; end screens and links that send viewers to the next video they should watch.
6. **Shorts and long videos.** Shorts reach new viewers; long videos explain, rank in search and sell. Decide the job of each before planning volume.
7. **Rhythm.** A schedule the client can keep. Ahrefs published one video a week [30]; no source sets an ideal frequency, so set it by capacity.

### Finding topics

- **YouTube's Trends tab** in Analytics shows top searches from your audience and your saved searches over the last 28 days, breakout videos from similar creators, and content gaps where viewers cannot find good results; some insights are limited by country, language and device [5].
- **Buyer questions.** Sales calls, support tickets, comments and reviews. A question a customer asks in their own words is a search someone else is typing.
- **YouTube search suggestions and competitor channels.** Which titles already rank, how old they are, and what they miss.
- **Google results.** Queries where Google shows videos are worth a watch page on the client's site [23].

### Titles and thumbnails

The title and thumbnail are one promise in two parts. The thumbnail shows the result, the problem, a before and after, or a face reacting; its words add to the title rather than repeat it.

- **Specs (as of 6 Nov 2026):** recommended 3840 x 2160 at 16:9, at least 640 pixels wide; Shorts thumbnails 2160 x 3840 at 9:16; JPG or PNG; up to 2 MB from mobile or 50 MB from a computer; custom thumbnails need a verified account [9]. A vertical video with a 16:9 thumbnail gets an automatic 4:5 thumbnail on some surfaces [9].
- **Test and compare** in YouTube Studio tests up to three titles, thumbnails or both on a computer. It picks the winner by watch time share, not click-through rate, shows a small control group the original, and usually finishes within two weeks. Results read Winner, Performed Same or Inconclusive. It is not available for Shorts, made-for-kids, mature-audience or private videos, and thumbnails below 1280 x 720 cause every test thumbnail to be downscaled [10]. Change one thing per variant; near-identical variants rarely produce a winner [10].
- **Honesty rules.** Titles, thumbnails or descriptions that lure viewers to a video that does not deliver are "malicious clickbait" under the spam policy [17]. Thumbnails that mislead about the content, or show sexual, violent or gory imagery, can be removed and earn a strike [11].
- **Build notes.** Check every concept at the smallest size it appears on a phone, keep the bottom-right corner free where the video length shows, and use no other creator's face or brand.

### Scripts that hold viewers

- **The audience retention report** shows where viewers keep watching. A gradual decline is normal; spikes mean rewatching or sharing (or confusion); dips mean skipping or leaving. "Typical retention" compares the video with your 10 latest videos of similar length [7].
- **Key moments** include the intro, the share of viewers still watching after the first 30 seconds. YouTube's advice is to make the title and thumbnail reflect the content and to experiment with the first 30 seconds. Key moments appear only on videos of 60 seconds or more with at least 100 views [7].
- **Method.** Write the title and thumbnail first. Open with the answer or the stake, not a channel plug. Give each section its own payoff. Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea per line. Mark B-roll and on-screen text. Use only facts the client supplies, and mark claims to check.
- **Fixing a published video.** Map each dip to the script line at that time, rewrite the line for next time, and move strong late moments earlier in future videos [7].

### Chapters, end screens and playlists

- **Chapters** go in the description: the first timestamp is 00:00, at least three timestamps in order, each chapter at least 10 seconds; manual chapters override automatic ones [12]. Google gives priority to key moments set by YouTube chapters [23].
- **End screens** need a video of at least 25 seconds and sit in the last 5 to 20 seconds, with up to four elements on a 16:9 video; the external link element needs YouTube Partner Program membership; made-for-kids videos cannot show them [13].
- **Playlists** group a series in viewing order and show up as their own traffic source [8].

### Shorts

Since 15 October 2024, square or vertical videos up to three minutes count as Shorts; use 16:9 to keep a video out of Shorts. A Short over one minute with an active copyright claim is blocked worldwide and cannot be recommended or earn money; most Shorts Audio Library songs can be used for up to 90 seconds [14]. Plan Shorts as their own format, or as cut-downs of long videos that point to the full version. Specs and music clearance are in the video producer kit.

### Reading analytics

- **Where:** YouTube Studio's Analytics tabs are Overview, Content, Reach, Engagement, Audience, Revenue (Partner Program only) and Trends. Reach shows impressions, impressions click-through rate, views and unique viewers; Engagement shows watch time, average view duration and "Engaged views"; Audience shows new, casual and regular viewers [4].
- **Impressions click-through rate** counts how often viewers watched after a registered impression. Videos shown widely on Home tend to have a lower rate; impressions on external sites and end screens are not counted. YouTube warns that high CTR with low average view duration and falling impressions can signal clickbait it is less likely to recommend [6].
- **Traffic sources** include Browse features, YouTube search, Suggested videos, Shorts, Playlists, Channel pages, End screens, Notifications, External and Direct or unknown [8]. Read each series by source: search growth means the topic map works; suggested and browse growth means viewers are satisfied enough to be shown more.
- **The 90-day review** reads the channel by series, traffic source and audience: what brings new viewers, what brings them back, what stalls, and three to five changes.

### Monetisation and policy

- **YouTube Partner Program** (as of 6 Nov 2026): 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months (Shorts feed views do not count) or 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days; 2-Step Verification, a linked AdSense for YouTube account and no active Community Guidelines strikes are also required. Updated YPP terms take effect on 1 February 2027 and must be accepted by 31 January 2027 [15].
- **Inauthentic and reused content.** On 15 July 2025 YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content": mass-produced, templated or barely varied videos, including AI-made ones from generic templates, cannot earn. Reused content (clips, compilations, reading others' text, reactions without commentary) cannot earn even with permission [16].
- **Spam.** Sub-for-sub schemes, bots or rewards to inflate views, likes or subscribers, low-effort repeated polls, and copy-paste comment promotion are banned [17].
- **Strikes.** A first violation usually brings a warning; policy training lets it expire after 90 days. A first strike blocks uploads and other actions for one week, a second within 90 days for two weeks, and a third within 90 days may end the channel. Deleting the video does not remove the strike [18].
- **Made for kids.** Every creator must say whether a video is made for kids, under an agreement with the FTC to help comply with COPPA; mixed-audience content aimed partly at children counts. Creators are legally responsible for the setting, and YouTube cannot give legal advice [19].

### Sponsorships and disclosure

- **YouTube's setting:** in YouTube Studio, under Paid Promotion, choose "Yes, my video includes branded content"; a label appears at the start of the video. Creators and partners must follow local law, and YouTube's page names the FTC (US) and ASA (UK) among the authorities [20].
- **US:** a disclosure only in the description is not enough. Put it in the video itself, at the start or right before the endorsement, both spoken and on screen, and disclose affiliate links both in the video and next to the links [24].
- **UK:** the ASA advises an identifier such as "Ad" in the title and/or thumbnail of a sponsored YouTube video, visible before the viewer clicks; the brand and the creator are both responsible [25].
- **EU:** consumer law requires brand partnerships and affiliate marketing to be disclosed; the Commission's Influencer Legal Hub explains how [26].

Deal terms, creator briefs and affiliate programmes belong to the affiliate and influencer marketing manager kit.

### AI-made or AI-changed content

YouTube requires disclosure of realistic content made or altered with AI: a real person seeming to say or do something they did not, altered footage of real events or places, realistic scenes that never happened, and AI music that is the main focus. No disclosure is needed for clearly unrealistic content, beauty or lighting filters, AI help with scripts, titles, thumbnails or captions, upscaling, cloning your own voice for voiceovers, or gameplay. The setting is "AI use" in the Attributes section of the upload. Disclosing does not limit reach or earnings; consistently failing to disclose can lead to labels being added, removal or suspension from the Partner Program [21]. In the EU, the AI Act's transparency rules, including clear labels on deepfakes, apply from August 2026 [27].

### Access and connected tools (as of 6 Nov 2026)

YouTube Studio has channel permission roles: Owner, Manager, Editor, Editor (Limited), Subtitle Editor, Viewer and Viewer (Limited). Viewer (Limited) and Editor (Limited) cannot see revenue; Editors can upload and publish but delete only drafts; invitations expire after 30 days [22]. Access is granted per tool in three levels: read (level 1), read and draft (level 2), and read, write and publish within the client's limits (level 3). Until the client says otherwise, I work at level 1. access.md lists what each level allows and how to connect.

### Real businesses, real lessons

- **Ahrefs (search-led growth).** Sam Oh reports that after the channel switched to topics with search demand, at one video a week, it passed 3 million minutes of watch time in about seven months; one video gained 28,700 views and over 1,400 subscribers in two weeks [30]. *Caveat:* the company's own account of 2019 results.
- **CSGO Lotto (undisclosed ownership and payments, 2017).** Two YouTube creators promoted a gambling site in their videos without saying they owned it, and the company paid other creators $2,500 to $55,000 to promote it. The FTC settlement bars them from presenting endorsers as independent users and requires clear disclosure of material connections [28].
- **Google and YouTube (children's privacy, 2019).** Google and YouTube agreed to pay $170 million over alleged COPPA violations on child-directed channels; the settlement required a system for channel owners to mark child-directed content and notices telling them that such content may be subject to COPPA [29]. This is why the made-for-kids setting exists.

## 4. Benchmarks and numbers

Each figure has a source, a date and a caveat. Platform figures were checked on 6 Nov 2026.

- **Impressions click-through rate:** half of all channels and videos range between 2% and 10%; new channels and videos with under 100 views can show a wider range [6]. *Caveat:* a range, not a target; CTR falls as a video is shown more widely.
- **Intro retention:** the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds, shown only for videos of 60 seconds or more with 100 or more views [7]. *Caveat:* YouTube publishes no "good" figure; compare with your own typical retention.
- **Partner Program thresholds:** 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days [15].
- **Shorts:** up to 3 minutes; a claimed Short over 1 minute is blocked [14].
- **Test and compare:** up to 3 variants, usually finished within two weeks, decided by watch time share [10].
- **End screens:** last 5 to 20 seconds, up to 4 elements, video at least 25 seconds [13]. **Chapters:** at least 3, each at least 10 seconds [12].
- **Strikes:** last 90 days; three in 90 days may end the channel [18].
- **Disclosure gap:** a 2018 Princeton study, cited by the European Commission, found only 10% of affiliate marketing on YouTube and Pinterest was disclosed [26]. *Caveat:* old data, cited secondhand.

No source I checked gives a trustworthy ideal upload frequency, video length or retention rate. Use the client's last 90 days as the baseline.

## 5. Decision rules

- If the client cannot say who the channel is for in one line, write the channel promise before any topic list, because every title, series and thumbnail tests against it.
- If a video idea has no search demand and no clear reason to be shared or recommended, drop it or turn it into a section of a better video [2][30].
- If a topic is searched, write the title in the words the viewer would type, because relevance starts with the title and description [2].
- If impressions click-through rate is high but average view duration is low, fix the promise or the opening, not the thumbnail, because YouTube reads that pattern as clickbait [6].
- If the intro figure is weak, rewrite the first 30 seconds to deliver what the title promised [7].
- If a dip repeats at the same kind of moment (sponsor read, long intro, recap), move or cut that kind of moment in the next script [7].
- If two thumbnails or titles are in doubt, run Test and compare on a video with enough impressions, change one thing per variant, and read watch time share rather than CTR [10].
- If a video is a Short over one minute, use only music you are sure is cleared, because a claim blocks it everywhere [14].
- If a channel mass-produces similar videos from a template, add real variation or expect to lose monetisation [16].
- If a video is paid for, gifted or carries affiliate links, tick YouTube's paid promotion setting and disclose in the video itself; in the UK add "Ad" to the title or thumbnail [20][24][25].
- If any realistic person, place, event or main music track was made or changed with AI, set "AI use" to Yes [21].
- If content could appeal to children, decide the made-for-kids setting with the client and their adviser, because creators carry the legal responsibility [19].
- If a step would publish, change a live video or touch settings, check the access level first: draft at level 2, act live only with level 3 and within the client's limits.

## 6. Common mistakes, and how to spot them

- **No promise.** Spot it: the channel page could belong to any firm in the sector; the last ten titles cover ten unrelated topics.
- **Vanity topics.** Spot it: videos about company news nobody searches for, with almost no search traffic [8][30].
- **Title and thumbnail that oversell.** Spot it: high CTR, early drop-off, falling impressions [6][17].
- **Long intros.** Spot it: the first 30 seconds hold a logo, a greeting and a "before we start"; intro retention lags the channel's typical figure [7].
- **Thumbnail text repeating the title.** Spot it: the same four words in both places.
- **Testing too many things at once.** Spot it: variants differ in face, colour, words and layout, so the result teaches nothing [10].
- **Tag stuffing.** Spot it: a description ending in a block of keywords [3].
- **Ignoring Shorts music rules.** Spot it: a 90-second Short with a chart song and a blocked status [14].
- **Undisclosed sponsorship.** Spot it: a sponsor read with no on-screen or spoken disclosure and the paid promotion box unticked [20][24].
- **Fake growth.** Spot it: subscriber swaps, bought views or giveaways for subscribing [17].
- **Made-for-kids by habit.** Spot it: cartoon-style videos marked "no" without thought, or adult content marked "yes" [19].
- **Reporting views only.** Spot it: a monthly report with views and subscribers but no traffic sources, returning viewers or business actions.

## 7. Quality bar: what an expert checks before shipping

1. Does the plan state the channel promise, the series and the goal each serves?
2. Is every topic tied to evidence: a search, a buyer question or a past result?
3. Does each title and thumbnail make one promise the video keeps in its opening?
4. Do thumbnails meet the spec and work at phone size, with no misleading imagery [9][11]?
5. Does the script deliver the promise early, give each section a payoff, and use only the client's facts?
6. Are chapters, end screens and playlists set so each video leads somewhere [12][13]?
7. Are sponsorships, affiliate links and realistic AI use disclosed in YouTube's settings and in the video [20][21][24][25]?
8. Is the made-for-kids setting a deliberate decision [19]?
9. Is anything at risk under the inauthentic content, reused content or spam policies [16][17]?
10. Does the report read results by series and traffic source and end with a short list of changes?

## 8. How this role writes

**Deliverables.**

- *Channel plan:* promise, audience, goal, series (format, length, rhythm), the job of Shorts and long videos, playlists and the three numbers to watch each month.
- *Topic map:* a table of working title, series, buyer stage, the search or question behind it, and why it can rank or be recommended.
- *Title and thumbnail sheet:* per video, the one-line promise, 5 to 10 titles, and three thumbnail concepts (focal point, face and expression, up to four words of text, colours with hex codes, layout) with a test note.
- *Script:* timed sections with the spoken words on one side and on-screen text, B-roll and graphics on the other; the opening written out in full; claims to check marked.
- *Analytics review:* by series and traffic source, with what to do more of, less of, and next.
- *Back-catalogue refresh:* per video, the old and new title, description opening, chapters and pinned comment, and the reason for the change.
- *Action log:* what changed, where, when and the result.

**Conventions.** Plain words and short sentences. Timestamps as MM:SS. Every platform rule carries its source and date ("YouTube Help, checked 6 Nov 2026"). Facts the client must confirm are marked "[check: ...]". Never promise views or subscribers.

## 9. Glossary

- **Channel promise:** one line saying who the channel is for and what every video gives them.
- **Series:** a repeatable format with its own playlist.
- **Impression:** a registered view of a video's thumbnail on YouTube [6].
- **Impressions click-through rate (CTR):** how often viewers watched after an impression [6].
- **Average view duration:** average time watched per view [4].
- **Audience retention:** the share of viewers still watching at each moment of a video [7].
- **Intro (key moment):** the share of viewers still watching after 30 seconds [7].
- **Typical retention:** your 10 latest videos of similar length, used as a comparison [7].
- **Traffic source:** where a view came from, such as YouTube search or Suggested videos [8].
- **Browse features:** Home, Subscriptions, Watch Later and similar surfaces [8].
- **Suggested videos:** recommendations beside or after other videos [8].
- **Test and compare:** YouTube Studio's test of up to three titles or thumbnails [10].
- **Watch time share:** the measure Test and compare uses to pick a winner [10].
- **Chapters:** timestamped sections listed in the description [12].
- **End screen:** clickable elements in a video's last 5 to 20 seconds [13].
- **Shorts:** square or vertical videos up to three minutes [14].
- **YouTube Partner Program (YPP):** the programme that lets channels earn from ads and other features [15].
- **Inauthentic content:** mass-produced or barely varied videos that cannot earn [16].
- **Reused content:** others' material without real commentary or changes [16].
- **Malicious clickbait:** titles, thumbnails or descriptions that promise what the video does not deliver [17].
- **Community Guidelines strike:** a penalty that limits a channel for a set time [18].
- **Made for kids:** the setting for videos directed at children, tied to COPPA in the US [19].
- **Paid promotion label:** YouTube's on-video label for branded content [20].
- **Material connection:** any payment, free product, job or family tie that must be disclosed [24].
- **Altered or synthetic content:** realistic media made or changed with AI that YouTube requires creators to disclose [21].

## 12. Defaults to avoid

An AI model with no context falls back on these choices, so they make work look like everyone else's. Avoid each one unless the client's captured brand or the agreed plan chooses it on purpose, and then say why. The full shared list is in tools/ai-default-patterns.md.

- **Every thumbnail in one look.** All thumbnails with the same face, arrow and colour layout. Keep a recognisable series look, but make the three concepts per video really different (section 8).
- **One title formula reused.** Every title built on one pattern, such as "I tried X for 30 days". When the topic is searched, use the words the viewer would type (section 5).
- **Headline formula clichés.** "You won't believe..." or "Everything you need to know about...". Make one promise the video keeps in its opening (section 7).
- **Product on a gradient.** Thumbnails with the product cut out on a gradient. Show the product in use, or the result.
- **Stock hype words.** Titles, descriptions and scripts with "Unlock", "Elevate", "Game-changer" or "In today's fast-paced world". Use the viewer's words.
- **Fake urgency.** "Watch before it's deleted" style lines. They break the title's promise; state what the video really delivers.
- **Generic stock-style images.** Stock imagery in thumbnails and B-roll. Use the creator's own footage and faces.
