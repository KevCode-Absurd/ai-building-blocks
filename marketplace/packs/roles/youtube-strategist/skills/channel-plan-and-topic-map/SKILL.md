# Channel plan and topic map

A business channel grows when viewers know what they get from every video and can find the next one. This skill turns a goal and a few hours a week into a promise, a small set of series, a topic map tied to real demand, and the paths between videos. It works from what the client shares; it cannot see live YouTube search volumes unless the client shares Trends tab screenshots or a research tool's export.

## When to use

- A new channel, or a channel restarting after months of silence.
- A channel with many one-off videos and no series.
- A channel that gets views but no leads, sales or returning viewers.
- Before a quarter's content calendar is set.

## What you need

- **Goal:** leads, sales, sponsors or reach, and the action a viewer should take.
- **Audience:** who buys, in their own words; the questions they ask in sales calls, support, comments and reviews.
- **Capacity:** hours a week, who appears on camera, what can be filmed.
- **Evidence:** a 90-day YouTube Studio export or screenshots (traffic sources, top videos, returning viewers) if the channel exists; Trends tab top searches and content gaps if available.
- **Competitors:** three channels the audience already watches.

**Access:** level 1 to read Studio. The plan needs no other access. Building playlists or editing end screens needs level 2 (private drafts) or level 3 (live, within the client's limits).

## Steps

1. **Write the channel promise.** One line: who it is for and what every video gives them. Test it against the last ten videos; if most do not fit, say so.
2. **Pick two or three series.** Each has a repeatable format, a rough length, and a reason the audience would watch the next episode. Good business formats: a tutorial series on the tasks buyers do, a customer-question series, a myth fix, a teardown or review, a build or process series. Name the series and give each its own playlist.
3. **Set the job of Shorts and long videos.** Shorts reach new viewers and can be cut from long videos; long videos explain, rank in search and sell. Shorts are square or vertical and up to three minutes; a claimed Short over one minute is blocked, so music must be cleared (YouTube Help, checked 6 Nov 2026).
4. **Gather demand.** Pull buyer questions verbatim. Add Trends tab top searches and content gaps if shared. Check YouTube search suggestions and the top results for each question: what ranks, how old it is, what it misses.
5. **Build the topic map.** Thirty ideas, each with: working title in the words a viewer would type, series, buyer stage (problem, options, choosing, using), the search or question behind it, and why it can rank or be recommended (gap, fresher answer, better demo, the client's unique proof). Drop ideas with no search and no reason to be shared.
6. **Order the first ten.** Lead with ideas that have clear demand and that the client can make well now. Put at least one in each series.
7. **Design the paths.** Playlists in viewing order for each series. For each video, the next video an end screen should point to (end screens sit in the last 5 to 20 seconds of videos of 25 seconds or more, up to four elements; not on made-for-kids videos). Channel page sections and a trailer outline for new visitors.
8. **Set the rhythm.** A schedule the client can keep. Say plainly that no official source sets an ideal frequency.
9. **Set the measures.** Three numbers per month tied to the goal, for example search traffic to the tutorial series, returning viewers, and clicks or enquiries from video links. Read them by series and traffic source.
10. **List the policy checks.** Sponsorships (paid promotion setting and in-video disclosure), realistic AI use (AI use setting), made-for-kids decisions, and the inauthentic content rule for templated videos.

## Checks

- The promise is one line and specific enough to exclude topics.
- Every topic traces to a buyer question, a search or a past result.
- Every series has a playlist and a reason to watch the next episode.
- The plan fits the hours the client stated.
- No promise of views, subscribers or monetisation.
- Platform rules carry a source and date.

## Output format

1. **Channel promise** and audience.
2. **Series table:** name, format, length, rhythm, job (search, recommend, sell), playlist name.
3. **Shorts and long-video roles.**
4. **Topic map:** 30 rows (working title, series, stage, demand evidence, why it can win), first ten marked.
5. **Paths:** playlists in order, end-screen targets, channel page sections, trailer outline.
6. **Measures:** three monthly numbers and how to read them.
7. **Policy checks** and facts to confirm.

## Example of good output (short)

*Illustrative structure, not a real business: a garden-centre shop with an online store, one staff member happy on camera, four hours a week. Copy the shape, never the content: every fact comes from the client's own context.*

**Promise:** "Small-garden fixes that work in a weekend, from people who sell the tools."

| Series | Format | Length | Job | Playlist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekend Fix | One problem, one fix, filmed in the yard | 8 to 12 min | Search | Weekend Fixes |
| Ask the Counter | One customer question answered at the till | Shorts | Reach new viewers | Ask the Counter |
| Tool Truth | Two tools tested side by side | 10 min | Help buyers choose | Tool Tests |

| Working title | Series | Stage | Demand evidence | Why it can win |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| How to fix a lawn full of moss | Weekend Fix | Problem | Asked at the counter weekly [check: count]; top results are older | Real before and after from the shop's own lawn [check: permission] |
| Hand pruner vs bypass loppers for small trees | Tool Truth | Choosing | Search suggestion; customer emails | Side-by-side cut test on the same branch |

**Paths:** each Weekend Fix ends on the matching Tool Truth video; each Short links its related long video.

**Measures:** search traffic to Weekend Fix; returning viewers; clicks to the shop from descriptions.

*Why this works:* one promise sorts every idea, each series has a job, and every topic shows the evidence behind it instead of a guessed search volume.
