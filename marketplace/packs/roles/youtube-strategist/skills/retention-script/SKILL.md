# Retention script

Viewers decide in the opening whether the video is what the title promised. YouTube's audience retention report shows where they stay, skip and leave, and its "intro" figure is the share still watching after 30 seconds. This skill writes scripts that deliver early and keep paying off, and it rewrites the lines where real viewers left. It uses only facts the client gives and marks claims to check. It cannot watch video; it works from scripts, transcripts and retention screenshots.

## When to use

- A new long video: tutorial, explainer, review, comparison or founder story.
- A video whose intro figure or average view duration lags the channel's typical retention.
- Turning notes or a webinar transcript into a tighter script.

## What you need

- **Promise:** the chosen title and thumbnail, or the one-line promise.
- **Viewer and outcome:** who watches and what they can do after.
- **Facts:** product notes, steps, numbers and proof the client can back.
- **Length:** a target, set by the content, not by a rule.
- **For a fix:** the transcript with timestamps and screenshots of the retention graph and key moments (intro, top moments, spikes, dips). Key moments appear only on videos of 60 seconds or more with at least 100 views.

**Access:** level 1 to read analytics. Scripts need no other access. Adding chapters or end screens to a live video needs level 3 within the client's limits; level 2 prepares them for a private upload.

## Steps

### Writing a new script

1. **Restate the promise** in one line and the outcome in one line.
2. **Write the opening in full.** Deliver the answer, show the result, or state the stake in the first lines, and show on screen what the thumbnail showed. No logo, greeting or "before we start". A short reason to stay to the end is fine if it is true.
3. **Plan sections, each with a payoff.** A step done, a mistake shown, a comparison settled. Order by what the viewer needs first. Put the strongest material early; viewers thin out over any video.
4. **Write for the ear.** Short sentences, contractions, one idea per line. Numbers said simply. Read it aloud; cut what trips the tongue.
5. **Add the picture.** For each section: B-roll, screen recording, on-screen text and graphics. Show what the words do not say.
6. **Place any sponsor or affiliate mention.** In the US, disclose in the video at the start or just before the endorsement, spoken and shown; in the UK, also add an identifier such as "Ad" to the title or thumbnail. Tick YouTube's paid promotion setting.
7. **Close with one next step.** Name the next video in the series and set the end screen to it (the last 5 to 20 seconds).
8. **Write chapters.** 00:00 first, at least three, each at least 10 seconds, titled in the viewer's words.
9. **Mark facts to check** as "[check: ...]" and AI use (realistic AI people, places or music must be disclosed in the AI use setting).

### Fixing a published video

1. **Read the intro figure** against the channel's typical retention. If weak, compare the first 30 seconds of the transcript with the title and thumbnail: does it deliver the promise?
2. **List the dips.** For each, the timestamp, what was said or shown, and the likely cause: a long setup, a repeated point, a sponsor read, a slow screen recording, a topic switch.
3. **List the spikes.** Rewatching can mean strong interest or confusion; read the line to tell which.
4. **Rewrite each dip line** for the next video in the series, and note structural fixes (move a top moment earlier, cut a recap).
5. **Fix what can be fixed now:** chapters that let viewers jump to what they came for, a clearer description opening, a pinned comment with the answer's key step.

## Checks

- The promise is delivered in the opening, in words and on screen.
- Every section has a payoff; no section repeats an earlier one.
- Read-aloud time fits the target.
- Every claim traces to the client's facts or is marked "[check]".
- Sponsor, affiliate and AI disclosures are planned where needed.
- Chapters follow YouTube's format.

## Output format

1. **Promise and outcome.**
2. **Script table:** time, section, spoken words, on screen (B-roll, text, graphics).
3. **Chapters** and **end-screen target**.
4. **Disclosures** and **facts to check**.
5. For a fix: **dip and spike table** (time, what happens, likely cause, rewrite) and **fixes to apply now**.

## Example of good output (short)

*Harbour Lane Accounting is a made-up business used only to show the format: a two-person bookkeeping firm with a channel for sole traders. All details are invented.*

**Promise:** "Set up a simple expenses sheet in 10 minutes, so tax time is not a scramble."

| Time | Section | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00:00 | Opening | "This is the sheet. Five columns. By the end, you'll have your own." | Finished sheet, full screen; cursor highlights the five columns |
| 00:20 | Why five columns | "Date, who you paid, what for, amount, receipt link. That's it." | Each column header appears as it is named |
| 01:10 | Build it | "Open a blank sheet. Type these headers." | Screen recording, zoomed in |

**Fix note (from a retention screenshot):** dip at 02:40 where the old script explained bookkeeping history. Rewrite: cut it and move the receipt-photo tip (a spike at 05:10) to 02:40.

**Chapters:** 00:00 The finished sheet / 00:20 The five columns / 01:10 Build it / 04:30 Add receipts.

*Why this works:* the opening shows the result the title promised, every section pays off, and the fix ties each change to a moment in the real retention graph.
