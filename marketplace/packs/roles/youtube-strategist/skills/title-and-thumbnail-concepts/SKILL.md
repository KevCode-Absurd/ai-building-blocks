# Title and thumbnail concepts

The title and thumbnail are one promise in two parts. The thumbnail shows the picture of the result or the problem; the title says it in the words a viewer would use. The video must keep the promise in its opening, or viewers leave and YouTube shows it less. This skill writes concepts and test plans; a designer, Canva or the client builds the files. It cannot see how a thumbnail looks unless the client shares an image.

## When to use

- Before filming, so the script can pay off the promise.
- Before upload, to pick the pair to publish and the pairs to test.
- When impressions click-through rate is low for a video's traffic sources.
- When a channel's thumbnails all look different and need a style system.

## What you need

- **The video:** topic, audience, what the viewer gets, and the opening 30 seconds or script if written.
- **The channel:** the channel promise, brand colours as hex codes, fonts, and five recent thumbnails.
- **Faces:** whether a person appears, and any face shots already taken.
- **Evidence:** impressions, click-through rate, average view duration and traffic sources for recent videos, if the channel exists.

**Access:** level 1 to read analytics. Level 2 to build designs in a copy marked "DRAFT" or upload a private video with its thumbnail. Level 3 to set the live thumbnail or start Test and compare on the listed videos, within the client's limits.

## Steps

1. **Write the promise in one line.** What does the viewer get, and why now? Everything below tests against it.
2. **Find the one picture.** Choose the image that shows the promise fastest: the result, the problem, a before and after, a comparison, or a face reacting to the result. One focal point.
3. **Write five to ten titles.** In the viewer's own words. For search-led videos, put the searched phrase early. Mix angles: the outcome, the mistake, the comparison, the question. No claim the video does not prove, and no numbers the client cannot back.
4. **Write three distinct thumbnail concepts.** For each: focal point; face and expression if used; text of four words or fewer that adds to the title rather than repeating it; the object or scene; background; colours as hex codes; layout (left, centre, right). Concepts should differ in idea, not just colour.
5. **Check the spec.** 16:9, recommended 3840 x 2160 and at least 640 pixels wide; JPG or PNG; up to 2 MB from mobile or 50 MB from a computer; a verified account for custom thumbnails. Shorts thumbnails are 9:16. If the video will be tested, keep thumbnails at 1280 x 720 or larger, or every test thumbnail is downscaled (YouTube Help, checked 6 Nov 2026).
6. **Check at phone size.** Can someone tell what it is at the smallest size it shows? Text readable, subject clear, bottom-right corner free where the video length sits.
7. **Check honesty.** Titles and thumbnails that promise what the video does not deliver are "malicious clickbait" under YouTube's spam policy, and misleading thumbnails can be removed with a strike. Use no other creator's face or brand. If the video is sponsored and the audience is in the UK, add an identifier such as "Ad" to the title or thumbnail, as the ASA advises.
8. **Pair them.** Name the best pair to publish and two alternatives.
9. **Write the test note.** Test and compare runs up to three variants on a computer, picks the winner by watch time share, and usually finishes within two weeks; it is not available for Shorts, made-for-kids, mature or private videos. Change one thing per variant. Older videos with steady impressions are safer to test first. Do not change the title or thumbnail during a test, or it stops.
10. **For a style system:** fixed rules for font, text size, colour pairs, face placement and layouts, with two or three templates, so every thumbnail looks like the channel.

## Checks

- Each thumbnail's text adds to its title, not repeats it.
- One focal point per concept; readable at phone size.
- The opening of the video keeps the promise.
- Spec and test limits met, with the date checked.
- No misleading imagery, other creators' faces or invented claims.
- Disclosure planned for sponsored videos.

## Output format

1. **Promise** (one line).
2. **Titles:** five to ten, best marked.
3. **Thumbnail concepts:** three, each with focal point, face, text, scene, colours (hex), layout, and a build note for Canva or a designer.
4. **Best pair and alternatives.**
5. **Test note:** which variants, what changes, what result to read.
6. **Checks passed** and facts to confirm.

## Example of good output (short)

*Lumen Bike Lights is a made-up business used only to show the format: a small maker of bicycle lights with a channel on riding safely at night. All details are invented.*

**Promise:** "See which light setup drivers actually notice at night."

**Titles:** "Which bike lights do drivers actually see? (Night test)" (best); "We tested 4 bike light setups from a driver's seat"; "Your bike light is bright. Is it visible?"

| Concept | Focal point | Text | Colours | Layout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Driver's-eye view of a dark road with one bright rider and one faint rider | "SEEN?" | Night blue #0B1D3A, light yellow #FFE45C | Road centre, riders left and right |
| B | Rider's face lit from the front, squinting at headlights | "4 SETUPS" | Black #111111, white #FFFFFF | Face left, light right |
| C | Split frame: same rider, two setups | "BEFORE / AFTER" | Grey #2E2E2E, yellow #FFE45C | Left/right split |

**Test note:** test A against C (both show the comparison; C states it). Read watch time share. The video's first 20 seconds show the driver's-eye view, so both keep the promise.

*Why this works:* one promise, three different ideas, short text that adds to the title, and a test that changes one thing.
