# Build for a completed business task

Use when building, changing or evaluating a repeatable agent workflow. For a routine one-off job, apply the relevant checks without adding a new intake or approval ceremony.

1. **Define the useful outcome first.** Record the job, accountable owner, baseline, target population, observation window, acceptance condition and cost/effort budget. Missing baseline means benefit unmeasured. Prioritize the evidenced business bottleneck; volume or automation rate alone is not value. Include review, rework and maintenance in net savings.
2. **Read the business before choosing a method.** Inventory permitted sources, scope, owner, revision/effective date, freshness requirement and access. Preserve policies with their exceptions. Separate operating data, approved brand facts, historical examples and practitioner advice. Unread is not absent. Search across required source classes, inspect the passages and parent qualifications, and retain conflicts. A recent retrieval of an older page is not current evidence.
3. **Give each step a contract.** Define required inputs, scope/account, output, owner, preconditions, validation and failure state. Use deterministic code for arithmetic, joins, schema checks, budgets and duplicate detection. Use model judgment for interpretation and creative choices. A suspected duplicate is flagged for review; it is not silently deleted.
4. **Verify actions in the destination.** Discover actual connector capabilities and current account access; do not infer access from a tool name. After an authorized mutation, read back the record independently and compare requested fields, status and scope. A successful request or generated receipt alone is insufficient. Preserve idempotency keys on uncertain retries; reconcile before repeating non-idempotent work. Bound retries and timeouts, and return a resumable handback with the last verified state.
5. **Maintain scoped memory.** Keep task state separate from durable facts and preferences. Store provenance, effective dates, scope and corrections; refresh or invalidate derived summaries when their source changes. Cache keys must include tenant/access scope, source and policy versions and relevant request parameters. Never reuse a stale price, stock, permission or consent decision merely because its wording matches.
6. **Test three different things.** Functional: correct output, retrieval coverage and appropriate tools, including when not to call them. Operational: timeout, rate limit, partial failure, retry, restart, concurrency and isolation, with observed latency/error denominators. Business: task acceptance, actual resolution, contribution or owner-selected outcome, including review effort and customer friction. One passing layer cannot stand in for the others. Test the authorized environment; local simulation does not certify a live provider.
7. **Expose evidence without exposing people.** Keep versioned, redacted records of source references, tool arguments/results, action status, artifacts and checks. Log observed decisions and concise reasons, not hidden model reasoning, credentials or unnecessary customer data. Retention and access follow the owner's policy. Deployed monitors require an actual owner, thresholds, response and instrumentation; a proposed dashboard is not running monitoring.
8. **Scale after the simple path works.** Start with one accountable agent and deterministic steps. Delegate independent work only when it benefits the task; hand off bounded inputs, authority, deliverables and acceptance tests. Add queues, storage or models for measured requirements, not fashion. Compare models and infrastructure on representative tasks, cost and operational limits; neither a vector database nor multiple agents proves quality.
9. **Release and recover deliberately.** Bind tests to exact prompt/tool/source/package versions, preserve first failures, and verify the resulting environment. State which capabilities are implemented, simulated or unavailable. Keep a rollback and manual recovery route. An unknown condition is not a pass, and a structural checker is not a semantic judge.

For human judgment, root-cause visibility, important relationships and optional learning, apply AUTOMATION-PRINCIPLES.md. Existing valid authorization remains valid within scope; these principles do not grant access or require repeated approval.

For multi-part questions, list the evidence needed for each decision, search missing parts separately and read policy exceptions with their parent section. A top-ranked passage is not complete coverage. Preserve tables, units, negation, source locations and raw text during parsing; normalize a separate search representation. Record unresolved gaps within the context budget. Retrieval similarity is not claim support: check consequential answer claims against the actual passages and current business records.
