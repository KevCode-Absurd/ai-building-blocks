# Voice match (v1)

## Writer
1. Read `context/brand-file/voice.md` first; if missing, use voice notes in the brief or `facts.md`, else write plain and calm.
2. Pick the channel voice that fits the deliverable (site, email, social, ads, docs, support, blog, B2B). Copy its person, formality, sentence length, punctuation and the brand's own terms.
3. Customer-facing copy must sound like the brand wrote it. For internal or analytical work, clarity beats house style: keep only the brand's terms and no-go words.
4. Never put in a phrase from the voice file's "words not seen" or "don't" list.
5. Hooks and headlines come from the persona's own lines when the brief or reviews give them: the words customers use for the problem, the moment or the result. Your own phrasing comes second.
6. Proof appears as a customer's words (a short quote, word for word) or one plain claim. Copy is not a report.
7. Counts stay out of customer copy. "N of our M reviewers say" or "N reviews mention" belongs in the owner's summary, not the ad or page. Use a count only when the count is the claim, such as a rating with its review total.
8. Internal analytics never appear in customer- or candidate-facing text: correlations, conversion or retention rates, cohort tables, test results, margins. Turn the finding into a plain promise or leave it out.

## Checker
1. Quote two lines from the draft. Compare each with the voice file's closest example: could it sit on the brand's own page?
2. Flag:
   - generic AI phrasing the brand never uses (list below)
   - wrong formality (too stiff, too chummy, hype where the brand is calm)
   - wrong person ("we" where the brand says "you", or the reverse)
   - wrong punctuation habits (exclamation marks, emoji, em dashes, title case, full stops on bullets, apostrophe style)
   - wrong sentence length or reading level for the channel
   - review counts, internal figures or analytics in customer or candidate copy (writer rules 7 and 8)
   - a hook in the writer's words when the brief gave the customer's own
3. Score voice under the owner's voice question for customer-facing work. Each flag costs a point; a no-go word costs two. For internal work, flag only lines that hurt clarity.

## Generic AI phrases to strip in any brand voice
- "In today's fast-paced world", "In an ever-evolving landscape"
- "Unlock", "unleash", "elevate", "supercharge", "empower"
- "Seamless", "game-changing", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge"
- "Delve into", "dive deep", "navigate the complexities"
- "It's not just X, it's Y"
- "Whether you're X or Y"
- "Look no further", "Say goodbye to"
- "We're excited to announce", "We're thrilled"
- "At the end of the day", "Here's the thing"
- Stacked triplets of adjectives, rhetorical-question openers, closing "Ready to...?" lines

## Channel-native short copy

Match the channel, not merely the company. Use one or two short public examples from that channel as private references; describe their voice traits instead of recycling campaign lines. A push needs a lock-screen reason to act, a support reply resolves the actual problem, and an internal update starts with the decision. Generic cheerful encouragement is not a substitute for the brand's characteristic language.

The final fresh reader checks the native piece without its setup notes. Keep compliant disclosure where it belongs, but do not turn every variant into the same dense notice. Keep platform-field limits in the brief-derived output contract, verified from the brief or a current primary specification.
