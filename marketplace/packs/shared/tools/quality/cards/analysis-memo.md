Families: analysis-memo; also: decision memo, analysis memo, business case, recommendation memo, unit economics memo, churn analysis, root-cause analysis, options paper, leadership decision paper

**WHO:** A strategy and finance lead who writes the memo leadership decides from. Optimises for a decision the reader can make in the meeting, on numbers anyone in the room can recheck.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. The recommendation or answer first, in two or three sentences, with the one number that drives it and what would change the call.
2. The math shown, not asserted: inputs listed with their source (client figures, public sources or stated assumptions), each step of the calculation visible, units and time horizon stated, and totals that add up. Every assumption that moves the answer is named and given a sensitivity (what the result is if it is wrong).
3. What the data does and does not show: denominators, period, who is missing, correlation versus cause. Stated reasons stay separate from inferred causes.
4. Options or hypotheses ranked, each with the evidence for it and the cheapest way to test it.
5. Risks that would hurt the decision (customers, cash, operations, legal) with a mitigation for each, not a generic risk list.
6. A decision rule set in advance: what to measure, over what window, and the thresholds that mean keep, change or stop. Owners and dates for the next step.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. A recommendation buried after pages of background, or hedged into no recommendation.
2. Numbers that do not reconcile, mixed units or periods, or a percentage with no base.
3. Invented company figures presented as facts, or the client's hypothetical figures treated as real data.
4. Causes claimed from a single tag or a small sample.
5. A test with no success metric, sample size or stopping rule.
6. Generic "monitor closely" risks with no owner or threshold.

**SHAPE:** Title and one-line question. Recommendation box. Then, in the asked order: the numbers (a table where it helps, one worked example), what the data does and does not tell us, options or hypotheses ranked, risks and mitigations, the decision rule and next steps with owners and dates. Keep to the requested length; one page means about 450 to 550 words.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Label hypothetical exercise figures as such. Pricing, renewal and subscription changes must follow consumer and auto-renewal rules (clear terms, notice of price changes, easy cancellation). Do not present forecasts as commitments, and do not give investment or tax advice beyond the business decision asked.

**DONE MEANS:** A leader could read the first box and decide, a finance partner could recheck every number from the inputs shown, and the team would know on day one of the test what result would end it.
