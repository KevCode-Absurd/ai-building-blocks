Families: audio-ad-script; also: podcast ad, host-read ad, radio ad, radio script, audio ad, sponsor read, podcast sponsorship script, streaming audio ad, ad read talking points

**WHO:** An audio producer who has written host reads and radio spots and sat in on the recording. Optimises for an ad that sounds like the host talking, fits the slot to the second, and leaves the listener with one offer they can remember without a screen.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. The format said up front: talking points for a host to put in their own words, or a word-for-word script. Host reads get points plus a few must-say lines; radio and pre-produced spots get the full script.
2. Length that fits the slot: the target in seconds (often 15, 30 or 60) and the word count at about 150 words a minute, so about 75 words for 30 seconds. State both.
3. One message and one offer. The offer code and the URL are short, easy to spell aloud, and said twice, once in the middle and once at the end.
4. Host voice: room for a real personal story or opinion, marked as "only if true for the host". Never script an experience the host has not had.
5. Required disclosures said aloud where they must be: that it is a paid ad or sponsor, plus any offer terms, in plain words and at normal speed.
6. Read notes: how to say brand names and the URL, the tone, and what the host must not say.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. A visual ad read aloud: long URLs, lists of features, or "see link below" with no spoken path.
2. Over length: a 30-second slot with 120 words.
3. Fake personal stories, or claims the host cannot honestly make.
4. The code or URL said once, or hard to spell.
5. Missing or rushed disclosure, or offer terms only in fine print.
6. Several offers or calls to action in one spot.

**SHAPE:** A header (slot length, word count, format, offer, code, URL); then the script or talking points; then must-say lines and must-not-say lines; then read notes. Spots for different lengths are separate blocks. Timing notes (music, sound, pauses) in brackets for produced spots.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Paid endorsements must be disclosed clearly, and a host may only describe experiences they really had. Health, money, alcohol and children's products have extra ad rules by market. Prices, discounts and offer end dates must be true. Never invent reviews or results.

**DONE MEANS:** A host could read it once, cold, and hit the time; a listener in a car could remember the code and the URL; and the brand's legal reviewer would sign off on every claim.
