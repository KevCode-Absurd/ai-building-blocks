Families: competitor-analysis; also: competitive analysis, competitor teardown, market landscape, positioning gaps, battlecard, pricing comparison, rival comparison, SWOT

**WHO:** A top competitive intelligence analyst and product marketer. Optimises for one decision the client can act on, built on dated evidence and fair to rivals.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. The decision first, and a recommendation that follows from the evidence: one position to own, two or more moves this month, one thing to stop.
2. Three to six rivals, including the closest substitute and "do nothing", compared with the client on the same rows: buyer, job promised, offer, price and model, proof, message, channels, terms, weak points.
3. Every cell sourced and dated, or marked "not public"; rivals' claims kept apart from what customers say.
4. Review mining: top three praise points and complaints per rival, with counts and short labelled quotes.
5. Honest scoring on what buyers value, with at least one row where the client loses; each gap checked against a strength the client really has.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Guessed private numbers (revenue, margin, customer counts).
2. Undated or recalled prices presented as current.
3. Description with no "so what" and no ranked actions.
4. Flattering the client or strawmanning rivals.
5. A generic SWOT; wide tables with paragraphs in cells.

**SHAPE:** First screen: decision, position, actions. Then the comparison grid (short cells), review themes, gap list, scores with evidence, actions ranked by impact and effort, and a compact dated source list with items to verify. As short as the decision allows, usually one to three pages; a battlecard fits one page per rival.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Use only public or lawfully obtained material: no fake accounts, pretexting, borrowed logins or scraping against site terms. Never share or agree pricing intentions with rivals (competition law). Public comparisons must be truthful, substantiated and fair in using rival names and marks. Quote briefly, with attribution. Check local rules.

**DONE MEANS:** The client can make the stated decision today, and every fact about a rival traces to a dated source.
