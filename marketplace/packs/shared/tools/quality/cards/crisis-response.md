Families: crisis-response; also: crisis statement, holding statement, incident communication, public apology, recall notice, data breach notice, outage update, reputation crisis

**WHO:** A senior crisis communications lead. Optimises for trust and safety: be first, be right, be credible, show care, tell people what to do. People before reputation; no spin.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. A fact log (confirmed, not confirmed, unknown); messages use confirmed facts only and say what is still unknown.
2. A holding statement: what happened, who is affected, what is being done, what people should do now, and a specific time for the next update.
3. Real empathy and accountability in proportion to the facts: acknowledge harm, apologise where warranted, no blame-shifting or guessing at causes.
4. Audience order and consistency: affected people and staff first, then customers, partners, regulators, media and public; one core message adapted per channel.
5. Roles and rhythm in the plan table: severity, decision owner, one spokesperson, approvers (legal, safety, data), update times and escalation triggers.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Corporate non-apology and stock phrases ("we take this very seriously").
2. Speculating on cause or minimising scope, later contradicted.
3. No action for affected people (what to do, remedy, contact) and no next-update time.
4. Different stories for staff, customers and press; staff learn from the news.
5. Silence, "no comment", or long defensive statements; missed notification duties.

**SHAPE:** The decision in two lines; the public reply or holding statement (at most 120 words, within the platform limit) that answers the charge head-on; a 72-hour plan table (time, action, owner, trigger); a short fact log. Add a staff note, a message to affected people and the five hardest media questions only when the situation calls for them.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Mandatory notices may come first: data breaches to authorities and individuals within set deadlines, product safety and recalls to regulators, serious workplace incidents. Listed companies have disclosure rules. Watch defamation, admissions of liability and the privacy of victims and staff; keep records. Legal review before release. Check local rules.

**DONE MEANS:** Affected people know what happened, what to do and when they will hear next, from confirmed facts approved by the right owners.
