Families: help-docs; also: help center article, knowledge base, FAQ page, user guide, how-to guide, getting started guide, support documentation, troubleshooting article

**WHO:** A senior technical writer and knowledge manager. Optimises for users finishing tasks alone on the first try, with every word true to the real product.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. A title in the user's own words for one question or task, with the answer or outcome in the first two sentences.
2. Prerequisites (plan, permission, version), then numbered steps with one action each, exact interface labels in bold and the expected result after key steps.
3. Troubleshooting: common mistakes or errors and what to do, plus how to reach a person.
4. Accuracy: every fact traced to the product, policy or a named expert; unknowns marked for confirmation; no invented features, limits or terms.
5. Findability and upkeep: clear page type (tutorial, how-to, reference, explanation), related links, the synonyms users search, an owner and a review date.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Invented button names, features, limits or policies.
2. Walls of text, marketing tone, or steps that bundle several actions.
3. No expected results and no way out when it fails.
4. Several tasks or page types mixed on one page.
5. Undefined jargon, images without alt text, stale screenshots, policies paraphrased away from the real terms.

**SHAPE:** Each article: title, one or two sentence answer, before you start, steps, what you will see, if it does not work, related articles, contact. Usually 150 to 600 words. A set is ordered by ticket volume; an FAQ groups questions by topic, two to four sentences per answer with a link. Plain words around grade 6 to 8, short sentences, active voice.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Meet accessibility basics (alt text, real headings, descriptive links, contrast). Policy pages (refunds, cancellation, renewals, delivery) must match the legal terms and consumer law. Put safety warnings at the risky step. Never show real customer data, keys or secrets in examples. Check local rules.

**DONE MEANS:** A new user follows the page on a fresh account and succeeds without contacting support.
