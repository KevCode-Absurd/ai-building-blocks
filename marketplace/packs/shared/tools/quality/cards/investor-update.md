Families: investor-update; also: shareholder update, monthly update, quarterly letter, board update, lender update, founder update, stakeholder report

**WHO:** An investor-relations lead who writes the same-format update every period. Optimises for trust and for an investor who can act in two minutes; bad news early, never hidden.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. A two-to-three-line summary on top: the headline result with a number and the top worry.
2. A fixed key-numbers table: main revenue or traction metric, change vs last period, cash, net burn, runway in months, plus one metric special to the business; same definitions every period, maths recomputed.
3. Wins and misses, one line each with a number; every miss has its cause and fix.
4. Up to three specific, forwardable asks naming who or what is needed and by when (an intro to a type of person, a hire, a decision).
5. Every figure traces to the founder's data; projections labelled and shown beside actuals; anything unverified marked.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Cheerleading tone; good news only; misses buried or left out.
2. Invented or rounded-up numbers; wrong growth, burn or runway maths.
3. Vague asks ("any intros welcome").
4. Essays and feature changelogs instead of a table and short lines.
5. Format or metric definitions change each period, so trends cannot be compared.

**SHAPE**
Email-ready text: subject line with period and headline; summary; metrics table; highlights; lowlights with fixes; top risk and response; asks; team and hiring; next period's focus; thanks. One version. Monthly: 300 to 600 words. Quarterly: up to two pages, adding what changed in the plan.

**RULES AND RISKS**
No promise or hint of returns. Mark it confidential; send only to the approved list. Anything inviting new investment is a regulated securities communication: check local rules with a lawyer first. Misstatements to investors can create liability. Keep customer and staff personal data out. Not investment, tax or legal advice.

**DONE MEANS:** An investor reading for 90 seconds knows the main result, the cash position, the biggest problem and exactly how they can help.
