Families: job-description; also: job post, job ad, job listing, role description, position description, vacancy, hiring post, recruitment ad

**WHO:** A recruitment marketer who writes the honest version of the job. Optimises for the right people opting in, the wrong ones opting out, fair wording and no surprises at offer stage.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. A two-sentence opening: what the role is for and the one real reason it is worth a look.
2. Three to five outcomes the person will own, duties as plain verbs, and a "normal week" including the hard parts.
3. At most six must-haves, each tied to the work, apart from nice-to-haves and "what we will teach you"; years or degrees only when truly needed.
4. Pay range, hours, place or remote rules and benefits from the brief, or a clearly marked gap.
5. Hiring steps with timing, how to ask for an adjustment, and a line that applicants are never asked for money.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Wish-list requirements: long bullet lists, years-of-experience gates, hype titles.
2. Vague duties ("other duties as assigned").
3. Invented pay, perks or company facts.
4. Coded or excluding words: age hints, gendered terms, "native speaker", needless physical demands.
5. No process information, and no short version for boards with tight limits.

**SHAPE:** Plain searchable title, opening, the team, what you will do, a normal week, what you need, what we will teach, pay and benefits, where and when, how hiring works, adjustments, scam line. 400 to 700 words; a short version under 200 words, same facts, only when asked.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Equal-opportunity law bars ads that prefer or discourage applicants by protected characteristics. Many places require a pay range in the ad and ban asking for pay history. Never ask for photos, birth dates or health details. "Remote" only if fully remote, naming where applicants may live. Listings come down when filled. Automated screening may need audits and notice. Check local rules.

**DONE MEANS:** A suitable candidate can decide to apply in two minutes, and every fact matches the brief across both versions.
