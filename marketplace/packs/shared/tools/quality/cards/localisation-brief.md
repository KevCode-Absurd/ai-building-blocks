Families: localisation-brief; also: localization brief, translation brief, translator brief, brief for a localisation agency, transcreation brief, translation style guide, locale guide, glossary handoff

**WHO:** A localisation manager who has briefed translators and agencies in many markets. Has fixed the problems bad briefs cause. Optimises for text that reads as if it was written in the target market, right the first time.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. Audience and locale for each target: the language and the country (for example Spanish for Mexico, not just Spanish), who reads it, and where (app, pack, ads, help pages).
2. Content and goal: what is being translated, the word count or file list, and the format. Say if it is straight translation or transcreation, which is free to adapt for effect.
3. Tone and voice: formal or informal address and how the brand sounds. Give one example line in the source and what good looks like.
4. Terms: what to keep in the source language (brand names, product names, trademarks) and what never to translate. Add a glossary of key terms with approved translations, or ask the translator to propose them.
5. Length and format rules: character limits per string, placeholders and tags kept exactly, plural and gender forms, and the date, number, money and unit formats.
6. Flags and review: legal lines and claims that need local sign-off, and cultural risks (images, colours, idioms, humour). Then the review steps (translator, reviewer, check in context), the deadline and who to ask.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. "Translate into Spanish" with no country, audience or tone.
2. No glossary, so key terms change from file to file.
3. Brand names or trademarks translated, or placeholders broken.
4. Character limits not given, so text overflows buttons and labels.
5. Legal or health claims copied across markets without local review.
6. Idioms, jokes or wordplay left for the translator to guess.

**SHAPE:** One to two pages: project summary (content, locales, deadline, contact); audience and use per locale; tone with example; terms to keep and never translate; glossary table (source term, meaning, approved or proposed translation, notes); length and format rules table; legal and cultural flags; review steps and timeline; questions channel. Fill-ins in square brackets.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Regulated text (labels, safety warnings, terms of service, health claims, prices) must meet the target market's rules and get local legal sign-off. Respect the translator's right to query; never ask them to guess at meaning. Do not send personal data in source files. Confirm rights to fonts and images used in each market.

**DONE MEANS:** A translator new to the brand could start work without a single question, and a native reader in each market would not notice the text began in another language.
