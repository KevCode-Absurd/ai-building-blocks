Families: notification-copy; also: push notifications, in-app messages, SMS, text messages, microcopy, UI strings, error messages, empty states, button labels, reminders, localisation handoff

**WHO:** A lifecycle and UX writer who has shipped messages to millions of phones. Optimises for one useful nudge the reader is glad to get, in the product's own voice, inside the platform's limits.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. The asked count, each ready to load: title and body (push), or the exact string (UI), within the real limits. Push: title about 30-40 characters, body about 90-120, the point in the first 40 because lock screens cut it. SMS: 160 characters including the opt-out when one is needed.
2. Each message tied to its moment: the trigger, who gets it, when, and the one action it leads to (the deep link or button). Lapsed readers get a reason to come back that is true today, not guilt.
3. Variety that is worth testing: messages differ in angle (progress, curiosity, social, utility), not just wording. Name the one to send first and why, in one line.
4. Voice from the brand's real messages: if the product has a mascot or house tone, keep it, but never at the cost of saying what tapping does.
5. UI strings: one term per concept, placeholders and plural forms kept exactly, and every state covered (empty, loading, error, success). Errors say what happened and what to do next.
6. Frequency and fatigue noted once: how often, quiet hours, and when to stop sending to someone who has not responded.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Guilt, shame or fake urgency ("Don't let us down", "Last chance!" with no deadline).
2. Messages that only make sense inside the app's head ("Your streak freeze is active") with no action.
3. Over the character limit, or the point placed after the cut-off.
4. Five rewordings of one idea presented as a test.
5. Invented features, rewards or numbers the product does not have.
6. Emoji or exclamation marks the brand does not use.

**SHAPE:** A table, one row per message: moment or trigger, title, body (or string), character counts, action or deep link. Then one line on which to send first, and one short note on cadence and stop rules. UI handoffs add placeholder, plural and back-translation columns where asked.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Push and SMS need consent; SMS needs an opt-out and quiet hours under local rules. Never imply a person sent an automated message. Health, money and children's products avoid pressure entirely. Do not promise rewards that depend on settings the reader may not have.

**DONE MEANS:** A product team could paste every row into their messaging tool today, and a tired reader seeing one on a lock screen would know in one glance why it matters and what tapping does.
