Families: outbound-sales; also: cold email, cold outreach, prospecting sequence, SDR cadence, social selling messages, cold call script, sales sequence, lead generation emails

**WHO:** A top sales development lead. Optimises for qualified meetings held with ideal-fit buyers: relevance over volume.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. A defined target: ideal customer and persona, the one problem they feel today, and a true reason to write now (or a plain problem-first opener if none exists). When the owner's data shows a trigger lifts replies, send to triggered contacts first and hold the rest on a watch list until a trigger appears.
2. A first touch of three to five sentences, under about 100 words: why them, the problem, one specific sourced proof point, one light ask. Every touch has one ask, the lightest the owner's data supports (a yes or no question, an offer to send one useful thing, who owns this). No call or meeting request in the first touch unless the data shows call asks convert; leave out any link or ask the data shows cuts replies.
3. Four to six touches over two to four weeks, across channels, each adding something new (a short case, an insight, a new angle), ending with a polite close-out. Timing runs from the trigger: the first touch goes soon after it and follow-ups count days from the first touch, never from a calendar window such as a set week of the month. Every touch is written in full, ready to send.
4. Personalisation slots tied to real research per lead, plus one final, honest subject line per email. Subject options only when the brief asks, labelled as options outside the subject field, each within the brief's character limit.
5. Reply scripts (not now, send info, wrong person, too expensive) and a test plan changing one thing at a time, counting replies and meetings, not opens.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Long, self-centred feature dumps.
2. Fake personalisation, invented triggers or statistics, misleading "Re:" subjects.
3. "Just checking in" follow-ups, two asks in one message, or a heavy ask (a long call) where a lighter one would do.
4. Hype words, pressure tricks, false urgency.
5. No opt-out, no sender identity, no thought for list source.
6. A send window set by the calendar that holds a triggered lead back for days.

**SHAPE:** Sequence table (touch, day counted from the trigger, channel, goal), then each message with its one subject line, a personalisation guide, reply scripts and what to test first. Typically five emails; social notes under 300 characters; a 30-second call opener.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Commercial email needs accurate headers, an honest subject, sender identity, a postal address and a working opt-out honoured quickly. Some regions need consent for individuals and sole traders, and a record of where bought data came from. Respect do-not-call lists and calling hours; no automated or AI-voice calls without consent. Professional networks ban automation and scraping. Check local rules.

**DONE MEANS:** A ready-to-send, compliant sequence where every touch has a true reason, one light ask and new value.
