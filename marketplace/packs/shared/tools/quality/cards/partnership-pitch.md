Families: partnership-pitch; also: partner proposal, partnership proposal, business development pitch, co-marketing pitch, reseller or referral pitch, alliance proposal, integration partnership, channel partner outreach

**WHO:** A business development lead who has signed and ended many deals. Optimises for a deal that pays both sides and survives its first dispute.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. A partner-specific opening: why this partner, why now, grounded in something true about their customers (a shared buyer, not a competitor).
2. Mutual value in numbers: what the partner earns or gains, with the working, and what we gain after their share.
3. A concrete deal: what each side gives, who pays whom, when, for how long, and what counts as a referral.
4. A small first step: pilot scope, owners, timeline, success measures, decision date.
5. Plain-word terms including the exit: exclusivity, customers both sides claim, refunds after commission, data sharing, notice to end; marked for lawyer review.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. All about us; no partner benefit.
2. Vague "synergies" with no numbers or owners.
3. Commission or discount set without margin maths.
4. Outreach that could go to any partner.
5. Only how the deal starts: no pilot, measures or way out.

**SHAPE**
Two parts. An outreach email under 150 words (hook, why them, one proof, one ask) plus two follow-ups under 75 words. Then a one-page proposal or 6 to 10 slides: summary, rationale, side-by-side value, deal model, who does what to reach customers, pilot and measures, contacts, terms summary, next steps. One version per partner type.

**RULES AND RISKS**
Competition law: no price fixing, no pressure on resale prices, no swapping competitively sensitive information. Affiliates disclose paid links. No rewards for recruiting partners rather than selling. Sharing customer data needs a lawful basis and an agreement. NDA before confidential details. No improper gifts. Marketing email needs consent. Check local rules; a lawyer drafts the agreement.

**DONE MEANS:** The partner sees in one minute what they get, what it costs them, how a 90-day pilot would run and how either side can walk away.
