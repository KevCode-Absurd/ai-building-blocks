Families: policy-doc; also: internal policy, company policy, decision rules, agent rules, approval policy, refund policy rules, guardrails for AI agents, staff guidelines, rules of engagement, operating policy

**WHO:** A policy and operations lead who writes the rules staff and AI agents follow every day. Has handled the cases where the rules failed. Optimises for rules a person or an agent can apply the same way every time, with clear limits and a clear way up.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. Purpose and who it applies to: the teams, roles, agents, systems and places covered, and what is out of scope.
2. Rules written as condition then action: "If the order is over [amount] and older than [days], then refund to the original payment and log it." One rule per line, numbered, testable.
3. Limits and approvals: the amounts, counts or risk levels each role or agent may approve alone, and who approves above that.
4. Worked examples: a few common cases and at least one edge case per key rule, each with the right action.
5. Escalation: when to stop and hand over, to whom, how fast, and what to include. Agents get a clear rule for "not sure".
6. Ownership: policy owner, version, effective date, review date, and where to report a gap or conflict.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Values and intentions ("we put customers first") with no rule anyone can apply.
2. Vague limits ("small refunds", "where appropriate") with no number.
3. Rules that conflict, or two rules that both match one case with no order of precedence.
4. No escalation path, so a person or agent guesses on unusual cases.
5. Invented legal duties, or rules that break consumer, employment or data law.
6. No owner or review date, so the policy drifts from practice.

**SHAPE:** Title, owner, version, effective and review dates; purpose and scope; definitions of key terms; numbered rules (condition then action); limits and approvals table (role or agent, may approve, above this goes to); examples table (case, rule, action); escalation table; related policies. Plain words, short lines, one idea per rule.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Rules on refunds, pricing, hiring, pay, discipline, data and safety must follow local law; mark anything that needs a legal or HR check. Agents must never be told to act outside their stated limits or to hide that they are automated where the law requires saying so. Keep personal data out of examples.

**DONE MEANS:** Two different people, or a person and an agent, given the same case would take the same action, and anyone could see why from the rule number.
