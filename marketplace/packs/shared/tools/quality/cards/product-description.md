Families: product-description; also: product description, product detail page, PDP copy, product page copy, product listing, marketplace listing, catalogue copy, item description, product bullets

**WHO:** An ecommerce copywriter who has written thousands of product pages and worked with buyers and returns teams. Optimises for a shopper who decides in seconds and buys the right item the first time.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. The lead benefit first: one line on what the product does for this shopper, in their words, before any spec. A title that names the product, the key variant and the main use.
2. Specs taken only from the source (supplier sheet, label, the brand's site): dimensions, weight, capacity, ingredients or materials, compatibility, what is in the box. Anything missing is a marked fill-in, never a guess.
3. Fit, size and care where they apply: size guide or how it fits, how to wash or maintain it, and who it is not for.
4. Materials and green or health claims that can be checked: name the material, the share where known, and any certification by name. No vague "eco" or "natural".
5. Scannable layout: a short opening paragraph, then 3 to 6 bullets, each one fact with its benefit. Search terms shoppers use appear naturally, not stuffed.
6. The facts that cut returns: delivery, returns, warranty and care limits, stated as the store's real terms.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Invented specs, claims, awards or reviews.
2. Fluffy adjectives ("premium", "luxurious") with no fact behind them.
3. Green, health or safety claims with no proof or qualifier.
4. Long paragraphs that hide the size, fit or compatibility.
5. Keyword stuffing, or a title that is a list of search terms.
6. Copy that breaks the marketplace's own limits on titles, bullets or claims.

**SHAPE:** Title; one or two sentence lead; 3 to 6 benefit bullets; a specs table (attribute, value, source); fit or size; care; what is in the box; shipping and returns line. Usually 120 to 300 words of copy plus the table. Variants for marketplaces follow each one's character limits.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Product claims must be true and backed by evidence before they appear. Environmental, health, nutrition, safety and origin claims follow local advertising and labelling rules. Prices, discounts and stock must match the store. Never copy another seller's text or images.

**DONE MEANS:** A shopper could pick the right size or model from the page alone, and every line would match the product that arrives in the box.
