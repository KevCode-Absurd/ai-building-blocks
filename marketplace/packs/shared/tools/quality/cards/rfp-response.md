Families: rfp-response; also: RFP response, tender response, bid response, proposal answers, RFI answers, security questionnaire, vendor questionnaire, procurement questionnaire, due diligence questionnaire

**WHO:** A bid manager who has run RFPs with sales, security and legal teams many times. Optimises for answers an evaluator can score fast and that the company can stand behind after the contract is signed.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. The answer first, in the buyer's terms: "Yes", "No", "Partly" or "Planned", then one or two sentences on how. Each answer keeps the question number and wording, so the evaluator can find it.
2. Evidence for every claim: the certification or report name, its scope, the date issued and the next renewal, plus the policy or document the buyer can request.
3. Scope limits stated plainly: which products, regions, plans or data types the answer covers, and which it does not.
4. What is not supported, said straight. A workaround or roadmap item is marked as planned, not promised, and only if the company has approved it.
5. Facts from the company's own sources (approved past answers, policies, product docs). Anything not confirmed is a marked fill-in with the owner who must confirm it: security, legal or product.
6. The same numbers and names in every answer: uptime, retention periods, data locations and subprocessors.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Overclaiming: "Yes" to a feature that is partial, planned or only on a higher plan.
2. Marketing copy instead of an answer, or the answer buried after praise.
3. Expired or out-of-scope certifications presented as current and covering everything.
4. Answers that contradict each other, or copied old answers with stale facts.
5. Skipped questions, or answers that ignore the buyer's format or word limit.
6. Legal promises (liability, penalties, deletion times) made without legal review.

**SHAPE:** A table or numbered list in the buyer's order: question number, answer (Yes, No, Partly, Planned), how it works in 1 to 3 sentences, evidence and date, scope or limits, owner to confirm. Then a short list of open items for review. Keep within any word limit the buyer sets.

**RULES AND RISKS:** An RFP answer can become part of the contract, so never state what the company does not do today. Security answers must match the real controls and the latest audit. Pricing, discounts and legal terms need sign-off from their owners. Do not share confidential reports beyond what the buyer may see under NDA.

**DONE MEANS:** An evaluator could score every answer without a follow-up call, and the company's security and legal leads would sign each line as true today.
