Families: sales-pitch; also: sales deck, sales presentation, sell sheet, one-pager, buyer pitch, sales proposal, line review pitch, pitch meeting

**WHO:** A top account executive and sales enablement lead. Optimises for a booked next step: the buyer's problem first, proof where doubt is highest, and material that works without the seller in the room.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. A named buyer (role, type of organisation, who else decides) and their problem in their own words, with the cost of leaving it alone.
2. A story arc: the shift in their world, the problem, what changes, how it works, proof, terms, next step; one point per slide, stated in its headline.
3. Proof matched to this buyer: sourced numbers with their base, named results only with permission, a short case; for resellers, sell-through, margin and trade terms.
4. The top objections answered honestly (price, switching risk, timing, the current alternative).
5. One specific ask (meeting, pilot, trial order) with dates and terms (unapproved ones marked as proposals), plus a leave-behind that reads alone.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. A feature dump about the seller instead of the buyer's outcome.
2. Invented ROI, logos, quotes or customer counts.
3. Fake personalisation and filler openers.
4. A vague close ("let me know your thoughts").
5. Ignoring how the buyer decides (budget cycle, procurement).

**SHAPE:** Open with 3 to 5 lines on the target and the ask. Deck: 10 to 14 slides, headlines of 12 words or fewer, bodies of 40 or fewer; a talk track when the deck will be presented. One-pager: problem, how it works, three proof points, short FAQ, call to action. Cold sequences follow the outbound-sales card.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Claims and comparisons must be true and substantiated. Commercial email needs sender identity, a postal address and a working opt-out (the US has no business-to-business exemption); some regions require consent to email individuals. Gifts fall under anti-bribery limits. Never reveal other clients' data without permission. Check local rules.

**DONE MEANS:** The buyer can say yes to a specific next step without the seller there.
