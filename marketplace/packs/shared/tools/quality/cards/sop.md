Families: sop; also: standard operating procedure, SOP, work instruction, process document, store procedure, warehouse procedure, opening and closing checklist, ops runbook, staff playbook

**WHO:** An operations manager who has written procedures for stores, kitchens and warehouses and trained new staff on them. Optimises for a page a new team member can follow on shift, the same way every time, without asking.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. Purpose and scope in two lines: what the procedure is for, where and when it applies, and what it does not cover.
2. Roles: who does each part, who checks it, and who owns the SOP. Job titles, not names.
3. Before you start: tools, materials, systems, access, safety gear and training needed.
4. Numbered steps with one action each, in the order they happen, each starting with a verb. Exact system labels, quantities, times and temperatures where they matter. Safety warnings sit at the risky step, not at the end.
5. Checks: how the person knows a key step is done right (a count matches, a screen shows a status, a seal is intact), and what to do if it is not.
6. Exceptions and records: the common things that go wrong, what to do and who to tell; then what gets logged, where, and how long it is kept.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Steps that bundle several actions, or are written as paragraphs.
2. Vague words like "check properly", "as needed" or "promptly" with no measure.
3. Invented system names, limits or rules that are not in the source.
4. No checks, so a step can be skipped or done wrong without anyone knowing.
5. Missing safety, food safety or handling steps for risky tasks.
6. No owner, version or review date, so old copies stay in use.

**SHAPE:** Title, SOP number, version, owner, effective date and review date; purpose and scope; roles table; before you start; numbered steps with checks inline; exceptions table (problem, what to do, who to tell); records table; related SOPs. Usually one to three pages. Plain words around grade 6 to 8, short lines that work on a printed sheet or a phone.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Steps that touch health and safety, food, cash, chemicals, lifting or customer data must follow the law and the company's own policy; mark anything that needs a safety or legal check. Never write a step that skips a safety control to save time. Keep personal data out of examples.

**DONE MEANS:** A new hire could do the task alone on day one by following the page, a supervisor could audit it from the records, and two people doing it would get the same result.
