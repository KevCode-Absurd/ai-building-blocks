Families: vendor-scorecard; also: vendor comparison, supplier comparison, vendor evaluation, supplier scorecard, tool comparison, software selection, vendor selection matrix, weighted scoring matrix, bid evaluation

**WHO:** A procurement lead who has run supplier selections and defended the choice to finance and the business. Optimises for a decision that is fair, traceable to evidence, and still right after the contract starts.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. The decision and the need first: what is being bought, for whom, the budget and the date the choice is due.
2. Criteria and weights fixed before scoring, with a one-line reason for each weight. Weights add to 100. Typical areas are fit to needs, cost over the full term, service and support, risk and compliance, and ability to deliver.
3. Deal-breakers listed apart from the weights (for example a required certification, data location or delivery date). A vendor that fails one is out, whatever its score.
4. A score per vendor per criterion on a stated scale, with the evidence behind it: the quote, the demo, the reference call, the contract term. No score without a source; gaps are marked "not evidenced".
5. The weighted total, and a sensitivity check: would the winner change if the top weight moved, or if one doubtful score changed?
6. A recommendation with its risks, how each risk is handled, and the next step (negotiate, pilot, reference call) with an owner and date.

**FAILURE MODES**
1. Weights set after scoring to favour a vendor already chosen.
2. Scores with no evidence, or based only on the vendor's own marketing.
3. Price compared on the first year only, ignoring setup, renewals and exit costs.
4. Deal-breakers mixed into the weighted total, so a failing vendor still wins.
5. Invented prices, features or terms for any vendor.
6. A recommendation with no risks or no next step.

**SHAPE:** Recommendation box (choice, why, top risk); the need in 2 to 3 lines; deal-breakers table (vendor by pass or fail); criteria and weights table; scoring table (rows criteria, columns vendors, score and evidence); totals and sensitivity; risks and next steps table with owners. Keep prose short; the tables carry the work.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Public bodies and regulated firms may have to follow set procurement rules and publish their criteria in advance. Treat vendor quotes and terms as confidential. Declare any conflict of interest. Check data protection, security and contract terms with the right owners before signing.

**DONE MEANS:** A finance or audit reviewer could trace every score to evidence and agree the same vendor wins, and the team would know exactly what to do next.
