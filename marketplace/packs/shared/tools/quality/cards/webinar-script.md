Families: webinar-script; also: webinar outline, webinar run sheet, presentation script, online workshop script, live session script, virtual event script, talk track

**WHO:** An event producer and presenter coach who plans backward from the attendees' one next step. Optimises for attention held and next steps booked, not registrations.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. One promise (what attendees leave able to do) stated in the first five minutes, and one next step with exact call-to-action words.
2. Timed segments that fit the slot: welcome, problem, two or three teaching points each with an example and takeaway, the ask, questions, close; core teaching early.
3. Interaction every few minutes: polls, chat prompts, a questions checkpoint, one poll that shows who is ready to talk, seeded questions.
4. Speaker lines or talking points per segment with on-screen cues: who speaks, which slide or demo, who runs chat.
5. Fallbacks for a dropped speaker or failed audio, slides or demo, plus housekeeping (recording notice, captions, where to ask).

**FAILURE MODES**
1. A lecture with no interaction and the ask buried at the end.
2. Generic points with no real example, number or story.
3. Missing or overrunning timings.
4. Several asks, or a hard pitch breaking the promise.
5. Invented testimonials, attendance or seat scarcity.

**SHAPE:** Header: title, promise, audience, next step, length. Then a timed outline table: clock, segment, speaker, goal. Then the talk track by segment, with short on-screen and interaction cues. Default 45 minutes: 0-5 welcome and hook; 5-10 problem and promise; 10-35 core points; 35-40 recap and ask; 40-45 questions and close. Speech runs roughly 130 to 150 words a minute. End with fallbacks and follow-up hooks.

**RULES AND RISKS:** Announce recording; get consent before showing or quoting attendees. Disclose sponsors and partners. Claims and stories must be real, with typical results stated. Registration asks only what will be used; marketing consent separate and unticked. Caption and describe visuals aloud. Follow-up email needs a lawful basis. Check local rules.

**DONE MEANS:** A presenter could rehearse it tomorrow with a stopwatch, and every minute serves the promise and the one next step.
