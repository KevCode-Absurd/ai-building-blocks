# General lessons (any company, any job)

Each lesson says what won and what to avoid. They sit under the rules of QUALITY-ENGINE-v3.md and never override them. None of them is a length limit: length follows the family profile and the reader (rule 3).

1. **Finish the deliverable; state proposals plainly (rule 1).**
   - Won: complete offers, owners, timelines and numbers stated plainly, with sign-off items listed once in the "To confirm" box; facts the research captured filled in with their exact wording; real, sourced values in public copy (signer, contact route, a real review quoted word for word); the setup that makes the kit true on the day (sample data loaded, notes ready where the team will look).
   - Avoid: blanks, placeholders, TBD, [Your name], [Insert POS] or bracket slots in the body; hedging every recommendation into a question. Open questions go in one short "To confirm" box at the end, outside the text that will be published or sent.

2. **Standard playbook first (rule 4).**
   - Won: the approach a top practitioner would normally take, done excellently, as the base creative drafts must beat. When a live version exists (a job posting, a page), start from it and table each change and why.
   - Avoid: clever moves that add risk, such as repeating an attacker's framing or inventing an untested metric; copying live gates unweighed, or changing them without flagging it.

3. **Lead with the decision, then the kit the reader will use (rule 3).**
   - Won: a one-screen summary up top (an at-a-glance table, a decision screen, a plan with arms, sample size, dates and rule), one-line definitions of technical terms, then every usable part (scripts, templates, checklists, readout templates, macros); short tables (two or three columns) with detail in step lists below.
   - Avoid: cutting usable parts to look short; padding that repeats the summary; tables of four or more columns with paragraphs in cells; the same definition or price paragraph pasted into every variant so different angles read alike (keep each required disclosure, short and fitted to its piece; say other shared lines once); reference tables inside a one-idea, one-button message (move them to optional blocks or build notes). If the request sets a page limit, check it with a real render.

4. **Trace every customer-facing sentence to its source wording (rule 1).**
   - Won: checking wording, not just numbers; quoting policy text, UI labels and click paths word for word; checking where every link and button actually goes; labelling your own rules of thumb as recommended practice, not company guidance.
   - Avoid: small invented details and drifted paraphrases next to real facts; calling reworded text "word for word"; unsourced "most people" or fit claims; advice no source gives.

5. **Treat every number as a claim and compute it in code (rule 1).**
   - Won: the source's own wording, base and date ("X reported in August", not "in August X happened"); per-day prices from exact prices on one basis (first order, renewal and one-time, with the billing period: every 28 vs 30 days changes the year); stating the direction of a gap; one primary metric everywhere.
   - Avoid: shifting the base; a plan reported as done; rounding from a truncated site figure or past what the source says; a lead argument resting on a figure marked unverified or recalled (if a key price cannot be fetched, get it another way: embedded page data, a partner notice, a dated tracker).

6. **Re-check time-sensitive facts live and date them (rule 1).**
   - Won: a live check of prices, offers, stock, assortment, store counts, deal status, landing pages and deadlines; a search for legal disputes involving the subject before scripting; rival facts from same-day brand pages or product feeds when a site blocks automated reads.
   - Avoid: undated figures that read as current; a closed deal described as pending; facts from review sites or retailer listings; calling a link broken from a bot-blocked fetch.

7. **Price and renewal disclosure follow the price everywhere.**
   - Won: renewal frequency and price beside every price the customer sees (hero, plan cards, sticky bars, final call to action, emails); "first order only" on any first-order guarantee; renewal date, the actual next charge and a visible cancel path in the first and pre-renewal email; days to act counted from the real cutoff.
   - Avoid: disclosure only in the hero; the intro price in a renewal notice; "pause" or "no fees" without a policy source; "a week" when the real window is six days.

8. **Merge, don't just pick (step 5).**
   - Won: grafting specific strengths from runner-up drafts and the plain draft into the base.
   - Avoid: discarding a losing draft whole when one part of it beat the winner.

9. **Make outputs paste-safe.**
   - Won: one {snake_case} token format, each filled by a named source, kept only for per-use data (names, order numbers, dates); a fallback line naming the sentence it replaces; branch labels, "pick when" / "do not use if" lines, chat versions and tags kept outside the pasted text; conflicts between sources listed with a recommended choice.
   - Avoid: double-brace tokens, arithmetic on tokens in the body, raw {curly} placeholders in third-party-facing text, a URL followed by punctuation; quietly picking one version of a conflicting policy.

10. **Edit for the reader, then leak-check (steps 6 and 9).**
    - Won: plain words, the decision first, scannable structure, one voice; sources in a visible footer; team-only notes clearly separated.
    - Avoid: internal file names, workflow words, notes to self. Run the leak-check tool and fix every hit.

11. **Pricing tests and price decisions.**
    - Won: profit per visitor as the primary metric, with a window that catches the first renewal (35 days for a 4-week plan); break-even tables per arm; decision tables and later gates fixed before launch; stated planning assumptions the owner can replace, plus a lookup table across traffic levels; for "should we change the price", a dated, gated path with a value step and break-even loss per option.
    - Avoid: blanks for cost, conversion and spread; "hold" with no proposed next move; a raise with no value step on top of a recent increase.

12. **Check the law and the platform rules for public-facing work.**
    - Won: on US cold email, a footer on every email with the sender's real name, postal address and a working opt-out, with the CAN-SPAM rules stated (no business-email exception, opt-outs honored within 10 business days); one disclosure line per creator deal type; for claims reviews, searching the live site for each draft's key words, a ready-to-paste line beside each risk rating and a "counsel can clear it if" box naming the evidence; on review sites, letting the public reply be the invitation and still writing the full follow-up for when contact happens.
    - Avoid: "add an unsubscribe line if your region requires it"; trimming the compliance block for length; identifying or reaching an anonymous reviewer through the CRM, usage data or reviewer details (many review platforms forbid it).
