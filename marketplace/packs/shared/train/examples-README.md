# Examples

This folder holds two or three of your past jobs, with every private name and identifying detail removed. Your agent studies them to see what your best work looks like: how you structure it, what you choose to include, and how you explain your decisions. Each example also shows a weaker version, because the gap between the two teaches more than the good one alone. Your agent must never copy their text or details into new work for another client.

Your coach helps you add them in stage 4 of instructions.md. You can also add them yourself by following the steps below.

## Before you add one

- Use only work that you produced yourself, or work that a client has agreed you may use to teach your agent. If you are not certain, leave it out.
- Remove anything that could identify a private person or client, such as names, brands, websites, emails, phone numbers, addresses, account numbers, and figures only one business would have. Check screenshots and file names too, because details often hide there.
- Replace those details with plain descriptions, such as "Client A, a family-run bakery with two shops".
- Work for a real business that is already public, such as a live ad or a published page, can keep the business's name. Add the link and the date you saw it, and claim nothing about its results that the public source doesn't show.
- Never include passwords, keys, logins or card numbers, even if they appeared in the original files.
- Describe the result honestly. If you never measured it, write "not measured" instead of estimating or rounding it up.
- Write every example as if a stranger might read it, because other people may see your pack if you share it.

## How to add one

Create one file for each example, with a short name that describes the job, such as examples/menu-page-rewrite.md. Then copy this structure into the file:

```markdown
# [Short title: what the job was]

Rights: [I made this work / The client agreed I may use it / Public work, with link and date]
When: [month and year]

## The brief
What the client asked for, in their words where you can, with names taken out. Include what was missing or unclear.

## What I delivered
The work itself, or the most telling part of it. A short excerpt that shows your choices beats a long paste.

## The result
What happened afterwards, as far as you know. Use "not measured" if you don't know.

## What made it good
The two or three choices that made this work better than an average job, and why you made them.

## A weaker version, and why
What an average professional, or plain AI, would have delivered for the same brief, in a few lines. Then the one or two things that make it weaker.

## What I would do differently
Optional. One or two things you would change now.
```

## Tips

- Choose jobs that show your judgment, such as a thin brief you sharpened, a client you talked out of a costly mistake, or a hard trade-off you got right.
- Mix one typical job with one hard one. Typical jobs teach your agent the everyday standard, and hard ones show it where the traps are.
- Spend the most time on "What made it good" and "A weaker version, and why". Those two sections teach your agent more than the rest.
