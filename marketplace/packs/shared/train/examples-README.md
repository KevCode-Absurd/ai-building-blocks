# Examples

This folder holds two or three of your past jobs, with every name and identifying detail removed. Your agent studies them to understand what your best work looks like, including how you structure it, what you choose to include, and how you explain your decisions. It must never copy their text or details into new work for another client.

Your coach helps you add them during stage 5 of instructions.md, but you can also add them yourself by following the steps below.

## Before you add one

- Use only work that you produced yourself, or work that a client has agreed you may use to teach your agent. If you are not certain, leave it out.
- Remove anything that could identify a person or a client, such as names, brands, websites, emails, phone numbers, addresses, account numbers, and figures that only one business would have. Check screenshots and file names as well, because details often hide there.
- Replace those details with plain descriptions, such as "Client A, a family-run bakery with two shops".
- Never include passwords, keys, logins or card numbers, even if they appeared in the original files.
- Describe the result honestly, and if you never measured it, write "not measured" instead of estimating or rounding it up.
- Write every example as if a stranger might read it, because other people may see your pack if you share or list your agent.

## How to add one

Create one file for each example, and give it a short name that describes the job, such as examples/menu-page-rewrite.md. Then copy this structure into the file:

```markdown
# [Short title: what the job was]

Rights: [I made this work / The client agreed I may use it]
When: [month and year]

## The brief
What the client asked for, in their words where you can, with names taken out. Include what was missing or unclear.

## What I delivered
The work itself, or the most telling part of it. A short excerpt that shows your choices beats a long paste.

## The result
What happened afterwards, as far as you know. Use "not measured" if you don't know.

## What made it good
The two or three choices that made this work better than an average job, and why you made them.

## What I would do differently
Optional. One or two things you would change now.
```

## Tips

- Choose jobs that show your judgement, such as a thin brief you sharpened, a client you talked out of a costly mistake, or a hard trade-off you got right.
- Mix one typical job with one hard one, because typical jobs teach your agent the everyday standard while hard ones show it where the traps are.
- Spend the most time on "What made it good", since that section teaches your agent more than any other part of the example.
