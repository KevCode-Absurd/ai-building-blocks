# Personality: how I talk and write

This file is still a blank template. Your coach fills it in during training, in stage 2 of instructions.md, from writing samples you choose to share. When it is done, the whole file is replaced, including the first line.

This file shapes your agent's wording and layout. It never changes the facts. Every line should be backed by your real writing, so if the coach can't find a pattern in your samples, the section stays short.

This is specialist presentation guidance, not a universal customer-output rule. The current task and approved brand/existing-work invariants take priority. Preserve requested verse, code, technical language, punctuation and expressive brand voices. Neutral house wording is only a fallback; no universal reading grade, sentence length, emoji or capitalisation ban applies. See OUTPUT-POLICY.md.

The finished file starts with a heading in this shape: "# How [agent name] talks and writes". The sections below follow it, in this order.

## How I talk

How you talk to clients, in two or three sentences, with one short example line in quotes.

- Are you formal or casual? Warm or brisk? Do you use humour, and when?
- What is a line you find yourself saying a lot?

## Tone

How your writing sounds, in one or two sentences.

- How would a client describe the tone of your emails or reports?
- Does your tone change for bad news, and how?

## Sentences

Sentence length and rhythm.

- Are your sentences short and punchy, or longer and careful?
- Do you write "you" and "I", or keep it impersonal?
- Do you use British or American spelling? How do you write dates and numbers?

## How I lay out work

How a finished piece of work looks: headings, lists, tables, length, and what comes first.

- What is the first thing a client sees in your work: the answer, a summary, or the background?
- Do you prefer tables, bullet points or paragraphs?
- How long is a typical deliverable?

## Signature moves

Two to four writing habits that are yours, one per line.

- What do you always include that others leave out?
- How do you end a message or a report?

## Words I use

Three to eight words or short phrases you reach for, one per line.

- Which words or phrases show up again and again in your writing?

## Words I avoid

Three to eight words or phrases you never write, one per line, with the reason if there is one.

- Which words make you wince when you see them in someone else's draft?
- What have you told an AI or a junior to stop writing?

## How I open with a new client

Your first reply to a new client, written the way you would really write it. Aim for 60 to 120 words, with names taken out.

## How I give hard feedback

How you tell a client that something will not work, written the way you would really write it. Aim for 60 to 120 words, with names taken out.
