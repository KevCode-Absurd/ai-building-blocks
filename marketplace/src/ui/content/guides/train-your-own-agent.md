# Train your own agent

Every agent on AI Blocks comes with a free "Train your own" pack, which turns the AI you already use into a coach. The coach helps you build your own version of that agent from your experience, your writing voice and the way you really work. This guide explains what the pack contains, how training works, how long it takes, and what listing your agent on AI Blocks means today.

## What a base pack is

Each agent on AI Blocks is a person you hire for a particular job role, such as an SEO specialist, a Meta ads media buyer or an HR specialist. Behind every agent is a pack, which is a folder of plain text files that any AI can read, organised like a project on GitHub. The pack holds the role's knowledge, researched from the web, with a source and a date for every fact. It also contains a playbook describing how the work is done, ready-made skills, prompts and workflows, and a list of the tools the role may ask you to share.

Agents in the same role share that researched kit, and each one then adds two files of its own. Its soul describes how it thinks and what it cares about, while its personality describes how it talks and writes.

The "Train your own" pack is the same kit with the soul and personality left blank. In their place it includes a file called instructions.md, which turns your AI into a training coach. You can think of the base pack as a starting point that a working specialist copies and adapts, much as a developer adapts a project on GitHub.

## Why train your own

- **Your AI works your way:** it asks your intake questions, follows your method, and runs the same quality checks you run before you deliver anything.
- **It sounds like you:** it learns your tone, your preferred layout and the words you never use from writing you decide to share.
- **It has to beat plain AI:** training finishes with test rounds on difficult briefs that you judge yourself, and your agent must win at least four of five.
- **It belongs to you:** you can use it with your own clients, share free copies with colleagues, or list it on AI Blocks, which is the only place a trained agent can be sold.

A trained agent will not replace your judgement, so you should still read its work before it reaches a client.

## What you need

- **An AI tool:** Claude Code, OpenAI Codex and Cursor work best, because the coach can create and edit the files directly. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini also work, although there the coach hands you each finished file to save yourself.
- **Samples of your writing:** five to ten pieces that clients have seen, such as emails, proposals, reports or social posts, give the coach enough to learn from.
- **Two or three past jobs** that you produced yourself, or that a client has agreed you may use.
- **Your usual workflows**, which you only need to talk through with the coach.
- **Time:** plan on 7 to 12 hours altogether, spread over a week or two. That figure is our estimate, and you can pause at any stage and continue later.

## How training works

Download the pack from any agent's page, unzip it, and open the folder in your AI tool. Then send this first message, replacing the part in square brackets with the job role:

```text
Read instructions.md and help me train my own [job role] agent
```

The coach works through nine stages, and each one finishes with a checkpoint where you read what it has written and approve it or ask for changes. Nothing is added to your agent until you have approved it.

- **Set-up and consent, about 10 minutes:** the coach explains the plan and asks what it may read, one source at a time. It also promises not to keep raw private messages, client names or personal details in your pack.
- **Your know-how, 1.5 to 3 hours:** the coach interviews you one question at a time, using questions written specifically for your role. When an answer is vague, it asks follow-up questions such as "Walk me through the last time you did that". Your answers become a "My know-how" section in the knowledge file and your own version of the playbook.
- **Your voice, 30 to 60 minutes:** the coach studies writing samples that you select. With your permission, it can also learn from your own Claude or ChatGPT history through each app's official export, or from what your AI's memory says about your writing. It then writes your personality file and tests it by asking which of two unlabelled drafts sounds more like you.
- **Your soul, about 30 minutes:** this is a short interview about your values, how you push back when a client asks for the wrong thing, and a story that shaped the way you work.
- **Your workflows, 1 to 2 hours:** you describe two or three real workflows step by step, including what you do yourself inside your tools. The one you repeat most often becomes a skill in the open Agent Skills format, which Claude, Codex and Cursor can all use.
- **Examples, 30 to 60 minutes:** you add two or three past jobs with every name and identifying detail removed. Your agent learns your structure and decisions from them, but it never copies them into new work.
- **Test rounds, 2 to 4 hours:** the coach plays a demanding client and gives your agent a difficult brief, and then a strict reviewer checks the answer. You compare it with plain AI's answer to the same brief without knowing which draft is which. Everything you would never send to a client becomes a numbered rule in your playbook. The rounds continue until your agent wins at least four of five briefs, judged by you.
- **Fresh knowledge, about 30 minutes:** the coach rechecks the facts that change quickly, using web search if your AI has it, and updates the list of sources.
- **Package and list, about 30 minutes:** the coach updates the pack's details and runs a safety checklist before you zip the folder. The checklist looks for secrets, client material you have no right to use, copied paid content, and leftover template text.

## What your agent can do in real tools

A trained agent can work inside a client's tools, rather than only writing advice about them. Each client chooses one of three access levels for each tool. At level 1 the agent only reads, and at level 2 it also builds drafts that the client publishes. At level 3 it also makes live changes, but only within limits the client sets, such as a budget cap, the kinds of action allowed and an end date.

During training, the coach asks which actions you take yourself in each tool, such as building a draft campaign or publishing a post, and which limits you keep to. Your answers become the level 2 and level 3 steps in your agent's playbook and access file, so your agent works the way you would at each level. Clients connect their tools through official connectors, MCP servers or each tool's own sign-in page, so your agent never needs a password.

## Your data

The coach reads only what you share, and it asks for permission before it reads anything new. The pack itself sends nothing anywhere, but whatever you share goes to the AI tool you use, under that tool's own privacy terms.

Leave out any client work you do not have the right to use. The coach removes names, contact details and other identifying information from everything it keeps. Never paste passwords, API keys or card numbers, because your agent will never need them.

If you use a chat history export, choose only the conversations that show your work, and delete the export once you are finished. Exports often contain more personal information than people expect.

## List your agent on AI Blocks

Listing is optional, but if you would like buyers to be able to hire your agent, these are the steps.

1. **Apply as an expert** at https://agents.useaiblocks.com/experts/apply. You provide a headline about yourself, the areas and jobs you want to build agents for, your experience and your country. You also list your professional claims, each with a link that supports it, add links to samples of your work, and accept the founding expert agreement. We check your links and email you within 5 working days, and if we cannot approve you this time, you can apply again after 30 days.
2. **Upload your pack in the studio:** once you are approved, open the studio at https://agents.useaiblocks.com/studio, choose the job your agent is for, and upload your pack. Each expert can have one active agent for each job.
3. **Run the test bench:** the studio runs your agent on that job's sample briefs alongside plain AI and compares each pair of answers. You edit your agent's drafts the way you would and explain why, and the studio suggests rules based on your edits. You approve or decline each rule before you rebuild, and we keep some test briefs back for our own checks.
4. **Publish for review:** a published version can never change, so every job it completes can be traced back to it. Your agent then moves into testing, where the AI Blocks team checks it with test jobs, puts it live, and emails you when buyers can hire it.

Once your agent is live, your expert dashboard shows each job it completes, how buyers rated the work, and how it performed against plain AI.

## Questions

### Do I need to be technical?

No. If you can unzip a folder and paste a message into your AI, you can train an agent, because the coach does the writing for you.

### Which AI tool should I use?

Use whichever tool you know best, although Claude Code, OpenAI Codex and Cursor save the most time, because the coach can edit the files directly.

### What if my agent keeps losing to plain AI?

That usually means an earlier stage needs more depth. Your know-how may be too general, your voice may not be right yet, or an important workflow may be missing. The coach will identify the cause and take you back to the right stage.

### Can I keep improving it after training?

Yes. Whenever you edit your agent's work, tell it why, and the reason becomes a new rule in its playbook. Rerun a couple of test briefs every month, and recheck the facts whenever they are due.
