The 7 ways a static ad makes its case

An ad can look like a note on a phone, quote a customer, or set 2 things side by side. Each way is called a family. The number is how many of your static ads argue that way, and a family at 0 is one you have never tested. 61 of your 644 are plain product or lifestyle pictures: they make no argument, so they are in no family.

  • Borrowed interface

    14 ads

    It looks like a screen or a printed surface the viewer already trusts: a note, a message, a search, a story.

    Such as: notes, a search bar, a forum post, a story frame, an email, a text thread.

    When to use it: Cold traffic is lost in the first second.

    Test this next

  • Borrowed authority

    42 ads

    Someone other than the brand appears to be talking: a customer, the press, a reviewer.

    Such as: a review, a testimonial, a news front, a podcast clip, a whiteboard.

    When to use it: The doubt is trust, not interest.

    Make one

  • The comparison

    72 ads

    2 states in 1 frame. The viewer does the sum, and so believes the answer.

    Such as: this against the usual way, old against new, problem against solution, myth against fact, before and after.

    When to use it: There is a real difference and nobody is doing the sum.

    Make one

  • The list

    88 ads

    A counted or ranked structure that promises a short read. The number is the hook.

    Such as: reasons why, signs that, a tier list, labelled callouts, a spec sheet.

    When to use it: The product needs several reasons, not 1.

    Make one

  • The negation

    0 ads

    The ad argues against itself, so the viewer stops defending.

    Such as: don't buy this, a warning, a side effect, we're sorry, not for everyone, a zero-star review.

    When to use it: The audience knows the product and is sceptical.

    Test this next

  • The offer

    32 ads

    The price is the creative. It needs no new idea.

    Such as: the offer, the bundle.

    When to use it: Inside a window with a real start and end. Then it comes down.

    Make one

  • The craft object

    335 ads

    Visibly made by a hand. It reads as effort, which money cannot fake.

    Such as: clay, a doodle, a handwritten note, a meme, text set on a close-up.

    When to use it: Everything around it in the feed looks rendered.

    Make one

1 of the 6 families to test has no ad: the negation. Test it and the thinnest of the rest (borrowed interface), each against what already runs, not against each other.

How each family goes wrong, and which to pick for your situation
Borrowed interface:
The borrowed screen is not exact (the type, the bar, the time): the viewer catches the fake instead of reading. It is never a real platform's own interface or logo.
Borrowed authority:
The source did not say it. The 1 family that can cost more than a lost test: the words are real, word for word, with their source.
The comparison:
The 2 states are not shown the same way: then it compares the lighting, not the product.
The list:
The number is padded: 7 weak reasons do worse than 3 real ones.
The negation:
The negation is not answered in the same frame: the viewer is left holding the objection.
The offer:
It runs all the time: a permanent sale is a price cut the margin has to carry.
The craft object:
The craft is too clean: the point is that a person made it.
  • Everything running is product or lifestyle photography: the comparison or the list. Atmosphere without an argument. These put a reason to buy into the frame.
  • Cold traffic never gets past 2 seconds: borrowed interface. It does not read as an ad for long enough for the hook to land.
  • People like the product and still do not buy: the negation. There is an objection nobody says. Raise it first, then answer it.
  • You are the expensive option: borrowed authority or the comparison. A doubt about price is settled by someone else saying it, or by the sum.
  • Retargeting is tired and discounts cover for it: the craft object. Effort is a reason to look again. A 4th discount card is not.
  • Reviews hold a real, specific, repeated complaint: the negation. Saying it first turns the complaint into credibility.
