Feedback and lessons

Guide, chapter 9 of 20

When you tell the app what is wrong with a finished piece of work, it keeps the note, works out what rule it implies, and gives that rule to every later script, plan, prompt and check. This is how the app gets better at a brand.

Lessons: the rules for this brand, the rules for every brand, and the notes they came from

Giving feedback

On any finished image (in a set, or on a production job), there is a box under it.

  • Type what is wrong, or press Speak and say it. A spoken note is transcribed; silence is not turned into words.
  • Make again renders the image again with your note as a correction, and keeps the note.
  • Saving the note alone keeps it without rendering.

Say what is wrong and what right looks like. "The pack is torn open at the side. It opens along the top edge, above the logo, and the gummies pour out of the top" becomes a usable rule. "Looks off" does not.

What happens to a note

flowchart TD
  N[Your note] --> S[Split into separate points]
  S --> O{For each point}
  O -- about this 1 image only --> X[Used for the re-render, not kept as a rule]
  O -- already a rule --> R[The rule is counted again]
  O -- new --> L[A new lesson]
  L --> G{True of any brand?}
  G -- no --> B[This brand's register, switched off until an admin says yes on the Lessons screen]
  G -- yes --> P[A proposed change to the recipe that made the piece. Waits for an admin on Skills]

The 2 registers

  • This brand's lessons live in the brand's folder (lessons.json). They cover how this product is opened, held and used, what this brand never shows, words it never uses.
  • General lessons live with the skills (skills/creative-lessons). They are true of any brand: a pack opens where it really opens; a hand has 5 fingers; nothing floats in a plain photograph.

Nothing a person says becomes a rule by itself (Kevin, 2026-10-06). A rule about this brand is kept switched off, under its note at the bottom of the Lessons screen, until an admin presses Add as a rule; deleting the note throws the rule away. A rule true of any brand becomes a proposed change to the recipe that made the piece, which an admin accepts or rejects on the Skills screen. Only the app's own checks go straight in, because you already said yes to them under What the checks keep finding. A note that repeats a rule the register has counts it again and nothing more: rewording a rule, like switching one back on, is an admin's call.

A lesson you move to general scope by hand is used for that brand first and marked to confirm. Press confirm on the Lessons screen to apply it to every brand.

When you approve or reject on the Production board

Dropping a card on Approved or Rejected asks for the reason.

  • Approved: what went well. Ticks that fit the kind of ad, and a box to type or speak into. Ticks alone are kept on the record. A few words become a lesson to keep doing, for that format.
  • Rejected: what went wrong, and how it could be better. You say what kind of problem it is, and the lesson is kept there:
You say it isThe lesson is used for
This kind of adThat format only: for example AI claymation videos
The brandAnything made for the brand
The script or copy it was made fromWriting scripts and copy
Only this adNothing: the note is kept and no lesson is made

Writers are given the "keep doing" lessons under their own heading, apart from the mistakes to avoid.

What a lesson contains

PartMeaning
RuleThe instruction, said both ways: what to do and what never to do
WhyWhat went wrong
CategoryProduct use, people and hands, composition, words, look, other
Applies toScripts and copy, images, video
ScopeThe whole brand, or 1 product
Only for a formatSet when a verdict on the board said "this kind of ad". The tag Only ... shows it; click the tag to use the lesson for every format
Keep doingSet when it came from an approval: something that worked, not a mistake
A photoOptional: a picture that shows it done right. It is sent to the image model whenever a picture shows the product in use

Product use lessons are treated as facts, not advice: they go into the product's truth sheet and every image that shows the product open or in a hand must follow them exactly.

Managing lessons

On the Lessons screen you can reword a lesson, change its category or scope, switch it off and on, attach a photo, or delete it. The log at the bottom shows every note, what was made of it, and a link to the image it was about.

Keep the list short and sharp. About 10 to 15 lessons per brand is what a model will hold to; more than that and the weaker ones crowd out the ones that matter. The app caps what it sends at 15, product facts first.

What the checks keep finding

Your feedback is 1 way the app learns. The other is its own checks. Every time a check finds something wrong (a product that does not match its photo, a hand drawn wrong, a line the voice check rewrote, a rule that was broken anyway), it is counted.

On the Lessons screen, What the checks keep finding lists anything that has come back 3 times, most frequent first, with what the checks said. Each row is 1 question:

  • Add as a rule. The failures are read into 1 rule for this brand (or for 1 product, or for every brand), and from then on it is in every prompt it applies to. This is the same reader that reads your feedback, so it costs about 1 cent.
  • Not a rule. It is put away. It is asked again only if it comes back 3 times more.

Where a failed image is shown on a set, a line above it says the same thing has failed before and links to the question.

A rule that is still being broken. Each lesson shows how often the checks found it broken since it was added ("Broken 39 times since"). The rules broken most are sent first. A rule that is in every prompt and still broken that often is one that words alone are not fixing: the row says so, and Sharpen the rule rewrites it from what the checks reported. If it is about something no product photo shows (a small pack inside the big one, the product open), attach a photo to the lesson: the image model can copy a photo, and can only guess from words.

A note that could not be read. If the reader fails on a note, the log says so and has a Read it again button.

Scripts and copy

The same loop runs on words. What the checks find in a script or in the words of an ad is sorted by its reason, so each row is 1 habit of the writer and can become 1 rule:

What the row saysWhat the rule becomes
A line that ends on a weak word or a trailing clauseEnd on the word that carries the point
The buyers' own words for the problem were not usedName the problem the way the buyers do
A line any brand in the category could have writtenSay what only this brand could say
A line without the brand's own soundWrite in the sound of the brand's own lines
A line that blames, lectures or frightens the buyerThe problem is the thing, never the buyer
A verb left uncontractedContract what a person contracts aloud
The "it's not X, it's Y" frameSay what the thing is
A line too long or too tangled to say in 1 breath1 idea to a sentence
A line that reads written, not saidThe word a person says aloud
The check "length fit" (or any other rule check) was not passedWritten from what the check reported

With a model switched on, the rule is written for your brand from the lines that failed, not copied from this table.

Read the counts on words with care. Most of these rows come from the 2 editors (the copy pass and the voice check). Each rewrites a script before you see it, and each finds about 5 things in any script: run a second time on 3 scripts it had just fixed, the voice check found 5 more in each (measured 2026-10-05). So a count of 50 is 50 lines the editor rewrote, not 50 scripts that failed, and it will not fall to zero. The rows are useful for what they name. They are not a score.

Is it getting better? The line under the section's heading gives what the rule checks found a script, over the last 10 scripts against the 10 before. Only the rule checks are counted there, because they read every script the same way. When a check is added, that number rises once for that reason alone.

A word rule that is broken. The voice check is sent the rules and says which one a line broke. That is counted on the rule ("Broken 4 times since"), the same as for images.

What you change by hand

When you rewrite generated words and save, that is the plainest feedback there is. Since phase 45 the app keeps it, and since phase 47 it keeps it at every place you can edit:

Where you editWhat is kept
A scriptEach line, its on-screen text, a hook, and which hook you made the main one
The words that run with a scriptThe primary text and the headline
An image adThe words on the image, the primary text, headline, description and button, the scene, and the image prompt
A video jobThe plan, the words of a storyboard frame, and which person you cast
A sprintAn ad's idea
A landing page, a persona, an angle, an offerThe field you changed

Each save keeps the text before and after. Code, not a model, finds the small changes inside it: a phrase taken out, a phrase put in place of another, a line made shorter, a number changed, capitals changed.

The same change 3 times. When you have made the same change 3 times for a brand, the app asks once: "You've made this change 3 times. Add it as a rule?" The questions are on the Lessons screen and on the script you are in.

  • Add as a rule. 1 small call words the rule from the phrase and up to 3 of the lines before and after (measured: 0.07 cents). The rule goes into the brand's lessons. A rule you mark as general is filed as proposed, and reaches every brand only when it is promoted.
  • Say no and it is put away.

The thresholds are the app's own judgement, not measured: 3 repeats, a phrase of 5 words or fewer, and a line counts as "made shorter" at 6 words or more cut to 70% or less.

Saving an offer from the Build an offer path records an edit too: where you change the name, the page line or the say line the model wrote, before you save.

When the list gets long

A prompt carries 15 lessons at most, so a long list would leave some out. When a brand has more than 12 rules in use for 1 kind of work (words, pictures or video), the app folds the ones that say the same thing into 1 rule. This is 1 small call (measured once: 0.11 cents on 9 rules).

  • The app's code checks every merge. A merge that adds or loses a number or a phrase in quotes is refused, and so is one that joins rules of different kinds.
  • Nothing is deleted. The old rules are switched off and marked with the rule that replaced them.
  • To do it by hand: pnpm lessons:compact <brand> --area words. Add --dry to see the result and write nothing. To undo a merge: pnpm lessons:compact <brand> --undo <id>.
  • There is no button for this on the Lessons screen yet.

A rule you added yourself (typed, given as feedback, or made from your own edits) is always sent to the brand's writers. Applying a rule to all brands is still your decision.

When feedback changes the method itself

A note can hold two kinds of thing. "Our pouch is always shown standing" is about this brand, and becomes a rule for this brand at once, as above. "The FAQ should open with delivery and returns" is true of any brand: it is about the method the app follows, which is written down in files called skills. A rule like that does not go straight in. The app works out the smallest change to the skill that made the piece and puts it on the Skills screen (Settings, then Skills in the row) as a before and after, with what the person said beside it.

  • Accept writes the change and the skill moves to its next version. Reject keeps the note and drops the change.
  • Every skill shows its version, its size in words and when it last changed. History lists every version; any version can be put back, and putting one back is itself a version, so nothing is lost.
  • Tighten asks for the same skill in fewer words. The result is refused if any heading, number or quoted phrase is gone or new, or if it is not really shorter; what survives is shown as a before and after like any other change, and nothing changes until it is accepted.
  • The person who gave the feedback never sees a skill. They see, on the Lessons screen, that their note led to a change and whether it was accepted.
  • A skill is shown by a readable name, never its folder: Static Ad Copywriter, not image-ad-copy; Winning Layout Library, not Static Ad Factory. The names live in 1 table (src/lib/skills/titles.ts), or a skill can carry its own title: in its front matter. Folders, skills_used and links are unchanged.

For now everyone who can open the app can accept a change. When several people use the app, this becomes an admin's job and every accepted version carries their name.

A landing page has the same feedback box as an image: under the page's score, What should the app get right next time?
