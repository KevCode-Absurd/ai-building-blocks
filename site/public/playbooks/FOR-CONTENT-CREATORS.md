# Playbook: Creators and media

Use this file with any skill, workflow or specialist from AI Blocks. It tells you how a creator or small media business works, so you can shape the task to fit it. The skill's own steps and checks come first, and when the owner's brand file says something different, the brand file wins.

## This business

This covers YouTubers, podcasters, newsletter writers, bloggers, streamers and creators on TikTok, Instagram and other social apps. Most run alone or with a small team. Money comes from platform ad revenue, brand deals, affiliate links, paid members and their own products. Every one of these depends on the audience's trust.

## Numbers that matter

Ask the owner for their own numbers. Do not guess them or fill gaps with typical figures.

- **Audience size by channel:** subscribers, followers or email readers on each channel. Count each channel on its own, since the same person may follow on several.
- **Views or reach per post:** how many people saw a piece of content. Take it from each platform's own stats for the same time window.
- **Engagement:** how many people liked, commented, shared or saved. Add those actions up, then divide by views or reach for that post.
- **Email open and click rate:** how many readers open an email and click a link. Divide opens, then clicks, by emails delivered.
- **Revenue per channel:** what each income stream earned. Add up ad revenue, sponsors, affiliates, members and product sales on their own.
- **Cost per piece:** what one video, episode or post costs to make. Add the owner's time at an hourly rate, plus pay for editors, tools and gear.
- **Paid member churn:** how many paying members leave. Divide members who cancelled by members at the start of the month.

## Stage by stage

### 1. Niche and content plan

- Pick a clear topic and a clear viewer, so people know why to follow.
- Study what already works in the niche, then plan an angle the owner can own.
- Plan a mix of content the owner can keep making for months.
- Watch for: copying a bigger creator's format without the owner's own view.

### 2. Site, brand and tools

- Set up a simple site or link page with the newsletter signup, contact details and media kit.
- Keep names, photos and channel descriptions the same across platforms.
- Choose tools that save the owner real time, such as editing, scheduling and email.
- Watch for: paying for many tools before there is a steady posting habit.

### 3. Search and discovery

- Use the words people search for in titles, descriptions and captions.
- Titles and thumbnails must match what the content delivers.
- Blog posts, show notes and transcripts help people find the work in Google and AI search.
- Watch for: clickbait titles that win the click and lose the viewer's trust.

### 4. Grow your audience

- Fit each post to the platform, its length and how people use it.
- Move followers to the email list, since the owner controls that channel.
- Work with other creators who share the audience.
- Watch for: chasing every new platform at once and posting nowhere well.

### 5. Sponsors, products and members

- Price brand deals from the owner's own numbers: reach, engagement, the work involved and usage rights.
- Only take sponsors the owner would recommend to a friend.
- Paid members and products should give clear value the free content does not.
- Watch for: a deal with vague usage rights, exclusivity or payment terms.

### 6. Make and publish

- Plan a schedule the owner can keep, then batch work like scripts and filming.
- Write scripts and outlines that get to the point fast.
- Check facts, links, sponsor lines and disclosures before anything goes live.
- Watch for: a schedule so tight that quality drops or the owner burns out.

### 7. Community and fans

- Reply to comments and messages in the owner's own voice.
- Set clear community rules and keep spaces safe and kind.
- Ask the audience what they want next, and show them you listened.
- Watch for: replying in a way that sounds automated or ignoring hard questions.

### 8. Money, legal and team

- Track income by stream and by month, and set money aside for tax.
- Read every brand contract for rights, deadlines, exclusivity and payment dates.
- Hire editors, designers or managers with clear briefs and written terms.
- Watch for: using music, clips or images the owner does not have the rights to use.

## Words this business uses

- **Niche:** the topic and audience a creator focuses on.
- **Hook:** the first line or first seconds that make someone stop and watch or read.
- **Thumbnail:** the small image shown before someone clicks a video.
- **Media kit:** a short document for sponsors with the creator's audience, numbers and prices.
- **Brand deal:** a paid partnership where a company pays the creator to feature it.
- **Usage rights:** what a sponsor may do with the creator's content, where and for how long.
- **Exclusivity:** a promise not to work with a sponsor's rivals for a set time.
- **Affiliate link:** a tracked link that pays the creator when someone buys through it.
- **Churn:** paying members or subscribers who cancel.
- **Repurpose:** turning one piece of content into others, like clips from a long video.

## Ask the owner first

If the brand file does not already answer these, ask before you start:

1. Which channels do you use, and which matters most right now?
2. Who is your audience, and what do they come to you for?
3. What are your own numbers for reach, engagement and income?
4. Is this piece sponsored or does it have affiliate links?
5. What topics, brands or claims will you not touch?
6. How often can you publish without it hurting quality?

## Rules

- Never invent numbers, results, reviews, testimonials, quotes or audience stats. Use only what the owner gives you, and mark any gap.
- Platform rules change. This includes YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and ad platforms. Tell the owner to check the current official help pages before acting. Do not state policy or payout details you are not sure of.
- Sponsored content, gifted products and affiliate links must be disclosed the way local law and the platform require. The owner should check what applies.
- Health, legal, money and safety claims need care. Never promise results, and never present a sponsor's claims as the owner's own tested facts unless the owner says so.
- Keep the owner's approval before anything is sent, published, bought or changed.
