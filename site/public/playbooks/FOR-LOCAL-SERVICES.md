# Playbook: Local businesses and shops

Use this file with any skill, workflow or specialist from AI Blocks. It tells you how a local business works, so you can shape the task to fit it. The skill's own steps and checks come first, and when the owner's brand file says something different, the brand file wins.

## This business

This covers salons, barbers, clinics, restaurants, cafes, hotels, gyms, trades, estate agents and retail shops. They serve people in one area, in person or at the customer's home. Money comes from bookings, walk-ins, orders, jobs, memberships, room nights, fees or sales in the shop. Most depend on people finding them nearby, trusting their reviews and coming back.

## Numbers that matter

Ask the owner for their own numbers. Do not guess them or fill gaps with typical figures.

- **Average spend:** what one customer pays per visit, order or job. Divide sales by the number of visits, orders or jobs in the same period.
- **Bookings or covers filled:** how full the diary, tables, classes or rooms are. Divide booked slots by available slots for each day or week.
- **No-show rate:** how many booked customers do not turn up. Divide no-shows by total bookings.
- **Enquiry to booking rate:** how many calls, messages or quote requests turn into a booking or job. Divide bookings by enquiries.
- **Repeat customer rate:** how many customers come back. Divide returning customers by all customers in a set period.
- **Cost to win a customer:** what ads and offers cost for each new customer. Divide spend by new customers it brought in.
- **Review count and rating:** how many reviews the business has and its average score. Take both from each review site the owner uses.
- **Gross margin:** what is left after the direct cost of each sale. Take sales, subtract the cost of stock, food or materials, then divide by sales.

## Stage by stage

### 1. Services, prices and area

- List each service, menu item or product with a clear price or price range.
- Name the area the business serves, and for trades, how far they travel.
- Price from the owner's real costs, time and margin.
- Watch for: prices that differ between the website, the menu and the booking page.

### 2. Website and booking

- The site shows what is offered, prices, opening hours, location and how to book, order or call.
- Make the main action easy on a phone: book, order, call or get directions.
- Booking businesses like salons and clinics need online booking. Walk-in businesses like cafes and shops need clear hours and directions.
- Watch for: opening hours on the site that do not match the shop door or Google.

### 3. Google Maps and local search

- Keep the Google Business Profile complete and correct: name, address, phone, hours, services and photos.
- Use the same name, address and phone number on every site and directory.
- Add real photos of the place, the team and the work.
- Watch for: holiday hours and closures that never get updated.

### 4. Local ads and social

- Use ads and offers to fill quiet days and times, not the times that are already full.
- Show real work: before and after photos for trades and salons, dishes for restaurants, rooms for hotels.
- Target the area the business serves.
- Watch for: discounts that bring one-time bargain hunters but no regulars.

### 5. Turn calls into bookings

- Answer calls, messages and booking requests fast, in a friendly voice.
- Give clear prices or a price range, the next free slot and what to do next.
- For trades and estate agents, follow up quotes and viewings on time.
- Watch for: missed calls and unanswered messages that go to a rival.

### 6. Run the day

- Plan staff around busy and quiet times.
- Keep stock, food or materials in line with what sells.
- Send booking reminders to cut no-shows, if the owner uses bookings.
- Watch for: one person holding all the know-how, so the day stops when they are off.

### 7. Reviews and regulars

- Ask happy customers for a review, in a simple way, at the right moment.
- Reply to every review, good or bad, politely and in the owner's voice.
- Give regulars a reason to return, like a loyalty card, membership or rebooking at checkout.
- Watch for: replying to a bad review in anger or sharing private details about the customer.

### 8. Money, legal and team

- Track cash in and out each week, and set aside money for tax and slow months.
- Stay on top of licences, insurance, hygiene, safety and staff rules that apply.
- Hire and train staff with clear steps for each task.
- Watch for: rules that differ by place and trade, so the owner should check local requirements.

## Words this business uses

- **Google Business Profile:** the free listing that shows a business on Google Maps and in local search.
- **Walk-in:** a customer who comes without booking.
- **No-show:** a customer who books and does not turn up.
- **Covers:** the number of diners a restaurant serves.
- **Quote:** a price given before a job starts, common in trades.
- **Rebooking:** booking the next visit before the customer leaves.
- **Footfall:** the number of people who come into a shop or venue.
- **Local search:** searches like "near me" where results depend on where the person is.

## Ask the owner first

If the brand file does not already answer these, ask before you start:

1. What kind of business is it, and do customers book, walk in, order or ask for quotes?
2. Which area do you serve, and where are you based?
3. What are your services or products, prices and opening hours?
4. When are you busy, and when are you quiet?
5. What real reviews, photos and results can you use?
6. Are there rules for your trade on claims, prices or health and safety?

## Rules

- Never invent numbers, results, reviews, testimonials or quotes. Use only what the owner gives you, and mark any gap. Never write reviews for the business. Before any reward for reviews, the owner should check the review site rules.
- Platform rules change. This includes Google Business Profile, review sites, Instagram, TikTok and ad platforms. Tell the owner to check the current official help pages before acting. Do not state policy details you are not sure of.
- Sponsored content and paid partnerships with local influencers must be disclosed the way local law and the platform require. The owner should check what applies.
- Health, legal, money and safety claims need care. Clinics, gyms and beauty treatments must not promise results or make medical claims they cannot back. Trades must not overstate safety or guarantees.
- Keep the owner's approval before anything is sent, published, bought or changed.
