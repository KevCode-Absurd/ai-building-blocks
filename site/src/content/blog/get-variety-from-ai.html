Why your AI gives you 20 of the same

Ask an AI for twenty advertising ideas and then read them side by side. You will often find the same idea wearing twenty different outfits: the same opening, the same promise and the same look, with a few words swapped around. That is very little use when you want to test what works, because twenty near copies teach you about as much as a single one.

The reason is fairly simple. A single conversation usually settles on one method, one template and one visual style, then repeats it with small changes. Every new idea is shaped by the ideas already on the page, so a long list gradually drifts back to the same few patterns. The answer is to treat your AI like a team of experts instead of one assistant, because different makers using different methods in separate chats produce work that genuinely differs.

Here is the short version of this guide:

  • Before you ask for any ideas, ask your AI to list the different kinds of work that exist for the job.
  • Decide the mix at the beginning: one kind, a mix, a wide spread, or "you decide".
  • If you have no particular preference, use 50% proven, 30% new and 20% wild card.
  • Give each kind its own maker in its own chat, so the makers can't copy each other's work.
  • Run a sameness check in a fresh chat, and replace the copies before you use anything.
  • Save your preferred mix in your me.md file, so you never have to explain it again.

Step 1: List the kinds of work that exist for the job

Real variety starts before anybody writes the first idea. Most people simply ask for "ideas" and receive whatever kind of idea the AI happens to reach for first. Instead, ask it to map the different kinds of output that exist for your particular job, and only then decide what you actually want it to make.

A kind is a real difference in method, format or look, not a new headline on the same idea. For video advertising, the kinds include a customer-style phone video, a claymation story, a podcast-style conversation between two people, a product demonstration and a founder talking to camera. For a business plan, the kinds are genuinely different routes to growth, not ten wordings of the same plan.

You can ask your AI to produce that list, or you can begin from one of the picker skills in the library. The Video ad script type picker gives you four different script types, each with its own opening and tone. The Meta concept diversity test planner sorts your current ads into 13 concept types and shows you where the gaps are. The Seven-bucket ad concept brainstormer creates ideas in seven separate groups, so they can't all turn out identical.

Prompt: List the kinds before the ideas

I need [what you need, for example: video ads for our spring launch] for [your business, in one line].
Who it is for: [your buyer]
What has worked for us before: [past winners, or "nothing yet"]

Don't write any ideas yet. First:
1. List every distinct kind of [output] that exists for this job. A kind is a different method, format or look, not a new wording. Aim for 8 to 12 kinds.
2. For each kind, give one line on how it works and one line on when it does well.
3. Mark each kind PROVEN (it has worked for us, from what I told you), NEW (we haven't tried it, but it fits) or WILD CARD (a long shot that could surprise us).
4. Then ask me one question and wait: "Do you want one kind, a mix, a wide spread, or should I decide? If one kind, which one?"

Use only the facts I gave you. Write MISSING for anything you need and don't have.

Step 2: Decide the mix before anyone makes anything

This is the question to settle at the very beginning, because the answer changes everything that follows. Do you want one kind, a mix of a few, a wide spread across many, or would you rather the AI decided for you? If you want only one kind, say exactly which one.

Step 3: Give each kind its own maker, in its own chat

Once you know the mix, don't ask a single chat to make everything. Give every kind its own maker in a separate chat, or in a separate project if your AI offers them. A maker that can't see the other makers' work has no way to copy it, and that separation is what keeps the kinds genuinely different from each other.

A maker can be an ordinary chat with a clear brief, or you can turn each one into a different expert. You could add a different skill to each chat, hire a different specialist from Hire an AI specialist, or give each maker a different mind from the Minds library. Our guide to using AI minds as expert advisers explains how to choose minds that think differently. For a bigger job, Plan an AI team helps you choose the specialists and decide who hands work to whom.

Give every maker exactly the same facts, but a different assignment. Use the brief below in each chat, changing only the kind and the number of pieces.

Prompt: Brief one maker for one kind

You are the maker for one kind of [output] only: [the kind, for example: a podcast-style chat between two people].
Other makers are working on other kinds. Stay inside yours.

Facts you may use:
[paste product details, prices, proof, buyer words, or attach your brand file]

Make [number] pieces of this kind.
- Each piece must have a different main idea: a different reason to buy, a different opening or a different moment in the buyer's day.
- Don't reuse an opening line, a joke or a phrase across pieces.
- Use only the facts above. Write MISSING where you need a fact I didn't give.

For each piece, give: a one-line summary, the piece itself, and one line on what makes it different from the others you made.

Step 4: Run a sameness check with fresh eyes

Even with separate makers, some pieces will turn out more similar than they first appear. So before you use anything, ask someone who didn't make the work to check it. A fresh chat works well for this, because it has no favourites and no memory of how much effort each piece took.

Paste in every piece without saying which maker produced which. Ask the checker to group the pieces that would feel identical to a customer, then to say which one to keep from each group. Replace the others with new pieces from the makers whose kinds are now missing from the set.

Prompt: Sameness check

You did not make any of the work below. Your job is to find copies.

The mix I asked for: [for example: 5 proven, 3 new, 2 wild card]
The pieces:
[paste every piece, numbered, with no maker names]

Do this:
1. Compare the pieces on six things: method, format, opening, main reason to buy, look or tone, and who it speaks to.
2. Group any pieces a buyer would see as the same idea, even if the words differ. Say why in one line per group.
3. From each group, keep the strongest one and mark the others REPLACE.
4. Check the mix: count proven, new and wild card pieces after the cuts, and say which kinds are now missing.
5. Score the whole set from 1 to 10 for real variety, with one line of reason.

Show the groups first, then the counts, then the score.

Asking for volume without getting clones

Sometimes you really do want a large number of pieces, such as thirty advertisements to test across a month. Volume is perfectly fine, as long as it comes from many different kinds rather than from one long list in a single chat.

  • Spread the volume across several makers, because four makers producing five pieces each will vary far more than one chat producing twenty.
  • Ask for more rough ideas than you need, then cut. Ask each maker for ten one-line ideas, choose the best three, and only then ask for the finished pieces.
  • Change only one thing at a time inside a kind when you want a clean test. If you are testing openings, keep everything else identical, so you know exactly what made the difference.
  • Put a firm rule in every brief, such as "no two pieces may open the same way", and let the sameness check enforce it.

Save your mix in your me.md so you never answer again

Answering the mix question every single time becomes tiresome quickly. A me.md is a short private file that tells every AI who you are and how you like your answers. It has a Preferences section for things like formats, language and spelling, and that section is a natural home for your variety rule.

Add a few lines like the ones below, then save the file. If you save it on AI Blocks, the parts each task needs are added to whatever you copy and download. You can also paste the short core into your AI's settings, so every new conversation starts with it.

Prompt: Add my variety rule to my me.md

Please add these lines to the Preferences part of my me.md, and show me the updated part:

- When I ask for ideas or options, first list the different kinds that exist for the job, then ask: "One kind, a mix, a wide spread, or should I decide?"
- If I say "you decide", or don't answer, use 50% proven, 30% new and 20% wild card. Label each piece with its group.
- Proven for me means: [the kinds that have worked for you, or "ask me"].
- Never give me near copies. Check the set for sameness before you show it to me.

Questions people ask

Why does AI keep giving me the same idea?

A single chat tends to stick with one method, one template and one visual style. Each idea is shaped by the ideas before it on the page, so long lists gradually drift back to the same few patterns. Separate makers, each working on a different kind, break that habit.

Is 50/30/20 a rule I have to follow?

No. It is the starting mix we recommend when you have no preference: half proven, a third new and one in five a wild card. Change it whenever you know better, for example by going mostly proven during a busy sales month.

What counts as a wild card?

A wild card is a kind you would not normally try, chosen deliberately because it might surprise you. It still uses your real facts and suits your brand. It should also be inexpensive to try, so that a miss costs you very little.

Do I really need separate chats?

For genuine variety, yes, because a maker that can see the other pieces tends to copy them or react against them. Separate chats or separate projects keep each kind clean. If you only need one kind, a single chat is perfectly fine.

Can I just ask for 50 ideas at once?

You can, but you should expect plenty of repeats. You will get more value from asking several makers for ten one-line ideas each, cutting those down to the best, and then running a sameness check on whatever remains.

How do I make my AI remember the mix I like?

Write it into the Preferences section of your me.md file, using the prompt above. Then save the file on AI Blocks, or put the short core into your AI's settings, and your AI will know your preferred mix from the very first message.
