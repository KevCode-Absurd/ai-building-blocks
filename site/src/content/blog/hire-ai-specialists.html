What does it mean to hire an AI specialist?

Hiring an AI specialist means giving your own AI a ready-made job description, a working method and a set of checks for one kind of work, such as customer support, search engine optimisation or paid ads. On AI Blocks you can choose from more than 1,000 specialist roles at Hire an AI specialist, or from 505 named agents on agents.useaiblocks.com. Both are free, and both run inside the AI you already pay for, such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

The specialist does not replace your judgement. It behaves more like a capable new colleague who arrives with a clear method, asks sensible questions and shows you its work, but who still needs a good brief and clear limits before it touches anything live.

Here is the short version of this guide:

  • Choose a specialist whose page describes the result you need, then read what it expects you to give it.
  • Download the pack, add every file to a project in your AI, and say "Read START_HERE.md".
  • Write a brief with the goal, the audience, your facts, the deliverable, a definition of done and your limits.
  • Start every tool at read-only access, and confirm the exact list of actions before the first live change.
  • Check the first few jobs yourself, and hire a second specialist when the job needs a genuinely different skill.

How to write a brief a specialist can act on

Most disappointing results come from thin briefs rather than weak specialists. A specialist cannot guess your prices, your audience or what "good" means to you, so a few minutes spent on the brief usually saves an hour of corrections. A strong brief covers six things.

  • The goal: the result you need, in one sentence, and the decision or action it supports.
  • The audience: who will read or use the result, and what they already know.
  • Your facts: prices, policies, product details and examples, with dates where they matter.
  • The deliverable: the exact format, such as a table, a draft email or a list of changes.
  • A definition of done: two or three tests the result must pass before you accept it.
  • Your limits: what is out of scope, what it may touch and what it must never do.

Before the specialist starts writing, settle one more question: do you want one kind of output, a mix, a wide spread, or "you decide"? If you want one kind, name it. If you have no preference, ask for roughly half proven ideas, about a third new ideas and one in five as a deliberate wild card, so you are not handed twenty versions of the same idea.

Prompt: Brief a new specialist

Read START_HERE.md and work as the specialist in this project.

The goal: [the result I need, and what it is for]
The audience: [who will read or use it, and what they already know]
My facts: [paste prices, policies, product details, examples, with dates]
The deliverable: [exact format, length and number of items]
Done means: [two or three tests the result must pass]
Out of scope: [what you must not do or change]

Variety: [one kind / a mix / a wide spread / you decide]. If I say "you decide", give about 50% proven ideas, 30% new ones and 20% wild cards, and label each one.

Before you start:
1. List anything missing that would change the result, and ask me only about those.
2. Show me a short plan of the steps you will take.
3. Wait for my yes before you produce the full work.

Never invent a fact, number or quote. Write MISSING: [what] where it belongs.

Set the access level before the first live action

The agent packs on agents.useaiblocks.com describe three access levels for each tool, and they are a sensible way to think about any specialist you hire. You choose the level separately for every tool, and the agent starts at reading until you say otherwise.

LevelWhat the specialist may doExample
1. ReadRead exports, screenshots or a viewer seat. No changesReview last month's ad results from an export
2. Read and draftBuild drafts inside the tool. You publish themPrepare a draft campaign or a draft post for you to approve
3. Read, write and publish within limitsMake live changes, but only inside limits you set, after you confirm the action listMove budget between ad sets up to a set cap until an end date

The specialist packs from /roles follow the same idea in their PERMISSIONS.md file. Reading and drafting are allowed by default, while sending, posting, spending, deleting and any live change need your approval every time. Limited freedom to act alone stays switched off until you write a specific limit into that file.

Whatever the level, ask for the exact list of actions before the first live change, and read it properly before you say yes. Connect tools through their official connectors or sign-in pages, and never paste passwords, API keys or card numbers into a chat, because a well-built specialist never needs them. Every action at levels 2 and 3 should be logged, so you can see later what was done and when.

Prompt: Confirm the action list before going live

Before you change anything live, stop and show me your action list.

Tool: [the tool, such as my ad account, store or inbox]
Access level I grant: [1 read / 2 read and draft / 3 live within limits]
Limits: [budget cap, kinds of action allowed, end date]

For each action, give me:
1. Exactly what you will change, from what to what.
2. Why, in one sentence, with the fact or number behind it.
3. How to undo it.
4. Whether it is inside the limits above. Mark anything outside them as NEEDS MY YES.

Do nothing until I reply "approved" to this exact list. After you act, log each action with the time and the result, and tell me about anything that did not go as planned.

Check the first jobs before you trust the specialist

A specialist's first five to ten jobs tell you far more than its profile does. Read every result in full, compare it with your definition of done, and note what you had to change. When you correct something, add a dated line to the pack's TRAINING.md file explaining what was wrong and what good looks like, because your AI reads that file on later jobs.

For anything that will reach customers, have the work checked by someone who did not make it. Paste the result into a fresh chat with a check skill, such as the Angry customer reply checker, or ask a second specialist to review it. Widen the specialist's access only when its record on that particular job has earned it.

Prompt: Review a specialist's first job

You did not make the work below. Review it as a strict outside checker.

The brief it was given: [paste the brief]
Done means: [paste the tests from the brief]
The work: [paste the result]

1. Mark each "done means" test PASS, FAIL or CAN'T TELL, with one line of reason.
2. List every fact, number or promise that is not in the brief's facts.
3. Name the three changes that would most improve it.
4. Write one short rule for TRAINING.md that would prevent the biggest problem next time.

Don't rewrite the work. Keep the review under 250 words.

When to hire more than one specialist

One specialist is enough when the request is specific and sits inside one skill, such as fixing the titles on ten product pages. Hire more than one when the job needs genuinely different expertise, such as research, then writing, then a compliance check, or when you want a second opinion from someone with a different method.

Give each specialist one seat with one job. The seats are a maker who produces the work, a critic who looks for weaknesses, a fact checker who compares every claim with your facts, and a judge who picks between options. Run them in separate chats or projects, so they cannot simply copy each other, then merge the results and check them against a short checklist. Plan an AI team lets you choose the specialists, decide who passes work to whom, check the plan and download it for your AI. A worked example: a jewellery shop's product pages

This is an invented example to illustrate the method. Imagine a small online jewellery shop with forty product pages that rarely appear in search results. The owner hires the SEO specialist from /roles, downloads the pack and adds it to a Claude project together with her org context and a list of current prices.

Her brief names the goal, which is better titles and descriptions for the ten best-selling pages, and sets the audience as gift buyers searching on their phones. Done means every title stays under sixty characters, every claim matches her product sheet and nothing changes on the live site. She grants level 1 access only, through an export of the pages, so the specialist reads and drafts but changes nothing.

The first batch comes back as a table of drafts. She runs prompt 3 in a fresh chat, which flags two descriptions that call silver-plated pieces "solid silver". She fixes the facts, adds a rule about metals to TRAINING.md and uploads the corrected drafts to her store herself.

Questions people ask

Is hiring an AI specialist free?

Yes. The specialist packs on /roles and the house agents on agents.useaiblocks.com are free to download. They run on your own AI plan, so any cost comes from the AI app you already use.

What's the difference between a specialist from /roles and a named agent?

Both give your AI a role, a method and checks. A specialist from /roles is a job role built from methods in the library. A named agent on the agents site also has a name, a personality and sample requests, and it loads through a single file in chat apps.

Can a specialist publish or spend money for me?

Only if you connect a tool and grant that level of access, and even then it should show you the exact action list and wait for your approval. Keep sending, publishing and spending behind your yes until the specialist has a clean record on that job.

Do I need to download anything?

Not necessarily. On a role page, Copy as one message gives you a single message for ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and on the agents site the Connector tab loads the agent into a connected AI with nothing to download.

How do I make a specialist better over time?

Record each correction in the pack's TRAINING.md, with what was wrong and what good looks like. If you want an agent built on your own know-how, the train your own AI agent guide walks you through it.
