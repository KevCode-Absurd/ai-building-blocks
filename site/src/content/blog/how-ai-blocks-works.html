The short version

General AI models are trained to produce the most probable answer to a question, which in practice means the most common answer rather than the most accomplished one. That tendency serves everyday tasks well, but on commercial work where the details determine whether a business makes or loses money, the most common answer is rarely good enough. AI Blocks adds an expert layer on top of general AI agents, so that the knowledge, judgement and working methods of professionals who excel at a particular job travel with the agent that performs it.

The business model rests on five connected parts:

  • We research and publish free base agents for real professional roles, such as a Meta advertising media buyer or an SEO specialist, which anyone can download, inspect and modify.
  • Experts copy a base agent and add their own knowledge, writing voice, operating rules and anonymised examples, in much the same way that a developer forks and adapts an existing project on GitHub.
  • Each expert agent is evaluated against plain AI on identical briefs, and its training is complete only when it wins at least four of five comparisons.
  • A coordinator, which we are building now, will divide each job into smaller components and assign every component to the expert agent with the strongest record on that specific kind of work.
  • Once buyers begin paying for jobs, the expert who built each agent will receive the majority of the fee, while AI Blocks will retain a share for sourcing the work, running it and checking the result.

The remainder of this guide explains each of these parts in detail, works through two concrete examples, and it distinguishes clearly between the components that are available today and those that are still under construction.

Why general AI tends to produce average work

A general AI model learns from an enormous body of public writing and then predicts the response that best fits the question in front of it. Because most of that writing reflects ordinary rather than exceptional practice, the model's answers gravitate toward the middle of the distribution. If you ask it for a marketing plan, it will typically return the plan it has encountered most frequently, presented clearly and confidently, but without the accumulated judgement of someone who has personally managed hundreds of campaigns.

This limitation matters most in work where small details determine the outcome. An experienced media buyer who has spent years inside advertising accounts knows which reported numbers deserve suspicion. They also know when a campaign should be left alone to stabilise, and when an apparently strong result is concealing a loss once product costs are counted. A general model has read about all of these situations. Even so, it does not carry an individual professional's tested method, and it has no reliable way to determine which of the many conflicting opinions in its training data would work for a business like yours.

General AI is therefore capable, but its capability is distributed evenly across every subject, whereas most commercial jobs demand genuine depth in one narrow area. Our earlier guide on putting a team of AI experts on one job describes the same pattern from the user's perspective, since a single conversation with one model tends to produce a single way of thinking about the problem.

What we mean by an expert layer

It helps to think of a general AI agent as a talented new employee who is intelligent, fast and willing, but who has never performed this exact job for a business like yours. The expert layer consists of everything an experienced professional would hand that new employee on the first day. It includes the questions they always ask before starting, the sequence of steps they follow, the quality checks they run before anything reaches a client, and the mistakes they have learned through experience to avoid.

On AI Blocks, that layer takes the form of a pack, which is a folder of plain text files that any AI model can read. A base pack contains the role's researched knowledge, with a source and a date recorded for each fact. It also includes a playbook, ready-made skills and prompts, and a list of the business tools the role may ask you to connect. An expert's version then adds their personal knowledge, a description of how they reason through decisions, their characteristic writing voice, and examples drawn from past work with every identifying detail removed.

Because the expert layer is written down in readable files, anyone using the agent can examine it, question its reasoning and improve it over time. A hidden prompt embedded inside a commercial application offers none of that transparency, since you cannot see why the AI behaved as it did, and you have no means of changing its instructions.

The foundation of the model is a library of base agents that we research and publish without charge. The AI Blocks Agents site currently lists 505 named agents across a wide range of business roles, and our main library contains more than 1,000 AI specialists that you can download and give to the AI you already use. Each one works inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or a coding assistant, so adopting them does not require learning a new application.

The packs are distributed under the AI Blocks Agent Pack Licence, which allows you to use and modify them free of charge, including for paid client work. You may also share free copies provided that you keep the original credit line. The licence does not permit you to sell a pack independently, because commercial sales take place through the marketplace, where experts will be compensated once paid jobs begin.

A well-designed base agent should already handle the majority of a job without any customisation, which gives every expert a strong starting point. Experts can then concentrate their effort on the contribution that only they can make, namely their own professional judgement, instead of rebuilding fundamental material from nothing. Keeping the base agents free also encourages wider adoption, and broader adoption ultimately generates more jobs for the experts who refine those agents.

How experts contribute their knowledge

Every agent on the site is accompanied by a free "Train your own" pack, which converts the user's AI into a training coach. The coach interviews the expert one question at a time, studies writing samples that the expert chooses to share, and turns their most frequently repeated workflow into a reusable skill. It then plays the role of a demanding client and runs a series of test rounds. In each round, the expert compares their agent's answer with plain AI's answer to the same brief. The expert does not know which response came from which source. Training concludes only when the expert's agent wins at least four of five difficult briefs, and our guide to training your own AI agent describes every stage in detail.

An expert who wants businesses to be able to hire their agent then applies through the expert application page. We verify the supporting links they provide for each professional claim, and once an application is approved, the expert uploads their pack in the studio. The studio runs the agent on sample briefs alongside plain AI, proposes new rules based on the edits the expert makes to its drafts, and withholds some briefs for our own independent checks. Our team then evaluates the agent with test jobs before it goes live, and while we test the marketplace, buyers can hire live agents without charge.

Throughout this process, the expert retains ownership of their method. They may use their trained agent with their own clients entirely outside AI Blocks, and they list it with us only if they want access to a wider audience of buyers. The arrangement resembles the relationship between Airbnb and its hosts, in which the host continues to own the property while the platform supplies the guests.

Assigning the right expert to each part of a job

The coordinator described in this section is not yet live. We are building it first for Meta advertising for online stores, working with a small number of pilot brands, and the examples below illustrate how it will operate once it is ready.

Most marketplaces match an entire job to a single person, but real professional work consists of distinct components, and no individual is the strongest performer on every one of them. A professional can be exceptional at building financial models while remaining only competent at explaining the story those models tell. Rather than selecting one person for the whole job, the coordinator will therefore divide the job into its components and select the most capable expert agent for each component separately.

Example: valuing a healthcare business

Consider a private equity firm that wants a valuation of a healthcare company it is considering acquiring. A single general agent would produce the financial model, the market assessment and the investment summary in one voice and at one consistent level of skill. With an expert layer in place, the coordinator will instead separate the job into distinct components and staff each one individually:

  • An agent trained by an accomplished financial modeller constructs the revenue forecast and the cash flow model.
  • An agent trained by a healthcare specialist examines the assumptions that only an industry insider would question, such as how reimbursement rules and patient volumes typically change over time.
  • An agent trained by an experienced valuation professional selects the valuation methods and the ranges that suit this particular type of transaction.
  • An agent trained by a skilled communicator transforms the analysis into a persuasive investment case that a senior partner can absorb in ten minutes.

The coordinator will then integrate these components into a single deliverable and send it to an independent reviewer before the firm receives it. Each component is therefore produced by the agent with the deepest expertise in that specific area, rather than by one agent with uniform, middling ability across all of them.

Example: managing Meta advertising for an online store

Media buying appears to be a single job, but practitioners recognise that it combines several distinct disciplines. One media buyer may be strongest at account structure, another at bidding strategy, a third at predicting which creative concepts will perform, and a fourth at increasing spend without sacrificing profitability. Most brands today rely on one individual or one agency team who excels at some of these disciplines while being noticeably weaker at others.

With an expert layer, each discipline can be assigned to the agent whose expert performs best in it, while the coordinator keeps every agent aligned with the same commercial objective for that brand. We selected this area first because the difference between average and expert work appears quickly and measurably in both sales and advertising spend. How the coordinator will divide and combine the work

The coordinator is the component of the system responsible for planning the work rather than performing it. Its first responsibility will be to understand the request, ask the small number of questions whose answers would materially change the approach, and define in writing what a completed result should look like. It will then decompose the job into components that are narrow enough for a single specialist to own completely.

Next, the coordinator will select an expert agent for each component, and it will base that choice on the specific skill required, the industry involved, the size of the business and each agent's recorded performance on comparable work. Every agent will operate in its own separate workspace with access only to the brief, the facts and the files relevant to its component. That separation prevents the agents from copying one another or converging on the same idea.

Finally, the coordinator will combine the completed components, confirm that they are consistent with one another, and pass the integrated result to a reviewer that produced none of it. Within each business tool, the client remains in control of how far an agent may act. They choose whether it may only read information, prepare drafts for the client to publish, or make live changes within limits the client defines, such as a budget ceiling and an end date. You can already plan this kind of team manually with Plan an AI team.

How we evaluate the work

An expert layer justifies its cost only if it consistently outperforms what plain AI would deliver on the same task. During training, the expert compares their agent's answers with plain AI's answers without knowing which is which, and the agent must win at least four of five comparisons. In the studio, the agent is tested again on sample briefs alongside plain AI, and our team evaluates it with test jobs before it becomes available to buyers.

We have also published a side-by-side evaluation of 80 ecommerce agents against plain AI, which includes real output excerpts and the judges' commentary. Our next step is to allow experts to contribute their own evaluation questions, which we call golden questions, together with the answers a highly skilled professional would give. Those questions will reveal quickly when an update to an underlying model causes an agent's performance to decline, and our how we test page explains our evaluation methods in more detail.

How the business generates revenue

AI Blocks operates as a two-sided marketplace, in which businesses supply jobs and experts supply agents capable of completing those jobs to a high standard. Our role is to attract buyers, run the work and verify its quality, and, once the coordinator is ready, to match each component of a job with the most suitable expert agent. App stores and freelance marketplaces perform a comparable function, since the platform supplies the customers while the creator retains the majority of each sale.

Hiring is currently free for buyers while we test the marketplace, so no money is changing hands in either direction yet. When paid jobs begin, prices will be set per job rather than charged as a monthly retainer, and larger engagements for larger businesses will cost proportionally more. Under the current draft agreement, experts will earn 70% of the net amount from jobs completed on the platform, and the first 100 approved experts will receive a founding rate of 75%. A lawyer will review that agreement before any payouts begin, and its final wording may change.

We are also committed to treating both sides of the marketplace fairly. Experts retain ownership of their methods and their examples, and we will not use their knowledge to build competing agents of our own. As the volume of jobs increases, more experts gain a reason to list their agents, and a broader selection of experts in turn attracts more businesses to the platform.

How to apply this approach in your own AI today

You do not need to wait for the automatic version in order to work this way. The three prompts below allow you to divide a job into components, brief a separate specialist for each component, and then combine and review the result, all within the AI you already use.

Prompt: Split a job into parts and name the skill each part needs

You are a coordinator. Don't do the job yourself. Plan who should do each part.

The job: [what I need, in one or two sentences]
My business: [what we sell, to whom, roughly how big we are]
What done looks like: [the file, plan or decision I want at the end]

Do this:
1. Ask me up to three questions that would change the plan, and wait for my answers before you go on. Mark anything you still have to guess as [assumed].
2. Split the job into 3 to 6 parts. Each part must be small enough for one specialist to own completely.
3. For each part, name the exact skill it needs (for example, "financial modelling", not "finance"), what the specialist needs from me, and what they hand back.
4. Say which parts depend on others, and the order to run them in.
5. Write a checklist of 5 to 8 points the joined result must pass. Each point must be something a stranger could check.

After that, assign each component to its own specialist in a separate conversation or project. You can find a specialist pack for most professional skills in AI specialists, and our guide on hiring an AI specialist explains how to brief one effectively. Add the pack's files to the conversation before you paste the next prompt.

Prompt: Brief one specialist for one part of the job

You are a specialist on a team, and you own one part of a bigger job. Other specialists are doing the other parts in other chats.

Your skill: [the exact skill, for example "bidding strategy for Meta ads"]
Your part: [paste the part from the coordinator's plan]
What you get: [paste the facts and files for this part only]
What you hand back: [paste what the coordinator said this part must produce]

Rules:
1. Before you start, read the specialist pack I've attached for this skill and follow its playbook and checks.
2. Stay inside your part. If you need something another part owns, say what you need instead of guessing.
3. Say which method you used and why it fits this business.
4. List every assumption you made, and mark any number you could not check.
5. Before you finish, check your work against the team checklist below and fix anything that fails.

Team checklist: [paste the checklist]

Once every component has been returned, combine them in a single conversation, and then ask a fresh conversation that contributed none of the work to review the integrated result.

Prompt: Join the parts and check the result

You are the checker for a job that several specialists worked on. You made none of this work.

The job: [paste the one-line job]
The parts: [paste each specialist's work, labelled Part 1, Part 2 and so on]
The checklist: [paste the checklist from the coordinator]

Do this:
1. Join the parts into one piece of work. Keep each specialist's content, and fix only gaps and clashes between parts.
2. List every place where two parts disagree, and say which one you kept and why.
3. Check the joined work against each checklist point. Mark each one pass or fail, with the line that proves it.
4. List every claim or number that no part supported with a source or a fact from me.
5. End with the three changes that would most improve the result.

Questions people ask

Is AI Blocks open source?

The base packs are free to download, inspect and modify, including for paid client work, and you may share free copies with others. They are distributed under the AI Blocks Agent Pack Licence rather than a standard open-source licence, because that licence prohibits reselling a pack outside the marketplace. The restriction exists to protect the experts who will earn income from the agents they train.

Do I need a new AI application to use the agents?

No. The packs consist of plain text files that work inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and coding assistants such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. You provide the pack to the AI you already use, and that AI performs the work according to the pack's instructions.

Can I use the coordinator today?

The automatic coordinator is not yet available. In the meantime, you can plan a team manually with Plan an AI team, or use the three prompts in this guide to divide a job, brief a specialist for each component and review the combined result. The automatic coordinator is being built first for Meta advertising for online stores.

How will you decide which expert is best suited to each part of a job?

The coordinator will consider how each agent performed against plain AI on comparable briefs, which skills and industries its expert trained it for, and how buyers rated its previous work. Because a published agent version can never be altered, every completed job can be traced back to the exact version of the agent that produced it.

What does an expert earn?

Experts earn nothing at present, because hiring remains free while we test the marketplace. When paid jobs begin, the current draft agreement gives experts 70% of the net amount from platform jobs, rising to 75% for the first 100 approved experts. The agreement is still a draft, and its terms may change before payouts begin.

Will AI Blocks absorb an expert's knowledge into its own agents?

No. Experts retain ownership of their methods, their writing voice and their examples, and we will not use that material to build competing agents of our own. Either party can terminate the agreement, and the expert's agent is removed from the marketplace within seven days.
