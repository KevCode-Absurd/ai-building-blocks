How do you write product descriptions that sell?

Good product descriptions answer the questions a buyer has before they'll pay. Start with the facts, meaning what the product is, what it's made of, its sizes, how to care for it and what's in the box. Then find the questions real buyers ask by reading your reviews and support emails, and turn each important feature into a benefit backed by proof. Finally, lay the page out so a shopper can scan it in ten seconds on a phone.

AI is genuinely useful here, as long as it writes from your facts rather than from its imagination. This guide gives you the method, a fact sheet template and a page layout. It also covers SEO basics for product pages, with prompts to write and check your product page copy.

How to write a product description, step by step

Step 1: Put the facts first

Every line of a good description rests on a fact, so collect the facts before anyone writes a word, whether that is you or your AI. If a fact isn't on the sheet, it doesn't belong on the page, because a missing fact is the gap an AI is most likely to fill with a guess.

FactWhat to write down
What it isThe plain name a buyer would search for
Who it's forThe buyer and the situation they're in
MaterialsExact materials and amounts, such as "100% cotton"
Sizes and measurementsEvery size, with real measurements and units
What's includedEverything in the box, and anything sold separately
Care and useWashing, charging, storage, setup time
ProofTests, certifications, awards and review ratings, each with its source
TermsDelivery, returns, warranty or trial period
LimitsWhat it doesn't do, or who it doesn't suit

The last row matters more than it appears to. Explaining who a product doesn't suit can prevent returns from customers it was never right for, and it also makes the rest of the page more believable.

Step 2: Find the questions buyers really ask

Buyers rarely ask whether something is "premium"; instead, they ask practical questions such as "will it fit my mattress?", "does it shrink?" or "is it hot to sleep on?". Your own records already contain these questions, in the following places.

  • Product reviews, including both your own and your competitors' reviews.
  • Support emails and chat transcripts, where customers ask questions in their own words.
  • The reasons customers give when they return items, which often reveal a missing fact.
  • Questions from sales calls, shop staff or comments on social media.

Paste a batch into your AI with the prompt below, which turns a disorganised pile of customer comments into a ranked list of questions your description must answer.

Prompt: find the questions your product page must answer

You are a customer researcher for an online shop. I want to find the questions buyers ask before buying this product.

Product: [name and link]
I am pasting customer words below: [reviews, support emails, return reasons, chat logs]

Do this:
1. List every question, doubt or worry in the customer words. Group the ones that mean the same thing.
2. Count how often each group appears, and rank them, most common first.
3. For each group, quote one short example in the customer's own words.
4. Mark which questions my current product page answers. My current page text: [paste it]
5. For each unanswered question, say which fact I need to answer it.

Rules:
- Use only what is in the text I pasted. Don't add questions you think buyers might have. List those separately, labelled "possible, not seen in the data".
- Remove names and contact details from quotes.

Output a table: question, how often, example quote, answered on page (yes, no, partly), fact needed.

Step 3: Turn features into benefits, with proof

A feature is what the product has, a benefit is what that feature means for the buyer, and proof is the reason they should believe it. Strong product page copy uses all three, so lead with the benefit, present the feature as the reason, and place the proof beside it.

FeatureBenefit (so what?)Proof
Double-wall steel bottleDrinks stay cold through a hot afternoonYour own test: hours tested, starting and ending temperature
Machine-washable coverSpills are a wash, not a ruined sofaCare label and wash temperature
Three sizesPick the one that fits your spaceMeasurement table with exact dimensions

These rows are invented examples that illustrate the structure, rather than claims about any real product. If you can't prove a benefit, reduce it to what you can honestly say, or remove it. "Keeps drinks cold for 24 hours" requires a test, whereas "double-wall steel to keep drinks cold" only requires the specification.

Step 4: Make it easy to scan

Assume the shopper is skimming on a phone, and give them a layout in which each part has a single job.

  1. Opening line: Write one or two sentences that say what it is, who it's for and the main reason to buy.
  2. Three to five key points: List short benefits, each with the feature that delivers it.
  3. Details: Give the materials, sizes, care instructions and contents, ideally in a table for specifications.
  4. Answers to top questions: Answer the two or three most common questions from step 2 in plain language.
  5. Proof: Show ratings, awards and test results, each with its source.
  6. Terms: Summarise delivery, returns and warranty in one or two lines, with a link to the full policy.

On a phone, the opening line and the first two points may be all a shopper sees before scrolling, so make sure they carry the main reason to buy.

SEO basics for product pages

SEO (search engine optimisation) means helping search engines understand your page, so that it can appear for the right searches. For product pages, the basics are straightforward and overlap heavily with good copywriting.

  • Use the words shoppers search for: Google's ecommerce guide, Where ecommerce data can appear on Google, recommends informative product descriptions that match the search terms shoppers use. Use the ordinary name buyers type, such as "linen duvet cover", rather than only your own range name.
  • Write each description yourself: Copying the manufacturer's text gives you the same page as every other shop selling that item. Your own facts, tests and answers make your page distinctive.
  • Write a clear title tag and meta description: The title tag is the page title that appears in search results. Put the product name and its main category near the start.
  • Add product structured data: Structured data is code that labels facts such as price, availability and reviews for search engines. Google's product structured data guide explains how to add it. Your shop platform or theme may add some of it already, so check before adding more.
  • Write useful image alt text: Alt text is a short written description of an image, which helps people using screen readers and helps search engines understand the picture. Describe what it shows plainly.
  • Keep facts consistent everywhere: The price, availability and specifications should match across the page, your structured data and any product feed.

Watch out. Don't stuff the keyword into every sentence, because the page should be written for the buyer. Use the main search phrase in the title, the opening line and once or twice more where it fits naturally.

How do you use an AI product description generator well?

Any AI chat tool can act as a product description generator, and the quality of the result depends mainly on what you give it. Provide your fact sheet, the buyer questions and your layout, and it writes a solid first draft; provide only a product name, and it fills the gaps with confident guesses.

Prompt: write a product description from your facts

You are an ecommerce copywriter. Write product page copy for one product, using only the facts I give you.

Ask me for anything missing from this list before you write:
- Product name, and the plain words shoppers search for
- Who it's for and the situation they're in
- My fact sheet: materials, sizes, what's included, care, proof with sources, terms, limits
- The top buyer questions, ranked
- My brand voice in three words, and two lines of copy I like
- Where it will appear: [my shop / Amazon / Google Shopping]

Write, in this order:
1. Opening line: one or two sentences. What it is, who it's for, the main reason to buy. Include the main search phrase once.
2. Three to five key points: benefit first, then the feature that delivers it. Each under 20 words.
3. Details: a short table of specs.
4. Answers to the top three buyer questions, one or two sentences each.
5. Proof: only proof from my fact sheet, with its source in brackets.
6. One line on delivery and returns.
7. A title tag under 60 characters and a meta description under 155 characters.

Rules:
- Use only facts from my fact sheet. If a point needs a fact I didn't give, write [FACT NEEDED: what] instead of guessing.
- No superlatives I can't prove: best, finest, perfect, number one.
- No health, safety or environmental claims unless they are in my fact sheet with a source.
- Plain words. Short sentences.

After the copy, list every claim you made and the fact sheet line it comes from.

Then check the draft with a second prompt in a fresh chat. A new conversation hasn't seen the drafting process, so it reads the copy without assumptions, much more like a shopper would.

Prompt: check a product description before it goes live

You are a strict product page editor. Check this product description before it goes live.

My fact sheet: [paste it]
The draft description: [paste it]
The top buyer questions: [paste them]

Check each item and answer pass or fail, with the line that shows it:
1. Every claim matches a line in the fact sheet.
2. No superlative or promise goes beyond the facts.
3. The opening line says what it is and who it's for.
4. The top three buyer questions are answered.
5. Sizes, materials and what's included are stated exactly.
6. The main search phrase appears in the opening line, and isn't overused.
7. A shopper on a phone gets the main reason to buy in the first two lines.
8. Delivery and returns are mentioned.

Then give me the fixed version, changing only the lines that failed. List each change.

A worked example: Brooklinen Classic Percale sheets

Brooklinen sells bedding, and its Classic Percale sheets are a good example of facts doing the selling. On its own pages, as of November 2026, Brooklinen describes them as 100% long-staple cotton with a 270 thread count. It says the one-over-one-under percale weave gives a crisp, matte feel, and it recommends them for people who sleep on the warmer side. Brooklinen also has a separate page explaining thread count, which answers that question on a page of its own.

What follows is an example draft written for this article, using only those public facts, so it is not Brooklinen's real copy and Brooklinen did not take part.

What goes in

  • The fact sheet lists 100% long-staple cotton, a 270 thread count, a percale weave, a crisp and matte feel, and suitability for warm sleepers.
  • The buyer questions you might find in sheet reviews include whether they feel cool, whether they get softer, what the thread count means and whether they fit a deep mattress.
  • The gaps are the fit and softening questions, which need facts that aren't in the public description we used, so prompt 2 would mark them [FACT NEEDED].

What comes out (example draft)

Crisp, matte cotton sheets for people who sleep warm. Classic Percale is woven one-over-one-under from 100% long-staple cotton, for a crisp, matte feel that suits warm sleepers.

Key points: a percale weave that feels crisp and matte. 100% long-staple cotton. A 270 thread count, and a plain guide to what that number means.

Will they fit my mattress? [FACT NEEDED: fitted sheet pocket depth]

Two things are worth noticing in this draft. First, every claim traces back to the brand's own page. Second, the draft refuses to guess the pocket depth. An inaccurate fit claim is the kind of mistake that can bring sheets back as a return.

Common mistakes, and what to do instead

Several of the mistakes below replace facts with vague language or bury the information buyers need, and the table pairs each one with a more effective approach.

MistakeDo this instead
Starting with the brand storyStart with what it is and who it's for. Put the story lower down.
Adjectives instead of facts ("luxurious", "premium")Say the material, the measurement or the test result.
Copying the manufacturer's textWrite your own from your fact sheet and buyer questions.
Giving the AI only the product nameGive it a full fact sheet and tell it to mark missing facts.
One long paragraphUse an opening line, short bullets, a spec table and answers.
Ignoring the questions in your reviewsAnswer the top three on the page, in plain words.
Hiding sizes and what's includedState them exactly, in a table, above the reviews.
Writing for search engines firstWrite for the buyer. Use the search phrase where it fits naturally.

Questions people ask

How long should a product description be?

A product description should be long enough to answer the buyer's main questions. A simple, familiar item might need 50 to 100 words plus a specification table, whereas an expensive or technical one needs considerably more. Remove anything that doesn't answer a question or support a claim.

Can I use AI to write product descriptions?

Yes, provided you give it real facts and check every claim it makes, although platforms may have their own rules. As of November 2026, Google's guidance on generative AI content says Merchant Center requires AI-written titles and descriptions to be labelled as AI-generated. Check the current rules for each place you sell.

What is the best AI product description generator?

The assistant you already use is usually enough, because ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all write well from a good brief. The brief matters more than the tool, which means a fact sheet, buyer questions, a layout and clear rules.

Should every product have a unique description?

Yes, wherever you can manage it, because copied text gives searchers and buyers no reason to choose your page. For nearly identical items, such as one product in six colours, a single shared description that names the colour is perfectly acceptable.

How do I write product descriptions for SEO?

Use the ordinary words shoppers search for in the title and opening line, and answer the questions they actually ask. Add structured data for price and availability, write alt text for every image, and let accurate, factual copy do the rest.
