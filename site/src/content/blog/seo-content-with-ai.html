Can AI SEO content rank on Google?

Yes, AI SEO content can rank, provided it is genuinely useful, because Google judges content by its quality rather than by whether a person or a machine wrote it. What Google treats as spam is content made mainly to manipulate search rankings, such as large numbers of thin pages produced in bulk.

The way to use AI for SEO, therefore, is to let it do the heavy lifting on research, outlines and first drafts. You then add what only you know, such as your experience, tests, customer questions and opinions, before editing thoroughly. This guide shows you how, step by step, with prompts you can copy and a check to run before you publish.

What does Google say about AI-generated content?

Google has addressed this directly, and here is what its own pages say, as of November 2026.

  • Quality matters, not the method: in its post on Google Search and AI-generated content (February 2023), Google says it focuses on the quality of content rather than how it is produced.
  • Automation to manipulate rankings is spam: the same post says that using automation, including AI, to create content mainly to manipulate search rankings violates its spam policies.
  • Bulk pages are a risk: Google's page on using generative AI content warns that mass-producing AI pages without adding value for users may break its rules on scaled content abuse.
  • Check every fact: that page also says AI can produce inaccurate content. It advises you to fact-check and review AI content before publishing, including titles, meta descriptions and image alt text.
  • Experience counts: Google's guide to creating helpful, reliable content asks whether your content demonstrates first-hand expertise. It also flags "mainly summarizing what others have to say without adding much value" as a warning sign.
  • Tell readers when it helps them: Google suggests explaining how content was made wherever readers would reasonably expect it, for example when automation played a significant part.

Put simply, Google allows AI content and acts against junk. An AI draft that you shape with real knowledge is acceptable, whereas a thousand AI pages repeating what every other page already says are not.

How to use AI for SEO content, step by step

Step 1: Pick one search and work out its intent

Search intent is what the searcher actually wants when they type a query, and the page that ranks is usually the one that delivers it in the format they expect. Before you write anything, search the phrase yourself and study what already ranks.

IntentThe searcher wantsExample searchPage that fits
LearnAn answer or a how-to"how to fill in thin eyebrows"Guide with steps
CompareHelp choosing"brow gel vs brow pencil"Comparison with a clear verdict
BuyA product to buy now"clear brow gel"Product or category page
FindA specific site or place"glossier boy brow"The brand's own product page

If the top results are all product pages, a blog post probably won't rank for that search. In that case, write your blog post for a related "learn" search and link it to the product.

Step 2: Collect the real questions

Good outlines come from questions people genuinely ask, rather than from what an AI guesses they might ask. Collect them from the following sources, each of which shows the words real people use.

  • The "People also ask" box and the related searches on Google's results page.
  • Your support emails, chat conversations and sales calls, where customers describe problems in their own words.
  • Reviews of your products and of your competitors' products.
  • Forums and comment threads where your potential buyers discuss the topic.
  • The Search Console Performance report, which shows the queries that already bring people to your site.

Step 3: Build the outline from those questions

This is where your AI becomes most valuable. Give it the search, the intent, your collected questions and the pages that rank today. Then ask for an outline that answers the main question first and covers what the top pages miss.

Prompt: build an SEO outline from real questions

You are an SEO content planner. Help me plan one article that deserves to rank.

The search phrase: [phrase]
Who searches it, and why: [one or two lines]
My business and what I sell: [one line]
Real questions people ask (from People also ask, support emails, reviews, forums): [paste them]
The top 3 to 5 pages that rank today: [paste their headings, or their links if you can read them]

Do this:
1. Say what the searcher most wants, in one sentence. Say whether a guide, a comparison or a product page fits best.
2. List what the ranking pages all cover, and what they miss or get thin.
3. Write an outline:
   - A short opening that answers the main question in two or three sentences
   - 6 to 9 headings, written as the questions people ask where natural
   - Under each heading: what to cover, and which of my real questions it answers
4. Mark each place where first-hand experience would help, with "[ADD EXPERIENCE: what kind]".
5. Suggest 3 pages on my site to link to, if I give you my page list: [paste page list, or say "none"].

Rules:
- Don't invent statistics, studies or quotes. If a point needs a source, write [SOURCE NEEDED].
- Don't pad. If a heading wouldn't help the searcher, leave it out.

Output: the outline as a numbered list, then the gaps list.

Step 4: Add the experience only you have

This is the step that makes your page worth ranking. An AI can summarise what is already published online, but it can't know what happened when you tried something, what your customers said or what you would change. That knowledge is your advantage.

For each "[ADD EXPERIENCE]" marker in the outline, write a few rough notes, and bullet points are perfectly adequate. Here are some ideas for what to include.

  • A test you ran, describing what you did and what you observed.
  • A photo you took yourself, showing a real result or a real mistake.
  • A question a customer asked, and how you answered it.
  • A mistake you made, and what you learned from it.
  • Your honest opinion, including situations where your product isn't the right choice.
  • Numbers from your own business, if you're comfortable sharing them publicly.

Step 5: Draft from your outline and notes

Give your AI the outline, your notes and your facts, and instruct it to write only from that material, in your voice. Ask it to flag anything it had to assume, so you can verify those points before publishing.

Prompt: draft an SEO article from your outline and notes

You are writing an article for my website. Write it only from the material I give you.

The search phrase: [phrase]
The outline: [paste the outline]
My first-hand notes: [paste your notes, tests, customer questions, opinions]
Facts and sources I can use: [paste them, with links]
My voice: [three words, and a paragraph I wrote that sounds like me]
Reader: [who they are, and what they already know]

Write the article:
- Answer the main question in the first two or three sentences.
- Use the outline headings. Keep paragraphs to three or four sentences.
- Use the search phrase in the first 100 words and in one heading. Elsewhere, only where it reads naturally.
- Put my first-hand notes where the outline marks [ADD EXPERIENCE]. Keep my words where you can.
- Use plain words and short sentences.

Rules:
- Never invent a statistic, study, quote, customer story or result. If a point needs one I didn't give, write [NEEDS SOURCE] and move on.
- Don't use filler openings or closing summaries.
- Don't claim personal experience I didn't give you.

After the article, list:
1. Every fact you used and where it came from.
2. Every [NEEDS SOURCE] mark.
3. Any sentence where you had to assume something.

Step 6: Edit like a reader, not a writer

Read the draft as the person who searched, and remove every paragraph that doesn't help them. Check each fact against its source, then read the whole piece aloud. AI drafts tend to repeat themselves and to sound similar from section to section, and reading aloud exposes that quickly. The checklist below covers the complete edit.

Step 7: Add internal links

Internal links are links from one page on your site to another, and they help readers find the next useful page while helping search engines understand how your pages relate. Link from the new article to your related guides and products. Use anchor text (the clickable words) that tells the reader what they will find, then return to two or three older pages and link them to the new one.

Step 8: Publish, watch, and update

Give a new page a few weeks before you judge its performance, and then check which searches it appears for in Search Console. If it appears for questions it doesn't answer well, add a section that answers them, and if facts become outdated, update them and change the date on the page.

What should you avoid with AI SEO content?

The biggest risk is the one Google names explicitly, which is scaled content abuse: publishing many pages made mainly to rank, without adding value. With AI, it is easy to drift into this by accident, so steer clear of the following patterns.

  • One page per keyword variation: "best running socks for men", "best men's running socks" and "top running socks men" can all be served by one good page.
  • Location pages with only the town name changed: if the service and the text are identical, the extra pages add nothing for readers.
  • Rewritten copies of the pages that rank: summarising the top results without adding anything new is one of the warning signs Google lists.
  • Publishing AI drafts unchecked: Google's own guidance says to fact-check and review AI content before it goes live.
  • Writing to a word count: Google's helpful content guide says it has no preferred word count, so the right length is whatever fully answers the question.
  • Fake experience: never let the AI write "we tested" or "our customers say" unless you really did and they really did.

A worked example: Glossier and Into The Gloss

Glossier is a useful brand to learn from here, because it grew out of content. According to Into The Gloss's about page, Emily Weiss founded the site in 2010 as a beauty site about the routines of inspiring women. The team behind it launched Glossier in 2014, and the site is now Glossier's editorial arm, so content built on real people's actual routines is first-hand experience at its core.

What follows is an example plan written for this article, which means it is not Glossier's real content plan, and Glossier did not take part.

What goes in

  • The search is a "learn" query: "how to make thin eyebrows look fuller".
  • The related product is Boy Brow, which Glossier describes as a creamy, brushable brow wax for shaping and grooming brows.
  • The real questions you would collect include whether brow gel makes brows look thicker, whether gel or pencil suits sparse brows, and how to stop brows looking stiff.
  • The first-hand material that only the brand has includes staff and reader routines, before-and-after photos taken with permission, and the questions its customer team hears.

What comes out

Prompt 1 would return an outline that answers the main question in the opening. Its headings would follow the real questions: "Does brow gel make brows look thicker?", "Gel or pencil: which suits sparse brows?" and "How do you stop brows looking stiff?". It would mark [ADD EXPERIENCE] wherever a routine, a photo or a customer question fits. It would also note that the article should link to the product page, and that it should say plainly when a pencil suits a reader better than a gel.

That last point matters, because an honest "this isn't for you if..." builds trust with readers. It is also exactly the kind of judgement that a summary of other pages can't provide.

The check to run before you publish

  • The opening answers the searcher's main question within two or three sentences.
  • The page type matches what ranks for the search, whether that is a guide, a comparison or a product page.
  • Every heading answers a question that real people actually ask.
  • The page includes something only you could contribute, such as a test, a photo, a customer question or an opinion.
  • Every fact has been checked against a source, and outside facts are linked.
  • The page contains no invented statistics, quotations, studies or customer stories.
  • No sentence says "we tested" or "our customers say" unless it is literally true.
  • The search phrase appears in the title, the first 100 words and one heading, without being stuffed elsewhere.
  • The title tag and meta description are accurate and written for people rather than for search engines.
  • Every image has plain alt text that describes what it shows.
  • The page links to two to five related pages on your site, and at least two older pages link back to it.
  • The article reads smoothly aloud, without repeated filler phrases.
  • You have scheduled a date to review the page and update its facts.

Questions people ask

Does Google penalise AI-generated content?

Not simply for being AI-made, because Google says it rewards quality however content is produced. It does take action against content created mainly to manipulate rankings, including large numbers of AI-produced pages that add no value.

Should I say my content was written with AI?

Google suggests explaining how content was made wherever readers would reasonably expect it, such as when automation did much of the work. A short note describing how you created and checked the page is usually enough.

Can AI blog posts rank on Google?

Yes, when they answer the search well and add something new. The posts that struggle are the ones that only restate what already ranks, so add your experience, verify your facts and edit thoroughly.

Which AI is best for SEO writing?

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can all plan and draft effectively, and the material you provide matters more than the tool. A clear outline, real questions and your own notes will outperform a clever prompt on a blank page.

How long should an SEO article be?

An SEO article should be as long as it takes to answer the question properly, since Google's helpful content guide says it has no preferred word count. Look at what ranks, then cover the question more usefully rather than simply at greater length.

How many AI articles can I publish a week?

There is no safe number to aim for, because what matters is whether each page adds real value. Publish only as many articles as you can properly research, enrich with experience and check.
