What is an AI Blocks prompt?

A prompt is a single message you paste into a chat with your AI to get one result back, such as ten headlines, a reply to an angry customer or a tidy set of meeting minutes. The prompts on AI Blocks are written for particular jobs, so almost every one already tells your AI what to do, what to hand back and what to check before it answers.

A prompt is the quickest thing in the library to try, because you only need to copy it, add your details and send it. That speed is also its limit, since a prompt does one job in one message and you paste it in again every time. When you find yourself repeating the same prompt every week, it is usually worth turning it into a skill, and this guide explains how.

The best results come from treating your AI like a small team rather than one assistant. You ask for real variety instead of twenty copies of one idea, and you have the answer checked by something that didn't write it.

Here is a summary of this guide, if you're short of time:

  • Pick a prompt for one specific job, and read the "Is it for you?" part of its page before you copy it.
  • Replace every placeholder with real details, and give your AI the facts it needs through your brand file or the "Fill it in" box.
  • Improve the answer in the same conversation with specific feedback, changing one thing at a time.
  • When you want options, decide the mix first and ask for versions that differ in kind, not only in wording.
  • Save the prompts that work, and turn any prompt you repeat regularly into a reusable skill.

The method, step by step

  1. Describe the job in one sentence, including what you'll provide, what you want back and who it is for.
  2. Find a prompt that fits on the Prompts tab of the library, and check its "Use it when" and "Skip it when" lists.
  3. Fill in every detail it asks for, using the "Fill it in" box, or by adding them underneath the prompt and replacing any parts in [square brackets].
  4. Add your context, such as your brand file, prices, audience and any rules, and ask your AI to write MISSING for anything absent.
  5. Send it and read the answer carefully against the checks listed on the prompt's page, before you use any of it.
  6. Improve it in the same conversation by explaining precisely what to change, one round at a time.
  7. Keep what works by saving the prompt, and turn it into a skill once you're using it every week or more.

Prompt or skill: which one do you need?

Prompts and skills on AI Blocks are close relatives, since both are instructions written for one particular job. The difference is how much information they carry and how often you'll use them. How to find a prompt and read its page

Open the library on the home page, choose the Prompts tab, and describe your job in the search box using everyday words, such as "refund reply" or "price rise letter". Open two or three results and compare them, because prompts with similar names often take quite different approaches to the same problem.

Each prompt page tells you what you give and what you get, and the "Is it for you?" lists show when to use the prompt and when to choose something else. Under "Other ways to use it", the Copy prompt button copies the full text so you can paste it into any chat. The Download prompt button at the top gives you the file instead, with upload steps for Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT and Gemini, which is useful if you want to keep it inside your AI.

How to fill in a prompt and give it context

A prompt can only work with the information you give it. When a detail is missing, your AI fills the gap with a safe, generic guess, which is why so many answers sound as if they were written for nobody in particular. The fix is nearly always more specific input rather than a cleverer prompt.

Many prompt pages have a "Fill it in" box, where you type your details into labelled spaces. When you choose Copy the request, you get the prompt and your details together in one message, ready to paste into your AI. Your draft stays on your device, and copying it doesn't send it anywhere. If you copy a prompt on its own instead, paste your details underneath it, and replace any parts in [square brackets] with real information before you send it.

Context is the information your AI needs about you and your business, and you shouldn't have to type it out every time. Set up your brand file from the site menu by answering five quick questions, and the prompts you copy afterwards can include it. For yourself as a person, a me.md file holds your work, your projects and the way you write, and the Ask your AI what it knows about you prompt helps you make one. Our guide to giving AI context about your business explains what to include.

How to improve the answer in a few rounds

Treat the first answer as a draft that still needs your judgement. Read it against the checks on the prompt's page, then ask for changes in the same chat, where your AI can still see the prompt and your details. Vague feedback such as "make it better" usually produces a different answer rather than a better one, so say exactly what to keep and what to change.

Change one thing per round wherever possible. If you ask for a shorter answer, a warmer tone and a new opening all at once, you won't know which request improved it. When a prompt keeps missing the point after two or three rounds, the request itself may be unclear, and the rough prompt rebuilder can rewrite it with a clear role, context, task, format and limits.

Prompt: Give clear feedback on a draft

Thanks. Before you change anything, here is my feedback on that answer.

Keep: [the parts that work, quoted or described]
Change: [one specific thing, for example "the opening is too formal for regular customers"]
Why: [what the reader needs that this part does not give them]
Facts to add or correct: [any fact that was wrong or missing]

Rules:
1. Change only what I listed. Leave everything else exactly as it was.
2. If my feedback conflicts with an earlier instruction, tell me before you choose.
3. Show the new version, then list in one line each what you changed.

How to ask for several distinct versions

Asking for "ten versions" often gives you ten variations of one idea, all built from the same method with slightly different wording. That happens because the AI picks one approach and repeats it. Real variety means different kinds of result, such as a story, a question or a list of numbers, not the same sentence rearranged.

Before you ask, decide exactly what you want. Do you want one kind of result, a mix, a wide spread, or would you prefer the AI to decide? If you only want one kind, say which one. If you have no preference, a sensible default is roughly half proven ideas that already work for you, three in ten new ones and one in five as a deliberate wild card.

Some prompts in the library are built this way. The video ad script angle chooser picks four different kinds of video script that open differently and sell on different reasons, rather than four copies of one script. The video opening line batch writer gives you ten different openings and suggests the two to film first.

Prompt: Ask for versions that really differ

I want [number] versions of [the thing you want, for example a welcome email for new customers].

About my business: [one or two lines, or my brand file below]
Who it is for: [the reader]
What has worked before: [past examples or results, or "nothing yet"]
The mix I want: [one kind / a mix / a wide spread / you decide]
If one kind, which one: [for example "short stories from customers"]

Do this:
1. First list the different kinds of [thing] that exist for this job, in one line each.
2. Choose kinds to match the mix. If I said "you decide", use about 50% proven, 30% new and 20% wild card, and label each version with its group.
3. Write one version per kind. Each one must differ in approach, not only in wording.
4. Finish with a sameness check: name any two versions that are too alike, and replace one of them.

Use only the facts I gave. Write MISSING for anything you need.

For even more variety, run each kind of version in its own separate chat, so one answer can't influence the next, then paste all of them into a fresh chat and ask which two are too similar. Our guide to getting real variety from AI takes this further.

How to save the prompts that work

When a prompt gives you a result you actually use, keep it somewhere you'll find it again. The Save button on any prompt page keeps it in My work, saving it in your browser at first, and once you sign in it keeps a copy in your account, so you can reach it on any device.

Save your completed version as well as the original, by keeping a simple document with the prompt, the details you added, and one line about what you changed to get a good answer. Next time you won't need to rediscover the feedback that worked. If you work in a project in ChatGPT or Claude, you can also paste a prompt you use there into the project's instructions, so chats in that project can follow it.

How to turn a prompt you repeat into a skill

If you paste the same prompt and the same background every week, you are doing work that a skill could do for you. A skill saves the instructions in a SKILL.md file, so your AI follows them whenever you ask for the skill by name, and it can hold more steps and checks than a prompt usually does.

The library includes prompts that help you make the change. The repeat job skill drafter turns a job you repeat into a draft skill file with a line describing when to use it and some test prompts. The skill file problem spotter checks a SKILL.md for weak lines before you rely on it or share it. For more careful testing, the prompt and skill tuner agent tests a prompt or skill against your own examples, changes one thing at a time and keeps only the changes that help.

Prompt: Turn my repeated prompt into a skill

I use the prompt below about [how often] for [the job]. Help me turn it into a reusable skill file called SKILL.md.

My prompt:
[paste the prompt you keep using]

What I usually add each time: [the background, facts or files I paste in]
What a good result looks like: [two or three signs it worked]
What has gone wrong before: [any repeated mistakes]

Do this:
1. Write a one-line description saying when the skill should be used.
2. Turn the prompt into numbered steps, and move the background I always add into a "What you need" list.
3. Add a short list of checks the AI must run before it answers, including my "gone wrong" items.
4. Add three test requests I can try, each with what a pass looks like.

Keep my facts and wording where they work. Do not invent rules I did not give you.

Once you have the file, add it to your AI the same way as any downloaded skill, and test it on two or three real jobs before you stop checking the results.

Questions people ask

Are AI Blocks prompts free?

Yes, every prompt is free to copy or download, and because your own AI does the work, it runs on whatever AI plan you already have.

Which AI can I use the prompts with?

A prompt is ordinary text, so it works in almost any chat AI, including Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini, without any installation. The How it works page has the full instructions for each app.

What do the square brackets in a prompt mean?

Some prompts use square brackets to mark a gap for your own details, such as [your product] or [who it is for]. Replace each one with real information before you send the prompt, or use the "Fill it in" box on the page instead.

Why does my AI give a different answer each time?

Chat AIs vary their wording from one answer to the next, so some difference is normal. If the answers differ in quality, give the prompt more specific facts and a clear format, and keep the version that works.

When should I use a skill instead of a prompt?

Use a skill when you do the same job regularly, or when the job needs several separate steps and checks. A prompt is better for a quick result, or for experimenting with an idea before you commit to it. You can browse all the guides for more ways to use AI Blocks.
