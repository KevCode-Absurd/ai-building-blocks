What is an AI Blocks skill?

A skill is a set of instructions saved in a plain text file called SKILL.md. It tells your AI how to do one particular job, step by step, and how to check its own work before it hands anything back. You add it to Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or a coding app once, then call on it whenever the same job comes round again.

AI Blocks is a free library of about 5,000 skills, workflows and prompts. A prompt is a single message for a quick result, while a skill holds the steps, rules and checks, so you don't have to type them out every time. Our guide to prompts, skills and workflows explains the difference in more detail.

The best results come when you treat your AI less like one assistant and more like a small team of experts. One skill makes the work, a different skill checks it, and you stay responsible for everything that goes out.

Here is the short version of this guide, if you're in a hurry:

  • Search the library for the job you want done, then read the "Is it for you?" part of the page before you download anything.
  • Download the skill and follow the upload steps for your AI, or copy it into a new chat for a quick first try.
  • Give the skill your real information, through your org context or the "Fill it in" box, and tell it to mark gaps instead of guessing.
  • Check the result with a second skill in a separate chat, so the work is judged by something that didn't make it.

The method, step by step

Here is the whole method in seven steps, and the sections below explain each one in more detail.

  1. Describe the job in one sentence, saying what you'll provide, what you want back and who it is for. "Turn my rough product notes into three shop descriptions for first-time buyers" is a job, whereas "help with marketing" is not.
  2. Find two or three skills that fit, then compare their pages, because skills with similar names often use quite different methods.
  3. Read the page carefully to check who it is intended for, what information you need to bring and what you'll receive.
  4. Add it to your AI by downloading it and following the instructions for your app, or copy it into a chat if you only need it once.
  5. Give it your information, including your org context, your notes and any numbers, and ask it to write MISSING wherever a fact is absent.
  6. Check the result with a different skill, running it in a separate chat on the draft and your facts, and nothing else.
  7. Record what happened: fix whatever the check found, keep the version you actually use, and say on the page whether the skill worked for you.

How to find the right skill

Start in the library on the home page. Choose the Skills tab, then describe the job in the search box using your own everyday words, such as "product descriptions" or "meeting notes". The search reads the full instructions as well as the names, so ordinary words usually work better than technical terms.

Don't stop at the first result, but open two or three skills that look similar and compare them, because skills with similar names can work in very different ways. One email skill might write from your customers' own words, while another might cut your existing draft down to a single clear request. Choosing the method that matches your problem matters more than choosing the most impressive name.

The "Skip it when" list on a page often points you towards a better fit for your situation. If you'd rather hire a whole specialist than use a single skill, the Specialists tab takes you to Hire an AI specialist, where each role arrives with its own skills, checks and limits.

How to read a skill page before you use it

A skill page is designed to answer three questions quickly: is this for me, what do I need to bring, and what will I get back? Read those parts first, since the complete detail sits further down, under Details, for when you want to see exactly how the skill works.

The main button on every skill page is Download skill, and directly underneath it are the upload steps for each AI. The table below summarises what the page says today. Menu names in these apps change often, so if a step doesn't match what you see, follow your AI's own help pages instead.

How to give a skill your information

A skill knows the method, but it doesn't know anything about your business. Without your information, even an excellent skill fills the gaps with safe, generic guesses. Most disappointing results come from a missing price, a vague description of the buyer or an out-of-date offer, rather than from a weak skill.

There are three ways to give a skill what it needs. First, set up your org context from the site menu, where you answer five quick questions so that everything you copy and download afterwards fits your business. Second, write a me.md, which is a short private file about you, your projects and the way you write. Third, use the "Fill it in" box on a skill page, where you paste your details and then Copy the request gives you the skill and your details in one message. Your draft stays on your device, and copying it doesn't send it anywhere.

Whatever information you provide, ask the skill to mark gaps rather than invent facts. Many AI Blocks skills already write MISSING when something is absent, and you can add the same rule yourself, as this prompt does.

Prompt: Run a skill with my facts

Use the [skill name] skill for this job.

The job: [what I give you, what I want back, and who it is for]
My facts:
[paste prices, product details, offers with their end dates, and any rules]
My audience: [who will read or use the result]
Where it will be used: [shop page, email, ad, internal note]

Rules:
1. Use only the facts above. Where a fact is missing, write MISSING and carry on.
2. Before you start, list any question whose answer would change the result. Ask at most three.
3. Follow the skill's own steps and checks. Tell me which checks passed and which could not run.

Give me the result first, then the list of checks, then anything marked MISSING.

How to check the work with a second skill

A skill checks its own work, and that certainly helps, but a maker reviewing its own draft naturally tends to approve of what it made. You get a far more honest review when the work is checked by something that didn't write it, in a separate chat that hasn't seen the reasoning behind it.

The library has hundreds of check skills built for exactly this purpose. The claim evidence checker matches each claim in your copy to your proof, and the anti-AI-slop copy editor finds lines that sound machine-written. The brand voice editor fixes off-brand words while leaving your claims alone, and for sales copy, the sales copy readiness checker gives you a ship, fix or rewrite decision.

Open a separate chat for the check, so the checker can't see how the maker reached its answer, and paste in only the draft and the original facts. Then take the findings back to the first chat, or make the fixes yourself. If there's no matching check skill for your particular job, this prompt does a simpler version of the same thing.

Prompt: Check a draft you didn't write

You are reviewing a draft that someone else wrote. You did not write it, so judge it as a strict editor would.

The job it was meant to do: [one sentence]
Who it is for: [the reader]
The facts it must rest on:
[paste the same facts the maker had]

The draft:
[paste the draft]

Check it against this list and mark each line PASS or FAIL, with the reason:
1. Every fact in the draft appears in the facts above.
2. It does the job in the sentence above, and nothing else.
3. The reader would understand it on one read.
4. Nothing in it promises more than the facts support.
5. [one check that matters for this job]

Then list the three most important fixes, in order. Do not rewrite the whole draft.

Here is an imaginary example to illustrate the idea. A small bakery asks a description skill to write about three loaves, using the owner's notes, prices and allergy information. In a separate chat, a claim checker then flags one line that calls a loaf "gluten free", which her notes never said. The check takes a few minutes and catches the one mistake that could have harmed a customer.

How to combine several skills on one job

Bigger jobs usually need more than one method. Research, writing and checking are different skills, and a skill designed for one of them generally does it better than a single skill that attempts all three. Think of each skill as an expert with its own way of working, and arrange them so that each one hands its result to the next.

Many skill pages include a "Before and after this skill" section, which shows what to run before the skill and what to run with its result, and lets you download your choices together in that order. For a job you'll repeat, you can build a workflow of your own or plan an AI team of specialists, and our guide to putting a team of AI experts on one job goes further.

When you want several options rather than one answer, decide on the mix before you start. Do you want one kind of result, a mix, a wide spread, or should the AI decide for you? If you have no preference, a sensible default is roughly half proven ideas, three in ten new ones and one in five as a deliberate wild card. Run one maker skill for each kind of result, each in its own chat, so they don't copy each other. Our guide to getting real variety from AI explains how this works in practice.

Let your AI find skills itself through MCP

If you use one AI for most of your work, you can connect it to AI Blocks once. MCP is a standard way for an AI to use outside tools, and our guide to what MCP is explains it in plain words. After you connect your AI, it can search the library, read a skill and use it for your job, without you downloading anything.

The setup page has instructions for Claude, ChatGPT, Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. Your plan must allow custom connectors, although the public library needs no sign-in. Once you're connected, you can ask for a job in your own words, for example "Use AI Blocks to find a skill that writes product descriptions, then use it on these three products." Every skill page also offers a line to send, under "Pull it from your AI", which asks your AI to fetch that exact skill.

A connected AI can also chain skills together on its own, so you can ask it to find a maker skill and a separate check skill, run the maker first, then run the check on the result. You still read the final work yourself before it goes anywhere.

Questions people ask

Are AI Blocks skills free?

Yes, you can download or copy any skill for free, and because your own AI does the work, it runs on whatever AI plan you already have.

Which AI apps can use the skills?

Each skill page has upload instructions for Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT and Gemini, and the How it works page adds instructions for Codex. You can also copy a skill into almost any chat for a single job.

What's the difference between copying and downloading a skill?

Copying gives you the main instructions to paste into a chat, which is quick for a single job. Downloading gives you a ZIP file containing SKILL.md and any supporting files, which you add to your AI once and then reuse.

Can I change a skill to fit my business?

Yes, and because the skill file is plain text, you can read it and edit it yourself. Under More options, every page also offers "Get help adapting this task" if you'd like assistance.

Do I need to connect my AI over MCP?

No, because downloading or copying works perfectly well on its own. Connecting is mainly useful if you rely on one AI for most of your work, since it can then find and fetch the right skill automatically. You can browse all the guides for more ways to use the site.
