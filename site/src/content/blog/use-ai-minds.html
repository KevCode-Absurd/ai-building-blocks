What an AI mind is, and what it isn't

An AI mind is a way of thinking that you can borrow for a particular problem. Each one is built from a well-known person's public work, such as their books, letters, talks and interviews, and it teaches your AI to approach a problem the way that person did. You bring a real problem from your business, and the mind applies its method, asks its usual questions and pushes back wherever that person would.

A mind is never the actual person. Charlie Munger's mind does not speak for Charlie Munger, and nobody behind any mind has endorsed what it tells you. It is an AI interpretation of public work, with its sources listed so you can check them yourself. Treat it like a sharp adviser who has studied someone's methods carefully, rather than a recording of the original.

AI Blocks has 505 minds across ten areas, from business, sales and marketing to design, writing, science and thinking. The most valuable habit is to treat your AI like a team of experts instead of one assistant, and minds make that easy, because each one brings a genuinely different method to the same problem.

Here is the short version of this guide:

  • Use a mind for its method and its questions, not for facts about the person or quotations from them.
  • Pick minds that would genuinely disagree about your question, so you hear more than one point of view.
  • Use a mind as a reviewer of work it didn't make, such as a draft, a business plan or a web page.
  • Seat three to seven minds on a council for a big decision, using the Advisory council skill.
  • Pair a mind with a specialist, so the specialist does the job and the mind questions it.
  • Stay honest, and never publish a mind's words as if the real person had said them.

How to use one mind, step by step

1. Start from your problem, not the famous name

Write down the decision or the piece of work in one or two sentences, and describe what a good outcome would look like. A mind is only ever as useful as the problem you give it to work on.

2. Find a mind whose method fits

Go to Minds and search by name or topic, or filter by role, such as founder, investor, marketer, seller or designer. Each card shows the mind's suggested seat and one example job to try. Open the profile, then read the list of jobs you can hire that mind for.

3. Download it or copy it into your AI

Use the main button to download the mind, or download it together with any skills you picked from their work. Then add the files to your AI, or put them in a project if your AI offers projects, so that every chat in that project can use the mind. If you only want the mind's instructions, open "Other ways to use it" on the page, copy the mind and paste it into a new conversation.

4. Give it your real facts and ask for its method

Minds argue far more usefully from your actual numbers, your draft or your org context than from guesses. Tell the mind everything relevant that you know, and ask it to mark any missing information instead of inventing it.

Prompt: Hire one mind for one job

Use the mind file I've attached for [mind's name]. Work on my problem the way their playbook says. Don't claim to be them.

My problem: [the decision or the work, in one or two sentences]
A good outcome would be: [what success looks like]
My facts: [numbers, the draft, the plan, or attach your org context]
Limits: [money, time, anything you won't do]

Do this:
1. Ask me the first questions [mind's name]'s method would ask. Wait for my answers.
2. Apply the method step by step, and name each idea you use.
3. Push back where their method says I'm wrong, and say why.
4. Give me what the method hands back, such as a checklist, a critique or a decision.

Write MISSING for any fact you need and don't have. Mark anything about the person that isn't in the file as [beyond the record].

Pick minds that disagree with each other

One mind gives you one view, and a single view can be confidently wrong. The real value appears when you put two or three minds side by side that would honestly disagree with each other. Choose one who pushes for action, one who looks for what could go wrong, and one who speaks for the customer.

Use a mind as a reviewer of finished work

One of the simplest and most valuable jobs for a mind is checking work that it didn't make. Whoever created a draft is usually the worst judge of it, and that is just as true of an AI as of a person. Open a fresh chat, add a mind, paste in the work and ask for a review from that mind's seat.

Different minds notice different problems, so the choice of reviewer matters. A usability mind such as Don Norman asks whether a visitor can see what to do on your page. An advertising mind such as David Ogilvy asks what the big idea is and whether it will sell. Running the same draft past both of them gives you two separate lists of improvements.

Prompt: A mind reviews my draft

You did not make the work below. Review it from the seat of [mind's name], using the attached mind file. Don't claim to be them.

What the work is for: [the goal and who it is for]
The work: [paste the draft, plan or page]

Do this:
1. Ask the question [mind's name] always brings to this seat, and answer it for this work.
2. List the three biggest problems their method would see, most serious first. For each, quote the exact part of the work and say why it is a problem.
3. Suggest one fix for each problem. Keep my facts and voice.
4. Say what is already working and should stay.
5. Give a verdict: ready, fix first, or start again.

Only use facts from the work and from me. Write MISSING if you need more.

Seat several minds on a council for a big decision

When the choice is real, with two or more good options and a genuine cost to getting it wrong, a council is worth the extra effort. On the Minds page, choose "Seat on council" for up to seven minds, then open the council in the builder. The builder adds the Advisory council skill after the minds, and you can download everything as a single workflow for your AI. You can also start from one mind: on its page, open "More options" and choose "Start a council".

The Advisory council skill runs the rounds of the discussion for you. Each member gives an opening view, the members challenge each other, and they rank the opening answers without knowing whose answer is whose. You then receive a short brief with the recommended decision, the strongest dissent, what would change the call and the first three steps.

Our guide on how to run an AI council before a big decision explains every round in detail, including practical ways to stop everyone simply agreeing.

Pair minds with specialists

Minds and specialists do different kinds of work, and they are most effective together. A specialist from Hire an AI specialist is set up to complete a job, with its methods, checks and permissions already written in. A mind brings a way of thinking that questions how that job was done. So let the specialist produce the work, and then let a mind review it from its seat.

Two files are attached: a specialist ([specialist's name]) and a mind ([mind's name]). Keep their jobs separate.

My facts: [paste them, or attach your org context]

Round 1, the specialist: do the job using its own method and checks. Show the work.

Finish with the revised work, a list of what changed and why, and anything still MISSING.

Limits, and how to stay honest

Minds are useful because they bring a clear method, but they also have real limits that you should remember every time you use one.

  • A mind is an AI interpretation of public work, and it can be mistaken about how the person really thought, so check the sources listed on the profile whenever a point matters.
  • Never publish a mind's words as a quotation from the real person, and never suggest in your marketing that they recommend your business, because they don't.
  • Living people receive extra care, so minds avoid claims about their private lives, their health or their opinions on current events.
  • Minds offer frameworks rather than personal advice. An investor's mind explains how they would think about a decision, and you should still consult a professional where money, law or health is involved.
  • A famous name doesn't make an answer correct, so judge the reasoning itself, and let the blind ranking on a council do its job.

Questions people ask

Is an AI mind the real person?

No. A mind is an AI that applies a person's way of thinking, built entirely from their public work. It isn't the person, it doesn't speak for them, and they haven't endorsed it. Every mind file says so clearly, and lists its sources with a last-checked date.

Which mind should I start with?

Begin with your problem rather than a favourite name. For a plan you want stress-tested, try a mind that inverts it, such as Charlie Munger, and for an offer, try a pricing mind like Alex Hormozi. For a web page, a usability mind is a sensible choice, and you should then add one mind who would disagree.

Can I use a mind in ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

Yes. Download the mind from its page and add the files to your AI, or copy the mind and paste it into a new chat. If your AI offers projects, put the files in a project so every conversation there can use them.

How many minds should I use at once?

One mind is usually enough for a review or a single job. For a big decision, three to five minds who pull in different directions tends to work well, and the council on the Minds page holds up to seven.

Can a mind give me investment or legal advice?

No. Minds explain how a person would think about a problem, but they don't give personal investment, legal, medical or tax advice. Check with a qualified professional before you act on anything in those areas.

Can I make a mind of my own?

Yes. Open My minds from the Minds page, where you can create a mind in your own voice, or import a mind file or a writing sample, and then use it across your workflows.
