# Graza: public facts

Checked: 15 Nov 2026. Every fact below has a source. Anything not confirmed is listed under Not verified.

Graza still trades. It is a private company. No sale or acquisition has been announced.

## Company

- **Founded:** October 2020, per CNBC. The product launched on 11 January 2022 [6]. Wikipedia gives 2021 [7].
- **Founders:** Andrew Benin (co-founder and CEO) and Allen Dushi (co-founder and COO) [6]. Casper co-founder Neil Parikh was an early investor, not a founder [6].
- **HQ:** Brooklyn, New York [8].
- **Ownership and funding:** Private [7]. Early money included $50,000 from Neil Parikh and $230,000 from angel investors in 2021 [6]. No full funding total from a reputable source was found.
- **Size signals:** CNBC reported more than $4 million in sales in 2022, more than $19 million by end of 2023, and a company projection of more than $48 million gross sales in 2024 [6]. A January 2026 press release calls Graza the 5th largest olive oil brand in the US [9]. CNBC said it was sold in more than 13,000 retailers in June 2024 [6].
- **Channels:** Own site (DTC, with subscriptions) [1]. Whole Foods nationwide [8][9]. Target lists Sizzle, Drizzle and Frizzle on target.com [10]. Inside Retail names Whole Foods, Target, Costco and Walmart [11]. Mayo went to Whole Foods and Central Market first, with Kroger, Publix and Sprouts to follow [9]. The site also has a store locator, wholesale and corporate gifting pages [1].

## Policies

- **Shipping:** US only. Orders take 3 to 5 days to process, then 3 to 6 business days to ship [3].
- **Free shipping:** The subscribe page says any order over $47 ships free and smaller orders pay $9 [5]. The cart code on the home page holds a hidden $52 free shipping setting [1]. Treat $47 as the stated rule.
- **Returns:** No returns, because the oil is a perishable food. If something goes wrong, customers email hello@graza.co and Graza promises to put it right [3].
- **Subscription:** Up to 19% off, monthly, free goodies from the 3rd order, pause or cancel any time [3][5].
- **Loyalty:** No points programme was found. There is an Ambassador Program [1][3].

## Positioning and customer

Graza is for everyday home cooks, not olive oil experts. Its big idea is that good olive oil should be used freely, every day, not saved for special meals. The squeeze bottle makes pouring easy and fun. It splits the job in two: one oil for cooking and one for finishing, plus a high heat oil for frying. It pitches itself as fresh, single origin oil from Jaén, Spain, at a fair price. Its newer mayo line targets shoppers who want to avoid seed oils [9].

## Voice

Three adjectives: playful, casual, confident.

Quote: "Made for eating, never heating." (Drizzle, on the home page [1]).

How they write: very short lines, puns and rhymes (Drizzle, Sizzle, Frizzle). Product names sit in quote marks, like "Sizzle". They talk to the reader directly and answer doubts head on, for example telling people EVOO is fine for cooking [2]. They use kitchen words (punchy, mellow, crisping, searing) and avoid lab or luxury language. The blog is called the Glog [1].

## Design language

- **Logo:** The wordmark "GRAZA" in all capitals. It uses a heavy, soft, flared display serif with curvy, groovy letter shapes, in dark olive green [12]. The Dieline called the type Spanish inspired [13].
- **Colours (from the site CSS [14]):**
  - Text and dark olive: #3C422E
  - Brand chartreuse: #D1E030
  - Background warm cream: #F6E6D9
  - Highlight pale cream: #FFF4EC
  - Secondary yellow: #FBD535
  - Accent light green: #9EEF80
  - Stroke green: #9FCD7A
- **Typefaces (from the site CSS [14]):** ITC Garamond Condensed for headings, GT Alpina Typewriter for body text, Apercu for small tags and labels.
- **Bottle and packaging:** The hero pack is an opaque squeeze bottle like the ones used in restaurant kitchens [13][15]. Both bottles are dark olive green. Sizzle uses a lighter yellow green as its second colour, Drizzle a brighter green [13]. Labels carry loose, hand drawn illustrations, not big logos. Drizzle shows an olive with a spout. Sizzle shows a woman in a polka dot dress squeezing oil into a pan [15]. The agency Gander made the identity and packaging [13]. Newer formats are aluminium refill cans (opaque, nitrogen sealed), glass bottles with a pop-up spout, sprays and jugs [3][7].
- **Photo style:** Bright food shots with the bottle in use in the kitchen. Inside Retail notes the bottle was designed to show up in cooking videos [11].

## Claims and rules to watch

- **Extra virgin:** Drizzle and Sizzle are sold as 100% extra virgin [2][3]. Frizzle is **not** extra virgin. It is a blend of olive pomace oil and extra virgin olive oil [3][8]. Gambero Rosso reported that the naturally refined wording for Frizzle drew concern as possibly misleading [16]. Note: the store's product feed files Frizzle under the extra virgin olive oil product type [2], so copy must not call Frizzle EVOO.
- **Origin:** Most oil is Picual from Jaén, Spain. Some bottles may come from other parts of Spain, from Alentejo in Portugal (Koroneiki) or from Bari in Italy (Coratina) [3]. CNBC noted Graza bought some olives from outside Spain during shortages [6]. So 100% Spanish or always-Jaén wording is not safe to claim.
- **Single origin, single varietal:** Graza says this per bottle [3].
- **Organic:** Farms use organic methods, but Graza is not USDA certified organic yet [3]. Do not call the oil organic.
- **Certifications stated:** Non-GMO certified. Packed in facilities certified allergen free and gluten free [3].
- **Smoke points:** Sizzle about 410°F, Frizzle up to 490°F, per GMA [8].
- **Sustainability:** Squeeze, glass and cans are recyclable once caps and spouts are removed and cleaned [3]. Recyclable claims fall under the FTC Green Guides.
- **Mayo:** Original Mayo uses EVOO plus pomace oil. Fancy and Garlic use 100% olive oil [9][17]. Seed oil free claims need to match each recipe.
- **Regulators:** FDA (food labelling and identity), USDA (voluntary US olive oil grade standards), FTC (advertising and green claims).
