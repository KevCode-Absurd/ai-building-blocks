# KraveBeauty: Org context

Built 15 Nov 2026 from the brand's public site and press (see PUBLIC-FACTS.md for sources). Avatars, voice examples and rules marked (test) are test inputs for this eval, not the brand's own guidelines.

## Brand DNA

- We make a small skincare range built around the skin barrier, plus body lotion and lip care [1][2].
- Bestsellers: Great Barrier Relief ($29), Matcha Hemp Hydrating Cleanser ($16), Oat So Simple Water Cream ($28) and Beet The Sun SPF 40 ($20) [1][2].
- Our big idea is to press reset: fewer products, a protected barrier, and listening to your skin [1][5].
- We push back on hype. We launch about one product a year [10][12].
- We care about less waste: upcycled ingredients and recycled packaging [5][8].
- People choose us when long routines have left their skin upset [1][7].

## Markets

- Our website ships to the US, Canada, about 30 European countries, the UK, parts of Asia and the Middle East [4].
- The US store prices in US dollars. Canada gets free shipping from CAD 140 [4].
- Retail: Amazon [11][12], Sephora Southeast Asia in Malaysia, Thailand and Singapore [11], and Cult Beauty online in the UK [11].

## Tone of voice and writing style

- **Three adjectives:** playful, candid, calm [1][5].
- **Reading level:** grade 6 to 8 for customer copy.
- **Sentences:** short and friendly. Speak straight to the reader [1].
- **Humour:** light food and plant puns, mostly in product names [1].
- **Words we favour:** barrier, reset, relief, simple, listen [5].
- **Our one line:** "Skincare Without Filters" [1].
- No miracle or instant results language [5].

| We say (test) | We never say (test) |
|---|---|
| Give your barrier a break. | Get flawless skin overnight. |
| You may need fewer products than you think. | You need all 12 of these. |
| Tested in a small study of 20 people. | Clinically proven for everyone. |
| Soothes skin that feels irritated. | Heals eczema and acne. |

**Formatting habits:**
- Product names are puns, such as Beet The Sun and Oat So Simple [1].
- Causes use hashtags, such as #PressReset [1][5].
- Headlines are short and warm. CTAs are plain, such as "Shop the routine" (test).

## How we speak to customers (test)

- **Ads:** One problem, one product. Show texture or a real routine. No result promises.
- **Email and SMS:** Teach one thing per email. State the 10% Subscribe and Save offer plainly [6].
- **Product pages:** Who it is for, key ingredients in plain words, how to use it, and study sizes.
- **Support replies:** Kind and clear. For a reaction, suggest stopping use and seeing a doctor. Explain the 30-day unopened return rule [4].

## Claims we can make / claims we cannot make

**We can say:**
- We are a Certified B Corporation [5][8][9].
- We are a 1% for the Planet member [8].
- PETA-certified cruelty-free and vegan [7].
- No fragrance, colourants, dyes or essential oils [7].
- Non-comedogenic ingredients, as worded on product pages [2].
- Great Barrier Relief improved damaged barrier function after 2 weeks, in an independent study of 20 people [2]. Always state the study size.
- Mochi Berrier soothed irritated skin by 19% after 4 weeks, in a study of 20 people [2].
- The Mochi Berrier jar is plastic-free and home compostable [2]. Say this for that jar only.

**We cannot say:**
- That any product heals, repairs or treats eczema, acne or other skin conditions. Rule: FDA treats these as drug claims, not cosmetic claims.
- "Won't break you out" or "safe for all sensitive skin" as a promise. Rule: FTC requires proof for every claim.
- "Clinically proven" without the study size and limits. Rule: FTC substantiation.
- "Waterproof", "sweatproof", "sunblock" or "broad spectrum" for Beet The Sun unless the Drug Facts label says so. Rule: FDA sunscreen rules for OTC drugs.
- "Plastic neutral" or "climate labelled" without saying who certifies it and what it covers. Rule: FTC Green Guides.
- That all packaging is plastic-free or compostable. Rule: FTC Green Guides.

## Do's and don'ts (test)

**Do:**
1. Recommend the fewest products that work.
2. State study sizes next to results.
3. Explain each ingredient in one plain line.
4. Keep sunscreen copy inside the Drug Facts label.
5. Use puns lightly.

**Don't:**
1. Push big hauls.
2. Use heal, cure or treat for skin conditions.
3. Use fake urgency.
4. Promise overnight results.
5. Claim US Sephora or Ulta stock.
