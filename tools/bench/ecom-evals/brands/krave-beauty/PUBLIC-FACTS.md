# KraveBeauty: public facts

Checked: 15 Nov 2026. Every fact below has a source. Anything not confirmed is listed under Not verified.

Other products: Great Body Relief ($25), Lip Barrier Relief ($14), Plumptuous Lip Jelly ($20), Oil La La ($29), Kale-Lalu-yAHA ($25), Makeup Re-Wined ($26) [1]. Catalogue size: the Shop All page lists about 30 items. About 13 are single products. The rest are duos, kits, jumbos, a refill, a pouch and a gift card [3].

## Company

- **Founded:** 2017 by Liah Yoo. The first product came out in December 2017 [10][12].
- **Founder:** Liah Yoo, born in Seoul. She worked in e-commerce strategy at AmorePacific from 2013 to 2015. She has run a skincare YouTube channel since 2011, with over 1.2 million subscribers [10]. Glossy calls her co-founder and CEO [12].
- **HQ:** Not confirmed. Glossy says Yoo splits her time between Seoul and New York City [12].
- **Manufacturing:** Most products are made in South Korea [12].
- **Funding:** Started with $50,000 of Yoo's savings. Described as bootstrapped with no outside funding [10]. In 2022 the brand set up Press Reset Ventures, a $1 million fund paid for from its profits [10].
- **Size signals:** WWD (March 2024) said industry sources put yearly sales in the eight figures, and that sales could double in 2024. The brand does not share revenue [11]. The brand launches about one new product a year [10][12].
- **Legal name:** Krave Beauty, LLC (B Corp directory listing) [9].
- **Sales channels:**
  - Own website, which ships to the US, Canada, about 30 European countries, the UK, parts of Asia and the Middle East [4]
  - Amazon [11][12]
  - Sephora Southeast Asia stores in Malaysia, Thailand and Singapore, from April 2024 [11]
  - Cult Beauty (UK, online), from January 2024 [11]
  - The About page names Sephora as a retail partner [5]

## Policies

- **Shipping:** Free standard US shipping on orders of $50 or more. Free shipping in Canada from CAD 140 (not on subscription orders) [4].
- **Returns:** 30 days after delivery. Items must be bought on kravebeauty.com and be unopened and unused. One refund per product per customer. The buyer pays return shipping [4]. Damaged or faulty items are fully covered [4].
- **Subscriptions:** Subscribe and Save gives 10% off. Skip, swap or cancel any time. Subscription orders of $50 or more ship free. Orders 3 and 4 include bonus gifts [6].
- **Loyalty:** No points or rewards programme found [1].
- **First order offer:** 10% off the first order for signing up to email [1].

## Positioning and customer

KraveBeauty is for people who feel overwhelmed by skincare and want a short, gentle routine that works. It pushes back on hype, fast launches and long routines. Its big idea is to press reset on skincare: use fewer products, protect the skin barrier and listen to what your skin needs. It suits all skin types and speaks most to people with sensitive, dry, acne-prone or damaged skin [1][5][7]. The brand also stands for less waste. It uses upcycled ingredients, recycled packaging and slow, careful launches [5][8].

## Voice

- **Three adjectives:** playful, candid, calm.
- **One quote:** "Skincare Without Filters" [1].
- **How they write:** Short, friendly lines that speak straight to the reader. Product names are puns on food and plants, such as Beet The Sun, Oat So Simple, Kale-Lalu-yAHA, Oil La La, 24 Carrot Retinal and Mochi Berrier. The brand uses hashtags for its causes, such as #PressReset and #SlowdownSkincare [1][5]. It explains ingredients in plain terms and favours words like barrier, reset, relief, simple and listen. It avoids miracle or instant results language and openly criticises overconsumption [5].

## Claims and rules to watch

- **Clinical claims:**
  - Great Barrier Relief: improves damaged skin barrier function after 2 weeks, based on an independent study of 20 people [2]
  - Mochi Berrier: soothes irritated skin by 19% and balances oil after 4 weeks, based on a study of 20 people [2]
  - Small study sizes need careful wording
- **Skin and health claims:** Great Barrier Relief is pitched at sensitive, eczema-prone skin and uses skin-healing tamanu oil [2]. Words like heal, repair and eczema can make a cosmetic look like a drug claim.
- **Sunscreen:** Beet The Sun is SPF 40 PA+++ with avobenzone 3%, homosalate 10% and octisalate 5% [2]. In the US, sunscreen is an over-the-counter drug. It must follow FDA sunscreen rules, including Drug Facts and limits on claims like water resistance.
- **Free from:** No fragrance, colourants, dyes or essential oils [7]. Non-comedogenic ingredients are claimed on product pages [2].
- **Animal claims:** PETA-certified cruelty-free and vegan [7]. The site badge says Animal Test-Free [1].
- **Green claims:**
  - Certified B Corporation [5][8][9]
  - 1% for the Planet member, giving at least 1% of yearly sales [8]
  - Plastic Neutral and Climate Label badges, with few details on the site [1][8]
  - Mochi Berrier jar is plastic-free and home compostable, made from a bio-based polymer called Vivomer [2]
  - Most packaging uses post-consumer recycled material [8]
  - A goal of at least one upcycled ingredient in every new product [8]
- **Rules that matter:** FDA cosmetics rules and the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act (MoCRA). The FDA sunscreen (OTC drug) rules. FTC rules that claims, like clinical results, need proof. FTC Green Guides for compostable, recyclable and plastic neutral claims. UK and EU cosmetics rules for UK and EU sales.
