## Brand DNA

- We sell vitamins and supplements, mostly by monthly subscription [1][2].
- Best sellers: Women's Multivitamin 18+ ($33 a month) and Prenatal Multivitamin ($39) [1].
- Newer lines: Perimenopause Health ($39) and Creatine Greens ($58) [7][10].

- **Three adjectives:** clear, science-led, warm.
- **Reading level:** grade 6 to 8 for customer copy.
- **Sentences:** short and plain, with a dry, knowing wit [4].
- **Humour:** yes, light. Our top loyalty tier is called Certified Skeptic [4].
- **Words we favour:** traceable, clinically studied, third-party tested, science [3].
- **Our one line:** "Our radical idea: supplements should work." [1]

**Formatting habits:**
- Every structure or function claim carries an asterisk to the FDA line [3].
- Headlines are short and a little witty [10].
- CTAs are plain, such as "Start your subscription" (test).

## How we speak to customers (test)

- **Ads:** One proof point per ad. Add the asterisk and FDA line to any benefit.
- **Email and SMS:** Explain the science in one plain paragraph. With the 40% offer, say renewals return to full price [1][4].

- **Support replies:** Warm and direct. The Happiness Guarantee covers the first ritual.com order only, within 30 days [14]. Point to my@ritual.com.

## Claims we can make / claims we cannot make

**We can say:**
- Structure or function claims already in use, such as brain health support and red blood cell formation, each with the asterisk and FDA line [3].
- Women's 18+ raised vitamin D and omega-3 DHA levels in 12 weeks, in a university-led trial [3]. Match the study exactly.
- Every lot is third-party tested for heavy metals and microbes [3].
- USP Verified (Women's 18+ only), NSF Certified (Creatine Greens only), Non-GMO Project Verified, Certified B Corporation [3][7].
- Vegan, gluten free and major allergen free [3][7].
- Bottles are made from 100% post-consumer recycled plastic [3].

**We cannot say:**
- That any product treats, cures or prevents a disease. Rule: FDA and DSHEA allow structure or function claims only.
- That Perimenopause Health replaces HRT or treats menopause symptoms. Rule: FDA disease claim limits. Treat HRT talk as sensitive [10].
- "Burns fat" or "weight loss" for Creatine Greens. Keep "support" and "may help" wording [7]. Rule: FTC health claim proof.
- "FDA approved". Supplements are not approved by the FDA. Rule: DSHEA.
- "Number one prenatal" without current proof [12]. Rule: FTC substantiation.
- USP or NSF on products that do not hold them. Rule: FTC deceptive claims.
- The 40% offer without saying it renews at full price. Rule: FTC subscription rules and state auto-renewal laws.
- Broad "sustainable" claims. Rule: FTC Green Guides.

## Do's and don'ts (test)

**Do:**
1. Lead with proof: source, study or test.
2. Add the asterisk and FDA line to every benefit.
3. Name the study length when you cite it.
4. Speak to women as smart skeptics.
5. Show renewal pricing next to every offer.

**Don't:**
1. Use miracle, cure or detox language.
2. Name diseases in benefit copy.
3. Stretch a certification to the whole range.
4. Hide subscription terms.
5. Mock other choices, such as HRT.
