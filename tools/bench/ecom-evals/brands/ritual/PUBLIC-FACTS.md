## Company

- **Founded:** Katerina (Kat) Schneider left her job to start Ritual in 2015, while pregnant. The first women's multivitamin launched in 2016. The prenatal launched in 2018 [5].
- **Founder:** Katerina Schneider, founder and CEO [5][6].
- **HQ:** Los Angeles. Press releases carry a Los Angeles dateline [7] and the site's BBB badge points to a Los Angeles listing [3]. The site does not name an HQ.
- **Ownership and funding:** Private. Founders Fund led a $10.5M Series A in 2017 [8]. A $25M Series B in Feb 2019, led by Norwest with Forerunner, took the total to $40.5M [9]. Beauty Independent puts total funding at $53M, last raised in 2019 [6]. Ritual is a Certified B Corporation [6][3].
- **Size signals:** More than $250M gross revenue in 2024, up from $100M in 2021, and reported as profitable [5][6]. It has sold over 25 million bottles [5] and says it has millions of customers [10]. It is in more than 5,000 retail doors [6].
- **Channels:** Own site (subscription first) [1]. Whole Foods from 2022, then Amazon, Wegmans and Target, per CNBC [5]. Beauty Independent says Target came first, in 2023 [6]. Ulta Beauty from July 2025 [11]. Walmart from Oct 2025, growing to about 1,900 stores [6]. Costco carries the Prenatal, in all US warehouses from Aug 2026 [12]. The site also links to International Shipping [1].

## Policies

- **Shipping:** Subscriptions always ship free, with no minimum order [4]. One-time purchases pay $10.99 shipping [13][3]. There is no free shipping threshold for one-time orders.
- **Returns:** Ritual calls it the Happiness Guarantee. Ritual refunds your **first** order in full within 30 days of purchase on ritual.com. You email my@ritual.com with proof of purchase. It does not cover later orders or other retailers [14].
- **Subscription:** Monthly, a 30-day supply every 30 days [3]. Subscribers save 12% on every order [4]. Snooze or cancel any time [4].
- **Loyalty:** Club Yellow. Six tiers based on how many months you subscribe, with no points. Perks include 20% off a new product at month 3, a free gift at month 4, and a gift at month 12 [4].

## Positioning and customer

Ritual is for women, mostly, who are wary of the supplement industry and want proof. The core buyer is a woman from her 20s to her 40s and up, moving through life stages: daily health, trying to conceive, pregnancy, postpartum and now perimenopause. About 40% of customers are entering perimenopause [10]. The brand stands for transparency: it shows where each ingredient comes from (Made Traceable), runs clinical studies and third-party tests every lot. It uses fewer, "essential" nutrients rather than mega doses. Its tagline casts both the brand and its buyers as skeptics [15]. It also pushes for supplement regulation reform [6].

## Voice

Three adjectives: clear, science-led, warm.

Quote: "Our radical idea: supplements should work." (home page [1]).

How they write: short, plain sentences with a dry, knowing wit, such as naming the top loyalty tier Certified Skeptic and joking that there are no points to hoard [4]. They lean on proof words: traceable, clinically studied, third-party tested, science. They speak to women directly and treat them as smart. Campaigns can be playful, such as the peri-dise pun in the perimenopause launch campaign [10]. Health claims always carry an asterisk to the FDA disclaimer [3].

## Claims and rules to watch

- **FDA disclaimer:** Every structure or function claim needs the asterisk and the line that the statements have not been evaluated by the FDA, and the product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease [3][7].
- **Structure and function claims in use:** brain health support, bone health upkeep, red blood cell formation, and antioxidant (free radical) claims on the women's multivitamin [3]. Perimenopause Health is sold as hormone balance support [1]. Creatine Greens claims muscle tone and strength (with exercise), fat metabolism and weight management support, and that chlorella may help reduce waist fat [7]. These must never become disease or treatment claims. Perimenopause Health is pitched as a non-hormonal option next to HRT, which is sensitive [10].
- **Clinical claims:** Women's 18+ cites a university-led trial showing higher vitamin D and omega-3 DHA in 12 weeks [3]. Claims must match the study exactly. Number one best-selling prenatal claims also need proof [12].
- **Certifications stated:** USP Verified (Women's 18+), Non-GMO Project Verified, Clean Label Project Purity Award, ClimeCo Certified, Certified B Corporation, NSF Certified (Creatine Greens), vegan, gluten and major allergen free [3][7].
- **Testing:** Every lot is third-party tested for heavy metals and microbes [3].
- **Sustainability:** Recycled plastic and sustainably harvested lichen D3 claims [3] fall under the FTC Green Guides.
- **Offers:** Auto-renew at full price after the 40% first order discount must be clear [1][4]. This touches FTC rules and state auto-renewal laws.
- **Regulators:** FDA (DSHEA, labels and structure or function claims), FTC (health claim substantiation, endorsements, green claims, subscriptions).
