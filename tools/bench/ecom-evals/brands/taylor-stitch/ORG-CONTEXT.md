# Taylor Stitch: Org context

Built 15 Nov 2026 from the brand's public site and press (see PUBLIC-FACTS.md for sources). Avatars, voice examples and rules marked (test) are test inputs for this eval, not the brand's own guidelines.

## Brand DNA

- We make classic, rugged menswear: shirts, sweaters, trousers, denim and outerwear, plus some bags and accessories [1].
- The site is menswear only today. Women's is not on sale [1][13].
- Signature pieces: The Jack shirt ($128 to $148), The Long Haul Jacket ($208) and The Ojai Jacket ($198) [1][2].
- We stand for clothes that last: better materials, repair, and a for-life guarantee on defects [2][4].
- Three friends started us in San Francisco in 2008, making custom shirts [7][12].
- People choose us to buy fewer, tougher pieces that work outdoors and in town [1][4].

## Markets

- US first, priced in US dollars [1].
- Free shipping and returns on every US order, with no minimum [1].
- Channels: our website, three stores (two in San Francisco, one in Seattle) and wholesale to independent boutiques [3][7].

## Tone of voice and writing style

- **Three adjectives:** rugged, plain spoken, quietly confident [1].
- **Reading level:** grade 6 to 8 for customer copy.
- **Sentences:** short and direct. Talk about fabric, build and use, not trends [1].
- **Humour:** very little. No slang [1].
- **Words we favour:** built, long haul, staples, timeless [1]. Use "responsibly" only next to a proven fact.
- **Our one line:** "Equipment for the everyday expedition" [1].

| We say (test) | We never say (test) |
|---|---|
| Built to wear for years. | The must-have trend of the season. |
| 100% organic cotton (only where true). | Eco-friendly clothing. |
| If it fails from a defect, we repair or replace it. | Guaranteed forever, no matter what. |
| Wear it on the trail, then to dinner. | This fit is totally epic, bro. |

**Formatting habits:**
- Headlines in title case, three to six words [1].
- Product names are "The" plus a name: The Jack, The Ojai, The Long Haul [1].
- CTAs are short and plain, such as "Shop The Jack" (test).

## How we speak to customers (test)

- **Ads:** One clear benefit, such as fabric or years of wear. Show real use.
- **Email and SMS:** Lead with the product and why it lasts. SMS stays short. Offers come with terms.
- **Product pages:** Fabric, fit, care and origin first. Add a size tip.
- **Support replies:** Calm and short. State the policy, such as 21-day returns or final sale on Last Call [6]. Give the next step.

## Claims we can make / claims we cannot make

**We can say:**
- Over 99% of our cotton products use 100% organic cotton [4]. Use this exact wording.
- Our merino is non-mulesed [4].
- Our leather is byproduct-sourced from Leather Working Group Gold-rated tanneries [4].
- We hold Leather Working Group, Fair Trade Apparel and Schoeller Textiles AG certifications [4].
- The Long Haul Guarantee repairs, or replaces, items with defects outside normal wear, for the life of the product [2][4]. Link the terms.
- Fibre content and origin exactly as product pages show, such as Vietnam or China [2].

**We cannot say:**
- "Sustainable", "eco-friendly" or "responsible from seed to sew" as a broad promise. Rule: FTC Green Guides (general green claims need specific proof).
- "Certified organic", GOTS or OCS. No certificate is named [4]. Rule: FTC Green Guides and FTC Textile labelling rules.
- That our supply chain is cradle-to-cradle. It is a goal [3]. Rule: FTC Green Guides.
- That our bags, tags and labels are recycled. The 2021 target has no update [4]. Rule: FTC Green Guides.
- "Made in USA". Items are made in Vietnam and China [2]. Rule: FTC Made in USA rules.
- "Lifetime guarantee" with no terms, or that it covers normal wear. Rule: Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act.
- That we are a B Corp. No status was found [4].

## Do's and don'ts (test)

**Do:**
1. Lead with fabric, build and how long it lasts.
2. Give size help on every product page.
3. Show the clothes in real outdoor and town settings.
4. Use the exact organic cotton wording [4].
5. Link the guarantee terms.

**Don't:**
1. Show or promise women's products. The range is menswear only today.
2. Chase trends or use slang.
3. Use broad green words without a fact next to them.
4. Promise refunds on Last Call items. They are final sale [6].
5. Mention Workshop or Restitch as live programmes.
