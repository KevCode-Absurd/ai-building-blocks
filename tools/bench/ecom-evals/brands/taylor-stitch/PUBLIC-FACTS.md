# Taylor Stitch: public facts

Checked: 15 Nov 2026. Every fact below has a source. Anything not confirmed is listed under Not verified.

## What it sells

Taylor Stitch makes classic, rugged menswear: shirts, sweaters, trousers, denim and outerwear, plus some bags and accessories. The home page title calls it classic men's clothing, and every shop menu link is a men's collection [1]. The brief for this file said menswear and womenswear. The current site does not sell womenswear. Women's appears only as a tick box on the email sign up form [1]. A women's line did launch in 2013 [13], but it is not on sale today.

## Company

- **Founded:** 2008 in San Francisco, by Michael Maher, Barrett Purdum and Mike Armenta [7][12]. They started out making custom shirts [12]. The site footer copyright starts at 2009, so the brand's own start date differs by a year [3].
- **HQ:** San Francisco, California [7][8].
- **Ownership:** Part of The Digital Stronghold, a group of digitally native men's lifestyle brands [7]. A 2022 NewSpring Capital release names Taylor Stitch as one of that group's brands, with The Tie Bar and Boston Scally [11]. No sale price or investor stake is public.
- **Leadership:** WWD named Luke McAlpine as general manager and head of brand in Feb 2025. No CEO was named [7].
- **Stores:** Three. Two in San Francisco (Valencia Street and Chestnut Street) and one in Seattle (315 E Pine Street) [3]. The Seattle store opened in spring 2025. It was the first new store in a decade and the first outside California [7].
- **Factory signal:** The Responsibility page describes a factory called Hubo with 80 sewing machine operators, living wages, healthcare and maternity leave [4].
- **Revenue and staff:** Not published by a reputable source.
- **Sales channels:** Own website, three stores, and a wholesale business with independent boutiques that WWD says is still growing [7]. Product pages show items made in Vietnam and China [2].

## Policies

- **Shipping:** The site banner says free shipping and returns on every domestic (US) order. There is no minimum order [1].
- **Returns:** 21 days from delivery. Items must be unworn, unwashed, unaltered, with tags on [6]. Refunds take about 7 business days to process after the return is checked in [6].
- **Final sale:** Last Call items cannot be returned for a refund or credit. Size swaps are allowed only if the size is in stock [6].
- **Guarantee:** The Long Haul Guarantee covers defects outside normal wear for the life of the product. The brand will repair, or replace if it cannot repair [2][4].
- **Loyalty:** The Common Club. Members earn 5% credit back on each purchase. Referred friends get 20% off their first order and the referrer gets $20 in credit. No tiers are listed [5].
- **First order offer:** 20% off the first order for signing up to email [1].
- **Subscriptions:** None found.

## Positioning and customer

Taylor Stitch is for men who want well made, classic clothes that last for years and work outdoors as well as in town. The brand pitches its clothes as gear for daily life and travel. It leans on durability, repair and better materials, such as organic cotton and non-mulesed merino. It speaks to a buyer who will pay more for fewer, tougher pieces and who likes a rugged, West Coast and outdoors style [1][4].

## Voice

- **Three adjectives:** rugged, plain spoken, quietly confident.
- **One quote:** "Equipment for the everyday expedition" [1].
- **How they write:** Short headlines in title case, often three to six words. Product names are The plus a name, like The Jack, The Ojai, The Long Haul. Copy talks about fabric, build and use more than trends. Section titles use light outdoors themes, such as North By Northwest and The Fabrics Of Fall. Little humour and no slang. Words they favour: built, long haul, staples, responsibly, timeless [1].

## Claims and rules to watch

- **Organic cotton:** The brand says over 99% of its cotton products use 100% organic cotton [4]. No organic certificate (such as GOTS or OCS) is named on the page [4].
- **Wool:** Merino is said to be non-mulesed [4].
- **Leather:** Said to be byproduct-sourced, from Leather Working Group Gold-rated tanneries [4].
- **Labour:** The page names Fair Trade Apparel among its certifications and says it aims to pay prevailing wages [3][4].
- **Broad green language:** Lines like Responsibly Built For The Long Haul, and talk of being responsible from seed to sew, are general claims [1]. The About page also calls a cradle-to-cradle supply chain a goal, not a current fact [3].
- **Old targets:** A goal to reach over 80% recycled poly bags, tags and labels by the end of 2021 still shows on the page, with no update [4].
- **Lifetime guarantee:** A for-life promise is a warranty and needs clear terms [2][4].
- **Certifications stated:** Leather Working Group, Fair Trade Apparel, Schoeller Textiles AG [4]. No B Corp status found.
- **Rules that matter:** FTC Green Guides (organic, recycled and responsible claims). The FTC Textile and Wool labelling rules (fibre content and country of origin). The FTC rules on written warranties (Magnuson-Moss Act) for the Long Haul Guarantee. FTC Made in USA rules if any US-made claim is made.

## Not verified

- **Womenswear today:** No women's products are on the current site. The 2013 women's line is not on sale [1][13].
- **Workshop:** Taylor Stitch used to run Workshop, a crowdfunded pre-order shop [15]. taylorstitch.com/workshop returned a 404 error on 15 Nov 2026. It may have closed.
- **Restitch:** A resale and repair programme launched in 2019 [14]. taylorstitch.com/restitch now redirects to the Responsibility page, and that page describes no resale programme [4]. Its current status is unclear.
- **Amazon:** No Amazon store found.
- **Revenue, staff count and investor names:** Not found from a reputable source.
- **Founding year:** Press says 2008. The site footer says 2009.
- **Logo shape:** Only the SVG title and description were read. The exact letterforms were not checked by eye.
- **Hubo factory:** Ownership of the Hubo factory (own or partner) is not clear from the page.
