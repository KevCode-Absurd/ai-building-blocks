# Meta budget decisions: 14 to 27 November

## The answer

- **3 cuts, 3 fixes, 2 holds, no scale yet.** No ad set makes money on both counts below, so none earns more budget today.
- **Spend drops from ₹44,900 a day to ₹27,200.** Today's run rate would spend ₹6.3 lakh in 14 days, over your ₹5.6 lakh cap. The plan spends ₹3,80,800 and keeps ₹1,79,200 in reserve, released only by rule 3.
- **Why:** last 14 days, Meta lost ₹26,986 even on Meta's own purchase count, and ₹1,78,869 on the store's count (Meta-tagged Shopify orders).
- **Effect:** profit after ads moves to a gain of ₹28,319 on Meta's count, and to a loss of ₹83,126 on the store's. Profit is the limit that binds, not cash or stock.

## What one order is worth

| Order type | Working | Profit per order placed |
|---|---|---|
| Delivered | ₹899 - ₹342 product - ₹95 fees | ₹462 |
| Prepaid | 98% x ₹462 - 2% returns x ₹160 | ₹450 |
| COD | 78% x ₹462 - 22% RTO x ₹160 | ₹325 |
| Your mix | 55% x ₹450 + 45% x ₹325 | **₹394** |

**Break-even ROAS on Meta's count:** ₹899 / ₹394 = **2.3**.

**Meta over-claims.** Shopify tagged 1,020 Meta orders; Meta claims 1,425, a ratio of 0.72. Retargeting and catalog over-claim most, since many of those buyers were coming back anyway. I gave them 0.50 (155 of their 310 purchases). Prospecting gets the rest: (1,020 - 155) / 1,115 = 0.78.

**Break-even ROAS on the store's count:** prospecting 2.3 / 0.78 = **2.9**; retargeting and catalog 2.3 / 0.50 = **4.6**.

**The rule:** cut when an ad set loses on both counts, hold when they disagree, scale only when both show profit.

2. **Razor Starter Kit, cut.** ₹463 per purchase against ₹366 break-even on its ₹849 orders, over 205 purchases. The kit keeps selling through ASC.
3. **Women Body Razor, cut.** ₹795 per purchase against ₹412 break-even. It loses ₹2,400 a day on Meta's own count.
4. **Beard Kit LAL, fix.** Barely above break-even, and frequency 3.4 at ₹230 CPM shows the audience wearing out. Rebuild the seed from delivered orders only, so Meta stops finding buyers like those who refused. Add 2 new ads. Same budget.
5. **Retargeting, fix.** Frequency 6.8 in 14 days. Narrow to add-to-cart and checkout visitors from the last 7 days, exclude 30-day buyers, halve the budget.
6. **UGC test, hold to day 7 (15 November).** Best CTR (1.9%) and CPM (₹150), but ₹581 per purchase. ROAS 2.3 or better over 7 days: move its best ad into ASC. Below: pause it and run the next concept in its slot.
7. **Gift Sets, cut.** ₹976 per purchase against ₹598 break-even on ₹1,268 orders.
8. **Catalog DPA, fix.** Stop paying twice for the same people: show it only to product viewers from the last 14 days who did not add to cart, excluding the retargeting pool and recent buyers.

**Runner-up I rejected:** moving the cut money into ASC to spend the full ₹40,000 a day. Its average already loses on the store's count, and extra rupees cost more than the average.

## Day-to-day rules

1. **Read 7-day ROAS** in Ads Manager (7-day click, 1-day view), by ad set. Never act on under 3 days of data.
2. **Lines.** Prospecting: below 2.3 cut, 2.3 to 2.9 hold, 2.9 or more for 7 days scale. Retargeting and catalog: below 2.3 cut, 2.3 to 4.6 hold, 4.6 or more scale.
3. **Scaling.** Raise 20% every 3 to 4 days from the reserve. After each raise, divide extra spend by extra purchases; stop when that passes ₹394.
4. **Learning.** An ad set needs about 50 purchases a week, about ₹2,500 a day at today's costs. Launch nothing smaller. Don't edit an ad set in learning unless ROAS is below 1.5 for 3 days.
5. **New concepts.** Kill at ₹1,200 spent (3 x ₹394) with 0 or 1 purchases. At 7 days, ROAS 2.3 or better moves into ASC.
6. **Frequency.** Prospecting over 3.5 in 7 days: add new ads, no raise. Retargeting or catalog over 5 in 14 days: shorten the window or cut 20%.
7. **Cap.** Never above ₹40,000 a day. Anything outside these rules goes to the owner.
8. **Every Monday:** divide Meta-tagged Shopify orders by Meta purchases (now 0.72). Outside 0.65 to 0.80, check pixel and UTMs before moving budget. Read RTO on orders placed 14 or more days earlier.
9. **Black Friday (27 November):** if you run a discount, recompute ₹394 at the sale price first.

## The biggest money lever: COD

45% COD x 22% refused means 9.9% of orders come back: about ₹57,000 of last fortnight's ₹5.8 lakh spend bought orders that never paid.

- Each order moved from COD to prepaid adds ₹125.
- Each refusal avoided is worth ₹622: ₹462 earned plus ₹160 not spent.

Confirm COD orders on WhatsApp before dispatch. Give a prepaid reason in ads and at checkout; you set the size of any prepaid offer. Remove refused buyers from customer lists and seeds. Every point of RTO cut raises the ₹394 line.

## Assumptions

- ₹899 is revenue before tax. If it includes 18% GST, every line here is too generous.
- The ₹95 fee applies to delivered orders only. A prepaid return costs ₹160 and the product is restocked.
- COD share and RTO are the same in every ad set; I had no split.
- Tagged orders (1,020) are a floor and Meta's 1,425 a ceiling, so calls need both counts to agree.
- First orders only. Repeat blade and refill orders would lift every line; I had no repeat data.
- Budget is ₹12 lakh per 30 days: ₹40,000 a day.
