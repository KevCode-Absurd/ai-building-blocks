# Eight Meta ad concepts for the Mokobara cabin suitcase

**The main bet:** your ads look alike because every one shows the case on a colour background. Meta tends to treat near-identical ads as one ad and show them to the same people. So these eight change the idea, not the backdrop. Each puts the case in a real moment of a work or weekend trip and leads with one approved fact.

**Make first:** 1 (the gate check), 2 (push, don't drag) and 3 (desk to departure). Reasons at the end.

| # | Concept | Angle | Buyer and awareness | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The gate check | Problem: cabin fit | Work flyers who dread the bag check at the gate. Problem aware, cold | Reels video, 15 s |
| 2 | Push, don't drag | Demo against the usual way: wheels | Flyers with an old two-wheel bag. Solution aware, cold | Split-screen video, 10 s |
| 3 | Desk to departure | Timing: fly straight from work | Professionals who leave for the weekend on Friday evening. Unaware, cold | Reels POV video, 20 s |
| 4 | Three days, one bag | Desire: travel light | People on 2 to 3 day work trips. Problem aware, cold | Creator Reels, 25 s |
| 5 | Out of sight | Risk removal: the lock | Flyers who hand bags to cabs and hotels. Product aware, warm | Static |
| 6 | Four checks | Buyer's checklist: all specs | People comparing cabin bags. Solution aware, warm | Carousel, 5 cards |
| 7 | Cost per trip | Value | Site visitors who did not buy. Most aware, retargeting | Static |
| 8 | The bin shove | Mechanism: hard shell | Flyers whose soft bags get squashed. Solution aware, cold | 6 s loop video |

## The eight concepts

### 1. The gate check
**Hook (0 to 3 s):** A boarding queue. A staff hand points at the bag sizer. The Mokobara case rolls in and sits. On screen: "Will it fit the cabin?"
**Visual:** A recreated gate set with no airline names. Sizer, aerobridge, case lifted into the overhead bin. End card: logo, ₹6,999, Shop Now.
**Primary text:** "Will it fit the cabin?" No one wants that question at the gate. The Mokobara cabin suitcase fits most Indian domestic airline cabin limits. Pack it, roll it on, and board with everyone else.
**Headline:** Made for the cabin. ₹6,999 (26 characters)
**Tests:** whether cabin-fit worry opens better with cold buyers than product-first ads.

### 2. Push, don't drag
**Hook (0 to 3 s):** Split screen, one long terminal corridor. Left: a traveller tilts and drags an unbranded two-wheel bag. Right: the same traveller pushes the Mokobara case upright beside them. On screen: "Drag it, or push it?"
**Visual:** Both sides walk in sync for 8 seconds. Close-up of the wheels turning sideways at a corner. End card with price.
**Primary text:** Long walk to the gate? The Mokobara cabin suitcase rolls on 360-degree spinner wheels. Push it upright beside you, turn corners without tilting, and keep your shoulder out of it.
**Headline:** Four wheels, any direction (26)
**Tests:** whether a visible demo beats a spec line on the same cold audience.

### 3. Desk to departure
**Hook (0 to 3 s):** POV at an office desk. A laptop closes. On screen: "Friday, 6 pm. Straight to the airport." A hand grabs the Mokobara case beside the desk.
**Visual:** Office lift, cab boot, terminal, overhead bin, then the case by the door of a weekend stay. Logo in the first 2 seconds and on the end card.
**Primary text:** Leave from the office, not from home. Pack the Mokobara cabin suitcase on Thursday night and take it to work. By Friday evening it is in the overhead bin and you are on your way.
**Headline:** Office to airport. ₹6,999 (25)
**Tests:** whether a real weekly moment pulls unaware buyers better than any product claim.

### 4. Three days, one bag
**Hook (0 to 3 s):** A real creator, on camera, over an open case: "Three days of meetings. One cabin bag. Let's see."
**Visual:** Packs on camera: shirts, a blazer, shoes, chargers. Zips it, sets the lock, rolls out. Film the real packing; if items do not fit, cut items, never fake it. "Paid partnership" label on.
**Primary text:** Three days of meetings in one Mokobara cabin suitcase. Watch what fits. Carry it on, skip the bag drop, and be out of the airport while others wait at the belt.
**Headline:** Pack for 3 days. Carry on. (26)
**Tests:** whether a real person packing beats studio footage, and gives Meta a clearly new kind of ad.

### 5. Out of sight
**Visual (4:5 and 9:16):** The case in a cab boot among other bags, cropped tight on the TSA-style lock. Logo top left. On image: "Locked when it's out of sight."
**Primary text:** Cab boots. Hotel cloakrooms. Airport trolleys. The parts of a trip where your bag is not in your hand. The Mokobara cabin suitcase has a TSA-style lock built in. Lock it before you hand it over.
**Headline:** Lock built in. ₹6,999 (21)
**Tests:** whether security is a real buying reason for warm visitors who have seen the product.

### 6. Four checks
**Card 1:** "4 checks before you buy a cabin bag" with the case and logo.
**Cards 2 to 5:** one spec each, case shown in use. Card headlines: "Fits the cabin" (14), "Polycarbonate shell" (19), "360-degree wheels" (17), "TSA-style lock" (14). Card 5 adds ₹6,999.
**Primary text:** Buying a cabin bag? Check these four things first. (50, inside the 80 carousel limit)
**Tests:** whether a checklist helps comparing buyers more than one message per ad.

### 7. Cost per trip
**Visual (4:5 and 9:16):** The case at a boarding gate. On image: "₹6,999. About ₹292 a trip." Price shown first and larger.
**Primary text:** ₹6,999 for the Mokobara cabin suitcase. Fly twice a month for a year and that is about ₹292 a trip. Warranty and returns are on the product page, so check them before you buy.
**Headline:** About ₹292 a trip (17)
**Working:** ₹6,999 ÷ 24 trips = ₹291.63, rounded to ₹292. 24 trips is my assumption (two a month).
**Tests:** whether value framing converts visitors who left without buying.

### 8. The bin shove
**Hook (0 to 3 s):** No voice. A crowded overhead bin. The case slides in, another bag presses against it, the door shuts with a clunk. On screen: "Hard shell. Made for the bin."
**Visual:** One shot, loops cleanly at 6 seconds. Logo on the case and in the last frame. Original sound only.
**Primary text:** The overhead bin is a contact sport. The Mokobara cabin suitcase has a polycarbonate hard shell. Slide it in, shut the bin, take your seat.
**Headline:** Polycarbonate. Cabin size. (26)
**Tests:** whether a cheap 6-second loop earns attention as well as longer stories.

## Build notes for every ad
- **Sizes:** Reels 9:16 at 1440 x 2560. Keep the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% each side free of text and logos. Feed 4:5 at 1440 x 1800. Carousel 1:1 at 1080 x 1080.
- **Brand:** Mokobara logo in the first 2 seconds of each video and top left on each static. Keep your current logo, type and colours.
- **Sound off:** burned-in captions. Original or royalty-free audio only.
- **Button and page:** Shop Now to the cabin suitcase product page.
- **Never show:** airline names or logos, rival bags' logos, or claims beyond the approved specs.

## Which three to make first, and why
1. **The gate check.** It leads with cabin fit, the one fact that makes this a cabin bag. Cold buyers already feel that worry. It is the furthest from your current ads.
2. **Push, don't drag.** It proves the wheels on screen instead of naming them. It needs no actors' lines, just one corridor.
3. **Desk to departure.** It reaches people who are not shopping yet, through a moment your buyers live every week.

Together they test three awareness levels and three angles, all in video, the biggest change from flat statics. Run each as its own ad set with three hook versions. Use the same audience and the same budget for each. Judge each ad set once it has spent three times your target cost per purchase: three or more purchases, keep; two, give it one more round; one or none, cut. Runner-up: 7 (cost per trip) in retargeting, as it is the cheapest to make.

## Assumptions
- I could not open the site, so voice and look follow your current ads.
- No reviews or results were approved, so none are quoted.
- Gate and bin scenes are recreated on a set, or filmed only where permitted.
- The creator in concept 4 is real, paid and labelled.
- Warranty and returns are linked, not described, until the exact terms are confirmed from the site.
