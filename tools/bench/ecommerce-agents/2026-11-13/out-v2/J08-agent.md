# Blue Tokai win-back programme

**Recommendation.** Win back 49,000 lapsing and lapsed online buyers in four waves, most recent first. Lead with help, not codes. Cap every offer at 15%, so none needs your sign-off. A random holdout in every wave shows real extra orders. On the planning guesses in section 6, the backlog adds about ₹3.4 lakh of gross profit and the always-on flow about ₹9 lakh a year.

**Economics.** Gross profit per order is ₹1,150 × 58% − ₹110 = **₹557**. A 10% code costs ₹115 and leaves ₹442. A 15% code costs ₹172.50 and leaves ₹384.50. Keeping at least half of normal profit would allow up to 24%, so the 15% cap is safe.

## 1. Who to target, and in what order

Your median gap from first to second order is 34 days. A buyer counts as lapsed at twice that: **day 68**.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 46 to 90 | 9,000 | Mon 23 Nov | Fri 22 Jan | Closest to the habit |
| 2 | 91 to 180 | 14,000 | Mon 30 Nov | Fri 29 Jan | Large and still warm |
| 3 | 181 to 365 | 15,000 | Mon 7 to Fri 11 Dec, in five daily slices | Fri 5 Feb | Slices protect inbox placement |
| 4 | Over 365 | 11,000 | Mon 14 Dec | Fri 12 Feb | One last ask, then stop |
| Excluded | 0 to 45, and active subscribers | 11,000 | | | Still buying |

Across every wave:

- **Fix first.** Anyone whose last order had a return, refund or complaint gets only the personal note in section 4, then no promotion for 60 days. If over 20% of a wave falls here, fix the cause first.
- **Repeat buyers (2 or more orders).** Step 1 is a plain-text note from the founder inviting a reply. No code before the final step.

## 2. Offer logic per group

| Group | Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 | Profit per returning order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 46 to 90 | No offer | No offer | Subscription option, no code | ₹557 |
| 91 to 180 | No offer | No offer | 10% off, ends 7 days later | ₹442 |
| 181 to 365 | No offer | 15% off, ends 14 days later | Last call and keep-me choice | ₹384.50 |
| Over 365 | 15% off and keep-me choice, one email | | | ₹384.50 |
| First order used a code | Same steps, capped at 10% | | | ₹442 |

**Why not one big code.** A code also pays people who would have come back anyway. A blanket 15% to all four groups, at the same lifts, loses about ₹17,000. In the 46 to 90 group it turns +₹1.29 lakh into −₹62,000.

**The rule a code must pass.** In waves 2 and 3, half the contacted customers get the code. It stays only if their return rate beats the no-code half by **26% (for 10%)** or **45% (for 15%)**, as 557 ÷ 442 = 1.26 and 557 ÷ 384.50 = 1.45. Otherwise the no-code version wins.

## 3. Sequence and timing per channel

| Step | Day | Email (62% opted in) | WhatsApp (40% opted in) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Reorder note with brew help | None |
| 2 | 7 | Help or offer, per group | One marketing template, alongside the email |
| 3 | 14 | Final note, offer end or keep-me choice | None |

- **Exit** on any order, an "email me less" click or a new support ticket.
- **WhatsApp once per lapse.** It is paid per message, and triggered journeys click far better than broadcasts.
- **Sunset.** Day 35: one "stay or go" email to anyone with no click, visit or order. Still nothing by day 49, after at least six emails in 120 days, ends marketing email. Opens do not count; privacy features inflate them.
- **From Mon 4 Jan, always on:** the same sequence fires at day 68 for every new lapse, with wave 1's no-code rules and a permanent 10% holdout.

## 5. How to measure true lift

1. **Hold out before sending.** Pick at random, by customer ID, among those reachable: 15% of wave 1, 10% of waves 2 and 3, 20% of wave 4. They get no win-back messages; regular newsletters go to both sides alike.
2. **Read at 60 days.** Compare return rate and gross profit per customer, net of codes and refunds, contacted versus held out. Ignore the email tool's credited revenue.
3. **Size check.** At the planning return rates, the holdouts detect lifts of about 3.4, 2.1, 1.5 and 1.0 points in waves 1 to 4. Smaller reads as "not proven".
4. **Decide.** Keep a wave if its profit lift beats its message cost, and a code only if it passes the section 2 rule.
5. **Cafe blind spot.** Some lapsed buyers may now buy in your cafes. Match cafe bills by phone number if you can; if not, the lift is understated.

## Assumptions

- Email and WhatsApp opt-ins overlap at random, so 77% of each group is reachable; wave 4 is email only (62%).
- A WhatsApp marketing message costs ₹1; email costs nothing extra.
- Every product named in a message is in stock.
