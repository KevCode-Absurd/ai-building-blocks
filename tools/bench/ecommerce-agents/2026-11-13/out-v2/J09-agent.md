# Brooklinen: 90-day growth plan (16 Nov 2026 to 14 Feb 2027)

## The answer first

- **The slowdown is a new-customer problem, and it is costing margin.** Returning orders grew 6%. New orders fell 11% while paid spend rose about 19%. Each first order now earns $71 after product, shipping, fees and returns, and costs $104 to win.
- **Goal metric:** contribution margin after ads, as a share of revenue (revenue minus product cost, shipping, fees, returns and ad spend). Estimated **7.8% now versus 13.6% a year ago.**
- **Day-90 target:** 13.7% (range 11.6% to 16.0%) at month-3 run rate, with revenue likely up 3.5% (range -4% to +12%). That is about **$63,000 more contribution a month for every $1M of monthly revenue.**
- **Biggest move:** take paid spend back to roughly last year's level, cutting the ad sets with the worst cost per first order, and cap cost per first order at $90.

## Assumptions

1. Cost per first order means all paid spend divided by new-customer orders.
2. First and returning order values were flat year on year. That lets the order changes split revenue: new customers were 59% of revenue last year and are 54% now.
3. Shipping, fees and returns move with revenue. Contribution margin excludes fixed costs.
4. A customer who repeats places one more $188 order within 18 months (cautious; your cohort data may show more).
5. Money figures are per $1M of monthly revenue. Multiply by your monthly revenue in millions.

## What the numbers prove

| Finding | The sum |
|---|---|
| All of the decline is new customers | New orders -11%, returning +6%, revenue -4% |
| You paid more for fewer customers | Spend = new orders x cost per first order: 0.89 x $104 vs 1.00 x $78 = +19% spend. Per $1M: $302k now vs $254k a year ago, for 2,900 vs 3,260 new orders |
| A first order now loses money | $188 x (52% - 14%) = $71 contribution vs $104 cost. Gross profit over cost: $98 / $104 = 0.94, down from 1.25. Below 1.0 the first order loses money |
| Repeat buying no longer covers it | 18-month value: $71 + 31% x $71 = $94. That is $10 lost per new customer now, against $16 earned a year ago |
| The board's number fell by 5.8 points | Ad spend went from 24.4% to 30.2% of revenue; 38% - 30.2% = 7.8% |
| The site did not cause the change | Conversion flat at 2.1% |

**The constraint in one sentence:** cost per first order ($104) is above what a new customer is worth in 18 months ($94), so more spend makes the margin worse.

## What needs checking (weeks 1 and 2)

1. **Are ads paying for customers you already have?** Pull the share of Meta and Google purchases from returning customers, and Google brand versus non-brand spend.
2. **Market or creative?** Compare cost per thousand views and click-through by channel with last year. Falling click-through points to tired creative.
3. **What does the last dollar buy?** Averages hide it. The top slice of budget can cost several times the average per new customer.
4. **Is brand demand falling?** If branded search volume fell, part of the problem is awareness.
5. **Did first-order discounts deepen?** Compare discount per first order with last year.
6. **What drives the 14%?** Split shipping, fees and returns by product and return reason.
7. **Read loyalty from cohorts.** Shopify's returning customer rate rises when acquisition slows, so a higher rate is not proof of better loyalty.

## The six moves, ranked by contribution and effort

Move 1 comes first because every other move is read through it. Moves 2 to 6 are sorted on likely contribution per 10 hours.

| # | Move | Owner (approver) | Hours | Contribution a month, low / likely / high | Per 10 hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New-customer measurement | Analytics lead (Head of Growth) | 40 | Enables the rest | - |
| 2 | Cut paid spend to a $90 ceiling | Paid media lead (CEO and Finance) | 60 | $24k / $32k / $37k | $5.3k |
| 3 | Bundle-led first order | Merchandising lead (Head of Growth) | 60 | $5k / $10k / $16k | $1.7k |
| 4 | Second-order email and SMS flows | Email and SMS lead (Head of Growth) | 40 | $2k / $5k / $9k | $1.3k |
| 5 | New creative for first-time buyers | Creative lead (Paid media lead) | 100 | $0 / $10k / $27k | $1.0k |
| 6 | Shipping and returns cost | Operations lead (Finance) | 80 | $3k / $5k / $10k | $0.6k |

The moves touch different numbers, so they add: about $63k a month likely.

**1. Measurement.** Week 1: a daily new-customer dashboard with new orders, cost per first order, new-customer revenue divided by ad spend and contribution after ads. Add a one-question post-purchase survey: "Where did you first hear about us?" Done when finance and paid read the same numbers.

**2. Cut paid spend.** On Meta, put existing customers in their own ad set with a spend cap and exclude them from prospecting. That is Meta's documented method for Advantage+ sales campaigns. On Google, add brand exclusions to Performance Max. Then cut about $45k a month per $1M, worst cost per first order first. Run it first in a matched half of US states for three weeks, so both halves share the season. Sum (likely, if the cut spend was buying customers at $250 each): $45k saved minus 180 lost orders x $71 = $32k. **Week 6 line:** if the removed spend bought new customers at more than $94 each, roll out. If under $94, restore it.

**3. Bundle-led first order.** Test a bundle-led offer for new visitors (sheet set with pillowcases or duvet cover) against today's pages, split 50/50. Primary metric: gross profit per new visitor. Guardrail: conversion. Sum (likely): $10 more per first order x 2,720 orders x 38% = $10k. **Week 7 line:** keep if gross profit per visitor is higher; stop if not.

**4. Second-order flows.** Build a cross-sell flow for first-time buyers (sheets to duvet covers and towels) and a winback at about twice the usual gap between orders. Hold back 10% of buyers to measure the real lift. Sum (likely): 3% more returning revenue x $455k x 38% = $5k. **Week 8 line:** stop any email that shows no lift over the holdout.

**5. New creative.** Brief 8 to 10 new concepts aimed at first-time bedding buyers. Launch in two batches (weeks 4 and 8). Sum (likely): 5% lower cost per first order at the same spend = 143 more orders x $71 = $10k. **Week 8 line:** continue if new concepts beat old ones on cost per first order by 5% or more. Otherwise move production money back.

**6. Shipping and returns.** Fix product page information for the top return reason on the ten highest-return products. Review carrier rates and packaging. Sum (likely): 0.5 point of revenue = $5k. **Week 8 line:** trailing four-week cost share at or below 13.7%, or stop and keep the page fixes only.

## Stop doing

1. Judging paid on platform-reported return or blended revenue per ad dollar. Returning revenue flows into both whether or not ads worked.
2. Raising any ad set's budget while its cost per first order is above $90 outside sale weeks.
3. Spending prospecting money on existing customers; email and SMS reach them.
4. Running a sitewide sale for new buyers without resetting the cost ceiling (below).

## Black Friday, Cyber Monday and January

Black Friday (27 Nov) and Cyber Monday (30 Nov) fall in week 2. Each 10 points of discount cuts first-order contribution by about $16. At 20% off it is $39, not $71. Sale buyers may repeat less, so cap sale-week cost per first order at $50 ($39 plus about half the usual repeat value). Use the same rule for any January sale. Start the spend cut in week 3, after the sale.

## Timeline

Week 1: move 1, the Meta cap, the Google brand split and checks 1 to 7. Week 2: sale weeks. Week 3: spend cut in test states, bundle test and flows go live. Week 4: creative batch 1. Week 6: cut rolls out or reverses. Weeks 7 and 8: bundle, flow and creative lines; batch 2. Week 13: day-90 read against the ranges.

Cash rises: spend falls about $45k a month per $1M from December, and creative comes from the existing budget.
