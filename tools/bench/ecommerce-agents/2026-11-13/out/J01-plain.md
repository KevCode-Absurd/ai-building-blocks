# Meta budget decisions: next 14 days

## The short answer

Last fortnight Meta spent ₹5,80,000 and lost money on first orders, even on Meta's own count. Cut three ad sets, shrink retargeting, keep the rest flat, and turn the UGC test into a creative pipeline. Planned spend falls to ₹3,82,200. A ₹1,20,000 reserve is released only when an ad set passes the scale rule.

## The numbers I judged on

**What one order is worth before ads (at ₹899):** ₹394.

- 45% COD × 22% refused = 9.9% of orders never land. Prepaid returns add 1.1%. So 89% of orders are kept.
- Kept sales ₹800, less product cost ₹304, shipping and fees on delivered orders ₹86, RTO cost ₹16, return shipping ₹1 = **₹394**.

**Break-even CPA on Meta's count: ₹394.** Meta claims 1,425 purchases. Shopify shows 1,020 Meta-UTM orders, 72% of that. The truth sits between. **On the strict (UTM) count, break-even CPA is ₹282** (₹394 × 0.72). Each ad set's break-even below uses its own order value.

**Why:**

- **ASC: hold.** It brings 43% of purchases and makes ₹23,400 on Meta's count, but loses on the strict count, so it has not earned more money yet. Cap spend on existing customers so it is not paid for buyers who would return anyway. Feed it winning test ads.
- **Razor Starter Kit: cut.** CPA is ₹97 over break-even after 21 days and CTR is a weak 1.1%. It loses ₹20,000 to ₹41,300 a fortnight. ASC already sells all products; move its two best ads there.
- **Women Body Razor: cut.** The worst set. CPA is nearly double break-even; it loses ₹33,800 to ₹44,100. A 0.8% CTR at ₹210 CPM says the ad is not landing. Test one women's concept in the test cell instead.
- **Beard Kit LAL: hold, refresh.** The only prospecting set near break-even on Meta's count. Frequency 3.4 on a 1% audience means it is wearing out. Add three new ads this week.

## Day-to-day rules

Check daily, using 7-day figures.

1. **Scale:** CPA at or under ₹300 and frequency under 3. Raise budget 20%, taking the money from the reserve. No more than once every 3 days per ad set.
2. **Hold:** CPA from ₹300 to ₹390. Change nothing.
3. **Trim:** CPA above ₹390 on two checks in a row. Cut budget 25%.
4. **Pause the ad set:** CPA above ₹470 after ₹15,000 spent.
5. **Pause an ad:** ₹1,200 spent with no purchase, or ₹2,500 spent with CPA above ₹600.
6. **Graduate a test ad into ASC:** CTR of 1.5% or more and CPA at or under ₹390 after 10 purchases.
7. **Frequency:** prospecting above 3.5, add new ads. Retargeting or DPA above 4, cut budget 25%.
8. **One change per ad set per 72 hours.** Do not touch a set in its first 3 days or while it is still learning, unless rule 4 or 5 applies.
9. **Once a week, reset the strict target:** ₹394 × (Shopify Meta-UTM orders ÷ Meta purchases). If that ratio rises, targets loosen. If it falls, they tighten.
10. **Higher-value baskets:** for any set whose order value is far from ₹899, use its own break-even from the table, not ₹394.

One lever outside the ad account: a prepaid order is worth ₹450 before ads, a COD order ₹326. Each order moved to prepaid adds about ₹124, so a prepaid offer in ads and checkout is worth testing.

## Assumptions

- A Meta "purchase" is an order placed, before refusals and returns.
- Refused and returned stock goes back on the shelf, so product cost is not lost.
- A prepaid return costs ₹80 in return shipping (half the ₹160 two-way cost). The ₹95 fee applies only to delivered orders.
- Each ad set's order value is its purchase value divided by its purchases.
- I judge on first-order profit only; no repeat data was given. If customers reorder blades often, targets can loosen.
- Scale decisions use the strict count; cut decisions use Meta's own count.
- The 14-day share is ₹12,00,000 × 14 ÷ 30.
