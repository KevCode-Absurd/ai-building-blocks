# Mokobara cabin suitcase: eight Meta ad concepts

## The problem with the current ads

Every ad now is the same picture: the bag on a coloured background. Meta shows similar-looking ads to similar people, so they compete with each other and fatigue together. The fix is not a better product shot. It is different angles, people, settings and formats, so each ad reaches a different buyer for a different reason.

## Assumptions

- Only the approved claims are used: polycarbonate hard shell, 360-degree spinner wheels, TSA-style lock, fits most Indian domestic airline cabin limits, ₹6,999. No weight, dimensions, colours or durability claims, because the brief does not give them.
- Warranty and returns are not described in the ads. Where they help, the copy points to the site.
- No airline names or logos, so the ads do not suggest a partnership.
- People in the ads are actors or creators. No ad presents a review or customer quote, because none was supplied.
- Awareness levels: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware.

## The eight concepts

### 1. The 6 am flight
- **Angle:** The bag fits the work-trip routine, not a studio.
- **For:** A consultant who flies Monday morning. Problem aware.
- **Hook (first 3 seconds):** Alarm at 4:45. On-screen text: "6 am flight. Again."
- **Format:** 9:16 Reel, 15 to 20 seconds, phone-shot POV.
- **Visual:** Grab the packed bag by the door, cab, glide through the terminal on four wheels, slide it into the overhead bin, seat belt click.
- **Primary text:** "Early flight, packed bag, no fuss. Hard polycarbonate shell, 360-degree spinner wheels, TSA-style lock. Fits most Indian domestic cabin limits. ₹6,999."
- **Tests:** Does real travel context beat the studio shot on scroll-stop and hold rate?

### 2. Will it fit?
- **Angle:** Remove the fear of the bag being sent to check-in.
- **For:** Weekend flyers who travel cabin-only to skip the belt. Problem aware.
- **Hook:** Text over a queue at the gate: "The moment you wonder if your bag fits."
- **Format:** 9:16 Reel, 10 to 15 seconds. Also a 4:5 Feed cut.
- **Visual:** Traveller in the boarding queue glancing at a generic sizer frame. Cut to the bag rolling past and into the overhead bin. End card: "Cabin size. Sorted."
- **Primary text:** "Built to fit most Indian domestic airline cabin limits. Walk on, stow it, walk off. No belt, no wait. ₹6,999."
- **Tests:** Is the cabin-size anxiety the strongest pain point for this buyer?

### 3. One finger
- **Angle:** Show the spinner wheels, do not just say them.
- **For:** People comparing bags who know what spinners are. Solution aware.
- **Hook:** A hand pushes the bag with one finger. Text: "One finger. Four wheels."
- **Format:** 9:16 Reel, 8 to 12 seconds, single take, wheel sound turned up.
- **Visual:** The bag spins and turns through tight spaces: a narrow aisle, a hotel corridor, a metro gate, a lift door.
- **Primary text:** "360-degree spinner wheels go where you go, sideways included. Hard polycarbonate shell. Cabin size. ₹6,999."
- **Tests:** Does a pure product demo beat lifestyle? A low-cost, high-proof format.

### 4. Desk to weekend
- **Angle:** One bag covers the work trip and the getaway.
- **For:** 25 to 32 year olds who do work trips and short holidays. Unaware.
- **Hook:** Laptop closes. Text: "Friday, 6 pm. Leaving straight from work."
- **Format:** 9:16 Reel, 20 seconds, top-down packing in fast cuts.
- **Visual:** Pack shirts, a laptop sleeve and shoes, then swap to swimwear and sandals. Bag zips, lock clicks, out the office door.
- **Primary text:** "Two nights for work. Two nights for you. One cabin-size bag for both. Hard shell, spinner wheels, TSA-style lock. ₹6,999."
- **Tests:** Does a leisure angle widen reach beyond work travellers?

### 5. Cost per trip
- **Angle:** ₹6,999 framed as a cost per trip.
- **For:** Price-sensitive buyers who already like the bag. Product aware to most aware.
- **Hook:** Big type: "₹6,999 ÷ 24 trips = ₹292 a trip."
- **Format:** 4:5 static for Feed, plus a 9:16 version.
- **Visual:** Bag on the left. Simple sum on the right. Small line: "Based on two trips a month for a year."
- **Primary text:** "Fly twice a month and that is 24 trips a year. At ₹6,999, the first year works out to about ₹292 a trip. Every year after that is extra. Hard shell, spinner wheels, cabin size."
- **Tests:** Does value framing lift conversion with warm audiences? Best for retargeting site visitors.

### 6. Four reasons, four cards
- **Angle:** Each spec gets its own moment.
- **For:** Shoppers who want details before they buy. Product aware.
- **Hook (card 1):** "4 things your cabin bag should do."
- **Format:** 1:1 or 4:5 carousel, five cards.
- **Visual:** Close-up per card. Shell surface: "Polycarbonate hard shell." Wheels: "360-degree spinners." Lock: "TSA-style lock." Bag in overhead bin: "Fits most Indian domestic cabin limits." Last card: full bag, price, "Shop now."
- **Primary text:** "Swipe for the four things that matter in a cabin bag. Warranty and returns are on our site. ₹6,999."
- **Tests:** Does a spec-led carousel convert better in Feed than single images?

### 7. Your old bag
- **Angle:** Contrast with the tired bag people already own, not a competitor.
- **For:** People still dragging a worn two-wheel bag. Unaware.
- **Hook:** Sound of a bag scraping on a footpath. Text: "You hear it too, right?"
- **Format:** 9:16 Reel, 15 seconds, split screen.
- **Visual:** Left: an unbranded old two-wheel bag tilted and dragged behind someone. Right: the Mokobara bag rolling upright on four wheels. Same route, same person.
- **Primary text:** "If you still drag your bag behind you, it is time. 360-degree spinner wheels, hard polycarbonate shell, TSA-style lock. ₹6,999."
- **Tests:** Can a "time to upgrade" trigger reach people not shopping for luggage?

### 8. Straight answers
- **Angle:** A team member answers the three common questions, lo-fi.
- **For:** People who clicked before but did not buy. Most aware.
- **Hook:** Person to camera: "Three questions we get about our cabin bag."
- **Format:** 9:16 Reel, 25 to 30 seconds, selfie style, captions on.
- **Visual:** Questions on screen in turn. "Will it fit in the cabin?" Puts it in an overhead bin. "Is the lock TSA-style?" Shows it. "Warranty and returns?" Points to the site link.
- **Primary text:** "Will it fit, is the lock TSA-style, and what if it is not right for you? Straight answers in 30 seconds. Full warranty and returns details on our site."
- **Tests:** Does a plain, human voice clear doubts better than polished ads for warm audiences?

## Make these three first

1. **Will it fit? (Concept 2).** It hits the sharpest worry of this buyer and uses the most useful approved claim. If it wins, it sets the main message for the whole account.
2. **One finger (Concept 3).** It proves a claim on camera, needs one person and one location, and can be shot in an afternoon. It is the cheapest test of demo against lifestyle.
3. **Cost per trip (Concept 5).** It is a static, so it is ready in a day. It gives retargeting something new and tells you whether price is the real barrier.

Why these three together: each tests a different reason to buy (fit, ease of use, value). They cover cold and warm audiences and mix Reels with Feed. None of them looks like the current product shots, so they will not compete with the old ads for the same people.

**How to run the test:** Put the three in one campaign with broad targeting and an equal budget for each ad. Keep the old product-shot ads running as the control. Judge Reels first on how many people watch past three seconds, then on cost per purchase. Give it at least a week before you cut anything. Build the next round from the winning reason, using concepts 1, 4, 7 and 8 as variations.
