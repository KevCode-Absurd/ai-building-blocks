# The Whole Truth protein bars: Meta launch copy set

Six angles, each built for a buyer who has been burned by "healthy" snacks with hidden sugar. Every line uses only the approved claims: 12 g protein per bar, no added sugar, sweetened with dates, ₹540 for a box of 6, and ingredients listed on the front of the pack. ₹90 a bar is ₹540 divided by 6.

Every primary text fits whole in the 125 characters Meta shows before "See more", so nothing gets cut. Headlines and descriptions stay within Meta's 27-character guide. Counts include spaces and punctuation.

## Launch these 4 first

Each ad uses the Shop Now button and links to the protein bar product page.

| Ad | Primary text | Headline | Description | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | P1 | H1 | D1 | The brand's signature proof. Does "ingredients on the front" win trust from cold buyers on its own? |
| B | P2 | H3 | D3 | The buyer's main fear. Does naming hidden sugar, then answering it with dates, beat leading with proof? |
| C | P3 | H2 | D1 | The functional need. Do gym-goers respond more to the protein number than to the sugar story? |
| D | P5 | H4 | D2 | Price framing. Does ₹90 a bar make ₹540 feel easier to say yes to? |

Why these four: A and B attack the same distrust from two sides (show the label, or name the problem). C and D test the two other reasons this audience buys: protein and price. P4 and P6 wait for round two, so the first test stays readable.

**How to run it so the result means something**

- Put each ad in its own ad set, with the same audience (urban, 22 to 40) and equal daily budgets.
- Use the same image or video in all four, so the words are the only thing that changes. Show the front of the pack clearly. Three of the four ads point to it.
- Read results at ad set level on 7-day click, after at least 7 days. Judge on purchases, not clicks. A high click rate with no sales is not a winner.
- Decide each ad set once it has spent 3 times your target cost per purchase. 3 or more purchases: keep. 2: give it one more target cost per purchase. 0 or 1: cut it.
- Replace the cut ad with P4 (office buyers) or P6 (the brand name), using H5 or H6 as its headline.

## Assumptions

- The ads run from The Whole Truth's Facebook and Instagram page, so the brand name shows above every ad. D1 and P6 also name it in the copy.
- The ₹540 box is live on thewholetruthfoods.com at that price, and that page is the landing page.
- English only for this round. Each line is short enough to translate later without losing the claim.
- "Display limits" means Meta's recommended lengths (125 / 27 / 27). Meta allows longer text but cuts it off on screen.

## To confirm before launch

1. The exact product page URL, and that it shows ₹540 for 6 today.
2. That "no added sugar" on the pack has been checked against FSSAI's claims rules. Never shorten it to "sugar-free" or "zero sugar" in comments or replies. Dates carry natural sugar, so those words would mislead.
3. Your target cost per purchase (what you can afford to pay for one sale), so the keep or cut rule above has a number.
