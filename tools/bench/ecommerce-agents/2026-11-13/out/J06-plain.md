Your daily cards sit in quick-access slots, so you can pay without digging. Spare cards and a few flat notes go in the hidden pocket, out of sight until you need them. A small coin pouch handles loose change.

It is made for people who mostly pay by card and keep a little cash for backup. If you carry a lot of notes every day, this is not your wallet, and we would rather tell you now.

The leather starts firm and softens over the first week of daily use. After that, it keeps getting better with age. A 3-year warranty covers it.

## How this copy cuts cash returns without hurting sales

- **Cash limits show up early.** The subtitle, a bullet, the specs, the fit section, the first FAQ and the add-to-cart line all say "a few flat notes." Buyers see it before they pay, not after the wallet arrives.
- **Limits are framed as a choice.** "Built for some cash, not a thick stack" tells buyers who the wallet is for. It doesn't apologise for what it can't do. The slim claim stays first because it is what owners praise most.
- **Wrong-fit buyers get somewhere to go.** The link to a full-note wallet keeps them shopping with you instead of returning.
- **The other known complaints are handled up front.** Mentioning the break-in week and the small coin pouch sets fair expectations. That helps stop new owners from deciding the wallet is faulty.
- **Track it.** Compare the return rate for "holds less cash than expected" and the conversion rate for 60 days before and after launch. If returns fall and conversion holds, keep the copy. If conversion drops, test moving the cash line from the subtitle to the bullets only, and leave the add-to-cart line as it is.
