# Brooklinen: 90-day growth plan

## The short answer

The slowdown is a cost-of-new-customer problem, not a site problem. Every new customer now costs $104 and returns $71.44 of contribution on the first order. So each one loses about $33 before any repeat purchase, up from about $7 a year ago. Returning customers are holding revenue up and hiding the loss. For 90 days we stop buying customers at a loss, measure them properly, and rebuild acquisition on what pays.

## What the numbers prove

| Fact | Working (from the brief) |
|---|---|
| A first order earns $71.44 before ads | $188 x (52% margin minus 14% shipping, fees and returns) |
| Each new customer loses $32.56 on the first order | $71.44 minus $104. A year ago: minus $6.56 |
| First-order gross profit no longer covers acquisition cost | $97.76 / $104 = 0.94, down from 1.25. Under 1.0 means the first order loses money |
| We spend about 19% more to get 11% fewer new customers | Cost per new customer x orders: 1.33 x 0.89 = 1.19 |
| At $104, a new customer is still about $10 short after 18 months | $71.44 plus 31% repeat x $71.44 = $93.59 |
| The break-even cost per new customer is about $94 | Same sum. Above $94 we lose money even after repeat |
| The $26 rise in cost costs $26,000 of contribution per 1,000 new customers | ($104 minus $78) x 1,000 |
| The site can carry paid traffic | 2.1% x $188 = 395 revenue per 100 visitors, above the 300 screen |
| Returning growth cushioned the fall | If order values held, new orders were about 59% of revenue a year ago. Minus 11% there and plus 6% on the rest nets to minus 4% |

Revenue is down 4%; new-customer contribution is down far more. The board's target lives in acquisition cost.

## What needs checking, and by when

1. **Which channel drove the rise (week 1).** We need new-customer cost by channel from store orders, not ad platforms. Meta is 55% of spend, so it is the first suspect, not a proven cause.
2. **Whether Google is paying for people who already know us (week 1).** Brand search often converts returning customers. If so, Google's true new-customer cost is higher than reported.
3. **Whether new-visitor conversion fell (week 1).** A flat 2.1% can hide a new-visitor drop, because more traffic now comes from returning customers, who convert better.
4. **Whether first orders changed (week 2).** Check discount depth, product mix and return rate on first orders against a year ago. I assumed $188 and 52% held.
5. **Creative fatigue or auction cost (week 2).** Check Meta frequency, cost per thousand views and click rate by month.
6. **Stock gaps in best-selling sheet sizes (week 2).** Sell-outs quietly raise ad cost.

## The six moves, ranked

Impact is monthly contribution per 1,000 new customers (or orders), to scale to your real count. These are planning numbers, not forecasts.

| # | Move | Likely impact | Effort | Owner / approver | Start |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set a hard $94 ceiling on new-customer cost and cut the spend above it | High: about $5,200 per $10,000 cut in the quarter | Low | Paid media lead / CEO | Week 2 |
| 2 | Split Google brand and non-brand; stop paying for returning buyers | Medium to high; sized in week 1 | Low | Paid search manager / growth lead | Week 1 |
| 3 | Rebuild Meta creative for new buyers | Up to $8,580 if Meta's cost falls 15% | Medium-high | Paid social manager and creative lead / growth lead | Week 3 |
| 4 | Raise first-order value with sheet and towel bundles | $4,560 per $12 of added order value | Medium | Merchandising lead / growth lead | Week 4 |
| 5 | Cut shipping, fees and returns from 14% to 13% of revenue | $1,880 per 1,000 orders, on all orders | Medium | Operations lead / finance lead | Week 3 |
| 6 | Bring the second order forward for first-time buyers | About $2,140 over 18 months for 3 more points of repeat | Low-medium | Retention lead / growth lead | Week 2 |

**1. Ceiling at $94.** Build a weekly table of spend and new customers by campaign from store orders, Meta on 7-day click. Cut the campaigns buying customers above $94 first. Example: a $10,000 slice buying at $150 brings 67 customers worth $4,763 in first-order contribution, so cutting it saves about $5,200 this quarter. Judge the last dollar, not the average: add spend only while total contribution rises. Expect fewer new orders. That is the point.

**2. Google brand.** Run brand and non-brand as separate budgets. Exclude existing customers from non-brand. Then halve brand bids in some regions for two weeks. If brand orders there hold within 5%, keep the cut everywhere.

**3. Meta creative.** Make new concepts that differ in angle, format and buyer moment, not recoloured old ads. Fund it from the money move 1 frees, not new budget. Judge on store-side new-customer cost. A 15% fall in Meta's cost cuts blended cost about 8%, or $8.58 per customer.

**4. First-order value.** Pair sheets with pillowcases, duvet covers or towels for first-time buyers. Every $12 added at 38% contribution adds $4.56. If a welcome discount runs, trimming it by 5 points adds $9.40 per order. Check its depth in week 2 first.

**5. Shipping, fees and returns.** Split the 14% into its three parts and attack the biggest: return reasons, carrier rates or payment fees. One point is $1.88 on every $188 order, new or returning.

**6. Second order.** Add a cross-category step to the post-purchase flow (sheets buyers see towels, and the reverse) at the 30 and 60 day marks. The day-90 read shows only the 60-day reorder rate of early cohorts, not the 18-month result.

## What to stop

- **Stop judging ads on platform return.** Judge them on store-side new-customer cost and contribution.
- **Stop raising spend to hit the revenue number.** No budget rises until blended new-customer cost sits under $94 for two straight weeks.
- **Stop the "other" 10% that cannot show a new-customer cost.** Pause each one that cannot prove it is under $94 by week 4.
- **Stop reading the returning-customer share as loyalty.** It rises when new orders fall. Track the share of each month's new customers who buy again.
- **Stop counting email and SMS's 22% as proof.** It is the platform's own claim. Judge the program on cohort repeat.

## 13 weeks and the stop lines

| Weeks | Work | Line that decides |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | New-customer cost by channel, Google split, first-order checks | Week 2: table is live and matches store orders within 5% |
| 3-6 | Spend cuts land; creative tests and cost work start | Week 4: blended cost under $98. Below that, continue. Above but falling, change. Flat, stop and rethink |

| 11-13 | Hold what works; read results | Day 90: blended cost $85 to $92; first-order loss under $20 per customer |

## Assumptions

- "Blended cost per first order" is total paid spend divided by new-customer orders.
- First-order value and margin were about the same a year ago.
- The 14% applies equally to first and repeat orders.
- A repeat buyer places at least one more order of about $188 within 18 months, so $94 break-even is cautious.
- No volumes were given, so impact is shown per 1,000 customers or orders.
- Owners are role titles to map to your team. Cash is comfortable, but the plan adds no new spend.
