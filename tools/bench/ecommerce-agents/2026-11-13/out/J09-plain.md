# Brooklinen: 90-Day Growth Plan

From the e-commerce growth lead. All figures are worked from the test inputs; section 8 lists assumptions.

## 1. The short answer

The slowdown comes from the cost of new customers, not the site. We pay 33% more for each new customer and get 11% fewer, while returning customers grow. On my assumptions, revenue fell 4% but contribution after marketing fell about 45%. This plan wins back contribution first and accepts slower new-customer growth while we fix cost.

## 2. What the numbers prove

| Finding | Working |
|---|---|
| The drop is all in new customers | New orders down 11%, returning up 6%, conversion flat at 2.1% |
| Each new customer costs 33% more | $78 to $104 |
| Paid spend is up about 19% for fewer customers | 0.89 x orders at 1.33 x cost = 1.19 |
| First orders now lose much more money | $188 x 38% left after product, shipping, fees and returns = $71.44. Minus $104 = a loss of $32.56 per first order (was $6.56) |
| New customers no longer pay back within 18 months | 31% repeat x $71.44 = $22.15 of later contribution, short of the $32.56 gap. Break-even cost per first order is about $94 |
| Contribution margin roughly halved | About 13.6% of revenue a year ago, about 7.8% now |

One rule of thumb drives the plan. Each $10 cut from cost per first order, at today's volume, adds about 2.9% of revenue in contribution.

## 3. What needs checking (weeks 1 to 2)

These are likely causes. The numbers do not prove any of them yet.

1. **Which channel drove the cost rise.** Split cost per first order by Meta, Google and other. Meta is 55% of spend, so creative fatigue or higher ad prices there could explain most of it.
2. **Paid money reaching people who already buy.** Check how much prospecting reaches past buyers and subscribers, what share of Google spend is brand search, and whether affiliates or coupon sites earn commission on returning customers. Each makes a "new" customer look more expensive.
3. **Where conversion moved.** Flat overall conversion can hide a drop in first-time visitors if returning visitors improved. Split it.
4. **Tracking loss.** If Meta and Google see fewer purchases than the store records, their bidding worsens and costs rise. Compare the two.
5. **Marginal cost.** The last 20% of spend in each channel likely costs far more than $104 per new customer. Find it with spend-step reviews and one holdout test.
6. **Discounts and returns on first orders.** Compare promo use and return rate on first orders against repeat orders.

## 4. The moves, ranked

Ranked by contribution impact for effort. Impact is yearly contribution gain as a share of current revenue. Gains overlap; do not add them.

| # | Move | Owner | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Profit ceiling on paid spend | Paid media lead | +2% to +3% | Low |
| 2 | Stop paying for existing customers | Paid media lead, with analytics | +1.5% per 5% of spend freed | Low |
| 3 | Lift first-order value with bundles | Merchandising lead, with site lead | about +1.7% | Medium |
| 4 | Second-order program | Email and SMS lead | about +0.9% this year, more later | Low to medium |
| 5 | New-buyer creative and landing pages | Creative lead, with paid media lead | +2.9% per $10 off cost per first order | Medium to high |
| 6 | Cut shipping and returns cost | Operations lead | about +1% per point saved | Medium |

**1. Profit ceiling on paid spend.** Set a hard ceiling of $94 cost per first order for every channel, campaign and keyword. That is the 18-month break-even. Run campaigns to a $90 target. Cut or cap anything above the ceiling for two straight weeks. Getting blended cost from $104 to $94 at today's volume is worth about 2.9% of revenue. Some new orders will be lost, which is right when those orders lose money.

**2. Stop paying for existing customers.** Exclude past buyers and subscribers from prospecting. Cap brand search bids. Pause brand terms in a few test regions to see how many orders arrive anyway. End affiliate commission on customers already in checkout, if the checks show it. Every 5% of spend moved off sales we would get anyway is worth about 1.5% of revenue.

**3. Lift first-order value with bundles.** Build three or four bundles, such as sheet set plus duvet cover, a towel set, and a complete bed. Keep gross margin at 52% or better. Point paid traffic at them. If shipping policy allows, test a free-shipping or gift threshold set just above $188. An 8% lift in first-order value, to about $203, with no drop in conversion, is worth about 1.7% of revenue. It also cuts the loss on each first order by about $5.70.

**4. Second-order program.** Returning orders are growing and carry almost no ad cost. Build a post-purchase series: care tips and a review request at day 7, a complete-the-bed offer at day 21, a towel or second-set offer at day 45, and a reorder prompt when sheets usually need replacing. Split email and SMS by first product bought. Lifting returning revenue 5% is worth about 0.9% of revenue. Each 1-point rise in the 18-month repeat rate adds about $0.71 per new customer and raises what we can afford to pay for one.

**5. New-buyer creative and landing pages on Meta.** Ship at least six new concepts every two weeks. Aim them at first-time buyer doubts: how the sheets feel, fit, wash and last. Send each to a matched landing or bundle page. Cut any concept above the ceiling once it has spent enough to judge. The upside is large, but results are slower and less certain, so it ranks fifth.

**6. Cut shipping and returns cost.** Pull the 20 most-returned products and their return reasons. Fix the top causes on product pages: size, color and feel. Review carrier rates and packaging. Each point taken off the 14% is worth about 1% of revenue.

## 5. What to stop

- Judging paid media on platform ROAS or revenue. Judge it on cost per first order and contribution.
- Raising budgets to hit revenue targets when cost per first order is above $94.
- Showing prospecting ads to past buyers and subscribers.
- Paying affiliate or coupon-site commission on customers already in checkout (confirm first).
- Sitewide discounts for revenue, unless they still make money after discount, shipping and ad cost.
- Broad site redesign this quarter. Conversion is flat; the problem sits upstream. Limit site work to bundles and landing pages.

## 6. Timeline

- **Weeks 1 to 2:** Run the section 3 checks. Set ceilings and exclusions. Start the brand search test. Set scorecard baselines.
- **Weeks 3 to 6:** Launch bundles and the post-purchase series. Run the first creative rounds. Start the returns review.
- **Weeks 7 to 10:** Scale what clears the ceiling and cut what does not. Ship product page fixes.
- **Weeks 11 to 13:** Read the holdout and brand tests. Reset budgets by channel. Set next quarter's targets.

## 7. Weekly scorecard

Review every Monday, 30 minutes, growth lead in the chair. Standing rule: anything above the $94 ceiling for two weeks is cut that day.

| Metric | Now | Day-90 target | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contribution margin after marketing | about 7.8% | 10% or more | Growth lead |
| Cost per first order, blended | $104 | $92 or less | Paid media lead |
| Cost per first order, Meta, Google, other | Set week 1 | Each $94 or less | Paid media lead |
| Share of paid spend reaching past buyers | Set week 1 | Under 5% | Paid media lead |
| New customer orders vs last year | down 11% | No worse than down 15% | Paid media lead |
| Average first order | $188 | $200 | Merchandising lead |
| Contribution per first order after ad cost | minus $32.56 | minus $15 or better | Growth lead |
| First-time visitor conversion | Set week 1 | Up 10% on baseline | Site lead |
| Returning customer orders vs last year | up 6% | up 8% | Email and SMS lead |
| 60-day second-order rate | Set week 1 | Up 2 points | Email and SMS lead |
| Shipping, fees and returns | 14% of revenue | 13% | Operations lead |

If the cost, order value and returns targets all land at today's new-order volume, margin reaches about 13%. The 10% target leaves room for lost new orders.

## 8. Assumptions

- Blended cost per first order is all paid marketing spend divided by new customer orders.
- First and repeat orders both average $188, flat year on year. So new customers were about 59% of revenue a year ago and 55% now, which gives the 13.6% and 7.8% margins.
- Shipping, fees and returns run at 14% on every order.
- Each repeat buyer places one repeat order within 18 months. Customers who buy more often improve payback, so $94 is a cautious ceiling.
- Email and SMS running costs are small and left out.
- Owners are roles; match them to people in week 1.
