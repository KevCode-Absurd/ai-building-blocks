# Plum Goodness: three bundles and a new free shipping threshold

## The short answer

- Launch three bundles priced at ₹729, ₹799 and ₹1,299. Each one clears the new threshold and adds a second product to the basket.
- Raise free shipping from above ₹499 to above ₹699. On my assumptions this adds about ₹8,000 of gross profit per 1,000 orders before any bundle gains. It stops paying if more than about 6% of all orders are lost.
- Test the bundles first, then test the threshold with a split. Judge both on gross profit per visitor after shipping, not on AOV.

## Assumptions

1. Margins apply to the price the shopper pays. I ignore GST, packing and payment fees.
2. Shoppers below the threshold pay a shipping fee that roughly covers your ₹70 cost.
3. The lip balm is costed at the makeup margin (55%), which is the cautious choice.
4. Blended margin across the range is about 63%.
5. Orders in the ₹500 to 699 band average ₹600.
6. Bundle components are priced at today's selling prices.

**A. Hair and Body Basics (₹729). Job: lift small single-item orders.**
40% of orders are under ₹500, and the shampoo and lotion sit right there. A shampoo-only order today earns ₹247 of gross profit. The bundle earns ₹337 after you pay shipping, so you gain about ₹90 per converted order. It also gets a hair or body buyer into a second category. The saving is modest because free shipping does half the persuading.

**B. Vitamin C Glow Duo (₹799). Job: turn the hero serum into a routine.**
Shoppers often buy one hero only, and the serum is your biggest one. A serum-only order earns ₹407 gross profit, or ₹337 after free shipping. The duo earns ₹427 after shipping, a gain of about ₹90 per order. Pitch it as "cleanse, then treat", not just a discount. This bundle matters most if you raise the threshold, because the ₹599 serum alone will no longer ship free.

**C. Head-to-Toe Gift Set (₹1,299). Job: gifting and big baskets.**
It serves the 14% of shoppers already spending ₹1,000 or more, gives gift buyers an easy choice, and makes A and B look like good value. It keeps 57% margin and earns ₹667 per order after shipping. Use it for gifting moments and new-customer ads.

Rules for all three:
- Never let a bundle discount go past 20%. The extra product and free shipping should do the work.
- Show the bundle on each component's product page ("Complete the routine"), not only on a bundles page.
- In the cart, when an order is short of the threshold, offer the ₹199 lip balm. It is the cheapest way across.

## Free shipping threshold: move from above ₹499 to above ₹699

**Why ₹699.** Your threshold today is 20% below your ₹620 AOV, so 60% of orders ship free. That costs ₹42,000 per 1,000 orders, about ₹42 an order or 6.8% of AOV. Much of that pays for shipping on orders that would have happened anyway. A threshold just above AOV pulls orders up. ₹699 also matches the edge of your ₹500 to 699 band, so the money below uses your real order data.

**Who is affected.** Only the 27% of orders between ₹500 and ₹699, about 270 per 1,000 orders. They ship free today and would not under the new rule. I assume they split three ways:

| What they do | Share | Orders per 1,000 | Money effect per order | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Add an item (mostly the ₹199 lip balm) to qualify | 40% | 108 | +₹109 extra gross profit | +₹11,772 |
| Pay the shipping fee | 45% | 122 | +₹70 shipping saved | +₹8,540 |
| Abandon the order | 15% | 40 | -₹378 lost profit + ₹70 saved = -₹308 | -₹12,320 |
| **Net change** | | | | **+₹7,992** |

So the change adds about **₹8 of gross profit per order** across all orders, before any bundle effect. Bundles A and B should push the "add an item" share above 40%, because they hand shoppers an easy, good-value way over ₹699.

**The break-even.** With 40% topping up, each lost order costs ₹378 more than an order that pays shipping. Profit falls to zero when lost orders reach about 61 per 1,000. That is 23% of the affected band, or about **6% of all orders**. If your test shows a loss bigger than that, keep ₹499.

**COD risk.** 40% of orders are cash on delivery. A refused COD parcel costs you shipping both ways. Track the return-to-origin rate by threshold arm and by bundle. If the gift set's COD refusal rate is clearly higher than your normal rate, make it prepaid only.

## How to test both safely

**Step 1: Bundles first (weeks 1 to 3), threshold stays at ₹499.**
- Show the bundle module on product pages to a random half of visitors. The other half sees today's pages.
- Primary metric: gross profit per visitor, after discounts and shipping.
- Also watch take rate, conversion, and how many duo buyers would have bought both items at full price anyway. If that is over half, cut its discount to 10%.
- Put the winning bundles live for everyone before Step 2, for a clean baseline.

**Step 2: Threshold test (weeks 4 to 7).**
- Split visitors 50/50: control at above ₹499, test at above ₹699. If your store cannot set shipping rules by visitor, split by region (pin codes) with matched past sales. Do not run alternate weeks, because demand swings too much week to week.
- Show the threshold in the header and a cart progress bar ("Add ₹X for free shipping") in both arms, so the only difference is the number.
- Run at least 14 days and at least 2,000 orders per arm, whichever is later. Avoid sale periods and festive weeks.
- Primary metric: gross profit per visitor after shipping.
- Read-outs: share of orders in each value band, top-up rate, cart abandonment at the shipping step, COD share, and return-to-origin rate.

**Stop rules (agree these before you start).**
- Stop the test arm early if its conversion rate is more than 10% below control for 3 days running.
- Never call a winner early. Wait for the full run.
- Roll out ₹699 only if gross profit per visitor beats control and lost orders stay under 6% of all orders.

**If ₹699 loses.** Try ₹599 next. It is a smaller step, but note the serum sits exactly at ₹599, so it would still not qualify on its own.
