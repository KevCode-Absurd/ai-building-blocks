# Job J05: Meta ad copy set for protein bars

**Business:** The Whole Truth (thewholetruthfoods.com), an Indian food brand known for listing ingredients on the front of its packs, selling protein bars, nut butters and more online.

**Role wanted:** ad copywriter.

Numbers and notes marked (test) are test inputs for this exercise, not the brand's real figures or guidelines. Treat them as the store's own.

**Brand notes (test):**
- Voice: blunt, witty, honest. We say "No added sugar. Not even a little." We never say "guilt-free" or "superfood."
- Reading level: grade 6. Short sentences. Numbers beat adjectives.
- Avoid: "healthy" as a label on its own, any claim not in the approved list, naming competitors, and scaring people about other brands.
- Show what's inside. If a line could sit on any snack brand's ad, cut it.
- We stand for the truth on the label: nothing hidden, nothing dressed up.

**Who we sell to (test):**
- Aditya, 27, a software engineer in Bengaluru who trains five days a week. He wants real protein without junk. What stops him: bars that taste like chalk or hide sugar. In his words: "Most protein bars are candy bars in gym clothes." "I check the back of every pack." "Tastes like a brownie, not a supplement."
- Priya, 32, an office worker in Gurugram. She wants a 4 pm snack that is sweet without the sugar. What stops her: price for a daily habit. In her words: "I need something at 4 pm that isn't a samosa." "Healthy snacks always hide sugar somewhere."

**Data (test):**

Product: a box of 6 protein bars, 12 g protein per bar, no added sugar, sweetened with dates, ₹540 per box. Approved claims: those in the previous sentence and "ingredients listed on the front of the pack". No comparisons with named competitors. Audience: urban gym-goers and office workers 22 to 40 who distrust "healthy" snacks with hidden sugar.
