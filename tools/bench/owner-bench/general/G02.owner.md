# G02 owner notes (judges only)

**Signals in the data:**
1. **Recruiting teams are the segment to win.** They are 5.5% of signups (2,100 of 38,000) but convert to paid at 9.4% with 7.8 seats, so about 1,540 new paid seats, roughly 57% of all new paid seats in the period (about 2,710). Coaching has 14,200 signups but only about 170 paid seats. A great answer picks recruiting and shows this math, instead of picking the biggest segment by signups.
2. **The hook is the customer's phrase, not "infrastructure."** 41% of survey answers name back and forth over email; Priya says "email tennis." Open source is a reason for only 7%. A great answer builds the hero around ending email tennis with candidates, keeps open source as supporting proof, and leads with round robin and routing forms (used by 88% and 74% of paying recruiting accounts), which the current page buries in section 6.
3. **An under-used asset that removes the main blocker.** The security page is visited by 48% of recruiting accounts that later pay, but is only in the footer. Priya says security will ask first. A great answer puts security proof near the demo button, and trims the 9-field demo form.

**What would delight me:**
- A clear segment choice with the seat math in two lines.
- A hero that uses Priya's words, e.g. ending email tennis, with a subhead naming round robin that respects time off.
- Sections in a sensible order: hero, how candidates book, fair load across interviewers, routing, security, proof, demo.
- Two hero variants that test genuinely different ideas (pain vs outcome), not word swaps.
- One deciding metric, such as demo requests or team signups from recruiting visitors, not clicks.

**What would annoy me:**
- Choosing coaching or "everyone" as the segment.
- Leading with "open source infrastructure" for a recruiter.
- Made-up customer logos, quotes, stats or integration claims presented as real.
- "#1", "the only", exclamation marks in buttons, or attacks on Calendly.
- Ten near-identical headlines.

**Facts a careful answer gets right:**
- Recruiting paid conversion 9.4% with 7.8 seats; about 1,540 seats, more than half of new paid seats.
- 41% cite back and forth; 7% cite open source.
- Routing forms 74%, round robin 88% among paid recruiting accounts.
- Security page seen by 48% of recruiting accounts that paid; only linked in the footer.
