# G08 owner notes (judges only)

**Signals in the data:**
1. **Churn is concentrated in Essentials with 8 or more channels.** 9.6% monthly churn vs 3.2% for 1 to 3 channels and 2.6% for Team at the same size; 52% blame per-channel cost and 21% wanted approvals, which Team already has. A great answer targets this group, for example a capped or flat bundle for 8+ channels, or a clear path to Team for freelancers, and does not raise prices on the small-business base.
2. **Yearly billing is under-used.** Only 19% pay yearly, yet yearly payers churn 0.9% a month vs 4.0%. The page defaults to monthly and never offers yearly after signup. A great answer includes a cheap, honest push to yearly (show yearly by default or offer it in-app after 60 days), which is a faster win than a price change.
3. **The grandfathering promise shapes the math.** Existing customers keep prices for 12 months with 60 days' notice, and the board wants results within two quarters. A great answer sees that any new price affects only new customers for a year, so the first-two-quarters gain must come from churn reduction and yearly uptake, not from repricing the base.

**What would delight me:**
- One clear recommendation with revenue math using our numbers.
- Seeing that the Essentials 8+ group is about $257,000 a month and losing about 9.6% of customers a month, and showing what cutting that churn is worth.
- A customer message in our voice: what changes, who it affects, why, and that current prices are kept.
- A first test with a stop rule (for example, a flat 8+ bundle offered to new signups and to at-risk 8+ customers, stop if net revenue per account falls by more than a set amount).
- Honest risks, such as cannibalizing Team.

**What would annoy me:**
- A menu of five pricing models with no pick.
- Raising prices across the board, or ignoring the 12-month promise.
- Generic "value-based pricing" theory.
- Dark patterns or fake urgency in the messaging.
- Wrong revenue math.

**Facts a careful answer gets right:**
- Essentials 8+: 3,900 customers x 11 channels x $6 is about $257,400 a month; 9.6% churn loses about 374 customers, about $24,700 of monthly revenue, each month.
- If that churn fell to the 4.1% of the 4-to-7 band, about 214 more customers stay each month.
- Yearly payers 19%; churn 0.9% vs 4.0% a month.
- Existing prices held 12 months, with 60 days' notice.
