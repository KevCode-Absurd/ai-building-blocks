# Meta budget decisions: 14 to 27 November

## The decision

Meta lost money in the last 14 days: ₹5,80,000 spent for ₹1,21,700 less gross profit than it cost, on the store's count. Even on Meta's own count it lost ₹28,600. No ad set earns a raise today. Scaling comes through the reserve rules, once ASC proves itself again.

- **Calls:** 0 scale, 3 hold, 2 fix, 3 cut.
- **Next 14 days:** ₹4,10,900 committed (₹29,350 a day) plus a ₹1,49,100 reserve released only by rule. Total cap ₹5,60,000.
- **Forecast at today's costs (projection):** store-count profit after ads moves from −₹1,21,700 to −₹33,100. On Meta's count, from −₹28,600 to +₹41,600. Meta-reported purchases fall from 1,425 to about 1,120. Fewer orders, much less loss.
- **Upside (projection):** if ASC returns to its weeks 1 to 3 cost of ₹262 to ₹281, the plan ends between +₹13,000 and +₹32,000 on the store's count.
- **First moves, Saturday 14 November:** turn "Dad's razor vs mine" back on inside ASC. Pause Starter Kit and Gift Sets. Narrow Women Body Razor to ages 25 to 34.

## What one order is worth

- Delivered order: ₹899 − ₹342 product (38%) − ₹95 fees = ₹462.
- Prepaid order placed: 98% delivered, 2% returned at ₹160 each = **₹450**.
- COD order placed: 78% delivered × ₹462 − 22% refused × ₹160 = **₹325**.
- At your 45% COD mix: **₹394** per order placed. Each ad set below uses its own order value, COD share and refusal rate.

Assumptions:
1. Meta claims 1,425 purchases; Shopify shows 1,020 Meta-tagged orders (72%). For prospecting I count 86% of Meta's purchases as real, the midpoint, because UTMs miss some orders from other devices and app browsers. For retargeting and catalog I use 72%, because those shoppers were already on the site.
2. A returned or refused parcel comes back sellable, so only the ₹160 shipping is lost.
3. The 14-day budget is ₹12,00,000 × 14 / 30 = ₹5,60,000.

Break-even cost per purchase, on Meta's count = profit per order × that share.

**Why, in rupees:**

- **ASC, fix the creative, keep the budget.** Cost per purchase rose every week: ₹262, ₹270, ₹281, ₹318, ₹340, ₹348. The jump began when "Dad's razor vs mine" was paused 18 days ago. That ad bought 560 purchases at ₹250, the best cost in the account. Turn it back on and add one ad on Rohan's line "No razor burn after a week."
- **Starter Kit, cut.** 74% of orders are COD and 36% of those are refused, so an order is worth only ₹271. It needs ₹233 per purchase and pays ₹463. ASC still sells the kit. Keep COD on the site: Rohan "ordered COD, wanted to see it first."
- **Women Body Razor, fix.** Ages 25 to 34 buy at ₹414. The other ages spent ₹46,000 for 30 purchases (₹1,533 each) and caused ₹34,800 of the ₹37,100 loss. Run 25 to 34 only. Add one ad on Ananya's line "Finally not a flimsy pink razor."
- **Beard Kit, hold.** ₹29 over break-even per purchase. Frequency 3.4, so it gets a new ad before more money.
- **Retargeting, cut by half.** Frequency 6.8 in 14 days at ₹310 CPM. Shrink it to 7-day add-to-cart and checkout visitors, excluding 30-day buyers. Catalog keeps product viewers who did not add to cart.
- **UGC test, hold.** 1.9% CTR at ₹150 CPM is the cheapest attention in the account, but 31 purchases is too few to judge. ₹4,000 a day buys about 50 purchases a week.
- **Gift Sets, cut.** ₹976 per purchase against a ₹533 break-even.
- **Catalog, hold.** The only ad set in profit on the store's count.

## How the reserve is released

- **ASC:** add ₹5,000 a day when its last 3 days cost ₹300 or less per purchase and 7-day frequency is under 2.5. Raise at most once every 3 days. If the 3-day cost then goes above ₹339, take that raise back. Weeks 1 to 3 ran ₹262 to ₹281 with the revived ad live, so this line is reachable.
- **UGC test:** on day 7, if its 7-day cost is ₹360 or less, move its best 2 ads into ASC and send its ₹4,000 a day with them. If it is above ₹500, pause it and return the money to the reserve.
- **Women 25 to 34:** if the 7-day cost is ₹330 or less, add ₹1,000 a day once.
- Reserve not released by 27 November stays unspent.

## Day-to-day rules for the team

1. At 11 am, check one number per ad set: last 3 days cost per purchase on Meta's count against its break-even. Nothing else moves a budget.
2. More than 20% over break-even for 3 days: cut that budget by 30%. For 7 days: pause it. Never pause ASC; give it new ads.
3. Change each ad set at most once every 3 days. Leave weak ads alone inside an ad set beating break-even; Meta rotates them.
4. Every Monday, add 3 new ads to ASC or the UGC test: product in use within 2 seconds, one benefit, real people, Rohan's or Ananya's words. Never "painless", "zero cuts" or "dermatologist approved". Pause a new ad after ₹1,000 spend with 0 or 1 purchases.
5. Every Monday, from the courier report, pause any ad with 50 or more orders and over 30% of its COD orders refused.
6. Retargeting or catalog frequency over 4 in 7 days: shorten the audience window. Prospecting frequency over 3 in 14 days: add new ads.
7. Every Monday, divide Shopify's Meta-tagged orders by Meta's purchases. If it falls below 65%, lower every break-even by the same ratio.
8. Hard caps: ₹40,000 a day in total and ₹5,60,000 for the 14 days.

## Bolder move: find out if retargeting pays at all

Run retargeting at ₹1,000 a day only in states that gave you half of the last 90 days' orders. The other half gets none. ₹14,000 moves to the reserve.

- **Read on 27 November:** retargeting pays only if the lit half beats the dark half by at least 39 Shopify orders (₹14,000 ÷ ₹358 profit per retargeting order), allowing for each half's usual share of orders.
- **If it fails:** move all retargeting money to ASC.
- **Risk:** if every retargeting sale were real, the dark half loses about 35 orders, or ₹12,500 of profit. That is less than the ₹14,000 saved.
- **First step:** pull 90 days of Shopify orders by state and split the states into two halves.
