# Brooklinen: 90-day growth plan

16 Nov 2026 to 14 Feb 2027. Goal metric: contribution margin after ads, as a share of revenue.

## The short answer

- The slowdown is a new-customer cost problem, centred on Meta and the linen sheet set.
- A first order costs $104 in ads and earns $71. Meta costs $121, up 68%.
- Contribution after ads is about 7.8% of revenue, down from 13.6% a year ago. Day-90 target: 12.9% (range 10.5% to 15.9%).
- Bad news: a Meta customer bought today loses money even after 18 months of reorders. Almost one in five linen sets comes back.
- This week: check Meta tracking against store orders, pull linen from Meta prospecting, and switch the cart and browse emails back on.

## What is causing the slowdown

### What the numbers prove

1. **It is a new-customer problem.** New orders fell 11%; returning orders rose 6%; conversion held at 2.1%.
2. **Meta is where the cost went.** Meta rose from $72 to $121 per first order; Google and other rose $4 each. With Meta at $72, blended cost would be $79, not $104.
3. **We spend more to get less.** Spend is up 19% (0.89 × $104 ÷ $78) for 11% fewer new customers.
4. **First orders lose money.** $188 × (52% − 14%) = $71, against $104. With the 31% who reorder within 18 months, a new customer is worth at least $94 ($71 × 1.31). Meta pays $121.
5. **Linen has a product fault.** Returns rose from 7% to 18%, for one reason: "Shrank after first wash." No other product moved more than a point. Linen is 16% of revenue and gets 40% of Meta spend.
6. **Email capture broke.** Sign-ups fell from 2.4% to 0.8% of new visitors after the pop-up went in March, and conversion did not rise. Browse and cart flows have been off since January. Flows earn 9% of email revenue; a common practitioner yardstick is about half.

### What still needs checking (weeks 1 and 2)

1. **Did the January platform move break Meta tracking?** Compare Meta's reported first orders with store-side new customers by week.
2. **Are linen ads the costly part of Meta?** Cost per first order by ad set.
3. **Why linen shrinks:** new fabric lot, finishing change or care label? Returns by production lot.
4. **Do linen-first buyers reorder less?** Repeat rate by first product.
5. **Did linen returns push up the 14% cost line?** If so, last year's margin was better than shown.

### Baseline, with working

New customers' share of revenue solves w × (−11%) + (1 − w) × 6% = −4%: 59% a year ago, 55% now. Ad spend share = that share × cost per first order ÷ $188: 24.4% then, 30.2% now. Contribution = 52% − 14% − ad share: 13.6% then, 7.8% now.

Assumptions: (1) order values for new and returning buyers held flat year on year; (2) email is 20 of the 22 points of email and SMS revenue; (3) half the revenue from rebuilt flows is truly new.

**The constraint:** a first order costs $104 and earns $71. Meta got 68% dearer, and 40% of its money sells the one product with a returns fault.

Moves 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 total 2.7 / 5.1 / 8.1 points: contribution of 10.5% / 12.9% / 15.9%. Move 4 lowers the same number as move 1, so it is not added.

**1. Reset Meta.** Add a post-purchase question, "Where did you first hear about us?", and judge Meta on store-side new customers plus the survey. Move linen's Meta budget (22% of paid spend) to classic and sateen, which return 5% and 6%. Lead with "Worth it after a year of washing." Keep Sarah's "nice hotel" line out of ads; it reads as a hotel-quality claim. Cut any ad set above $94 per first order for 14 days. Black Friday is in week 2: hold the cap, no sitewide discount. Sizing: Meta spend is 16.6% of revenue; the same customers at $100 saves 16.6 × (1 − 100 ÷ 121) = 2.9 points.

**2. Rebuild browse and cart flows.** Browse: five emails showing the product viewed. Cart: trigger on "added to cart", first email 30 to 45 minutes later, six emails over ten days. No codes; answer doubts on feel and washing. Sizing: flows at 20% of email revenue add 2.75% of revenue × 38% contribution × half new = 0.5 points.

**3. Bring back email sign-up.** A light form that loads after the page, opens after 15 seconds, and never covers the first mobile screen. Split 50/50 against no form, with conversion and page speed as guardrails. Offer early news of new colours, not a discount. Sizing: the welcome flow earns about 1.8% of revenue; sign-ups from 0.8% to 2.0% add 2.7% of revenue × 38% × half = 0.5 points.

**4. Answer Sarah's doubt.** She balks at $200 or more for sheets she has not touched. Put feel, weight and real reviews that mention washing beside the price on classic and sateen. Split test on paid traffic, with return rate as guardrail. Sizing: conversion from 2.1% to 2.2% makes paid customers 4.5% cheaper: 30.2 × (1 − 2.1 ÷ 2.2) = 1.4 points.

**5. Fix linen shrink.** Week 1: wash-test five sets three times; hold any lot with above-average shrink returns. Week 3: put the measured shrink and care line on the linen page and order email, and point no-shrink shoppers to sateen. Then fix it with the supplier by pre-washing or a size allowance. Sizing: 16% of revenue × 9 points fewer returns × 60% of price saved per avoided return (38% if resold, 86% if written off) = 0.9 points.

**6. "New stuff" emails.** Mike: "I'd buy more if I heard about new stuff." A post-purchase flow (care tips, then towels or a second set at 60 days) and a monthly "new this month" email. Sizing: returning customers are 45% of revenue; 4% more of their orders adds 1.8% of revenue × 38% × half = 0.3 points.

Budget: no new ad money. Meta savings are held, released only to a channel under $94 per first order on two reads.

## Stop doing

1. Promoting linen on Meta until new-shipment returns stay under 10% for four weeks.
2. Funding any ad set above $94 per first order.
3. Judging Meta on its own reported orders.
4. Trading sign-ups for speed: it cost two-thirds of sign-ups and no conversion.

## Bolder move: a swatch kit as Meta's front door

Sell a fabric swatch kit on Meta, priced at cost plus postage and fully credited to a first sheet order. First step: 10% of Meta budget for four weeks from week 5. Size: if kit buyers become first orders at $80 all-in, the test saves 0.6 points; across all of Meta, 5.6. Risk: buyers stop at the kit. Stop if kit-led first orders cost over $94 at week 8.
