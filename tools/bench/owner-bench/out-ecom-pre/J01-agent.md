# Meta budget decisions: 14 to 27 November

## The decision

Cut two ad sets, fix four, hold two and relaunch your best ad. Spend ₹3,78,000 for sure over the next 14 days. Keep ₹1,82,000 in reserve for Advantage+ shopping (ASC), released only when it sells at or under ₹282 a purchase. On the store's own order count, the last 14 days lost ₹1,85,300 on first orders. The new split should cut that loss to about ₹63,200 and still bring in about 790 Meta-tagged orders, against 1,020 now.

The number that drives it: **Meta claims 1,425 purchases, but Shopify tagged only 1,020 orders to Meta.** So only 72% of what Meta reports turns up as a store order. Every target below is set on that basis.

## How break-even is worked out

One worked example, ASC:

1. Order value: ₹5,36,000 / 610 = ₹879.
2. Profit on a delivered order: ₹879 minus 38% product cost minus ₹95 shipping and fees = **₹450**.
3. Prepaid order: 98% kept, 2% returned at ₹160 = **₹438**.
4. COD order: 81% delivered, 19% refused at ₹160 each = **₹334**.
5. ASC is 58% prepaid and 42% COD, so one placed order is worth **₹394**.
6. Meta's purchases × 0.72 = store orders. So the most ASC can pay per purchase **on Meta's numbers is ₹394 × 0.72 = ₹282.**
7. Last 14 days: 610 × 0.72 = 437 store orders × ₹394 = ₹1,72,100, minus ₹2,10,000 spend = **minus ₹37,900.**

Each ad set gets the same sum with its own order value, COD share and refusal rate.

## Day-to-day rules for the team

1. **One target per ad set:** the break-even column. Judge on the last 7 days. Check daily; act only when a rule says so.
2. **Release the reserve to ASC.** From day 4, when ASC's last 3 days cost ₹282 or less, raise it ₹3,000 a day, at most once every 3 days. After each raise, divide extra spend by extra purchases. Above ₹282, go back and stop raising.
3. **Cut.** If an ad set's 7-day cost is 25% or more above its break-even, cut its budget 30%. Pause it if it stays above break-even for 14 days.
4. **New ads.** Pause any new ad at ₹850 spend (3 × ₹282) with 0 or 1 purchase. Move a concept into ASC when it reaches 30 purchases at ₹282 or less.
5. **Leave winners alone.** Do not switch off weaker ads inside an ad set that hits target. One budget change per ad set every 3 days at most.
6. **Frequency.** If retargeting or catalog goes above 4 in 7 days, cut it 20%.
7. **Every Monday, recheck two numbers.** Shopify Meta-tagged orders divided by Meta purchases (now 0.72): if it moves 0.05 or more, redo the break-even table. Courier refusals by ad set, on orders placed 14 days earlier: if one rises 10 points, redo that row.

## The payment lever

A prepaid order is worth ₹450 and a COD order ₹325, using store averages. That is ₹124 more per order that switches. On the starter kit the gap is ₹201 (₹419 against ₹218). A prepaid reward under ₹124 pays on orders that switch, but buyers who already prepay get it too. Rohan "wanted to see it first", so keep COD and give a reason to prepay. You decide the offer.

## What the data does not tell us

- The 0.72 is one read, from UTM tags. Tags miss some real sales (views, device switches), so the truth sits between the two profit columns. Calls that hold on both counts are firm: cut Starter Kit and Gift Sets, fix Women Body Razor. Calls that depend on the gap get lower spend, not more.
- Retargeting and catalog credit is usually the most inflated, so their rows are best case.
- The ASC rise lines up with the paused ad. That is timing, not proof. The relaunch tests it.
- First orders only. The export has no repeat-purchase data.

## Assumptions

30-day month, so ₹5,60,000 for 14 days. Each ad set's costs scale with its own order value. A returned prepaid order costs ₹160 and loses the sale, like a refusal. No stock limit (the warehouse can ship 3x).
