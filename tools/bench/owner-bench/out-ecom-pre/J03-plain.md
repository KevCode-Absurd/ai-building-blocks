# Eight Meta ad concepts: cabin suitcase

## What the numbers tell us

- Men 25 to 40 take 70% of spend (₹4,20,000 of ₹6,00,000). They cost ₹1,615 per purchase with a 0.8% CTR.
- Women 25 to 40 cost ₹1,058 per purchase, about 35% less, with a 1.4% CTR. They get only 18% of spend but bring 26% of purchases (104 of 396).
- Your biggest buyer group, work flyers (34%), buys to skip the baggage belt. Your top review theme is quiet wheels (290 mentions). Neither shows up in a flat product shot on a colour background.
- When every ad looks the same, Meta has little to choose between. So each concept below uses a different angle, scene and format.

## Assumptions

- Claims stay inside the approved specs: polycarbonate shell, 360-degree spinner wheels, TSA-style lock, "fits most Indian domestic airline cabin limits", "about 2.6 kg", ₹6,999. Warranty and returns are pointed to the site, never stated.
- Lines in quotes come from your review table. Pull the exact review text before launch. Lines from your customer research are used as script ideas, not as named testimonials.
- Creator ads use real buyers or clearly labelled paid partners.
- Scuffs show on dark colours (85 reviews). Lifestyle concepts lead with lighter colours.

## The eight concepts

### 1. Out before the belt
- **Angle:** Cabin-only means you leave first.
- **For:** Work flyers, mostly men 25 to 40. Problem aware: they hate the belt wait but may not see the bag as the fix.
- **Hook:** On screen at second zero, over a crowded baggage belt: "Out of the airport before the belt starts."
- **Format:** Reels 9:16, 15 seconds, first-person view. 4:5 cut for Feed.
- **Visual:** Off the plane, case rolling beside him. Past the belt crowd. Cab door shuts. The case is in every shot.
- **Primary text:**
  Two flights a month. Zero belt time.
  Cabin size. Spinner wheels. About 2.6 kg.
  Land. Roll. Leave.
  Built for the way you travel now.
- **Tests:** Does time saved beat looks for men 25 to 40? Judge against their ₹1,615 cost per purchase.

### 2. No drama at the gate
- **Angle:** Remove the fear of a failed gate check.
- **For:** Flyers who want to go cabin-only but do not trust the size. Solution aware.
- **Hook:** "Will it pass the gate check?" Then the case slides into a cabin sizer.
- **Format:** Reels, 9 seconds, one take, made to loop. Plus a 1:1 static with size call-outs.
- **Visual:** Close shot of the case entering the sizer. Dimensions from the product page on screen. Small line: "Check your airline's limit."
- **Primary text:**
  "Fit the overhead bin, no drama at the gate."
  Sized to fit most Indian domestic airline cabin limits.
  Check yours. Then pack and go.
- **Tests:** Does tackling the top worry lift CTR among people already sold on cabin-only?

### 3. The 5 am exit
- **Angle:** Wheels so quiet you notice.
- **For:** Everyone. Unaware: nobody shops for quiet wheels until they hear them.
- **Hook:** Sound on, no music. A case glides down a dark hallway at 5 am. Text: "5 am. Nobody woke up."
- **Format:** Reels 9:16, 10 seconds, sound-led. Captions for sound-off viewers.
- **Visual:** Low shot on the wheels in a sleeping home, then a lift lobby, then a cab. Soft morning light. Product centre frame.
- **Primary text:**
  The 5 am exit. No rattle.
  360-degree spinner wheels that just glide.
  Quiet wheels are the top theme in our 1,150 reviews.
- **Tests:** Does a sound hook stop the scroll better than a product shot? Watch 3-second view rate and CTR first, then cost per purchase.

### 4. Lift it yourself
- **Angle:** You can handle it alone.
- **For:** Women 25 to 40 who travel solo. Problem aware: heavy bags put them off.
- **Hook:** First three seconds: she lifts the case into the overhead bin in one move. Text: "No asking for help."
- **Format:** Reels creator video, 20 seconds, selfie style.
- **Visual:** Home door, cab, aisle, the lift, then she sits down. "About 2.6 kg" on screen at the lift.
- **Primary text:**
  Overhead bin. One lift. Done.
  About 2.6 kg, with smooth spinner wheels.
  For weekends on your own terms.
- **Tests:** Your cheapest audience got generic ads. Can a message made for them scale while holding near ₹1,058?

### 5. Where is it from?
- **Angle:** Design that gets noticed.
- **For:** Couples on weekend trips (29% of buyers) and design-minded solo travellers. Product aware: they browse for good-looking luggage.
- **Hook:** First card: "People ask where it's from."
- **Format:** Carousel 4:5, five cards.
- **Visual:** One colour per card, each in a real trip moment: a hotel doorway, a station platform, a hill road, a beach-house step. Lighter colours first. Last card: all colours side by side.
- **Primary text:**
  Pick the one that suits your trip.
  Polycarbonate shell. Clean lines. Quiet wheels.
  Thoughtful design for the modern Indian traveller.
- **Tests:** Same looks angle as today, new setting. Do real travel scenes beat flat colour backgrounds? This is the direct test of your current style.

### 6. The per-trip maths
- **Angle:** Reframe the ₹6,999 price.
- **For:** Frequent flyers who visited but did not buy. Product aware. Run to site visitors.
- **Hook:** "₹6,999. Or about ₹292 a trip."
- **Format:** Static 4:5 and 1:1.
- **Visual:** The case beside an airport seat. Large, clean type with the number.
- **Primary text:**
  Two flights a month is 24 trips a year.
  That is about ₹292 a trip in year one.
  Warranty and returns are on our site.
- **Tests:** Is price the real block for people who looked and left? Watch cost per purchase in retargeting.

### 7. What 1,150 buyers say
- **Angle:** Proof from real buyers.
- **For:** Most aware: site visitors, cart abandoners, and people 41 and over.
- **Hook:** "4.6 stars from 1,150 reviews."
- **Format:** Carousel, one card per review theme.
- **Visual:** Card 1: the rating. Then wheels (290 mentions), cabin fit (210), easy to lift (160), looks (150). Each card pairs a real review line with a real photo of the case in use.
- **Primary text:**
  We asked. You told us.
  Quiet wheels. Fits the cabin. Easy to lift. Looks sharp.
  4.6 stars from 1,150 reviews.
- **Tests:** Does proof cut cost in retargeting? Does it help the 41-plus group, now at ₹2,188 per purchase?

### 8. For the one who is always flying out
- **Angle:** A gift that gets used.
- **For:** Gift buyers (15% of buyers) shopping for a partner or parent who travels. Product aware.
- **Hook:** "For the one who's always on a flight."
- **Format:** Reels, 12 seconds, plus a 4:5 static.
- **Visual:** The case handed over at home. The receiver spins it, rolls it across the floor and smiles. Warm, real home light.
- **Primary text:**
  For the one who is always on a flight.
  A cabin bag that looks good and rolls quiet.
  Pick their colour.
- **Tests:** Is gifting its own pocket of demand? If it works, bring it back for festivals and birthdays.

## Make these three first

**1. Out before the belt (Concept 1).** This is where most money goes. Men 25 to 40 take 70% of spend at the highest cost per purchase in the 25 to 40 range. Work flyers are your biggest buyer group, and their reason to buy is missing from today's ads. Small gains here matter most. As an illustration only: at the same spend, ₹1,300 per purchase instead of ₹1,615 would mean about 63 more purchases a month.

**2. Lift it yourself (Concept 4).** Women 25 to 40 already convert best on ads not made for them. A message built around their main reason to buy is the cleanest way to learn if this audience can take more budget at a good price.

**3. The 5 am exit (Concept 3).** Quiet wheels are your most common review theme. It speaks to both audiences. And a sound-led Reel looks and feels nothing like your current ads, which gives Meta a truly new option.

**How to run them:** Put the three in one campaign with broad targeting. Keep your current best ad running as the control. Judge on cost per purchase, not CTR alone. Give each concept at least three times your current blended cost per purchase (₹1,515, so about ₹4,500) before you cut or scale it. Then bring in Concepts 2 and 5 next, and use 6 and 7 for retargeting.
