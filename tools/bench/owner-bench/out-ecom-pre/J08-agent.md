# Blue Tokai win-back programme: lapsed coffee buyers

## The answer in brief

- **Fix delivery before you message the people it drove away.** Late coffee is the top reason subscribers left: 39% of 2,400, or 936 people. On-time delivery fell from 95% to 79% after the June courier change.
- **Start with people who have a clear reason to return:** the just-lapsed, then the 5,200 who bought Monsoon Dark, a coffee we stopped. Then everyone else, newest first.
- **Lead with help, not money.** "Tell us how you brew, and we'll suggest a coffee" answers Aarav's "I don't want to choose from 40 coffees again." The most anyone gets is 10% off, on the last step. That is inside the 15% team limit.
- **Measure with a 10% random holdout,** on gross profit per customer, not the email tool's revenue.
- **Worth:** about ₹2.2 to ₹6.7 lakh gross profit from lapsed buyers, plus ₹3.3 lakh per 100 returning subscribers. Planning figures, not forecasts.

## Who counts as lapsed

The median gap between first and second order is 34 days. So a customer is lapsed at **68 days** (2 x 34). At 46 to 67 days they get a normal reorder reminder instead.

The 46 to 90 band is not split in the data. I assume an even spread: 9,000 x 23/45 = **4,600** at 68 to 90 days. Cancelled subscribers sit inside these bands; take them out and use the subscriber track below.

## Offer logic

**Gross profit per order:** ₹1,150 x 58% = ₹667, minus ₹110 shipping and fees = **₹557**. A 10% code costs ₹115 and leaves ₹442. 15% costs ₹173 and leaves ₹384. Nothing above 10% is needed to start.

| Group | Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Their coffee, a brew tip | Brew match | No offer |
| B | "It's gone; let us pick your next coffee" | Our closest current roast | 10% off |
| C | What's new in their roast | Brew match | 10% off |
| D | Beans matched to their gear | Brew match | 10% off |
| E | Brew match | The roast they bought, named | 10% off |
| F | Stay or go | None | None |

**Rule for the code:** it also reaches people who would have bought anyway, at ₹115 each. A true extra order earns ₹442. So the code pays only if more than 1 in 5 code orders is a true extra (115 / 557 = 21%). Every end date is real and never reset.

## Measuring true lift

1. **Holdout:** before Wave 1, pick 10% of each group at random by customer ID. They get no win-back messages but the same regular campaigns.
2. **Read at Day 60** of each wave: gross profit per customer, after discounts and shipping, contacted minus held out. Also the share who ordered. Guardrails: unsubscribes, spam complaints, WhatsApp blocks.
3. **Size:** the pooled holdout for groups A to E is about 2,080 (10% of 20,832). Working figure: if 5 in 100 return on their own, a 2-point lift needs 16 x 0.05 x 0.95 / 0.02² = 1,900 per side. The pooled read clears that. Single groups do not (C's holdout is about 730), so read them as direction only.
4. **WhatsApp test:** among people opted in to both, half get Day 6 on WhatsApp, half by email. Keep WhatsApp if its extra gross profit beats its cost.
5. **Decision rule, set now:** keep a group's sequence if contacted beats held out on gross profit per customer. If the range includes zero, run it again. If negative, cut the offer step.

## What it is worth

Contacted in groups A to E: 20,832 x 90% = **18,750**. Each extra returning customer is worth ₹442 (assumes they used the code), plus a 28% chance of one more order (your repeat rate) x ₹557 = ₹156. Total **₹598**.

|---|---|---|
| 2 | 375 | ₹2.2 lakh |
| 4 | 750 | ₹4.5 lakh |
| 6 | 1,125 | ₹6.7 lakh |

**Subscribers:** one who returns for 6 monthly deliveries is worth 6 x ₹557 = ₹3,342, or ₹3.3 lakh per 100. If 240 return (10% of 2,400), that is ₹8.0 lakh.

**The bigger prize:** 60,000 customers in 24 months is about 30,000 a year. Each point of 12-month repeat rate is worth about 300 x ₹557 = ₹1.7 lakh a year. Back from 28% to 34% is about ₹10 lakh a year in second orders alone.
