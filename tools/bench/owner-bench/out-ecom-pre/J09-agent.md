# Brooklinen: 90-day growth plan

## The answer first

The slowdown is a new-customer problem, and most of it traces to Meta. Returning orders are up 6%. New orders are down 11% because each first order now costs $104, up from $78. Meta alone went from $72 to $121. Had Meta held at $72, blended cost would be about $79 today.

The second leak is linen. Its return rate jumped from 7% to 18%, mostly "shrank after first wash", and 40% of Meta spend promotes it.

The plan: cap what we pay for a new customer, fix linen before selling more, and switch lost email flows and sign-ups back on. Paid spend does not rise.

**Goal metric:** contribution after ads, as a share of revenue. My estimate: 13.6% a year ago, 7.8% now. Working target for week 13: 11%.

## What the numbers prove

1. **Growth stalled at the front door.** New orders down 11%, returning orders up 6%.
2. **Meta explains nearly all of the cost rise.** Google and other channels each rose $4. Meta rose $49.
3. **Every first order now loses money.** A $188 first order keeps $71.44 after product cost (52% margin) and shipping, fees and returns (14%). At $104 to acquire, that is a $33 loss. A year ago it was $7.
4. **Meta buyers do not earn it back.** Add one repeat order for the 31% who return within 18 months: $71.44 + (31% x $71.44) = $93.59. That is break-even, so the cap is $94. Meta ($121) is above it. Google ($92) is just under. Other ($80) clears it.
5. **Linen is the one product that broke.** Its return rate rose 11 points; every other line moved 0 or 1. Weighted by revenue, linen adds about 1.8 of the 1.9-point rise in overall returns. Sarah: "Returned the linen, it shrank."
6. **Email capture fell by two thirds.** Sign-ups dropped from 2.4% to 0.8% of new visitors after the pop-up came off. Conversion stayed at 2.1%, so the faster site did not sell more.
7. **Browse and cart flows have been off since January.** Flows bring only 9% of email revenue, all from the welcome flow.

## What still needs checking (weeks 1 and 2)

- **Is linen driving Meta's cost?** Split Meta cost per first order into linen and non-linen ad sets.
- **Why linen shrinks now, and who returns it.** Wash-test current stock against last year's spec; ask the supplier what changed; split returns by new and repeat buyers.
- **What browse and cart flows earned last year.** This replaces my working figure for move 3.
- **Is Meta's $121 real?** Check it against a post-purchase "How did you hear about us?" question.
- **Returning-order value,** and whether revenue is net of refunds.

## The moves, ranked

**Unit:** gain in contribution, in points of current revenue, at the week-13 run rate. Every $10 off blended cost per first order is worth 2.9 points ($10 / $188 x 54.5% of revenue from first orders).

| 1 | Re-cut paid to a $94 cap | Paid media lead | 2 | 1.5, 2.9, 4.3 | 1.45 |
| 2 | Fix linen shrink and say it plainly | Product lead, with CX | 2 | 0.7, 1.0, 1.8 | 0.50 |
| 3 | Rebuild browse and cart flows | CRM lead | 1 | 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 | 0.38 |

Effort 1 is one owner, under two weeks; 2 is several teams or a supplier. Score is likely gain divided by effort. The gains overlap (pausing linen ads shrinks what move 2 saves; move 4 feeds move 3), so together they are likely worth about 4 points. I plan on 3: 7.8% to about 11%.

**1. Re-cut paid to a $94 cap.** Pause linen prospecting on Meta until move 2 passes its wash test. Cut any ad set above $94 once it has spent $940 (ten first orders at the cap). Refresh classic and sateen creative around Sarah's moment: a first home, her own bed, a plain price. Move freed budget to Google in 20% weekly steps while added spend stays under $94. Sizing: Meta at $110, $100 or $90 takes blended cost to $99, $94 or $89.
*Lines:* Meta at or under $110 by week 4; blended at or under $96 by week 8. Miss either and cut Meta further.

**2. Fix linen shrink.** Wash test and supplier call in week 1. By week 2, the linen page states the measured shrink and care steps next to the size picker, and points buyers who want no shrink to sateen or classic. Sizing: low is a 10% return rate with returns resold (8 points x 16% of revenue x 52% margin). High is back to 7% with washed returns written off (11 x 16% x 100%).
*Lines:* returns on new orders at or under 12% by week 4 and 10% by week 8, or stop selling the batch.

**3. Rebuild browse and cart flows.** Trigger on viewed product, added to cart and started checkout, with a 10% holdout. Sizing: flows earn about 2% of revenue today (9% x 22%). Working figure: rebuilt flows add 0.5%, 1% or 2% of revenue at 38% contribution.
*Lines:* live with holdout by week 3; beating the holdout by week 8, or rewrite.

**4. Bring back email sign-up, built fast.** The form loads after the page, never shifts the layout, and shows on a second page view. Lead with fit help ("Which sheets suit the way you sleep?"), not a discount. Split 50/50 against no form, with conversion as the guardrail. Sizing: the welcome flow earns about 2% of revenue at 0.8% sign-ups. At 1.6%, 2.0% or 2.4%, counting a quarter, a quarter or half of the new welcome revenue as extra, it adds 0.5%, 0.75% or 2% of revenue at 38%.
*Lines:* sign-ups at 1.6% or more by week 7 with conversion held, or test an offer.

**5. Tell repeat buyers what is new.** Mike: "I'd buy more if I heard about new stuff." Build a post-purchase flow: care tips at day 7, matching towels at day 30, a second set at day 60. Add a monthly "new in" email sorted by what each buyer owns. Sizing: returning orders are about 45.5% of revenue; a 1%, 3% or 5% lift, at 38%.
*Lines:* returning orders above +6% year on year by week 8, or change the angle.

## What to stop

- **Stop putting 40% of Meta budget behind linen** while it returns at 18%.
- **Stop judging Meta on its own reported results.** Use blended new-customer cost.
- **Stop adding spend to chase revenue.** The board asked for contribution.
- **Do not reach for sitewide discounts.** 20% off a $188 order cuts its gross profit from $97.76 to $60.16, and breaks our own rule.

## Assumptions behind the sums

Returning orders average $188, which splits revenue 54.5% first orders, 45.5% returning. Cost per first order is paid spend divided by first orders. The 52% margin and 14% cost line held a year ago. Linen units follow its revenue share. Email and SMS together earn 22% of revenue.
