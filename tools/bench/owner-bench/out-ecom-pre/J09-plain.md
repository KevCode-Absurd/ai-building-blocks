# Brooklinen: 90-day growth plan

## The short answer

The slowdown is a new-customer problem, not a site or loyalty problem. We are paying a third more for each new customer, and almost all of that rise is Meta. Meta puts 40% of its budget behind the linen set, and linen returns jumped from 7% to 18% because it shrinks. On top of that, we stopped catching visitors who don't buy right away: the sign-up pop-up is gone and the abandonment emails are off. Fix the product, pay only for customers who pay back, and restart the free channels.

## What the numbers prove

1. **Acquisition broke; retention held.** New customer orders fell 11% while returning orders rose 6%. Conversion is flat at 2.1%.
2. **We spend about 19% more to get 11% fewer new customers.** 0.89 x ($104 / $78) = 1.19.
3. **Meta explains almost all of the cost rise.** With today's spend mix, if Meta had stayed at $72, blended cost would be about $79, not $104. Google moved $4. Other moved $4.
4. **Each new customer now loses money on the first order.** $188 order x 38% (52% margin less 14% shipping, fees and returns) = $71 of contribution before ads. Against $104 to acquire, we lose $33 per first order. A year ago, at today's order value, we lost $7.
5. **Meta customers do not pay back within 18 months.** With a 31% repeat rate, a new customer is worth about $94 over 18 months ($71 + 31% x $71). Meta at $121 loses $27 each. Google at $92 roughly breaks even. Other at $80 makes $14.
6. **Linen is leaking profit.** The extra 11 points of linen returns, on 16% of revenue, is about 1.8% of total revenue refunded. Washed, shrunk sheets are unlikely to be resold, so most of that is lost gross profit.
7. **Our free channels shrank.** Email sign-ups fell from 2.4% to 0.8% of new visitors, so list growth is down two-thirds. Flows earn only 9% of email revenue, because only the welcome flow is running.

## What still needs checking

| Question | Why it matters | How to check | Owner | By |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Why does linen shrink now? | Decides if we fix a batch, a supplier or a care label | Wash-test current stock against older stock; match returns to lot numbers; check supplier or finishing changes | Product and sourcing lead | Week 2 |
| Is Meta's rise caused by linen, tracking or tired ads? | Decides whether to fix, cut or rebuild | Split Meta cost per first order for linen vs other ads; compare Meta's purchase count to store orders since the January move; check when the rise started and ad frequency | Paid media lead | Week 1 |
| Did new-visitor traffic fall, or new-visitor conversion? | Tells us if the gap is reach or persuasion | Split traffic and conversion by new vs returning, year on year | Analytics lead | Week 1 |
| What did browse and cart flows earn before January? | Sets the target for the rebuild | Pull flow revenue for the 6 months before the move | CRM lead | Week 1 |
| What do repeat orders earn? | My payback math assumes they match first orders | Repeat order value and margin, last 12 months | Finance lead | Week 2 |

**Assumptions I made:** blended cost per first order is total paid spend divided by all first orders. Spend shares a year ago were close to today's (the math reproduces both $78 and $104, so this holds). Refunds sit inside the 14% cost line. Unit return rates roughly match revenue return rates. Repeaters place one repeat order in 18 months at first-order value and margin.

## The moves, ranked by gross profit impact and effort

| # | Move | Gross profit impact | Effort | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stop advertising linen until it passes a wash test, then fix it | High: up to about 1.8 points of revenue back, plus ad spend no longer wasted on returns | Low to pause; medium to fix | Product and sourcing lead, paid media lead |
| 2 | Reset paid spend to payback rules | High: every $100k cut from ad sets at $121 adds about $23k to $41k in contribution | Low | Paid media lead |
| 3 | Turn browse and cart abandonment flows back on | Medium to high: near-free revenue at 38% contribution | Low | CRM lead |
| 4 | Bring back email sign-up without slowing the site | Medium: back to 2.0% would be 2.5 times today's list growth | Medium | CRM lead, web lead |
| 5 | Cut avoidable returns on core products | Low to medium: 1 point less on each of the other four named lines is about 0.7% of revenue | Low | Merchandising lead |
| 6 | Give repeat buyers a reason to come back sooner | Medium, slower: each extra point of repeat rate adds about $0.71 per new customer | Medium | CRM lead |

**1. Linen.** Pull linen from Meta and from email campaigns this week. Keep it on sale with a plain washing note on the product page. Restart ads only once a fresh batch passes the wash test. As one customer put it: "Returned the linen, it shrank."

**2. Paid reset.** First orders break even at $71; customers break even at $94 over 18 months. No ad set stays above $94 for two weeks running. Cut Meta first, starting with linen. Shift money to Google and other channels in small steps, only while they stay under $94, since costs rise as you scale. If Meta's purchase count is far below store orders, fix tracking before judging Meta further. Accept fewer new orders for a few weeks: the board asked for contribution.

**3. Abandonment flows.** They worked before January and were never rebuilt. Rebuild browse, cart and checkout abandonment for email and SMS by week 3. No discounts in the first message: a reminder, the product, and real reviews about how it washes.

**4. Email sign-up.** We gave up two-thirds of sign-ups for speed, and conversion did not move. Bring back a light form that loads after the page and appears on scroll or exit. Test it against no form, with conversion rate and load time as guardrails. Lead with what Sarah needs, not a coupon. Test a swatch offer: "Touch it before you commit. We'll send you swatches." It answers her main fear about spending $200 on sheets she has not felt.

**5. Avoidable returns.** Each top reason is a page fix. Down comforter "too warm": add a warmth guide by sleeper type. Sateen "feel": a plain crisp-vs-silky comparison. Classic sheets and towels "colour": true-colour photos in daylight and a swatch link.

**6. Repeat buyers.** Mike says, "I'd buy more if I heard about new stuff." Add a post-purchase flow (towels after sheets, a second set after 60 days), a new-arrivals email for past buyers, and a refresh reminder. No sitewide discounts.

## What to stop doing

- Promoting linen anywhere until the wash test passes.
- Scaling any ad set above $94 per first order.
- Judging Meta on its own reported results. Use cost per kept first order and contribution.
- Treating site speed and email capture as either/or.
- Chasing revenue with sitewide sales. It cuts margin and breaks the brand.
- Reporting revenue on its own. Contribution comes first in every weekly review.

## The 90 days

- **Weeks 1 to 2: stop the leaks.** Pause linen ads. Apply the $94 rule. Run all five checks. Start the wash test.
- **Weeks 3 to 6: rebuild.** Abandonment flows live. Sign-up form test live. Product page fixes for comforter, sateen and colour. Linen cause found and fix ordered.
- **Weeks 7 to 13: grow what works.** Move budget toward ad sets under $94. Launch repeat-buyer flows. Restart linen ads only after a fresh batch passes.
