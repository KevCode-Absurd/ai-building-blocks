# Plum Goodness: three bundles and a ₹599 free shipping line

## The short answer

- **Launch three bundles built from what shoppers already buy together:** the Vitamin C Duo (₹869), the Wash Day Duo (₹749) and the Vitamin C Serum Pair (₹1,079).
- **Move free shipping from ₹499 to ₹599, the serum's price.** The serum still ships free alone. If shoppers stay, you keep up to about ₹1.08 lakh more profit a month. It stops paying if more than 15% of affected shoppers walk away, so test it first.
- **Test one change at a time:** bundles in weeks 1 to 4, the shipping line in weeks 5 to 8. Each is a 50/50 split on the same days, judged on profit per visitor.

All money is gross profit after product cost, shipping and refused COD orders. I used about 17,500 orders a month (3,100 pair orders ÷ 19% ÷ 31% ÷ 3 months).

### 1. Vitamin C Duo, ₹869: the routine your ads lead with

For Kavya, who asks which face wash goes with the serum. Your top pair: about 1,030 orders a month.

- Shoppers who already buy both pay ₹75 less. You make about ₹70 less on each: about ₹72,000 a month.
- Each serum-only shopper who upgrades adds ₹149.
- So it pays once duo orders rise by about 485 a month (+47%).

Page line: "Not sure which face wash goes with your vitamin C serum? This one. Both for ₹869. You save ₹75, and shipping is free. 100% vegan, always."

### 2. Wash Day Duo, ₹749: gets one-item orders over the line

For budget shoppers like Ishita, who buy one thing at a time because of shipping. A ₹399 shampoo pays ₹49 shipping, and 29% of COD orders under ₹500 are refused. The duo ships free, in the 17% band.

- Current pairs (about 467 a month) earn ₹46 less each: about ₹21,300 a month.
- Each shampoo-only shopper who upgrades adds ₹156.
- So it pays once duo orders rise by about 136 a month (+29%).

Page line: "Shampoo and conditioner made to go together. ₹749 for both. That's ₹49 off, and shipping is free."

### 3. Vitamin C Serum Pair, ₹1,079: the reorder

Ishita only reorders the serum. One delivery instead of two saves a ₹70 shipment, and orders of ₹1,000 and up see only 10% COD refusals.

- If it only replaces two separate orders, you make ₹16 less (₹595 vs ₹611).
- If it adds a bottle that would not have been bought, you make ₹290 more.
- So it pays if just 1 in 19 pair buyers buys an extra bottle.

Page line: "Love your vitamin C serum? Get two in one delivery for ₹1,079. That's just under ₹540 a bottle."

**Runner-up:** a Body Duo (lotion + body wash), your smallest pair.

**For all three:** show real item prices. No "was" price you never charged, no countdown timers, and no "only 2 left" unless true.

## Free shipping: move to ₹599, after a test

| Free shipping from | Orders a month losing free shipping | Extra profit if all stay and pay ₹49 | Stops paying if more than this walk away |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹499 (today) | 0 | ₹0 | Not needed |
| **₹599** | **about 2,370** | **about ₹1.08 lakh** | **15% (about 355 orders)** |
| ₹699 | about 4,740, including every serum-only order | about ₹2.16 lakh | 14% |
| ₹799 | about 5,850 | about ₹2.67 lakh | 13% |

Why ₹599:

- **It protects your hero.** ₹699 or ₹799 would put a ₹49 fee on a serum-only order, your most reordered product. A lost reorder costs more than one order.
- **Going higher does not cut refusals.** The rate is 17% from ₹500 to ₹999.
- **It nudges small carts into a routine.** A ₹544 cart (face wash + lip balm) now has a reason to become the Vitamin C Duo.

Show the rupee gap on product pages and in the cart, so nothing surprises Ishita: "You're ₹254 away from free shipping. Make it the Vitamin C Duo and save ₹75."

**Runner-up:** keep ₹499 if the test fails.

## How to test both safely

Split visitors 50/50 at random, both sides live on the same days. Skip sale and festival weeks. Four weeks gives about 8,100 orders per side, enough to see a change of about 3% or more.

| | Test 1: bundles (weeks 1 to 4) | Test 2: shipping line (weeks 5 to 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Side A | Today's store | ₹499, winning bundles live |
| Side B | Bundles on product pages and in cart | ₹599, winning bundles live, rupee gap shown |
| Main measure | Gross profit per visitor | Gross profit per visitor |
| Guardrails | Conversion on serum, face wash and shampoo pages; COD refusal rate | Orders per visitor; serum-only orders; COD refusal rate |
| Stop early | After 2 weeks, side B profit per visitor over 5% below A | After 2 weeks, side B orders per visitor over 5% below A |
| Keep it if | Profit per visitor higher; each bundle beats its break-even (Duo pair orders +47%, Wash Day +29% vs A) | Profit per visitor higher; orders per visitor no more than 2% lower |

- **Read each bundle on its own.** Drop any that misses break-even; keep the rest.
- **Watch serum orders for 8 weeks after.** A pair that only pulls reorders forward shows up as fewer later orders.
- **Never test two prices for one bundle.** Change its contents or display instead.
- **Why 2%:** 355 lost orders is about 2% of all orders. If the result is unclear after four weeks, run two more. Still unclear? Keep ₹499.

## Set-up steps

1. Create the three bundles as products, with item prices shown beside the bundle price.
2. Add a bundle picker under "Add to cart" on the serum, face wash and shampoo pages, plus a cart bar showing the rupee gap.
3. Set up the split and tag every order by side; track the measures above weekly.

## How the money was worked

Profit per order = delivered share × (price + shipping paid − product cost − ₹70) − refused share × ₹130, where refused share = 40% COD × that order value's refusal rate.

Vitamin C Duo: product cost 32% × ₹944 = ₹302; refused share 0.4 × 17% = 6.8%; 0.932 × (₹869 − ₹302 − ₹70) − 0.068 × ₹130 = ₹454. Break-even: ₹72,200 lost on current pairs ÷ ₹149 per upgrade = about 485.

₹599 line: an affected order that stays pays ₹49 × 93.2% delivered = ₹45.70; one that leaves loses ₹259 (a ₹550 order). 45.7 ÷ (45.7 + 259) = 15%.

Assumptions: mixed orders at 65% margin; orders spread evenly inside each value band; 40% COD at every order value; refused products go back into stock; the ₹400 to 499 band pays shipping today.
