# Brooklinen: 90-day growth plan

## The answer on one page

**What is really causing the slowdown:** Meta got far more expensive, and 40% of its budget pushes a linen set that now comes back 18% of the time. Meanwhile two cheap sources of first orders were switched off: the email sign-up form and the browse and cart flows. Returning customers are fine. New customers are the problem, and each one now loses money on the first order.

**The bad news:**
- A $188 first order leaves $71 after product, shipping, fees and returns. It costs $104 to win. That is a $33 loss per new customer, up from $7 a year ago.
- Contribution margin after ads has fallen from about 13.6% of revenue to about 7.8%.
- Paid spend rose about 19% to buy 11% fewer new customers.
- Move 1 gives up some first-order revenue. For the board's goal, it is worth it.

**Day-90 goal:** contribution margin after ads from about 7.8% to 11% or more, with revenue no more than 5% below today's run rate.

**This week:** pause linen prospecting ads on Meta, put the old sign-up form back, and check Meta's purchase tracking after the January platform move.

**Assumptions** (they change sizes, not order):
1. Dollar figures are per $1M of monthly revenue. Scale them to yours.
2. New and repeat orders both average about $188, as last year. So new customers are about 55% of orders and paid spend about 30% of revenue.
3. A repeat buyer places one more $188 order within 18 months. So $94 is the most a first order can cost and break even.

## What the numbers prove

- **The slowdown is new customers.** New orders are down 11%. Returning orders are up 6%.
- **The cost rise is almost all Meta.** Meta went from $72 to $121 (up 68%). Google and Other rose about 5%. With Meta at $72, blended cost would be about $79, not $104.
- **Linen is broken.** Returns jumped from 7% to 18% of units, mostly "Shrank after first wash." Every other product is flat or up one point. Linen is 16% of revenue but gets 22% of all paid spend.
- **Removing the form cost two-thirds of sign-ups for nothing.** Sign-ups fell from 2.4% to 0.8% of new visitors. Conversion stayed at 2.1%.
- **Flows are nearly off.** Only welcome runs, earning 9% of email revenue.
- **At $121, a Meta first order never pays back,** even by month 18. Google at $92 about breaks even.

## What still needs checking (weeks 1 and 2)

- **Did the January move break Meta tracking?** The flows died in that move. If the server-side purchase link (Conversions API) was not reconnected, Meta optimises half blind. Check when the cost jumped, and whether Meta's purchase count is within 10% of the store's.
- **What linen ad sets cost per first order.** We know their budget share, not their cost.
- **Why linen shrinks.** New batch, finish, supplier or care steps? Test-wash current stock against an older batch.
- **Whether the site actually got faster** without the form, and what the old flows earned.
- **Whether linen buyers come back less.** Pull 90-day repeat by first product.

Add a one-question post-purchase survey ("Where did you first hear about us?") so Meta is judged on two reads, not its own count.

## The moves, ranked by gross profit and effort

Contribution is gross profit after product, shipping, fees, returns and ads, per month, per $1M of monthly revenue, at full run rate.

| # | Move | Contribution a month | Owner | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pause linen on Meta; cap every ad set at $94 per kept first order | +$27k ($19k to $33k) | Paid social lead | Days |
| 2 | Bring back the old sign-up form, loaded late | +$10k ($5k to $15k) | Email lead, web developer | 1 week |
| 3 | Rebuild browse and cart abandonment flows | +$7.5k ($4k to $11k) | Email lead | 2 weeks |
| 4 | Fix linen shrinkage at the source and on the page | +$9k ($6k to $13k) | Product and sourcing lead | 6 to 10 weeks |
| 5 | "What's new" flow for repeat buyers | +$3.5k ($1.7k to $6.9k) | Email lead | 3 weeks |

**1. Pause linen on Meta, then cap Meta.** Linen ads spend about $66k a month. At $121 they buy about 549 first orders worth $39k before ads, so pausing nets about $27k. Assumed input: linen ad sets cost about Meta's average; at $100 to $140 the gain is $19k to $33k. Move money only to ad sets beating $94 per kept first order: Classic and Sateen on Meta first, then Google. Lead with the customer's own words, "Worth it after a year of washing." Money that cannot clear $94 stays unspent; if none moves, up to $103k of first-order revenue goes. Stop line: blended cost not under $100 by week 4, cut Meta further.

**2. Sign-up form.** Relaunch the old form unchanged; it earned 2.4%. Load it after the page renders, on time, scroll or exit. Welcome earns about 2% of revenue (22% × 9%) from 0.8% sign-ups. At 2.4%, it adds about 4% of revenue, $15k at full rate. Assumed input: a new sign-up is worth what one is worth today. Ramp-up and buyers who would have bought anyway put day 90 near $10k. Stop line: under 1.5% at week 4, test the trigger; mobile load slower than the week-1 baseline, delay the script.

**3. Browse and cart flows.** They reach shoppers you already paid for, so orders keep the full 38% contribution. Assumed input: together they earn what welcome earns now, 2% of revenue (range 1% to 3%). No discount in the first email. Stop line: under 1% of revenue by week 6, fix timing and triggers.

**4. Linen.** Each point off linen's return rate is worth about $1,600 a month, assuming washed returns cannot be resold. From 18% to 10% is worth $13k at today's linen sales, nearer $6k once Meta stops pushing linen; do not add it in full to move 1. If a batch or supplier changed, claim against it and stop selling that batch. Put the measured shrinkage and care steps on the product page and a care card. Point anyone who wants no shrink to Classic or Sateen. Linen returns to Meta only after new-order returns stay under 10% for four weeks.

**5. Repeat buyers.** Mike: "I'd buy more if I heard about new stuff." Build a post-purchase flow: towels and a second set after the first order, then new colours and products. Assumed input: it lifts repeat orders, about 45% of orders, by 2% (range 1% to 4%). Stop line: under 0.5% of revenue at week 8, cut to one email.

## What to stop doing

- Stop prospecting with linen on Meta until returns are fixed.
- Stop adding budget to any ad set above $94 per kept first order.
- Stop judging Meta on its own reported orders.
- Stop trading sign-ups for speed. Load the form late, not never.
- Do not fill the gap with sitewide discounts. They cut the margin the board wants.

## Appendix: the working

- First-order contribution: $188 × (52% − 14%) = $71.44. Even product margin alone, $97.76, is under $104.
- New share of orders: −11% × n + 6% × (1 − n) = −4% gives n = 59% a year ago, 55% now.
- Paid spend share of revenue: 55% × $104 ÷ $188 = 30.2% now; 59% × $78 ÷ $188 = 24.4% a year ago. Contribution margin: 38% minus each, so 7.8% and 13.6%.
- Spend change: 0.89 × $104 ÷ $78 = 1.19. Break-even cost: $71.44 × 1.31 = $94.
- Linen on Meta: 30.2% × 55% × 40% = 6.6% of revenue, $66k. $66k ÷ $121 = 549 orders × $71.44 = $39k. Net $27k.
- Day 90: $27k + $10k + $7.5k + $3.5k + $6k (linen low end, no double count) = $54k. Contribution goes from $78k to $132k on revenue about 4% lower, near 13.8% at full run rate; 11% allows for ramp-up.
