# Plausible: 90-day SEO plan for trials

**16 Nov 2026 to 14 Feb 2027**

## The decision

Put every writer slot into comparison and "no cookies" pages. Stop writing general blog posts. In October, comparison pages turned 3.2% of clicks into trials; blog posts turned 0.25%. That is 46 trials per comparison page against fewer than 1 per post.

**Bad news first.** Organic clicks fell 13% since May (108,900 to 95,100). The blog lost 22,500. We will not chase them: at the blog's trial rate, they were worth about 56 trials a month.

**Projected gain.** About 42 to 125 more trials a month by February, on top of October's 1,316. At 38% trial to paid, that is 16 to 48 more paying customers a month.

**First move:** the writer rewrites /vs-google-analytics, starting Monday.

## Stop doing

- **New marketing tips and privacy news posts.** 140 posts made 96 trials.
- **Rewriting old posts to win back clicks.** Leave them live and unchanged. One exception: the 2022 cookie banner post. It answers Maya's own question, "Can I finally get rid of the cookie banner?"
- **Judging SEO by total clicks.** Report trials by page group.
- **Touching /vs-matomo's copy** (9.0% CTR at position 2.8). Update only its facts and date.

**Owners and rules.** Writer: pages. Developer: templates and links. You: a review on the first Monday of each month.

- **Keep** a change if its page's trials hold or rise 4 weeks after Google recrawls it.
- **Roll back** a rewrite that drops more than one position on its main query after 4 weeks.
- **Scale:** any "[rival] vs plausible" query with 1,000+ monthly impressions and no page takes the next free slot.
- **Break-even:** a page must beat the blog average of 0.7 trials a month. Every page here aims well above it.

## Week 1 for the writer

**Monday 16 Nov.** Export 3 months of queries for /vs-google-analytics. List what the top 5 results for "google analytics alternative" answer that we don't. Replace the title and meta, and log the date.

**Friday 20 Nov.** Check every GA4 claim against Google's help pages, add "Last checked 20 Nov 2026" and publish. Then start /vs-fathom: save Fathom's own pricing and feature pages, with the date seen.

## Bolder move: a page Tomas can forward

In Month 3, write "Plausible for agencies: what to tell your DPO". It lists what Plausible collects and what it doesn't, with a one-page summary an agency can forward to a client's data protection officer.

- **Size (projection):** 5 agency trials a month at 38% is about 2 new agencies, each running 20 or more sites.
- **First step:** ask three agency customers what their DPO asked. Use those questions as headings.
- **Risk:** little search demand, so it relies on sharing. No "100% GDPR compliant" promise; a legal adviser checks the wording before launch.

- The writer publishes in the CMS; the developer day is for templates and links.
