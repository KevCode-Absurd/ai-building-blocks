# Cold email sequence: Controllers after a pricing change

**The call:** Send only to Controllers and Heads of Finance at 50 to 300 person SaaS companies whose pricing page just changed. Expect 6.8% to 7.9% replies. **First move this week:** pull every Controller the data tool flagged in the last 60 days and queue them for the next start window.

## Who to load

- Controllers and Heads of Finance at B2B SaaS companies with 50 to 300 people (about 1,900 on the new list).
- Only those whose pricing page changed in the last 60 days, as flagged by the weekly data tool.
- One person per company: whoever owns month-end close.

Why: this group was 27% of pilot contacts but booked 38 of 57 meetings. A pricing-page change got 7.9% replies, against 1.8% for a generic opener.

## Who to leave out

| Leave out | Pilot result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| CFOs, 1,000+ employees | 3 meetings from 1,600 | They pass it to their team. |
| CFOs, 50 to 300 | 10 meetings from 1,100 | The Controller owns the close. |
| RevOps leads | 6 meetings from 800 | They don't own the close. |
| Controllers with no recent pricing change | Generic opener: 1.8% | Watch list. Load them the week a flag fires. |

Also leave out last quarter's pilot contacts, current customers, open deals and opt-outs. That leaves out about 4,100 of the 6,000 new contacts.

## What to expect (projection from the pilot)

- **Reply rate: 6.8% to 7.9%.** Floor: the Controller pilot rate. Top: the pricing-trigger rate. Plan on the floor.
- **Positive replies: 3.4% to 4.0%.** Half of Controller replies were positive in the pilot.
- **Meetings: about 3 per 100 loaded** (the pilot booked 38 from 1,300).

## Send rules

- Plain text in every step. In the pilot, step 1 with no links got 4.1% replies; a case study link got 1.7% and a calendar link 1.2%.
- Start step 1 between the 12th and the 15th, so step 4 lands by the 30th, before close. Flags that fire during close wait for the next window.
- Stop at any reply.
- Each email ends with the sender's name and title, Chargebee's postal address and: "Reply 'stop' and I won't email again."
- {{pricing_change}} is a few words from the flag, such as "added a usage-based tier".

## The sequence

### Step 1, Day 1

**Subject option A:** The new pricing and your rev rec model
**Subject option B:** Month-end after a pricing change

Hi {{first_name}},

I saw {{company}} {{pricing_change}} on its pricing page in {{month}}.

For a lot of Controllers, a pricing change means rebuilding the rev rec model by hand. Often on top of a billing setup engineering built years ago.

A 140-person SaaS company moved billing and revenue recognition to Chargebee. Their month-end close went from 9 days to 4.

How many days did your last close take?

If you'd like to hear how they got there, reply and I'll walk you through it in 20 minutes.

### Step 2, Day 4

**Subject:** Billing belongs to engineering

Hi {{first_name}},

A fair worry with any billing change: billing belongs to engineering, and finance doesn't want to own a migration.

But when pricing changes, the extra work lands on finance. Engineering ships the new plan. You rebuild the model.

Who at {{company}} would own a change to billing, you or engineering?

Reply with a name and I'll set up one 20-minute call for both of you.

### Step 3, Day 9

**Subject:** After your audit, not during

Hi {{first_name}},

If the timing is wrong, that's fine. Nobody should touch billing in the middle of an audit.

When the time is right, a first call is simple. We map your current close, step by step, and show where the days go.

When does your next audit wrap up?

Reply with the month and I'll write back then, not before.

### Step 4, Day 15

**Subject:** Last note from me

Hi {{first_name}},

This is my last note for now.

If your close still runs on spreadsheets, going from 9 days to 4 is worth 20 minutes.

Is a faster close something you're working on this year?

If yes, reply and I'll send two times that miss your close week. If not, I'll check back the next time {{company}} changes its pricing.

## When they reply

- **"Send me something":** the 140-person story in three plain lines, then offer 20 minutes.
- **"Not now" or "we're in an audit":** log the month, set a task, stop.

**Test first:** subject A against B, half of each batch. Count replies and meetings, not opens.

**Bolder move:** send 300 watch-list Controllers this sequence with a close-first opening line. Projection: 1.8% to 6.8%, so 5 to 20 replies. Risk: they are used up before a trigger fires.

**Assumption:** close runs through roughly the first nine working days of each month, as your Controllers describe.
