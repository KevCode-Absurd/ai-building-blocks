# Support hire: job post, scorecard and application

**Assumptions**
- This hire covers 00:00 to 08:00 UTC: 27% of tickets, a 7.1-hour median first reply, 81% satisfaction, and only 1 of 14 support people in Asia-Pacific.
- One opening. After the application: a 60-minute interview with two support teammates, then 30 minutes with the support lead.

## 1. The job post

### Customer Support Specialist, Asia-Pacific hours (remote, $82,000)

Customers who write to us while the Americas and Europe sleep wait too long for an answer. We're hiring someone to give them a real one, in their daytime, for $82,000 a year.

**The hard parts, up front**
- **The hours.** 00:00 to 08:00 UTC, Monday to Friday. Local times are below.
- **Weekends.** One weekend in six, you work Saturday and Sunday and get two weekdays off in return. That's about 8 or 9 weekends a year.
- **Quiet hours.** Most of the team is asleep. You'll solve most things alone and leave clear notes.

| Where you live | Your hours |
|---|---|
| Manila, Singapore, Perth | 8 am to 4 pm |
| Tokyo, Seoul | 9 am to 5 pm |
| Sydney, Melbourne | 10 am to 6 pm (11 am to 7 pm in summer) |

**What you'll do**
- Answer customer emails about Basecamp and HEY, and fix the actual problem instead of just closing the ticket.
- Dig into tricky problems until you find the cause.
- Write replies so clear the customer doesn't need to write back.

**A normal week**
We work 40 hours a week, and we mean it. From May to August, we work 4-day weeks. We write things down instead of calling meetings, in Basecamp and HEY.

**How we judge the work**
In your first year, we look at two things: how happy customers are with your help, and what your teammates say about working with you.

**What you need**
- You write clear, warm English that a stressed person can follow.
- You've explained things to upset people in any job. Support, teaching, a hotel desk: it all counts.
- You stay with a hard problem until you find what's really wrong.
- You can work the hours and weekends above.

**What we'll teach you**
Basecamp, HEY and our help desk tool. You don't need a support job title, help desk experience or a degree.

**Pay and place**
$82,000 a year, the same wherever you live. Fully remote.

**How hiring works**
1. **Apply (about an hour).** No resume or cover letter. Answer one real customer email in up to 250 words, and tell us if the hours work for you. Applications are open for two weeks. We read them with names hidden.
2. **Hear back within 3 weeks of closing.** Everyone hears from us, yes or no.
3. **Video interview, 60 minutes,** with two support teammates. We'll walk through the schedule together.
4. **Call with the support lead, 30 minutes.** You'll hear back within a week of each call.

Need an adjustment? Tell us in the form. We will never ask you for money or bank details.

## 2. How the application works

In 2025, 2,100 resumes took 160 reviewer hours. Across our last 9 hires, the written reply exercise tracked first-year performance at 0.62; interview impression (0.11), tool experience (0.07) and years (0.04) barely did. So the exercise becomes the application.

**The form**
1. **The reply.** A real ticket from the last 90 days that took several replies to solve, details removed, plus the facts a new hire would have. Same ticket for everyone.
2. **The schedule.** "Can you work 00:00 to 08:00 UTC, Monday to Friday, plus one weekend in six?" Yes or no. The only knockout.

**Reading**
- Show applicants as numbers, not names.
- All three reviewers first score the same 20 replies alone on scorecard rows 1 and 2, then agree what a 3 looks like.
- Pass 1: one reviewer scores each reply, in daily batches from opening day.
- Pass 2: a second reviewer scores every reply at 4 or more, without seeing the first score.
- The team invites about 10 people to interview, using the reply scores as evidence.

| Projected step, 2,100 applicants | Working | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Calibration | 20 × 3 min × 3 reviewers | 3 |
| Pass 1 | 2,100 × 2 to 3 min | 70 to 105 |
| Pass 2 | 15% score 4+: 315 × 4 min | 21 |
| **Total** | | **94 to 129** |

That's 6 to 9 hours per reviewer per week over five weeks, against 160 hours last time. Nine hires is a small sample, so recheck the correlations after this round.

**Bolder move:** in the decline email, ask applicants who scored 4+: "May we contact you next time?" The next round then starts from a scored shortlist. Risk: people move on, so give returners a fresh ticket.

## 3. The scorecard

Score each row alone, 1 to 5, before any discussion. Final score = sum of (score × weight) ÷ 100. Discuss only rows where scorers differ by 2+. Never scored: years, tools, degrees, "overall impression".

| What we score | Where | Weight | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solves the problem | Reply | 30% | Misreads it or points them elsewhere | Fixes the main issue; misses a second one | Fixes it fully and covers what the customer didn't ask |
| Clear, warm writing | Reply | 25% | Jargon or cold; customer must ask again | Clear and polite; a bit long or canned | Plain, short, human; calms the customer |
| Finds the real cause | Interview | 15% | Guesses or hands off at once | Sensible steps when prompted | Explains how they narrowed it down and confirmed the fix |
| Calm with upset people | Interview | 15% | Defensive or over-promises | Polite, but ignores the worry | Names the worry, sets a clear expectation |
| Works alone, writes handovers | Interview | 15% | Waits for someone to decide | Handles routine cases; thin notes | Decides within limits; notes a teammate can act on |

**Interview questions, same order for everyone, then "What happened next?"**
- Cause: "Tell me about a problem where the first answer was wrong. What did you check, in what order?"
- Upset people: "Tell me about someone who was upset with you. What did you say first?"
- Alone: "Tell me about deciding something with nobody to ask. What did you write down?"

**Pass or fail:** open each interview by reading out a sample month with a rotation weekend, then ask, "Does this work for your life?" Two of the three recent hires who left within a year said the rotation wasn't clear when they applied.

Check local hiring rules for each country before posting.
