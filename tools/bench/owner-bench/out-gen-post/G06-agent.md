# Where we lose to Semrush, and the five moves that matter

## The answer

We lose to Semrush in one place: **agencies that need client reports and PPC research in one tool.** Agencies cause 60% of the trials we lose to Semrush. Elsewhere, our biggest leaks are our own: usage limits and the gap between plans.

- **Lose:** agency trials (54% of agency losses go to Semrush) and, in small numbers, Advanced customers (28% of Advanced exits).
- **Tie:** keyword data (41% vs 44%).
- **Win:** backlink data and, narrowly, site audit (45% vs 38%).
- **Not a Semrush problem:** Lite churn, the in-house price jump, solo creators and enterprise procurement.

**Do first, this month:** start the Monday client report beta with agency customers (move 1) and split-test an agency onboarding track for the 21,000 multi-site free accounts (move 3).

**Stop:** treating PPC feature parity as the fix, and sending multi-site free accounts the same emails as everyone else.

**Don't spend on:** discounts for solo creators (91% of their losses are not to Semrush; they leave on price) or extra backlink roadmap, where we already lead 64% to 21%.

## Here's the data

Periods: trials, last two quarters. Webmaster Tools, last 90 days. Survey of 600 SEOs who used both tools, last year.

**Trials we lose, per 100 new trials**

| Segment | Trials lost | Lost to Semrush | Share of all Semrush losses |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-house SEO team | 28.9 | 5.2 | 24% |
| Agency, 10+ clients | 24.0 | 13.0 | 60% |
| Solo creator or blogger | 23.5 | 2.1 | 10% |
| Enterprise | 6.9 | 1.3 | 6% |
| **All** | **83.3** | **21.6** | **100%** |

Working: trials lost = share of trials × (1 − trial-to-paid), then × each segment's Semrush share. Today 16.7 of every 100 trials convert.

**Churn.** Blended monthly churn is 4.9%. Lite produces 70% of churned customers. Lite usage-limit exits alone are 32% of all churn, or 1.6% of paying customers every month. Advanced exits to Semrush are 1.9% of churn: few customers, but on our second-highest plan.

**Survey.** With 600 people, each share has a margin of about ±4 points, so the 3-point keyword gap is a tie. PPC (8% vs 71%) and reporting (12% vs 66%) are not close.

**Webmaster Tools.** Multi-site accounts are 7% of free accounts but 42% of free-to-paid conversions. They convert at 9.2%; the other 289,000 at 0.9%. They look like agencies, yet get generic emails.

The thread: the agency owner is "paying for two tools" and hates it. Enterprise customers leave to consolidate vendors (40% of exits). Semrush wins where it already does the second tool's job.

## Five moves, ranked by impact

### 1. Build the Monday client report for agencies

Our agency buyer says "My clients don't care about DR. They care about leads." So the report leads with organic traffic, rankings won and pages fixed. It is white-label and sends itself every Monday. We skip PPC parity (a 63-point gap); the report is what the client sees weekly.

- **Owner:** Product, reporting team.
- **First step this month:** recruit 30 agency customers into a beta report built only from data we already have (rank tracking, site audit, traffic estimates).
- **How we'll know:** agency trial-to-paid rises from 11% to at least 14% within two quarters, and agency losses going to Semrush fall below 45%.
- **Projection:** 14% to 17% agency trial-to-paid adds 0.8 to 1.6 paid customers per 100 trials, or 5% to 10% more new paid customers.

### 2. Sell a mid-project top-up on Lite

"I ran out of credits halfway through an audit" matches Lite's top exit reason (46% hit a usage limit). It also feeds the in-house complaint about the "price jump between plans."

- **Owner:** Pricing and billing.
- **First step this month:** at 80% of the Lite limit, offer half of new Lite customers a one-off credit top-up. The other half sees today's upgrade prompt.
- **How we'll know:** usage-limit exits fall in the test group, Lite churn drops below 7.9%, and in-house trial-to-paid rises above 24%.
- **Projection:** halving usage-limit exits takes Lite churn to about 6.1% and blended churn from 4.9% to about 4.1% a month.

### 3. Give multi-site free accounts their own onboarding

The 21,000 accounts with 3+ domains convert at ten times the rate of the rest. They are our cheapest agency pipeline.

- **Owner:** Lifecycle marketing.
- **First step this month:** split the 21,000 in half. One half gets an agency track: setting up client sites, a Monday report preview, and which plan fits 3+ sites. The other half keeps today's emails.
- **How we'll know:** 90-day conversion beats the 9.2% control. Each extra point is about 210 more paid accounts per 90 days.

### 4. Ship an enterprise security and procurement pack

Enterprise trials convert best (31%) and only 19% of losses go to Semrush. The top loss reason, security review, is ours to fix.

- **Owner:** Sales operations, with Security.
- **First step this month:** list every security question from the last two quarters' lost enterprise deals, then publish one pack of standard answers and documents.
- **How we'll know:** fewer losses cite procurement, review time shortens, and enterprise trial-to-paid rises above 31%.

### 5. Bolder move: a free Monday report inside Webmaster Tools

Let multi-site free accounts send one free, Ahrefs-branded weekly report per verified site. Paid plans unlock competitor sections and white-label. The free tier becomes the agency's reporting habit before they compare tools.

- **Owner:** Product and Growth.
- **First step this month:** scope it on the move 1 beta, then add it as a third arm of the move 3 test.
- **How we'll know:** multi-site 90-day conversion reaches 12%, about 590 more paid accounts per 90 days.
- **Risk:** data cost, and a paid report that feels less special. Cap it at one report per site.

**Assumptions:** "losses" means trials that did not convert, and the Semrush share applies to those. No revenue per plan was given, so moves are ranked by customers affected and closeness to the Semrush gap, not dollars.
