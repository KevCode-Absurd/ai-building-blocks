# Todoist onboarding series: 5 emails

**The decision:** teach two habits and nothing else. At day 30, users who typed a recurring task stayed at 52%, users on a second device at 48%, and users who did both at 67%, against 11% for those with only 1 to 4 tasks. Only 8% do both today, and the current series asks for neither. The series stops once a user has done both.

**First move this week:** build emails 2 and 3 on the "first task added" and "recurring task created" triggers, and hold back 10% of signups as a control group.

Assumptions: the tool can skip an email when a listed event already happened; a web sign-in on any computer counts as a second device; every button opens Todoist on the user's signup platform.

## The series at a glance

| # | Sends | Job | Skip if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Right after signup | Add five tasks | None |
| 2 | 24 hours after first task, or day 2 | Make a recurring task | Already made one |
| 3 | 24 hours after first recurring task, or day 4 | Sign in on a second device | Already on two |
| 4 | 3 days with no activity (days 2 to 14, once) | Come back with one task | None |
| 5 | Day 8 | Do the missing habit | Did both |

Never two emails within 24 hours. Every email carries Doist's postal address and a one-click unsubscribe.

## Email 1: Get it out of your head

**Goal:** 5 or more tasks in 7 days. Today 31% do; they stay at 29%.

**Subject:** Get it out of your head
**Preview:** Start with whatever is on your mind.

Welcome to Todoist. Your to-dos might live on sticky notes, in your inbox and in your head. Let's put them in one place.

Today's tip: add five things. Don't sort them. Just type them as they come, like "Call the dentist" or "Return library books Friday." Todoist sees "Friday" and sets the date.

Once the list holds it, your head doesn't have to.

**Screen:** Quick Add with "Return library books Friday" typed and the date set.
**Button:** Add my first five tasks

## Email 2: Type it once

**Goal:** a recurring task within 48 hours. Today 14% make one in week one.

**Subject:** Type it once, see it every week
**Preview:** Todoist reads dates like "every Monday."

Some tasks come back again and again. Rent. Trash night. The Monday team update.

Today's tip: type the repeat in plain words, right in the task. Todoist sets the schedule. Try "Pay rent every 1st" or "Take out the trash every Tuesday."

The task comes back on its own. You don't have to remember to remember it.

**Screen:** Quick Add with "Pay rent every 1st" typed and the repeat showing.
**Button:** Add a repeating task

## Email 3: One list, every device

**Goal:** a second-device sign-in within 72 hours. Today 22% do it in week one.

**Web and desktop signups (59%)**

**Subject:** Add tasks from your phone, too
**Preview:** Same list, right in your pocket.

Ideas show up in line at the store, not just at your desk.

Today's tip: get Todoist on your phone and sign in with this email address. Everything you've added is already there. Next time, type it where you are: "Buy stamps."

**Screen:** the phone app showing the same Today list as the computer.
**Button:** Get the phone app

**Mobile signups (41%)**

**Subject:** Your list is on the web, too
**Preview:** Sign in on any laptop. Same tasks.

Your list lives on your phone, and that's fine. But some days you're at a laptop at school or work.

Today's tip: go to todoist.com there and sign in with this email address. Your tasks are waiting. It's handy for bigger lists, like "Book flights" or "Pick project topic."

**Screen:** the web app showing the same list as the phone.
**Button:** Open Todoist on the web

Mobile signups rarely use a computer. If this version converts at under half the web version's rate after 4 weeks, replace it with a second recurring-task email.

## Email 4: A calm restart

**Goal:** any activity within 48 hours.

**Subject:** Start again with just one task
**Preview:** There's nothing to catch up on.

Lists get busy, and life gets busier. That's normal.

Today's tip: open Todoist and add one thing on your mind right now, like "Book a haircut." One task is a fine place to start again.

**Screen:** the Today view with a single task.
**Button:** Add one task

## Email 5: The step that's left

**Goal:** both habits by day 14. Today 8% do both in week one.

**Subject:** Two small habits that make it stick
**Preview:** Here's the one step left for you.

People who stick with Todoist tend to let tasks repeat and keep their list on more than one device.

*No recurring task yet, or neither habit:* Your next step: type one task that repeats, like "Pay rent every 1st." It works on the device you already use.
**Button:** Add a repeating task

*Recurring task, one device:* Your next step: sign in on a second device with this email address. Your tasks will be there.
**Button:** Get the phone app (mobile signups: Open Todoist on the web)

**Screen:** the image from email 2 or 3, matching the version.

## What to drop

| Current email | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 welcome | Keep, rewrite | Best clicks (22% web). Asks for five tasks now: 1 to 4 kept only 11%. |
| Day 1 projects and labels | Drop | A feature tour; labels can't be tracked or tied to retention. |
| Day 2 shortcuts | Drop | 0.3% clicks from mobile signups, 41% of signups. Ten tips, not one. |
| Day 4 invite your team | Drop | Worst unsubscribes (1.4%). Shared projects reach 6% and can't be triggered. |
| Day 7 upgrade to Pro | Move out | 1.1% clicks, 1.1% unsubscribes, before habits form. Send after day 30 to active users. |

## How we'll know it worked

- Judge each email on its goal event, not opens, against the control group (no onboarding emails; about 6,000 people a month).
- Series goal: share doing both habits by day 14, up from 8%. Each extra point is 1,800 people a quarter.
- Final test: day-30 active rate against the control group, read at 8 weeks.
- Guardrail: pause any email whose unsubscribe rate passes 0.5%.

**Bolder move:** send email 2 one hour after the first task, not 24, while web users may still be at the screen. First step: try it on half of web signups for 4 weeks. Risk: it can feel pushy; watch the guardrail.
