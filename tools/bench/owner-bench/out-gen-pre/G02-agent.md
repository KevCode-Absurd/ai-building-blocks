# Cal.com for recruiting teams: segment, page and hero test

## Win recruiting teams

Recruiting teams are 5.5% of signups (2,100 of 38,000) but bring 57% of paid seats: 9.4% pay within 60 days at 7.8 seats each, or 733 paid seats per 1,000 signups against 12 for solo coaches.
They also start fastest (71% book within 7 days), yet today's page shows them no recruiting example and hides the security page they read before buying.

**Assumptions:** seats stand in for revenue, since no prices were given. The page lives at cal.com/recruiting, linked from the homepage and used for recruiting ads and search. The one action is "Book a demo", because Priya buys for a team and must pass a security review.

### 3. How it works

**Heading:** One link does the routing, the fair split and the reminders

1. **Routing form.** The candidate answers a few questions, like role and stage. They land on the right interview.
2. **Round robin.** Candidates only see times when an interviewer is free. Bookings are shared across the team, so no one gets buried.
3. **Reminders.** Candidates and interviewers get a reminder before each interview, so fewer slots go to waste.

**Proof:** the routing form and the booking page, side by side. Then: "In their first 30 days, paying recruiting teams use round robin (88%), routing forms (74%) and reminders (61%)."

### 6. Questions

**What if an interviewer is on PTO?** Round robin reads each interviewer's connected calendar before it offers a time. Time off blocked in the calendar is skipped. If it is not in the calendar, round robin cannot see it, so ask your team to block time off.

## Hero test

**Version A, time saved:** as in section 1. It leads with the top reason people go looking: back and forth over email (41%).

**Version B, fair load:**

- **Headline:** Spread interviews fairly across every interviewer
- **Subhead:** Candidates only see times when an interviewer is free, and round robin shares the bookings evenly. Routing sends each one to the right interview. One link, no back and forth.
- **Button:** Book a demo
- **Proof under the button:** "88% of paying recruiting teams use round robin in their first 30 days."

B leads with what paying recruiters actually use, though only 12% named fairness as why they looked. Only the headline, subhead and proof line change.

**The metric that decides:** qualified demo requests per unique visitor. A qualified request comes from a company of 100 or more people, read from the form's company size field. It is the closest number to paid seats you can read within weeks. A hero that pulls in solo users could win on clicks and lose on revenue.

**How to run it:** split visitors 50/50. Fix the sample before launch: n per version ≈ 16 × p × (1 − p) ÷ d², where p is the current qualified demo rate and d the smallest lift worth having. If p is 1% and you want to spot 1.3%, that is about 17,600 visitors per version. Run whole weeks and stop only there.
