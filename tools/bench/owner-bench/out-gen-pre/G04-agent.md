# Cold email sequence: Controllers whose pricing just changed

**Who and when:** Controllers and Heads of Finance at B2B SaaS companies with 50 to 300 people, sent in the week their pricing page is flagged as changed. In the pilot, this role replied at 6.8% and booked 38 of the 57 meetings. The pricing-change opener replied at 7.9%, against 1.8% for a generic opener.

**Format for every step:** plain text, no links, no attachments. Links cut step 1 replies from 4.1% to 1.7% (case study) and 1.2% (calendar). Each email has one question, and that question is the ask.

## The four emails

### Step 1 (Day 1)

**Subject A:** your new pricing page
**Subject B:** new pricing and month-end close

Hi {first_name},

I saw {company} added {pricing_change} to your pricing page.

When pricing changes on a billing setup engineering built years ago, finance often has to rebuild the rev rec model by hand.

One 140-person SaaS company moved billing and revenue recognition to Chargebee. Their month-end close went from 9 days to 4.

Is the new pricing adding days to your close?

{sender_name}

### Step 2 (Day 4)

**Subject:** 5 days back every month

Hi {first_name},

One more number on that 140-person team.

Going from a 9-day close to a 4-day close gives them back 5 days a month. Over a year, that is 60 fewer days in close.

What changed: they moved billing and rev rec into one system.

Would 20 minutes next week to walk through how they did it be useful?

{sender_name}

### Step 3 (Day 9)

**Subject:** billing, engineering and the audit

Hi {first_name},

Two fair worries we hear from controllers. "Billing belongs to engineering." And "I'm not touching billing in the middle of an audit."

Both make sense. A move like this works best when engineering is in it from the first call. It can also be planned to start after your audit, not during it.

Who else at {company} would need to be part of that first conversation?

{sender_name}

### Step 4 (Day 16)

**Subject:** last note on month-end

Hi {first_name},

I'll stop here.

If close isn't a problem right now, no need to reply. If the next pricing change lands on your desk, I'm happy to share how that team got from 9 days to 4.

Should I check back after your next audit?

{sender_name}

**Footer on every email:** the sender's full name, Chargebee's postal address, and this line: "Not the right person, or not interested? Reply 'no' and I won't email again." Remove anyone who opts out within 10 business days.

**Filling {pricing_change}:** use only what the flag shows, such as "usage-based pricing" or "new tiers". If the flag is unclear, write "made changes to".

## Who to load

1. **Load all ~1,900 Controllers and Heads of Finance at 50 to 300-person SaaS companies.** One contact per company.
2. **Send in this order each week:**
   - First, anyone whose company the data tool flagged for a pricing-page change in the last 60 days. Use the email above.
   - Next, companies hiring a billing or revenue operations role (3.1% in the pilot). Swap the first line for: "I saw {company} is hiring a {role}."
   - Last, everyone else. Swap the first line for: "Every time sales changes pricing, someone in finance rebuilds the rev rec model." Keep the rest.
3. **If a contact is flagged later,** restart them on the trigger version only if they have not yet received step 1.

## Who to leave out

- **CFOs at 1,000+ companies (1,600 in the pilot):** 0.2% positive replies, 3 meetings. Their usual answer is "Send it to my team."
- **CFOs at 50 to 300-person companies:** 10 meetings from 1,100 contacts (0.9%), against 2.9% for controllers. We reach the same company through the controller.
- **RevOps leads:** 6 meetings from 800 contacts (0.75%).
- **Also exclude:** pilot opt-outs and no's, current customers, open opportunities, and companies outside 50 to 300 people.

That leaves out about 4,100 of the 6,000 new contacts.

## What to expect

| Measure | Rate | On 1,900 contacts |
|---|---|---|
| Replies | 6.8% | about 129 |
| Positive replies | 3.4% | about 65 |
| Meetings booked | 2.9% | about 55 |

These are the pilot's controller rates, with no lift added. The pilot booked 57 meetings from 4,800 contacts. This plan aims for about the same from 40% of the sends.

The pricing trigger and no-link format should do better, but the pilot did not split them by role, so do not bank the lift. For the flagged group, aim for 7.9%, the trigger's pilot rate across all roles. If any group is under 5% after 300 sends, review its first line before sending more.

**First test:** subject A against subject B on step 1, nothing else changed. Judge on replies and meetings, not opens.

## Assumptions

- The trigger and format results cover all 4,800 pilot contacts, not only controllers.
- Steps 2 to 4 follow the step 1 format rule (no links), since only step 1 was tested.
- A named rep sends from their own inbox. US email rules are the baseline; check local rules for UK and EU contacts.
