# Chargebee: 4-step cold sequence for Controllers after a pricing change

**Who:** Controllers and Heads of Finance at B2B SaaS companies with 50 to 300 people.
**When:** within 60 days of a change to their public pricing page.

This is where the pilot points. That segment had the best reply rate (6.8%) and booked 38 of the 57 meetings. The pricing-page trigger had the best opener (7.9%). It also hits their top pain: "Every time sales changes pricing, I rebuild the rev rec model."

All four emails are plain text with no links. A case study link (1.7%) or calendar link (1.2%) cut replies against no links (4.1%).

Fields: {{first_name}}, {{company}}, {{pricing_change}} (for example "added a usage-based tier").

---

## Step 1: Day 1

**Subject option A:** your new pricing tiers
**Subject option B:** rev rec after the pricing change

Hi {{first_name}},

I saw {{company}} {{pricing_change}} on your pricing page recently.

For most finance teams, that means rebuilding the rev rec model in a spreadsheet again. Then close runs long.

A 140-person SaaS company cut its month-end close from 9 days to 4 after moving billing and revenue recognition to Chargebee.

How many days does your close take right now?

If it runs past five, reply "yes" and I will send a one-page summary of what they changed.

Thanks,
{{sender_name}}

---

## Step 2: Day 4

**Subject:** who owns billing at {{company}}?

Hi {{first_name}},

A lot of controllers tell me billing belongs to engineering, so finance works around it. The workaround is usually a spreadsheet that has to change every time pricing does.

Chargebee is built so finance and engineering share one billing setup. A new tier should not mean a new rev rec model.

When pricing changes at {{company}}, who rebuilds the model: you or engineering?

If it is you, reply "steps" and I will send the three steps that team took to stop doing it.

Thanks,
{{sender_name}}

---

## Step 3: Day 9

**Subject:** timing around your audit

Hi {{first_name}},

Moving billing is a fair worry, and nobody should do it during an audit.

You would pick the timing. A sensible window is after fieldwork wraps, not in the middle of it.

When does your next audit wrap up?

Reply with the month and I will check back then, not before.

Thanks,
{{sender_name}}

---

## Step 4: Day 16

**Subject:** close your books faster

Hi {{first_name}},

I have not heard back, so I will assume the timing is off. No problem.

If a nine-day close and rebuilt spreadsheets after each pricing change sound familiar, the 9 to 4 day close I shared is the reason I wrote.

Is a faster close on your list this year, or should I check back next quarter?

A one-word reply is plenty: "now" or "later."

Thanks,
{{sender_name}}

## Who to load

1. **Load:** Controllers and Heads of Finance at SaaS companies with 50 to 300 people, from the 1,900 in the new list, whose pricing page was flagged as changed in the last 60 days.
2. **Load weekly.** Each week, add the newly flagged contacts. Send step 1 within a few days of the flag, while the change is fresh.
3. **Hold the rest of the 1,900** on a watch list. Load each one when their pricing page changes. Do not send them the generic version: generic openers got 1.8%.

## Who to leave out

- **CFOs at 1,000+ companies** (1,600 in the pilot). 0.2% positive replies and 3 meetings. They forward to their team and are tied to ERP contracts.
- **CFOs at 50 to 300 companies.** 1.1% positive replies. The Controller at the same company owns the close and is the better door.
- **RevOps leads.** 0.9% positive replies, and the lowest open rate (49%).
- **Anyone from the 1,300 pilot Controllers** who already got the old sequence. I assume they should rest for at least 90 days. Then reload them only if their pricing page changes.
- Anyone who replied "later" in step 4. Move them to a reminder for next quarter.

That means about 4,100 of the 6,000 new contacts stay out of this sequence.

## What reply rate to expect

**Plan on about 7% replies. Count anything from 6.8% to 7.9% as on track.**

- 6.8% is what this segment did in the pilot, with mixed openers and the same sequence for everyone. That is the floor.
- 7.9% is what the pricing trigger did across all segments. Aiming the trigger at the best segment should land at or above it. I do not multiply the two gains, because the pilot did not test them together.

**Per 100 contacts loaded,** using the pilot rates for this segment:
- about 7 replies;
- about 3 to 4 positive replies (pilot: 3.4%);
- about 3 meetings (pilot: 38 meetings from 1,300, or 2.9%).

**If all 1,900 are loaded over time,** that is roughly 130 replies, 65 positive replies and 55 meetings.

**Check after the first 300 sends.** If replies sit below 6.8%, test the two step 1 subject lines against each other, and check that {{pricing_change}} names the real change. A vague line reads like the generic opener.

## Assumptions

- Send days are calendar days; weekend sends move to Monday.
- The one-page summary and three steps use only the approved anonymous case.
