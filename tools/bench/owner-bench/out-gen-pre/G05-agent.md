# Customer support specialist: job post, scorecard and application

## 1. The job post

### Customer Support Specialist (remote, 00:00 to 08:00 UTC)

We're hiring one person to help Basecamp and HEY customers by email, in the hours our team covers least. The pay is $82,000 a year, the same wherever you live. We work 40 hours a week, and we mean it.

**Why this job matters**
More than a quarter of our customers write to us between 00:00 and 08:00 UTC. Right now they wait a median of 7.1 hours for a first reply. The rest of the day, it's 35 to 41 minutes. You'll help close that gap.

**What you'll do**
- Answer customer emails about Basecamp and HEY in our own help desk tool.
- Fix the problem, not just reply. Try what they tried, and ask one clear question when needed.
- When something is broken, write it up clearly for the people who fix it, and tell the customer what happens next.
- Work in Basecamp and HEY. We write things down instead of holding meetings.

In your first year, we judge your work on two things: how happy customers are with your help, and what your teammates say.

**The hard parts**
- Your day runs 00:00 to 08:00 UTC. Most of the team is in the Americas and Europe and offline for much of it. You'll often make the call yourself and write down what you did.
- You work one weekend in six and get two weekdays off in return. That's about 8 or 9 weekends a year. If that doesn't fit your life, please tell us now, not in month two.
- Some customers are stressed. Staying calm and kind is the job.
- There's a lot to learn at first: two products and our help desk tool.

**What you need**
- You write clearly and warmly.
- You've helped people with problems in some job. Support, teaching, hospitality, retail: it all counts.
- You stay calm when someone else isn't.
- You can work on your own and keep track of loose ends.
- You can work 00:00 to 08:00 UTC and take your turn on weekends.

**What you don't need**
No degree, no years in support, no Zendesk experience. We'll teach you our tool. For our last nine support hires, years and tool experience told us almost nothing. A written customer reply told us the most, so that's what we ask for.

**Pay and time**
- $82,000 a year, the same wherever you live.
- Fully remote. 40-hour weeks, and 4-day weeks from May to August.

**How hiring works**
1. **Apply (about 30 minutes).** No resume. Confirm the hours, answer one customer email and one short question.
2. **Written exercise (60 minutes).** Answer three customer emails, when it suits you.
3. **Video call (45 minutes)** with two support team members. Everyone gets the same questions.
4. **Decision and offer.**

Applications are open for two weeks. Everyone hears back within three weeks of closing, yes or no. After steps 2 and 3, we reply within a week.

If you need a change to any step to take part fairly, tell us in the form. We will never ask you for money or bank details.

## 2. The scorecard

Score alone, right after each step. Use 1 to 5; 2 and 4 sit between. Weighted score = each score times its weight, added up, divided by 100. In the debrief, discuss only rows where reviewers differ by 2 or more.

| What we score | Weight | Where we see it | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Written reply | 40 | Application, step 2 | Wrong or missing answer, or cold and full of jargon | Correct and polite, but the answer or next step is buried | Answer first, correct, warm and short; the customer knows exactly what to do |
| Gets to the real fix | 20 | Step 2, call | Stock answer or a link, then closes | Solves the stated problem, misses the likely cause | Finds the real cause and says what happens next |
| Calm with upset people | 15 | Step 2 (one angry email), call | Defensive, or matches the customer's heat | Polite but stiff; skips what the problem cost them | Names what went wrong, owns our part, stays steady and kind |
| Works alone, in writing | 15 | Step 2 handover note, call | Waits to be told; vague notes | Handles clear cases alone; usable notes | Sound calls alone; notes a teammate can act on |
| Learns new things | 10 | Call | No example | One general example | Specific: what they learned, how, what changed |

**Not scored:** years, tool experience, degrees, past employers or impression. For our last nine hires, years (0.04), tool experience (0.07) and impression (0.11) barely tracked first-year results; the written reply did (0.62). Nine is a small sample, so recheck after this hire.

## 3. How the application should work

Last time, 2,100 resumes and cover letters took 160 reviewer hours. Now the application starts the written reply exercise.

**The form**
1. Can you work 00:00 to 08:00 UTC, and one weekend in six? Yes or no.
2. Reply to this customer in 200 words at most: "I can't see the project my teammate says she added me to. I have a client meeting in an hour."
3. A time you fixed someone's problem properly, not just quickly. 150 words at most.
4. Anything you need from us to take part fairly.

Reviewers see only an ID and answers 2 and 3.

**The review**
- First, all three reviewers score the same 10 replies alone, then agree what 1, 3 and 5 look like.
- From the day applications open, one reviewer scores each reply on the "Written reply" row only.
- Every 5 gets a second, blind reader. Two 5s go to step 2; a split goes to the third reviewer.
- A "no" on hours gets a kind decline, after a person checks.
- Aim for about 20 at step 2, which adds a short handover note.

**Working figures, if 2,100 apply again**

| Task | Sum | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Calibration | 3 people × 1 hour | 3 |
| First read | 2,100 × 2 minutes | 70 |
| Second read | 10% score 5: 210 × 2 minutes | 7 |
| Third read | half split: 105 × 2 minutes | 3.5 |
| **Total** | | **83.5** |

About half of last time: 28 hours each over five weeks. The 2 minutes and 10% are guesses; time the first 50 replies and adjust.

**To confirm**
- This hire covers 00:00 to 08:00 UTC, our slowest window. If not, change the title, hours lines and form question 1.
- The one-week reply after steps 2 and 3.
- Whether to pay for the step 2 exercise.
