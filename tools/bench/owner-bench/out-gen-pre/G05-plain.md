# Customer Support Specialist: job post, scorecard and application plan

**Assumptions.** This hire covers 00:00 to 08:00 UTC. That window gets 27% of tickets, but its median first response is 7.1 hours and satisfaction is 81%, against 35 to 41 minutes and 95 to 96% elsewhere. Only 1 of our 14 support people lives in Asia-Pacific. I estimate a reviewer can score a 200-word reply in about 2 minutes.

## 1. The job post

### Customer Support Specialist (remote, Asia-Pacific hours)

**$82,000 a year, the same wherever you live.**

When something goes wrong in Basecamp or HEY, customers write to us. Your job is to write back and fix it.

**What you'll do**
- Answer customer emails in our own help desk tool.
- Dig into problems until they make sense. Reproduce bugs and write clear notes for our programmers.
- Write things down for teammates instead of holding meetings.

**Your hours**
You'll work 00:00 to 08:00 UTC. That's 08:00 to 16:00 in Singapore, Manila or Perth. Right now these hours are our weak spot: customers who write then wait about 7 hours for a first reply. You'll help fix that.

We work 40 hours a week, and we mean it. From May to August, we work 4-day weeks. The job is fully remote.

**The weekend rotation**
You'll work one weekend in six and get two weekdays off in return. Past hires told us this wasn't clear when they applied, so we're saying it up front.

**The hard parts**
- You'll write all day, often to people who are stressed or angry.
- Few teammates are online during your hours. You'll often work things out alone and leave clear notes for the next shift.
- You'll start most days with an overnight queue.

**How we judge the work**
Not by tickets per hour. We look at how satisfied customers are and what your teammates say about working with you.

**Who we're looking for**
You don't need a degree, a support job title or experience with any help desk tool. If you've spent years explaining things to stressed people, in a classroom, a hotel or a restaurant, you already do a big part of this job. How you write to a customer tells us the most, so that's what we'll ask for.

**How to apply**
No resume. No cover letter. The form asks three things:
1. Can you work 00:00 to 08:00 UTC and one weekend in six? (Yes or no.)
2. Reply to this customer in 200 words or fewer:
   > "I've been locked out of my HEY account since this morning. I reset my password twice and never got the email. My client is waiting on an invoice I sent from that account. This is the second time this month. I'm paying for this. What is going on?"

   We're reading how you write, not whether you know the fix.
3. In 100 words or fewer, why this job?

**What happens next**
Applications are open for 2 weeks. We read every reply with your name hidden. Everyone hears back, yes or no, within 3 weeks of closing. If it's a yes, you'll do a longer written exercise, then two video conversations with the team.

## 2. The scorecard

Reviewers score alone, 1 to 5 per line, before any discussion. Multiply by the weight and add up (top score 5.0).

In our last 9 support hires, the written reply score predicted first-year performance (0.62). Interview impression (0.11), tool experience (0.07) and years in support (0.04) barely did. So we don't score tool experience, years or degrees. Hours and weekend rotation are a yes-or-no gate, not a score.

| What we score | Weight | Where we see it |
|---|---|---|
| Writing to customers | 50% | Application reply, longer exercise |
| Getting to the bottom of a problem | 20% | Longer exercise |
| Writing for teammates | 15% | A handoff note in the longer exercise |
| Conversation | 15% | Two video calls, same questions for all |

**Writing to customers (50%)**
- 1: Generic or defensive. Misses the real question, or hides behind policy.
- 3: Polite and correct, but reads like a template or buries the next step.
- 5: Sounds like a person. Names the real worry, gives one clear next step, sets an honest expectation, stays short.

**Getting to the bottom of a problem (20%)**
- 1: Guesses at a cause, or passes it on with no detail.
- 3: Asks sensible questions and checks the obvious, then stops at the first likely answer.
- 5: Separates what is known from what is guessed, checks it, and spots patterns across emails.

**Writing for teammates (15%)**
- 1: A programmer would need several follow-up questions to act.
- 3: The facts are there, but out of order or too long.
- 5: Someone starting the next shift cold can act on it without asking anything.

**Conversation (15%)**
- 1: Vague about hard customers. Unsure about the hours or working alone.
- 3: Real examples, and some thought about quiet hours with few people online.
- 5: Specific stories of fixing problems properly, a plan for working alone, real interest in calm, written work.

**Decision rule:** offer only at 4.0 or higher, with no line below 3.

## 3. How the application should work

Last round took 160 reviewer hours for 2,100 resumes and letters, about 4.6 minutes each.

1. **Put the best signal first.** The customer reply replaces the resume and letter.
2. **Gate on hours and weekends.** A "no" gets a kind automatic reply that day. This also targets early leavers: 2 of the 3 who left within a year said the rotation wasn't clear.
3. **Enforce word limits in the form.** 200 words for the reply, 100 for the "why."
4. **Calibrate, then read blind.** All three reviewers score the same 20 replies and discuss differences. Then hide names and split the pile. Each reply gets one score, on the "Writing to customers" scale only.
5. **Know the hours before you start.** At 2 minutes a reply, 2,100 replies take 70 hours, about 23 each. That's 90 hours less than last round. A second reviewer re-reads every 4 and 5; if 10% score that high, that adds about 7 hours.
6. **Shortlist small.** Send the top 15 to the longer exercise and the top 5 from that to conversations.
7. **Keep the promise.** Every applicant gets a yes or no within 3 weeks of closing. For the shortlist, "yes" means "you're through to the next step."
