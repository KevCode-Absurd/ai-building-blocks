# Where we lose to Semrush, and what to do about it

## The answer

We lose to Semrush in one place: **agencies**. Agencies make up 27% of new trials but 60% of all trials we lose to Semrush. They leave for client reports and PPC research in one tool, our two weakest areas by far.

We do not lose on data. SEOs who use both tools pick our backlink data 64% to 21%. Most other losses are about price and limits. Our biggest leak, Lite customers hitting a usage limit mid-project, has nothing to do with Semrush.

**Position to own:** the data SEOs trust, packaged into the report an agency sends every Monday. We should not try to match Semrush on PPC research this year.

How sure: high on where the losses sit (our own trial and churn data). Medium on which agency fix works, so move 1 starts with calls.

## Where we lose and where we don't

| Area | Result | What the data says |
|---|---|---|
| Agency trials | **Lose** | 54% of lost agency trials go to Semrush: 13 of every 100 trials we start. |
| PPC and ads research | **Lose** | 71% pick Semrush, 8% pick us. |
| Client reporting | **Lose** | 66% pick Semrush, 12% pick us. |
| Advanced plan | **Lose, small** | Top exit is "moved to Semrush" (28%), about 2% of all churn. |
| Backlink data | **Win** | 64% pick us, 21% pick Semrush. |
| Site audit | **Near tie** | 45% to 38%, at the edge of the survey's margin. |
| Keyword data | **Tie** | 41% to 44%. |
| In-house, solo, enterprise trials | **Mostly not Semrush** | 82%, 91% and 81% of these losses go elsewhere, driven by price, plan jumps and procurement. |

### How the numbers were worked

- **Trial losses per 100 new trials.** Trials times (1 minus trial-to-paid) times the Semrush share. Agency: 27 × 89% × 54% = 13.0. In-house: 5.2. Solo: 2.1. Enterprise: 1.3. Total: 21.6. Agency share: 13.0 ÷ 21.6 = 60%.
- **Monthly churn.** Weighted by plan share, total churn is 4.9% a month. Lite causes 70% of it. Lite usage-limit exits are 32% of all churn. Advanced moves to Semrush are 1.9%.
- **Free accounts.** 310,000 × 1.5% = 4,650 paid in 90 days. Multi-site accounts give 1,932 of them: 7% of accounts, 42% of conversions. The rest convert at 0.9%.
- **Survey margin.** With 600 people, a gap needs about 7 points to be clear. Backlinks, PPC and reporting pass. Site audit and keywords do not.

## Five moves, ranked by impact

### 1. Build the Monday client report for agencies

Agencies say: "I need one report I can send every Monday." Their clients "care about leads," not DR. Give them a scheduled, white-label report on rankings, traffic and leads.

- **Owner:** Product (reporting), with the agency growth team.
- **First step this month:** call 20 agency trials lost to Semrush last quarter. Ask: was it the report or the PPC research that decided it? Scope version one from the answers.
- **How we'll know:** agency trial-to-paid rises from 11% to 15% within two quarters of launch, and the share of agency losses going to Semrush falls below 45%. At 15%, that is 1.1 more paid customers per 100 trials, or 6.5% more new paid customers overall.

### 2. Stop the Lite usage cliff

Customers run out of credits mid-audit, and going up one plan costs too much. Offer a one-off usage top-up on Lite, so customers can finish a project without jumping a whole plan.

- **Owner:** Pricing, with the growth team.
- **First step this month:** pull every Lite account that left last quarter citing a usage limit. Find which limit they hit and when. Price the top-up from that.
- **How we'll know:** run it as a test on half of new Lite accounts. Success is Lite monthly churn falling from 7.9% to 6.5% within three months. That alone cuts total monthly churn from 4.9% to 4.3%.

### 3. Give multi-site free accounts their own onboarding

21,000 free accounts manage three or more domains. They convert at 9.2%, six times the average, yet get the same emails as everyone. Many look like agencies. Send them an agency track: multi-site projects and, once built, the Monday report.

- **Owner:** Lifecycle marketing.
- **First step this month:** tag the 21,000 accounts and split them in two. One half gets the new track. The other keeps today's emails.
- **How we'll know:** 90-day conversion in the new track reaches 10.7% or more against the control. Groups of 10,500 are large enough to spot that lift (about 6,250 each are needed). Each point of lift across 21,000 accounts is 210 more paid accounts per 90 days.

### 4. Clear the enterprise security review before it starts

Enterprise converts best (31%) but loses on procurement and security review. 19% of those losses go to Semrush. Have answers ready first.

- **Owner:** Sales, with Security.
- **First step this month:** list every enterprise trial lost in the last two quarters and where each stalled. Build one security pack from the common questions.
- **How we'll know:** enterprise trial-to-paid rises from 31% to 35% over the next two quarters, and average time in security review falls.

### 5. Publish the backlink data as proof, dated

Link data is our clearest win; in-house SEOs call it "the one I trust." Teach with it: publish a dated study of how we measure link data, method shown.

- **Owner:** Content marketing.
- **First step this month:** agree the study method and data period with the data team.
- **How we'll know:** in-house trial share rises above 38% and trial-to-paid holds at 24% or better in the quarter after release.

## What we should not do

- **Don't chase PPC research parity this year.** Semrush leads 71% to 8%. Move 1 tests whether a better report wins agencies back without it.
- **Don't discount for solo creators to beat Semrush.** Only 9% of their losses go to Semrush. The free Webmaster Tools tier already serves them.

## What this is built from

Our own trial, churn, survey and free account figures only. No live web data. Targets are thresholds to judge results, not forecasts.

**Assumptions:**

- "Losses" means trials that did not become paid.
- Without prices or trial volumes, moves are ranked by share of losses and churn, not revenue.
