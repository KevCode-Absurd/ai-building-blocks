# Ahrefs vs Semrush: where we lose, and five moves

Data as of November 2026: trials and losses for the last two quarters, churn by plan, a survey of 600 SEOs who used both tools in the last year, and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools (AWT) for the last 90 days.

## The short answer

We lose to Semrush in one place: agencies. They want client reports and PPC research in one tool, and Semrush wins both by wide margins. Almost everywhere else, our losses come from our own pricing and limits, not from Semrush.

## Where we lose

Per 100 new trials, about 22 end up with Semrush. Most of them are agencies.

| Segment | Share of trials | Trials lost to Semrush, per 100 trials | Share of all Semrush losses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency, 10+ clients | 27% | 13.0 | 60% |
| In-house SEO team | 38% | 5.2 | 24% |
| Solo creator | 25% | 2.1 | 10% |
| Enterprise | 10% | 1.3 | 6% |

Agencies: 27 trials x 89% not converting x 54% to Semrush = 13.0.

Agencies are 27% of trials but 60% of what we lose to Semrush. Their top loss reason is "client reports and PPC research in one tool." The survey agrees: Semrush's two biggest leads are PPC research (71% vs 8%) and client reporting (66% vs 12%).

After the sale, "moved to Semrush" is the top Advanced exit reason (28%). Agencies paying for both tools are one budget review from dropping one.

## Where we don't lose

- **Backlink data.** 64% pick us, 21% pick Semrush. A 43-point lead.
- **Site audit.** 45% vs 38%. A 7-point lead.
- **Keyword data.** 41% vs 44%. With 600 people, the margin of error is about 4 points, so this is a tie. Watch it.
- **In-house, solo and enterprise deals.** 82%, 91% and 81% of these losses do not go to Semrush. The reasons are our plan price jump, price for small sites, and procurement.

## Our biggest leak is not Semrush

Blended monthly churn is about 4.9%. Lite is 44% of customers but 70% of the customers who leave each month. Its top exit reason, "hit usage limit mid-project" (46%), alone causes about a third of all monthly churn. At 7.9% a month, only 37% of a Lite group is still with us after 12 months.

By count, Advanced customers leaving for Semrush are about 2% of monthly churn, though likely more by revenue.

## Five moves, ranked by impact

### 1. Become the agency's Monday report

**Owner:** Product (reporting), with agency sales.

Build a scheduled, branded client report. It should join our rank, traffic and link data with the client's own ad and analytics data, so it shows leads, not just DR. Do not try to match Semrush on PPC research now. That is a 63-point gap and a long build. The report is the reason agencies keep a second tool.

**First step this month:** Interview 20 agencies that chose Semrush in the last two quarters and 20 Advanced customers who left. Collect the actual report each one sends on Monday. Write the spec from those reports.

**How we'll know:** Agency trial-to-paid rises above 11%. The share of agency losses going to Semrush falls below 54%. Advanced exits to Semrush fall below 28%. If we win back a quarter of the agency trials now lost to Semrush, new paid customers rise from 16.7 to 19.9 per 100 trials, a 19% gain.

Ranked first because it moves agency trials, Advanced churn and Move 3 at once.

### 2. Take the cliff out of the Lite limit

**Owner:** Pricing, with Growth.

Hitting a limit mid-audit should cost a little more, not end the account. Add a warning before the limit and a paid credit top-up. Then review the jump to Standard, the top in-house loss reason.

**First step this month:** Pull every Lite account that hit its limit in the last 90 days. Offer a top-up to half of them at their next limit. Hold back the other half.

**How we'll know:** Lite churn tagged "usage limit" falls in the test group vs the holdout. Halving those exits would cut Lite churn from 7.9% to about 6.1%, and total monthly churn from 4.9% to about 4.1%.

### 3. Give multi-site AWT accounts their own path

**Owner:** Lifecycle marketing.

The 21,000 free accounts with 3 or more domains are 7% of AWT users but 42% of its paid conversions. They convert at 9.2%. Everyone else converts at about 0.9%. Yet they get the same emails as everyone else.

**First step this month:** Write a separate email track for this group. Focus it on managing many sites and on the client report from Move 1. Send it to half the group and hold back the rest.

**How we'll know:** 90-day conversion in the test half beats the holdout. Each one-point lift adds about 210 paid customers every 90 days.

### 4. Ship an enterprise procurement pack

**Owner:** Sales, with Security.

Enterprise converts best (31%), but 69 of every 100 enterprise trials still don't buy. The top reason is procurement and security review, not the product.

**First step this month:** List every security and legal question from lost enterprise deals in the last two quarters. Publish one ready pack: security answers, data handling and standard contract terms.

**How we'll know:** Enterprise trial-to-paid rises above 31%. Days from trial to signed contract fall. Losses tagged "procurement" fall.

### 5. Teach with our link data

**Owner:** Content marketing.

Backlinks are our clearest lead. Publish studies built on our own link data, each with a date and a clear method, aimed at in-house SEO leads. Point readers to free AWT. Don't name or attack Semrush.

**First step this month:** Pick one study topic, run it, and publish it with its date and method.

**How we'll know:** In-house share of trials rises above 38% and trial-to-paid above 24%.

## What we should not do

- **Chase PPC research parity now.** It is Semrush's biggest lead. Move 1 removes the main reason agencies need it.
- **Cut prices for solo creators.** They convert at 6% and 91% of their losses don't go to Semrush. A price cut won't win a fight we aren't in.

## Assumptions

- "Losses" means trials that did not convert to paid.
- Churn shares are by customer count. We have no revenue per plan.
- Multi-site AWT accounts are mostly agencies and freelancers. Move 3's test will confirm or reject this.
