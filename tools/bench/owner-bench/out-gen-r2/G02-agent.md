# Cal.com for recruiting teams: hero and landing page

## The segment to win: Team, recruiting and interviews

Recruiting teams are 5.5% of signups (2,100 of 38,000) but bring 57% of new paid seats: 2,100 × 9.4% paid × 7.8 seats = about 1,540 of 2,717 seats in 90 days.
Each recruiting signup is worth 0.73 paid seats, four times team sales demos (0.17) and 61 times solo coaching (0.012), yet today's page never shows a recruiting example.

**Assumptions**
- This is its own page for recruiting traffic, linked from the homepage menu. The homepage hero stays as it is.
- Round robin offers only times when an interviewer's connected calendar is free. I claim no hiring-tool integration or PTO feature beyond that.
- Every screen shown is from a demo account and marked "Example".

## The page, top to bottom

**Page title:** Interview scheduling for recruiting teams | Cal.com
**Meta description:** Candidates book the right interviewer from one link. Round robin spreads the load. Open source, so you can self-host.

### 1. Hero (Version A)

**Headline:** Let candidates book interviews without the back and forth
**Subhead:** Send one link. Candidates pick a time, and round robin gives it to the next free interviewer on your panel.
**Button:** Book a demo
**Proof line under the button:** 71% of recruiting teams who signed up in the last 90 days got their first booking within 7 days.
**Visual:** a real booking page, "Engineering interview, 45 min", with open times from a six-person panel. Button and proof line sit on the first phone screen.

### 2. One link instead of an email thread

**Headline:** Get your Monday back
**Copy:** Put one booking link in your outreach email. The candidate picks a time. The interview lands on both calendars. No more email tennis.
**Proof, beside the copy:** "41% of 1,240 people we surveyed went looking for a scheduling tool because of email back and forth."
**Visual:** a five-message email thread next to one link.
**Button:** Book a demo

### 3. Round robin that spreads the load

**Headline:** Share interviews fairly across your panel
**Copy:** Add all six interviewers to one interview type. Candidates see open times across the panel. Round robin books the next interviewer in line, so no one gets buried. If PTO is on someone's calendar, they're skipped.
**Proof, beside the copy:** "88% of paying recruiting teams use round robin in their first 30 days."
**Proof to collect for this spot:** a named recruiting customer's quote on fairer interview load, with written permission.
**Visual:** the round robin settings screen with six interviewers.
**Button:** Book a demo

### 4. Routing forms (moved up from section 6)

**Headline:** Send each candidate to the right interviewer
**Copy:** Ask two questions before booking, such as role and level. Their answers send them to the right panel. A senior engineer never lands on the sales panel.
**Proof, beside the copy:** "74% of paying recruiting teams use routing forms in their first 30 days."
**Visual:** a two-question form, then the booking page it sends the candidate to.
**Button:** Book a demo

### 5. Reminders

**Headline:** Reminders go out on their own
**Copy:** Set reminders for candidates and interviewers once. Every booking gets them. No one on your team has to chase.
**Proof, beside the copy:** "61% of paying recruiting teams turn on automated reminders in their first 30 days."
**Why it earns a section:** no-shows were the second reason people gave for looking (18%). The copy promises reminders, not fewer no-shows, since no figure backs that yet.
**Button:** Book a demo

### 6. Security (moved up from the footer)

**Headline:** Know where your data lives
**Copy:** Security will ask, so here is the answer. Cal.com is open source, so they can read the code. Run it on your own servers, or let us host it. Either way, you own your scheduling, with no lock-in.
**Button:** Read our security and compliance page
**Why here:** 48% of recruiting teams that later paid visited that page, now linked only from the footer. Name only the standards that page already lists.

### 7. Fits your hiring tools

**Headline:** Works where you already reach candidates
**Copy:** Your booking link is just a link. Paste it into your ATS email templates, job posts and recruiter signatures. Interviewers connect calendars once.
**Proof:** a screenshot of the link inside an ATS email template. Add logos only for integrations live today.
**Button:** Book a demo

### 8. Closing call to action and demo form

**Headline:** See it set up for a panel like yours
**Copy:** Tell us how many interviewers and interviews you run. We'll show you round robin and routing forms built for that.
**Form:** cut from 9 fields to 4: work email, company size, number of interviewers, interviews per week.
**Button:** Book a demo
**Under the button:** Open source. Host it yourself or with us. No lock-in.

## Two heroes to A/B test

| | Version A (control) | Version B |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Let candidates book interviews without the back and forth | Interviews shared fairly across your whole panel |
| Subhead | Send one link. Candidates pick a time, and round robin gives it to the next free interviewer on your panel. | Candidates book from one link. Round robin gives each slot to the next free interviewer, so no one gets buried. |
| Angle | The top reason people look: email back and forth (41%) | The feature paying recruiting teams use most: round robin (88%) |

Button, proof line and visual stay the same, so only the message changes.

**The one metric that decides the winner:** completed demo requests per unique visitor to the page.

**Stop rule:** run in whole weeks until each version has about 13,400 visitors, and do not stop early. Working: if the page converts near today's 3.1%, spotting a lift to 3.7% needs 16 × 0.031 × 0.969 ÷ 0.006² ≈ 13,400 visitors per version. Check paid recruiting accounts at 60 days, but don't decide on them.
