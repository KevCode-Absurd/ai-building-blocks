# Cold email sequence: Controllers after a pricing change

**The decision.** Send this sequence only to Controllers and Heads of Finance at SaaS companies with 50 to 300 people. Send it within two working days of a flagged pricing-page change. Expect about 8% replies (range 7% to 10%), against 3.1% across the whole pilot.

Three pilot numbers set this plan:

- **Who:** Controllers and Heads of Finance were 27% of pilot contacts but booked 38 of the 57 meetings (67%).
- **When:** a pricing-page change in the first line got 7.9% replies. A generic opener got 1.8%.
- **How:** a plain-text step 1 with no links got 4.1% replies, against 1.7% with a case study link and 1.2% with a calendar link. So no email carries a link.

## The sequence

Day 1 is the day the contact is loaded. Stop on any reply, bounce or opt-out. The sending tool adds the postal address and unsubscribe line.

Merge fields: `{{first_name}}`, `{{company}}`, and `{{change}}`, which the rep writes in plain words from the flag, such as "added a usage-based tier".

### Step 1. Day 1

**Subject option A:** {{company}}'s new pricing
**Subject option B:** Rev rec after a pricing change

Hi {{first_name}},

I saw {{company}} {{change}} on its pricing page.

New pricing usually lands on finance as a rebuilt rev rec model, sitting on billing logic engineering wrote years ago. Close gets a little longer each time.

One 140-person SaaS company was closing the month in 9 days. After moving billing and revenue recognition to Chargebee, it closes in 4.

Did the pricing change add days to your close?

{{rep_name}}
Chargebee

### Step 2. Day 4

**Subject:** The next pricing change

Hi {{first_name}},

The new pricing at {{company}} probably won't be the last. Sales will change tiers again, and the rev rec model gets rebuilt again.

When billing and revenue recognition sit in one system, a pricing change is set up once. It is not rebuilt in a spreadsheet at month-end.

That is how the 140-person team I mentioned got from a 9-day close to 4.

Worth 20 minutes to walk through how your new tiers would run from billing to rev rec?

{{rep_name}}
Chargebee

### Step 3. Day 9

**Subject:** Billing, engineering and the audit

Hi {{first_name}},

Two fair reasons finance teams hold off on this. Billing belongs to engineering. And nobody wants to touch billing in the middle of an audit.

Both are about timing and ownership. A move can be planned for after the audit, with engineering in the room from the start, so finance isn't carrying it alone.

The goal stays the same: fewer days at month-end and numbers you can trace back to billing.

Who else at {{company}} would need a say in a billing change?

{{rep_name}}
Chargebee

### Step 4. Day 16

**Subject:** Closing the loop

Hi {{first_name}},

I'll stop here, since I haven't heard back.

If the next pricing change turns month-end into nine days and three spreadsheets, I'm glad to show how a 140-person SaaS team took its close from 9 days to 4.

Should I check back after your next pricing change?

{{rep_name}}
Chargebee

## Who to load

1. **This week:** every Controller or Head of Finance among the 1,900 at SaaS companies with 50 to 300 people whose pricing page changed in the last 60 days (new tiers or usage-based pricing). The pilot measured this trigger over 60 days, so that is the window.
2. **Every week after:** the new names the data tool flags, loaded within two working days of the flag.
3. **Everyone else among the 1,900** waits on a watch list and enters the week their pricing page changes.

## Who to leave out

| Leave out | Pilot result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| CFOs, 1,000+ people | 0.2% positive replies, 3 meetings from 1,600 | "Send it to my team." Locked into an ERP. |
| CFOs, 50 to 300 people | 1.1% positive, 10 meetings from 1,100 | One third of the Controller rate. |
| RevOps leads, 50 to 300 people | 0.9% positive, 6 meetings from 800 | Weakest replies after large-company CFOs. |
| Controllers with no pricing change in 60 days | Not sent yet | Held on the watch list. A generic opener got 1.8%. |

## What to expect

This is a projection from the pilot, not a promise.

| Per 100 contacts loaded | Pilot, all contacts | This plan |
|---|---|---|
| Replies | 3.1 | 8 (range 7 to 10) |
| Positive replies | 1.4 | 4 (range 3.5 to 5) |
| Meetings booked | 1.2 | About 3.4 (range 3 to 4.3) |

**Working.** The floor is 7%: Controllers alone reached 6.8%, and the pricing trigger alone reached 7.9% with weak segments mixed in. The pilot never measured the two together. I have assumed the role adds at most a quarter on top of the trigger rate, which gives the 10% ceiling (7.9% x 1.25). Controllers turned half their replies into positive ones (3.4% of 6.8%), and 38 of their 44 positive replies booked a meeting (86%). Those two ratios give the positive reply and meeting rows.

**Assumption.** The flag will catch only part of the 1,900, so the numbers are per 100 contacts.

**What to watch:** replies and meetings per 100 after the first 200 sends, not opens.
