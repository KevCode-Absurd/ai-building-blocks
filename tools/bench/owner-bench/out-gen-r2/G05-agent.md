# Customer Support Specialist: post, scorecard and application

**Assumption:** this hire covers 00:00 to 08:00 UTC, the window with 27% of tickets, a 7.1-hour median first response and 81% satisfaction. Only 1 of 14 support people lives in Asia-Pacific.

## 1. The job post

### Customer Support Specialist (fully remote, 00:00 to 08:00 UTC)

We're hiring someone to help Basecamp and HEY customers while most of our team sleeps. If you like fixing problems properly, in writing, read on.

**The hard parts, up front**
- **The hours.** You work 00:00 to 08:00 UTC, Monday to Friday. Right now, people who write in those hours often wait hours for an answer. You're here to change that.
- **Weekends.** One weekend in six, you work Saturday and Sunday. You get two weekdays off in return. Please be sure this fits before you apply.
- **Fewer people online.** You'll often make the call yourself, then hand off in writing.
- **It's all writing.** Every answer, all day.

**What you'll do:** answer customer emails about Basecamp and HEY and fix the real problem, not just close the ticket. Flag it early when several customers report the same thing. Write handoff notes the next shift can act on.

**A normal week:** five eight-hour days in our own help desk tool. We talk in Basecamp and HEY, in writing, not meetings.

| Where you live | Your hours (summer in brackets) |
|---|---|
| Singapore, Manila, Perth | 08:00 to 16:00 |
| Tokyo | 09:00 to 17:00 |
| Sydney, Melbourne | 10:00 to 18:00 (11:00 to 19:00) |
| Auckland | 12:00 to 20:00 (13:00 to 21:00) |
| US Pacific | 16:00 to 00:00 (17:00 to 01:00) |

Live anywhere these hours work for you.

**What you need**
- You write clearly and kindly to people who are stressed.
- You keep digging until you find the real cause.
- You can work these hours, plus one weekend in six.

That's it. No degree. No support job title. No help desk experience. We'll teach you our tools. Explaining things to stressed people in a classroom or a hotel counts.

**How we judge the work:** in your first year, we look at how customers rate your help and what your teammates say about your work.

**Pay and terms:** $82,000 a year, the same wherever you live. Fully remote. We work 40 hours a week, and we mean it. Four-day weeks from May to August.

**The team:** 14 people. 8 in the Americas, 5 in Europe, 1 in Asia-Pacific. We hope you'll stay for years.

**How hiring works**
1. **Apply.** No resume, no cover letter. A few short questions and one customer reply (below), about 30 minutes. Applications are open for two weeks.
2. **Hear back.** Everyone hears from us, yes or no, within three weeks of the close.
3. **If you move on:** a 30-minute written exercise at home, then a 45-minute call with two of the team. Each step comes with a date for our reply.
4. **Offer.**

Need an adjustment, like more time? Tell us in the form. We will never ask you for money.

**Your application reply**

Priya writes: "I invited my client to our project yesterday. She says she never got the invite. We meet in three hours and she needs the files. I'm really stressed."

On our side, the invite went to dana@hartleydesgn.com. Priya's own emails give her client's address as dana@hartleydesign.com. You can fix the address and re-send the invite.

Write your reply to Priya in 200 words or fewer. You don't need to know Basecamp.

## 2. Interview scorecard

**Weight rule:** across our last 9 hires, the written reply tracked first-year performance at 0.62, interview impression at 0.11. So written work carries 85%. Tool experience (0.07) and years in support (0.04) carry nothing.

| What we score (weight, from) | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clear, warm writing** (35%, both written tasks) | Hard to follow, canned or cold. | Clear, polite, a bit stiff. | Short, plain, warm. Answers the worry first. One next step. |
| **Fixes the real problem** (30%, Priya reply) | Deflects ("check your spam") or misses the typo. | Finds the typo and re-sends. Ignores the deadline. | Fixes the cause, helps her make the meeting, says what to do if it still fails. |
| **Owns the quiet hours** (20%, shortlist exercise) | Promises what it can't, or a weak handoff. | Honest reply. Handoff lists the facts. | Flags a wider problem now. Tells Sam what's known and when he'll hear next. Handoff needs no follow-up. |
| **Calm judgment** (15%, call) | No real example. | A real example with clear steps. | A real example. Checks what the person understood. Says what they'd change. |

**Shortlist exercise (30 minutes):** It's 03:00 UTC. Sam writes: "None of my team can log in. We've tried everything." In the last hour, three other customers wrote about login trouble. Your own login works. The next shift starts at 08:00 UTC. Write (a) your reply to Sam and (b) your handoff note for that shift.

**Call questions, same order for everyone:**
1. "Tell us about a time you helped someone upset and confused. What did you do first? What would you change?"
2. "Tell us about a time you had to decide something alone because nobody was around to ask."

**Not scored:** years in support, help desk tools, degrees, overall impression. **Pass or fail:** can work the stated hours and one weekend in six.

Each person scores alone, before discussion. Discuss only rows two or more points apart. Total = each score times its weight, out of 5.

## 3. How the application should work

1. **The form.** Name, email, time zone. A yes or no on the hours and weekend rotation; a "no" ends the form with a kind note. The Priya reply, capped at 200 words. Optional: "Anything else we should know?" (100 words) and "Any adjustment you need?"
2. **Read blind.** Reviewers see only the reply, no name. They score the first two rows.
3. **Calibrate first.** All three reviewers score the same 20 replies and agree on a 1, 3 and 5.
4. **Read daily while open,** split three ways.
5. **Second read.** Replies averaging 4 or more get a second reviewer. If both agree, that person gets the shortlist exercise.
6. **Close the loop.** Everyone else gets a kind, final note within three weeks.
7. **Repeat the weekend rotation** on the call and in the offer. Two of three first-year leavers said it wasn't clear.

**Projection:** if 2,100 apply again and a first read takes 2 minutes (assumed), first reads take 70 reviewer hours, not 160.
