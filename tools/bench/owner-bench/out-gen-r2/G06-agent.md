# Ahrefs vs Semrush: where we lose, where we don't, and what to do

## The answer

**We lose to Semrush in one place: agencies.** Agencies are 27% of new trials but 60% of the trials we lose to Semrush. They leave for client reports and PPC research in one tool. Elsewhere, Semrush is a minor cause of loss.

**The bad news: our biggest customer leak is not Semrush.** It is our own Lite plan. Lite holds 44% of paying customers and churns 7.9% a month. That is 70% of the customers who churn each month. The top exit reason is "hit usage limit mid-project" (46%). Semrush is the top exit reason on one plan only, Advanced, and that is about 9 customers per 10,000 a month.

**Position to own:** the SEO data marketers trust, packaged so an agency can send it to a client every Monday.

**First move this month:** split the 21,000 multi-site Webmaster Tools accounts into their own onboarding track. They already convert at 9.2%, six times the free-tier average, and get the same emails as everyone else.

## Where we lose

Per 1,000 new trials, last two quarters:

| Segment | Trials | Paid | Lost | Lost to Semrush | Share of all Semrush losses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agency, 10+ clients | 270 | 30 | 240 | 130 | 60% |
| In-house SEO team | 380 | 91 | 289 | 52 | 24% |
| Solo creator | 250 | 15 | 235 | 21 | 10% |
| Enterprise | 100 | 31 | 69 | 13 | 6% |
| **Total** | **1,000** | **167** | **833** | **216** | **100%** |

Here's the data on why. Among 600 SEOs who used both tools, Semrush leads on PPC research (71% to 8%) and client reporting (66% to 12%). Those are the two things agencies say they need.

## Where we don't lose

- **Backlink data.** 64% say we're better, 21% say Semrush. That is 3 to 1.
- **Site audit.** 45% to 38%, a lead.
- **Keyword data.** 41% to 44%. With 600 people, the margin of error is about 4 points, so call it a tie.
- **In-house teams.** 82% of their lost trials go somewhere other than Semrush. Their top reason is our price jump between plans.
- **Solo creators.** 91% of their losses are not Semrush. They say we're too expensive for their traffic.

Outside agencies, our losses are self-inflicted: plan limits, plan jumps and enterprise paperwork. Those are cheaper to fix than a rival's lead.

## Four moves, ranked

Rule: paying customers won or kept per unit of effort, quickest money first. Each projection rests on one named assumed input. Projections, not results.

### 1. Give multi-site free accounts their own onboarding track

- **Why:** 21,000 accounts (7% of free users) produce 1,932 of 4,650 paid conversions in 90 days (42%). Running 3+ domains, many are likely agencies and freelancers: the segment Semrush wins.
- **Owning team:** Lifecycle marketing.
- **First step this month:** tag every account with 3+ verified domains. Write a 5-email track that teaches how to report SEO progress to clients as traffic and leads. Hold back 10% on the current emails as a control.
- **How we'll know:** 90-day conversion of the track vs the control. Projection: if conversion rises from 9.2% to 11% to 12% (assumed lift), that is 378 to 588 extra paid accounts per 90 days.

### 2. Let Lite users buy more usage instead of jumping a plan or leaving

- **Why:** usage-limit exits are 3.6% of Lite customers every month, about 160 per 10,000 paying customers. In-house trials name the price jump between plans as their top loss reason. The persona: "I ran out of credits halfway through an audit." One fix answers both: a usage top-up on Lite.
- **Owning team:** Pricing, with Product.
- **First step this month:** add an in-app alert at 80% of the Lite limit. Offer a one-off top-up to half of Lite accounts. Keep the other half as a control.
- **How we'll know:** Lite monthly churn and the share of exits citing usage limits. Projection: if the top-up saves 30% to 50% of usage-limit exits (assumed save rate), Lite churn falls from 7.9% to 6.1% to 6.8%. That keeps 48 to 80 customers per 10,000 each month.

### 3. Ship a ready security and procurement pack for enterprise

- **Why:** enterprise trials convert best (31%), and the top loss reason is procurement and security review. That is paperwork, not product.
- **Owning team:** Sales, with Security.
- **First step this month:** pull the security questionnaires from enterprise deals lost in the last two quarters. Write standard answers to every repeated question. Send the pack on trial day one.
- **How we'll know:** days in security review, and enterprise trial-to-paid. Projection: if the pack wins 5% to 10% of the 69 lost enterprise trials per 1,000 (assumed win rate), that adds 3 to 7 customers to today's 31.

### 4. Build the Monday client report agencies send without editing

- **Why:** this is the one place Semrush beats us on product. 54% of agency losses go to Semrush. As the agency persona says: "My clients don't care about DR. They care about leads." The report leads with traffic and leads.
- **Owning team:** Product.
- **First step this month:** interview 20 agency trials lost to Semrush in the last two quarters. Ask what their Monday report contains, line by line. Spec from those answers only.
- **How we'll know:** agency trial-to-paid (now 11%) and the share of agency losses going to Semrush (now 54%). Also watch Advanced churn to Semrush (28% of Advanced exits). Projection: if the report wins back 10% to 20% of Semrush-bound agency losses (assumed), that is 13 to 26 more paid per 1,000 trials. Overall trial-to-paid would go from 16.7% to 18.0% to 19.3%.

It ranks last because it takes longest to build, not because it matters least.

## What to stop

Stop any plan to match Semrush on PPC research. They lead 71% to 8%, and closing that gap is a long build against their clear strength. We accept losing agencies who must have PPC in one tool.

## Assumptions

- "Losses" means trials that did not convert to paid in the same two quarters.
- Segment shares and rates apply to one shared trial pool, so the per-1,000 figures are comparable.
- The survey covers only SEOs who used both tools, so it shows preference among people who know both, not the whole market.
