# Todoist onboarding series: 5 emails

**The decision:** build the series around two habits. Users who typed a recurring task and signed in on a second device in week one were 67% active at day 30. Only 8% do both today. Users who added just 1 to 4 tasks were 11% active, and they are 44% of signups. Each email pushes one step toward those habits.

**What goes:** drop the shortcuts, team invite and Pro upgrade emails, and replace projects and labels.

## The series at a glance

| # | Trigger or send day | What it gets the user to do | How we'll know it worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Signup, sent at once | Add 5 tasks | First task within 24 hours; share with 5+ tasks by day 7 (now 31%) |
| 2 | 1 day after first task added | Type a recurring task | Recurring task within 3 days of send (now 14% of signups in week one) |
| 3 | 1 day after recurring task created; day 4 if none yet. Two versions by signup platform | Sign in on a second device | Second device within 3 days of send (now 22%); both habits by day 14 (now 8% by day 7) |
| 4 | 3 days with no activity, once only | Add one task | Any activity within 3 days of send |
| 5 | Day 10, only users with no recurring task | Type a recurring task | Recurring task within 3 days of send |

**Rules:** skip any email whose goal action already happened. Send at most one email in 24 hours; if two fall due, the triggered one goes first. Judge on clicks and goal actions, not opens.

## The emails

### Email 1: Welcome

**Subject:** Get it out of your head (23)
**Preview:** Start with five things on your mind.
**Screen:** the add-task field on their signup platform, with "Call the dentist" typed in.

Welcome to Todoist.

Your to-do list may live in a few places right now. Notes, emails, the back of a receipt. Let's put it in one.

Today's tip: add five things that are on your mind. Big or small, it doesn't matter. Type them as they come:

- "Call the dentist"
- "Buy milk"
- "Send Sam the report"

No need to sort them yet.

**Button:** Add five tasks

### Email 2: Repeating tasks

**Subject:** Type it once. Todoist repeats it. (33)
**Preview:** Add how often in plain words and you're done.
**Screen:** the add-task field with "Pay rent every 1st" typed, the date picked out.

Some tasks come back again and again. You don't need to add them each time.

Today's tip: type how often, right in the task.

- "Pay rent every 1st"
- "Take out the bins every Monday"
- "Water the plants every 3 days"

Todoist reads the date and brings the task back when it's due. Think of one thing you do every week. Add it now.

**Button:** Add a repeating task

### Email 3A: Second device (web and desktop signups, 59%)

**Subject:** Your list, in your pocket (25)
**Preview:** Add things while you're out. They'll be here.
**Screen:** a phone showing the same tasks as their computer.

Tasks show up when you're away from your desk. In line at the store. On the bus. Walking out of a meeting.

Today's tip: put Todoist on your phone and sign in with the same account. Everything you added here is already there. Change it on one, and the other keeps up.

Next time you're in line, try "Pick up dry cleaning Friday".

**Button:** Get the phone app

### Email 3B: Second device (mobile signups, 41%)

**Subject:** When you're at a computer (25)
**Preview:** Same list, bigger screen. Nothing to set up.
**Screen:** todoist.com in a browser, showing the same tasks as their phone.

You add tasks on the go. That's what your phone is for.

For the times you do sit at a computer, at work, school or home, your list can be there too.

Today's tip: open todoist.com in any browser and sign in with the same account. All your tasks are already there. Anything you change shows up on your phone.

**Button:** Open Todoist on the web

Phone-first users are rarely at a computer, so this version never says they should be.

### Email 4: Restart small

**Subject:** Start again with one thing (26)
**Preview:** No need to catch up. Just add what's next.
**Screen:** an Inbox with one task in it.

Weeks get busy. Lists get messy. That's normal.

You don't need to fix your whole setup. Today's tip: add the one thing you most need to remember right now. Just one.

- "Book the car in for a service"
- "Reply to Jo about Saturday"

Todoist will hold it, so your head doesn't have to.

**Button:** Add one task

### Email 5: Your weekly rhythm

**Subject:** Three tasks you'll never retype (31)
**Preview:** Find the ones that repeat. Type how often.
**Screen:** a list with three recurring tasks, each showing its next date.

Most weeks have a rhythm. The things that come back are the easiest to forget, and the easiest to hand to Todoist.

Today's tip: pick three things that return every week. Type them with how often:

- "Call Mom every Sunday"
- "Team check-in every weekday at 9am"
- "Pay phone bill every 15th"

**Button:** Add a repeating task

All emails sign off "The Todoist team" with the usual unsubscribe link.

## What to drop from the current series, and why

| Current email | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1, Projects and labels | Replace with email 2 | 6.1% web clicks, but labels can't be tracked or tied to retention. Setup is the "perfect on Sunday, gone by Wednesday" trap. Recurring tasks mean 52% retention. |
| Day 2, 10 keyboard shortcuts | Drop | 0.3% clicks from mobile signups, 41% of users. Ten tips breaks the one-tip rule. |
| Day 4, Invite your team | Drop | Worst unsubscribe rate (1.4%), 0.9% clicks. Shared projects reach 6% of signups at 34% retention, below both habits, and can't be triggered. |
| Day 7, Upgrade to Pro | Remove from onboarding | 1.1% clicks against 1.1% unsubscribes: one lost subscriber per click. It asks for money before most users have a habit. |
| Day 0, Welcome | Keep the slot, rewrite | 22% and 19% clicks show the moment works. The ask becomes five tasks: 29% retention against 11%. |

## How we'll know the series works

Keep 10% of new signups on the current series for 8 weeks, about 11,000 people at last quarter's pace. Compare the two groups on share doing both habits by day 14, day-30 retention and unsubscribes per email.

## Assumptions

- The email tool can filter each email on the listed events and split by signup platform.
- Email 4 sends once per user, using the tool's own "no activity" rule.
