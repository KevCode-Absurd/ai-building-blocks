# Rothy's Google plan for the next 30 days

## The short answer

Your 4.0 ROAS (sales per ad dollar) is real, but half of it is people already searching "Rothy's". Brand terms cost $26,700 and brought $479,520. Everything else cost $213,300 and brought $482,880. That is a 2.26 ROAS.

You keep 40% of each sale, so break-even is 2.5 (1 ÷ 0.40). Non-brand Google lost about $20,000 last month ($482,880 × 0.40 − $213,300). All your Google profit came from brand searches.

More bad news: 212 pointed flat variants (sizes 5 to 7.5) show no ads. Those sizes drive about 16% of site revenue (46% × 34%).

The plan: fix the feed on Day 1, stop paying Performance Max (PMax) for brand searches and traffic below 2.5, and move that money to bags, "washable" and all-day comfort searches.

## Feed and title fixes

**1. Price mismatch (Day 1).** Google disapproves a product when the feed price differs from the page price. Find what changed on the site on 21 October for sizes 5 to 7.5: a price edit, a sale or an app update. Make each variant's feed price match its page price exactly, and make each variant link open that size. Turn on automatic item updates as a backstop, then request a review. Google rechecks within hours to days.

**2. Titles.** Google matches searches mainly from the title. Put searched words first: product type, washable, comfort use, material, colour, size, then brand.

- Now: "The Pointed Flat | Black"
- New: "Women's Pointed Flats - Washable, Comfortable Work Flats - Recycled Bottle Knit - Black - Size 6 - Rothy's" (106 characters)

Bags follow the same order. Descriptions open with "Made to walk, made to wash." then the fact: knit from thread spun from recycled plastic bottles. Load titles as a supplemental feed on half the pointed flat items. After 14 days, compare impressions and clicks with the other half, then roll out.

## First week, in order

1. **Day 1:** Fix the price mismatch and request review.
2. **Day 1:** Raise bags Shopping to $967 a day. Remove bags from PMax listing groups so they stop competing.
3. **Day 1:** Raise brand Search to $800 a day. Once it is serving, turn on the Rothy's brand exclusion in PMax, so brand searchers always see an ad.
4. **Day 2:** Change PMax in one edit, so it relearns once: remove image and video assets. Turn off auto-created assets and final URL expansion. Add "sustainable shoes" and its variants from the search report as negative keywords. Set target ROAS 3.0 and $3,667 a day.
5. **Day 3:** Launch the new Search campaign: one ad group for "washable shoes" and "machine washable flats", one for "comfortable work flats" and "flats for standing all day", with "rothys" as a negative. Start on Maximize conversion value; set target ROAS 3.0 after 15 conversions. Headlines lead with comfort and washing.
6. **Day 4:** Load the new titles on half the pointed flat items.
7. **Day 7:** Read each line's 7-day ROAS against 2.5, store new-customer orders against the week before, and disapprovals, which should be zero.

**Stop rules.** If PMax is under 2.5 on Days 8 to 14, move $1,000 a day of its budget to the reserve. If store new-customer orders fall more than 10% and brand Search did not pick them up, turn the brand exclusion off.

**Assumptions.** Google's conversion values match store sales, and the 40% contribution holds across products. The price mismatch is a feed or page error, not a real price gap by size.
