# Decision memo: 3-month prepaid plan test

**To:** Leadership team | **From:** Finance | **Date:** 12 November 2026
*Plan figures are hypothetical exercise numbers, not company data. Policy facts are from gruns.co, checked 12 November 2026.*

## Recommendation

**Run the 60-day test, with three design rules. Keep the plan only if it brings in about 3% or more new subscribers in total, not just a different mix of the same subscribers.**

On these numbers, a prepaid customer is worth **$23.60 less** than a regular subscriber over six boxes, even if prepaid buyers renew as well as regular subscribers stay. It only pays if it wins customers we would not otherwise get.

1. **When the term ends, prepaid rolls onto the regular $79 plan, not into another $199 term.** A second discounted term gives away about $28 per renewing customer over boxes 4–6.
2. **Run it as a randomized split.** Half of new visitors see the regular plan only (control) and half see both plans (test), with the same marketing spend. Without a control group, we can't tell new customers from customers who just switched plans.
3. **Price is the only change.** Both arms keep our 30-day money-back guarantee, which refunds the whole first order, so $199 on prepaid. After day 30, cancelling prepaid stops the roll-on; paid boxes still ship, with no partial refunds. No stacking with promo codes.

## The math: contribution per customer over six boxes

We ship every 4 weeks, so a "month" here is one 4-week box cycle. The prepaid term covers boxes 1–3 and ends at **day 84**.

Contribution per box is $79 − $15 product − $9 fulfillment = **$55** on the regular plan. Prepaid is $199 − (3 × $24) = **$127** for the first three boxes.

**Renewal assumption:** at the end of the term, **75.4%** of prepaid buyers continue at $79, the same share of regular subscribers still active at box 4. Then churn is 6% per cycle. This is generous, because renewal dates usually show a drop-off.

| Box | Regular: still active | Regular contribution | Prepaid: still active | Prepaid contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100.0% | $55.00 | 100% | $127.00 (paid up front) |
| 2 | 91.0% | $50.05 | 100% | included |
| 3 | 82.8% | $45.55 | 100% | included |
| 4 | 75.4% | $41.45 | 75.4% | $41.45 |
| 5 | 70.8% | $38.96 | 70.8% | $38.96 |
| 6 | 66.6% | $36.62 | 66.6% | $36.62 |
| **Total** | 4.87 boxes, $384.40 revenue | **$267.62** | 5.13 boxes, $367.09 revenue | **$244.03** |

*Rows are rounded. Totals use unrounded figures.*

**Why prepaid loses $23.60:** over boxes 1–3 it brings in $17.31 less revenue ($199 vs $216.31 expected), and ships 0.26 more boxes ($6.29). $17.31 + $6.29 = $23.60.

**Sensitivity**, with 30% of new subscribers choosing prepaid:

| Prepaid buyers continuing to box 4 | Prepaid 6-box contribution | vs regular | Lift in total new subscribers needed to break even |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90.6% (break-even) | $267.62 | $0 | 0% |
| **75.4% (base case)** | **$244.03** | **−$23.60** | **+2.7%** |
| 60% (renewal drop-off) | $220.18 | −$47.44 | +5.6% |
| 45% | $196.88 | −$70.74 | +8.6% |

These leave out payment fees, refunds and CAC. Marketing spend is equal in both arms, so the lift column already counts spreading that spend over more subscribers.

## Risks

1. **Cannibalization (the main risk).** Committed customers who would have stayed anyway take the 16% discount. The control group measures this.
2. **Skimming hides in the mix.** If prepaid pulls our best customers away, regular-plan retention in the test arm drops. Judge the whole arm, not each plan alone.
3. **Bigger refunds.** A guarantee refund on prepaid reverses $199, not $79. Prepaid cash is deferred revenue, recognized as each box ships, and unshipped boxes are a refund liability.
4. **Auto-renewal rules.** Going from $199 to $79 is both the end of a discounted price and a fee change under California law (Bus. & Prof. Code §17602). The law requires a notice before each, and its 1 July 2025 update added the fee-change notice. We need separate consent to the roll-on at checkout and a click-to-cancel link. We also send a notice **7 to 21 days before the first $79 charge** (day 63 to 77), which meets both the 3–21 day and 7–30 day rules. It goes to every prepaid buyer.

## What we'd need to see at day 60 to keep it

| Measure (test arm vs control) | Keep if |
|---|---|
| Projected 6-box contribution per visitor (model above) | Test ≥ control |
| Total new subscribers per visitor | At least +3%, and the 95% confidence interval excludes zero |
| Box-2 retention of *regular* subscribers in the test arm | Within 1 point of control (otherwise prepaid is skimming) |
| Prepaid buyers who have turned off the roll-on by day 60 | 25% or less (in line with at least 75% renewal) |
| Prepaid money-back refunds | No more than 1 point above control's first-order refund rate |

**Kill early** if contribution per visitor in the test arm is clearly below control at day 30, or prepaid refunds pass 5%.

**Sample size:** a 2.7% lift is small. At a 5% checkout conversion rate, detecting it needs about 400,000 visitors per arm (about 120,000 for a 5% lift). If 60 days can't reach that, extend the test.

**Timing:** day 60 decides whether the test keeps running. The renewal drop-off can't be seen until day 84, so the final keep decision comes at about day 95, once early buyers have passed their first $79 charge.
