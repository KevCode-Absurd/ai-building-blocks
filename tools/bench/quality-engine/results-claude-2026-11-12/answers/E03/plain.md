# Product Support Engineer

**Remote, North American time zones · Salary: [range]**

Software teams use Linear to plan and build their products. When something doesn't work the way they expect, they write to us. Many of the people writing in are developers and product managers who know their tools well and want a precise answer, not a script.

As a Product Support Engineer, you'll answer those questions, dig into reports that look like bugs, and turn what you find into issues our engineers can act on without needing to ask a follow-up question. You'll also improve our help docs so the next person can find the answer without writing in.

## Your first 90 days

**Days 1 to 30.** Learn Linear in depth: issues, projects, cycles, workflows, integrations and the API. Read past conversations, pair with the support team, and start answering questions with someone reviewing your replies.

**Days 31 to 60.** Take a full share of the queue. Reproduce reported bugs on your own, write them up for engineering, and follow each one through to a fix or a clear answer for the customer.

**Days 61 to 90.** Pick an area, such as integrations or imports, and become the person the team turns to for it. Ship improvements to the help docs based on what customers ask most often. Bring the patterns you're seeing to product and engineering.

## What we're looking for

- Two or more years in support engineering, technical support or a similar role at a software company.
- You're comfortable with the technical side of a product: APIs, webhooks, JSON, browser developer tools and how teams use Git.
- You reproduce problems methodically. You narrow a vague report down to clear steps, the expected result and the actual result.
- You write clearly and briefly, and you match your tone to the person you're helping.
- You know when to solve something yourself and when to bring in an engineer, and you give them what they need when you do.
- You work well on a remote team that relies on writing.

## Nice to have

- You've used Linear or a similar issue tracker on a software team.
- You can write small scripts in JavaScript, TypeScript or Python, or you've worked with GraphQL.
- You've written or maintained help docs or a knowledge base.
- You've worked as a developer, QA engineer or product manager.

## How the interview process works

1. **Intro call (30 minutes).** A conversation with someone on the support team about your background and this role. Bring your questions.
2. **Written exercise.** You'll answer a few customer questions like the ones we get and turn a messy bug report into a clear issue. It should take about two hours, on your own schedule.
3. **Technical conversation (60 minutes).** You'll work through a bug with one of our engineers: reproducing it, narrowing it down and deciding what to send to engineering.
4. **Final conversation (45 minutes).** You'll talk with me, the head of support, about how you work, what you want from your next role and anything still open from earlier steps.

We'll keep you updated after each step, and you'll hear back from us either way.

**To apply,** send your resume and a short note about a support problem you solved that you're proud of.
