# Product Support Engineer

**Remote, North American time zones · Salary: [range]**

Software teams use Linear to plan and build their products. When something doesn't work the way they expect, they write to us. Most are developers and product managers who know their tools well and want a precise answer, not a script.

You'll answer those questions, dig into reports that look like bugs, and turn what you find into issues our engineers can act on without a follow-up question. You'll also improve our help docs so the next person finds the answer without writing in.

## Your first 90 days

**Days 1 to 30.** Learn Linear in depth: issues, projects, cycles, workflows, integrations and the API. Read past conversations, pair with the support team, and answer questions with someone reviewing your replies.

**Days 31 to 60.** Take a full share of the queue. Reproduce reported bugs on your own, write them up for engineering, and follow each one through to a fix or a clear answer for the customer.

**Days 61 to 90.** Own an area, such as integrations or imports, and become the person the team turns to for it. Update the help docs based on what customers ask most. Bring the patterns you see to product and engineering.

## What we're looking for

- Experience troubleshooting software for its users, in support engineering, technical support, QA or a similar role.
- Comfort with the technical side of a product: APIs, webhooks, JSON, browser developer tools and how teams use Git.
- A methodical way of reproducing problems. You turn a vague report into clear steps, the expected result and the actual result.
- Clear, brief writing, with a tone that fits the person you're helping.
- Judgment about when to solve something yourself and when to bring in an engineer, and what to hand them when you do.
- Comfort on a remote team that works mostly in writing.

## Nice to have

- You've used Linear or a similar issue tracker on a software team.
- You can write small scripts in JavaScript, TypeScript or Python, or you've worked with GraphQL.
- You've written or maintained help docs.

## How the interview process works

1. **Intro call (30 minutes).** You and I talk about your background, the role and the salary range. Bring your questions.
2. **Support conversation (45 minutes).** With someone on the support team, you'll talk through how you'd answer a few real-style customer questions.
3. **Technical conversation (60 minutes).** With one of our engineers, you'll work through a bug: reproduce it, narrow it down and decide what to send to engineering.
4. **Paid work trial (2 to 5 days).** You'll do real support work alongside the team, on a schedule we agree together. We share the daily rate before you start.

Most candidates go from first call to offer in four to six weeks. You'll hear from us after every step, either way.

If you need an adjustment at any step, such as more time or a different format, tell us and we'll arrange it. We will never ask you for money or bank details to apply.

**To apply,** send your resume and a short note about a support problem you solved that you're proud of.
