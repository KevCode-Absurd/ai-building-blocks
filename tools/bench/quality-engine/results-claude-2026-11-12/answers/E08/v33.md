# Why small-business accounts go inactive in year one, and what to test next

**To:** Strategy & Research
**Re:** First-year inactivity in the Q1 small-business cohort
**Note:** All cohort figures below are hypothetical exercise numbers, not Ramp data.

## Summary

Of 2,000 small-business accounts opened in Q1, 520 (26%) were inactive by month 12. About two-thirds of those (roughly 333 accounts, or 64%) were tagged with reasons we can plausibly influence: low card adoption or no accounting connection. The other third closed, were acquired, or moved spend to a competitor.

Our main hypothesis: accounts that never spread Ramp past the founder's own card, and never connect it to the books, never make Ramp part of how the business runs. Next quarter we should split new accounts into three equal cells (control, a card-adoption program, an accounting-setup program) and test spend-drop outreach on existing accounts. Each test is judged on one early metric within 60 days, with ship rules set in advance. Twelve-month retention confirms later. We should also fix how we define and tag inactivity.

## What the data says

| Group | Accounts | Share of cohort |
|---|---|---|
| Opened in Q1 | 2,000 | 100% |
| Active at month 12 | 1,480 | 74% |
| Inactive at month 12 | 520 | 26% |
| – Low adoption (≤2 cards) | ~213 | 10.7% |
| – Never connected accounting | ~120 | 6.0% |
| – Closed or acquired | ~94 | 4.7% |
| – Moved spend elsewhere | ~94 | 4.7% |

*Reason counts are rounded from the percentages, so they add up to 521.*

Active accounts averaged 9 cards issued, and 92% had accounting connected. They look like teams using Ramp. Many inactive accounts look like one person trying a card.

Price is not what keeps these accounts small. Ramp's Free plan is $0 per user, and cards are unlimited at no extra cost (ramp.com/pricing, checked 12 Nov 2026). An account with two cards could have twenty for the same price, so the gap is more likely setup and habit than cost.

If we cut the two controllable groups in half, inactivity would drop from 26% to about 18%. That's the size of the prize.

## What the data does not tell us

**It shows correlation, not cause.** Nine cards and a live accounting sync may be signs of a larger, more organized business, not reasons it stays.

**We see one reason per account.** An account that issued two cards, never connected accounting, and then moved to a competitor fits all three buckets. "Moved spend" may often be the end point of low adoption, not a separate cause.

**We lack the same measures for inactive accounts.** We don't know the card count or connection rate across *all* inactive accounts, only for the ones tagged that way. So we can't say how strongly these traits predict going inactive.

**The tags are subjective.** Account managers chose them after the fact. "Low adoption" is the easiest tag when nobody knows the real reason.

**There's no timing or segment view.** We don't know whether accounts dropped off in month 2 or month 11, or how results split by size or industry.

**"Inactive" is not defined here.** A free account never cancels, so inactivity shows up only as missing spend. We need one rule, for example no card spend or bill payments for 60 days.

## Three hypotheses, ranked

### 1. Accounts that stay with one or two cards never become the company's spend system

**Why it ranks first:** It's the biggest bucket (41% of inactive accounts), it's controllable, and the gap is wide (9 cards vs. two or fewer). It may also explain some "moved spend" accounts.

**Experiment:** Randomize new accounts into control or a "spread the card" program. The program asks the admin during setup to add employees and set up cards for recurring software bills. Accounts with fewer than three active cards get a prompt at day 14. Accounts still at two or fewer get a short account manager call at day 30.

**Success metrics:**
- Primary: share of accounts with 3+ active cards by day 60
- Secondary: card spend in each of weeks 6 through 12; distinct cardholders
- Confirmation: inactivity at months 6 and 12
- Guardrails: declined transactions, support tickets

### 2. Without an accounting connection, Ramp saves no time, so it's easy to drop

**Why it ranks second:** It's controllable, and the gap is large (92% connected among active accounts). But it's a smaller bucket (23%), and it's the step most likely to be blocked by someone outside the company: the bookkeeper.

**Experiment:** Randomize new accounts into control or "done-with-you" setup. In the test arm, connecting accounting is a required setup step. It offers the existing "Invite an accounting firm" flow, plus a 20-minute setup session for accounts not connected by day 21.

Two product facts shape this test (support.ramp.com, checked 12 Nov 2026). QuickBooks Online and Xero don't need the Plus plan (NetSuite and Sage Intacct do), so a Free account can finish this step. And the accounting-firm invite rejects personal email domains such as Gmail or Yahoo, so a bookkeeper who works from a personal address can't accept it. The test arm should offer the documented fallback right in the step: invite them as an internal Accounting user.

**Success metrics:**
- Primary: accounting connection rate by day 30
- Secondary: share of transactions synced and coded each month; whether the bookkeeper logs in; how many firm invites fail on email domain
- Confirmation: inactivity at months 6 and 12
- Guardrail: drop-off during setup (a required step could cost us accounts before their first transaction)

### 3. Accounts that move spend show early warning signs we're not acting on

**Why it ranks third:** "Moved spend" is 18% of inactive accounts. It's partly beyond our control, and we know the least about it. This test is as much about learning as fixing.

**Experiment:** Flag existing accounts whose monthly card spend falls 50% or more below their own three-month average. Randomize them into a holdout or an outreach arm. Outreach is a scripted call that asks why spend is falling and offers help, such as limit reviews.

**Success metrics:**
- Primary: share of flagged accounts back to 75% or more of baseline spend within 60 days
- Secondary: reasons for the drop, coded into a fixed list
- Guardrail: account manager hours per flagged account

## Sample size and decision rules

Assume a quarter brings about 2,000 new accounts, as Q1 did. Three equal cells give about 667 accounts each. Each test arm is compared with control, so the 5% significance level is split across two comparisons (2.5% each).

Worked check for the early yes/no metrics, at 80% power and the worst-case 50% baseline:
(2.24 + 0.84) × √(2 × 0.5 × 0.5 ÷ 667) ≈ 0.084.
So each cell can reliably detect a change of about **8 percentage points**. Experiment 3 needs about 670 flagged accounts per arm for the same sensitivity.

Twelve-month inactivity needs more. Detecting a fall from 26% to 21% takes about 1,370 accounts per arm. That is just over two quarters of new accounts per cell, so keep the cells running into a third quarter before reading retention.

| Test | Primary metric, read at | Ship | Rework and retest | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Spread the card | 3+ active cards, day 60 | Up 8+ points, guardrails flat | Up, but under 8 points | Flat or down, or declines and tickets rise |
| 2. Accounting at setup | Connected, day 30 | Up 8+ points, setup drop-off not higher | Up, but under 8 points | Flat, or setup drop-off rises |
| 3. Spend-drop outreach | Back to 75% of spend, day 60 | Up 8+ points vs. holdout | Up, but under 8 points | Flat, or hours per account too high to scale |

## What we should start measuring

1. **A written definition of inactive,** applied by rule, plus the month each account crossed it.
2. **Early adoption signals for every account:** days to first transaction, cards active by days 14, 30 and 60, and accounting connection date.
3. **Multiple reason tags from a fixed list,** with one marked primary.
4. **Firmographics at signup:** headcount, industry, sales channel, and whether an outside bookkeeper is involved.
5. **A monthly spend trend per account,** so we see decline as it happens.
6. **Exit reasons in the customer's own words** for "moved spend" accounts, collected within two weeks of the drop.

With these, next year's memo can say which early behaviors *predict* inactivity, and which are just signs of a smaller business.
