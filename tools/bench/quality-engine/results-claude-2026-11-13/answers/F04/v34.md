## 5.1 Where is data stored, and can it be pinned to the EU?

Atlassian Cloud runs on AWS infrastructure. By default, data is distributed across AWS regions. With **data residency**, your organisation can pin in-scope product data to a chosen location. Data residency is available for Jira and Confluence on the Standard, Premium and Enterprise plans.

For a European insurer, we recommend the **EU** location (Frankfurt and Dublin) or the **Germany** location (Frankfurt). Other locations include the UK, Switzerland, the USA, Canada, Australia, Japan, Singapore, South Korea and India.

**Data that can be pinned:**

- **Jira:** issues and field content, comments, attachments, board and sprint data, project configuration such as workflows, in-app notification data and search data.
- **Confluence:** pages and blogs, comments, attachments, page metadata, whiteboards, databases, in-app notification data and search data.

**Data that cannot be pinned:**

- User account information, held in a central identity service with replicas around the world.
- Product and user analytics, and app, audit and operational logs.
- Cached content and data in transit, each held for up to 30 days.
- Confluence permission settings and space keys.
- Connected DevOps data in Jira, such as commits, builds and deployments.

**For your migration:** backups follow the pinned location, but backups made before a pin stay where they were. We recommend pinning your sites to the EU before your data is migrated.

Third-party Marketplace apps store data according to their own design. Apps with the "Runs on Atlassian" badge are hosted on Atlassian's Forge platform and offer data residency (see 5.5).

## 5.2 How is data encrypted at rest and in transit?

**In transit:** Customer data sent over public networks is encrypted with TLS 1.2 or higher, with perfect forward secrecy.

**At rest:** Data drives that hold Jira and Confluence customer data and attachments use full-disk AES-256 encryption. Database backups are also encrypted with AES-256. By default, Atlassian manages the encryption keys through the key management service of its cloud provider, AWS.

**Customer-managed keys (CMK):** Cloud Enterprise customers can buy CMK as an add-on. CMK lets you encrypt your Jira and Confluence data with keys held in your own AWS Key Management Service account. You control those keys and can rotate or revoke them. Atlassian documents CMK for new Cloud Enterprise sites, and says tooling for existing sites is in development. If your migration uses new cloud sites, CMK can be set up before any data is moved.

## 5.3 Which certifications do we hold?

Independent auditors assess Atlassian Cloud. The table lists the certifications and attestations that cover Jira and Confluence Cloud. Each report or certificate sets out its exact scope, and you can request them through the Atlassian Trust Center. Some reports are shared under a non-disclosure agreement.

| Framework | Status for Jira and Confluence Cloud |
|---|---|
| ISO/IEC 27001:2022 | Certified. The certificate extends to the additional controls in ISO/IEC 27018:2019 for protecting personal data in public clouds. |
| SOC 2 Type II | Independent attestation, issued yearly for a 12-month period. Available under NDA. |
| BSI C5:2020 Type 2 | Completed for Jira Cloud, Jira Service Management and Confluence Cloud. |
| CSA STAR Level 1 | Self-assessment (CAIQ v4.0.3) in the CSA STAR Registry, last renewed 12 February 2026. |

**GDPR:** Atlassian offers a Data Processing Addendum, which includes Standard Contractual Clauses for international transfers.

**DORA:** Insurers are in scope of the EU Digital Operational Resilience Act. Atlassian publishes "DORA Contractual Requirements" and a "DORA Information Register" for financial entities. It lists Jira Cloud Enterprise and Confluence Cloud Enterprise among the relevant products.

## 5.4 How do administrators control access?

The Enterprise plan includes **Atlassian Guard Standard** at no extra cost. Administrators manage all of the controls below in Atlassian Administration (admin.atlassian.com).

- **Single sign-on (SSO):** SAML SSO with your identity provider, such as Microsoft Entra ID or Okta. Enterprise supports more than one identity provider. Authentication policies let you set different rules for different users, such as requiring two-step verification.
- **User provisioning (SCIM):** Users and groups are created, updated and deactivated automatically from your identity provider. When a person leaves, their access ends when they are deprovisioned.
- **IP allowlisting:** Administrators can limit access to Jira and Confluence to approved IP address ranges, such as your corporate network or VPN.
- **Domain verification and managed accounts:** Once you verify your company domains, you can manage every account that uses them, including accounts created before the migration.
- **Audit logs:** Organisation audit logs record changes to your organisation's setup and configuration.
- **Mobile app policies:** You can set security rules for Jira and Confluence data accessed through mobile apps.

**Atlassian Guard Premium** is available as an add-on. It adds features such as data classification and detection of suspicious user activity.

## 5.5 How are third-party Marketplace apps vetted?

Every Marketplace cloud app must meet Atlassian's mandatory security requirements. They are checked when an app is first listed and with each update.

- **Automated scanning:** Atlassian's EcoScanner platform screens apps continuously for code vulnerabilities, supply-chain risks, malware and misconfigurations. If an app fails a requirement, Atlassian starts remediation with the partner straight away.
- **Partner checks:** All partners pass personal and business identity checks. They are contractually bound to fix security bugs within set deadlines. They must publish a privacy policy covering how data is collected, stored and retained, their sub-processors and where data is stored.
- **Penetration testing:** Atlassian runs a programme that connects partners with CREST-certified testers. You can filter the Marketplace for apps that take part.
- **Trust badges:** "Runs on Atlassian" apps are hosted on Forge, limit data leaving Atlassian and offer data residency. "Atlassian Enterprise Certified" apps meet further enterprise needs for security, compliance, reliability and privacy, and must take part in a bug bounty programme. This badge replaces the "Cloud Fortified" badge, which ends on 31 December 2026.

**Your controls:** In admin.atlassian.com, administrators can see every installed app in one place. They can stop users from installing apps, require approval first, limit an app's access to particular projects or spaces, and review app activity in audit logs.

Partners provide their apps under their own terms. We recommend reviewing each app you plan to use. We can support an app inventory and risk review as part of the migration.

## 5.6 What happens to your data when your contract ends?

**Export:** You can export your data at any time during the subscription. This includes Jira and Confluence backups and exports, and user data from Atlassian Administration. We recommend a final export before the end date.

**After your contract ends:** The site is deactivated 15 days after the subscription period ends. For paid sites, Atlassian keeps the data for **60 days** after the subscription period ends. During that time the site can be reactivated with its data intact. After that, the data is **permanently deleted** and cannot be recovered.

**Backups:** Atlassian takes automated daily database backups for its own disaster recovery. These are encrypted, kept for 30 days and then deleted on a rolling basis. They are not used to restore data a customer has deleted.

**DORA exit requirements:** Atlassian's DORA guidance maps DORA's contract requirements, such as data retrieval before termination, to its terms.

**Marketplace apps:** Data held by third-party apps outside Atlassian's infrastructure is deleted under each partner's own terms.
