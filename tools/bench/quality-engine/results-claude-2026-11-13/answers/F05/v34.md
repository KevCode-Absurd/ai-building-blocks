# Refund Exceptions Outside the Cancellation Policy

**Community Support | One-page guide for agents**

Use this when a guest asks for money back that the listing's cancellation policy doesn't cover. Work through the four checks in order, so the same case gets the same answer from any of us.

## The four checks, in order

1. **Is it another process?** If yes, route it (see below). Stop here.
2. **Has the guest asked the host?** If not, help them ask first.
3. **Any goodwill exception in the last 12 months?** Check case history. If yes, decline.
4. **Does it meet every "say yes" test?** If yes, approve up to your limit.

## Route it elsewhere

- **Serious illness** (the guest's or a travel companion's) or **a government travel ban or restriction:** transfer to **Major Disruptive Events (MDE)**. Don't offer goodwill or promise an outcome. Say: "I'm passing this to the team that handles illness and travel bans."
- **A problem with the listing itself:** the host cancelled, the guest can't get in, the place isn't clean or safe, or it's very different from the listing. These go under the **Rebooking and Refund Policy**, not goodwill. The guest must report it within 72 hours of finding the problem.

**Asking the host:** the guest can message the host, or send a request in the **Resolution Centre** (Request money). They can do this up to 60 days after a completed stay.

## Your limit

You can approve a goodwill refund without asking anyone, up to **whichever is lower**:

- **$300**, or
- **50% of the nightly total** (nightly rate x nights booked, not counting cleaning fees, service fees or taxes)

Anything above that goes to a **team lead**.

**One per guest, per 12 months.** No exceptions.

## When to say yes

Say yes when **all** of these are true:

1. The cause was outside the guest's control (for example, an airline cancelled their flight, or their car broke down on the way).
2. They told us or the host as soon as they reasonably could.
3. They can show you something that backs it up.
4. No goodwill exception in the last 12 months.

## When to say no

- They changed their mind, found a cheaper place, or their plans shifted.
- They waited days to tell anyone, with no good reason.
- They can't or won't share any evidence.
- They already had an exception in the last 12 months.

**How to say no:** "I've looked at this carefully. This booking's cancellation policy doesn't cover it, and it doesn't meet what we need for an exception. I know that's not what you hoped to hear." Then explain what they *can* do, like asking the host directly.

## Evidence to ask for

Ask for one clear item that matches the story. Don't ask for more than you need.

- **Flight cancelled or badly delayed:** the airline's cancellation or delay notice
- **Car breakdown or accident:** a tow, repair or police report receipt
- **Other events:** anything dated that shows what happened and when

Never ask for medical records. If it's medical, it's MDE.

## How to log the decision

Log every exception case, yes or no. Paste this into the case note and fill it in:

`GOODWILL EXCEPTION | Reason: | Evidence: | Nightly total: $ | Cap: $ | Asked: $ | Approved: $ | Decision: approved / declined / escalated / sent to MDE / sent to Rebooking | Last exception: none / date`

Add the **goodwill exception** tag to approved cases only, so the 12-month check works.

## Worked examples

**Case 1: Airline cancellation, within your limit**
Guest booked 2 nights at $150. Strict policy, nothing refundable this close to check-in. Their flight was cancelled the morning of check-in and they sent the airline's email.
- Nightly total: $300. 50% = $150. Lower of $300 and $150 = **$150**.
- No exception in the last 12 months.
- **Decision:** approve up to $150. If they want the full $300, that's a team lead call.

**Case 2: Breakdown on the way, cap is $300**
Guest booked 5 nights at $200. Their car broke down on the way and they cancelled on arrival day. They sent a tow receipt dated that day.
- Nightly total: $1,000. 50% = $500. Lower of $300 and $500 = **$300**.
- **Decision:** you can approve $300. They're asking for $1,000, so if they won't accept $300, escalate to a team lead with your notes.

**Case 3: Second request in a year**
Guest wants a goodwill refund after missing a train. Case history shows a goodwill exception 8 months ago.
- **Decision:** decline, even if the story is solid. Log it as declined with the date of the earlier case.

## When you're unsure

Don't guess and don't promise. Tell the guest you're checking, and ask a team lead.
