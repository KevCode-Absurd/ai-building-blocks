# Localisation brief: "Split the cost with friends" (Japan)

**To:** Tokyo translation agency | **From:** Airbnb Product Marketing | **Launch:** next quarter | **Market:** Japan (ja-JP)

**What we need from you:** Japanese UI strings, push notifications and email copy for the feature, plus 2 to 3 options for each button and push title, with a short back-translation for each. Please follow Airbnb's existing Japanese glossary and style guide (we will send it with the string file).

## 1. The feature and who it's for

The person who books a stay can invite the friends they're travelling with to pay their own share through Airbnb. Each person pays with their own payment method. The booker no longer has to pay for everyone and then chase people for money. *(We will confirm the exact payment flow, such as deadlines and what happens if someone doesn't pay, in the string file notes.)*

**Main users:** friends aged about 20 to 35 booking a group stay. This could be a weekend trip, a ski trip, a trip for a concert or festival, or a reunion. We also want to reach the person who organises these trips. In Japan this is often the 幹事 (*kanji*), the one who books, pays and collects money from everyone.

## 2. Core message

**Book together, pay separately. Nobody gets stuck covering everyone.**

Supporting points, in order of priority:
1. Everyone pays their own share inside the app, so nobody has to collect money.
2. Everyone can see who has paid, so nobody has to ask awkwardly.
3. The organiser doesn't have to put the full cost on their own card.

## 3. Tone for Japanese

- **Polite but warm.** Use です/ます style as the base. Make it sound like a friendly, helpful service, not a bank or a government office.
- **Don't overuse keigo.** Keep 尊敬語/謙譲語 for formal moments only, such as payment errors, cancellations and legal notices. Feature screens and push notifications should sound light and natural.
- **Not childish.** Use emoji sparingly or not at all, and avoid heavy slang. One friendly sentence-ending is fine, but don't stack them.
- **Talk about the shared trip, not about money owed.** The feature should feel like it takes away an awkward task. It should never feel like Airbnb is collecting a bill on someone's behalf.

## 4. Terms that stay in English (Latin script)

- **Airbnb** (never written in katakana)
- Card brand names as shown at checkout (Visa, Mastercard, JCB and so on)

Please use the existing glossary terms for all other Airbnb concepts, such as ホスト and ゲスト. The feature name itself should be translated, not kept in English. Please give us 2 to 3 name options.

## 5. Words and ideas to avoid

- **Debt or owing:** anything like 借金, "you owe" or "pay me back". Payment reminders must never sound like debt collection.
- **Remittance words** like 送金, unless our Japan legal team approves them. Money-transfer terms have a specific legal meaning in Japan.
- **Pressure:** don't say or imply "pay now or the trip is cancelled" outside of system messages that legal has approved.
- **Claims we can't prove:** 無料, 最安, 簡単に必ず, 即時 and similar, unless the string notes confirm they are true. Japan's misleading-advertising rules are strict.
- **Naming or shaming:** reminders go only to the person who hasn't paid. Never tell the group that a named friend is "late".
- **Use 請求 (bill or charge) with care.** It's fine for receipts. In friendly copy, please suggest a softer word.

## 7. Formatting yen and dates

- **Yen:** use a half-width ¥ and comma separators, with no decimals or spaces: **¥12,345**. This matches how prices already appear in the app. Write 円 only where the style guide says to, such as in legal text. Don't mix the two forms on one screen.
- **Uneven splits:** yen has no smaller unit, so a total may not divide evenly. Don't promise "exactly equal" shares. We will tell you who covers any leftover yen.
- **Dates:** year-month-day with the weekday: **2026年1月15日(木)**. Leave out the year when it's clear from context, as in **1月15日(木)**. Use the Western calendar, not Reiwa years.
- **Times:** 24-hour format, **15:00**, in Japan Standard Time. Payment deadlines must always show both the date and the time.

## 8. Cultural points that should shape the copy

- **割り勘 (*warikan*), or splitting the bill, is normal and comfortable in Japan.** Groups expect to split costs, so the copy doesn't need to sell the idea of splitting. It should sell the fact that the organiser no longer has to deal with collecting.
- **The organiser does a lot of work.** The 幹事 usually books, pays first and then collects money, often in cash or by PayPay. Copy aimed at the organiser should acknowledge that this work is real and feel considerate. It shouldn't feel like a sales pitch.
- **Splits aren't always equal.** 傾斜割り勘 (*keisha warikan*) means older or senior members pay more, and people sometimes split by room or by nights stayed. Avoid copy that assumes everyone always pays the same amount.
- **Asking friends for money feels awkward.** People avoid direct reminders. Our reminders should come from Airbnb, sound neutral and give a clear deadline, so friends never have to chase each other.
- **People expect precision.** Japanese users expect exact amounts, clear deadlines and clear rules for cancellations and refunds. Being vague here will lose trust faster than a formal tone will.
