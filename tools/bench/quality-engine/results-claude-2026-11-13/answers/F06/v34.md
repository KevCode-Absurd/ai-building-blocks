# Localisation brief: "Split the cost with friends" (Japan)

**To:** Tokyo translation agency | **From:** Airbnb Product Marketing | **Launch:** next quarter | **Market:** Japan (ja-JP)

**What we need:** Japanese UI strings, push notifications and email copy, with 2 to 3 options for each button and push title, each with a back-translation and character count. Follow Airbnb's Japanese glossary and style guide, which comes with the string file.

## 1. Who it's for

The person who books a stay can invite their friends to pay their own share through Airbnb, each with their own payment method. Nobody has to pay for everyone and then chase money. It's for groups of friends travelling together: weekend trips, ski trips, concerts, festivals, reunions. Above all it's for the organiser, the 幹事 (*kanji*), who books, pays and collects from everyone.

## 2. Core message

**Book together, pay separately. Nobody gets stuck covering everyone.**

Supporting points, in priority order:
1. Everyone pays their own share in the app, so nobody has to collect money.
2. Everyone can see who has paid, so nobody has to ask awkwardly.
3. The organiser doesn't have to put the full cost on their own card.

## 3. Tone

- **Polite but warm.** です/ます in sentences. A friendly service, not a bank or a government office.
- **Buttons and labels follow airbnb.jp:** short noun phrases or plain verb forms, not です/ます. Live examples: 検索をはじめる, ホストになる, すべて表示.
- **Keigo only where it's needed:** payment errors, cancellations and legal notices. Feature screens and pushes stay light, with few or no emoji and no heavy slang.
- **Talk about the shared trip, not money owed.** Airbnb takes away an awkward task. It never collects a bill.

## 4. Terms that stay in English

- **Airbnb**, never in katakana, as on airbnb.jp.
- Card brand names as shown at checkout (Visa, Mastercard, JCB).

Use the glossary for other Airbnb terms, such as ホスト and ゲスト. Translate the feature name and give us 2 to 3 options.

## 5. Placeholders and names

- Keep placeholders exactly as written, braces included: {name}, {amount}, {date}. Move them wherever Japanese word order needs.
- Write a friend's name as {name}さん.
- Don't build sentences from joined fragments. Tell us if a string can't work as one full sentence.

## 6. Words and ideas to avoid

- **Debt or owing:** 借金, "you owe", "pay me back". Reminders must never sound like debt collection.
- **Remittance words** such as 送金, unless our Japan legal team approves. Money-transfer terms have a legal meaning in Japan.
- **Pressure:** no "pay now or the trip is cancelled" outside legal-approved system messages.
- **Unproven claims:** 無料, 最安, 簡単に必ず, 即時, unless the string notes confirm them. Japan's misleading-advertising law (景品表示法) is strict.
- **Naming or shaming:** reminders go only to the person who hasn't paid, never to the group.
- **請求:** fine on receipts. In friendly copy, suggest a softer word.

## 7. Character limits

| Element | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| In-app button | **12 characters** | Every character counts as 1, full-width or half-width. No line breaks. |
| Push title | **20 characters** | ¥, digits, commas and "JPY" all count. Also give a version without the amount. |
| Push body | No fixed limit | Put the point first. Lock screens cut long bodies. |

## 8. Yen and dates

Match how airbnb.jp shows them today.

- **Yen:** **¥107,500 JPY**, with nights in brackets: **¥107,500 JPY (5泊)**. That amount alone uses 12 of the 20 title characters, so keep amounts out of push titles where you can.
- **Uneven splits:** yen has no smaller unit, so don't promise "exactly equal" shares. We'll tell you who covers leftover yen.
- **Dates:** **1月15日**; ranges as **12月13日～18日**. Add the year, as in 2026年1月15日(木), when it isn't clear from context. Payment deadlines add the weekday: **1月15日(木)**. Western calendar, not Reiwa years.
- **Times:** 24-hour, Japan Standard Time: **15:00**. Deadlines always show date and time.

## 9. Cultural points

- **割り勘 (*warikan*), splitting the bill, is normal in Japan.** Don't sell splitting. Sell the end of collecting.
- **The 幹事 does real work:** books, pays first, then collects in cash or through apps such as PayPay, which has a group payment feature. Recognise that work; don't pitch at it.
- **Splits aren't always equal.** In 傾斜割り勘 (*keisha warikan*) senior members pay more, and groups may split by room or nights. Don't assume equal shares.
- **Asking friends for money is awkward.** Reminders come from Airbnb, sound neutral and give a clear deadline.
- **Precision builds trust:** exact amounts, clear deadlines, clear cancellation and refund rules.
