# Supplier scorecard: paper bags for bricks inside boxes

**For:** Sourcing Steering Group | **Need:** 600m bags a year, all sites | **Suppliers:** A (Germany), B (Poland), C (Mexico)
*The supplier data is for this exercise only.*

## Summary

- **No single supplier can meet our need.** The largest, Supplier A, can make 400m bags a year, which leaves us 200m short. So a split award isn't just the better option. It's the only option that covers 600m.
- **Supplier A scores highest overall (8.31 out of 10), and Supplier B is close behind (7.76).** B is the cheapest. A has the most capacity and a shorter lead time.
- **Supplier C (6.65) is held back mainly because its FSC certification is still pending.** If C gets certified, its score rises to 7.55. C is also the only supplier close to Monterrey, our largest factory.
- **Recommendation:** split the award three ways: **A 300m, B 200m, C 100m** for Monterrey. C's share depends on FSC certification. This costs **€12.30m a year**. That's €0.45m (3.8%) more than the cheapest workable split. In return, no supplier runs above 80% of its capacity, we have 350m bags of spare capacity, and supply comes from two regions instead of one.

## 2. Criteria and weights

| Criterion | Weight | How it's scored (0 to 10) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | 30% | 10 × (lowest price ÷ this supplier's price) |
| Capacity | 25% | 10 × (capacity ÷ 600m), with a maximum of 10 |
| Lead time | 15% | 10 × (shortest lead time ÷ this supplier's lead time) |
| Certification | 15% | FSC certified = 10; pending = 4 |
| Location risk | 15% | Judgment score (see reasons below) |

**Why these weights:** Cost carries the most weight because this is a high-volume, low-cost item. A 0.1 cent change per bag is worth €0.6m a year. Capacity comes second because none of the suppliers can cover the full volume. Certification gets 15%, but it also works as a **pass or fail gate**: we won't put product in boxes until the paper is FSC certified, however high the score.

**Location risk:**
- **A (9):** It's inside the EU, with no customs checks on the way to our European factories in Billund, Kladno and Nyíregyháza. Road freight is short. The main risk is that our European supply depends heavily on one region.
- **B (9):** The same EU advantages as A, and it's very close to Kladno and Nyíregyháza. Its long lead time is already counted under the lead-time criterion, so we don't count it again here.
- **C (7):** It's close to Monterrey, which is low risk for Monterrey's own supply. But bags for Europe would have to cross the Atlantic, so C works best for Monterrey only. C is also more exposed to changes in North American trade policy and border delays.

## 5. Split award options

All options cover 600m bags. Costs use the quoted prices and **don't include freight** (see the next steps section).

| Option | Split (A / B / C) | Annual cost | Average per bag | Share of each supplier's capacity used (A / B / C) | Spare capacity | Supply regions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single supplier | Not possible | n/a | n/a | A would be 200m short | n/a | n/a |
| 1. Lowest cost | 350 / 250 / 0 | €11.85m | €0.01975 | 88% / **100%** / 0% | 50m | Europe only |
| 2. A-heavy | 400 / 200 / 0 | €12.00m | €0.02000 | **100%** / 80% / 0% | 50m | Europe only |
| **3. Recommended** | **300 / 200 / 100** | **€12.30m** | **€0.02050** | **75% / 80% / 33%** | **350m** | **Europe + Americas** |
| 4. Larger C share | 250 / 200 / 150 | €12.45m | €0.02075 | 63% / 80% / 50% | 400m | Europe + Americas |

**How the costs are worked out:**
- Option 1: (350m × €0.021) + (250m × €0.018) = €7.35m + €4.50m = **€11.85m**
- Option 2: (400m × €0.021) + (200m × €0.018) = €8.40m + €3.60m = **€12.00m**
- Option 3: (300m × €0.021) + (200m × €0.018) + (100m × €0.024) = €6.30m + €3.60m + €2.40m = **€12.30m**
- Option 4: (250m × €0.021) + (200m × €0.018) + (150m × €0.024) = €5.25m + €3.60m + €3.60m = **€12.45m**

**Why Option 3 is better than Options 1 and 2:**
- **No supplier runs at full capacity.** In Options 1 and 2, one supplier works at 100%. Then any breakdown, paper shortage or rise in demand turns straight into missing bags. Option 3 keeps every supplier at 80% or below.
- **Spare capacity rises from 50m bags to 350m bags.** If any one supplier stopped completely, the other two could cover most of its volume. That includes A's 300m: B has 50m spare and C has 200m spare.
- **We'd no longer depend on one region.** Options 1 and 2 put all supply in Central Europe, a few hundred kilometres apart. Option 3 adds a supplier near Monterrey with an 8-week lead time.
- **The extra cost is small and may shrink.** It's €0.45m a year more than Option 1, or €0.00075 per bag. Shipping bags from Europe to Monterrey costs extra freight and takes longer in transit. The quotes don't show that cost yet, but it will reduce the gap.

We don't recommend Option 4. It costs another €0.15m, and C's spare capacity matters less than its certification.

## 6. Recommendation and conditions

1. **Award A 300m bags a year (50%) and B 200m bags a year (33%)** on 2 to 3 year agreements. Each agreement should allow volume to flex by ±20%, so that either supplier can take more if the other has a problem.
2. **Award C 100m bags a year (17%) for Monterrey, on condition that it gets FSC certification.** No purchase order should be placed until C has a valid FSC chain-of-custody certificate. Agree a deadline in the contract.
3. **Until C is certified, use Option 1** (A 350m, B 250m) and ship to Monterrey from Europe. Keep extra safety stock to cover the longer transit time.
4. **Plan around B's 14-week lead time.** Ask B to hold buffer stock on site, or give B rolling forecasts that are fixed for at least 14 weeks.

## 7. Next steps before the final decision

- **Confirm Monterrey's real yearly bag volume.** This scorecard assumes 100m. C's share should match Monterrey's actual demand.
- **Get freight and delivery-terms quotes** to each site, so we can compare total landed cost, not just price per bag.
- **Get C's FSC timeline in writing,** including the auditor's name and the planned audit date.
- **Run quality and line trials** at one European site and at Monterrey for every supplier, including tests with the packing machines.
- **Check each supplier's finances,** and confirm whether their capacity figures are free capacity or include volume already promised to other customers.
