# The owner rubric: judging work the way the person who asked for it would

Use this for any agent's output, in any field. The judge acts as the owner who wrote the brief and will live with the result. They know their business, their data, their brand and their customers, and their private notes are given to the judge. The question is never "is it long, structured or clever?" It is "would I be glad I got this, and can I use it?"

Score each dimension from 1 to 10. A 10 is what the best specialist you could hire would hand you. A 5 is usable only after real rework. A 1 means you would bin it.

| # | Dimension | The owner's question | A 10 looks like | A 3 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fit to my case** | Is this about my business, my data and my question? | Built on my numbers and my situation; it catches the signals in what I gave it (the segment that wins, the paused winner, the tracking gap, the constraint) and acts on them | Generic advice that would fit any company; misses or misreads what my data shows |
| 2 | **New insight** | Did it tell me something I didn't know, or show me a gap in my thinking or my ability to execute? | Names one or two things I hadn't seen, with the evidence, and says plainly where my current approach is wrong | Restates the brief or common knowledge |
| 3 | **Priorities** | Does it put the highest-leverage, lowest-effort moves first? | A clear order with the reason for it; the first step is something I can do this week; it says what to stop or skip | A flat list; everything is "important"; no first step |
| 4 | **Reasoning** | Does it explain why this will work for us, and does the logic hold? | Each recommendation says why it fits our case; no contradictions; trade-offs and risks named | Assertions with no why; claims that contradict each other or the data |
| 5 | **Correct** | Are the numbers, facts and rules right? | Every number traces to my data with the working shown; no invented facts, results or reviews; platform and legal rules right | Arithmetic errors that change a decision; made-up facts; risky claims |
| 6 | **Clear** | Can I read it fast and know what to do? | Plain words at the right reading level, no jargon, scannable, short where it can be | Dense, padded, jargon-heavy, or so long that the point gets lost |
| 7 | **Voice and audience** | Does it sound like us and speak to our customer in their words? | For customer-facing work: our voice, our words and none of our banned ones, tailored to the persona and using their phrases. For internal work: pitched right for the reader | Off-brand tone, generic marketing voice, the wrong audience |
| 8 | **Range and originality** | Does it give me real options and fresh ideas? | Genuinely different angles, formats or concepts, with at least one idea I wouldn't have thought of | Ten versions of one idea; clichés |
| 9 | **Ready to use** | Could I run this tomorrow with a few tweaks? | Complete, finished and ready to paste, launch or hand to the team; no placeholders | A draft of a draft: missing parts, placeholders, homework for me |

## Verdict fields

- **use_as_is**: "as is", "with small tweaks", "with major rework" or "not usable".
- **biggest_strength** and **biggest_mistake**: one sentence each, specific.
- **what_would_make_it_a_10**: one sentence.
- **winner**: when two outputs are compared, the one the owner would rather have paid for. Call "tie" only when you would truly pay the same for either.
- **overall**: 1 to 10, the owner's gut score. It is not an average: one serious fault (wrong numbers, off brand, generic) can sink it.

## Rules for judges

- Read the owner's notes first. They are what the owner knows: the signals in the data, what would delight them and what would annoy them.
- Do not reward length, headings or confidence. Padding, hedging and repetition count against an output.
- A wrong number that changes a decision, a false claim, or an off-brand line in customer-facing copy is a serious fault.
- Check two or three numbers that drive the main decision against the brief.
- Judge the work, not the format, unless the format makes it harder to use.
